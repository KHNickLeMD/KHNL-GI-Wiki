## Summary

This ASGE Standards of Practice Committee guideline uses GRADE methodology to provide evidence-based recommendations on screening for pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (the meaning of "pancreatic cancer" throughout the document) in individuals at increased risk due to genetic susceptibility. Pancreatic cancer is a rare but lethal disease (lifetime incidence ~1.6%, 5-year survival ~10%, 3% of new cancers and 8% of cancer deaths in the US in 2020). Because ~80% present with advanced/inoperable disease while screen-detected/early-stage disease has dramatically better survival (93% 10-year survival for stage 0; 34%-39% 5-year survival for stage I), the panel evaluated whether stage-shifting via screening of high-risk individuals improves outcomes. The 2019 USPSTF recommendation against screening explicitly did not apply to high-risk genetic populations.

The guideline focuses on BRCA1 and BRCA2 because of their higher population prevalence, while also addressing (non-GRADE, for the age-to-start question) familial pancreatic cancer (FPC), familial atypical multiple mole melanoma (FAMMM), Peutz-Jeghers syndrome, ataxia-telangiectasia (ATM heterozygotes), Lynch syndrome, hereditary pancreatitis, and PALB2. FPC kindreds are defined as having at least a pair of first-degree relatives with pancreatic cancer without an association with a known hereditary cancer syndrome.

A systematic review/meta-analysis of 25 studies underpinned the recommendations. Pooled yield for any high-risk lesion was 3.1% (95% CI 2.2%-4.3%); for resectable/borderline-resectable lesions 2.1% (95% CI 1.4%-3.1%); 60% of screen-detected cancers were resectable or borderline-resectable (vs ~20% of symptom-detected). No adverse events from screening EUS or MRI were reported. However, harms were notable: low-yield pancreatic surgery occurred in 2.8% of all screened patients but 46.6% of patients who underwent surgery, and the pooled adverse-event rate among operated patients was 19.9%. Screening was associated with psychological benefit (distress declined over time) and was cost-effective in high-risk populations.

The panel made conditional recommendations (low to very low quality of evidence) for screening, for annual frequency, for EUS and/or MRI as modalities, and for genetic-condition-specific starting ages. The key change from prior guidance ("What Is New"): all patients with a BRCA1/2 pathogenic variant — regardless of family history of pancreatic cancer — should undergo screening (previous guidelines limited screening to those with a family history).

## Key Findings / Claims (Complete Recommendation List — verbatim/near-verbatim)

The guideline addressed 5 GRADE clinical questions yielding numbered recommendations. Stronger recommendations are stated as "we recommend…"; weaker (conditional) as "we suggest…."

- **Recommendation 1.** "In individuals at increased risk of pancreatic cancer because of genetic susceptibility, we suggest screening for pancreatic cancer compared with no screening." *(conditional recommendation, low quality of evidence)*

- **Recommendation 2.** "In patients at increased risk of pancreatic cancer because of genetic susceptibility, we suggest screening with EUS, EUS alternating with MRI, or MRI based on patient preference and available expertise." *(conditional recommendation, very low quality of evidence)*
  - **EUS may be preferred:** as the initial screening test; for patients at very high risk for pancreatic cancer like Peutz-Jeghers syndrome and FAMMM; when EUS can be combined with screening upper endoscopy or colonoscopy (eg, Lynch and Peutz-Jeghers syndrome); when there is a contraindication to MRI (eg, claustrophobia, contrast allergy, implanted metal, and renal failure). A linear-array echoendoscope may be preferable over a radial echoendoscope (RCT: detected more pancreas lesions, 82% vs 67%, P < .001).
  - **MRI may be preferred:** for patients at increased risk of adverse events from anesthesia or endoscopic procedures; for patients who place a high value on avoiding invasive testing; when MRI may be combined with other imaging (eg, enterography for Peutz-Jeghers syndrome). A contrast-enhanced exam using intravenous agents is preferred, a minimum of 1.5-T magnet should be applied using phased-array coils, and a 3-T magnet may have additional advantage in detection of small pancreatic lesions because of superior soft tissue resolution.

- **Recommendation 3a.** "In individuals with BRCA2 pathogenic variant, we suggest screening for pancreatic cancer compared with no screening." *(conditional recommendation, very low quality of evidence)*

- **Recommendation 3b.** "In individuals with BRCA1 pathogenic variant, we suggest screening for pancreatic cancer compared with no screening." *(conditional recommendation, very low quality of evidence)*

- **Recommendation 4.** "In individuals at increased risk of pancreatic cancer because of genetic susceptibility, we suggest that annual screening be performed." *(conditional recommendation, very low quality of evidence)*

- **Recommendation 5.** "In individuals at increased risk for pancreatic cancer because of genetic susceptibility, we suggest that the starting age for screening should vary based on the underlying genetic condition." *(conditional recommendation, very low quality of evidence)*

- **Recommendation 6 — starting ages (non-GRADE; "for each of the following conditions, we recommend the following starting ages"):**
  - (a) **BRCA2 pathogenic variant:** age 50 or 10 years earlier than the youngest relative with pancreatic cancer.
  - (b) **BRCA1 pathogenic variant:** age 50 or 10 years earlier than the youngest relative with pancreatic cancer.
  - (c) **PALB2 pathogenic variant:** age 50 or 10 years earlier than the youngest relative with pancreatic cancer.
  - (d) **FPC syndrome:** age 50 or 10 years earlier than the youngest relative with pancreatic cancer (screening is recommended for all first-degree relatives of affected family members).
  - (e) **FAMMM syndrome:** age 40 or 10 years earlier than the youngest relative with pancreatic cancer.
  - (f) **Peutz-Jeghers syndrome:** age 35 or 10 years earlier than the youngest relative with pancreatic cancer.
  - (g) **Heterozygotes for ATM pathogenic variant with first- or second-degree relative with pancreatic cancer:** age 50 or 10 years earlier than the youngest relative with pancreatic cancer.
  - (h) **Lynch syndrome with first- or second-degree relative with pancreatic cancer:** age 50 or 10 years earlier than the youngest relative with pancreatic cancer.
  - (i) **Autosomal-dominant hereditary pancreatitis:** age 40.

### Supporting data points
- Pooled yield of screening for high-risk lesions: 3.1% (95% CI 2.2%-4.3%); for resectable/borderline-resectable lesions: 2.1% (95% CI 1.4%-3.1%).
- 60% of screen-detected cancers were resectable/borderline-resectable (95% CI 43.7%-74.4%) vs ~20% of symptom-detected (population data: only 20% of symptomatic cancers resectable/borderline-resectable, 30% locally advanced, 50% metastatic).
- One study: 3-year survival significantly higher in screen-detected vs noncompliant symptomatic individuals (85% vs 25%).
- Harms: pooled low-yield pancreatic surgery 2.8% of all screened (defined as surgery not yielding cancer, high-grade dysplasia, or grade III pancreatic intraepithelial neoplasia); among 181 operated patients, 46.6% had low-yield surgery; pooled adverse-event rate among operated patients 19.9% (95% CI 7.4%-43.4%). No adverse events from screening EUS or MRI.
- **BRCA2 risk:** pooled RR 5.1 (95% CI 3.9-6.3) → absolute lifetime risk to age 80 ~5.2%; pooled SIR 7.2 (95% CI 1.5-13.0) → cumulative lifetime risk ~7.4%.
- **BRCA1 risk:** pooled RR 1.9 (95% CI 1.0-2.8) → cumulative lifetime risk ~3.5%; pooled SIR 3.7 (95% CI 2.5-4.8) → cumulative lifetime risk ~3.8%. Magnitude lower than BRCA2 and not clearly crossing the 5% lifetime-risk threshold; panel still suggested screening, citing selection bias, overlapping CIs (up to 4.8%), and equivalent chemotherapy response between BRCA1/2 cancers.
- BRCA1/2 germline pathogenic variants affect up to 7% of all patients diagnosed with pancreatic cancer; ~2 of 3 BRCA-associated pancreatic cancers would be missed if screening were limited to those with a family history — hence the panel did **not** require a family history of pancreatic cancer for BRCA1/2 carriers to be screened.
- Modeling: progression from localized to advanced stage can occur within 1 year → basis for annual screening.
- Age-to-start logic: screening starts ~1 standard deviation below mean age of cancer diagnosis for that population; for conditions with ≥10 RR (Peutz-Jeghers, FAMMM) screening starts 2 SD below mean age.
- BRCA-deficient/homologous-recombination-deficient tumors respond to platinum-based chemotherapy and PARP inhibitors (eg, maintenance olaparib), supporting screening in this population.
- EUS was more sensitive than MRI at detecting small solid lesions in 2 studies (almost all solid cancers found only by EUS), though differences in pooled high-risk-lesion yield between EUS and MRI did not reach significance.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- Diverges from 2019 USPSTF (recommends against screening) — but USPSTF explicitly excluded high-risk genetic populations, so not a true contradiction.
- BRCA1 lifetime risk (~3.5%-3.8%) does not clearly cross the 5% high-risk threshold adopted by the panel; the screening suggestion for BRCA1 carriers rests on weak/very-low-quality evidence and panel judgment.
- Quality of evidence is low to very low throughout; no clinical trials compared screened vs unscreened controls. Patients must be counseled on the substantial harms (low-yield surgery, perioperative adverse events) before enrollment.
- Future-directions gaps: role of other risk factors (age, male sex, obesity, smoking, alcohol) in genetic carriers; need for biomarkers/circulating tumor cells/DNA; management/interval after a normal baseline exam is undefined.
