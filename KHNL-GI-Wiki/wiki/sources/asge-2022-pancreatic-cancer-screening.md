## Summary

This American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) Standards of Practice Committee guideline gives evidence-based recommendations on screening for [[pancreatic-cancer|pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma]] (the meaning of "pancreatic cancer" throughout the document) in individuals at increased risk due to genetic susceptibility — see [[pancreatic-cancer-screening|high-risk pancreatic cancer surveillance]] for the synthesised wiki pathway. Pancreatic cancer is a rare but lethal disease (lifetime incidence ~1.6%, 5-year survival ~10%, 3% of new cancers and 8% of cancer deaths in the US in 2020). Because ~80% present with advanced/inoperable disease while screen-detected/early-stage disease has dramatically better survival (93% 10-year survival for stage 0; 34%-39% 5-year survival for stage I), the panel evaluated whether stage-shifting via screening of high-risk individuals improves outcomes. The 2019 US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) recommendation against screening explicitly did not apply to high-risk genetic populations.

The guideline focuses on [[brca-pathogenic-variants|BRCA1 and BRCA2]] because of their higher population prevalence. Frequency and starting age were addressed in a **non-GRADE format** for a named list of genetic susceptibility conditions: [[familial-pancreatic-cancer|familial pancreatic cancer (FPC)]], [[fammm-syndrome|familial atypical multiple mole melanoma (FAMMM)]], [[peutz-jeghers-syndrome|Peutz-Jeghers syndrome]], ataxia telangiectasia due to mutation in the ataxia telangiectasia mutated (ATM) gene, [[lynch-syndrome|Lynch syndrome]], and [[hereditary-pancreatitis|hereditary pancreatitis]]. PALB2 is not in that list but does receive a starting age in Recommendation 6. FPC kindreds are defined as kindreds containing at least a pair of first-degree relatives with pancreatic cancer without an association with a known hereditary cancer syndrome; modeling studies suggest autosomal-dominant inheritance of a rare allele as the likely etiology.

A systematic review/meta-analysis of 25 studies underpinned the recommendations. Pooled yield for any high-risk lesion was 3.1% (95% confidence interval [CI] 2.2%-4.3%); for resectable/borderline-resectable lesions 2.1% (95% CI 1.4%-3.1%); 60% of screen-detected cancers were resectable or borderline-resectable (vs ~20% of symptom-detected). No adverse events from screening [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]] or [[mri-mrcp|magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)]] were reported. However, harms were notable: low-yield pancreatic surgery occurred in 2.8% of all screened patients but 46.6% of patients who underwent surgery, and the pooled adverse-event rate among operated patients was 19.9%. Screening was associated with psychological benefit (distress declined over time) and was cost-effective in high-risk populations.

The panel made conditional recommendations (low to very low quality of evidence) for screening, for annual frequency, for EUS and/or MRI as modalities, and for genetic-condition-specific starting ages. The key change from prior guidance ("What Is New"): all patients with a BRCA1/2 pathogenic variant — regardless of family history of pancreatic cancer — should undergo screening (previous guidelines limited screening to those with a family history).

## Key Findings / Claims (Complete Recommendation List — verbatim/near-verbatim)

**How the document labels its own recommendations.** It numbers them itself — Recommendations 1, 2, 3a, 3b, 4, 5, 6 — each boxed in the text and collected in Table 1 (Summary of recommendations). Grades below are the document's own wording. Three clinical questions were posed in the GRADE format (Questions 1, 2, and 3a/3b); frequency (Question 4) and starting age (Question 5) were addressed in a **non-GRADE format**, yet their boxed recommendations and Table 1 still carry *(conditional recommendation, very low quality of evidence)*. **Recommendation 6 (the starting ages) carries no grade at all** and is the only one phrased "we recommend." Per the Methods: stronger recommendations are typically stated as "we recommend…," weaker recommendations are indicated by phrases such as "we suggest…."

**Outcome definitions used throughout** (these define what counts as a positive screen):
- **Yield of screening** = detection of any high-risk lesion, pancreatic cancer, high-grade dysplasia, or grade III pancreatic intraepithelial neoplasia (PanIN).
- **Surgically resectable and borderline-resectable lesions** = any **T1-3** and **N0-2** pancreatic cancer, high-grade dysplasia, or grade III PanIN.
- **Harms** = harms from screening tests, rate of low-yield pancreatic surgery in the screened population, and rate of adverse events from pancreatic cancer surgery resulting from positive screening tests.
- **Low-yield surgery** = surgery that did not yield cancer, high-grade dysplasia, or grade III PanIN.

- **Recommendation 1.** "In individuals at increased risk of pancreatic cancer because of genetic susceptibility, we suggest screening for pancreatic cancer compared with no screening." *(conditional recommendation, low quality of evidence)*

- **Recommendation 2.** "In patients at increased risk of pancreatic cancer because of genetic susceptibility, we suggest screening with EUS, EUS alternating with MRI, or MRI based on patient preference and available expertise." *(conditional recommendation, very low quality of evidence)*
  - **EUS may be preferred:** as the initial screening test; for patients at very high risk for pancreatic cancer like Peutz-Jeghers syndrome and FAMMM; when EUS can be combined with screening [[upper-endoscopy|upper endoscopy]] or [[colonoscopy]] (eg, Lynch and Peutz-Jeghers syndrome); when there is a contraindication to MRI (eg, claustrophobia, contrast allergy, implanted metal, and renal failure). A linear-array echoendoscope may be preferable over a radial echoendoscope (randomized controlled trial [RCT]: detected more pancreas lesions, 82% vs 67%, P < .001).
  - **MRI may be preferred:** for patients at increased risk of adverse events from anesthesia or endoscopic procedures; for patients who place a high value on avoiding invasive testing; when MRI may be combined with other imaging (eg, enterography for Peutz-Jeghers syndrome). A contrast-enhanced exam using intravenous agents is preferred, a minimum of 1.5-T magnet should be applied using phased-array coils, and a 3-T magnet may have additional advantage in detection of small pancreatic lesions because of superior soft tissue resolution.

- **Recommendation 3a.** "In individuals with BRCA2 pathogenic variant, we suggest screening for pancreatic cancer compared with no screening." *(conditional recommendation, very low quality of evidence)*

- **Recommendation 3b.** "In individuals with BRCA1 pathogenic variant, we suggest screening for pancreatic cancer compared with no screening." *(conditional recommendation, very low quality of evidence)*

- **Recommendation 4.** "In individuals at increased risk of pancreatic cancer because of genetic susceptibility, we suggest that annual screening be performed." *(conditional recommendation, very low quality of evidence)*

- **Recommendation 5.** "In individuals at increased risk of pancreatic cancer because of genetic susceptibility, we suggest that the starting age for screening should vary based on the underlying genetic condition." *(conditional recommendation, very low quality of evidence)*

- **Recommendation 6 — starting ages.** No grade is attached. Stem as published: "For each of the following conditions, we recommend the following starting ages:"

| | Condition | Start screening at |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | BRCA2 pathogenic variant | age **50** or 10 y earlier than the youngest relative with pancreatic cancer |
| (b) | BRCA1 pathogenic variant | age **50** or 10 y earlier than the youngest relative with pancreatic cancer |
| (c) | PALB2 pathogenic variant | age **50** or 10 y earlier than the youngest relative with pancreatic cancer |
| (d) | FPC syndrome | age **50** or 10 y earlier than the youngest relative with pancreatic cancer (screening is recommended for all first-degree relatives of affected family members) |
| (e) | FAMMM syndrome | age **40** or 10 y earlier than the youngest relative with pancreatic cancer |
| (f) | Peutz-Jeghers syndrome | age **35** or 10 y earlier than the youngest relative with pancreatic cancer |
| (g) | Heterozygotes for ATM pathogenic variant **with a first- or second-degree relative with pancreatic cancer** | age **50** or 10 y earlier than the youngest relative with pancreatic cancer |
| (h) | Lynch syndrome **with a first- or second-degree relative with pancreatic cancer** | age **50** or 10 y earlier than the youngest relative with pancreatic cancer |
| (i) | Autosomal-dominant hereditary pancreatitis | age **40** |

### Supporting data points
- Evidence base: 25 studies. By modality — 6 used EUS only (n = 338), 5 used MRI only (n = 455), 14 used a combination of EUS and MRI (n = 2460).
- Pooled yield of screening for high-risk lesions: 3.1% (95% CI 2.2%-4.3%); for resectable/borderline-resectable lesions: 2.1% (95% CI 1.4%-3.1%).
- **Pooled yield by modality — no significant difference.** High-risk lesions: EUS 4.0% (95% CI 1.7%-9.1%), MRI 2.4% (95% CI 1.0%-5.4%), combination 3.1% (95% CI 2.1%-4.6%). Resectable lesions: EUS 3.9% (95% CI 1.7%-8.5%), MRI 1.8% (95% CI 0.8%-4.0%), combination 1.7% (95% CI 1.0%-3.0%). The trend favoring EUS may reflect referral bias (higher-risk patients more likely to undergo EUS).
- Six studies (n = 350) reported on adverse outcomes from screening EUS or MRI and found none; evidence rated down for imprecision, so overall quality very low.
- 60% of screen-detected cancers were resectable/borderline-resectable (95% CI 43.7%-74.4%) vs ~20% of symptom-detected (population data: only 20% of symptomatic cancers resectable/borderline-resectable, 30% locally advanced, 50% metastatic).
- One study: 3-year survival significantly higher in screen-detected vs noncompliant symptomatic individuals (85% vs 25%).
- Harms: pooled low-yield pancreatic surgery 2.8% (95% CI 1.9%-4.1%) of all screened; among the 181 patients who underwent pancreatic surgery, 46.6% (95% CI 34.2%-59.4%) had low-yield surgery; pooled adverse-event rate among operated patients 19.9% (95% CI 7.4%-43.4%). No adverse events from screening EUS or MRI. EUS and MRI are safe but costly and may result in overdiagnosis.
- **BRCA2 risk:** pooled relative risk (RR) 5.1 (95% CI 3.9-6.3) → absolute lifetime risk to age 80 ~5.2%; pooled standardized incidence ratio (SIR) 7.2 (95% CI 1.5-13.0) → cumulative lifetime risk ~7.4%.
- **BRCA1 risk:** pooled RR 1.9 (95% CI 1.0-2.8) → cumulative lifetime risk ~3.5%; pooled SIR 3.7 (95% CI 2.5-4.8) → cumulative lifetime risk ~3.8%. Magnitude lower than BRCA2 and not clearly crossing the 5% lifetime-risk threshold; panel still suggested screening, citing selection bias, overlapping CIs (up to 4.8%), and equivalent chemotherapy response between BRCA1/2 cancers.
- **BRCA1/2 combined:** of the 25 included studies, 8 (n = 375) reported on BRCA1/2; pooled yield of screening for high-risk lesions in these individuals 8.6% (95% CI 4.5%-16.0%). Yield was **not** reported separately for BRCA1 vs BRCA2, and no harms data specific to BRCA1/2 were available.
- **BRCA1 — how to act on the weak signal:** given the potentially low rate of pancreatic cancer in this population, clinicians should **initiate screening with caution**; patients should be made aware of their risk of developing pancreatic cancer and the potential harms from screening; **screening may not be warranted in patients who place a high value on avoiding harms from medical interventions**.
- No significant difference in pancreatic cancer risk between men and women for either BRCA1 or BRCA2; reporting on family history within these cohorts was limited.
- BRCA1/2 germline pathogenic variants affect up to 7% of all patients diagnosed with pancreatic cancer; ~2 of 3 BRCA-associated pancreatic cancers would be missed if screening were limited to those with a family history — hence the panel did **not** require a family history of pancreatic cancer for BRCA1/2 carriers to be screened.
- **Basis for annual screening (Question 4):** no study assessed patient outcomes by screening frequency. Of the 25 included studies, 9 reported screening intervals, of which **8 screened annually or sooner**. Two tumor-progression models (one from mean age differences between early- and advanced-tumor patients, one from imaging done before diagnosis) both predicted that progression from localized to advanced stage could occur **within 1 year**.
- Age-to-start logic: screening starts ~1 standard deviation below mean age of cancer diagnosis for that population; for conditions with ≥10 RR (Peutz-Jeghers, FAMMM) screening starts 2 standard deviations below mean age.
- BRCA-deficient/homologous-recombination-deficient tumors respond to platinum-based chemotherapy and poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors (eg, maintenance olaparib), supporting screening in this population.
- EUS was more sensitive than MRI at detecting small solid lesions in 2 studies (almost all solid cancers found only by EUS), though differences in pooled high-risk-lesion yield between EUS and MRI did not reach significance.
- **Threshold for "high risk":** lifetime risk of pancreatic cancer **>5%** *or* RR **>5** — proposed elsewhere as the threshold for identifying high-risk individuals and **adopted by this panel**. Also found to be cost-effective at that level of risk.
- **Who the guideline explicitly does *not* endorse screening for** (stated under Question 5, ungraded):
  - **Family history of pancreatic cancer not meeting FPC criteria** — approximately **2-fold** increased risk; degree of relatedness and age at onset in the index patient **do not appear to affect** pancreatic cancer risk. "Pancreatic cancer screening for these patients is also generally not recommended."
  - **Non-genetic risk factors alone** — diabetes, older age, cigarette smoking, [[obesity]], or a history of [[chronic-pancreatitis|chronic pancreatitis]] increase risk but to a **lesser degree** than the genetic conditions above. These are stated to be **outside the purview of this guideline**; they were addressed by the 2019 USPSTF guidelines, which did not recommend pancreatic cancer screening for asymptomatic persons with these risk factors.
- ASGE guidelines are reviewed for update approximately **every 5 years**, or sooner if new data may influence a recommendation.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- Diverges from 2019 USPSTF (recommends against screening) — but USPSTF explicitly excluded high-risk genetic populations, so not a true contradiction.
- BRCA1 lifetime risk (~3.5%-3.8%) does not clearly cross the 5% high-risk threshold adopted by the panel; the screening suggestion for BRCA1 carriers rests on weak/very-low-quality evidence and panel judgment.
- Quality of evidence is low to very low throughout; no clinical trials compared screened vs unscreened controls. Patients must be counseled on the substantial harms (low-yield surgery, perioperative adverse events) before enrollment.
- Future-directions gaps: role of other risk factors (age, male gender, obesity, smoking, alcohol) in genetic carriers; need for biomarkers (neither EUS nor MRI reliably diagnoses pancreatic cancer precursors or high-grade PanIN) and circulating tumor cells/DNA; management/interval after a normal baseline exam is undefined.
- **Internal labelling inconsistency.** The Methods state that frequency and starting age were addressed "using a non-GRADE format," yet Recommendations 4 and 5 are published with GRADE labels *(conditional recommendation, very low quality of evidence)*; only Recommendation 6 is left ungraded. Both are reproduced above as published.
- **Supersedes earlier guidance on who to screen.** The "What Is New" box states the change explicitly: previous guidelines limited screening to BRCA1/2 carriers with a family history of pancreatic cancer; this one screens all carriers. Where [[aga-2020-pancreas-cancer-screening|AGA 2020]] (older tier-1) still requires ≥1 first-degree relative with pancreas cancer for BRCA1/2 and PALB2, and directs that MRI and EUS be used **in combination**, this guideline is newer and wins; both positions are carried on [[pancreatic-cancer-screening|high-risk pancreatic cancer surveillance]].
- **No operative guidance.** The document gives no management algorithm for an abnormal screening result — no lesion size, growth rate, or duct threshold that triggers surgery.
