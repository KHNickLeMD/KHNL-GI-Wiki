## Summary

AGA Expert Review covering the whole arc of *Clostridioides difficile* infection (CDI) management in adults — prevention, diagnosis, severity classification, first-episode and recurrent treatment, fulminant disease, and microbiota restoration. Fourteen Best Practice Advice (BPA) statements developed from expert opinion and literature review; because no systematic review was performed, the statements carry **no evidence-quality or strength ratings**. Approximately 365,200 US cases annually; fulminant disease occurs in 1%–3% of cases with a 30%–40% mortality rate.

The update repositions several long-standing practices. **Fidaxomicin is favored first-line for all nonfulminant CDI** (not just high-recurrence-risk patients), vancomycin remains acceptable, and **metronidazole is restricted to fulminant disease only** (as IV adjunct). **Oral vancomycin prophylaxis during systemic antibiotics is no longer advised**, and **probiotics are not advised** for primary or secondary prevention. **Bezlotoxumab is reported as no longer commercially available.** Severity thresholds re-admit the **creatinine ratio (>1.5 × baseline)** alongside the absolute cutoff.

Diagnosis is framed as clinical-plus-laboratory: compatible symptoms (typically ≥3 unformed stools in 24 h after antibiotics) plus multistep testing (GDH or PCR → toxin A/B EIA), with the explicit instruction to **treat a PCR+/toxin− patient when clinical suspicion is high**. Test of cure is discouraged; retesting only for relapsing/worsening diarrhea. A **≥50% reduction in loose/liquid stools within 3 days (no later than 5)** defines symptomatic response and is the trigger to reconsider the diagnosis.

Microbiota restoration is the backbone of recurrence prevention: fecal microbiota–based therapy after a second recurrence (third episode), or earlier in patients at high risk (age >65, immunocompromised, skilled-nursing-facility resident) or after a morbid/refractory first episode. For fulminant CDI, **multi-dose conventional FMT via lower endoscopy** is advised (2–3 administrations typically needed); the FDA-approved products are neither studied nor approved in this setting. Long-term low-dose suppressive vancomycin (125 mg daily) is the fallback for patients who cannot receive or have failed microbiota therapy.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Best Practice Advice statements (verbatim, as published):**

1. Prevention of *C difficile* infection should include proper barrier protection and disinfection with United States Environmental Protection Agency–approved sporicidal agents in health care settings. Patients should be counseled on the importance of handwashing and to consider disinfecting areas of the home potentially contaminated with *C difficile* spores, to prevent reinfection. Reassurance should be provided that *C difficile* infection transmission to unaffected family members is uncommon.
2. *C difficile* infection is a clinical diagnosis that requires compatible symptoms (typically acute diarrhea, following antibiotics) in combination with supportive laboratory testing. Multistep testing is preferred for best diagnostic accuracy; however, patients with discordant testing who have a high clinical suspicion for *C difficile* infection should be treated.
3. Clinicians should classify hospitalized patients with *C difficile* infection as having nonsevere, severe, or fulminant disease at diagnosis. White blood cell count >15,000 cells/uL and/or creatinine >1.5 × baseline are indicative of severe *C difficile* infection and increased risk for recurrence and poor outcomes. Patients with additional findings of shock, ileus, or megacolon have fulminant *C difficile* infection, which can be fatal.
4. Fidaxomicin (200 mg twice daily oral for 10 days) is a narrower spectrum agent, and given lower recurrence rates, is favored as first-line therapy for nonfulminant *C difficile* infection. However, given practical considerations, vancomycin (125 mg 4 times daily oral for 10 days) is also acceptable therapy. Metronidazole should not be used outside of fulminant disease.
5. Cholestyramine and other bile acid–binding agents should not be used as monotherapy or concurrently with oral vancomycin or fidaxomicin. Psyllium fiber can be used to bulk stools in patients who have recovered from the infection.
6. Following treatment of *C difficile* infection, clinicians should not perform routine test of cure. Stool testing should only be performed for persistent worsening of diarrheal symptoms.
7. First *C difficile* infection recurrence (second episode) within 8 weeks of completing anti–*C difficile* infection therapy should be treated with either a tapering course of vancomycin or fidaxomicin. Treatment of *C difficile* infection should include a risk assessment for *C difficile* infection recurrence and consideration of microbiota restoration therapy to prevent recurrence.
8. Fecal microbiota-based therapies (fecal microbiota spores, live-brpk, fecal microbiota, live-jslm, or conventional fecal microbiota transplant) should be offered after treatment of a second recurrence (third *C difficile* infection episode). Microbiota restoration therapy may be considered after treatment of an initial infection or first recurrence in patients who are at high risk for further recurrence or in patients whose initial *C difficile* infection episode was particularly morbid or difficult to treat.
9. Prolonged, low dose, suppressive vancomycin regimen (125 mg daily) for secondary prophylaxis may be considered in patients with multiple recurrent episodes who are not candidates for fecal microbiota-based therapies due to ongoing comorbidities, limited life expectancy, or ongoing/frequent systemic antibiotics or who have failed multiple courses of fecal microbiota-based therapy.
10. Fulminant *C difficile* infection should be managed by a multidisciplinary team (gastroenterology/infectious disease, surgery, medicine/critical care) and treated with high-dose vancomycin 500 mg every 6 hours (oral or enteral), with metronidazole 500 mg intravenously every 8 hours, and if ileus is present, rectal vancomycin 500 mg every 6 hours should be added.
11. Multi-dose fecal microbiota transplantation (fresh-directed donors, or institutional stool banks) delivered via lower endoscopy, should be considered in individuals with fulminant *C difficile* infection. Fecal microbiota, live-jslm (Rebyota), and fecal microbiota spores, live-brpk (Vowst) have not been studied and are not approved for treatment of severe and/or fulminant *C difficile* infection. Due to high risk of *C difficile* infection recurrence, patients who recover from severe or fulminant *C difficile* infection should be maintained on a suppressive vancomycin regimen until a final fecal microbiota transplantation can be administered as an outpatient.
12. Use of vancomycin is not advised during systemic antibiotic administration to prevent *C difficile* infection. Probiotics are not advised to prevent an initial or recurrent *C difficile* infection. To promote restoration of healthy gut microbiota, patients should be instructed to consume a healthy diet including a variety of fruits and vegetables, rich in both soluble and insoluble fiber.
13. During the treatment of *C difficile* infection or the prevention of its recurrence, the use of proton pump inhibitors should be evaluated. If a legitimate indication exists for their use, proton pump inhibitor discontinuation is not necessary.
14. Individuals with a history of *C difficile* infection should be specifically counseled to avoid unnecessary antibiotic therapy.

**Supporting detail (from the narrative, decision-critical):**

- **Epidemiology / colonization:** ~365,200 US cases/year. Healthy adult community colonization 4%–15%; hospital colonization 3%–21%; up to **50% in long-term care residents** — the reason PCR alone over-diagnoses. Household absolute risk of acquisition **<5%**; spores survive **up to 6 months** on surfaces.
- **Severity:** WBC >15,000 cells/µL and creatinine **>1.5 mg/dL or 1.5 × premorbid baseline**. Fulminant = systemic toxicity/end-organ dysfunction — hypotension/shock, ileus, toxic megacolon; **1%–3%** of cases; mortality **30%–40%**. In the authors' experience pseudomembranes are **universally present** in fulminant disease; their absence should prompt search for an alternative cause. Outpatient CDI is more likely nonsevere — additional severity labs case-by-case.
- **Fidaxomicin vs vancomycin:** recurrence 6%–11% fewer events with fidaxomicin. Louie RCT clinical cure noninferior (mITT 88.2% vs 85.8%; per-protocol 92.1% vs 89.8%). Cochrane: symptomatic cure without recurrence at 1 month **71% vs 61%** (RR 1.17, 95% CI 1.07–1.27). Generic fidaxomicin is FDA-approved.
- **Practical dosing note:** oral vancomycin QID doses may be given during **waking hours** — patients need not wake overnight for exact q6h dosing.
- **Response definition:** ≥50% reduction in loose/liquid bowel movements per day **within 3 days (no later than 5)**; failure to reach it → reconsider diagnosis (inadequate response, other infection, IBS).
- **Retesting:** only for relapse of **≥3 watery stools/day sustained ≥2 days or worsening**, consistent with prior episodes. Lab confirmation strongly encouraged over empiric treatment (also often required for insurance approval of microbiota therapies).
- **Recurrence definition:** clinically significant diarrhea + positive stool testing within **8 weeks** of completing therapy; ~90% occur within 8 weeks, but experts also count events within **1 year** as recurrence/reinfection.
- **First recurrence — switch drug class:**
  - Initial fidaxomicin → **vancomycin taper/pulse**: 125 mg QID × 14 d → 125 mg BID × 14 d → 125 mg daily × 14 d → 125 mg every third day × 14 d; **or** fidaxomicin **EXTEND** pulsed protocol (200 mg BID days 1–5, then once daily on alternate days, days 7–25).
  - Initial vancomycin → **fidaxomicin**, standard 10-day (200 mg BID) or EXTEND pulsed.
  - Tapers **beyond 60 days** have not been studied and are unlikely to add benefit.
  - Ongoing recurrence risk factors (continuing antibiotics, cytotoxic chemotherapy) → continue **suppressive vancomycin 125 mg daily** after the 10-day course, then taper off or bridge to microbiota therapy.
- **Earlier microbiota therapy — the high-risk criteria:** age **>65 years**, immunocompromised, **resident of a skilled nursing facility**; or prior CDI that was severe, required hospitalization, or was prolonged/difficult to treat. Per product labeling, both FDA-approved products are approved regardless of number of recurrences.
- **Microbiota therapy sequencing:** complete a standard antimicrobial course → remain on suppressive oral vancomycin (**at least 125 mg daily**) until therapy is approved and accessible → stop antibacterial therapy **1–4 days before** microbiota restoration.
- **Immunocompromise:** conventional FMT recommended in **mildly/moderately** immunocompromised adults; **against any fecal microbiota–based therapy in severe immunocompromise** — cytotoxic chemotherapy, recent stem cell transplant, severe neutropenia (**ANC <500/µL**), advanced/untreated HIV (**CD4 <200/mm³**). Because severe immunocompromise is usually transient, **defer** microbiota therapy and continue suppressive vancomycin.
- **Suppressive vancomycin data:** without intervention, further recurrence risk after multiple recurrences **>40%**; recurrence **>30%** after stopping vancomycin prophylaxis. No emergence of vancomycin-resistant enterococci observed. Oral vancomycin produced **no detectable serum levels in 98%** of patients even with renal insufficiency → negligible ototoxicity risk.
- **Bezlotoxumab is no longer commercially available.**
- **Fulminant management:** high-dose vancomycin 500 mg q6h PO/enteral **for at least the first 48 hours**; IV metronidazole 500 mg q8h (secreted into colon); rectal vancomycin 500 mg q6h if ileus. After clinical response (**typically 3–5 days**) narrow to vancomycin 125 mg QID monotherapy. Minimum **10 days**, authors favor **14 days**. **No response or worsening after 48 hours → FMT or colectomy.** Avoid empiric non-CDI antibiotics; if coinfection requires them, continue low-dose suppressive vancomycin (125 mg daily or BID) alongside.
- **FMT in severe/fulminant CDI:** AGA guideline data — 5 observational studies, 647 patients; mortality **RR 0.37 (95% CI 0.23–0.59)**, **NNT 4** to prevent 1 death, no difference in serious adverse events. **Refractory severe CDI (authors' working definition):** hospitalized, ongoing diarrhea and persistent leukocytosis after **5–7 days** of appropriate therapy (oral vancomycin 125 mg q6h). Most patients need **2–3 administrations** of liquid FMT via lower endoscopy. Vancomycin may be held on the day of FMT, otherwise continued. **Not advised with bowel perforation or obstruction/high-grade colonic stricture**; caution in severe immunocompromise. Response tracked by pseudomembranes, diarrhea resolution, leukocytosis, and CRP. Stool-bank donor material outside an IND is prohibited by FDA; locally procured FMT falls under FDA enforcement discretion.
- **After recovery from severe/fulminant CDI:** vancomycin 125 mg QID to a total of **14 days**, then **125 mg once or twice daily** until outpatient FDA-approved microbiota therapy can be arranged.
- **Oral vancomycin prophylaxis (OVP):** the only RCT showed no statistically significant difference in recurrent CDI; OVP was associated with a **13.5% absolute reduction** but the trial was **not powered** to detect it. Authors do not use OVP for secondary prophylaxis; instead close observation, prompt testing (a standing order for *C difficile* testing expedites workup) and treatment.
- **Diet:** low-fiber diet after antibiotics prolongs susceptibility. Advise varied fruits/vegetables with soluble and insoluble fiber; avoid ultra-processed foods; do **not** prescribe "bland" or clear-liquid diets during CDI. Citrulline (watermelon) inhibits recurrence in mice — human trials underway.
- **Antimotility agents:** contraindicated in **untreated CDI, fulminant CDI, and severe colonic IBD**. Loperamide not suggested; if used, shortest duration, and **only after anti-CDI therapy has started** (theoretical risk of toxin retention and toxic megacolon).
- **PPIs:** pooled observational data — recurrent CDI **24% in PPI users vs 18% in nonusers**; largest RCT (>17,000 patients, 3 years) found no significant increase (**11 vs 4 cases; NNH 1800** with daily pantoprazole); recent meta-analyses found no significant difference vs placebo, H2RA, or P-CAB. **No evidence supports dose reduction** — either a legitimate indication exists (continue) or it does not (discuss cessation).
- **Antibiotic stewardship:** prophylactic antibiotics are **no longer routinely recommended** before dental procedures for most patients with prosthetic joints or certain heart conditions; patients should not fear appropriately indicated antibiotics.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Metronidazole for nonsevere CDI.** [[acg-2021-cdiff]] permits metronidazole 500 mg TID × 10 days for low-risk younger outpatients with minimal comorbidities. This 2026 CPU states metronidazole **should not be used outside of fulminant disease** (BPA 4). Newer same-tier source wins: [[clostridioides-difficile]] now asserts metronidazole is fulminant-only; the ACG low-risk carve-out is flagged on the page as superseded.
- **Oral vancomycin prophylaxis during systemic antibiotics.** [[acg-2021-cdiff]] Rec 18 conditionally recommends OVP 125 mg daily in defined high-risk patients receiving systemic antibiotics; [[aga-2026-cdiff-ibd]] BPA 12 says clinicians *may consider* OVP as secondary prevention in IBD. This CPU (BPA 12) advises **against** vancomycin during systemic antibiotics to prevent CDI, citing the single RCT's non-significant result. Newest same-tier source wins for the general adult page; the IBD-specific CPU is retained as narrower guidance on the IBD population. Surfaced on [[clostridioides-difficile]].
- **Probiotics for CDI prevention.** [[aga-2020-probiotics]] gives a **conditional recommendation FOR** named probiotic formulations to prevent CDI in adults and children on antibiotics (low quality, benefit concentrated at >15% baseline risk). This CPU states **probiotics are not advised to prevent an initial or recurrent CDI**. Newer same-tier AGA source wins; [[probiotics]] now asserts "not advised for CDI prevention" with the 2020 conditional-FOR flagged as superseded.
- **Severity — creatinine threshold.** IDSA 2018 (as carried by [[acg-2021-cdiff]]) uses an **absolute creatinine >1.5 mg/dL**, having deliberately dropped the 2010 ratio because a baseline is often unavailable. This CPU's BPA 3 uses **>1.5 × baseline**, with the narrative accepting **either** >1.5 mg/dL or 1.5 × premorbid baseline. The page now states both are acceptable and keeps the version history, since the absolute cutoff remains the fallback when no baseline exists and the ratio remains the better discriminator in chronic kidney disease.
- **Microbiota-therapy antibiotic washout.** [[aga-2024-fmt]] specifies **1–3 days** (1 day with a bowel purge, 3 without); this CPU says **1–4 days**. Both are ranges around the same practice; the page states the AGA 2024 purge-conditioned rule and notes the 2026 CPU's wider window.
- **Bezlotoxumab.** [[acg-2021-cdiff]] Rec 19 conditionally recommends bezlotoxumab in high-risk patients. This CPU reports it is **no longer commercially available**. The ACG criteria are retained on [[clostridioides-difficile]] for reference but marked as not currently actionable.
- **First-recurrence vancomycin taper — regimen differs.** [[acg-2021-cdiff]] example: 125 mg QID × 10–14 d → BID × 7 d → daily × 7 d → q2–3 d × 8 weeks. This CPU example: 125 mg QID × 14 d → BID × 14 d → daily × 14 d → every third day × 14 d. Both are explicitly examples; the CPU notes taper dosing/duration "may differ based on individual practice" and that tapers beyond 60 days are unstudied. Both regimens are shown on the page.
- **Open question:** whether OVP is genuinely ineffective or the trial was simply underpowered (13.5% absolute reduction, non-significant) — the CPU states additional research is needed.
