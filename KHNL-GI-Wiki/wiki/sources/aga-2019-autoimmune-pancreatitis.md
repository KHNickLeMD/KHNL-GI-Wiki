## Summary

A brief practical review of autoimmune pancreatitis (AIP) written for the AGA's "Here and Now" clinical-practice column. The authors adopt the ICDC framework, which classifies AIP as **type 1 (lymphoplasmacytic sclerosing pancreatitis)**, **type 2 (idiopathic duct centric pancreatitis, IDCP)**, or **AIP not otherwise specified** — and then, because natural history, diagnostic criteria, and management differ so much between the two, they **restrict the term "AIP" to type 1 and call type 2 "IDCP" throughout**. Every claim below follows that nomenclature.

The central clinical problem is that AIP is a chronic, steroid-responsive fibroinflammatory disease that **mimics pancreatic cancer** — it is found in 2%–3% of surgical resections done for presumed pancreatic cancer — and it is uncommon enough that the diagnosis is rarely top of mind. AIP typically presents as **painless obstructive jaundice** in a man in his seventh decade; IDCP presents as **acute pancreatitis** in half of cases, in a younger patient, and is strongly linked to inflammatory bowel disease (especially ulcerative colitis). Diagnosis rests on a combination of serology (serum IgG4), imaging (parenchymal and ductal), other organ involvement, histology, and response to steroids — the five cardinal features of the ICDC, which the authors state has the greatest sensitivity of the published criteria sets.

Management is **primarily medical and steroid-based** — response to corticosteroids approaches 100% in both types — with close follow-up for occult malignancy. ERCP and biliary stenting are **rarely indicated**: the international consensus advises treating mild jaundice without signs of infection with steroids alone. Surgery is reserved for suspected malignancy. After treatment, serum IgG4 has **no value** for predicting relapse or diagnosing recurrence, and histologic re-evaluation is not recommended. Relapse is the dominant long-term problem in type 1 (up to 60%, vs <10% in IDCP), occurring during the steroid taper or after discontinuation; elevated pre-treatment IgE and other-organ involvement with proximal bile duct stricture or IgG4-sclerosing cholangitis mark the high-relapse-risk group who may warrant long-term maintenance with steroids or an immunomodulator. Steroid intolerance or relapse is treated with steroids plus an immunomodulator, or with rituximab.

**Two important limits of this source, stated plainly:** (1) it *names* the ICDC and says what its cardinal features are, but it **does not print the ICDC's level 1 / level 2 component criteria**, so the criteria set cannot be applied from this paper alone; (2) the entire treatment algorithm — **drug, dose, duration, taper, response-assessment interval, maintenance regimen, rituximab schedule** — lives in **Figure 1**, which is a raster image with no text layer. None of those numbers appear anywhere in the article's prose. See *Contradictions / Open Questions*.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Definitions and nomenclature**
- AIP is a **chronic, steroid-responsive fibroinflammatory disease of the pancreas**.
- **ICDC classification:** type 1 AIP (= lymphoplasmacytic sclerosing pancreatitis), type 2 AIP (= idiopathic duct centric pancreatitis, IDCP), or **AIP not otherwise specified**.
- Because of the significant differences between type 1 and type 2 in natural history, diagnostic criteria, and management, the authors **restrict "AIP" to lymphoplasmacytic sclerosing pancreatitis** and **term type 2 "IDCP."**

**Epidemiology**
- Incidence and prevalence "remain largely unknown."
- One study from Japan: **incidence 1 per 100,000**; **prevalence estimated at 5%–6% of all patients with chronic pancreatitis**.
- AIP **often mimics pancreatic cancer** and is identified in **2%–3% of surgical resections for presumed pancreatic cancer**.

**Natural history / sequelae**
- Long-term sequelae of persistent pancreatic inflammation: **exocrine insufficiency and diabetes**, similar to other causes of chronic pancreatitis.
- **No clear evidence of an increased risk of pancreatic cancer** in AIP patients; data suggest the risk is **similar to other causes of chronic pancreatitis**.

**Presentation**
- **AIP most commonly presents with obstructive jaundice.**
- **50% of IDCP patients present with acute pancreatitis.**
- Obstructive jaundice in AIP is due to either **distal biliary ductal compression from pancreatic head swelling** or a **proximal biliary stricture**.
- **Despite the intense inflammation, patients typically have no or mild abdominal pain.**

**IgG4-related disease and other organ involvement (OOI)**
- AIP belongs to the spectrum of **IgG4-related disorders**. OOI includes: **bile duct stricture, interstitial nephritis, orbital pseudotumor, IgG4-related plasmacytic exocrinopathy of the salivary gland, pulmonary interstitial fibrosis or nodules, and mediastinal or retroperitoneal fibrosis.**
- AIP can also present with **isolated pancreatic involvement in 50%** of patients.

**IBD association (IDCP)**
- **Inflammatory bowel disease, especially ulcerative colitis (UC), is strongly associated with IDCP**; a majority of patients are diagnosed with IBD **before or concurrently with** IDCP.
- Cumulative probability of an IDCP diagnosis after a diagnosis of UC: **0.2% at 1 year, 0.6% at 5 years, 0.8% at 10 years.**

**Serology**
- IgG4 is a **distinctive serologic marker for AIP**; a level **≥2 times the upper limit of normal (280 mg/dL)** is considered **level 1 evidence per ICDC** for the diagnosis of AIP, with **sensitivity 53% and specificity 99%.**
- **Pancreatic cancer patients can have elevated IgG4**, but **only 1% have levels above 2 times the upper limit (280 mg/dL).**
- **An elevated IgG4 is not characteristic of IDCP**, and **no reliable serologic marker is currently available to aid in the diagnosis of IDCP.**

**Imaging**
- Characteristic radiologic features on **CT or MRI**: **diffuse parenchymal enlargement ("sausage shape")**, **low attenuating capsule ("rim enhancement")**, and, **less commonly, a focal mass-like lesion or diffuse pancreatic atrophy.**
- **MRCP or ERCP** may reveal **diffuse or multifocal narrowing of the pancreatic duct without upstream dilatation.**
- **Focal mass-like pancreatic lesions are more commonly identified in IDCP** — noted in **approximately 35%** of patients.

**Tissue acquisition and histology**
- **EUS-guided fine-needle aspiration cytology has poor sensitivity, even with larger-diameter needles.**
- **EUS-guided fine-needle biopsy, which preserves the gland architecture, is needed for tissue diagnosis**; despite concern for post-procedure complications, **smaller cohort studies demonstrated safety and efficacy of fine-needle biopsy** in the diagnosis of AIP.
- **The ICDC guidelines recommend use of core biopsy in the setting of focal mass and/or obstructive jaundice, when cancer is excluded and the diagnosis is unclear.**

**Diagnostic criteria**
- "Numerous guidelines have been proposed" to diagnose AIP and IDCP and to differentiate them from pancreatic cancer, on the basis of a combination of the characteristics in Table 1.
- **ICDC has the greatest sensitivity among these guidelines** and uses **cardinal features of: pancreatic imaging including parenchyma or ducts; serology; other organ involvement; histology of the pancreas; and response to steroid therapy.**
- ⚠ **The ICDC level 1 / level 2 definitions for each cardinal feature are not printed in this article.**

**Management (prose statements)**
- Management of AIP and IDCP is **primarily medical**, as summarized in Figure 1, **but warrants close follow-up and monitoring for the possibility of underlying malignancy**, because AIP and IDCP are relatively uncommon and have **common imitators such as pancreatic cancer**.
- **Endoscopic interventions such as ERCP and biliary stenting are rarely indicated in the setting of obstructive jaundice.** Although biliary stenting reduces the risk of cholangitis and facilitates obtaining cytologic brushings to differentiate benign from malignant strictures, **ICDC recommend treating mild jaundice with no signs of infection with steroid therapy alone.**
- **Surgical interventions are indicated in the setting of suspected malignancy.**

**Who is treated**
- **Symptomatic** patients with **obstructive jaundice, acute pancreatitis, abdominal pain, and back pain** are considered for treatment.
- **Asymptomatic** patients are considered for treatment **only** in the setting of a **persistent pancreatic mass on imaging** or **persistent liver function test abnormalities in the setting of IgG4-sclerosing cholangitis / OOI.**

**Monitoring after treatment**
- **After treatment there is no value to following serum IgG4 levels to predict relapse or diagnose recurrence.**
- Despite the efficacy of corticosteroid therapy, **normal pancreatic morphology and function may not be restored**, because resolution of the inflammation makes the **fibro-atrophic component of disease more prominent**.
- **Histologic re-evaluation is therefore not recommended after treatment.**

**Relapse**
- Recurrence or relapse after initial response is noted in **up to 60% of AIP patients**, **during steroid tapering or after discontinuation of corticosteroids**.
- **Higher risk of relapse:** patients with **elevated pre-treatment immunoglobulin E levels** and **OOI with proximal bile duct stricture or IgG4-sclerosing cholangitis**. These patients **can be considered for long-term maintenance with steroids or immunomodulator regimens** (as described in Figure 1).
- Take-home message: **intolerance to steroids or relapse is treated with a combination of steroids and immunomodulators, or rituximab.**

### Table 1 — Characteristic Differences Between Autoimmune Pancreatitis and Idiopathic Duct Centric Pancreatitis

| Feature | Autoimmune pancreatitis (type 1 / lymphoplasmacytic sclerosing) | Idiopathic duct centric pancreatitis (type 2 / IDCP) |
|---|---|---|
| Age at presentation | 7th decade | 5th decade |
| Male gender | 75% | 50% |
| Other organ involvement / association with IgG4-related disease | Present in ~50% | None |
| Association with IBD | Low | High (10%–20%) |
| Serum IgG4 elevation | Present in ~66% | Present in ~25% |
| *Histologic findings —* lymphoplasmacytic infiltration | Present | Present |
| *Histologic findings —* storiform fibrosis | Present | Present |
| *Histologic findings —* obliterative phlebitis | **Characteristic** | Rarely noted |
| *Histologic findings —* granulocyte epithelial lesion | **Absent** | **Characteristic** |
| *Histologic findings —* periductal inflammation | Present | Present |
| *Histologic findings —* IgG4 staining | ≥10 cells/high-power field | <10 cells/high-power field |
| Response to steroids | ~100% | ~100% |
| Relapse risk | Up to 60% | <10% |

*Table 1, reproduced as text. The source's own footnote: "Adapted with permission from Hart PA et al., Recent Advances in Autoimmune Pancreatitis. Gastroenterology 2015."*

### Text-extraction notes (read before quoting numbers)

`pdftotext` in this environment silently drops the glyphs `≥`, `≤`, and `×`, and renders the tilde `~` as the letter `w`. Three consequences in this source, each resolved from the PDF's own glyph geometry (word bounding boxes) plus the article's parallel wording — **no comparator was supplied from memory**:

1. **Serum IgG4 cutoff.** Extracted as "a level 2 times the upper limit of normal (280 mg/dL)". The word box for that "2" is **13.2 units wide and taller than the surrounding line** (251.4–264.4 vs 254.0–263.1), whereas the genuine standalone "2" three sentences later is **5.5 units wide at normal height** — i.e. a math glyph was dropped immediately before the digit. Direction is fixed by the article's own next sentence: *"only 1% have levels **above** 2 times the upper limit (280 mg/dL)."* Recorded as **≥2× ULN**. `>` and `<` survive extraction intact elsewhere in this file, so the dropped glyph is `≥` (or `≤`, excluded by sense).
2. **IgG4 staining, Table 1.** Extracted as "10 cells/high-power field" for AIP against "<10 cells/high-power field" for IDCP. The two cells have **identical word-box widths (14.92 units)**, so the AIP cell carried a one-character comparator that was dropped. Recorded as **≥10 cells/high-power field**.
3. **Approximation sign.** "w50%", "w66%", "w25%", "w100%" in Table 1 are the font's mapping of `~`; recorded as ~50%, ~66%, ~25%, ~100%.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

**Internal to this source**
- Table 1 lists **serum IgG4 elevation present in ~25% of IDCP**, while the prose states **"an elevated IgG4 is not characteristic of IDCP."** Both are reproduced as written; read together they mean a minority of IDCP patients have a raised IgG4 but it carries no diagnostic weight for type 2.
- The article says the **ICDC has the greatest sensitivity** among proposed criteria sets, but reports **no sensitivity figure** for ICDC itself, nor the figures for the criteria sets it is being compared with.

**Decision gaps — content this source names but does not supply**
1. **Figure 1 (the management algorithm) is not recoverable.** The figure is a raster image with no text layer; `pdftotext`, in both `-layout` and `-bbox` modes, returns the caption only. PyMuPDF is not installed in this environment and image extraction is permission-blocked, so the figure could not be cropped to `raw/assets/` either. Everything the figure carries is therefore missing from the wiki: **corticosteroid agent, induction dose, induction duration, taper schedule, the interval at which response is reassessed, the definition of non-response, the specific immunomodulator regimens and their doses, the rituximab regimen, the maintenance duration, and the relapse branch of the algorithm.** The prose gives only the *categories* ("steroids and immunomodulators, or rituximab") and never a milligram or a week. **Do not fill these from memory.** Recovering them needs either (a) figure capture from this PDF once PyMuPDF/image permissions are available, or (b) ingesting **Okazaki K, Chari ST, Frulloni L, et al. International consensus for the treatment of autoimmune pancreatitis. Pancreatology 2017;17:1–6** (this article's ref 3), which is the underlying treatment consensus.
2. **The ICDC component criteria are not printed.** The article names the five cardinal features (imaging of parenchyma/ducts, serology, OOI, histology, steroid response) and says a serum IgG4 ≥2× ULN is "level 1," but it never gives the **level 1 vs level 2 definitions for the other four features**, nor the **combination rules** by which the ICDC assigns *definite* / *probable* type 1, *definite* / *probable* type 2, or *AIP not otherwise specified*. The criteria set therefore **cannot be applied from this page alone**. Needs **Shimosegawa T, Chari ST, Frulloni L, et al. International consensus diagnostic criteria for autoimmune pancreatitis. Pancreas 2011;40:352–358** (this article's ref 10).
3. **The steroid trial as a diagnostic test is unspecified.** "Response to steroid therapy" is one of the five ICDC cardinal features, but this source gives no dose, no trial duration, and no definition of what counts as a response — the very parameters that make a steroid trial safe to run in a patient who might have pancreatic cancer.
4. **Histologic criteria are qualitative only.** Table 1 grades features as present/absent/characteristic; apart from the IgG4 cells/HPF cutoff, no counts, thresholds, or definitions (e.g. what constitutes a granulocyte epithelial lesion) are given.
5. **No monitoring schedule.** The article says follow-up must be close and must watch for occult malignancy, and that serum IgG4 is useless for this, but specifies **no imaging modality, no interval, and no duration** of surveillance.
6. **No guidance on exocrine insufficiency or diabetes**, which it names as the long-term sequelae — no screening test, interval, or treatment.

**Against existing wiki content**
- No contradiction found. `[[chronic-pancreatitis]]` and `[[acute-pancreatitis]]` already describe AIP type 1 vs type 2 in one-line form (IgG4-related vs IBD-associated with granulocytic epithelial lesions); this source is consistent with them and far more specific.
- `[[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis]]` carries IgG4 cutoffs in **g/L** from `[[aasld-2022-psc]]` (>5.6 g/L suggests IgG4-SC) for a different question — excluding IgG4-SC in a PSC workup. This source's cutoff is in **mg/dL** and is for diagnosing pancreatic AIP. Different units, different questions, no conflict; do not merge the two thresholds.
