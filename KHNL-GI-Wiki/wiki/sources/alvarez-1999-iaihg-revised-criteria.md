## Summary

The IAIHG's original "Brighton" criteria (1993) carried **two parallel instruments**: a **descriptive set** for routine clinical classification into definite or probable autoimmune hepatitis (AIH), and a **numeric scoring system** intended for selecting homogeneous research cohorts. By 1998 both had been applied widely enough to be audited. This report is that audit and the resulting revision.

**The problem found was specificity, not sensitivity.** Across six published studies (983 patients), the scoring system was **97%–100% sensitive** for AIH — but when applied to patients it should have excluded, it failed in a characteristic direction. In **242 patients with chronic hepatitis C** presenting with autoimmune features, specificity ranged **66.1%–92.0%**; in **143 patients with biliary disorders** (primary biliary cirrhosis [PBC], primary sclerosing cholangitis [PSC], autoimmune cholangitis) it fell to **44.8%–64.9%**. Almost none scored "definite" — the failure was **upgrading into the "probable" band**, usually by only one or two points, driven by positive autoantibody scores, mildly raised immunoglobulins, low ratios of alkaline phosphatase (ALP) to aminotransferase, and **inadequate negative weighting against histological evidence of biliary disease**.

**The revision therefore re-weighted the score against its competing diagnoses** rather than adding sensitivity: the drug-history penalty went from −1 to **−4**, antimitochondrial antibody (AMA) seropositivity from −2 to **−4**, biliary histology from −1 to **−3**, and absent interface hepatitis to **−5**. The ALP:AST ratio was re-cut so that ratios of 1.5–3.0 score **0** instead of +2. Response categories were simplified to **complete response (+2) and relapse (+3)**, with "no response", "treatment failure" and "partial response" deleted. Re-analysis of 114 PSC patients raised specificity for excluding AIH from **64.9% to 89.5%**.

The panel is explicit about the instrument's standing: the scoring system is **"intended mainly for research purposes"**; in routine practice **the descriptive criteria should usually suffice**; the point values are **"to some extent arbitrary… qualitative… and do not reflect overall severity"**, so scores **should not be subjected to mathematical manipulations for statistical analysis**.

### Table 6 — the explanatory notes that change how it is scored

- **ALP:AST ratio is a ratio of *elevations above the upper normal limit (ULN)***, i.e. (IU/L ALP ÷ ULN ALP) ÷ (IU/L AST ÷ ULN AST) — **not** a ratio of raw values. Scoring raw values is the error.
- **Titres assume indirect immunofluorescence on rodent tissue, or HEp-2 cells for ANA.** Lower titres — **especially of anti-LKM-1 — are significant in children and should be scored at least +1**.
- **Viral markers:** score IgM anti-HAV, HBsAg, IgM anti-HBc, anti-HCV and HCV-RNA. If a viral aetiology is suspected despite seronegativity, test for other hepatotropic viruses (cytomegalovirus, Epstein-Barr virus).
- **"Biliary changes"** means granulomatous cholangitis, or severe concentric periductal fibrosis with ductopenia on an **adequate** biopsy, and/or a substantial periportal ductular reaction with copper accumulation.
- **The optional parameters** (other defined autoantibodies, HLA) should be used **only in patients seronegative for ANA, SMA and anti-LKM-1**. "Other defined" autoantibodies are those with published detection methodology and relevance to AIH: pANCA, anti-LC1, anti-SLA, anti-ASGPR, anti-LP, anti-sulfatide. **HLA DR3/DR4 applies mainly to North European caucasoid and Japanese populations.**
- **Response points are added to those accrued for features at initial presentation**, and may be assessed at any time.

### Table 4 — the revised *descriptive* criteria (the instrument for routine practice)

| Feature | Definite | Probable |
|---|---|---|
| **Liver histology** | Interface hepatitis of **moderate or severe** activity, ± lobular hepatitis or central-portal bridging necrosis, **without** biliary lesions, well-defined granulomas, or prominent changes suggesting another aetiology | Same as for definite |
| **Serum biochemistry** | Any aminotransferase abnormality, especially if ALP is **not markedly** elevated. **Normal α1-antitrypsin, copper and ceruloplasmin** | As for definite, but abnormal copper/ceruloplasmin **may be included provided Wilson disease has been excluded** by appropriate investigation |
| **Serum immunoglobulins** | Total globulin *or* γ-globulin *or* IgG **>1.5 × ULN** | **Any** elevation of globulin / γ-globulin / IgG above ULN |
| **Serum autoantibodies** | ANA, SMA or anti-LKM-1 at **>1:80**; **AMA seronegative** | As for definite but at titres **≥1:40**; patients seronegative for these but **seropositive for other specified antibodies may be included** |
| **Viral markers** | Seronegative for current hepatitis A, B and C infection | Same as for definite |
| **Other aetiological factors** | Alcohol **<25 g/day**; no history of recent hepatotoxic drug use | Alcohol **<50 g/day** and no recent hepatotoxic drugs; **larger amounts may be included if there is clear evidence of continuing liver damage after abstinence or drug withdrawal** |

### Table 7 — definitions of response (needed to score the response lines)

- **Complete response** — *either*: marked symptom improvement **and** return of AST/ALT, bilirubin and immunoglobulins **completely to normal within 1 year**, sustained **at least a further 6 months** on maintenance therapy, or a liver biopsy during this period showing **at most minimal activity**; *or*: marked symptom improvement with **≥50% improvement of all liver tests in the first month**, with AST/ALT continuing to fall to **<2 × ULN within 6 months**, or a biopsy within 1 year showing only minimal activity.
- **Relapse** — *either*: AST/ALT rising to **>2 × ULN**, or a biopsy showing active disease, with or without return of symptoms, after a complete response; *or*: return of symptoms severe enough to require increased or reintroduced immunosuppression, accompanied by any AST/ALT rise, after a complete response.

### What the revision changed, and why

- **Drug history** −1 → **−4**, to weight against drug-induced AIH-like syndromes (minocycline named as the typical example).
- **AMA seropositivity** −2 → **−4**, and **biliary histology** −1 → **−3**, to weight against biliary disease.
- **No histological evidence of interface hepatitis** → **−5**, since interface hepatitis is so characteristic of definite AIH.
- **ALP:AST ratio** re-cut: the original gave +2 for <3.0 and −2 for >3.0. Because a high proportion of definite-AIH patients have ratios <3.0 but PSC patients do too, **1.5–3.0 now scores 0** — removing the deduction without penalising most AIH patients.
- **Response categories simplified** to complete response and relapse; "no response", "treatment failure" and "partial response" **deleted**, because a poor response does not exclude AIH.
- **Obligatory exclusion of hepatotropic viruses other than A, B and C is no longer required**, nor is a history of parenteral exposure; alcohol specifications simplified; the separate specification for antibody titres in children was dropped from the descriptive criteria (while the scoring notes retain that low titres matter in children).
- **Validation of the change:** re-analysis of **114 PSC patients** (Boberg data) — of 38 (33.3%) originally classified probable AIH, only **10 (8.8%)** remained so. Overall specificity for excluding AIH in PSC rose from **64.9% to 89.5%**. Two patients originally called definite AIH remained definite.

### Standing of the instrument — the panel's own words

- The scoring system is **"intended mainly for research purposes"**; in routine clinical practice **"the descriptive criteria should usually suffice"**.
- It is **designed to be applied to features at initial presentation**. Use for **suspected recurrence after liver transplantation**, or for **reassessing long-standing disease, has not been validated**.
- **"The values assigned are to some extent arbitrary. They are qualitative in nature and do not reflect overall severity of disease. Scores should not therefore be subjected to mathematical manipulations for statistical analysis."**
- Whether the system can define true **overlap syndromes** "is yet to be determined".
- **Definite AIH should not be diagnosed without liver histology**, and consultation with a hepatopathologist is strongly recommended.
- **There are no pathognomonic morphological features of AIH.**

### Other reaffirmed clinical points

- **70–80%** of AIH patients have significant titres (≥1:40) of ANA or SMA or both; **3–4%** (mainly young females) have anti-LKM-1; **up to 20% have none of these antibodies**.
- **pANCA** at high titre occurs in up to **90%** of AIH sera and is **rare in chronic viral hepatitis**, making it useful for distinguishing AIH from viral hepatitis with autoantibodies; it is also **relatively rare in PBC** but frequent in AIH.
- Significant ANA/SMA titres occur in **20–40%** of chronic hepatitis B or C, and anti-LKM-1 in **up to 6%** of chronic hepatitis C.
- **Type 1 (ANA/SMA positive) and Type 2 (anti-LKM-1 positive)** is the only subdivision in common usage, and it is **not exclusive**; long-term outcome is similar in the two groups.
- **Cholangiography is recommended** in all patients with an initial diagnosis of definite or probable AIH **who do not respond to corticosteroids**.
- **"AMA-positive AIH" is called a terminological contradiction** — most such patients are likely to have PBC.
- **"Cholestatic AIH"** — approximately 10% of AIH patients with markedly elevated ALP and γ-glutamyl transferase without histological evidence of biliary disease; a recent long-term follow-up suggests they are **not a clinically distinct subgroup**.
- Interferon therapy is generally safe in most viral hepatitis with autoantibodies, but **screening for autoantibodies (particularly anti-LKM-1) before starting it is recommended**, with careful monitoring.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Superseded for routine practice, not withdrawn.** [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]] (tier 1, newer) directs clinicians to the **simplified** score for typical patients and keeps this revised score for **complex or unusual features**. The wiki follows the guidance: the simplified score leads on [[autoimmune-hepatitis]], this one is the documented fallback.
- **The two scores disagree about what to measure.** This system scores **female sex, HLA type, other autoimmune disease, alcohol intake, drug history and response to therapy**; the simplified score drops all of them, keeping only autoantibodies, IgG, histology and viral exclusion. Neither paper reconciles the two — [[hennes-2008-simplified-aih-criteria]] states that direct comparison was impossible in its own cohort, because AIH there was *defined* by these descriptive criteria.
- **No "low score" threshold exists in either paper.** [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]] instructs reassessment with the revised system "whenever the simplified system yields a low score" without a number, and neither derivation paper supplies one. The bands here are pre-treatment >15 / 10–15 and post-treatment >17 / 12–17; below 10 is simply not AIH by this instrument.
- **The panel disclaims the use the wiki's readers are most likely to make of it.** The score is for research, is validated only at **initial presentation**, and is explicitly **not** validated for post-transplant recurrence or for reassessing long-standing disease — which is precisely the "complex case" setting AASLD routes to it. Stated on the page.
- **Two instruments in one paper, and they are not interchangeable.** The descriptive criteria (Table 4) and the scoring system (Table 5) give independent classifications; the panel's position is that the descriptive criteria suffice clinically.
- **Date:** published 1999, >10 years old, and ingested only because a current tier-1 guidance still routes decisions to it and prints neither its criteria nor its bands.
