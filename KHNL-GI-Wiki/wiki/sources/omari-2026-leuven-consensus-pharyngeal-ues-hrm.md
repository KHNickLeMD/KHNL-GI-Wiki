## Summary
- First classification scheme for **pharyngeal and upper esophageal sphincter (UES) motor disorders** on **pharyngeal high-resolution manometry-impedance (P-HRM-I)**, built on the 2020 Core Outcome Set (COS) of metrics (Abstract; Section 1, p.2).
- Three products (Section 1, p.2): a standard **swallow challenge protocol** (Figure 4), **criteria for UES dysfunction** (Figure 5), **criteria for pharyngeal contractile dysfunction** (Figure 8). Disorders fall into UES dysfunction, pharyngeal contractile dysfunction, or both (Section 9, p.13).
- P-HRM-I is a **complement** to videofluoroscopic swallow study (VFSS) or flexible endoscopic evaluation of swallowing (FEES), not a replacement; interpretation needs a multidisciplinary team (otolaryngology, gastroenterology, rehabilitation medicine, speech pathology/deglutology) (Section 3, p.3).
- **No numeric normal values are set.** "High", "Low", "Short", "ABSENT/LOW/HIGH" are judged against system-specific normal ranges; the paper acknowledges a paucity of universally available system-specific normal values (Section 9, p.14). Worked examples in Figures 6, 7 and 9 use one centre's (Flinders Medical Centre) normal ranges.
- Explicitly **no guidance on intervention decisions**; the panel did not consider whether treatment based on these criteria is effective (Section 1, caveat 3, p.2).

## Key Findings / Claims

### Methods (Section 2, p.2)
- Delphi process; **26** experts (all authors), multidisciplinary, international.
- **15-member subgroup** drafted proposals online from December 2023; finalized August 17, 2024; in-person meeting in Leuven, Belgium, September 21, 2024.
- Voting scale 1–9 (1–3 disagreement, 4 slight disagreement, 5 neutral, 6 slight agreement, 7–9 agreement); unscored statements counted as neutral (5).
- **Consensus = ≥70% scoring 7–9 and ≤20% scoring 1–3**; anything else = no consensus.
- Two voting rounds (first before the meeting; statements revised at the meeting; second round reached consensus on **all** statements).
- **Agreement 74%–100%; disagreement 0%–4%** across statements. Per-statement wording and scores are published only in the online supporting information; the article reports the range, not a score per statement.
- The article numbers no statements and gives no evidence grades. **Numbering below (L1…) is the wiki's**, with the article's section, figure or table as locator.

### Caveats and scope (Section 1, p.2)
- **L1** — Complementary instrumental investigations that visualize swallow function are an **essential adjunct** (they may show altered bolus transit or airway protection that manometry misses); if not done before, perform them **simultaneously or schedule them before** P-HRM-I.
- **L2** — A patient with no dysfunction by these criteria **cannot be considered to have "normal" swallowing**; also consider esophageal-phase abnormalities.
- **L3** — **No guidance for clinical intervention decisions.** P-HRM-I can measure change after interventions, but the panel did not consider whether interventions based on these criteria are effective.
- **L4** — **No recommendations** on: puree and solid challenges; natural swallowing challenges (sip, drinking); sensory stimulation; volitional swallowing tasks; biofeedback training (future research).

### Indications (Section 3, p.3; Figure 1; Table 1)
- **L5** — Before any P-HRM-I: clinical situation understood, **oropharyngeal anatomy examined under laryngoscopic or X-ray guidance**, and **aspiration risk ascertained**.
- **L6** — Most often used as a complementary test **after VFSS or FEES**; simultaneous fluoroscopy (videomanometry/manofluorography) can streamline assessment.
- **L7** — Where possible, use pharyngeal manometry **with impedance** (P-HRM-I) so all COS metrics can be derived.
- **L8** — Figure 1 caption: if VFSS/FEES has not been done, it is **strongly recommended** that pharyngeal manometry be done **simultaneously with VFSS (Video P-HRM-I)**; if VFSS/FEES has been done, P-HRM-I can proceed **stand-alone**.

**Figure 1 decision tree (p.3), as a table:**

| Step | Question | Yes | No |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entry | Oropharyngeal dysphagia complaint | → Step 1 | — |
| 1 | Has FEES/VFSS been recently done? | → Step 3 | → Step 2 |
| 2 | Known/clear history of pharyngeal or UES dysfunction **and** low risk of aspiration? | **Video P-HRM-I** → Step 3 | **FEES or VFSS** → Step 3 |
| 3 | Is treatment plan clear? | **Proceed with planned treatment/rehabilitation** | → Step 4 |
| 4 | Impaired UES opening, gross aspiration, pharyngeal residue, or delay in bolus transit? | **Video P-HRM-I or Standalone P-HRM-I** → back to Step 3 | **Other tests** (upper airway endoscopy, sensory testing, esophageal manometry, etc.) → back to Step 3 |

**Table 1 — Suggested indications and contraindications (p.4):**

| | Do P-HRM-I | Do NOT do P-HRM-I |
|---|---|---|
| **Strongly recommended** | Suspected UES dysfunction from history or other instrumental tests (VFSS/FEES) · Quantifying propulsive and restrictive forces to guide treatment decisions or set baseline swallowing function · Measuring change after interventions (e.g., myotomy, botulinum toxin injection, dilatation) or as objective biofeedback during rehabilitation | Known significant nasal obstruction · History of nasal-cavity trauma, sinonasal surgery, recent clinically significant epistaxis, skull base or mid-facial fractures · High risk of bleeding |
| **Suggested** | VFSS difficult or impossible (e.g., cannot be transported, contrast allergy) · To rule out any swallowing dysfunction in possible functional dysphagia · To quantify progression of a degenerative or neuromuscular disorder · To monitor objective change over time · To identify subtle changes when previous instrumental assessments are normal or do not explain symptoms | Cannot comprehend or follow instructions, or agitation · Cannot tolerate the catheter (discomfort, gagging) · Severe restriction (e.g., stricture) of the pharyngoesophageal segment* · High aspiration risk not mitigable by bolus size or compensatory techniques* |

*\* Table 1 note: stand-alone manometry not recommended; may be deemed safe with concurrent direct vision/guidance (X-ray or flexible endoscopy).*

### Metrics (Section 4, pp.3–7; Table 2; Figure 2)

**Table 2 — Swallow function metrics (p.5):**

| Domain | Metric (abbreviation, units) | Definition | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| UES relaxation and opening | Hypopharyngeal intrabolus pressure (IBP, mmHg) | Pressure **1 cm superior of the UES apogee** at the time of maximum hypopharyngeal distension (from impedance) | Abnormally **high** = elevated hypopharyngeal pressure in the bolus proximal to the UES |
| | UES integrated relaxation pressure (UES IRP, mmHg) | Extent of UES relaxation: **median of the lowest nonconsecutive 0.20–0.25 s of e-sleeve pressure** | Abnormally **high** = elevated residual pressure during UES relaxation as the bolus transits |
| | UES maximum admittance (UES MaxAd, mS) | Extent of UES opening: **highest admittance** during trans-sphincteric bolus flow | Abnormally **low** = diminished distension area of the bolus through the UES |
| | UES relaxation time (UES RT, s) | Duration of UES relaxation: e-sleeve pressure interval **below 50% of baseline or 35 mmHg, whichever is lower** | Abnormally **short** = diminished interval of UES relaxation |
| Pharyngeal contractile strength | Velopharyngeal contractile integral (VCI, mmHg·s·cm) | Mean pressure in a space–time box over the velopharynx × duration (s) × length (cm) | **Low** = velopharyngeal hypocontractility (ABSENT or LOW on topography); **high** = hypercontractility |
| | Mesopharyngeal contractile integral (MCI, mmHg·s·cm) | Same, mesopharyngeal box | **Low** = mesopharyngeal hypocontractility (ABSENT or LOW); **high** = hypercontractility |
| | Hypopharyngeal contractile integral (HPCI, mmHg·s·cm) | Same, hypopharyngeal box | **Low** = hypopharyngeal hypocontractility (ABSENT or LOW); **high** = hypercontractility |
| | Hypopharyngeal peak pressure (HPeakP, mmHg) | Mean of pressure peaks along the length of the hypopharyngeal box | (no interpretation given in Table 2) |

- UES pressure is measured by the **e-sleeve** method (maximum axial pressure within the UES region over time); IRP and RT come from pressure alone; IBP and MaxAd need impedance (Figure 2 legend, p.6).
- UES opening is driven by intraluminal bolus forces, hyolaryngeal traction (mylohyoid, anterior and posterior digastric) and tissue compliance, given a competent palate and larynx (Section 4.1, p.4).
- **Hypopharyngeal contraction:** both HPCI and HPeakP have been used; head-to-head comparisons suggest **HPeakP may have superior statistical confidence** for separating patients from controls and predicting aspiration and postswallow residue; HPeakP validated for pharyngeal propulsion in **postradiotherapy cricopharyngeal stricture** (Section 4.3, pp.7–8).
- **Hypocontractility** usually indicates impaired neuromuscular function; associated with incomplete clearance, residue and postswallow aspiration. **Hypercontractility** may be a compensatory upstream response to outflow disorders (reported in Parkinson's disease and obstructive sleep apnea) (Section 4.3, p.6).

**Bolus pressurization patterns (Section 4.2, pp.4–6; Figure 3, p.7):**
- Proposed marker of a pharyngeal outflow disorder: bolus pressurization peak **exceeding 20 mmHg above the predeglutitive hypopharyngeal baseline** (impedance confirms distension, separating it from a lumen-occluding contraction).
- Lower margin of the pressurization zone approximates a high-pressure gradient that may localize a restricted UES opening.

| Pattern | Duration vs UES relaxation | Hypopharyngeal stripping contraction after bolus transit | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Type 1** — pan-pressurization | Sustained | None visible; simultaneous pan-pressurization | Sustained restriction at the UES; never or rarely seen in healthy swallowing |
| **Type 2** — distal-compartmentalized pressurization | Sustained | Present, with compartmentalized pressurization of the distal pharynx | Same as Type 1 |
| **Type 3** — transient pressurization | Transient, nonsustained, early–mid relaxation | — | Basis needs exploration: may reflect cricopharyngeal timing abnormality, but also seen with structural restriction, poor orolingual control, impaired hyolaryngeal elevation |

- Typing is partly subjective; the importance of distinguishing types is not established (Section 4.2, p.4).

### Swallow challenge protocol (Section 5, pp.8–9; Figure 4, p.8)
- **L9** — Each bolus challenge **ideally repeated a minimum of three times**.
- **L10** — Include **thin liquid (International Dysphagia Diet Standardization Initiative [IDDSI] Level 0)**, then a **thickened liquid (IDDSI Levels 2, 3 and/or 4)**; thicker boluses can also be considered for higher-risk patients or poor tolerance of thin fluids.
- **L11** — No optimal viscosity specified (unknown). Prefer fluids of known shear rheology: viscosity in SI units (mPa·s, Pa·s or cP) at **50 s⁻¹ (oral phase)** and **300 s⁻¹ (pharyngeal phase)**.
- **L12** — Monitor the patient throughout; **truncate** if the patient feels "full" or fatigued or tolerates poorly (unable/unwilling to take boluses, significant pain, overt aspiration or respiratory distress, change in arousal, e.g., vagal response).
- **L13** — May include dry (saliva) swallows; **always include 5 mL thin liquid** as the conservative starting challenge; **always aim to include 10 mL thin** and **5 mL and/or 10 mL thickened** swallows; **aim to include 20 mL thin** when tolerated.
- **L14** — Give by spoon or syringe; instruct to swallow the bolus all at once. Challenges producing multiple swallows (**piecemeal/partial** = bolus divided across ≥2 successive swallows; **clearing** = incomplete transfer needing further swallows) should be **redone** if pharyngeal function seems sufficient for a single swallow.
- **L15** — Follow the recommended order for standardization; may be modified for safety and tolerance; volumes and viscosity adjusted case by case.

**Figure 4 protocol (p.8), as steps:**

| Step | Action | Next |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Catheter placement and **≥5 min accommodation** | → 2 |
| 2 | **3 × 5 mL thin liquid** (known to be tolerated by most patients) | Tolerated and consumed in a single swallow? Yes → 3; No → 3 × 5 mL viscous → 3 × 10 mL viscous (volume response to viscous) |
| 3 | **3 × 10 mL thin liquid** | Not tolerated and/or multiple swallowing → revert to 5 mL viscous swallows; otherwise → 4 |
| 4 | **1–3 × 20 mL thin liquid** (response to volume) **and/or 1–3 × 10 mL viscous** (response to viscosity); order interchangeable by tolerance and clinician preference | End |

- Grey-shaded steps (10 mL thin, 20 mL thin, 10 mL viscous) are **needed to reliably identify the UES region as the primary area of failure**; pharyngeal contractile dysfunction can be identified with **any** bolus swallows (Figure 4 annotations).

### Diagnosis of UES dysfunction (Section 6, pp.9–11; Figure 5, p.9)
- **L16** — **Definition:** failure of normal manometric relaxation and/or distension of the pharyngo-esophageal segment (PES).
- **L17** — Best identified by **higher-volume (≥10 mL)** swallows; without ≥10 mL swallows many patients with dysfunction potentially amenable to cricopharyngeal disruption will be missed; false negatives are worse with inadequate propulsion or piecemeal/partial swallows.
- **L18** — **Core criterion:** the **majority of ≥10 mL swallows (two out of three; excluding piecemeal/partial swallows)** show **two or more** abnormal COS metrics: **high IBP, high UES IRP, low UES MaxAd, or short UES RT**.
- **L19** — High IBP and/or high UES IRP give the strongest support; **unconfirmed if both UES IRP and IBP are normal**, even if UES MaxAd and UES RT are abnormal.
- **L20** — **Stimulus–response required:** abnormal parameters must become more evident as bolus volume and/or viscosity increases.
- **L21** — **Unconfirmed** if abnormal relaxation/distension appears only with dry swallows, 5 mL challenges, or swallows with incomplete single-swallow transfer (piecemeal/partial, pharyngeal hypocontractility); also unconfirmed if stimulus–response is lacking.
- **L22** — Unconfirmed cases **may be supported** by VFSS: structural pathology at the PES, reduced PES opening diameter, reduced PES opening duration, or early PES closure.
- **L23** — **Supported** by Type 1 and Type 2 pressurization; **may be supported** by Type 3 pressurization and by **augmented pharyngeal contractility** (upstream compensation).
- **L24** — **Functional lumen imaging probe (EndoFLIP):** detects reduced PES distensibility in structural disease (radiation-induced stricture, Zenker's diverticulum); research tool during endoscopy; needs sedation, sedation/relaxant effects unclear, few normative data — **no specific recommendation** (Section 6, pp.10–11).

**Figure 5 algorithm (p.9), as a table.** The first three questions (shaded) are the requirements for a diagnosis on P-HRM-I alone.

| Step | Question | Yes | No |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **At least 3 trials of 10 mL** swallows done (excluding piecemeal and partial)? | → 2 | → 1b |
| 1b | Majority of swallows with **≥2 abnormal features** (IBP or UES IRP must be abnormal)? | → 4 | **Inconclusive** (potential false negative due to insufficient volume) |
| 2 | Majority of swallows with **≥2 abnormal features** (IBP or UES IRP must be abnormal)? | → 3 | → 2b |
| 2b | Poor or weak pharyngeal contractility? | **Inconclusive** (potential false negative due to inadequate propulsion) | **Insufficient evidence of UES dysfunction** |
| 3 | One or more abnormal features **more evident as bolus volume and/or viscosity increases**? | **Evidence of UES dysfunction** | → 4 |
| 4 | Confirmatory/supportive findings? | **Evidence of UES dysfunction** | **Insufficient evidence of UES dysfunction** |

Confirmatory/supportive findings box (Figure 5):
- **Confirmed by VFSS:** structural pathology at the PES; reduced PES opening diameter; reduced PES opening duration or early PES closure.
- **Supported by P-HRM-I:** pan- (Type 1) pressurization; sustained (Type 2) pressurization.
- **May be supported by:** transient (Type 3) pressurization; augmented pharyngeal contractility as an upstream compensatory response.

### Diagnosis of pharyngeal contractile dysfunction (Section 7, pp.11–13; Figure 8, p.12)
- **L25** — Primarily an **abnormal sequential segmental contraction**; criteria assess **vigor only** — timing and coordination are not captured by the current COS contractile metrics.
- **L26** — Diagnosis requires the **majority of bolus swallows (two out of three)** to show **ABSENT or LOW** contractile pressures in **one or more** pharyngeal segments. Majority **HIGH** in ≥1 segment → usually compensatory, although it may represent dysfunction.
- **L27** — Can be identified with **any** bolus swallows; may be identified with dry swallows.
- **L28** — **VCI, MCI, HPCI and mean HPeakP** recommended as measures of contractile strength; other metrics at the analyst's discretion until more data.
- **L29** — Hypocontractility is strongly associated with impaired pharyngeal emptying and postswallow residue.
- **L30** — Hypercontractility unrelated to a swallowing maneuver (e.g., effortful swallow) can be global or fragmented; commonly compensatory, often with UES dysfunction.
- **L31** — **Combined pharyngeal and UES disorder** is considered when UES dysfunction criteria are also met: **hypercontractility + UES dysfunction** → within the spectrum of UES dysfunction; **hypocontractility + UES dysfunction** → combined disorder, where visual instrumental investigations such as videofluoroscopy are **essential** (Section 7, pp.12–13).
- On topography, **low** pressures suggest a weak lumen-occlusive contraction; **absent** suggests a nonocclusive contraction (Figure 9 legend, p.13).

**Figure 8 classification (p.12), applied in order:**

| Order | Majority of bolus swallows show… | Diagnosis | Note (figure) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ABSENT or LOW contractile pressures across **all** segments (velopharynx, mesopharynx, hypopharynx) | **Global pharyngeal hypocontractility** | Strongly associated with impaired pharyngeal emptying → postswallow residue |
| 2 | ABSENT or LOW in **some** segments (incl. a mixture of hypo- and hypercontractile segments — text) | **Fragmented pharyngeal hypocontractility** | Same |
| 3 | HIGH in **some or all** segments | **Pharyngeal hypercontractility** | Commonly compensatory, often with UES dysfunction |
| 4 | None of the above | **Not pharyngeal contractile dysfunction** | — |

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **Internal inconsistencies:**
  - Table 2 HPCI row defines the metric as "HCI" in its definition text.
  - Table 2 gives **no interpretation** for HPeakP, though Section 7 recommends it.
  - **VFSS findings:** Figure 5 box says UES dysfunction is "**Confirmed** by VFSS findings"; Section 6 text says unconfirmed dysfunction "**may be supported** by" the same VFSS findings.
  - **Type 2 naming:** "distal-compartmentalized" (Section 4.2), "sustained distal compartmentalized" (Figure 3), "Sustained- (Type 2)" (Figure 5); the Figure 7 Case A legend calls a pattern "transient Type 2" although Type 2 is defined as sustained.
  - **Swallow count:** Figure 5 asks for "at least 3 trials of 10 mL"; text requires "the majority of ≥10 mL swallows (two out of three)"; Figure 5 caption says swallows "of at least 10 mL".
  - **Dry swallows:** Figure 8 caption says "bolus swallows of any volume"; Section 7 adds contractile dysfunction "may be identified with dry swallows".
  - **Timing of VFSS:** caveat L1 allows VFSS/FEES simultaneously **or** beforehand; Figure 1 routes patients without a clear history and low aspiration risk to FEES/VFSS first, and its caption "strongly recommends" simultaneous Video P-HRM-I when VFSS/FEES has not been done.
  - Working group named "International Pharyngeal HRM Working Group" (Section 1) and "International Pharyngeal Manometry Working Group" (Section 9).
- **No numeric cutoffs:** every abnormality ("high", "low", "short", ABSENT/LOW/HIGH) depends on system-specific normal ranges the consensus does not supply.
- **Source standing:** international, industry-funded expert consensus, not a US society guideline; the wiki has no US guideline on pharyngeal manometry.
- **vs [[zenkers-diverticulum]] ([[esge-2020-gi-motility-part-2]]):** ESGE R2 suggests against manometry as standard in Zenker's workup (reserve for individual cases); Leuven strongly recommends P-HRM-I when UES dysfunction is suspected but does not address Zenker's workup specifically — no direct conflict.
- **vs [[dysphagia]] ([[asge-2014-dysphagia]], [[rome-v-2026-functional-esophageal-disorders]]):** consistent — videofluoroscopy stays first-line; Leuven positions P-HRM-I as complementary.
- **vs [[globus]] ([[rome-v-2026-functional-esophageal-disorders]]):** Rome V notes UES pressure abnormalities in globus are inconsistently reported; Leuven does not address globus.
- **Term collision:** "IRP" on [[chicago-classification-v4]] (esophagogastric junction) is a different metric from UES IRP here (median of lowest nonconsecutive 0.20–0.25 s of UES e-sleeve pressure).
