## Summary

The paper that puts the **Bristol Stool Form Scale (BSFS)** into the literature as a usable instrument, and the only document in `raw/` that prints its **seven type descriptions**. The guidelines across the wiki subtype irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) by it, titrate lactulose by it and gate entry to refractory-constipation algorithms by it, but none reproduces the scale.

The scale was built deliberately for clinical use: an earlier 8-point scale correlated almost perfectly with transit time (r = 0.93) but was **too complicated for untrained observers**, and some of its descriptions applied only to stools seen on a flat dry surface rather than under water in the toilet bowl. The authors devised a simpler, **water-closet-relevant seven-item scale in everyday language** — the version still in use.

What this study adds beyond the scale itself is **responsiveness**: it shows the score tracks *change* in transit, not just a static correlation. In 66 healthy women, whole-gut transit time (WGTT) was measured with radiopaque marker pellets, then deliberately altered with senna (n = 44) or loperamide (n = 43). At baseline, WGTT correlated with defecatory frequency (r = 0.35, P = 0.005) and stool output (r = −0.41), but **best with stool form (r = −0.54, P < 0.001)**. Change in WGTT likewise correlated best with **change in stool form (r = −0.65, P < 0.001)** — better than change in frequency (r = 0.41) or stool output (r = −0.54). Senna cut median WGTT from 67 to 41 h and raised the stool form score from 3.6 to 4.6; loperamide moved every measure the opposite way (52 → 72 h, 3.9 → 2.8).

Stool form was also the **least variable** of the measures on repeated baseline assessment — coefficient of variation 13.6%, against 17.5% for WGTT, 17.9% for defecatory frequency and 39.3% for stool output. The authors' conclusion is the one the guidelines act on: **stool form is a better guide to transit time than how often the patient goes**, and it can be used to monitor response to treatment in constipation, diarrhea and IBS.

## Key Findings / Claims

**The scale itself (Table I, verbatim)**

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Type 1** | Separate hard lumps, like nuts |
| **Type 2** | Sausage-shaped but lumpy |
| **Type 3** | Like a sausage or snake but with cracks on its surface |
| **Type 4** | Like a sausage or snake, smooth and soft |
| **Type 5** | Soft blobs with clear-cut edges |
| **Type 6** | Fluffy pieces with ragged edges, a mushy stool |
| **Type 7** | Watery, no solid pieces |

- The direction of the scale is **type 1 = slow transit → type 7 = rapid transit**; the paper describes type 1 as "the discrete lumps of slow transit" and type 7 as "liquid stools of rapid transit".
- The subject, not the clinician, picks the type: the technique "involves no exposure to radiation, no stool handling, and no discomfort except the mild unpleasantness of subjects having to inspect their own faeces and decide which of several descriptions fits best".

**Performance**

| Relationship | Correlation |
|---|---|
| Baseline WGTT vs **stool form** | **r = −0.54**, P < 0.001 (the strongest of the three) |
| Baseline WGTT vs stool output | r = −0.41, P = 0.001 |
| Baseline WGTT vs defecatory frequency | r = 0.35, P = 0.005 |
| **Change** in WGTT vs **change in stool form** | **r = −0.65**, P < 0.001 (the strongest) |
| Change in WGTT vs change in stool output | r = −0.54, P < 0.001 |
| Change in WGTT vs change in defecatory frequency | r = 0.41, P < 0.001 |

- **Reproducibility:** coefficient of variation on replicated baseline measurements — **stool form 13.6%** (the least variable), WGTT 17.5%, defecatory frequency 17.9%, stool output 39.3%.
- **Responsiveness to a deliberate change in transit** (median values): senna WGTT 67 → 41 h with stool form score 3.6 → 4.6; loperamide WGTT 52 → 72 h with stool form score 3.9 → 2.8. All P < 0.001.
- Baseline in 66 healthy women: median WGTT 62.7 h, stool form score 3.6, stool output 923 g/week, interdefecatory interval 24.0 h.

**Interpretation the authors draw**

- **Stool form predicts transit better than defecation frequency**, which in some studies "has had no predictive value at all".
- Fluctuating stool form scores in IBS reflect an **objective abnormality of intestinal function**, not just symptom reporting.
- Stool form recordings can distinguish **true diarrhea and true constipation from the pseudo-diarrhea and pseudo-constipation of IBS**.
- Clinicians can use the scale to **monitor treatment response** in constipation, diarrhea or IBS, and the authors recommend building stool-form recording into gastrointestinal research protocols.

**Limitations the paper states**

- The correlation with transit is significant but **imperfect**: observer error, the possibility that form is set by residence time in one colonic segment (e.g. the sigmoid, whose contractions mould the stool) rather than overall passage time, and other unidentified factors besides transit.
- **Types 2 and 5 are easily confused** — the authors suggest the wording could be improved by referring type 5 to "the usually easier passage of type-5 stools".
- Subjects were **66 healthy women** (45 premenopausal, 21 postmenopausal), none obese and none on antibiotics within 3 months — not a patient population, and not men.
- Baseline WGTT happened to differ before senna versus before loperamide, a chance finding the authors address by analysing the 23 volunteers who took both.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **No contradiction with any ingested guideline** — the guidelines use the scale and this paper defines it; nothing in it competes with a recommendation.
- ⚠ **Tier and cards:** this is primary research (a validation study), **not** a guideline, consensus or clinical practice update. It supplies definitions the guidelines rely on, but under *Guideline-sourced cards only* it generates **no Anki cards**.
- **The paper gives the descriptions, not the clinical bands.** Cut-points such as "Bristol 1–2 = constipation", "6–7 = diarrhea" or a titration target come from the guidelines that use the scale, not from Lewis & Heaton, and stay on the pages that carry those recommendations.
- The scale was validated against **whole-gut transit time in healthy women**; its performance in disease states, in men, and against segmental transit is not addressed here.
