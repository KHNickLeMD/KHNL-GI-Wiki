## Summary

The LI-RADS Lexicon is a **25-page, single-table terminology standard** — not a guideline and not an algorithm. Each row is one term, with columns for the **2021 definition**, **context of use** (Broad = general liver imaging vocabulary; LI-RADS = term defined for use inside LI-RADS), **applicable modalities** (CT, MRI, CEUS, US), general comments, LI-RADS-specific comments, deprecated synonyms, **type of term** (imaging phase · imaging feature, general · major feature · ancillary feature favouring malignancy/HCC · LR-M feature · LR-TIV feature · technical term · type of contrast agent), and date approved.

It carries **no recommendations, no GRADE ratings and no numbered statements** — its purpose is that one word means one thing across the CT/MRI Core, the CEUS Core and the US Surveillance Core.

Its practical value over the Cores is threefold: it collects the **definitions in one place** rather than scattered through three algorithm documents; it names the **synonyms the committee considers deprecated** (e.g. "arterial hypervascularity", "wash in", "fibrous capsule", "intralesional fat", "tumor capsule"), which is what makes legacy reports ambiguous; and it classifies **which kind of term each is**, so a reader can tell a major feature from an ancillary one without consulting the algorithm.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Imaging phases

| Term | Definition (2021) |
|---|---|
| **Imaging phase** | A time range after intravenous contrast injection with characteristic changes in enhancement of liver parenchyma, vessels, and — for some agents — bile ducts |
| **Arterial phase (AP)** | A postcontrast phase when hepatic artery and branches are **fully enhanced AND** hepatic veins are **not** enhanced more than liver by antegrade flow |
| **Early arterial phase** | Subtype of AP (CT/MRI) when the **portal vein is not enhanced or is enhanced less than liver** |
| **Late arterial phase** | Subtype of AP (CT/MRI) when the **portal vein is enhanced more than liver** |
| **Portal venous phase (PVP)** | Postarterial phase acquired **no more than 2 minutes** after injection, when portal and hepatic veins are enhanced more than liver |
| **Delayed phase (DP)** | Postarterial phase acquired **at least 2 minutes** after injection of an **extracellular agent or gadobenate**, when portal and hepatic veins are enhanced more than liver |
| **Transitional phase (TP)** | Postarterial phase with a **hepatobiliary agent**, when liver vessels and parenchyma are of **similar** signal intensity — between the portal venous and hepatobiliary phases |
| **Hepatobiliary phase (HBP)** | Postcontrast phase with a hepatobiliary agent, when liver parenchyma is **intended to be hyperintense to hepatic blood vessels** |
| **Late phase (LP)** | Postarterial phase on **CEUS** after the PVP, when portal and hepatic veins are enhanced but **less than in PVP** |
| **Postarterial phase** | General term for imaging after the arterial phase |
| **Postarterial extracellular phase (ECP)** | **PVP and DP** if an extracellular agent or gadobenate is given; **PVP only** if gadoxetate is given |

### Contrast agent classes

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Extracellular agents (ECAs)** | Predominantly extracellular distribution after IV injection |
| **Hepatobiliary agents (HBAs)** | Sufficient hepatobiliary uptake and excretion to allow HBP imaging |
| **Blood pool agents (BPAs)** | Distribute mainly in the vascular space after IV injection |

### Enhancement features

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Arterial phase hyperenhancement (APHE)** | Enhancement in arterial phase more than liver, resulting in brightness higher than liver |
| **Nonrim APHE** | Subtype of APHE that is **NOT** mainly in observation periphery |
| **Rim APHE** | Subtype of APHE that **is** mainly in observation periphery |
| **Peripheral discontinuous nodular APHE** | Areas of enhancement that during the arterial phase are initially round or globular and distributed **discontinuously along the periphery**, then **rapidly expand** to fill the lesion entirely or nearly so |
| **Peripheral discontinuous nodular enhancement** | Same morphology in the early postcontrast phases, but in subsequent phases expanding and **approximately paralleling the blood pool in brightness** |
| **Spokewheel, centrifugal APHE** | Enhancement beginning as an internal focus and rapidly expanding outward in a **radial, spoke-wheel** pattern |
| **Washout** | Reduction in enhancement from earlier to later phase resulting in **hypoenhancement relative to liver** |
| **Nonperipheral washout** | Subtype of washout **NOT** mainly in observation periphery |
| **Peripheral washout** | Subtype mainly in observation periphery |
| **Early washout** | CEUS subtype with onset **<60 s** after injection |
| **Late washout** | CEUS subtype with onset **>60 s** after injection |
| **Mild washout** | CEUS subtype — observation becomes less enhanced than liver but **not devoid** of enhancement |
| **Marked washout** | CEUS subtype — observation becomes **black or "punched out"** while background liver is still enhancing |
| **Fade** | Reduction in enhancement relative to liver **from hyperenhancement to isoenhancement or minimal hyperenhancement** in all later phases — *not* washout |
| **Corona enhancement** | Periobservational enhancement in late arterial or early portal venous phase, **contiguous with and surrounding** all or part of the observation |
| **Delayed central enhancement** | Postarterial pattern where the **inner** part of the observation is more enhanced than the periphery |
| **Parallels blood pool enhancement** | Temporal pattern in which enhancement approximates blood pool in **all** phases |

**Washout patterns by modality (lexicon):** CT or MRI — hyperenhancing → hypoenhancing, or isoenhancing → hypoenhancing; **if a hepatobiliary agent is given, washout must be assessed *before* the transitional phase**. CEUS — the same two, **plus** hypoenhancing → unequivocally more hypoenhancing.

### Capsule, morphology and other features

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Capsule** | Smooth, uniform, sharp border on CT or MRI around **most or all** of an observation |
| **Enhancing capsule** | Capsule visible as an **enhancing rim** in PVP, DP or TP |
| **Nonenhancing capsule** | Capsule that does **not** show enhancement on any image |
| **Targetoid** | Target-like morphology on CT or MRI — centre and periphery have **different** imaging characteristics |
| **Targetoid diffusion restriction** | Restricted diffusion **greatest in the periphery** |
| **Targetoid TP or HBP appearance** | TP/HBP hypointensity where the **periphery is more hypointense than the centre** |
| **Mosaic appearance** | Any combination of internal nodules, compartments or septations within a solid or mostly solid mass |
| **Nodule-in-nodule appearance** | A smaller inner nodule within a larger outer nodule |
| **Enhancing soft tissue in vein** | Enhancing soft tissue in vein, **regardless of whether a parenchymal mass is visualised** |
| **Undistorted vessels** | Vessels traversing an observation **without displacement, deformation or other alteration** |
| **Blood products in mass** | Blood products in a mass, **in the absence of biopsy, trauma or intervention** |
| **Diffusion restriction** | Intensity higher than liver on diffusion-weighted images **not caused only by T2 shine-through** |
| **Mild-moderate T2 hyperintensity** | Higher than liver, **similar to or lower than non-iron-overloaded spleen**, and lower than simple fluid |
| **Marked T2 hyperintensity** | Higher than non-iron-overloaded spleen and **as high as or almost as high as simple fluid** |
| **Fat in mass, more than adjacent liver** / **Fat sparing in solid mass** | More fat in a mass than in liver / less fat in a solid mass than in fatty liver |
| **Iron in mass, more than liver** / **Iron sparing in solid mass** | More iron in a mass than in liver / less iron in a solid mass than in iron-overloaded liver |

### Size and growth

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Size** | **Largest outer-edge-to-outer-edge dimension** of an observation |
| **Growth** | Definite size increase of a mass that **cannot be explained only by technique differences, artifact, measurement error, or interval hemorrhage** |
| **Threshold growth** | Size increase of a mass by **≥50% in ≤6 months** |
| **Subthreshold growth** | Size increase less than threshold growth — **any of**: size increase **<50% over any time period** · **any** size increase over an interval **>6 months** · **a new mass of any size** |
| **Size reduction** | Spontaneous decrease in size over time not explained only by technique differences, artifact or measurement error |
| **Size stability ≥2 years** | No change in size measured on serial exams **≥2 years apart** |

### Ultrasound and general terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Hyperechoic / isoechoic / hypoechoic** | Echogenicity higher than / equal to / lower than a reference tissue, organ or structure |
| **Parenchymal distortion** | Parenchymal area on ultrasound with ≥1 of: **ill-defined area of heterogeneity · refractive shadow · loss of normal hepatic architecture** |
| **Refractive shadowing** | Linear shadows from the **lateral edges** of an observation (which may be well- or ill-defined) |
| **US visibility as nodule** | Unenhanced US visibility as a discrete nodule or mass **corresponding to a CT- or MRI-detected observation** |
| **Perfusion alteration** | **Nonmasslike** change in blood supply to an area of liver |
| **Observation** | Area distinctive compared to liver at imaging |
| **Lesion** | An observation that **represents** a pathologic abnormality |
| **Pseudolesion** | An observation that **may simulate but does not represent** a pathologic abnormality |
| **Mass** | Space-occupying lesion that **distorts or destroys** parenchyma or other anatomic structures |
| **Nodule** | **Spherical or oval** mass |
| **Nonmasslike** (adjective) | Without distorting or destroying parenchyma or other anatomic structures |
| **Treated lesion** | Lesion treated by any therapy |
| **Locoregional therapy** | A therapy that targets a specific lesion or part of the liver **without physically removing it** |
| **Continuous imaging** | Acquisition without pause or interruption (US/CEUS, typically 10–20 frames/second) |
| **Intermittent imaging** | A series of brief CEUS acquisitions, each a few seconds, repeated at **about 30–60 second** intervals with no imaging in between |
| **Multiphase imaging** | Acquisition at **two or more** different phases after IV contrast injection |

### Term-type classification

The lexicon labels every term, which is how a reader tells what a feature does: **imaging phase · imaging feature, general · imaging feature, major · ancillary feature favouring HCC in particular · ancillary feature favouring malignancy in general, not HCC in particular · LR-M feature · LR-TIV feature · technical term · type of contrast agent**. It also defines the feature classes themselves — **major feature** (used to assign LR-3/4/5, reflecting relative probability of HCC), **ancillary feature** (adjusts category, increases diagnostic confidence, or detects observations difficult to visualise on other sequences), **LR-M features**, and **LR-TIV features**.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **No contradictions with the Cores.** Each Core defines the terms it uses and this table is their shared source; where both print a definition, they agree.
- **Low marginal yield, and that was known before ingest.** [[acr-2018-li-rads-ct-mri-core]] and [[acr-2024-li-rads-us-surveillance-core]] are both ingested and each is self-contained. The lexicon's net-new content is the **deprecated-synonym lists**, the **term-type classification**, and definitions for terms the Cores use without restating (fade, corona enhancement, mosaic appearance, nodule-in-nodule, the CEUS washout subtypes).
- **Undated beyond "2021" / "5/2021".** The file carries no version number or citation line and is maintained as a living web document; a later revision would not be detectable from the copy in `raw/`.
- **Deprecated synonyms are recorded here, not on the entity page.** Examples the lexicon flags as less preferred: *arterial hypervascularity, wash in, increased contrast enhancement in late hepatic arterial phase* (for APHE); *capsule appearance, pseudocapsule, tumor capsule, tumor pseudocapsule, fibrous capsule, fibrous pseudocapsule* (for capsule) and *capsular enhancement, delayed enhancing rim* (for enhancing capsule); *intravascular contrast agents* (for BPAs); *interstitial phase, equilibrium phase, late dynamic phase, late venous phase* (for DP); *sustained central enhancement, centripetal progressive enhancement* (for delayed central enhancement); *impeded diffusion, high DWI signal* (for diffusion restriction); *steatotic nodule, intralesional fat, fatty metamorphosis* (for fat in mass).
- ⚠ **Not cardable in practice.** It is an ACR standard (tier 1 by type), but it is a vocabulary list: definitions of terms whose *use* is carded on [[li-rads]] from the Core documents. Carding a definition that no decision rests on fails the high-yield test, and the decision-bearing ones are owned by [[li-rads]]. **No cards.**
