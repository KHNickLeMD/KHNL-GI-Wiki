## Summary

Baveno VII ("Personalized Care for Portal Hypertension") is the seventh international consensus workshop of the Baveno Cooperation (EASL consortium). It reframes cirrhosis care around **risk stratification by non-invasive tests (NITs)** rather than universal endoscopy, and around the presence or absence of **clinically significant portal hypertension (CSPH)** within the compensated stage. Statements span 9 topics: (1) HVPG as gold standard, (2) NITs for cACLD and portal hypertension, (3) management after removal/suppression of the primary aetiology, (4) non-aetiological therapies (statins, aspirin, anticoagulation, albumin), (5) prevention of first decompensation, (6) acute variceal bleeding, (7) prevention of further decompensation and recompensation, (8) splanchnic vein thrombosis (Budd-Chiari, non-cirrhotic PVT), and (9) other vascular liver disorders (PVT in cirrhosis, PSVD). Each statement is GRADE-graded A–D (evidence) and 1/2 (strength), and tagged Unchanged / Changed / New vs Baveno VI.

Central operational advances: **CSPH is now defined as HVPG ≥10 mmHg**; a **"rule of 5" for LSM by transient elastography (10-15-20-25 kPa)** grades decompensation risk; **LSM <15 kPa + platelets >150 ×10⁹/L rules out CSPH** while **LSM ≥25 kPa rules it in** (viral/alcohol/non-obese NASH); **Baveno VI criteria (LSM <20 kPa + platelets >150 ×10⁹/L) rule out high-risk varices** and allow endoscopy to be avoided; and **NSBBs (carvedilol preferred) prevent first decompensation in patients with CSPH** (PREDESCI), independent of the ability to measure HVPG, obviating screening endoscopy in treated patients. The workshop also formalizes **cirrhosis recompensation** criteria, **pre-emptive (early) TIPS** criteria in acute variceal bleeding, and a full framework for **porto-sinusoidal vascular disorder (PSVD)** and splanchnic vein thrombosis.

Because AASLD's 2023 Practice Guidance on portal hypertension and varices adapts and updates this consensus for US practice, the [[portal-hypertension]] wiki page follows AASLD 2023 where the two overlap (newer guideline-tier source); Baveno VII is the foundational consensus and the home of several concepts (recompensation, PSVD criteria, splanchnic-vein-thrombosis nomenclature) captured here and on the entity pages.

## Key Findings / Claims

Statements are reproduced near-verbatim with their GRADE grade `(evidence.strength)` and change status. Research-agenda items are omitted (not recommendations).

### 1) HVPG as a gold standard
- **1.9** HVPG >5 mmHg indicates sinusoidal portal hypertension. (A.1) (Unchanged)
- **1.10** In viral- and alcohol-related cirrhosis, HVPG is the gold standard for CSPH, **defined as HVPG ≥10 mmHg**. (A.1) (Changed)
- **1.11** In primary biliary cholangitis a pre-sinusoidal component may exist that HVPG cannot assess; HVPG may underestimate PH prevalence/severity. (B.1) (New)
- **1.12** In NASH-related cirrhosis, although HVPG ≥10 mmHg remains strongly associated with clinical signs of PH, these can also occur at HVPG <10 mmHg in a small proportion. (C.2) (New)
- **1.13** In chronic liver disease with clinical signs of PH (varices, ascites, collaterals) but HVPG <10 mmHg, [[porto-sinusoidal-vascular-disorder|porto-sinusoidal vascular disorder (PSVD)]] must be ruled out. (B.1) (New)
- **1.14** In alcohol/viral cirrhosis, a decrease in HVPG in response to NSBBs is associated with reduced risk of variceal bleeding or other decompensating events. (A.1) (Changed)
- **1.18** CSPH (by HVPG ≥10 mmHg or clinical manifestations) is associated with higher risk of decompensation and mortality after liver resection for HCC. (A.1) (New)
- **1.19** In candidates for non-hepatic abdominal surgery, **HVPG ≥16 mmHg** is associated with increased short-term post-surgical mortality. (C.1) (New)
- **1.20** PPG should be measured before and after TIPS insertion. (A.1) (New)
- **1.23** In variceal bleeding undergoing TIPS, reduction of absolute PPG to **<12 mmHg** gives near-complete protection from PH bleeding (preferred target); a relative reduction ≥50% from baseline may also be useful. (A.1 / B.2) (New)
- (Description/technique statements 1.1–1.8, trial-design 1.15–1.17, 1.21–1.22, 1.24 as in source.)

### 2) Non-invasive tools for cACLD and portal hypertension
- **2.1** Elastography enables early identification of untreated/active chronic liver disease at risk of CSPH, decompensation, and liver-related death. (A.1) (Changed)
- **2.3** Both terms "cACLD" and "compensated cirrhosis" are acceptable but not interchangeable. (B.1) (Changed)
- **2.4** LSM by TE **<10 kPa** rules out cACLD (absent other signs); **10–15 kPa** suggestive; **>15 kPa** highly suggestive of cACLD. (B.1) (Changed)
- **2.5** LSM <10 kPa carries a negligible 3-year risk (<1%) of decompensation/liver-related death. (A.1) (New)
- **2.9** A **"rule of 5" for LSM by TE (10-15-20-25 kPa)** denotes progressively higher relative risks of decompensation and liver-related death, independent of aetiology. (B.1) (New)
- **2.10** LSM 7–10 kPa with ongoing liver injury: monitor case-by-case for progression to cACLD. (C.2) (New)
- **2.11** TE can give false positives; an index LSM ≥10 kPa should be repeated fasting or complemented with a serum fibrosis marker (FIB-4 ≥2.67; ELF ≥9.8; FibroTest ≥0.58 [alcohol/viral], ≥0.48 [NAFLD]). (B.2) (New)
- **2.12** In cACLD, LSM could be repeated every 12 months to monitor changes. (B.2) (New)
- **2.13** A clinically significant decrease in LSM (reduced risk of decompensation/death) = decrease of **>20% with LSM <20 kPa, or any decrease to LSM <10 kPa**. (C.2) (New)
- **2.15** **LSM <15 kPa + platelet count >150 ×10⁹/L rules out CSPH** (sensitivity and NPV >90%) in cACLD. (B.2) (New)
- **2.16** In virus/alcohol-related and non-obese (BMI <30) NASH-related cACLD, **LSM ≥25 kPa is sufficient to rule in CSPH** (specificity and PPV >90%). (B.1) (Changed)
- **2.17** For LSM <25 kPa, the **ANTICIPATE model** predicts CSPH risk: **LSM 20–25 kPa + platelets <150 ×10⁹/L, or LSM 15–20 kPa + platelets <110 ×10⁹/L → CSPH risk ≥60%**. (B.2) (New)
- **2.18** In NASH-related cACLD the ANTICIPATE-NASH model (LSM, platelets, BMI) may predict CSPH (further validation needed). (C.2) (New)
- **2.19** Compensated cirrhosis patients not candidates for NSBBs should undergo screening endoscopy if **LSM by TE >20 kPa or platelets <150 ×10⁹/L**. (A.1) (New)
- **2.20** Patients avoiding screening endoscopy: follow with yearly TE + platelet count; if LSM rises >20 kPa or platelets fall <150 ×10⁹/L, perform screening endoscopy. (D.1) (Unchanged)
- **2.21** **Spleen stiffness measurement (SSM) by TE** in viral-hepatitis cACLD: **SSM <21 kPa rules out** and **SSM >50 kPa rules in** CSPH. (B.2) (New)
- **2.22** In patients not candidates for NSBBs who meet Baveno VI criteria for endoscopy (LSM >20 kPa or platelets <150 ×10⁹/L), **SSM <40 kPa by TE** identifies low probability of high-risk varices → endoscopy can be avoided. (C.2) (New)

### 3) Management of ACLD after removal/suppression of the primary aetiology
- **3.1** Removal/suppression = SVR in HCV, HBV suppression (no HDV), long-term alcohol abstinence. (A.1) (New)
- **3.4** Removal/suppression leads to meaningful HVPG decreases in most patients and substantially reduces decompensation risk. (A.1) (Changed)
- **3.5** Absence/resolution of CSPH after removal/suppression prevents hepatic decompensation. (B.1) (Changed)
- **3.7** HCV cACLD achieving SVR with post-treatment LSM <12 kPa and platelets >150 ×10⁹/L can be discharged from PH surveillance (no CSPH); continue HCC surveillance. (B.1) (New)
- **3.8** Baveno VI criteria (**LSM <20 kPa + platelets >150 ×10⁹/L**) can rule out high-risk varices in HCV/HBV cACLD after SVR/suppression. (B.1) (New)
- **3.9** cACLD on NSBB with no evident CSPH (LSM <25 kPa) after removal/suppression: reassess endoscopy at 1–2 years; if no varices, NSBB can be discontinued. (C.2) (New)

### 4) Impact of non-aetiological therapies
- **4.1** Statins should be encouraged in cirrhosis with an approved indication (may decrease portal pressure and improve survival). (A.1 / B.1) (Changed)
- **4.2** In Child-Pugh B/C cirrhosis, statins at **lower dose (simvastatin max 20 mg/d)** with close monitoring for muscle/liver toxicity; in Child-Pugh C benefit unproven → use restrictively. (A.1 / D.1) (Changed)
- **4.3** Aspirin should not be discouraged when there is an approved indication (may reduce HCC, liver-related complications, death). (B.2) (New)
- **4.4** Long-term albumin may reduce complications and improve transplant-free survival in uncomplicated ascites, but no formal recommendation yet. (B.2) (New)
- **4.5** Short-term albumin is indicated for SBP (A.1), AKI >stage 1A (C.1), large-volume paracentesis (A.1), and with terlipressin for HRS-AKI (B.1). (New)
- **4.6** Primary antibiotic prophylaxis in selected high-SBP-risk patients (GI haemorrhage; Child-Pugh C with low-protein ascites). (B.1) (New)
- **4.7** Secondary antibiotic prophylaxis is indicated after previous SBP. (A.1) (New)
- **4.8** Rifaximin is indicated for secondary prophylaxis of hepatic encephalopathy. (A.1) (New)
- **4.9** Rifaximin should be considered for prophylaxis of overt HE in patients with prior overt HE undergoing elective TIPS. (B.2) (New)
- **4.11** Anticoagulation should not be discouraged when there is an approved indication (may reduce liver-related outcomes with/without PVT and improve survival). (B.1) (Changed)
- **4.12** DOACs are as safe/effective for cardiovascular events in Child-Pugh A/B cirrhosis as in non-cirrhotics (B.2); **DOACs not recommended in Child-Pugh C outside protocols**. (B.2) (New)

### 5) Prevention of (first) decompensation
- **5.4** Events defining decompensation: overt ascites (or pleural effusion, SAAG >1.1), overt HE (West Haven ≥II), and variceal bleeding. (B.1) (New)
- **5.14** NSBBs (propranolol, nadolol, or carvedilol) should be considered to prevent decompensation in patients with CSPH. (B.1) (New) — carvedilol has added anti-α-adrenergic vasodilatory effect → greater portal-pressure reduction.
- **5.15** **Carvedilol is the preferred NSBB** in compensated cirrhosis (more effective at reducing HVPG, trend to greater decompensation prevention/tolerance, improves survival vs no therapy in CSPH). (A.1 / B.1) (Changed)
- **5.16** The decision to treat with NSBBs should be taken when clinically indicated, **independent of the ability to measure HVPG**. (B.2) (Unchanged)
- **5.17** Compensated patients on NSBBs for decompensation prevention **do not need screening endoscopy** (it will not change management). (B.2) (New)
- **5.19** In compensated patients with high-risk varices and NSBB contraindication/intolerance, **endoscopic band ligation** is recommended to prevent first variceal bleeding. (A.1) (Changed)
- **5.20** No indication to use NSBBs in patients without CSPH. (A.1) (Unchanged)

### 6) Acute variceal bleeding (AVB)
- **6.2** Restrictive transfusion, target **Hb 7–8 g/dl** (individualize). (A.1) (Unchanged)
- **6.3** Intubation before endoscopy in altered consciousness or active haematemesis. (D.1) (New)
- **6.5** Vasoactive drugs (**terlipressin, somatostatin, or octreotide**) as soon as possible, continued **2–5 days**. (A.1) (Changed)
- **6.6** Terlipressin can cause hyponatremia (esp. preserved liver function) — monitor sodium. (B.1) (Unchanged)
- **6.7** Antibiotic prophylaxis is integral, instituted from admission. (A.1) (Unchanged)
- **6.9** IV **ceftriaxone 1 g/24 h** in advanced cirrhosis / high quinolone-resistance settings / prior quinolone prophylaxis. (D.2) (Changed)
- **6.17** After resuscitation, upper endoscopy **within 12 h** of presentation. (B.1) (Changed)
- **6.19** Pre-endoscopy **erythromycin 250 mg IV 30–120 min** before (if no QT prolongation). (B.1) (Unchanged)
- **6.21** **Ligation** is the recommended endoscopic therapy for acute oesophageal variceal bleeding. (A.1) (Unchanged)
- **6.22** **Tissue adhesive** (N-butyl-cyanoacrylate/thrombin) for isolated gastric varices and GOV2 extending beyond the cardia. (A.1) (Unchanged)
- **6.24** Haemostatic powder cannot be recommended as first-line endoscopic therapy for AVB. (D.1) (New)
- **6.27** **Pre-emptive TIPS** (PTFE-covered) within 72 h (ideally <24 h) in patients bleeding from oesophageal or GOV1/2 varices meeting any of: **Child-Pugh C <14 points**, or **Child-Pugh B >7 with active bleeding at endoscopy**, or **HVPG >20 mmHg** at haemorrhage. (A.1) (Changed)
- **6.28** In pre-emptive-TIPS candidates, ACLF, HE at admission, and hyperbilirubinemia should **not** be considered contraindications. (B.1) (New)
- **6.29** In refractory bleeding, balloon tamponade or self-expandable metal stents (SEMS) as a bridge to PTFE-covered TIPS; SEMS as efficacious as tamponade and safer. (B.1) (Changed)
- **6.30** Failure despite combined pharmacological + endoscopic therapy → salvage PTFE-covered TIPS. (B.1) (Changed)
- **6.31** TIPS may be futile in **Child-Pugh ≥14** or **MELD >30 + lactate >12 mmol/L**, unless short-term transplant is envisioned (case-by-case). (B.1 / D.1) (New)
- **6.34** Aim of treatment is lowering portal pressure, not correcting coagulation abnormalities. (B.1) (New)
- **6.36** FFP transfusion is not recommended in AVB (won't correct coagulopathy; risks volume overload/worse PH). (B.1) (New)
- **6.38** Recombinant factor VIIa and tranexamic acid are not recommended in AVB. (A.1) (New)
- **6.40** For GOV2, IGV1, and ectopic varices, BRTO could be an alternative to endoscopic treatment or TIPS if feasible and locally available. (D.2) (New)

### 7) Prevention of further decompensation & recompensation
- **7.1** "Further decompensation" (higher mortality than first): a second PH-driven event (ascites, variceal bleeding, or HE) and/or jaundice; recurrent variceal bleeding; recurrent ascites (>3 large-volume paracenteses/year); recurrent HE; SBP and/or HRS-AKI; or (in bleeders) ascites/HE/jaundice after recovery. (B.1) (New)
- **7.4** TIPS should be considered in **recurrent ascites (>3 LVP within 1 year)**, irrespective of varices or bleeding history. (A.1) (New)
- **7.5** Ascites + low-risk varices (small [<5 mm], no red signs, not Child-Pugh C): traditional NSBBs or carvedilol may prevent first bleed. (B.2) (Changed)
- **7.6** Ascites + high-risk varices (large [≥5 mm], red signs, or Child-Pugh C): traditional NSBBs or carvedilol **preferred over EVL** for first-bleed prevention. (B.1) (Changed)
- **7.7** In ascites, dose-reduce/discontinue NSBBs if persistently low BP (SBP <90 mmHg or MAP <65 mmHg) and/or HRS-AKI; re-initiate once BP recovers / HRS-AKI resolves; if remaining intolerant, use EVL. (B.1) (Changed)
- **7.8** First-line secondary prophylaxis of recurrent variceal bleeding: **traditional NSBBs or carvedilol + EVL**. (A.1) (Changed)
- **7.9** TIPS is the treatment of choice for rebleeding despite NSBB/carvedilol + EVL. (B.1) (Unchanged)
- **7.13** NSBBs are first-line for preventing recurrent bleeding from portal hypertensive gastropathy (PHG). (A.1) (Unchanged)
- **7.22** **Recompensation** implies at least partial regression of the structural/functional changes of cirrhosis after removal of the aetiology. (A.1) (New)
- **7.23** Clinical **recompensation** (expert consensus) requires ALL of: (a) removal/suppression/cure of the primary aetiology; (b) resolution of ascites (off diuretics), of HE (off lactulose/rifaximin), and absence of recurrent variceal haemorrhage for ≥12 months; (c) stable improvement in liver function (albumin, INR, bilirubin). (C.2) (New)
- **7.24** Because CSPH may persist despite recompensation, **NSBBs should not be discontinued unless CSPH resolves**. (B.1) (New)

### 8) Splanchnic vein thrombosis — Budd-Chiari & non-cirrhotic PVT
- **8.3** In all adults with primary splanchnic vein thrombosis, test for **JAK2 V617F** in peripheral blood. (A.1) (Unchanged)
- **8.4** If JAK2 V617F negative, consider calreticulin and JAK2-exon12 mutations and NGS. (A.1) (Changed)
- **8.5** If no MPN driver mutation, discuss bone marrow biopsy with haematology to rule out MPN, irrespective of blood counts. (B.2) (Changed)
- **8.6–8.8** [[budd-chiari-syndrome|Budd-Chiari syndrome (BCS)]] = obstruction of hepatic venous outflow (small hepatic veins → IVC/right atrium); "BCS" is the preferred designation for any **primary** hepatic venous outflow obstruction; **secondary** = extrinsic compression (e.g. tumour). (A.1 / D.1) (Unchanged/New/Changed)
- **8.11–8.12** Do not biopsy to diagnose BCS when imaging shows outflow obstruction; biopsy only if small-hepatic-vein obstruction is not seen on imaging. (B.1) (Unchanged/Changed)
- **8.13–8.14** Nodules in BCS are frequent and usually benign but HCC can occur → periodic imaging + AFP; a **6-month interval** can be proposed. (B.1 / C.1) (Changed/New)
- **8.18–8.19** Manage BCS by a **stepwise approach** (anticoagulation → angioplasty/stent/thrombectomy/thrombolysis → TIPS → liver transplant) at experienced centres; **long-term anticoagulation for all primary BCS**. (B.1) (Unchanged/Changed)
- **8.20** Avoid unfractionated heparin (HIT risk); reserve for special situations (GFR <30, pending procedures). (D.2) (New)
- **8.24–8.25** The **BCS-TIPS prognostic index score** can predict outcome when TIPS is considered; **liver transplantation should be considered for a high BCS-TIPS PI score (>7)** before TIPS, or for uncontrolled manifestations despite the stepwise approach. (B.1 / C.1) (Changed)
- **8.26** BCS presenting as acute liver failure → urgent liver transplantation; perform emergency TIPS if possible, independent of listing. (C.1) (New)
- **8.27–8.31** Non-cirrhotic **PVT**: thrombus in portal vein trunk/branches; **portal cavernoma** = porto-portal collateral network from prior obstruction; diagnose by Doppler/CT/MR angiography (non-enhancing intraluminal material); use standardised nomenclature (Table 1 below). (D.1 / B.1) (Changed/New)
- **8.35–8.39** Non-cirrhotic **recent PVT**: start therapeutic anticoagulation immediately (LMWH → VKA; DOACs in selected cases without triple-positive antiphospholipid syndrome); anticoagulate **≥6 months**; long-term if permanent prothrombotic state. (B.1 / C.2) (Changed/New)
- **8.44** Risk of intestinal infarction/organ failure ↑ with recent PVT and persistent severe abdominal pain despite anticoagulation, bloody diarrhoea, lactic acidosis, bowel loop distention, or occlusion of 2nd-order SMV radicles → multidisciplinary early intervention. (C.2) (New)
- **8.52** Non-recanalised thrombosis → screen for gastroesophageal varices within 6 months; if none, repeat at 12 months then every 2 years. (B.1) (Unchanged)

### 9) Other vascular liver disorders — PVT in cirrhosis & PSVD
- **9.1–9.2** LMWH/VKA accepted for splanchnic-vein thrombosis; DOACs have no major safety concerns in non-cirrhotic vascular liver disease with preserved liver function (caution if Child-Pugh B / CrCl <30; not recommended in Child-Pugh C outside protocols). (A.1 / C.2) (Unchanged/New)
- **9.3** Screen for PVT in all potential liver transplant candidates, at the time of HCC screening. (D.2) (Changed)
- **9.5** Anticoagulate cirrhosis with **(i)** recent (<6 mo) completely or partially (>50%) occlusive PVT of the trunk ± SMV extension, **(ii)** symptomatic PVT (any extent), or **(iii)** PVT in potential transplant candidates (any degree). (C.2) (New)
- **9.7** Consider anticoagulation for minimally occlusive (<50%) trunk PVT that progresses on short-term (1–3 mo) follow-up or compromises the SMV. (C.2) (New)
- **9.8** Maintain anticoagulation until recanalization or ≥6 months; continue after recanalization if awaiting transplant. (C.1) (New)
- **9.11–9.13** Initiate with LMWH, maintain with LMWH/VKA/DOAC; DOAC caution in Child-Pugh B, not recommended in Child-Pugh C. (C.1 / B.2) (Changed/New)
- **9.14** **PSVD** is a broad clinico-pathological entity (non-cirrhotic portal fibrosis, idiopathic/non-cirrhotic intrahepatic portal hypertension, nodular regenerative hyperplasia, obliterative portal venopathy, hepatoportal sclerosis, incomplete septal cirrhosis). (B.1) (New)
- **9.15** Absence of portal hypertension does not rule out PSVD; PVT and common liver diseases can coexist with PSVD. (B.1) (New)
- **9.16** Suspect PSVD when: signs of PH contrast with atypical cirrhosis features (HVPG <10 mmHg; LSM <10 kPa; smooth surface, no segment-IV atrophy; vein-to-vein communications), or PH/liver-test abnormalities in a condition associated with PSVD, or unexplained liver-test abnormalities without PH signs. (B.1) (New)
- **9.18–9.19** Diagnosis requires an **adequate liver biopsy (≥20 mm)**, exclusion of cirrhosis and other PH causes, plus one of three criteria combining specific PH features and PSVD-specific histology (Table 2). (C.1 / C.2) (New)
- **9.21–9.22** Endoscopic variceal screening required at PSVD diagnosis; the non-invasive Baveno VII varices criteria **cannot** be applied to PSVD. (C.1 / B.1) (New)
- **9.29–9.30** TIPS can treat severe PH complications; liver transplantation is an option in selected PSVD with severe/refractory complications or advanced liver dysfunction. (C.2 / D.2) (New)

### Table 1 — Standardised nomenclature for PVT / portal cavernoma
| Feature | Definition |
|---|---|
| Recent | Thrombus presumed present <6 months |
| Chronic | Thrombus present/persistent >6 months |
| Completely occlusive | No persistent lumen |
| Partially occlusive | Clot obstructing >50% of lumen |
| Minimally occlusive | Clot obstructing <50% of lumen |
| Cavernous transformation | Gross porto-portal collaterals, original PV not seen |
| Progressive / Stable / Regressive | Interval change in size/occlusion |

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **CSPH cut-off numbers** are concordant with AASLD 2023 (both use HVPG ≥10 mmHg and the same LSM/platelet rule-out/rule-in thresholds); no conflict — AASLD 2023 adapted this consensus.
- **Carvedilol dosing** is specified by AASLD 2023 (6.25–12.5 mg/day), not by Baveno VII; the wiki keeps the AASLD dose.
- **PSVD** LSM cut-offs to exclude cirrhosis "cannot be proposed yet" (Baveno VII, 8.34/9.16) — a genuine open question left on the [[porto-sinusoidal-vascular-disorder]] page.
- **BCS-TIPS PI / Rotterdam BCS index component point values** are not enumerated in Baveno VII — the actionable LT threshold (>7) is captured; the component breakdown remains a decision gap needing the primary index publication.
