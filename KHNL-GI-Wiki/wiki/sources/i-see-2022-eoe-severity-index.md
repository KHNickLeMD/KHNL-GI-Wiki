## Summary

The paper that defines the **Index of Severity for Eosinophilic Esophagitis (I-SEE)** — the instrument [[acg-2025-eoe]] tells providers they "could consider" using to assess baseline severity, but whose components and point values that guideline does not print. This is the primary source for the index itself.

The problem the conference set out to solve is that **eosinophil counts are not a severity scale**. Peak eosinophil count is required to *define* active disease (≥15 eosinophils per high-power field, eos/hpf), but counts correlate only modestly with symptoms and do not capture fibrostenosis or complications; a patient with 100 eos/hpf is not necessarily worse off than one with 20. Nor does any single existing instrument combine domains: the EoE Endoscopic Reference Score (EREFS) grades endoscopy, the EoE Histologic Scoring System (EoEHSS) grades histology, and the symptom instruments are age-split and research-oriented. I-SEE's design response is to score across **three domains — symptoms and complications, inflammatory features, and fibrostenotic features** — with endoscopy and histology appearing inside *both* the inflammatory and the fibrostenotic rows rather than as standalone domains. That split is the paper's central conceptual move: it forces simultaneous assessment of what is inflamed and what is remodeled, because those two drive different treatments.

Scoring is deliberately crude and office-usable: each selected feature contributes **1, 2, 4 or 15 points**, summed to a total that bands as **<1 inactive, 1–6 mild, 7–14 moderate, ≥15 severe**. The **15-point boxes are automatic-severe** — a single qualifying complication or an esophagus that will not admit a standard adult endoscope puts the patient in the severe band regardless of everything else.

The authors are explicit that this is a first iteration: **I-SEE is not yet validated**, and Table 3 lists the research agenda, including whether severity categories predict outcomes and whether index use changes practice.

## Key Findings / Claims

### The index (Table 1)

To be assessed at initial diagnosis and then at each visit, with recall only between visits. Select the box the patient fits for each row; **for boxes with more than one element, each selected feature gets points.** Severity depends on an accurate diagnosis, i.e. isolated esophageal eosinophilia ≥15 eos/hpf with other etiologies excluded.

**Total score: <1 inactive EoE · 1–6 mild active EoE · 7–14 moderate active EoE · ≥15 severe active EoE**

| Domain / feature | 1 point | 2 points | 4 points | 15 points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Symptoms** | Weekly | Daily | Multiple times per day **or** disrupting social functioning | — |
| **Complications** | — | Food impaction with emergency-room visit or endoscopy (*patient ≥18 years*) | • Food impaction with emergency-room visit or endoscopy (*patient <18 years*)<br/>• Hospitalization due to EoE | • Esophageal perforation<br/>• Malnutrition with body mass <5th percentile or decreased growth trajectory<br/>• Persistent inflammation requiring elemental formula, or systemic corticosteroid, or immunomodulatory treatments |
| **Inflammatory — endoscopy** (edema, furrows and/or exudates) | Localized | Diffuse | — | — |
| **Inflammatory — histology** | 15–60 eos/hpf | >60 eos/hpf | — | — |
| **Fibrostenotic — endoscopy** (rings, strictures) | Present, but endoscope passes easily | Present, but requires dilation or a snug fit when passing a standard endoscope | — | Cannot pass standard upper endoscope; repeated dilations (*in an adult ≥18 years*); **or any dilation** (*in a child <18 years*) |
| **Fibrostenotic — histology** | — | Basal zone hyperplasia (BZH) **or** lamina propria fibrosis (LPF) (or dyskeratotic epithelial cells [DEC] / surface epithelial alteration [SEA] if no lamina propria) | — | — |

Footnotes that change the scoring:

- **Immunomodulatory** medications include biologics, azathioprine/6-mercaptopurine, or other immune-targeted treatments.
- **The peak eosinophil count must be specifically quantitated** both for diagnosis and to allow monitoring; the table itself uses thresholds and ranges only.
- "Snug fit" means an endoscope of **10–14 mm** in the esophagus. Esophageal diameter can be measured by balloon-sizing, dilation effect, or barium esophagram.
- **After esophageal dilation in any patient, symptoms of dysphagia and food impaction cannot be reliably assessed** — a key reason dilation history, not symptom report, carries the fibrostenotic weight.
- Endoscopic features are best assessed clinically with **EREFS**; the **worst overall finding for the esophagus** should be scored, and if a stricture is identified the diameter should be estimated.
- If lamina propria is absent or unevaluable, **DEC/SEA can be used to predict LPF** — but points for DEC or SEA predict the *presence*, not the severity, of fibrosis.

### Worked examples (Table 2 — the authors' own)

| Case | Features | Arithmetic | Band |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12-year-old, daily abdominal pain, no complications, localized inflammatory endoscopy, 45 eos/hpf, no fibrostenotic features | symptoms 2 + inflammatory endoscopy 1 + inflammatory histology 1 | **4** | Mild active |
| 32-year-old man, daily dysphagia, 2 prior emergency-department visits for food bolus impaction, diffuse exudates and edema, 35 eos/hpf, BZH on biopsy, stricture precluding passage of a standard endoscope | 2 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 15 + 2 | **24** | Severe active |
| 17-year-old, chest pain/heartburn/dysphagia multiple times per day causing eating-related anxiety and social isolation, no complications, diffuse exudates/edema/furrows, 100 eos/hpf, rings but scope passes easily, BZH and LPF | 4 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 2 | **11** | Moderate active |

### Design decisions worth carrying

- **Symptom severity is judged primarily by frequency**; the specific symptoms are not specified and are left to the provider. In children they may include abdominal pain, vomiting, heartburn/reflux, chest pain, unintentional weight loss, feeding difficulty (refusal, failure to progress, trouble swallowing) and failure to thrive; in adolescents and adults, dysphagia, transient food impaction with spontaneous clearance, heartburn and chest pain.
- **Social disruption counts as a 4-point symptom** — missed work or school, fear of eating in public, social isolation.
- **Food impaction is weighted higher in children** (4 points vs 2 in adults) because impactions are less frequent in children and may represent early-onset fibrostenosis.
- **Endoscopic severity does not use EREFS cut-points.** The panel deliberately chose elements that are objective and easy to recognize clinically, because current data do not support prespecified EREFS scores for determining severity and endoscopists do not frequently detect strictures or narrowing on endoscopy.
- **Any dilation requirement in a child or adolescent is interpreted as severe disease**; only *repeated* dilations meet that criterion in an adult.
- Luminal-diameter ranges discussed at the conference but **deliberately not adopted into the table**: normal ≥20 mm, mildly decreased 15–19 mm, moderate 10–14 mm, severe 5–9 mm (failed passage of an adult upper endoscope), critical <5 mm (failed passage of a pediatric upper endoscope). The panel simplified to the three endoscope-passage categories instead.
- **Histologic inflammatory activity is split at 15–60 vs >60 eos/hpf** for this first iteration, reflecting the traditional focus on eosinophil numbers and the fact that >60 eos/hpf garners a maximum score in the EoEHSS.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **No contradiction with [[acg-2025-eoe]]** — ACG 2025 flags I-SEE as something providers *could consider* and calls its role in matching severity to treatment an explicit knowledge gap. This source agrees: it states plainly that **I-SEE has yet to be validated** and that extensive further study is needed to assess longitudinal use, the relation to treatment, and the association of severity categories with outcomes.
- **No treatment is attached to any band.** The index classifies; it does not tell the clinician what to do with a moderate or severe score. The authors name that linkage as a future goal, modelled on the asthma guidelines.
- **Domain weighting is unvalidated.** Whether a 15-point automatic-severe box, or the 1/2/4/15 scale generally, correctly reflects patient outcomes is untested; Table 3 lists mixing of categories (especially mild/moderate and moderate/severe) and whether separate indices are needed for children and adults as open questions.
- **Fibrostenotic grading was acknowledged to need validation** at the time of writing: it does not account for the longitudinal extent or the presence of multiple strictures, and dilation practices vary between physicians.
