## Summary

- Updates the 2006 USMSTF guidance on endoscopic surveillance after curative-intent resection of colorectal cancer (CRC).
- **Population:** resected TNM stage I–III (Dukes A–C) CRC, plus selected resected stage IV.
- **Out of scope:** carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), liver computed tomography (CT), chest radiographs. Studies in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), prior CRC, or hereditary CRC syndromes were excluded from the review.
- **Covers:** perioperative clearing, timing of post-resection [[colonoscopy]], extra local surveillance for rectal cancer, CT colonography (CTC), fecal tests.
- **Core message:** post-resection colonoscopy serves two jobs — clear the colon of synchronous neoplasia, then prevent metachronous CRC. Surveillance colonoscopy is linked to better overall survival but not CRC-specific survival; more intensive colonoscopy schedules have not improved survival and may add harm.

### Grading and format

- Modified Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) system.
- **Evidence quality (Table 1):** A high · B moderate · C low · D very low.
- **Strength:** strong = "we recommend"; weak = "we suggest".
- **Recommendations are not numbered** in the document. There are six boxed statements: five graded, one (fecal tests) ungraded.
- **"High-quality" colonoscopy** throughout = reaches the cecum or anastomosis + adequate bowel preparation + careful examination by an operator who meets adenoma detection benchmarks (conventional adenoma detection in >25% of average-risk screening colonoscopies).

## Key Recommendations

*Unnumbered in the source; labels below are topic names, not source numbers.*

| Topic | Recommendation (condensed; force and qualifiers as given) | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Perioperative clearing | **We recommend** high-quality perioperative clearing colonoscopy for patients with CRC — **preoperatively**, or **within 3–6 months after surgery** if the CRC is **obstructive**. Goals: find synchronous cancer; find and completely resect precancerous polyps. | Strong; low-quality evidence |
| First surveillance colonoscopy | **We recommend** the first surveillance colonoscopy **1 year after surgery** (or 1 year after the perioperative clearing colonoscopy) after curative resection of colon **or** rectal cancer. Rectal cancer has additional recommendations (below). | Strong; low-quality evidence |
| Later intervals | **We recommend** that after the 1-year exam the next is at **3 years** (i.e., 4 years after surgery or perioperative colonoscopy), then **5 years** (i.e., 9 years after), then **every 5 years** until diminishing life expectancy outweighs the benefit. If **neoplastic polyps** are found, use published polyp-surveillance intervals. These intervals **do not apply to Lynch syndrome**. | Strong; low-quality evidence |
| Rectal cancer — local surveillance | Patients at increased risk of local recurrence — localized rectal cancer resected **without total mesorectal excision (TME)**; **transanal local excision** (transanal excision or transanal endoscopic microsurgery); **endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD)**; or **locally advanced** rectal cancer that did **not** get neoadjuvant chemoradiation followed by TME surgery. **We suggest** local surveillance with **flexible sigmoidoscopy or endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) every 3–6 months for the first 2–3 years** after surgery, **in addition to** colonoscopic surveillance for metachronous neoplasia. | Weak; low-quality evidence |
| Obstructive CRC | When obstructive CRC prevents complete colonoscopy, **we recommend CTC** as the best alternative to exclude synchronous neoplasms; **double-contrast barium enema** is acceptable if CTC is unavailable. | Strong; moderate-quality evidence |
| Fecal tests | Evidence is **insufficient to recommend routine** fecal immunochemical test (FIT) or fecal DNA for surveillance after CRC resection. | Ungraded |

## Key Findings / Claims

### Surveillance and survival

- Background: up to 40% with locoregional disease recur; 90% of recurrences within 5 years.
- Observational databases and randomized controlled trial (RCT) meta-analyses: surveillance colonoscopy → lower **overall** mortality, not lower **CRC-specific** mortality.
  - Cochrane review (more intensive follow-up): all-cause 5-year mortality odds ratio (OR) 0.73 (95% confidence interval [CI] 0.59–0.91); recurrence found 6.75 months earlier.
  - Meta-analysis of 11 RCTs: overall mortality hazard ratio (HR) 0.75 (0.66–0.86); more asymptomatic recurrences found (relative risk [RR] 2.59), more curative surgery attempted (RR 1.98); no difference in cancer-specific mortality.
  - Benefits of intensive multimodality follow-up show mainly where frequent CEA was used. Colonoscopy vs none → better overall survival; **colonoscopy frequency** → no survival effect.
- FACS (Follow-Up After Colorectal Surgery) RCT: CT + CEA → more curative-intent resection of recurrence, no survival gain vs minimal follow-up.
- Chinese RCT (n = 326): intensive colonoscopy (every 3 months × 1 y, every 6 months × 2 y, then yearly) vs routine (6, 30, 60 months) → 5-year survival 77% vs 72% (P = 0.25). Intensive arm: 3 complications (2 bleeds needing admission, 1 perforation needing laparotomy); routine arm none.
- Intraluminal/anastomotic recurrence is uncommon (especially proximal to the rectum) and usually comes with unresectable extraluminal disease → intensifying colonoscopy only to find it is unlikely to help.
- Three RCTs were ongoing at publication (Table 2): COLOFOL, GILDA, FFCD PRODIGE 13.

### Perioperative clearing

- Synchronous cancer prevalence in CRC: 0.7% to ~7%.
- Colonoscopy preferably preoperative; may be deferred 3–6 months if incomplete from malignant obstruction (3-month floor allows recovery). Intraoperative colonoscopy proposed, not commonly practiced.
- Netherlands Cancer Registry (n = 5157): 93 metachronous CRCs (1.8%) at 7–356 months; 40.8% within 36 months. Attributed to missed lesions 43%, nonadherence to surveillance 43%, incomplete resection 5.4%, de novo 5.4%.
- Synchronous adenomas/advanced adenomas → higher risk of later metachronous neoplasia.
- After CTC clearing for obstructive CRC, **colonoscopy rather than CTC is prudent for the first postoperative exam** — CTC may miss or not report diminutive and flat lesions.
- [[serrated-polyposis-syndrome|Serrated polyposis syndrome (SPS)]]: described as the most common polyp syndrome; raises CRC risk in right and left colon; usually recognized at the diagnostic colonoscopy or in post-resection surveillance. SPS needs more frequent colonoscopy → consider SPS when serrated lesions are multiple and/or large.

### Metachronous cancer

- About two-thirds of metachronous cancers are asymptomatic, TNM I–II (Dukes A–B), and reoperated with curative intent.
- Cumulative incidence ~0.3–0.35% per year; can appear decades later. All segments at risk; in older survivors risk may stay raised only in the proximal colon.
- Pooled post-resection studies: 253 metachronous cancers in 15,803 patients (1.6%); ~30% within 2 years of resection. Excluding Lynch syndrome: ~33% within 3 years.
- Most early "metachronous" cancers are probably prevalent lesions missed at index diagnosis.
- Supporting data for the 1-year exam: health maintenance organization cohort — 20/652 (3.1%) second primary CRC, 9 within 18 months; Veterans Affairs (VA) Cooperative Study 380 — 4 of 5 within 18 months; Wang RCT — 5 of 9 within 3 years. One registry subgroup (n = 155, mean first post-op colonoscopy 478 ± 283 days) found no metachronous CRC but 3 anastomotic recurrences and 28 adenomas in 24 patients (5 >1 cm).
- Later intervals rest on weaker evidence, borrowed largely from post-polypectomy surveillance of high-risk adenomas.
- Patients with known or suspected [[lynch-syndrome|Lynch syndrome]] (tumor testing, age, family history, tumor features) → genetic counseling and Lynch-specific surveillance. The USMSTF recommends that all CRCs be studied for Lynch syndrome.

### Rectal cancer

- >80% of anastomotic recurrences involved rectal or distal colon cancer.
- TME lowers local recurrence. Transanal excision and transanal endoscopic microsurgery (used for T1 and some T2) recur locally more often than radical surgery. ESD is used in some centers for rectal cancer with superficial submucosal invasion.
- Unclear whether sigmoidoscopy or EUS is better, or which interval is ideal.
- EUS can find extraluminal recurrence before intraluminal signs and allows sampling of nodes or subepithelial lesions; ~10% of rectal recurrences are found by EUS alone.
- No controlled trials show intensive EUS improves rectal cancer survival. Recommendation carried over from 2006. Multidisciplinary management (gastroenterologist, colorectal surgeon, oncologist).

### CT colonography

- Obstructing CRC case series (n = 284): sensitivity 88.6%, negative predictive value 97.4% for synchronous advanced neoplasia proximal to the obstruction.
- Intravenous-contrast CTC can also exclude distant metastases; caution with complete obstruction (perforation risk from gas insufflation).
- UK multicenter RCT (n = 3838, symptomatic): CRC or large polyp detection 7.3% CTC vs 5.6% barium enema (RR 1.31; 95% CI 1.01–1.68; P = 0.039); CTC missed 3 of 45 CRCs, barium enema 12 of 85.
- Postoperative CTC (n = 742): sensitivity 100% for cancer, 81.8% for advanced neoplasia; 11 extracolonic recurrences also found.
- Limits: low sensitivity for flat and diminutive (<5 mm) lesions and nonadenomatous lesions (e.g., sessile serrated polyps); needs expertise to tell postoperative change from recurrence; extraluminal use needs intravenous contrast. Optimal timing in surveillance undefined.

### Fecal tests

- Guaiac tests are inferior to FIT for screening.
- FIT study (n = 1736 with personal/family history of colorectal neoplasia; 24% prior CRC; annual FIT after ≥2 colonoscopies): CRC and advanced adenoma diagnosed a median ~2 years earlier when FIT positive. Unclear whether this held for the prior-CRC subgroup; baseline exam quality unknown.
- Fecal DNA markers clear from stool after resection; role in surveillance not studied.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Adenoma detection benchmark:** this document's "high-quality" exam uses conventional adenoma detection >25% overall. Newer sources set higher, different targets — [[aga-2021-colonoscopy-quality|AGA 2021]] adenoma detection rate (ADR) ≥30%; [[usmstf-2020-followup-colonoscopy|USMSTF 2020]] ≥30% men / ≥20% women. Reconciliation on [[colonoscopy-quality-indicators]].
- **Polyps found during post-CRC surveillance:** this document defers to "published guidelines for polyp surveillance intervals", but the current polyp guideline ([[usmstf-2020-followup-colonoscopy|USMSTF 2020]]) excludes people with a personal history of CRC from its scope. Neither document states the interval for this case.
- **Sigmoidoscopy vs EUS** for rectal local surveillance, and their ideal interval: unresolved in the document.
- **Optimal timing of CTC** in post-resection surveillance: undefined.
