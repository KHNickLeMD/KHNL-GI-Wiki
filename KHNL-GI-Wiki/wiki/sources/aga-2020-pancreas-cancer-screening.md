## Summary

A 13-statement AGA Clinical Practice Update defining **who** to screen for pancreas cancer, **when to start**, **with what**, and **how often**. Explicitly *not* a formal systematic review — it is expert opinion built on reports of pancreas cancer screening studies in high-risk individuals, and it deliberately scopes itself to **primary screening rather than management of abnormal results**. That scope boundary matters: it is why the CPU supplies start ages and intervals in detail but says almost nothing about operative thresholds.

The framing is that pancreas cancer is too rare to screen broadly — age-adjusted incidence 12.9/100,000 p-y against a death rate of 11.0/100,000 p-y, general-population lifetime risk ~1.3% — so the entire program depends on isolating populations whose lifetime risk exceeds the conventional **>5%** high-risk threshold. 85–90% of cases are sporadic; only 5–10% are familial and 3–5% due to inherited genetic syndromes. **BPA 5 is a hard negative: do not screen average-risk individuals.**

Two eligibility routes are given. **Familial pancreas cancer** — a kindred with pancreas cancer in ≥2 first-degree relatives not meeting criteria for another hereditary syndrome — where risk scales with the number of affected relatives (8–12% lifetime risk with 2 FDRs, **40% with ≥3 FDRs**) and with earlier age of onset (kindreds with onset <50 y carry RR 9.3). And **genetic syndromes**, where the CPU is careful to split the list: Peutz–Jeghers, hereditary pancreatitis, and CDKN2A carriers qualify on the mutation alone, whereas Lynch, BRCA1, BRCA2, PALB2, and ATM require **≥1 first-degree relative with pancreas cancer** as well. That conjunction is the operative rule and is easy to lose.

Start ages are **50, or 10 years younger than the earliest familial onset**, dropping to **40** for CDKN2A and PRSS1 hereditary pancreatitis and **35** for Peutz–Jeghers. **MRI and EUS are used in combination.** The surveillance interval is **12 months** absent concerning lesions, compressing to **6–12 months** for low-risk lesions, **3–6 months** for indeterminate lesions, and **3 months** for high-risk lesions when resection is not planned — and **new-onset diabetes in a high-risk individual is itself a trigger** to add diagnostic studies or shorten the interval. Screening stops when the individual is more likely to die of something else or is no longer a resection candidate.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Best Practice Advice — all 13, verbatim

> **BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 1:** Pancreas cancer screening should be considered in patients determined to be at high risk, including first-degree relatives of patients with pancreas cancer with at least 2 affected genetically related relatives.

> **BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 2:** Pancreas cancer screening should be considered in patients with genetic syndromes associated with an increased risk of pancreas cancer, including all patients with Peutz–Jeghers syndrome, hereditary pancreatitis, patients with CDKN2A gene mutation, and patients with 1 or more first-degree relatives with pancreas cancer with Lynch syndrome, and mutations in BRCA1, BRCA2, PALB2, and ATM genes.

> **BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 3:** Genetic testing and counseling should be considered for familial pancreas cancer relatives who are eligible for surveillance. A positive germline mutation is associated with an increased risk of neoplastic progression and may also lead to screening for other relevant associated cancers.

> **BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 4:** Participation in a registry or referral to a pancreas Center of Excellence should be pursued when possible for high-risk patients undergoing pancreas cancer screening.

> **BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 5:** Clinicians should not screen average-risk individuals for pancreas cancer.

> **BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 6:** Pancreas cancer screening in high-risk individuals should begin at age 50 years, or 10 years younger than the initial age of familial onset. Screening should be initiated at age 40 years in CKDN2A and PRSS1 mutation carriers with hereditary pancreatitis and at age 35 years in the setting of Peutz–Jeghers syndrome.

> **BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 7:** Magnetic resonance imaging and endoscopic ultrasonography (EUS) should be used in combination as the preferred screening modalities in individuals undergoing pancreas cancer screening.

> **BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 8:** The target detectable pancreatic neoplasms are resectable stage I pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma and high-risk precursor neoplasms, such as intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasms with high-grade dysplasia and some enlarged pancreatic intraepithelial neoplasias.

> **BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 9:** Screening intervals of 12 months should be considered when there are no concerning pancreas lesions, with shortened intervals and/or the performance of EUS in 6–12 months directed towards lesions determined to be low risk (by a multidisciplinary team). EUS evaluation should be performed within 3–6 months for indeterminate lesions and within 3 months for high-risk lesions, if surgical resection is not planned. New-onset diabetes in a high-risk individual should lead to additional diagnostic studies or change in surveillance interval.

> **BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 10:** Decisions regarding therapy directed towards abnormal findings detected during screening should be made by a dedicated multidisciplinary team together with the high-risk individual and their family.

> **BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 11:** Surgical resection should be performed at high-volume centers.

> **BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 12:** Clinicians should consider discontinuing pancreas cancer screening in high-risk individuals when they are more likely to die of non-pancreas cancer–related causes due to comorbidity and/or are not candidates for pancreas resection.

> **BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 13:** The limitations and potential risks of pancreas cancer screening should be discussed with patients before initiating a screening program.

### Epidemiology and risk magnitude
- 2019 US: ~56,770 diagnosed, 45,750 died. Third-leading cause of cancer death, projected **second by 2030**.
- Age-adjusted incidence **12.9/100,000 p-y**; death rate **11.0/100,000 p-y**.
- 5-year survival by stage: metastatic **2.9%**, regional **12.4%**, localized **37.4%**.
- General-population lifetime risk ~**1.3%**; **>5% lifetime risk** is the threshold used to define high-risk individuals.
- **85–90%** of cases sporadic; **5–10%** familial; **3–5%** inherited genetic syndromes. Genetic basis remains unexplained in ~**90%** of inherited susceptibility.
- **Familial pancreas cancer definition:** kindred with pancreas cancer in **≥2 first-degree relatives** not meeting criteria for another hereditary cancer syndrome.
- Lifetime risk by number of affected FDRs: **2 FDRs → 8–12%** (6.4-fold); **≥3 FDRs → 40%** (32-fold).
- Earlier onset raises risk: kindreds with onset **<50 years → RR 9.3**.
- BRCA2 is the most common identifiable genetic factor — found in **5–17%** of familial pancreas cancer kindreds.
- Deleterious germline mutation predicts neoplastic progression among FPC relatives: **HR 2.85** (95% CI 1.0–8.18) for cumulative incidence of cancer, high-grade dysplasia, or worrisome imaging features.
- Mean age at diagnosis in familial pancreas cancer: **68 years**. Mean age of onset by syndrome — Peutz–Jeghers **40.8 y**, hereditary pancreatitis **54 y**, CDKN2A/FAMMM **59 y**. Lynch lifetime risk **3.7%**; hereditary pancreatitis **40%** lifetime risk related to chronic pancreatitis.
- Lesions increase in individuals **>50 years**; high-grade dysplasia increases **>65 years**.

### Non-genetic risk factors and new-onset diabetes
- Additional risk factors: tobacco use, [[chronic-pancreatitis|chronic pancreatitis]], obesity, diabetes.
- New-onset diabetes typically **precedes cancer diagnosis by 36 months**.
- New-onset diabetes in an individual **>50 years with a history of smoking or weight loss**: estimated **8-fold** increased relative risk, mean age 71 years.
- ⚠ The CPU states additional data are required on how much these factors shift onset in high-risk individuals.

### Yield and benefit
- Meta-analysis, 1,551 familial high-risk individuals across 16 studies: pancreas cancer, PanIN3, or IPMN with high-grade dysplasia detected in **1.82%** (30 subjects). **6%** (93 patients) underwent surgery; surgical pathology showed **low-grade lesions in 68.1%** of resections.
- USPSTF 2019 systematic review, 13 studies / 1,317 high-familial-risk patients (CT, MRI, or EUS; known-genetic-syndrome-only populations excluded): 18 pancreas cancers = **15.6 cases per 1,000 persons**. 57 underwent surgery — 14 pancreas cancer, 38 precursor lesions, 5 neuroendocrine tumors/liver hyperplasia/serous cystadenoma.
- CDKN2A MRI cohort (n=79): 9 invasive cancers over median 4 years; **NNS 11** to detect and treat 1 pancreas cancer.
- Cohort of 354 high-risk individuals: median survival **5.3 y** (IQR 1.2–11.1) in 20 diagnosed during surveillance vs **1.4 y** (IQR 0.4–3.5) in 4 who did not undergo recommended surveillance.
- Screen-detected cancers: 1-year survival **90%**, 5-year **60%**; **90%** resectable when detected during surveillance.

### Modality
- **MRI + EUS (linear array) in combination** — high sensitivity, low risk profile.
- Complementary rather than redundant: **MRI more sensitive for cystic lesions, EUS for solid lesions**; good concordance for lesion size, number, location.
- EUS-FNA allows precise lesion sampling.
- Diagnostic yield ranges are extremely wide: EUS-based **0 (97.5% CI 0.0–16.9) to 68.2 (95% CI 14.3–186.6)** per 1,000 (9 studies, 885 patients); MRI-based **0 (97.5% CI 0.0–16.9) to 75 (95% CI 15.7–203.9)** per 1,000 (8 studies, 842 patients).
- ⚠ **Inability to reliably detect and distinguish PanINs is a stated limitation** of current imaging.
- ERCP has been **supplanted** as a screening modality because of pancreatitis risk.

### Harms
- Perioperative mortality after pancreatectomy for neoplastic disease **3.7–4.6%** historically, more recent estimates closer to **2%**; surgery for screen-detected lesions in 48 high-risk individuals had **no mortality** and morbidity comparable to other indications.
- Total pancreatectomy morbidity/mortality must be weighed against **20%** risk of metachronous pancreas cancer in the remnant pancreas.
- Risk of resections of uncertain benefit — benign/low-risk lesions and already-metastatic disease; current imaging **lacks specificity to distinguish low- from high-grade precursor lesions**.
- Procedural harms: IV contrast, EUS-guided pancreas biopsy, anesthesia, and workup of incidental non-pancreatic findings.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Start-age concordance, with one addition.** BPA 6's ages (50 / 40 for CDKN2A and PRSS1 / 35 for Peutz–Jeghers) agree with [[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening|ASGE 2022]]. This CPU adds the **"or 10 years younger than the initial age of familial onset"** clause, which ASGE's fixed-age table does not carry, and it cites ACG as the origin of that rule. Where a kindred's earliest onset is <60, this clause starts screening earlier than the fixed table would.
- **Family-history requirement — a real divergence from ASGE 2022.** This CPU requires **≥1 first-degree relative with pancreas cancer** for BRCA1, BRCA2, PALB2, ATM, and Lynch. [[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening|ASGE 2022]] screens **all BRCA1/BRCA2 and PALB2 carriers regardless of family history**. ASGE 2022 is the newer guideline and its position is what [[pancreatic-cancer-screening]] asserts; this divergence is surfaced on that page.
- **BRCA1 risk estimate differs between sources.** Here RR 2.26 (95% CI 1.26–4.06); ASGE 2022 gives RR 1.9 / SIR 3.7. Same direction, different magnitude — both below the >5% lifetime-risk threshold the CPU itself names.
- **Management of abnormal findings is explicitly out of scope** ("The focus is on primary screening rather than management of abnormal screening test results"). No operative size threshold appears here — the BD-IPMN ≥2 cm figure on [[pancreatic-cancer-screening]] comes from [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer|ACG 2015]], not this CPU.
- **No formal evidence grading.** Expert review, not a systematic review; BPA statements carry no GRADE strength or certainty ratings.
- **Screening yield ranges are uninterpretably wide** (EUS 0–68.2 per 1,000; MRI 0–75 per 1,000) and the CPU does not reconcile them. It also states that data on the impact of screening programs on pancreas cancer morbidity and mortality are still required — i.e. no mortality-benefit claim is made.
