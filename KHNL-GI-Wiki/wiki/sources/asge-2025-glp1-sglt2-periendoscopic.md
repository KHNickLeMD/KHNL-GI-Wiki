## Summary

American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) position statement, prepared by the Association of Bariatric Endoscopy and the Standards of Practice Committee, on how to manage patients taking **glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs)** and **sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT-2) inhibitors** around endoscopy. Produced by **modified Delphi** over 6 months (October 2023–March 2024) with an 11-member expert panel — 6 gastroenterologists, 2 anesthesiologists, 2 endocrinologists, 1 gastrointestinal (GI) motility expert — across two voting rounds. **Consensus was defined as ≥75% agreement**; the initial survey threshold for overall response was 70%.

The document's organizing idea is that the answer depends on **procedural urgency, not on the drug alone**. It therefore triages into three scenarios, each with its own flowchart: **urgent/emergent procedures in hospitalized patients**, **time-sensitive outpatient procedures** (must be done within 1 week), and **elective procedures** (can be deferred several weeks without affecting outcomes). In the first two, ASGE recommends **against delaying** the procedure; the holding rules bite mainly in the elective setting.

**There is no GRADE assessment and no evidence grading of any kind** — these are consensus statements with the panel's vote distribution reported in the discussion. The panel is explicit about the thin evidence base: larger cohort studies have **not** demonstrated an increased risk of pulmonary aspiration in the periendoscopic period, two large claims-database studies conflict, and gastric-emptying scintigraphy findings correlate poorly with clinical symptoms and are non-reproducible in up to 30% of patients with gastroparesis symptoms.

For **SGLT-2 inhibitors** the concern is entirely different — not aspiration but **euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA)**, precipitated by the fasting and fluid shifts of bowel preparation. **Colonoscopy poses the particular risk** because of the long relative fasting period; upper endoscopy, with its short fast, has not been reported to increase euDKA risk.

## Key Findings / Claims

### The 11 consensus statements (the document's own numbering)

**Good clinical practice standards (all patients on GLP-1 RAs)**

1. ASGE **recommends immediate pre-procedure evaluation for GI symptoms** — severe nausea, vomiting, regurgitation when lying supine, abdominal bloating, abdominal distention, abdominal pain — **suggestive of possible delayed gastric emptying**, for all patients on GLP-1 RAs.
2. ASGE **recommends a detailed discussion regarding possible risk of aspiration** with all patients on GLP-1 RAs undergoing endoscopic evaluation.
3. ASGE **suggests a liquid diet 24 hours before** endoscopic procedure for all patients on GLP-1 RAs.

**Urgent or emergent procedures in hospitalized patients**

4. ASGE **recommends against delaying** required diagnostic or therapeutic urgent or emergent endoscopy for hospitalized patients on GLP-1 RAs.
5. ASGE **suggests obtaining an anesthesia consult** for hospitalized patients on GLP-1 RA medication requiring urgent or emergent endoscopy.
   - **A.** If the patient **has symptoms** suggestive of delayed gastric emptying → use aspiration, **"full stomach" precautions** (general anesthesia with rapid sequence induction and intubation with cuffed endotracheal tube, **or avoid deep sedation**).
   - **B.** If the patient **does not have symptoms** → **point-of-care gastric ultrasound, when available**, may be used to determine the gastric volume.

**Time-sensitive outpatient procedures**

6. ASGE **recommends against delaying** time-sensitive outpatient diagnostic or therapeutic endoscopy for patients on GLP-1 RAs.
7. ASGE **suggests anesthesia consultation** for patients on GLP-1 RAs requiring time-sensitive outpatient endoscopy.
8. ASGE **suggests holding GLP-1 RAs 24 hours before** the procedure for patients **on daily dosing** who require outpatient time-sensitive endoscopy.

**Elective procedures**

9. ASGE **suggests holding GLP-1 RAs before elective** endoscopy as follows:
   - **A.** Daily dosing → hold **at least 24 hours** before the procedure.
   - **B.** Weekly dosing → hold **at least 7 days** before the procedure.
   - **C.** **Moderate sedation or anesthesia-directed sedation** for patients who **have stopped** GLP-1 RAs at the recommended interval **and have no symptoms** suggestive of delayed gastric emptying.
   - **D.** **Anesthesia consultation and multidisciplinary discussion** for patients who **either did not stop** at the recommended interval **or have symptoms** suggestive of delayed gastric emptying despite holding appropriately.
10. ASGE **suggests consideration for bridging the antidiabetic therapy** for patients who will be stopping GLP-1 RAs **1 week before elective endoscopy**. *(The only statement with unanimous agreement — 12/12 strongly agreed.)*

**SGLT-2 inhibitors**

11. ASGE **suggests holding SGLT-2 inhibitors 3 to 4 days before elective endoscopy procedures.** For patients who did **not** hold the medication as recommended, ASGE suggests **either rescheduling the procedure or obtaining a basic metabolic panel before the procedure to evaluate for anion gap acidosis.** *(12/12 agreement.)*

### Supporting detail worth carrying

- **The text and the table disagree on oral semaglutide.** Table 1 gives semaglutide (subcutaneous **and oral**) a **1-week** hold. The discussion under Statement 8 says the panel **suggests holding oral semaglutide a day before the procedure**, reasoning that oral semaglutide is absorbed in the stomach and food impedes absorption, so it is taken fasting. Oral semaglutide was also the reason for the neutral/dissenting votes on Statement 8.
- **Why daily drugs are held only 24 h:** exenatide immediate release, liraglutide, lixisenatide have half-lives of **2.4, 13 and 3 hours** respectively — short enough that a 1-day hold is practical.
- **euDKA risk factors:** fasting, low-carbohydrate diet, intercurrent infection or illness, dehydration, relative insulin deficiency, surgical stress. **Lower body mass index may raise risk.** euDKA presents with **elevated or near-normal blood glucose**, which is what makes it easy to miss.
- **Glycemic consequence of holding a GLP-1 RA (the basis for Statement 10):** GLP-1 RAs are associated with basal insulin reductions of **10%–20%** and prandial insulin requirement reductions of **10%–40%**, with up to **54%** of people able to come off prandial insulin altogether — so a 1-week hold can unmask substantial hyperglycemia.
- **Evidence the panel itself flags as weak:** no larger cohort study has demonstrated increased pulmonary aspiration risk; one claims study found a slight increase (32 events in >30,000 patients), another found no significant difference among >3,500 users and 20,000 nonusers; gastric-emptying scintigraphy correlates poorly with symptoms. A pooled delay in gastric emptying half-time of **36 minutes** was found in a meta-analysis of randomized trials. Retained food >10% at 4 hours rose from **7% to 37%** after 13 weeks of weekly semaglutide in one study.
- **Point-of-care gastric ultrasound:** endorsed as an option where expertise and equipment exist, but the panel states the **evidence supporting routine use remains limited**.
- **Practical constraint the panel names:** full-stomach precautions may not be feasible in **office-based or ambulatory settings**, which may require performing the procedure in a hospital-based setting instead.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Internal inconsistency on oral semaglutide** (above): Table 1 says 1 week, the Statement 8 discussion says a day before. The wiki carries both with the discrepancy named, rather than silently picking one.
- **Against [[aga-2024-glp1-endoscopy|AGA 2024]]:** the AGA clinical practice update advises an **individualized approach, not routine cessation**, and endorses the multisociety position that **no data support stopping GLP-1 RAs before elective endoscopy**. ASGE 2025 does specify routine hold intervals for elective procedures. Both are tier-1; **ASGE 2025 is newer by publication date** and governs the elective-hold question, but the two agree on the points that matter most — symptom assessment drives sedation strategy, and urgent or time-sensitive procedures should **not** be delayed.
- **Liquid diet vs medication hold:** the panel notes future data may show a 24-hour liquid protocol **negates the need to hold GLP-1 RAs** at all, and that whether colonoscopy requires extended bowel preparation in these patients is unresolved.
