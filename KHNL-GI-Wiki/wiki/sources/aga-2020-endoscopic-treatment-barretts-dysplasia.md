# AGA Clinical Practice Update on Endoscopic Treatment of Barrett's Esophagus With Dysplasia and/or Early Cancer: Expert Review (2020)

## Best Practice Advice (Complete, Verbatim — 16 Statements, Ungraded)

*The consolidated 16-statement list as numbered by the document itself (abstract, p. 760).*

1. In BE patients with confirmed low-grade dysplasia, a repeat examination with high-definition white-light endoscopy should be performed within 3–6 months to rule out the presence of a visible lesion, which should prompt endoscopic resection.
2. Both BET and continued surveillance are reasonable options for the management of BE patients with confirmed and persistent low-grade dysplasia.
3. BET is the preferred treatment for BE patients with high-grade dysplasia (HGD).
4. BET should be preferred over esophagectomy for BE patients with intramucosal esophageal adenocarcinoma (T1a).
5. BET is a reasonable alternative to esophagectomy in patients with submucosal esophageal adenocarcinoma (T1b) with low-risk features (<500-μm invasion in the submucosa [sm1], good to moderate differentiation, and no lymphatic invasion) especially in those who are poor surgical candidates.
6. In all patients undergoing BET, mucosal ablation should be applied to 1) all visible esophageal columnar mucosa; 2) 5–10 mm proximal to the squamocolumnar junction and 3) 5–10 mm distal to the gastroesophageal junction, as demarcated by the top of the gastric folds (ie, gastric cardia) using focal ablation in a circumferential fashion.
7. Mucosal ablation therapy should only be performed in the presence of flat BE without signs of inflammation and in the absence of visible abnormalities.
8. BET should be performed by experts in high-volume centers that perform a minimum of 10 new cases annually.
9. BET should be continued until there is an absence of columnar epithelium in the tubular esophagus on high-definition white-light endoscopy and preferably optical chromoendoscopy. In case of complete endoscopic eradication, the neosquamous mucosa and the gastric cardia are sampled by 4-quadrant biopsies.
10. If random biopsies obtained from the neosquamous epithelium demonstrate intestinal metaplasia/dysplasia or subsquamous intestinal metaplasia, a repeat endoscopy should be performed and visible islands or tongues should undergo targeted focal ablation.
11. Intestinal metaplasia of the gastric cardia (without residual columnar epithelium in the tubular esophagus) should not warrant additional ablation therapy.
12. When consenting patients for BET, the most common complication of therapy to be quoted is post-procedural stricture formation, occurring in about 6% of cases. Bleeding and perforation occur at rates <1%.
13. After complete eradication (endoscopic and histologic) of intestinal metaplasia has been achieved with BET, surveillance endoscopy with biopsies should be performed at the following intervals: for baseline diagnosis of HGD/esophageal adenocarcinoma: at 3, 6, and 12 months and annually thereafter; and baseline diagnosis of low-grade dysplasia: at 1 and 3 years.
14. Endoscopic surveillance post therapy should be performed with high-definition white-light endoscopy, including careful inspection of the neosquamous mucosal and retroflexed inspection of the gastric cardia.
15. The approach to recurrent disease is similar to that of the initial therapy; visible recurrent nodular lesions require endoscopic resection, whereas flat areas of columnar mucosa in the tubular esophagus can be treated with mucosal ablation.
16. Patients should be counseled on cancer risk in the absence of BET, as well as after BET, to allow for informed decision-making between the patient and the physician.

## Section-Level Best Practice Advice Blocks (Additional Detail)

The body of the update repeats its advice in per-section **BEST PRACTICE ADVICE** blocks, each numbered independently within its own section. Several of those statements carry operative detail that the consolidated 16-item list above does not contain. They are reproduced verbatim here, labelled by the section they appear under — the document gives them no global numbers.

**Low-Grade Dysplasia (p. 761):**

1. The reading of LGD in BE should be confirmed by an experienced gastrointestinal pathologist.
2. In BE patients with confirmed LGD, a repeat examination within 3–6 months with HD-WLE and preferably optical chromoendoscopy should be performed to rule out the presence of a visible lesion, which should prompt endoscopic resection (see section on HGD).
3. Both BET and continued surveillance are reasonable options for the management of BE patients with confirmed and persistent LGD.

**High-Grade Dysplasia (p. 762):**

1. The reading of HGD in BE should be confirmed by an experienced gastrointestinal pathologist.
2. The diagnosis of flat HGD should prompt a repeat HD-WLE (6–8 weeks) to evaluate for the presence of a visible lesion; these visible lesions should be removed by EMR.
3. BET is the preferred treatment, over esophagectomy, for BE patients with HGD.

**Which Therapy Should Be Applied? (p. 763):**

1. Resection of visible lesions followed by mucosal ablation is recommended for patients undergoing BET.
2. In all patients undergoing BET, mucosal ablation should be applied to a) all visible esophageal columnar mucosa; b) 5–10 mm proximal to the squamocolumnar junction; and c) 5–10 mm distal to the GEJ, as demarcated by the top of the gastric folds (ie, gastric cardia) using a focal device in a circumferential fashion.

**Practical Ground Rules (p. 764):**

1. Mucosal ablation therapy should only be performed in the presence of flat BE without signs of inflammation and in the absence of visible abnormalities. In case of incomplete endoscopic healing, biopsies should preferably be avoided and ablation therapy should be postponed for at least 6 weeks.
2. Patients should use a proton pump inhibitor at bid dosing throughout the treatment phase.

**Potential Harms (p. 765):**

1. When consenting patients for BET, the most common complication of therapy to be quoted is post-procedural stricture formation, occurring in about 6% of cases. Bleeding and perforation occur at rates <1%.
2. EMR of >50% of the circumference of BE is associated with higher rates of stricture and therefore extensive resection of flat BE should be avoided.

**How Should Post-Therapy Endoscopic Surveillance Be Performed? (p. 765–766):**

1. Endoscopic surveillance post therapy should be performed with HD-WLE, including careful inspection of the neosquamous mucosal and retroflexed inspection of the gastric cardia.
2. During surveillance post-therapy, 4-quadrant biopsies should be obtained from the gastric cardia and the esophageal neosquamous mucosa to rule out intestinal metaplasia and dysplasia.

**How Should Post-Therapy Recurrences Be Managed? (p. 766):**

1. BET should be performed by experts in high-volume centers that perform a minimum of 10 new cases annually.
2. The approach to recurrent disease is similar to that of the initial therapy; visible recurrent nodular lesions require endoscopic resection, whereas flat areas of columnar mucosa in the tubular esophagus can be treated with mucosal ablation.

## Who Should Undergo BET — Evidence by Histology

| Histology | Position | Supporting data cited by the update |
|---|---|---|
| Nondysplastic BE | Not addressed — BET not recommended by current guidelines, so the update excludes this group | Paucity of evidence |
| **Confirmed LGD** | BET **and** continued surveillance are both reasonable, once LGD is confirmed **and persistent** | Overall annual progression of *all* LGD to EAC 0.5%; expert confirmation by >1 pathologist raises it — 13.4%/yr confirmed vs 0.49%/yr down-staged (Curvers); 9.1% vs 0.6% (Duits); persistent LGD (present on 2 consecutive endoscopies within 1 year) 7.65%/yr vs 2.32%/yr (Kestens). SURF RCT: absolute risk reduction for HGD/EAC **25%** (*P* < .001) and for EAC alone **7.4%** (*P* = .03); annual progression in the surveillance arm 12.5%. Meta-analysis of 19 studies / 2746 LGD patients: RR of any progression with RFA **0.14** (95% CI 0.04–0.45; *P* = .001) |
| **HGD** | **BET is the preferred treatment**, over esophagectomy | Flat HGD → EAC **5%–8% per year**. Truly flat HGD is uncommon — most HGD patients have a visible lesion on HD-WLE. Overholt RCT: 2-fold higher progression without photodynamic therapy (28% control vs 13% PDT; *P* = .0014). AIM-Dysplasia RCT: 8-fold higher progression without RFA (19% control vs 2.4% ablation; *P* = .04) |
| **T1a (intramucosal) EAC** | **BET preferred over esophagectomy** | T1a is confined to the mucosal layer with a minimal chance of lymph node or distant metastasis. Esophagectomy: >30% morbidity in most series, up to 6% mortality with any esophageal resection (a large recent Netherlands series reported 1.7%). Meta-analysis of 7 retrospective + prospective studies, 870 patients (510 BET, 360 esophagectomy): no significant difference in CE-D (RR 0.96; 95% CI 0.91–1.01); higher dysplasia recurrence with BET (RR 9.50; 95% CI 3.26–27.75); no difference in 1-, 3-, 5-year survival (RR 0.99; 95% CI 0.94–1.03); cancer-related deaths 0.2% vs 0.3% (*P* = .84); significantly fewer adverse events with BET (RR 0.38; 95% CI 0.20–0.73; *P* = .004) |
| **T1b (submucosal) EAC** | BET is a **reasonable alternative** to esophagectomy **only** with low-risk features, especially in poor surgical candidates | Lymph node involvement in submucosal EAC can be **up to 45%** overall. Manner: 19 patients with sm1 (invasion limited to the first third of the submucosal layer) treated by EMR — CE-D 76.9%; during 5-year follow-up metachronous cancers in 5/19 (26%) with resolution in 4/19 (21%); no tumor-related deaths. Manner low-risk sm1 series (n = 66; defined as polypoid or flat with sm1, good to moderate differentiation, no lymphatic invasion): CE-D **87%** (53/61), metachronous neoplasia **19%** (10/53), lymph node metastasis **1.9%** (1/53) over mean follow-up 47 months |

**T1b low-risk criteria (all must hold), verbatim from Best Practice Advice 5:** **<500-μm invasion in the submucosa (sm1)** · **good to moderate differentiation** · **no lymphatic invasion**.

**Multidisciplinary rule, verbatim:** "EAC patients being considered for BET should be discussed in a multidisciplinary tumor board setting (involving a gastroenterologist, oncologist, pathologist, and surgeon), taking into account patient preferences and comorbidities." [[endoscopic-submucosal-dissection|ESD]] "can also be considered in these patients when invasive EAC is suspected, the lesions are large in size and sessile, en bloc resection is required, and local expertise in this technique is available."

## Technique and Ablation Targets

- **Resect first, then ablate.** "Focal complete endoscopic resection of any visible lesion, no matter how subtle, should be performed." If the endoscopist is not trained to perform [[endoscopic-mucosal-resection|EMR]], **referral to an expert is recommended, rather than biopsy.**
- **Focal EMR + ablation beats stepwise radical EMR:** randomized data show radical EMR causes a markedly higher rate of esophageal strictures — **88% vs 14%** in the focal EMR group.
- **Multiband mucosectomy may be preferable** to the EMR cap technique — similar specimens and side-effect profiles, but faster and less expensive.
- **Why resection cannot be skipped:** "endoscopic resection is the only reliable means of distinguishing mucosal from submucosal cancers and to diagnose lymphatic invasion and poorly differentiated cancers. Ablation of misdiagnosed cancers with any of these features is a suboptimal treatment with potentially adverse outcomes."
- **Endpoint of ablation:** CE-IM, not CE-D — endoscopic resection of visible lesions followed by surveillance of the residual flat segment alone yields **14.5%–36.7%** recurrent HGD or adenocarcinoma.
- **[[radiofrequency-ablation|RFA]] is the society-recommended first-line ablation** for flat-type dysplastic BE or BE after resection of visible lesions, on level I evidence of superiority over surveillance. The update explicitly notes that "the most appropriate use of alternative ablation modalities in treatment algorithms remains to be determined" given the absence of head-to-head data.

**RFA device protocol as used in most clinical trials (verbatim detail):**

| Step | Device / setting |
|---|---|
| Initial therapy, BE segments **≥3 cm** | Balloon device (Barrx 360 RFA Balloon Catheter) |
| Residual metaplasia and dysplasia | Focal device (**12 J/cm²**; Barrx 90 RFA Focal Catheter), at **2- to 3-month intervals** |
| Circumferential device algorithm | Single application → debridement of the treated area using lavage and a soft cap to remove debris → second application ("one-clean-one"). Alternative algorithms may lead to higher stricture incidence or lower CE-IM rates |
| Focal device algorithm | 2 applications in rapid succession → debridement → 2 additional applications ("two-clean-two"). A randomized trial showed a simplified **3 applications without cleaning** is noninferior, saves time, and eliminates cleaning of the ablation zone and catheter |

**The hardest area to treat — the GEJ / "neo-z-line":** the area immediately above the upper end of the gastric folds is less effectively ablated by balloon-based RFA because gastric folds and widening of the hiatal hernia reduce mucosal contact with the electrodes; endoscopy is generally not reliable for assessing residual BE there; and it is a common site of neoplastic recurrence. Hence Best Practice Advice 6's requirement to treat this area circumferentially with **focal/targeted** therapy.

## End Points and Practical Ground Rules

- Ablation sessions are scheduled **every 2–3 months** until complete endoscopic eradication of all columnar epithelium in the tubular esophagus is achieved; therapy may extend over a period of **more than 1 year**.
- Adequate assessment of success requires a **completely healed** esophageal mucosa, HD-WLE and/or optical chromoendoscopy, and a **retroflexed inspection** of the gastric cardia.
- Endoscopic assessment of complete eradication is reliable for **columnar islands or tongues extending ≥1 cm proximal to the gastric folds**. For the gastric cardia it is not — poor interobserver agreement, and detailed inspection even with optical chromoendoscopy is unable to detect intestinal metaplasia. **Random biopsies of the cardia are therefore required** to document histologic absence of intestinal metaplasia.
- **Post-eradication biopsy sampling:** most clinical studies have obtained 4-quadrant random biopsies **every 1–2 cm throughout the length of the original Barrett's segment**; the yield is low when the neosquamous epithelium has been carefully inspected. Biopsies should be obtained **only in the absence of erosive esophagitis**.
- **Buried Barrett's:** accidental sampling of small residual columnar islands yields a histologic diagnosis of "buried Barrett's" in **21%** of biopsies, whereas this finding occurs in **0.01%** of biopsies obtained from neosquamous epithelium.
- **Incomplete healing:** postpone treatment for at least **6 weeks** and verify adequate acid-suppressive therapy. **No biopsies** should be taken in this setting — reactive inflammatory change cannot be reliably distinguished from residual dysplasia, and in the absence of endoscopic signs of neoplastic progression the indication for ablation will not be altered by the result.
- **Do not take routine biopsies of flat BE before ablation sessions** — blood may inhibit optimal energy transfer to the tissue.
- **Acid suppression during therapy:** a [[proton-pump-inhibitors|PPI]] at **twice-daily** dosing is used almost uniformly in all studies. European RFA studies have generally added an H₂-receptor antagonist and sucralfate for a short duration after every ablation session; comparative studies on the optimal drug regimen are lacking. By maximizing acid-suppressant therapy before ablation, **there is no need for a baseline 24-hour pH measurement**, although this may be indicated in selected cases (poor squamous regeneration after endoscopic resection, refractory BE, persistent erosive esophagitis).
- **Worst adverse outcome of the treatment period, verbatim:** "failing to recognize and treat an invasive cancer while continuing the ablation sessions. This occurrence may place the patient outside of the window of opportunity for curative endoscopic treatment." Every ablation session therefore starts with careful endoscopic inspection using HD-WLE and preferably optical chromoendoscopy to exclude visible abnormalities requiring resection instead of the scheduled ablation.

## Harms

Meta-analysis of 37 studies, 9200 patients treated with RFA with or without EMR:

| Event | Rate (95% CI) |
|---|---|
| Overall complication rate | **8.8%** (6.5%–11.9%); *P* < .0001 |
| Stricture | **5.6%** (4.2%–7.4%) |
| Bleeding | **1%** (0.8%–1.3%) |
| Perforation | **0.7%** (0.3%–2.1%) |
| Significant post-procedure pain | **3.8%** (1.9%–7.8%) — although most patients noted some post-procedural chest discomfort |

**Complete EMR vs EMR + RFA** (9 studies, n = 774 complete EMR vs 11 studies, n = 751 EMR + RFA):

| Event | Complete EMR | EMR + RFA | OR (95% CI); *P* |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stricture | 33.5% | 10.2% | 4.73 (1.61–13.85); *P* = .005 |
| Bleeding | 7.5% | 1.1% | 6.88 (2.19–21.62); *P* = .001 |
| Perforation | 1.3% | 0.2% | 7.00 (1.56–31.33); *P* = .01 |

Both increasing BE length and prior EMR in RFA-treated patients were associated with a higher adverse event rate.

## Post-Therapy Surveillance and Recurrence

**Surveillance intervals after CE-IM (endoscopic *and* histologic), verbatim (Best Practice Advice 13):**

| Baseline (worst pre-treatment) diagnosis | Surveillance endoscopy with biopsies |
|---|---|
| **HGD / EAC** | **3, 6, and 12 months, and annually thereafter** |
| **LGD** | **1 year and 3 years** |

- These intervals come from models built on the US Radiofrequency Ablation Registry (3105 patients) and the UK National Halo Registry (373 patients), which were associated with identifying surgically unresectable cancers at rates **<1/1000 endoscopies**. The update adds: "Although the model only suggested surveillance at 1 and 3 years post-LGD BET, **it may be reasonable to continue surveillance every 2–3 years after that**."
- **Recurrence is real and late:** in a multicenter study of 594 patients achieving CE-IM, 151 developed recurrent BE over median follow-up 2.8 years; cumulative recurrence risk of any BE within 2 years **19%**, with an additional **49%** risk over the next 8.6 years.
- Rate of recurrence of intestinal metaplasia after initially successful ablative therapy is approximately **8%–10% per patient-year**, may occur more commonly early, and is most often found just proximal to the top of the gastric folds.
- **Where to look:** most recurrences after CE-IM occur at the cardia and can be easily overlooked in the antegrade position — a retroflexed inspection with particular focus on the area within **5–10 mm** above the GEJ is essential. Although the majority of recurrences are detected in the distal 2 cm of the esophagus, the entire neosquamous mucosa should be sampled starting immediately above the GEJ.
- **Recurrence risk factors (multivariate):** baseline dysplasia HR **1.71** (95% CI 1.03–2.84) and long-segment BE HR **1.59** (95% CI 1.01–2.51). **Protective:** BET performed at high-volume facilities (>10 ablation procedures annually) vs low-volume centers (<3 annually), HR **0.19** (95% CI 0.05–0.68).
- **Cardia intestinal metaplasia is not in itself a treatment indication.** It is found in up to **25%** of adult patients without endoscopic evidence of BE when sampled at a single endoscopy with 1–2 biopsies from the cardia. Post-ablation studies find intestinal metaplasia on a per-patient basis in **30%–50%** across multiple follow-up endoscopies with 4 random biopsies per session, but in the majority of cases it is detected at a single occasion and not during further follow-up, and it occurs randomly in time — arguing against residual or recurrent disease.
- **Treatment of recurrence:** in the absence of visible lesions (which require endoscopic resection), any recurrent columnar epithelium in the tubular esophagus can be treated effectively by **any ablation tool** (eg, RFA, [[argon-plasma-coagulation|argon plasma coagulation]], and cryotherapy). Biopsies of flat areas suspicious for recurrent disease will lead to partial removal and may hamper targeted ablation at the subsequent endoscopy.

**Cost-effectiveness and patient-reported outcomes:** "Without exception, all analyses suggest that ablative therapy with RFA is cost-effective for the management of BE with HGD, providing higher quality-adjusted life expectancy than surgery." In most analyses BET is **dominant** — higher quality-adjusted life expectancy at lower cost than surgery. Most studies suggest ablation of BE with LGD is cost-effective compared with endoscopic surveillance; analyses in nondysplastic BE are split. Patients with dysplastic BE undergoing EMR + RFA report decreased worry about EAC or the prospect of esophagectomy; patients with HGD or T1 EAC report better quality of life on the SF-36 and EORTC QLQ-C30 than surgically treated patients, but higher scores on the Worry of Cancer Scale. BE patients tend to **overestimate** their risk of adenocarcinoma and appear accepting of endoscopic intervention even when its effectiveness is much lower than is commonly reported.

## Summary

This update is the AGA's procedural playbook for Barrett's endoscopic therapy (BET) — what to treat, how to treat it, when to stop, and how to follow. It deliberately excludes nondysplastic BE and concentrates on dysplastic BE and T1 cancer. The histologic gradient it sets out is: HGD and T1a EAC → BET is preferred over both surveillance and esophagectomy; confirmed **and persistent** LGD → BET and surveillance are *both* reasonable, a deliberate non-verdict; T1b EAC → BET is a reasonable alternative to esophagectomy only when all three low-risk features hold (sm1 <500 μm, good-to-moderate differentiation, no lymphatic invasion), and especially in poor surgical candidates.

The technical core is *resect, then ablate*. Every visible lesion — "no matter how subtle" — is removed by focal endoscopic resection first, because resection is the only reliable way to separate mucosal from submucosal cancer and to identify lymphatic invasion and poor differentiation; ablating a misdiagnosed cancer is the update's named worst outcome. Radical/complete EMR of the whole segment is rejected on harm grounds (88% vs 14% stricture rate in randomized data). RFA is the ablative modality with level I evidence, and the update specifies the actual device algorithms (circumferential "one-clean-one", focal "two-clean-two" or the noninferior simplified three-application approach, 12 J/cm², 2–3-month intervals).

Its most distinctive contribution is the **anatomic ablation target**: not just the visible Barrett's segment, but 5–10 mm proximal to the squamocolumnar junction *and* 5–10 mm distal to the GEJ (the gastric cardia), treated circumferentially with a focal device. This exists because the GEJ/neo-z-line is simultaneously the area balloon RFA treats worst, the area endoscopy assesses worst, and the area where recurrence most often appears. The same logic drives the post-therapy advice: retroflexed inspection of the cardia, 4-quadrant biopsies of cardia and neosquamous mucosa, and the specific instruction that intestinal metaplasia of the cardia alone — without residual columnar epithelium in the tubular esophagus — does **not** warrant more ablation.

Finally, the update sets operating conditions: BET at high-volume centers doing ≥10 new cases annually; twice-daily PPI throughout the treatment phase; ablation only on flat, healed, non-inflamed mucosa, with therapy postponed at least 6 weeks and biopsies avoided if healing is incomplete; and consent that quotes stricture at about 6% with bleeding and perforation each <1%.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Who gets treated**
- Flat HGD → EAC at **5%–8% per year**; truly flat HGD is uncommon and most HGD patients have a visible lesion on HD-WLE. Ulcerated lesions within HGD raise suspicion for invasive cancer and curative BET is probably not feasible.
- All visible lesions should be endoscopically resected to rule out invasive adenocarcinoma — for diagnostic purposes, not only therapeutic.
- Pooled BET efficacy meta-analysis: **CE-D 93.4%** (95% CI 90.8%–96.1%; I² 46%) and **CE-IM 73.1%** (95% CI 63%–83.1%; I² 93.3%) with RFA; for focal EMR **CE-D 94.9%** (95% CI 92.2%–97.5%; I² 72%) and **CE-IM 79.6%** (95% CI 75.2%–84.1%; I² 52.48%).
- Esophagectomy carries >30% complication rates in most series (anastomotic leak, stricture, pneumonia, prolonged mechanical ventilation, chronic reflux) and up to 6% mortality; a large recent Netherlands series reported 1.7%.

**LGD specifics**
- Overall annual progression of all LGD to EAC: **0.5%** (meta-analysis).
- Expert confirmation stratifies: 13.4%/yr (confirmed) vs 0.49%/yr (down-staged) — Curvers; 9.1% vs 0.6% — Duits.
- **Persistence** (LGD present on 2 consecutive endoscopies within 1 year) → **7.65%/yr vs 2.32%/yr**.
- SURF RCT (136 BE patients with confirmed LGD; biopsy diagnosis confirmed at minimum by an expert pathologist before enrollment): ablation reduced absolute risk of progression to HGD/EAC by **25%** (*P* < .001) and to EAC alone by **7.4%** (*P* = .03); annual progression rate of confirmed LGD in the study was **12.5%**.
- Surveillance remains acceptable for LGD: in SURF, **14%** of patients were excluded because they were up-staged to HGD or cancer upon entry to the trial, with HD-WLE required before enrollment; annual surveillance (target biopsies of visible abnormalities + 4-quadrant biopsies every 1–2 cm) usually detects neoplastic progression at a stage amenable to BET and rarely requires esophagectomy; no patients randomized to surveillance developed unresectable cancer or cancer-related deaths.
- Data suggest an accurate LGD diagnosis "is best achieved by confirmation of LGD by 1 or more pathologists with expertise in gastrointestinal histology."

**Technique numbers**
- Radical (stepwise) EMR vs focal EMR: esophageal stricture **88% vs 14%**.
- EMR of **>50%** of the circumference of BE is associated with higher stricture rates — avoid extensive resection of flat BE.
- Residual flat BE after resection, if only surveyed rather than ablated: recurrent HGD or adenocarcinoma **14.5%–36.7%**.

**End points**
- Endoscopic assessment of CE-IM is reliable for columnar islands/tongues **≥1 cm** proximal to the gastric folds, and unreliable at the gastric cardia.
- Buried Barrett's on biopsy of small residual columnar islands: **21%**; on biopsy of neosquamous epithelium: **0.01%**.

**Post-therapy**
- Post-CE-IM surveillance per baseline grade: HGD/EAC at **3, 6, 12 months then annually**; LGD at **1 and 3 years**, with continuation every 2–3 years thereafter described as reasonable.
- Recurrence of intestinal metaplasia ~**8%–10% per patient-year**; cumulative any-BE recurrence **19% at 2 years** plus a further **49%** over the next 8.6 years.
- High-volume facility (>10 ablations/yr) vs low-volume (<3/yr): recurrence HR **0.19** (95% CI 0.05–0.68).

**Future directions named by the source**
- Improved pathologic standardization for LGD; molecular markers (P53, TP53, aneuploidy) to better predict progression.
- Standardizing the **distal border** of the BE segment up to which mucosal ablation should be applied — trials have variably ablated "the entire BE segment", "ablation therapy directed to their gastroesophageal junction", or "the gastroesophageal junction … ablated circumferentially, irrespective of its endoscopic appearance."
- Whether metaplasia/dysplasia appearing after CE-IM is true recurrence or failure to obliterate — its rapidity "is counterintuitive, given the postulated years of sequential molecular changes required for cancer to develop in BE."
- Whether neosquamous mucosa (CDX2-positive on analysis) is as functional as native squamous epithelium is undetermined.

- **[[radiofrequency-ablation]]** — the actual device algorithms and energy setting (balloon for segments ≥3 cm; focal 12 J/cm²; one-clean-one and two-clean-two; the noninferior simplified 3-application protocol) and why the GEJ is the hardest area to ablate.
- **[[esophageal-adenocarcinoma]]** — the T1a-vs-esophagectomy comparative meta-analysis and the T1b sm1 low-risk criteria with the Manner outcome data.
- **[[proton-pump-inhibitors]]** — bid dosing throughout BET; no baseline 24-hour pH study needed when acid suppression is maximized.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

| Question | AGA 2020 CPU | Other ingested source | Adjudication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Timing of the confirmatory endoscopy after confirmed LGD | **Within 3–6 months** on HD-WLE and preferably optical chromoendoscopy | [[aga-2016-low-grade-dysplasia-barretts\|AGA 2016 CPU]]: **8–12 weeks** under twice-daily PPI. [[aga-2025-barretts-surveillance\|AGA 2025]]: by an expert endoscopist **within 6 months** on high-dose acid suppression | AGA 2025 is the newest tier-1 source and governs the wiki's primary statement (within 6 months); the 2020 window (3–6 months) and the 2016 window (8–12 weeks) sit inside it and are recorded as the earlier positions |
| Confirmatory endoscopy after **flat HGD** | Repeat HD-WLE at **6–8 weeks** to find a visible lesion for EMR | No other ingested source gives an HGD-specific re-look interval | AGA 2020 is the only ingested guidance for this interval — it stands on [[barretts-esophagus]] |
| Post-CE-IM surveillance after baseline LGD | **1 year and 3 years**; continuing every **2–3 years** thereafter described as reasonable | [[aga-2024-barretts-eet\|AGA 2024]]: 1 and 3 years, then revert to NDBE intervals. [[acg-2022-barretts\|ACG 2022]]: every 2 years thereafter. AGA 2016: annually × 2 years then every 3 years | AGA 2024 is the newest and governs; AGA 2020 is concordant on the first two endoscopies and its "every 2–3 years after" is compatible with both later positions |
| Post-CE-IM biopsy protocol | 4-quadrant biopsies **every 1–2 cm throughout the length of the original Barrett's segment**, plus cardia | ACG 2022 / AGA 2025: 4-quadrant from the **high cardia just below the Z line** plus the **distal 2–3 cm** of neosquamous epithelium in separate containers; random biopsies above that range have very low yield | The newer, narrower protocol governs; AGA 2020 itself already notes the yield of the long-segment biopsies "is low when the neosquamous epithelium has been inspected carefully" |
| Which ablation modality | RFA, on level I evidence; alternative modalities' place "remains to be determined" | [[aga-2024-barretts-eet\|AGA 2024]] and [[asge-2018-barretts-eet\|ASGE 2018]]: RFA preferred | Concordant |
| Volume threshold for BET | **≥10 new cases annually per center** | [[barretts-esophagus]] currently carries ≥10 new HGD/early EAC cases **per endoscopist** per year (ESGE threshold); the recurrence data in this update use >10 ablation procedures annually per facility | Both thresholds are reported with their own denominators — they are not interchangeable |

**Open questions raised by the source itself:**
- The optimal distal border of ablation is unstandardized — trials variably ablated the entire BE segment, the GEJ, or the GEJ circumferentially irrespective of appearance.
- Whether post-CE-IM metaplasia/dysplasia is true recurrence or incomplete initial obliteration is unresolved, and the answer could change post-CE-IM surveillance intervals.
- There is no head-to-head comparison of ablation devices, nor of combinations of modalities.
- The optimal peri-ablation drug regimen (PPI alone vs PPI + H₂-receptor antagonist + sucralfate) has not been compared.
