## Summary

- Update of the 2009 ACG [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer]] (CRC) screening guideline; framed around key questions KQ1a–KQ9
- Literature search 1980 to October 2020 (emphasis 2008 onward); observational studies and randomized controlled trials (RCTs) in men and women aged ≥40 y
- Excluded: familial cancer syndromes (hereditary nonpolyposis CRC, polyposis syndromes), human immunodeficiency virus (HIV), post-transplant
- **21 numbered recommendations** (Table 1), each with a strength (strong = "we recommend"; conditional = "we suggest") and a GRADE quality of evidence
- Scope: average-risk screening age (Recs 1–3), modalities and intervals (Recs 4–8), family history (Recs 9–12), [[colonoscopy]] quality (Recs 13–16), aspirin (Recs 17–18), organized screening and adherence (Recs 19–21)
- **Colonoscopy and fecal immunochemical test (FIT) are the primary screening modalities** (Strong / Low); other tests are for those unable or unwilling to have colonoscopy or FIT
- Age 45–49 start is **conditional / very low**; age 50–75 is **strong / moderate**
- No separate key-concept statements; the discussion adds performance data (Table 2) and special considerations (false-positive stool test, risk-score tailoring)

## Key Findings / Claims

*Recommendation grades are in [[#Recommendations]]; this section carries the supporting detail.*

**Screening age (discussion):**

- Age 45 rationale: CRC incidence in people <50 y has doubled; American Cancer Society (ACS) 2018 made a **qualified** recommendation to start at 45; US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) and Multi-Society Task Force (MSTF) recommendations at the time began at 50
- Modeling: starting at 45 instead of 50 → ~25 more life-years gained per 1,000 screened
- Concern: resources for ~21 million more people; raising uptake in ages 50–75 to the 80% target would prevent ~3× as many CRC deaths at ~66% less cost than lowering the start age to 45
- Best modality for ages 45–49 not known
- Beyond 75: decide on health status, screening history, benefits/harms, and patient values — not age alone
  - Polypectomy benefit is delayed 7–10 y → limited benefit if life expectancy <7–10 y
  - Cited USPSTF position: ages 76–85 individualize (most appropriate for the never-screened, those healthy enough for CRC treatment, and those without substantially limited life expectancy); **≥86 y not recommended** (competing causes of mortality)
- Black Americans: CRC incidence 24% (men) and 19% (women) higher than White Americans; outreach needed to reduce disparities

**Modalities (Table 2 — performance characteristics):**

| Test | Performance | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FIT** (cutoff 20 µg/g stool) | Sensitivity 79%, specificity 94% for CRC | Noninvasive; no complications; at home; programmatic screening possible | Positive → colonoscopy; repeat annually; low sensitivity for advanced adenomas; does not detect sessile serrated lesions (SSLs) |
| **Multitarget stool DNA (mtsDNA)** | Sensitivity 92%, specificity 87% for CRC; long-term effect on CRC incidence/mortality unknown | Noninvasive; at home; better than FIT alone for advanced adenomas and large serrated lesions | Positive → colonoscopy; repeat interval unknown (3 y proposed); costlier than FIT; overtesting after positive test + negative colonoscopy |
| **Septin 9** | Sensitivity 48%, specificity 91% for CRC | Minimally invasive; added to routine blood draw | Low CRC sensitivity; repeat interval unknown; positive → colonoscopy |
| **Colonoscopy** | 100% detection rate for CRC; post-colonoscopy CRC (PCCRC) 3%–9%; observational CRC incidence ↓31%–71%, mortality ↓65%–88% | Diagnostic and therapeutic; detects cancer and precursors; interval q10 y | Operator dependent; bowel prep and sedation; complications 4–8 per 10,000 |
| **Flexible sigmoidoscopy** | 90%–100% sensitivity for distal colon CRC; CRC incidence ↓21%, mortality ↓26% | Less invasive; low complication risk | Positive → colonoscopy; repeat every 5–10 y; enema prep |
| **Computed tomography (CT) colonography (CTC)** | 90%–100% for CRC; poor sensitivity for flat lesions and SSLs | No sedation; fewer complications than colonoscopy | Positive → colonoscopy; bowel prep; extracolonic follow-up; limited trained radiologists |
| **Colon capsule (CC)** | Sensitivity 81%, specificity 93% for polyps ≥6 mm | Minimally invasive; no sedation | Bowel prep; positive → colonoscopy; repeat interval unknown |

- **FIT vs guaiac fecal occult blood test (gFOBT):** FIT has largely replaced gFOBT — higher sensitivity for CRC and advanced adenoma with comparable specificity; single sample; no diet or medication restriction; better adherence
- FIT: US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) requires a positive/negative report (no quantitative value reported)
- Annual FIT preferred over biennial (larger effect of annual fecal occult blood test [FOBT] in trials)
- **Positive mtsDNA + negative high-quality colonoscopy → no additional testing** (no upper endoscopy, abdominal CT, or early repeat colonoscopy) unless symptoms or labs indicate; negative mtsDNA → repeat in 3 y per manufacturer
  - High-quality colonoscopy = complete, adequate prep, endoscopist with adequate adenoma detection rate (ADR) → routine follow-up intervals
- Flexible sigmoidoscopy: lower effectiveness in women; for those unwilling to have colonoscopy or FIT
- CTC and CC: options for those unable to have colonoscopy or FIT, if locally available and reimbursed; both need diagnostic colonoscopy if positive
  - SSL detection lower with CTC than colonoscopy (0.8% vs 3.1%); CTC extracolonic findings in 27%–69% of studies
- Septin 9: FDA-approved for average-risk adults ≥50 y who refuse other screening; not an optimal modality
- **Colonoscopy harms:** bleeding 8 per 10,000; perforation 4 per 10,000; higher with polypectomy and older age
- **Intervals:** no RCTs compare intervals; low CRC risk for ≥10 y (up to 20 y) after a negative screening colonoscopy

**Colonoscopy quality (discussion):**

- ADR thresholds as reported by ACG:
  - 2002 MSTF: 25% men, 15% women
  - 2015 joint task force: **25% overall, 30% men, 20% women**, any screening colonoscopy; only conventional adenomas count (SSLs excluded)
  - Recent data: treat 30% men / 20% women as **minimums**; **aspirational 45%–50%** for a mixed-gender population
  - FIT-positive colonoscopies: exclude from routine ADR (also mtsDNA-positive); MSTF minimum in FIT-positive populations **45% men, 35% women**; aspirational may reach ≥75% in males
- ADR <20% → >10-fold higher PCCRC risk (Kaminski); each 1% ADR increase → 3% ↓ CRC incidence and 5% ↓ fatal CRC
- Withdrawal time (WT): average ≥6 min in normal colonoscopy; very-high-level detection tracks with average **9 min**; record WT in every report (time cecum intubated)
- Cecal intubation rate (CIR): passage of the tip into the caput, photographed (appendiceal orifice, ileocecal valve, terminal ileum if intubated); current US recommendations **≥90% overall and ≥95% in screening patients**
- PCCRC ≈ 3%–9% of CRCs; predilection for proximal colon

**Family history (discussion):**

- 3%–10% of US adults have a first-degree relative (FDR) with CRC; average risk ~2-fold
- Take a **3-generation family history**; watch for features of an inherited syndrome (clustering on one side, young age at diagnosis, synchronous/metachronous cancers)
- Risk falls with the screenee's age and with older age of the affected relative; rises with number of affected relatives
- "Advanced polyp" includes advanced adenoma and advanced serrated polyps (SSL ≥10 mm, SSL with dysplasia, traditional serrated adenoma)
- FIT in FDRs of CRC patients (1 RCT): advanced neoplasia 4.2% (FIT) vs 5.6% (colonoscopy), no CRCs missed with FIT

**Aspirin (discussion):**

- USPSTF 2016 review: no CRC effect within 10 y (relative risk [RR] 0.99, 0.85–1.15); 40% reduction at 10–19 y (RR 0.60, 0.47–0.76)
- Bleeding (USPSTF review, 10 primary-prevention trials): +0.29 major gastrointestinal (GI) bleeds and +0.11 hemorrhagic strokes/intracranial bleeds per 1,000 person-years

**Organized screening and adherence (discussion):**

- Organized program = explicit policy (age, method, interval), defined target population, implementation team, decision/care team, quality assurance, method to identify cancers in the population
- Kaiser Permanente Northern California mailed-FIT program: screening rate 38.9% (2000) → 82.7% (2015)
- Aspirational screening target >80%

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **ACS 2018 age-45 start is reported here as *qualified*.** ACG's own age-45 recommendation is conditional / very low; the ACS and USPSTF documents themselves are summarized in [[aga-2023-crc-screening-risk-stratification]] Table 1.
- **CIR target:** Rec 16 grades only **≥95% in screening subjects**; the ≥90% overall figure is cited in the discussion as the current US recommendation, not a graded ACG recommendation.
- **ADR threshold:** Rec 14 uses <25% as the remedial-training trigger, while the discussion favors 30% men / 20% women as minimums; [[aga-2021-colonoscopy-quality]] (same year, same tier) sets ≥30% for both sexes.
- **Aspirin:** Rec 17 rests mainly on the USPSTF 2016 review plus long-term cohort and trial follow-up; [[aga-2021-crc-chemoprevention]] uses an upper age bound (<70 y) with ≥10-y life expectancy rather than ACG's 50–69 y.
- **Septin 9 vs newer blood tests:** Rec 6 predates the cell-free DNA tests covered by [[aga-2025-crc-blood-tests]].
- **Ongoing trials named (results pending at publication):** Colonoscopy vs Fecal Immunochemical Test in Reducing Mortality from Colorectal Cancer trial (1-time colonoscopy vs annual FIT), a Spanish colonoscopy vs biennial FIT trial, Nordic-European Initiative on Colorectal Cancer trial (colonoscopy vs no screening), and Screening of Swedish Colons (colonoscopy or FIT vs no screening).
- **Research gaps named by the guideline:** risk-stratification tools for ages 45–49; accurate blood-based tests; 5-y colonoscopy intervals in people with 1 FDR with CRC <50 y whose own colonoscopies are negative.
- MtsDNA: optimal repeat interval and long-term outcomes not established.
