## Summary

Tier-1 AGA Institute Clinical Practice Update, commissioned and approved by the AGA Institute Clinical Practice Updates Committee and the AGA Governing Board, with internal and external peer review. Its stated purpose was "to rapidly review the emerging evidence and provide timely expert recommendations regarding the management of patients with inflammatory bowel disease during the coronavirus disease 2019 pandemic."

The document answers a set of questions listed in its Table 1 (risk of infection, risk of coronavirus disease 2019 [COVID-19], effect of bowel inflammation, outcomes, whether therapies increase or protect against risk, whether to modify therapy, infusion centres, and management of the exposed / infected / confirmed-COVID-19 patient). Its operative content is a **three-scenario therapy framework** — the patient with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) who is **not infected** with severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2), the patient who is **infected but has no manifestations of COVID-19**, and the patient with **confirmed COVID-19** — rendered as a decision algorithm in Figure 1. Guidance is explicitly built on the contemporaneous British Society of Gastroenterology and International Organization for the Study of Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IOIBD) consensus statements, because no outcome data existed.

Its central clinical position is that patients with IBD are **not** at baseline increased risk of SARS-CoV-2 infection or of developing COVID-19, that remission should be maintained rather than therapy pre-emptively stopped, and that the de-escalation of immunosuppression is graded to the presence and severity of the viral illness, not to its mere possibility.

**Format note — no numbered Best Practice Advice, no evidence grades.** This is an **Expert Commentary**, not a Best Practice Advice CPU. It issues **no numbered Best Practice Advice statements and assigns no GRADE or evidence rating of any kind**. Its only numbered list is a closing set of **six Take-Home Points**, reproduced verbatim below. Do not cite it as graded, and do not assign it recommendation numbers.

**Time-limited content.** Epidemiology, the testing landscape, and the investigational-therapy discussion (tocilizumab, sarilumab, baricitinib, hydroxychloroquine) describe the state of knowledge in early April 2020 and are of historical value only. The therapy-holding framework and the endoscopy/personal-protective-equipment guidance are the parts with durable teaching value.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Take-Home Points (verbatim, as numbered by the document)

1. "COVID-19 is the disease caused by the SARS-CoV-2 virus, but patients with IBD do not appear to be at higher risk for infection with SARS-CoV-2 or development of COVID-19."
2. "Patients with IBD who do not have infection with SARS-CoV-2 should not discontinue their IBD therapies and should continue infusion schedules at appropriate infusion centers."
3. "Patients with IBD who have known SARS-CoV-2 but have not developed COVID-19 should hold thiopurines, methotrexate, and tofacitinib. Dosing of biological therapies should be delayed for 2 weeks of monitoring for symptoms of COVID-19."
4. "Patients with IBD who develop COVID-19 should hold thiopurines, methotrexate, tofacitinib, and biological therapies during the viral illness. These can be restarted after complete symptom resolution or, if available, when follow-up viral testing is negative or serologic tests demonstrate the convalescent stage of illness."
5. "The severity of the COVID-19 and the severity of the IBD should result in careful risk–benefit assessments regarding treatments for COVID-19 and escalating treatments for IBD."
6. "Please submit cases of IBD and confirmed COVID-19 to the SECURE-IBD registry at covidibd.org."

### COVID-19 severity strata used by the Figure 1 algorithm
*These are the decision inputs that select the therapy column; the algorithm's columns are unusable without them.*

| Stratum | Definition (as printed in Figure 1) |
|---|---|
| **Mild COVID-19** | Not hospitalized, **OR** hospitalized with oxygen saturation (SpO₂) > 94% and no evidence of pneumonia |
| **Moderate COVID-19** | Hospitalized patient with hypoxia, radiographic evidence of pneumonia |
| **Severe COVID-19** | Patient requiring mechanical ventilation ± pressors, or evidence of end-organ damage |

Figure 1 footnote — **symptoms and findings of COVID-19** used to trigger testing: fever (83%–99%); cough (59%–82%); fatigue (44%–70%); anorexia (40%–84%); shortness of breath (31%–40%); sputum production (28%–33%); myalgias (11%–35%); headache, confusion, rhinorrhea, sore throat, hemoptysis, vomiting, and diarrhea (<10%); lymphopenia (83%); computed tomography chest showing bilateral, peripheral ground-glass opacities.

### Three-scenario therapy framework

**1 — IBD patient NOT infected with SARS-CoV-2**
- Stay on IBD therapies; goal is **sustaining remission**, "ideally defined as a composite of both symptomatic (clinical) remission and objectively confirmed inflammation control (endoscopic improvement and normalized laboratory values)."
- Maintain current regimens; **avoid relapse due to nonadherence** — relapsing IBD strains available medical resources, may require steroid therapy, or necessitate hospitalization, "outcomes that are all much worse than the known risks of existing IBD therapies."
- Practise strict social distancing, work from home, meticulous hand hygiene, separate from known infected individuals.
- **Continue infusion centre therapy**, provided the centre has a COVID-19 screening protocol. The protocol should include: prescreening of patients for exposure to or symptoms of COVID-19; fever checks at the door; **adequate spacing between chairs (minimum of 6 feet)**; masks and gloves used by providers and provided to patients; adequate deep cleaning after patient departure.
- **Elective switching to injectable therapies is not recommended** — an earlier trial switching patients on infliximab to adalimumab was associated with relapses (SWITCH).
- **Home infusions are not recommended**: many uncontrolled variables, and a serious risk that a nurse-provider travelling from home to home may become infected and act as a vector to other patients.

**2 — IBD patient infected with SARS-CoV-2 but WITHOUT manifestations of COVID-19**
- Move to **lower doses of prednisone (<20 mg/day)** or transition to budesonide when feasible.
- **Hold temporarily:** thiopurines, methotrexate, tofacitinib.
- **Monoclonal antibody therapies (anti-tumour necrosis factor [anti-TNF] therapies, ustekinumab, or vedolizumab) — delay dosing for 2 weeks** while monitoring for development of COVID-19. The CPU notes these agents may continue having systemic or tissue effects despite discontinuation, as the half-lives are not readily acknowledged by the general considerations.
- **Restarting therapy after 2 weeks is reasonable** if the patient has not developed manifestations of COVID-19.
- Stool testing for SARS-CoV-2 is "not likely to be adopted" and its clinical significance in this setting remains to be seen, even though virus persists in stool longer than in nasopharyngeal swabs.

**3 — IBD patient with CONFIRMED COVID-19, with or without bowel inflammation**
- **Considered safe, may be continued:** aminosalicylates, topical rectal therapy, dietary management, antibiotics. Oral budesonide "is likely safe as well and can continue if it is needed for ongoing control of the IBD."
- **Systemic corticosteroids should be avoided and discontinued quickly**, if possible, with appropriate caution for adrenal insufficiency from chronic corticosteroid use.
- **Discontinue during the acute illness:** thiopurines, methotrexate, tofacitinib.
- **Anti-TNF therapies and ustekinumab should also be held** during the viral illness.
- **Vedolizumab** — the IOIBD group "was uncertain whether holding vedolizumab was necessary in this situation, but in a patient whose IBD is stable, holding it during the time of viral illness is appropriate."
- **Resume therapies** after symptom resolution, or when SARS-CoV-2 re-testing is negative, or when entering the convalescent phase of immunity.

### Digestive symptoms in a patient with IBD and COVID-19
- **First, exclude known enteric infections** such as *Clostridioides difficile* or other gastrointestinal pathogens.
- **Second, confirm active inflammation with nonendoscopic approaches** — C-reactive protein, fecal calprotectin, or cross-sectional imaging — "although these tests should be interpreted with caution, as they may be abnormal due to COVID-19."
- If results suggest relapsing IBD, treatment should be based on the activity of the inflammation and the severity of the IBD.

### Hospitalized patient with severe IBD and COVID-19
- Given poor outcomes in severe acute respiratory syndrome and respiratory syncytial virus patients treated with high-dose corticosteroids, and data suggesting cyclosporine and tacrolimus interfere with SARS-CoV replication, the CPU suggests **limiting intravenous steroids to 3 days**, at which point the decision to proceed to a calcineurin inhibitor or infliximab is made.
- **Cytomegalovirus testing may be done as a serum polymerase chain reaction** to avoid the need for colonoscopic procedures, with ganciclovir started if quantitatively suggestive of active infection. Clearance of cytomegalovirus is enhanced when IBD therapy is added to ganciclovir, and thiopurines and cyclosporine may have anti-coronavirus properties.
- If a patient is hospitalized for IBD and also has milder or incidentally identified COVID-19, **address the severe acute issues from the IBD** and apply the standard algorithms for hospitalized IBD care.
- **Surgical consultation is advised as per standard clinical practice.** The desire to minimize surgical intervention during the pandemic puts more emphasis on finding an effective medical "stop-gap," but some patients will still require surgery.
- For **mildly active disease**, use the safer therapies above. For **moderate to severe disease**, holding therapies may not be safe or practicable; the risks and benefits of escalating IBD therapy must be weighed against the severity of the COVID-19. For an outpatient with mild COVID-19 symptoms, IOIBD supports using any of the usual treatments that would be considered pre-COVID-19.

### Endoscopy during the pandemic
- Per multi-society recommendations, **only urgent and emergent endoscopic procedures should be performed** — those situations "where [the] endoscopic procedure will urgently change management."
- Scenarios in IBD that might prompt endoscopy: the need to obtain **biopsies to diagnose new severe IBD**; to **exclude cytomegalovirus if noninvasive tests are equivocal**; or in patients with severe disease or suspected cancer where mucosal inspection might direct surgical intervention.
- **Personal protective equipment:** the AGA Institute recommends **N95 (or N99 or powered air-purifying) respirators instead of surgical masks, and double-gloving**, for health care workers performing **both upper and lower gastrointestinal endoscopies, regardless of COVID-19 status**.

### Risk, outcomes, and disease-course claims
- Patients with IBD "do not appear to have a baseline increased risk of infection with SARS-CoV-2 or development of COVID-19," despite immunosuppressive therapy and the need to attend infusion centres and endoscopy units.
- It is unclear whether inflammation of the bowel per se is a risk for infection with SARS-CoV-2, "but it is sensible that patients with IBD should maintain remission in order to reduce the risk of relapse and need for more intense medical therapy or hospitalization."
- **SECURE-IBD registry** (covidibd.org): of **164 patients** reported at time of writing, patients with severe IBD (by Physician's Global Assessment) and COVID-19 were **more likely to be hospitalized** related to their IBD or COVID-19 or both. The authors describe this as too early for definitive conclusions.
- **Wuhan IBD Center experience: 318 patients.** The health care team instituted immediate alerts to stay at home and practise strict social distancing; despite being at the epicentre of the outbreak, **none of their patients subsequently developed COVID-19**.
- Gastrointestinal symptoms in COVID-19 generally: diarrhea (patient-defined) in **10.1%** of hospitalized patients with COVID-19 in Wuhan (**16.7%** of those in the intensive care unit); another study found roughly half of patients had digestive symptoms at presentation with pneumonia, only about one-third diarrhea; Zhejiang Province cohort had a much lower incidence of gastrointestinal symptoms (**11.45%**).
- Mechanism: SARS-CoV-2 enters cells via the angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 receptor, primed by transmembrane protease serine 2. Angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 receptors appear most expressed in intestine but are found in lung, tongue, and pancreas. Virus is detectable in stool, so fecal transmission has been postulated but **not confirmed**; one report confirmed viral particles in stool but at quantities that were not infectious.

### Investigational COVID-19 therapies discussed (historical, April 2020)
- Tocilizumab (interleukin-6 blocker) — had positive phase 2 data in Crohn's disease and was being studied in COVID-19; sarilumab likewise anti-interleukin. Baricitinib (but not tofacitinib), a Janus kinase inhibitor, may interfere with viral entry by inhibiting AP2-associated protein kinase-1–mediated endocytosis. Hydroxychloroquine had been used for IBD in uncontrolled fashion. The CPU directs readers to clinicaltrials.gov for updates rather than endorsing any of these.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **No contradictions with existing wiki content.** No other ingested source addresses IBD management during a pandemic or the handling of IBD immunosuppression during acute viral infection.
- **Superseded in its epidemiology and investigational-therapy content.** Written with data current to early April 2020 (164 SECURE-IBD cases; no vaccines; tocilizumab, hydroxychloroquine, baricitinib all speculative). Every prevalence figure, outcome estimate, and candidate-therapy statement is of historical interest only. The therapy-holding framework and endoscopy guidance are what carry forward.
- **Ungraded throughout.** No numbered Best Practice Advice and no GRADE ratings — weight accordingly against any graded source. Much of the therapy guidance is explicitly imported from the British Society of Gastroenterology and IOIBD consensus statements, neither of which is in the wiki corpus.
- **Open questions the CPU names explicitly:** whether bowel inflammation itself alters susceptibility to SARS-CoV-2; whether holding vedolizumab is necessary at all (IOIBD was uncertain); the clinical significance of persistent SARS-CoV-2 in stool; whether serologic testing can define the phase of infection well enough to time therapy restart; whether anti-TNF therapy might actually reduce progression to acute respiratory distress syndrome.
- The CPU gives **no threshold for "complete symptom resolution"** and no defined interval for repeat viral testing before restarting therapy; the restart trigger is left to clinical judgement.
