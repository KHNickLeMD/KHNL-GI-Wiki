## Summary

This practice guidance updates the 2023 AASLD Practice Guidance on the clinical assessment and management of nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), now called metabolic dysfunction–associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD). The impetus for the update was the August 2025 accelerated FDA approval of semaglutide (Wegovy formulation, 2.4 mg/week subcutaneous injection) for the treatment of MASH with moderate-to-advanced fibrosis (stages F2–F3), based on interim results from the phase 3 ESSENCE trial. At 72 weeks, semaglutide achieved both co-primary histologic endpoints: MASH resolution without worsening of fibrosis (62.9% vs. 34.3% placebo, p<0.001), and ≥1 stage reduction in liver fibrosis without worsening of MASH (36.8% vs. 22.4% placebo, p<0.001). Final approval awaits long-term outcome data expected in 2028 or thereafter.

The guidance addresses practical patient selection using non-invasive tests (NITs) rather than liver biopsy, which is deemed impractical for most patients. The recommended approach is to use NITs — including imaging-based tests (VCTE with LSM 8–15 kPa, or MRE with LSM 3.1–4.4 kPa) and blood-based tests (ELF 9.2–10.5) — as first-line tools to identify candidates with MASH and stages 2–3 fibrosis. A sequential strategy beginning with FIB-4 calculation is endorsed, with elastography or blood-based NITs as second-line confirmation. Liver biopsy within the prior 6–12 months may be used to determine disease stage but is not routinely required for semaglutide candidacy.

Monitoring and safety considerations are detailed, emphasizing that hepatic panels are only needed as clinically indicated given semaglutide's favorable hepatic safety profile in ESSENCE (no discontinuations due to liver enzyme elevations). The most common adverse events are gastrointestinal (nausea, diarrhea, constipation, vomiting), generally mild and transient. Rare but serious risks include acute kidney injury (from dehydration), symptomatic gallbladder disease, pancreatitis, thyroid C-cell tumors, retinopathy progression (in T2DM), and lean mass loss. Semaglutide is contraindicated in MASH cirrhosis and in patients with a personal or family history of MTC or MEN2.

Treatment response is assessed by the same NITs used for candidacy selection at 72 weeks. Clinically meaningful improvement is suggested by VCTE LSM decrease ≥30%, MRE LSM decrease ≥20%, ALT decrease ≥17 U/L or ≥20%, or ELF decrease ≥0.5. Non-response is suggested if ALT or NITs worsen. The guidance also discusses semaglutide's broader cardiometabolic benefits (CV risk reduction from SELECT and SUSTAIN-6 trials, CKD progression reduction from FLOW trial) and its mechanism as a GLP-1 receptor agonist mediating hepatoprotection indirectly through weight loss, glycemic control, and reduction in systemic inflammation.

## Key Findings / Claims

- **FDA approval:** Semaglutide (Wegovy, 2.4 mg/week SC) received accelerated FDA approval in August 2025 for MASH with moderate-to-advanced fibrosis (F2–F3)
- **ESSENCE trial efficacy (72 weeks, n=800 interim):**
  - MASH resolution without fibrosis worsening: 62.9% vs. 34.3% placebo (p<0.001)
  - ≥1 stage fibrosis reduction without MASH worsening: 36.8% vs. 22.4% placebo (p<0.001)
- **Liver biopsy not required** for semaglutide candidacy; NITs are preferred for patient selection
- **NIT candidacy thresholds:**
  - VCTE LSM 8–15 kPa (recommended); 15–20 kPa (may be used with individualized decision)
  - MRE LSM 3.1–4.4 kPa (recommended); 4.4–5.0 kPa (may be used with exclusion of cirrhosis)
  - ELF 9.2–10.5 (recommended); 10.5–11.3 (may be used with exclusion of cirrhosis)
  - FIB-4 <1.3 rules out advanced fibrosis (F3+) in 99% of primary care patients; do NOT use alone as a rule-in test for semaglutide eligibility
- **Cirrhosis exclusion:** VCTE >20 kPa, MRE >5.0 kPa, or ELF >11.3 with/or portal hypertension evidence → semaglutide not approved for this indication
- **Alcohol:** Mild use (up to 20/30 g/day in women/men) was permitted in ESSENCE; higher use requires individualized decision and PEth/AUDIT-C assessment
- **Treatment response (beneficial, at 72 weeks):** VCTE LSM decrease ≥30%; MRE LSM decrease ≥20%; ALT decrease ≥17 U/L or ≥20%; ELF decrease ≥0.5
- **Non-response:** NITs suggesting worsening or progression to cirrhosis → stop semaglutide
- **Sub-threshold improvement without cirrhosis progression:** Benefit uncertain → re-evaluate strategy (consider re-optimizing lifestyle, other therapy with/without stopping semaglutide)
- **Hepatic safety:** Favorable in ESSENCE — no discontinuations due to elevated liver enzymes; routine LFT monitoring only as clinically indicated
- **Key GI adverse events:** Nausea 36.2% vs. 13.2%; diarrhea 26.9% vs. 12.2%; constipation 22.2% vs. 8.4%; vomiting 18.6% vs. 5.6%; decreased appetite 14.0% vs. 2.8%
- **Gallbladder disease:** Meta-analysis of 103,371 patients shows 37% increased risk with GLP-1 RAs; cholelithiasis 1.6% vs. 0.7% in ESSENCE
- **Lean mass loss:** ~13% of total weight loss is lean mass; ~39% of weight lost is lean mass; appendicular skeletal muscle declining 9–10% over 2 years; monitor with protein intake 1.2–1.5 g/kg/day and resistance training
- **Resmetirom combination:** Combined use at semaglutide 2.4 mg/week has not been studied; combination was not excluded from MAESTRO-NASH; no notable safety signals in subgroup data
- **Lifestyle modification** remains cornerstone of MASLD/MASH management alongside semaglutide
- **CV benefit:** SELECT trial (n>17,000) showed 1.5% absolute risk reduction in composite CV outcomes over 40 months in non-diabetic individuals with obesity/overweight and established CV disease
- **CKD benefit:** FLOW trial (n=3533) — semaglutide 1.0 mg weekly showed 24% relative risk reduction in kidney failure, CV death, and other renal events vs. placebo in T2DM+CKD; trial stopped early for efficacy
- **MASH weight loss targets:** ≥5% to reduce steatosis; 7–10% to target inflammation; ≥10% to reverse fibrosis

## Recommendations

| Recommendation | Strength | Evidence Quality |
|---|---|---|
| Candidates for semaglutide should have MASH with stages 2–3 fibrosis, identified using NITs such as VCTE (8–15 kPa), MRE (3.1–4.4 kPa), or ELF (9.2–10.5), rather than liver biopsy, which is impractical and unnecessary for most patients | Practice Guidance | Based on FDA approval criteria and ESSENCE trial data |

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Conflict with existing nafld-masld.md stub:** The current disease script states semaglutide is "emerging data, not yet FDA-approved specifically for MASH" — this is now outdated. The August 2025 accelerated FDA approval of Wegovy for MASH F2–F3 supersedes this. Updated accordingly in the disease script.
- **Resmetirom + semaglutide combination:** Whether these two FDA-approved MASH agents can or should be combined remains unstudied. MAESTRO-NASH subgroup data shows similar rates of MASH resolution when GLP-1 RAs were taken alongside resmetirom at baseline, but formal combination therapy has not been evaluated.
- **Long-term outcomes:** FDA approval is accelerated; full approval awaits the complete ESSENCE trial results expected in 2028 or later. Long-term durability, discontinuation effects (STEP 1 extension: reversal of cardiometabolic improvements on withdrawal), and survival benefit remain to be proven in MASH.
- **NIT correlation with histology:** No individual NIT reliably predicts histologic response at the patient level. The thresholds for treatment response (VCTE ≥30%, ELF ≥0.5, etc.) are based on aggregate ESSENCE data, not validated at individual level.
- **MASH cirrhosis:** Semaglutide is not approved for cirrhosis. Phase 2 data (Cusi 2023) in compensated cirrhosis did not identify liver safety concerns, but this is insufficient for recommendation outside of the approved indication.
- **Lean mass loss concern:** The 13% reduction in lean mass (~39% of total weight lost) is clinically important especially for older/sarcopenic patients with chronic liver disease, where sarcopenia carries prognostic significance. Optimal mitigation strategies are based on expert consensus, not RCT data in MASH.
- **Alcohol use:** NIT cutpoints used for semaglutide candidacy may differ in patients with higher alcohol use (MetALD range), and this must be considered until additional data are available.
