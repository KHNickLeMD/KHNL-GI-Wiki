## Summary

This practice guidance updates the 2023 American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD) Practice Guidance on the clinical assessment and management of [[nafld-masld|nonalcoholic fatty liver disease]] (NAFLD), now called metabolic dysfunction–associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD). The impetus for the update was the August 2025 accelerated FDA approval of [[semaglutide]] (Wegovy formulation, 2.4 mg/week subcutaneous injection) for the treatment of metabolic dysfunction–associated steatohepatitis (MASH) with moderate-to-advanced fibrosis (stages F2–F3), based on interim results from the phase 3 ESSENCE trial. At 72 weeks, semaglutide achieved both co-primary histologic endpoints: MASH resolution without worsening of fibrosis (62.9% vs. 34.3% placebo, p<0.001), and ≥1 stage reduction in liver fibrosis without worsening of MASH (36.8% vs. 22.4% placebo, p<0.001). Final approval awaits long-term outcome data expected in 2028 or thereafter.

The guidance addresses practical patient selection using non-invasive tests (NITs) rather than liver biopsy, which is deemed impractical for most patients. The recommended approach is to use NITs — including imaging-based tests (vibration-controlled transient elastography [VCTE] with liver stiffness measurement [LSM] 8–15 kPa, or magnetic resonance elastography [MRE] with LSM 3.1–4.4 kPa) and blood-based tests (Enhanced Liver Fibrosis [ELF] 9.2–10.5) — as first-line tools to identify candidates with MASH and stages 2–3 fibrosis. A sequential strategy beginning with Fibrosis-4 Index (FIB-4) calculation is endorsed, with elastography or blood-based NITs as second-line confirmation. Liver biopsy within the prior 6–12 months may be used to determine disease stage but is not routinely required for semaglutide candidacy.

Monitoring and safety considerations are detailed, emphasizing that hepatic panels are only needed as clinically indicated given semaglutide's favorable hepatic safety profile in ESSENCE (no discontinuations due to liver enzyme elevations). The most common adverse events are gastrointestinal (nausea, diarrhea, constipation, vomiting), generally mild and transient. Rare but serious risks include acute kidney injury (from dehydration), symptomatic gallbladder disease, pancreatitis, thyroid C-cell tumors, retinopathy progression (in type 2 diabetes mellitus [T2DM]), and lean mass loss. Semaglutide is not approved for MASH cirrhosis and should not be prescribed solely for that indication; patients with compensated cirrhosis receiving it for another FDA-approved indication should be monitored carefully. Figure 1 lists it as not recommended with cirrhosis, concomitant active liver disease, pregnancy, or a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2 (MEN2).

Treatment response is assessed by the same NITs used for candidacy selection at 72 weeks. Clinically meaningful improvement is suggested by VCTE LSM decrease ≥30%, MRE LSM decrease ≥20%, alanine aminotransferase (ALT) decrease ≥17 U/L or ≥20%, or ELF decrease ≥0.5 (Figure 2 adds MRI-PDFF decrease ≥30%). Non-response is suggested if ALT or NITs worsen. The guidance also discusses semaglutide's broader cardiometabolic benefits (cardiovascular [CV] risk reduction from SELECT and SUSTAIN-6 trials, chronic kidney disease [CKD] progression reduction from FLOW trial) and its mechanism as a glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist mediating hepatoprotection indirectly through weight loss, glycemic control, and reduction in systemic inflammation.

## Key Findings / Claims

- **FDA approval:** Semaglutide (Wegovy, 2.4 mg/week subcutaneous) received accelerated FDA approval in August 2025 for MASH with moderate-to-advanced fibrosis (F2–F3)
- **ESSENCE trial efficacy (72 weeks, n=800 interim):**
  - MASH resolution without fibrosis worsening: 62.9% vs. 34.3% placebo (p<0.001)
  - ≥1 stage fibrosis reduction without MASH worsening: 36.8% vs. 22.4% placebo (p<0.001)
- **Liver biopsy not required** for semaglutide candidacy; NITs are preferred for patient selection
- **NIT candidacy thresholds:**
  - VCTE LSM 8–15 kPa (recommended); 15–20 kPa (individualized decision — this band was present in **up to 19% of ESSENCE participants**)
  - MRE LSM 3.1–4.4 kPa (recommended); **4.4–5.0 kPa** per the abstract / **4.5–5.0 kPa** per the body text (individualized, with cirrhosis and portal hypertension excluded)
  - ELF 9.2–10.5 (recommended); 10.5–11.3 (individualized, with cirrhosis and portal hypertension excluded)
  - FIB-4 <1.3 excludes advanced fibrosis (F3 or greater) in 99% of all patients and 95% of patients with multiple cardiometabolic risks in the primary care setting; FIB-4 ≥1.3 has a weaker positive predictive value; FIB-4 should not be used as a single rule-in or rule-out test for semaglutide eligibility
- **Cirrhosis exclusion:** VCTE >20 kPa, MRE >5.0 kPa, ELF >11.3, and/or evidence of portal hypertension → semaglutide not approved for this indication
- **Not recommended (Figure 1):** cirrhosis (LSM via VCTE >20 kPa, MRE >5 kPa, or ELF score >11.3); concomitant active liver disease; pregnancy, personal or family history of MTC, or MEN2
- **Alcohol:** Mild use (up to 20/30 g/day in women/men) was permitted in ESSENCE; moderate use requires an individualized decision; higher (MetALD-range) use was excluded. Assess alcohol in everyone with both Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test–Consumption (AUDIT-C) and phosphatidylethanol (PEth) testing
- **Treatment response (beneficial, at 72 weeks):** VCTE LSM decrease ≥30%; MRE LSM decrease ≥20%; ALT decrease ≥17 U/L or ≥20%; ELF decrease ≥0.5; MRI-PDFF decrease ≥30% (Figure 2)
- **Non-response (futility):** ESSENCE gives no data to identify non-responders at 72 weeks; the document recommends previously recommended criteria — lack of decline in ALT or liver stiffness on VCTE, or worsening NIT parameters (p.1335, Figure 2); NITs suggesting worsening or progression to cirrhosis → stop semaglutide
- **Sub-threshold improvement without cirrhosis progression:** Benefit uncertain → re-evaluate strategy (consider re-optimizing lifestyle, other therapy with/without stopping semaglutide)
- **Hepatic safety:** Favorable in ESSENCE — no discontinuations due to elevated liver enzymes; routine liver function test (LFT) monitoring only as clinically indicated
- **Key gastrointestinal (GI) adverse events:** Nausea 36.2% vs. 13.2%; diarrhea 26.9% vs. 12.2%; constipation 22.2% vs. 8.4%; vomiting 18.6% vs. 5.6%; decreased appetite 14.0% vs. 2.8%
- **Gallbladder disease:** Meta-analysis of 103,371 patients shows 37% increased risk with GLP-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs); per the Wegovy Prescribing Information as cited by the document, cholelithiasis 1.6% vs 0.7% and cholecystitis 0.6% vs 0.2% (semaglutide vs placebo); history of gallbladder disease is not an absolute contraindication, but caution is warranted and patients should be educated on biliary symptoms (p.1332)
- **Persistent or severe GI symptoms:** may require temporary dose reduction, slower titration, or supportive medications such as antiemetics, prokinetics, or antidiarrheals (p.1332)
- **Lean mass loss (two distinct denominators — do not conflate):** in a dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA) subgroup of 140 subjects, semaglutide recipients lost **6.92 kg of lean mass vs 1.48 kg** on placebo, an **~13% reduction in lean mass relative to baseline**; separately, lean mass loss constitutes **~39% of total weight reduction**. Appendicular skeletal muscle volume declines **9–10% over 2 years**, most pronounced in older adults and those with baseline sarcopenia. Mitigate with protein intake 1.2–1.5 g/kg/day and resistance training; sarcopenia carries prognostic weight in chronic liver disease
- **[[resmetirom|Resmetirom]] combination:** minimal information on combined use, and none at semaglutide 2.4 mg/week; MAESTRO-NASH excluded patients who **initiated or dose-modified** a GLP-1 RA within 6 months of randomization, while 14% were on a stable GLP-1 RA (maximum 1 mg/week) for >6 months at baseline biopsy and showed similar MASH resolution and fibrosis improvement, with no notable differences in safety or tolerability
- **Lifestyle modification** remains cornerstone of MASLD/MASH management alongside semaglutide
- **CV benefit:** SELECT trial (n>17,000) showed 1.5% absolute risk reduction in composite CV outcomes over 40 months in non-diabetic individuals with obesity/overweight and established CV disease
- **CKD benefit:** FLOW trial (n=3533) — semaglutide 1.0 mg weekly showed 24% relative risk reduction in kidney failure, CV death, and other renal events vs. placebo in T2DM+CKD; trial stopped early for efficacy
- **MASH weight loss targets:** ≥5% to reduce steatosis; 7–10% to target inflammation; ≥10% to reverse fibrosis

### Key safety considerations (Table 3, selected rows)

| Safety concern | Monitoring / risk factors | Clinical actions |
|---|---|---|
| Acute kidney injury (AKI) | Serum creatinine and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) at baseline, during initiation and titration; higher risk with vomiting, diarrhea or dehydration | Ensure adequate hydration; hold or reduce dose during significant GI intolerance; reassess renal function until recovery |
| Acute pancreatitis | Severe, persistent abdominal pain (± vomiting); amylase/lipase if symptomatic | Discontinue immediately if suspected; avoid rechallenge after a confirmed episode. Text (p.1332): should not be prescribed with a history of pancreatitis |
| Gallbladder disease | Right upper quadrant pain or biliary colic; ultrasound if symptomatic | Educate on biliary symptoms; caution with history of gallbladder disease (not an absolute contraindication, p.1332) |
| Thyroid C-cell tumors (MTC risk) | Review history of MTC or MEN2; neck mass, hoarseness, dysphagia | Contraindicated in MTC/MEN2; educate on symptoms; routine calcitonin testing not required |
| Hypoglycemia (with insulin/secretagogues) | Glucose in patients on insulin or sulfonylureas | Adjust insulin/sulfonylurea dose; educate on recognition and management |
| Heart rate increase | Pulse periodically; ask about palpitations or tachycardia | Caution in arrhythmia-prone patients; reassess if persistent tachycardia |
| Diabetic retinopathy and ocular complications | In T2DM: baseline ophthalmology evaluation if not recently done, then periodic; blurred vision or sudden changes | Avoid rapid glucose lowering; refer for any new visual symptoms |

## Recommendations

The document neither numbers nor grades its statements (no strength or evidence-quality rating); the list below is unnumbered and ungraded.

| Statement |
|---|
| Candidates for semaglutide should have MASH with stages 2–3 fibrosis, identified using NITs such as VCTE (8–15 kPa), MRE (3.1–4.4 kPa), or ELF (9.2–10.5), rather than liver biopsy, which is impractical and unnecessary for most patients |
| In patients with VCTE 15–20 kPa, MRE 4.4–5 kPa, or ELF 10.5–11.3, an individualized decision to treat should be based on exclusion of cirrhosis with another confirmatory NIT, or cross-sectional imaging excluding nodular-appearing liver contour and signs of portal hypertension, or a platelet count <150,000/mm³ |
| FIB-4 should not be used as a single rule-in or rule-out test for semaglutide eligibility; AASLD promotes a sequential testing strategy with elastography or blood-based NITs after calculating FIB-4 |
| Liver biopsy within the prior 6–12 months may be used to determine disease stage for semaglutide candidacy if no substantial weight change has occurred; a baseline NIT before initiating semaglutide would be beneficial to enable longitudinal non-invasive assessment of treatment response |
| Semaglutide (Wegovy) is not approved for the treatment of MASH cirrhosis (VCTE >20 kPa, MRE >5.0 kPa, ELF >11.3, and/or evidence of portal hypertension); it should not be prescribed solely for this indication |
| Those with compensated cirrhosis who are receiving semaglutide for another FDA-approved indication should be monitored carefully (a single phase 2 trial in compensated cirrhosis, Loomba 2023, did not identify liver-related safety concerns) |
| Alcohol consumption should be assessed with both AUDIT-C and PEth testing; decisions to initiate semaglutide for non-cirrhotic MASH in those with moderate alcohol consumption must be individualized, and where possible, patients should be enrolled in clinical trials |
| A hepatic function panel is recommended only as clinically indicated (not on a fixed schedule) among patients receiving semaglutide for treatment of MASH |
| Clinicians should monitor for rare but serious risks: acute kidney injury (from dehydration), symptomatic gallbladder disease, pancreatitis, thyroid C-cell tumors, retinopathy progression, and lean mass loss (actions per risk: Table 3 above) |
| Active suicidal ideation → semaglutide should not be initiated until the patient is stable and under the care of a mental health professional (p.1331) |
| Before treatment initiation (Table 2): assess for active suicidal thoughts; evaluate for thyroid nodules; consider retinal exam prior to GLP-1 RA therapy in T2DM patients if not done in past 12 months; obtain hepatic function panel, NIT of fibrosis, and NIT of steatosis |
| At 72 weeks: obtain retinal exam per society guidelines; assess hepatic function panel, NIT of fibrosis, and NIT of steatosis |
| On treatment monitoring: monitor for nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, abdominal pain, depression or suicidal thoughts; obtain pregnancy test, hepatic function panel, and right upper quadrant ultrasound (RUQUS) for symptomatic gallstone disease as clinically indicated |
| For semaglutide use in patients with [[gastroparesis]]/delayed gastric emptying: evaluate baseline gastric motility in at-risk patients; avoid in severe gastroparesis; consider gastric emptying evaluation if symptoms persist |
| Semaglutide is contraindicated in individuals with a personal or family history of MTC or MEN2 |
| For procedural safety: document GLP-1 RA use pre-procedure; coordinate with local anesthesia for fasting interval; communicate with anesthesia/endoscopy teams regarding fasting protocols |
| Lean mass loss should be mitigated: maintain protein intake 1.2–1.5 g/kg/day; encourage resistance training; monitor closely in older/sarcopenic adults |
| A decrease from baseline to 72 weeks of VCTE LSM ≥30%, MRE LSM ≥20%, magnetic resonance imaging–proton density fat fraction (MRI-PDFF) ≥30%, ALT ≥17 U/L or ≥20%, or ELF ≥0.5 likely reflects a beneficial response to semaglutide |
| Non-response: lack of decline in ALT or liver stiffness on VCTE, or worsening NIT parameters (p.1335, Figure 2); NITs suggesting worsening or progression to cirrhosis → stop semaglutide |
| Sub-threshold improvement in NITs without evidence of progression to cirrhosis → benefit uncertain; re-evaluate strategy, which may include re-optimizing lifestyle interventions, considering other therapy, with or without stopping semaglutide |
| Lifestyle modification (nutrition, exercise, behavior modification) and optimal control of comorbid metabolic conditions should be maintained regardless of semaglutide treatment status |
| Combination use of resmetirom with semaglutide 2.4 mg/week has not been studied; nor have the two drugs been compared for relative efficacy |

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Superseded framing:** earlier wiki text described semaglutide in MASH as "emerging data, not yet FDA-approved." The August 2025 accelerated FDA approval of Wegovy for MASH F2–F3 supersedes that; [[nafld-masld]] has been updated.
- **Resmetirom + semaglutide combination:** Whether these two FDA-approved MASH agents can or should be combined remains unstudied. MAESTRO-NASH subgroup data shows similar rates of MASH resolution when GLP-1 RAs were taken alongside resmetirom at baseline, but formal combination therapy has not been evaluated.
- **Long-term outcomes:** FDA approval is accelerated; full approval awaits the complete ESSENCE trial results expected in 2028 or later. Long-term durability, discontinuation effects (STEP 1 extension: reversal of cardiometabolic improvements on withdrawal), and survival benefit remain to be proven in MASH.
- **NIT correlation with histology:** No individual NIT reliably predicts histologic response at the patient level. The thresholds for treatment response (VCTE ≥30%, ELF ≥0.5, etc.) are based on aggregate ESSENCE data, not validated at individual level.
- **MASH cirrhosis:** Semaglutide is not approved for cirrhosis. Phase 2 data (Loomba R et al., Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 2023; ref 8) in compensated cirrhosis did not identify liver safety concerns, but this is insufficient for recommendation outside of the approved indication.
- **Lean mass loss concern:** The 13% reduction in lean mass (~39% of total weight lost) is clinically important especially for older/sarcopenic patients with chronic liver disease, where sarcopenia carries prognostic significance.
- **Alcohol use:** NIT cutpoints used for semaglutide candidacy may differ in patients with higher alcohol use (MetALD range), and this must be considered until additional data are available.
- **Internal inconsistency in the source itself:** the abstract gives the MRE individualized-decision band as **4.4–5 kPa** while the body text gives **4.5–5.0 kPa** — which leaves 4.4 kPa simultaneously in the candidacy band (3.1–4.4) and the individualized band by the abstract's reading. Both figures are recorded above rather than silently picking one.
