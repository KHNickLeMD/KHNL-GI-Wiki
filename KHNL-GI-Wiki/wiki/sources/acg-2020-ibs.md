## Summary

First ACG clinical guideline for irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) using GRADE. 25 statements (9 diagnostic, 16 therapeutic). Endorses a **positive diagnostic strategy** using Rome IV criteria rather than diagnosis-of-exclusion (Strong/High for cost-effectiveness). Recommends **limited targeted testing**: serology for [[celiac-disease|celiac]] in diarrhea-predominant IBS (IBS-D) (Strong/Moderate); fecal calprotectin/lactoferrin + C-reactive protein (CRP) in IBS-D to exclude [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]] (Strong/Moderate); **against** routine stool pathogens, colonoscopy <45y without alarms, IgE food allergy panels. Therapeutics emphasize **low-fermentable oligosaccharides, disaccharides, monosaccharides, and polyols (FODMAP) trial, soluble fiber, tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs), gut-directed psychotherapy, peppermint oil** across subtypes; **secretagogues ([[linaclotide]]/[[plecanatide]]/[[lubiprostone]])** and **tegaserod** for constipation-predominant IBS (IBS-C); **[[rifaximin]], alosetron, eluxadoline** for IBS-D. **Strong** recommendation **against fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT)** (very low quality). **Conditional** recommendations **against** polyethylene glycol (PEG) for global IBS-C symptoms (it improves stool consistency only), against antispasmodics, against probiotics, and against bile-acid sequestrants for global IBS-D symptoms.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Diagnosis (Strong recommendations)**

- Serologic celiac testing in IBS-D (Strong/Moderate; odds ratio [OR] 4.48 for biopsy-proven celiac disease [CD])
- In patients **without alarm features** and with suspected IBS and diarrhea symptoms, to rule out IBD: **fecal calprotectin and CRP** (Strong/**Moderate**); **fecal lactoferrin** as the alternative to calprotectin (Strong/**Very low**). The guideline grades these separately — there is no single "moderate-to-very-low" grade.
- **Positive diagnostic strategy** over exclusion (Strong/High for cost-effectiveness; Consensus for time-to-treatment)

**Diagnosis (conditional, consensus, and against)**

- Against routine stool pathogen testing (Conditional/Low)
- Against routine colonoscopy <45y without alarms (Conditional/Low)
- Against testing for **food allergies and food sensitivities** in all patients with IBS unless there are reproducible symptoms concerning for a food allergy (Consensus)
- **Anorectal physiology testing** (anorectal manometry [ARM] and balloon expulsion test [BET] and/or defecography — see [[anorectal-manometry]]) in **patients with IBS** — any subtype — with symptoms suggestive of a pelvic floor disorder and/or refractory constipation not responsive to standard medical therapy (Consensus)
- An accurate **IBS subtype** improves therapy (Consensus)

**IBS-C therapeutics**

- **Against** PEG for global IBS-C (Conditional/Low) — improves stool only, not pain/global
- **Chloride channel activator (lubiprostone)** for global IBS-C (Strong/Moderate)
- **Guanylate cyclase-C (GC-C) agonists — linaclotide 290 µg once daily, plecanatide 3 mg once daily** — for global IBS-C (Strong/High). These are the only two doses this guideline states; it does not give a plecanatide 6 mg dose.
- **Tegaserod 6 mg twice daily (BID)** in women <65 with ≤1 cardiovascular (CV) risk factor failing secretagogues. ⚠ **The document grades this statement two different ways**: Table 2 prints *"Strong/conditional recommendation; low quality of evidence"*, the in-text recommendation box prints *"Conditional recommendation; low quality of evidence."* Contraindicated with **more than 1 CV risk factor**.

**IBS-D therapeutics**

- **Against** bile-acid sequestrants for global IBS-D (Conditional/VLow)
- **[[rifaximin|Rifaximin]]** for global IBS-D (Strong/Moderate) — a **2-week course**, with up to **2 additional retreatment courses** for symptom recurrence. Number needed to treat (NNT) 9. **ACG 2020 states no rifaximin dose anywhere in the document.**
- **Alosetron 0.5–1.0 mg BID** (the Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy [REMS]-restricted prescribing dose) for women with **chronic (>6 months), severe** IBS-D who previously failed response to traditional therapies (Conditional/Low) — REMS for ischemic colitis
- **Eluxadoline 100 mg BID**, with **75 mg BID** for some patients, for IBS-D (Conditional/Moderate). Use the 75 mg dose in **mild-to-moderate hepatic impairment**; do **not** use in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C). **Contraindicated** in: prior pancreatitis, **absent gallbladder**, history of alcoholism/alcohol abuse/addiction, or >3 alcoholic drinks per day.
- **Lubiprostone 8 µg BID** — FDA-approved in adult women with IBS-C (listed here as the dose the guideline prints for the IBS-C secretagogue above)

**Cross-subtype therapeutics**

- **Soluble fiber (psyllium)**, NOT insoluble (bran) (Strong/Moderate)
- **Against** antispasmodics for global symptoms (Conditional/Low)
- Peppermint oil for global symptoms (Conditional/Low)
- **Against** probiotics (Conditional/VLow)
- **Limited low-FODMAP trial** (Conditional/VLow)
- **TCAs** for global symptoms (Strong/Moderate; doses in Table 7: amitriptyline 50–100 mg, desipramine 25–100 mg, nortriptyline 25–75 mg)
- **Gut-directed psychotherapy** (cognitive behavioral therapy [CBT], hypnotherapy, mindfulness) (Conditional/VLow)
- **Strong AGAINST FMT** for IBS (Strong/VLow)

**TCA dosing (Table 7), and the safety rules that go with it:** amitriptyline 50–100 mg, imipramine 50–100 mg, desipramine 25–100 mg, nortriptyline 25–75 mg daily. **Start low** (e.g. amitriptyline 10 mg or desipramine 10 mg) with gradual titration. **Do not use** in known bundle branch block or QT prolongation.

**IBS subtyping (Statement 7) runs on Bristol stool form, off therapy:** IBS-C = >25% Bristol Stool Form Scale (BSFS) 1–2 **and** <25% BSFS 6–7; IBS-D = >25% BSFS 6–7 **and** <25% BSFS 1–2; mixed IBS (IBS-M) = >25% of each; unclassified IBS (IBS-U) = cannot be determined. Assess with **daily diaries for 2 weeks**, while the patient is **off any therapy that could affect bowel pattern**.

**Supplemental Table 2 — cardiovascular risk factors to consider when evaluating a woman with IBS-C for possible [[tegaserod]] use.** This is the list the guideline's "contraindicated with more than 1 CV risk factor" gate is counted against; the body text does not print it.

- History of cardiovascular disease
- History or presence of hypertension
- Active smoking
- History or presence of diabetes mellitus
- History or presence of hyperlipidemia
- **Age >55 years at baseline**
- **Obesity (BMI >30 kg/m² at baseline)**

**Supplemental Table 1 — diagnostic accuracy of serologic and fecal testing for differentiating [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]] from IBS** (with cutoffs; a summary of testing for *any* organic disease in IBS-D and chronic diarrhea, not limited to IBS-D studies).

| Test | Cutoff | Sensitivity | Specificity | LR+ | LR− |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ESR | 10–15 mm/h | 0.54–0.78 | 0.46–0.95 | 1.0–16.3 | 0.3–0.98 |
| CRP | 5–6 mg/L | 0.73 (0.64–0.80) | 0.78 (0.58–0.91) | 3.4 (1.05–5.71) | 0.35 (0.27–0.42) |
| Fecal lactoferrin | 4.0–7.25 µg/g | 0.79 (0.73–0.84) | 0.93 (0.63–0.99) | 11.5 (−10.7–33.8) | 0.22 (0.17–0.28) |
| Fecal calprotectin | 24.3–30 µg/g | 0.92–0.98 | 0.96–0.98 | 28–59 | 0.01–0.07 |
| Fecal calprotectin | 50–60 µg/g | 0.81 (0.75–0.86) | 0.87 (0.78–0.92) | 6.12 (2.75–9.49) | 0.21 (0.14–0.28) |
| Fecal calprotectin | 100–164 µg/g | 0.64 (0.49–0.77) | 0.90 (0.72–0.97) | 6.23 (1.28–13.75) | 0.4 (0.21–0.58) |

*LR+ = positive likelihood ratio · LR− = negative likelihood ratio · ESR = erythrocyte sedimentation rate · CRP = C-reactive protein.* Modified by permission from the guideline's reference 40.

⚠ **The calprotectin bands are the decision content here:** a cutoff around **25–30 µg/g** is what gives the near-complete rule-out (LR− 0.01–0.07); at **100–164 µg/g** sensitivity falls to 0.64 and a negative result no longer excludes IBD.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Rome IV based; Rome V (2026) modifies IBS criteria (frequency ≥3 days/month, discomfort re-added) — IBS-D rifaximin/alosetron evidence still strongest evidence base in field
- Tegaserod re-approval is fragile (CV concerns); access varies
- Eluxadoline–pancreatitis signal in post-cholecystectomy patients limits use
- IBS criteria differ slightly between this 2020 ACG (Rome IV) and Rome V — update planned in next ACG cycle
