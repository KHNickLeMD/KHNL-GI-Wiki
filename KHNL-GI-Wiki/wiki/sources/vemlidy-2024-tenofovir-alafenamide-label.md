## Summary

The FDA prescribing information for **tenofovir alafenamide (TAF)**, the third of the three preferred nucleos(t)ide analogues for chronic hepatitis B (CHB) and the one [[aasld-idsa-2025-chb-treatment|AASLD/IDSA 2025]] prefers over tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF) in renal or bone disease. It was fetched to close the last third of a dose gap the guideline itself leaves open: the guideline gives **no adult milligram dose and no creatinine clearance (CrCl) cut-points** for any of the three agents.

The dose is a **single 25 mg tablet once daily, taken with food** — and unlike TDF, **there is no renal dose adjustment at all**: none is required down to CrCl ≥15 mL/min, and none for end-stage renal disease (ESRD, CrCl <15 mL/min) **on chronic hemodialysis**, where the only instruction is to dose **after** the session. The single renal restriction is the one the wiki already carries from the guideline: **TAF is not recommended in ESRD not receiving chronic hemodialysis**. That is the whole renal story, and its simplicity relative to TDF's modelled interval table is itself a reason to choose TAF in kidney disease.

The label adds one restriction the wiki did not have and that cuts against a rule currently on the page: **TAF is indicated only in compensated liver disease, and is not recommended in decompensated (Child-Pugh B or C) hepatic impairment.** The wiki's decompensated-cirrhosis row, taken from AASLD/IDSA 2025, says to treat decompensated cirrhosis with entecavir, TDF **or TAF** indefinitely. The guideline governs the page under source priority, but the labelled restriction is a real constraint a prescriber will meet and is now stated beside it.

Pre-treatment and on-treatment testing are specified and are more prescriptive than for entecavir: **test for HIV-1 before starting** (TAF alone must not be used in HIV-1), and **before or when starting, and on a clinically appropriate schedule thereafter, assess serum creatinine, estimated CrCl, urine glucose and urine protein in all patients — plus serum phosphorus in chronic kidney disease.** There is **no bone-mineral-density warning**, which is the labelled counterpart of the guideline's preference for TAF in bone disease.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Indication.** Chronic HBV infection in **adults and pediatric patients ≥6 years of age weighing ≥25 kg** with **compensated liver disease**.

**Contraindications: none listed.**

**Dose:**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Dose (adults and pediatric ≥6 y, ≥25 kg) | **25 mg once daily**, one tablet |
| Food | **With food** |
| Tablet strength | 25 mg tenofovir alafenamide (= 28 mg tenofovir alafenamide fumarate) |

**Renal impairment — no dose adjustment, one prohibition:**

| Renal status | Action |
|---|---|
| Estimated **CrCl ≥15 mL/min** | **No dosage adjustment** |
| **ESRD (CrCl <15 mL/min) on chronic hemodialysis** | **No dosage adjustment**; on dialysis days **give after completing hemodialysis** |
| **ESRD not receiving chronic hemodialysis** | **Not recommended** |
| Pediatric renal impairment | **No data** — no dose recommendation possible |

**Hepatic impairment:**

| Child-Pugh class | Action |
|---|---|
| **A (mild)** | **No dosage adjustment** |
| **B or C (decompensated)** | **Not recommended** |

**Testing before initiation and on therapy** (label section 2.1):

- **Test for HIV-1 before starting.** TAF alone must not be used in HIV-1 infection.
- **Before or when starting, and during treatment on a clinically appropriate schedule, assess in all patients:** serum creatinine, estimated CrCl, **urine glucose** and **urine protein**.
- **In chronic kidney disease, also assess serum phosphorus.**

**Boxed warning: posttreatment severe acute exacerbation of hepatitis B.** Discontinuing anti-hepatitis B therapy, TAF included, may cause **severe acute exacerbations**. Monitor closely with **clinical and laboratory follow-up for at least several months** after stopping; resuming anti-hepatitis B therapy may be warranted.

**Warnings and precautions:**

| Warning | Action |
|---|---|
| **HBV / HIV-1 co-infection** (5.2) — risk of selecting HIV-1 resistance; safety and efficacy in co-infection **not established** | **Offer HIV antibody testing to all HBV-infected patients before starting**; if positive, use an appropriate antiretroviral combination regimen |
| **New onset or worsening renal impairment** (5.3) — postmarketing acute renal failure, **proximal renal tubulopathy** and **Fanconi syndrome** with TAF-containing products; risk higher with impaired renal function or concurrent nephrotoxic agents including non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) | Assess creatinine, estimated CrCl, urine glucose and urine protein as above; **discontinue TAF** for a clinically significant fall in renal function or evidence of Fanconi syndrome |
| **Lactic acidosis / severe hepatomegaly with steatosis** (5.4), including fatal cases, reported with nucleoside analogues including TDF | **Suspend TAF** for clinical or laboratory findings suggestive of lactic acidosis or pronounced hepatotoxicity — which may include hepatomegaly and steatosis **without marked transaminase elevation** |

- **Most common adverse reaction** (≥10%, all grades): **headache**.
- **No bone-mineral-density warning and no bone-monitoring instruction** appear on this label, in contrast to the TDF label ([[viread-2018-tenofovir-df-label]]).

## Relevance to Wiki

- **[[hbv-nucleoside-analogs]] → closes the last third of the declared dose gap.** With this label and [[viread-2018-tenofovir-df-label]], all three preferred agents now have adult doses, administration rules and renal cut-points on the page; the sentence recording that TDF and TAF doses were absent is removed.
- **Gives the renal contrast that justifies agent choice.** TAF needs **no** renal adjustment above CrCl 15 mL/min and none on hemodialysis; TDF needs interval extension from CrCl <50 mL/min and has **no recommendation at all** below 10 mL/min off dialysis. That is the labelled content behind the guideline's "use TAF instead of TDF in renal disease".
- **Adds a decompensated-cirrhosis constraint the wiki did not carry** — see Contradictions.
- **Adds the pre-treatment and on-treatment renal laboratory set** (creatinine, CrCl, urine glucose, urine protein, ± phosphorus), which AASLD/IDSA 2025 does not specify.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- ⚠ **TAF in decompensated cirrhosis — label vs guideline.** The label indicates TAF for **compensated** liver disease only and states it is **not recommended in Child-Pugh B or C**. [[aasld-idsa-2025-chb-treatment|AASLD/IDSA 2025]] recommends treating **decompensated** cirrhosis with entecavir, TDF **or TAF** indefinitely. Under Content Guide → *Source priority* the **guideline governs the page**, so [[hbv-nucleoside-analogs]] and [[cirrhosis]] continue to list all three agents — but the restriction is surfaced on both the drug page and here, because it is the kind of mismatch a pharmacy will raise. The guideline is also the newer document (2025 vs the 3/2024 label revision).
- **No contradiction on the renal points** — the guideline's "TAF is not recommended at CrCl <15 mL/min when not yet on dialysis" is the label's own restriction, repeated accurately.
- **A label is not a guideline** and sits outside the wiki's three-tier scheme. Authoritative for dose, administration and labelled safety; not for treatment indication or agent selection where a society guideline speaks.
- **Pregnancy is not captured here.** The wiki's TAF-in-pregnancy stance comes from [[aasld-idsa-2025-chb-treatment]] and [[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]], both newer and both guidelines.
