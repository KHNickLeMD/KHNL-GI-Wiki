# AGA Clinical Practice Guideline: Coagulation Disorders in Cirrhosis (2021)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Article:** [AGA Clinical Practice Guideline: Coagulation Disorders in Cirrhosis (2021). Gastroenterology (AGA Clinical Practice Guideline).](https://doi.org/10.1053/j.gastro.2021.08.015)
- **Year:** 2021
- **Journal:** Gastroenterology (AGA Clinical Practice Guideline)
- **DOI:** [10.1053/j.gastro.2021.08.015](https://doi.org/10.1053/j.gastro.2021.08.015)
- **Type:** American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE)-based guideline

---

## Summary

This guideline addresses the **rebalanced hemostasis** of cirrhosis — abnormal prothrombin time (PT)/international normalized ratio (INR) and platelet counts do **not** reliably predict bleeding — and discourages routine correction of lab abnormalities before procedures. It anchors the vault's [[cirrhosis-hemostasis]] concept.

---

## Key Recommendations

All six Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome (PICO) questions, numbered and graded as the guideline numbers and grades them (Table 3). "Common gastrointestinal (GI) procedures" is given in each statement with the same six examples: paracentesis, thoracentesis, variceal banding, colonic polypectomy, endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP), and liver biopsy.

| # | Recommendation | Strength / certainty |
|---|---|---|
| 1A | In stable cirrhosis undergoing common GI procedures, the AGA makes **no recommendation** regarding viscoelastic testing (VET) before procedures to predict bleeding risk | No recommendation / knowledge gap |
| 1B | In stable cirrhosis **with known baseline abnormal coagulation parameters** undergoing common GI procedures, **suggests against** extensive preprocedural testing, including repeated measurement of prothrombin time (PT)/INR or platelet count | Conditional / very low |
| 2A | In stable cirrhosis undergoing common GI procedures, **suggests against routine** use of blood products (e.g., fresh frozen plasma [FFP] or platelets) for bleeding prophylaxis. *Comment:* applies to the majority of stable patients, who usually do not have severe thrombocytopenia or severe coagulopathy; with severe derangement undergoing a high-bleeding-risk procedure, weigh benefits and risks (transfusion reactions, procedure delay) **in consultation with a hematologist** | Conditional / very low |
| 2B | In thrombocytopenia and stable cirrhosis undergoing common procedures (**and in particular "low-risk" procedures**), **suggests against routine** use of thrombopoietin receptor agonists (TPO-RAs) for bleeding prophylaxis. *Comment:* patients who place high value on an uncertain reduction in procedural bleeding and low value on the increased risk of portal vein thrombosis (PVT) may reasonably select a TPO-RA | Conditional / very low |
| 3 | In **hospitalized** patients with cirrhosis who otherwise meet standard guidelines for venous thromboembolism (VTE) prophylaxis, **suggests standard anticoagulation prophylaxis** over no anticoagulation | Conditional / very low |
| 4 | In patients with cirrhosis, **suggests against routine screening** for PVT. *Comment:* **does not apply to patients listed for liver transplantation**; patients valuing the uncertain benefits of screening over the harms of treatment would reasonably select screening | Conditional / very low |
| 5 | In cirrhosis with **acute or subacute nontumoral PVT**, **suggests anticoagulation over no anticoagulation** for treatment | Conditional / very low |
| 6 | In cirrhosis and **atrial fibrillation with an indication for anticoagulation**, **suggests anticoagulation over no anticoagulation**. *Comment:* patients with more advanced cirrhosis (Child-Turcotte-Pugh class C) and/or low CHA₂DS₂-VASc scores who value avoiding bleeding over stroke reduction could reasonably choose no anticoagulation | Conditional / very low |

**Supporting detail that carries the decisions:**
- No PT/INR or platelet threshold predicting bleeding was found; the threshold for severe thrombocytopenia or coagulopathy "could not be clearly defined" and remains clinical judgment. Reviewed studies typically defined coagulopathy as **INR >1.8 and/or platelets <50,000/µL**; *in vitro* data suggest **>55,000/µL** provides adequate substrate for thrombin generation.
- Procedure bleeding risk was split **low vs high at 1.5%** (literature review + expert interpretation).
- Markers that did track with bleeding: progressive decompensation by Child-Turcotte-Pugh class (after variceal banding, polypectomy — especially larger polyps — and endoscopic sphincterotomy), acute-on-chronic liver failure, and acute kidney injury.
- VET did not change post-procedural bleeding (relative risk [RR] 0.33, 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.01–7.87) or mortality to 90 days (RR 1.05, 95% CI 0.45–2.44), with a clear trend toward lower blood-product use.
- TPO-RA thrombotic events ≈ **1% at 30 days** for avatrombopag and lusutrombopag.
- VTE incidence in cirrhosis typically **0.5–1.9%**; the guideline names the **Padua Prediction Score** and **IMPROVE** model without printing their components; IMPROVE counts liver disease (**INR >1.5**) as a bleeding risk factor with RR **2.18**.
- PVT: anticoagulation increased complete or partial recanalization (RR 2.27, 95% CI 1.73–2.98) and was associated with less portal hypertensive bleeding (RR 0.34, 95% CI 0.16–0.75). No qualifying DOAC studies; **no data favour one anticoagulant over another**.
- Atrial fibrillation: benefit appears to outweigh bleeding risk at a **CHA₂DS₂-VASc score ≥2**; major bleeding on anticoagulation vs untreated rate ratio 1.91, lower with direct-acting oral anticoagulants than vitamin K antagonists (RR 0.62, 95% CI 0.45–0.85).

## Clinical Relevance

Reframes cirrhosis coagulopathy: stop "fixing the INR," avoid routine prophylactic FFP/platelets before common GI procedures, and **do give VTE prophylaxis**. Directly supports [[cirrhosis-hemostasis]] and intersects periprocedural management in [[portal-hypertension]] and [[acg-2025-perioperative-cirrhosis|perioperative cirrhosis care]].
