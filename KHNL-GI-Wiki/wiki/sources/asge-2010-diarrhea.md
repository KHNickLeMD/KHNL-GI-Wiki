## Summary

ASGE Standards of Practice statement on **when and how to use endoscopy in the workup of diarrhea**, updating a 2001 ASGE document. It distinguishes acute (≤4 weeks) from chronic (>4 weeks) diarrhea and focuses primarily on chronic diarrhea, with separate algorithms for **immunocompetent** and **immunocompromised** hosts. Core message for immunocompetent patients: stool/laboratory testing is first-line; endoscopy is reserved for persistent, unexplained, or treatment-refractory symptoms. When colonoscopy is done for chronic diarrhea, **random biopsies of both the right and left colon are required even when the mucosa is normal**, because microscopic colitis is patchy and left-sided-only sampling misses cases.

For small-bowel/malabsorptive presentations, EGD with ≥4 duodenal biopsies (even if endoscopically normal) is used to evaluate celiac disease and other mucosal disorders; capsule endoscopy and enteroscopy are *not* recommended routinely. In immunocompromised hosts (HIV, post-transplant, GVHD) the infectious differential is broader (notably CMV), endoscopic biopsy may diagnose tissue-invasive disease missed by stool/serologic testing, and yield rises at lower CD4 counts. For suspected acute GVHD after HSCT, flexible sigmoidoscopy with distal-colon biopsy is the preferred initial test (distal colon highest yield; duodenal biopsy carries hematoma risk).

## Key Findings / Claims
- Diarrhea defined by stool weight (>200 g/day), loose/liquid consistency, and/or frequency (>3/day); 4-week cutoff separates acute (≤4 wk) from chronic (>4 wk).
- Endoscopy is **not warranted** for initial evaluation of acute diarrhea; consider it for persistent symptoms, inconclusive routine testing, or empirical-therapy failure.
- Diagnostic yield of colonoscopy in chronic diarrhea ~7–32%; IBD and microscopic colitis are the most common findings.
- Microscopic colitis (collagenous + lymphocytic) ~10% of chronic-diarrhea referrals; endoscopically normal mucosa; **patchy distribution → biopsy both right and left colon** (left-only sampling can miss it).
- Sodium phosphate preps can cause mucosal changes mimicking IBD (usually distinguishable on histology); NSAIDs can cause terminal-ileal changes mimicking IBD/Crohn's.
- Terminal-ileum intubation recommended in chronic-diarrhea colonoscopy; routine biopsy of normal-appearing TI is low yield (0–4.2%).
- EGD with small-bowel biopsy for chronic diarrhea/malabsorption with inconclusive colonoscopy or positive celiac serology; obtain **≥4 duodenal biopsies** for suspected celiac disease (4 biopsies → 100% diagnostic in one series).
- Capsule endoscopy and enteroscopy **not recommended** for routine chronic-diarrhea evaluation (modest yield, no tissue, capsule-retention risk).
- Immunocompromised hosts: stool testing first-line; CMV common — PCR/culture/serology positivity may not equal tissue-invasive disease, so biopsy (± in situ hybridization/IHC) may be needed.
- HIV: colonoscopy has higher yield and is more cost-effective than sigmoidoscopy (39% vs 22%); yield rises with CD4 <100; proximal-colon biopsies capture pathogens/lymphomas beyond sigmoidoscope reach.
- GVHD: flexible sigmoidoscopy + distal-colon biopsy preferred initial test (distal-colon sensitivity 82–95%); duodenal biopsy risks hematoma (esp. acute GVHD + thrombocytopenia); EGD added if sigmoidoscopy negative or upper-GI symptoms predominate.

### Recommendations (verbatim, with GRADE evidence quality)
1. Stool and laboratory tests should be the initial step for the evaluation of clinical scenarios suggestive of infectious diarrhea. *(moderate quality)*
2. In patients with chronic unexplained diarrhea, we suggest colonoscopy with random biopsies of the right and left side of the colon. Sigmoidoscopy is an alternative option, although this may miss right-sided organic disease. *(low quality)*
3. We recommend intubation of the terminal ileum during colonoscopy for patients undergoing evaluation of chronic diarrhea. *(moderate quality)* There are insufficient data to determine whether biopsy of an endoscopically normal-appearing terminal ileum should be routinely performed, but the yield of this is likely low.
4. We recommend EGD with small-bowel biopsy in patients with chronic diarrhea or suspected malabsorption and inconclusive evaluation after colonoscopy with biopsy, and in patients with positive celiac serology. *(moderate quality)*
5. We recommend obtaining a minimum of 4 duodenal biopsy specimens for evaluation of suspected celiac disease. *(moderate quality)*
6. Enteroscopy is not recommended for the routine evaluation of chronic diarrhea but may be useful for evaluation of small-bowel disease when other investigations are nondiagnostic. *(low quality)*
7. VCE is not recommended for the routine evaluation of chronic diarrhea. *(low quality)*
8. In patients with HIV and diarrhea, we suggest either flexible sigmoidoscopy or colonoscopy if laboratory evaluation is nondiagnostic. *(low quality)*
9. In the absence of a diagnosis on flexible sigmoidoscopy, we recommend a full colonoscopy with biopsy and/or EGD with biopsy for HIV patients with persistent diarrhea. *(moderate quality)*
10. In patients with suspected GVHD and diarrhea, we suggest flexible sigmoidoscopy with distal colon biopsies as the initial endoscopic evaluation. *(low quality)* In the event of negative colonic histology findings or when upper GI symptoms predominate, we recommend an EGD with biopsies. *(moderate quality)*
