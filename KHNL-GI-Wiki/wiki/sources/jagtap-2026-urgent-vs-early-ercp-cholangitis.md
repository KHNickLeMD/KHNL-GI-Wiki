## Summary

Single-centre, open-label, superiority RCT at a high-volume Indian tertiary centre (~10,000 ERCPs/year) testing **timing of biliary drainage within the guideline-recommended 48-hour window** for acute cholangitis. 304 adults with **mild-to-moderate** acute cholangitis (TG18 criteria) were randomised 1:1 to **urgent ERCP (<24 h)** vs **early ERCP (24–48 h)**. Severe cholangitis, severe pancreatitis, cholecystitis, pregnancy, altered anatomy, and high-grade (Bismuth III/IV) strictures were excluded.

The primary outcome — **30-day all-cause mortality** — did not differ (3.95% urgent vs 6.58% early; HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.25–1.93; p=0.47). No secondary clinical outcome (day-3/day-30 organ failure, in-hospital mortality, length of stay, readmission, reintervention) differed after multiplicity adjustment. **Urgent ERCP roughly doubled post-ERCP adverse events** (17.1% vs 9.2%; RR 2.03, 95% CI 1.02–4.07; unadjusted p=0.04), driven by **post-ERCP haemorrhage** (10.5% vs 3.3%). The DSMB **halted enrolment for lost equipoise** — a modest, non-significant mortality signal outweighed by accumulating procedural harm (post hoc NNT to prevent one death = 38; NNH to cause one adverse event = 13).

On multivariate analysis, **urgent ERCP** (OR 2.16) and **biliary sphincterotomy** (OR 9.76) were independent predictors of post-ERCP adverse events, and both independently predicted post-ERCP haemorrhage. **Malignant biliary obstruction was the only independent predictor of 30-day mortality** (stratified Cox HR 5.27, 95% CI 1.86–14.90; p=0.002); ERCP timing and age were not. The authors conclude that for stable mild-to-moderate cholangitis, "pause, assess, optimise, and proceed" (fluid resuscitation, coagulopathy correction, antibiotics) within 24–48 h is as effective and safer than reflexive urgent drainage.

**Underpowering caveat:** the trial was powered for an 11-point absolute mortality difference (19% vs 8%, from prior data); the observed difference was 2.6 points, giving ~18% power — small mortality benefits or harms cannot be excluded.

## Key Findings / Claims

- **Primary — 30-day mortality (ITT):** 3.95% (6/152) urgent vs 6.58% (10/152) early; HR 0.70 (0.25–1.93), p=0.47. Absolute difference −2.63 points (95% CI −7.64 to 2.38). No difference by PP.
- **Post-ERCP adverse events (ITT):** 17.1% urgent vs 9.2% early; RR 2.03 (1.02–4.07), p=0.04. PP: 17.8% vs 8.1%; RR 2.44 (1.19–5.01), p=0.01. (Did not survive Holm-Bonferroni adjustment.)
  - **Post-ERCP haemorrhage** was the dominant AE: **10.5% urgent vs 3.3% early**.
  - **Post-ERCP pancreatitis** rates not significantly different (all received rectal indomethacin 100 mg prophylaxis).
- **Independent predictors (multivariate logistic):**
  - Post-ERCP AE: urgent ERCP OR 2.16 (1.06–4.39), p=0.033; biliary sphincterotomy OR 9.76 (2.28–41.66), p=0.02.
  - Post-ERCP haemorrhage: urgent ERCP OR 3.61 (1.27–10.20), p=0.016; sphincterotomy OR 9.29 (1.22–70.74), p=0.031.
  - 30-day mortality: **malignant biliary obstruction** HR 5.27 (1.86–14.90), p=0.002 — the only predictor; age and timing NOT associated.
- **No mortality events occurred in mild cholangitis** (quasi-complete separation forced dropping grade from the Cox model). ~75% of the cohort had moderate cholangitis.
- **Secondary outcomes (ITT, all NS):** day-3 organ failure 9.2% vs 11.2%; day-30 organ failure 11.8% vs 17.1%; in-hospital mortality 1.97% vs 3.28%; median LOS 6.94 vs 7.84 days; 30-day readmission 9.9% vs 5.9%; reintervention 6.6% vs 4.6%.
- **Procedural context:** mean time-to-ERCP 13 h 27 min (urgent) vs 32 h 54 min (early). ERCP technical success ~92% (ITT). Commonest aetiology choledocholithiasis (~36%), then stent dysfunction, malignant, benign obstruction. Rectal indomethacin prophylaxis used; sphincterotomy withheld in patients on antiplatelets/anticoagulants.
- **Post hoc harm–benefit:** NNT (prevent one death, urgent) = 38; NNH (one extra AE, urgent) = 13 → unfavourable.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Vs retrospective/observational literature** (Du, Lyu, Iqbal meta-analyses/subset analyses cited within) that favoured earlier drainage and reported lower in-hospital mortality with ERCP <24 h: those were non-randomised, heterogeneous, and did not control for severity. This RCT finds no benefit to <24 h and net harm in mild-to-moderate disease.
- **Vs guideline framing** (ASGE 2021, TG18: ERCP within 48 h): not contradicted — the RCT supports the 48-h window but argues against reflexively compressing it to <24 h for stable non-severe patients.
- **Generalisability:** single-centre, high-volume expert unit; open-label; severe cholangitis excluded; underpowered for the observed (small) mortality difference (~18% power). Findings do not apply to **severe** cholangitis, where urgent drainage remains standard.
