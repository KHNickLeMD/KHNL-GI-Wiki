## Summary

Derivation paper for the **Rotterdam prognostic index** in [[budd-chiari-syndrome|Budd-Chiari syndrome (BCS)]] — the score the hepatology guidelines name but do not print. An international multicentre cohort of **237 patients** with newly diagnosed non-malignant BCS, diagnosed 1984–2001 at the Dutch academic hospitals, the Mayo Clinic (Rochester), Hôpital Beaujon (Clichy) and Hôpital Louis Mourier (Colombes).

Multivariate Cox regression identified **four baseline variables** independently associated with transplantation-free survival: **hepatic encephalopathy, ascites, prothrombin time and serum bilirubin**. Their regression coefficients were used directly as item scores in a linear prognostic formula, and the distribution of total scores was cut at its upper and lower quartiles to define **three prognostic classes** with markedly different 5-year survival — **89% (class I), 74% (class II), 42% (class III)**.

The second question the paper asks is whether **portosystemic shunting (PSS)** helps. Analysed as a time-dependent covariate — which removes the response-time bias that inflates shunt results in uncontrolled series — PSS showed a **trend toward improved survival only in class II**, the intermediate-prognosis group and the largest subgroup. Mortality risk rose the later the shunt was placed.

The score is a **derivation without external validation in this paper**, and the cohort predates modern stepwise BCS management. The current guidelines restrict it accordingly — see *Contradictions / Open Questions*.

## Key Findings / Claims

*Numbering below is the wiki's — the paper states findings in prose and tables and numbers nothing.*

### Cohort and Endpoint
- **282 patients** with BCS identified; excluded were hepatic outflow obstruction from malignancy (n = 35), BCS after liver transplantation (n = 3) and diagnosis at autopsy (n = 7) → **237 analysed**.
- BCS defined as hepatic venous outflow obstruction regardless of cause or level, from the small hepatic veins to the inferior vena cava (IVC) entrance into the right atrium. **Congestive heart disease and sinusoidal obstruction syndrome were excluded** as separate entities.
- Diagnosis established by **Doppler ultrasound, computed tomography, magnetic resonance imaging or venography**. Histologic or non-specific radiologic features suggestive of BCS were **not** accepted as diagnostic.
- **Endpoint: transplantation-free survival** — followed from diagnosis until death, orthotopic liver transplantation, study closure (1 January 2001) or last visit.
- Median age **35 y** (range 13–76); **67% female**. Follow-up 2 days–203 months (median 44 months).
- Outflow obstruction site: hepatic veins **62%**, IVC **7%**, both **31%**. Portal vein obstruction in 34 (14%).
- Ascites 84%, hepatomegaly 76%, splenomegaly 51%, encephalopathy 10%, variceal bleeding 8%. Eleven patients were asymptomatic.
- Median Child-Pugh score **8** (range 5–14); cirrhosis at biopsy in 11 of 138 biopsied.
- **How the variables were measured:** encephalopathy by the **Glasgow Coma Scale**; ascites, hepatomegaly and splenomegaly by ultrasonography or other radiographic methods; myeloproliferative disorders by bone marrow examination, overt forms only.

### Overall Survival
| Time from diagnosis | Transplantation-free survival | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| 1 year | 82% | 77–87 |
| 5 years | 69% | 62–76 |
| 10 years | 62% | 54–70 |
| 15 years | 59% | 51–68 |

- During follow-up **52 patients (22%) died** and **29 (12%)** underwent orthotopic liver transplantation; 20 were lost to follow-up.
- Causes of death: liver failure 17, postoperative multiorgan failure 12, sepsis 4, new malignancy 2, cardiovascular disease 3, cerebrovascular accident 2, variceal bleeding 1, combinations 8.

### The Four Independent Predictors
Multivariate Cox regression, stratified by country, in the **205 patients with complete data** (Table 3):

| Variable | *P* | Risk ratio | 95% CI | Item score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Encephalopathy — present vs absent | <.001 | 3.58 | 1.87–6.86 | **1.27** |
| Ascites — present vs absent | .08 | 2.83 | 0.87–9.22 | **1.04** |
| Prothrombin time — international normalized ratio (INR) >2.3 vs ≤2.3 | .02 | 2.05 | 1.13–3.70 | **0.72** |
| Bilirubin (µmol/L), continuous | .07 | 1.004 | 1.00–1.01 | **0.004** |

- **Item scores are the natural logarithms of the corresponding risk ratios** — this is how the formula was built, not an arbitrary point assignment.
- Age, sex, site of outflow obstruction and alanine aminotransferase were entered and eliminated by stepwise backward selection. **Albumin added nothing** when tested in the model.
- Variables significant on univariate analysis but **not** independent: Child-Pugh score (*P* < .001), sodium (*P* = .03), creatinine (*P* = .01), albumin (*P* = .02).

### The Rotterdam Prognostic Index

> **Score = (1.27 × encephalopathy) + (1.04 × ascites) + (0.72 × prothrombin time) + (0.004 × bilirubin)**

- **Encephalopathy:** present = 1, absent = 0.
- **Ascites:** present = 1, absent = 0.
- **Prothrombin time:** INR **>2.3** = 1, INR **≤2.3** = 0.
- **Bilirubin:** continuous, **in µmol/L** — risk increased by 0.004 per µmol/L. The formula is not calibrated for mg/dL.
- Observed total scores ranged **0.02 to 4.03**.
- Class boundaries are the **upper and lower quartiles of the score distribution**, not clinically derived cut-points.
- **Prothrombin time in the French sample** was recorded as Quick time: Quick **100%–44%** was taken as INR **≤2.3**; Quick **<44%** as INR **>2.3**.

### Prognostic Classes and Survival
| Class | Total score | n | 5-year survival | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I** — good | 0 to 1.1 | 55 | **89%** | 79–99 |
| **II** — intermediate | >1.1 to 1.5 | 95 | **74%** | 65–83 |
| **III** — poor | ≥1.5 | 55 | **42%** | 28–56 |

- *P*trend < .0001; class I vs II *P* = .013; class II vs III *P* = .0001.
- Sensitivity analysis using **death alone** as the endpoint (censoring the 29 transplanted patients at intervention): the classification **remained predictive**, *P* < .001.

### Anticoagulation
- **171 of 237 (72%)** received anticoagulants.
- **No significant survival benefit in the whole cohort** — relative risk (RR) **1.05** (95% CI 0.62–1.76). Unchanged when anticoagulation plus PSS was treated as a separate category (RR 0.80; 0.61–1.05).
- By class: class I **RR 0.14** (0.02–1.21) — a trend only; class II RR 0.88 (0.39–2.01); class III RR 1.3 (0.50–3.04).
- The authors caution that **the reason anticoagulants were withheld is often unknown**, so this comparison is confounded.

### Portosystemic Shunting
- **117 patients (49%)** underwent PSS. Indications in all centres: **refractory ascites, deterioration of liver function, or both**.
- **106 of 117 (91%)** were shunted within the first year (median 1 month, range 0–132).
- Shunt types: mesocaval 42 (36%), portocaval 35 (30%), mesoatrial 10 (9%), mesoinnominate 6 (5%), splenorenal 4 (3%), cavoatrial 2 (1%), portoatrial 1 (1%); **[[tips|transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS)]] in 17 (15%)**. Shunt failure in 16 (14%).
- **Mortality risk rose the later PSS was performed** (Cox analysis testing proportionality):

| Interval diagnosis → PSS | *P* | Relative risk | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|
| <1 month (n = 63) | .83 | 1.07 | 0.57–2.00 |
| 1–6 months (n = 39) | .002 | 3.05 | 1.53–6.09 |
| >6 months (n = 15) | .009 | 4.15 | 1.42–12.12 |

- Because of this, **the effect of PSS could not be analysed in the whole population** — the proportional-hazards assumption fails. Only **class II** held an equal mortality risk after prolonged follow-up, so only there was a time-dependent analysis feasible.
- **Class II: trend toward improved survival with PSS — RR 0.63 (95% CI 0.26–1.49), *P* = .29.** 52 of 95 class II patients (55%) were shunted; 5-year survival 82% with PSS vs 68% without.
- Separate analysis by shunt type showed **similar results for surgical shunting and TIPS**.

### Comparison With Child-Pugh
- The index "closely resembles" the Child-Pugh score — it shares encephalopathy, ascites, prothrombin time and bilirubin, but **not albumin**.
- Discriminative analysis using the **Akaike information criteria** found the prognostic model **superior to Child-Pugh** in predicting outcome in BCS (data not shown in the paper).

## Limitations
- **Derivation only — no external validation cohort in this paper.** The classes were defined and evaluated in the same 205 patients.
- **Retrospective**, from chart review; 205 of 237 had complete data for all four variables.
- **Treatment was not randomized.** PSS and anticoagulation were given by physician choice, so every therapy comparison is confounded by indication even after time-dependent adjustment.
- Cohort diagnosed **1984–2001**, before the current stepwise BCS algorithm and before routine TIPS.
- The ascites and bilirubin coefficients are retained in the formula despite *P* values of **.08** and **.07** respectively.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **The guidelines restrict this score; the paper does not.** [[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension|Baveno VIII]] 8.36 states that available BCS prognostic scores "should not determine individual patient management alone, but can be used for research purposes" (level of evidence 3, strong). [[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]] key concept 26 agrees that prognostic scoring systems are not helpful in guiding choice of therapy. **The guidelines win on [[budd-chiari-syndrome]]**; the components are recorded there for reference, not as a management trigger.
- **The endpoint is characterized differently.** ACG 2020 describes the Rotterdam score as validated for *intervention-free* but not *transplant-free* survival. This paper's stated primary endpoint is **transplantation-free survival**, and a sensitivity analysis using death alone also held (*P* < .001). The discrepancy is unresolved by the documents in the wiki.
- **The Baveno VII position is superseded.** [[baveno-vii-2022-portal-hypertension|Baveno VII]] 8.24–8.25 endorsed the separate **BCS-TIPS prognostic index** with a score >7 as a trigger to consider transplantation before TIPS; Baveno VIII withdrew it. **The BCS-TIPS index is a different score from the Rotterdam index and its components are not in this paper** or in any other wiki source.
- **Class-II shunting is a trend, not a result.** RR 0.63 with a confidence interval crossing 1 (0.26–1.49) and *P* = .29. The paper calls for prospective confirmation; none is in the wiki.
