## Summary
- Acute kidney injury (AKI) occurs in up to **60%** of hospitalized patients with [[cirrhosis]] and raises morbidity, mortality and progression to chronic kidney disease (CKD). Earlier ADQI VIII/ICA criteria (2012) were revised by ICA in 2015; this 2023 meeting (ADQI XXIX + ICA) refines the AKI and hepatorenal syndrome (HRS) definitions, reviews epidemiology, pathophysiology, biomarkers, prevention and treatment, and proposes post-discharge care.
- **Definitions:** AKI, acute kidney disease (AKD) and CKD by Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) criteria, now **including urine output (UO)**; a new baseline serum creatinine (SCr) hierarchy (lowest stable value in 3 months → most recent up to 12 months → lower of admission SCr or SCr back-calculated from estimated glomerular filtration rate [eGFR] 75 mL/min/1.73 m²); complete recovery = SCr back within 0.3 mg/dL of baseline.
- **New HRS-AKI criteria (Box 1):** cirrhosis with ascites + KDIGO AKI + no improvement within **24 h** of adequate volume resuscitation (when clinically indicated) + no strong evidence of an alternative primary cause. The **48-h albumin challenge is dropped** (strong recommendation against), and tubular injury, proteinuria or pre-existing CKD **no longer exclude** HRS-AKI. HRS type 1/2 → HRS-AKI, HRS-AKD, HRS-CKD by timing.
- **Treatment:** crystalloids (preferably balanced) first-line for fluid resuscitation; vasoconstrictor (terlipressin first-line) + 20–25% albumin started **immediately** once HRS-AKI is diagnosed; terlipressin escalated every 24 h if SCr has not fallen 25%; stop rules (SCr within 0.3 mg/dL of baseline, severe adverse reaction, no improvement after 48 h at maximum tolerated doses, renal replacement therapy [RRT] indicated, 14 days); liver transplantation (LT) as definitive treatment; expedited LT evaluation after any AKI in decompensated cirrhosis. Prevention by a "kidney-liver health" (KLH) assessment, nephrotoxin stewardship, and albumin after large-volume paracentesis (LVP) and in spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP) — not systematically for other infections or to target a serum albumin level. Post-discharge follow-up stratified by AKI severity and liver status, KLH review within 1 month.

### Key points box (p. 164)
- AKI in cirrhosis defined by KDIGO: SCr rise **≥0.3 mg/dL (26.5 µmol/L) within 48 h** or **≥50% from baseline** known or presumed within the prior 7 days, and/or **UO ≤0.5 mL/kg for ≥6 h**.
- Lowest stable SCr in the previous 3 months "may be used" for diagnosis and staging; none in 3 months → most recent value up to 12 months prior.
- HRS-AKI is an AKI phenotype specific to advanced cirrhosis with ascites; may coexist with tubular injury, proteinuria and/or pre-existing CKD.
- HRS-AKI criteria (a)–(d) as in Q3.2 below.
- Recommends against systematic albumin for 48 h as a requisite for HRS-AKI diagnosis.
- Vasoconstrictor (terlipressin first-line) + 20–25% albumin started immediately once HRS-AKI is diagnosed.

### Q1 — Harmonizing AKI, AKD, CKD and renal recovery definitions between KDIGO and ICA (p. 164)
- **Q1.1** — In cirrhosis, **recommend** defining AKI by KDIGO criteria: SCr rise **≥0.3 mg/dL (26.5 µmol/L) within 48 h**, or **≥50% from baseline** known or presumed within the prior 7 days, and/or **UO ≤0.5 mL/kg for ≥6 h**. *(strong recommendation, grade A)*
- **Q1.2** — In cirrhosis, **recommend** defining AKD and CKD by KDIGO criteria. *(strong recommendation, grade A)*
- **Q1.3** — In cirrhosis, **recommend** defining **complete recovery** from AKI as return of SCr to **within 0.3 mg/dL (26.5 µmol/L) of baseline**. *(strong recommendation, grade B)*
- Rationale (p. 164):
  - ICA had defined and staged AKI by SCr criteria only; oliguria is a sensitive, early marker linked to worse outcomes in critically ill cirrhosis. UO may be low at baseline with ascites; UO measurement outside the intensive care unit (ICU) is often inaccurate and diuretics confound it — still, monitor UO closely when possible.
  - **CKD** = persistent glomerular filtration rate (GFR) **<60 mL/min/1.73 m²** and/or markers of kidney damage for **>3 months**. **AKD** = structural/functional abnormality (GFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m² or SCr rise >50%) within **≤3 months** not meeting AKI or CKD definitions. AKI is a subset of AKD — every patient with AKI has AKD.
  - No universal definition of post-AKI renal recovery; creatinine criteria may overestimate recovery (muscle loss in critical illness).

### Q2 — Reference (baseline) SCr (pp. 164–166)
- **Q2.1** — **Recommend** using the **lowest stable SCr in the previous 3 months** for diagnosis and staging of AKI; none in 3 months → **most recent value up to 12 months prior** may be used. *(strong recommendation, grade D)*
- **Q2.2** — No known baseline SCr → **suggest** using the **lower of** SCr on admission **or** SCr back-calculated from an **eGFR of 75 mL/min/1.73 m²** as the reference. *(weak recommendation, grade B)*
- Rationale:
  - Known values beat imputation; with several values, the median reduces outlier bias; with large fluctuations, clinical judgment picks the baseline. When using a value up to 12 months old, check that the fall in function is truly acute, not progressive CKD.
  - Evaluate all patients with presumed AKI for pre-existing CKD (history, exam, laboratory data, renal ultrasound).
  - ICA had suggested the first admission SCr when no baseline exists — may underestimate AKI and miss community-acquired AKI. KDIGO method (lower of admission SCr or eGFR-75 back-calculation) studied in a retrospective cohort of 3,458 patients with cirrhosis: patients developing stage 3 AKI had average admission SCr 1.6 mg/dL, back-calculated 1.0 mg/dL, known baseline 1.1 mg/dL.
  - These rules aid diagnosis and do not replace clinical judgment; AKI remains a clinical diagnosis.

### Q3 — Diagnostic criteria for HRS-AKI (pp. 166–167)
- **Q3.1** — HRS-AKI is a phenotype of AKI specific to patients with **advanced cirrhosis and ascites**; it may also occur with **tubular injury, proteinuria and/or pre-existing CKD**. *(not graded)*
- **Q3.2** — **Recommend** these HRS-AKI criteria: (a) cirrhosis with ascites; (b) SCr rise **≥0.3 mg/dL (26.5 µmol/L) within 48 h** or **≥50% from baseline** known or presumed within the prior 7 days and/or **UO ≤0.5 mL/kg for ≥6 h**; (c) **no improvement in SCr and/or UO within 24 h** after adequate volume resuscitation (when clinically indicated); (d) **no strong evidence for an alternative explanation** as the primary cause of AKI. *(not graded)*
- **Q3.3** — **Recommend against** systematic administration of **albumin for 48 h** as a requisite for diagnosing HRS-AKI. *(strong recommendation, grade D)*
- **Q3.4** — **Recommend** replacing the historical terms HRS type 1 and 2 with **HRS-AKI, HRS-AKD and HRS-CKD**, depending on timing and duration of kidney dysfunction. *(strong recommendation, grade D)*

**Box 1 (p. 167) — ICA-ADQI new diagnostic criteria for HRS-AKI**
- Cirrhosis with ascites
- SCr rise ≥0.3 mg/dL (26.5 µmol/L) within 48 hours or ≥50% from baseline known or presumed within the prior 7 days and/or urinary output ≤0.5 mL/kg for ≥6 hours
- No improvement in SCr and/or urine output within 24 hours after adequate volume resuscitation (when clinically indicated)
- No strong evidence for an alternative explanation as the primary cause of AKI
- Box footnote: underlying kidney disease does **not** exclude superimposed HRS-AKI; HRS-AKI may coexist with other causes of AKI. **Examples of alternative causes:** septic shock requiring vasopressors, drug-induced AKI, obstruction, acute glomerular injury. HRS-AKI / HRS-AKD / HRS-CKD by timing and duration.

**Rationale (pp. 166–167):**
- Ascites is a *sine qua non* of HRS. HRS = functional AKI from reduced renal perfusion (arterial haemodynamic changes, overactive endogenous vasoactive systems, systemic inflammation) that persists despite adequate fluid resuscitation and may reverse with vasoconstrictors.
- **Assess intravascular volume in every patient presenting with AKI.**
  - Clinical/haemodynamic evidence of **volume depletion** → assess response to fluid resuscitation **within 24 h**, so HRS-AKI is diagnosed and treated early.
  - **Euvolemic or volume-overloaded** → 48 h of albumin to diagnose HRS-AKI is not appropriate (fluid accumulation) and may delay terlipressin.
  - **Volume status equivocal or hard to assess** → fluid challenge of **250–500 mL crystalloid** or **1–1.5 g/kg of 20–25% albumin** may be given; no improvement in SCr and/or UO within 24 h → consider HRS-AKI.
- Seek strong evidence for an alternative **primary** cause: septic shock requiring vasopressors, acute glomerular injury, obstruction, nephrotoxin-induced AKI (where function is expected to improve after drug withdrawal). Urine sediment and damage markers may help detect acute glomerular and/or severe tubular damage; biomarker thresholds remain to be determined (Table S2, supplement).
- Isolated proteinuria may reflect comorbidity (metabolic syndrome, diabetes-related kidney disease); pre-existing CKD and/or proteinuria **does not rule out** HRS-AKI.
- A **provisional** HRS-AKI diagnosis may be made on the best available evidence and excluded later. Co-existing structural damage may still respond to vasoconstrictors; **lack of vasoconstrictor response should trigger re-evaluation for other causes but does not exclude co-existing HRS-AKI.**
- Former HRS type 1 (rapid fall in function) is most often precipitated by infection, especially SBP; variceal bleeding and LVP without sufficient albumin also implicated. Former HRS type 2 = slower, chronic decline with refractory ascites.

### Q4 — Epidemiology and outcomes (pp. 167–168)
- **Q4.1** — AKI and AKD are common in cirrhosis; prognosis depends on the severity of kidney and liver disease. *(not graded)*
- **Q4.2** — Risk of de novo CKD is high after AKI and is associated with worse clinical outcomes. *(not graded)*
- Rationale:
  - Any AKI (even **stage 1**) raises mortality at 30 days, 90 days and 1 year vs no AKI, even after recovery.
  - Strongest AKI risk factors: CKD, sepsis, SBP, ascites.
  - In-hospital RRT needed in **5–47%**, with mortality **60–80%**. RRT independence unlikely if not achieved by **3 months** post-discharge; occurs in only **26%** within 1 year.
  - AKD (by KDIGO, or AKI persisting beyond 7 days) incidence **~30%**; risk factors older age, stage 2/3 AKI, CKD, diabetes, ascites, infection, community-acquired AKI.
  - De novo CKD in **14–25%** of AKI survivors; associated with readmission, further AKI, refractory ascites, bacterial infections.

### Pathophysiology (pp. 168–169; Figure 2)
- Susceptibility follows portal hypertension: splanchnic vasodilatation (bacterial translocation, systemic inflammation) → reduced effective central blood volume → sodium/water-retaining and vasoconstrictor neurohumoral activation → ascites, extreme renal vasoconstriction, HRS-AKI.
- **Cardiac:** "cirrhotic cardiomyopathy". Nonselective beta-blockers (NSBBs) linked to more HRS-AKI and higher mortality in selected patients with refractory ascites and documented inappropriate cardiac output (CO), but two recent studies found higher CO in HRS-AKI; the key mechanism may be inability to raise CO under stress. Interventions that worsen this (NSBBs, unguided volume expansion) may impede renal recovery; whether protecting CO improves renal function is unknown.
- **Inflammation:** pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) → cytokines, vasodilators, reactive oxygen species → renal impairment, particularly in acute-on-chronic liver failure (ACLF); renal tubular Toll-like receptor 4 upregulated in AKI.
- **Bile acids:** tubular toxicity shown; bile acid and bilirubin thresholds linked to AKI in severe cholestasis largely unknown.
- **Figure 2 (p. 166) — conceptual model:** inputs = cirrhosis/portal hypertension and kidney health (NKD, AKD or CKD). Pathways: bacterial translocation → PAMPs/damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) → systemic inflammation → inflammatory cytokines, reactive oxygen species, nitric oxide → splanchnic arterial vasodilation and cardiac dysfunction → ↓effective circulating volume → ↑endogenous vasoconstrictor systems (renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system, sympathetic nervous system, antidiuretic hormone) → sodium and water retention (→ ascites, hyponatremia) and reduced renal blood flow; bile acids/bile casts act directly on the kidney. Exposures marked: angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors/angiotensin receptor blockers, NSBBs, volume overload, diuretics, bleeding, diarrhoea, LVP without albumin, infections, hepatitis B virus flare, alcohol-associated hepatitis, nephrotoxins, volume depletion. Outputs on a spectrum: **NKD** · **functional change (HRS-AKI)** · **structural damage (acute tubular injury [ATI])** · **functional + structural (HRS-AKI + ATI)**; arrows show progression and regression/recovery between phenotypes.

### Q5 — Susceptibility and trajectory (p. 168)
- **Q5.1** — Modifiable and non-modifiable factors affect susceptibility to AKI and determine severity and trajectory of recovery. *(not graded)*
- Rationale: susceptibility = liver factors (severity, ACLF, decompensation), kidney factors (CKD, baseline function), cardiovascular status (cirrhotic cardiomyopathy), comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes), external factors (modifiable: infection, liver disease aetiology, nephrotoxins, volume depletion; non-modifiable: comorbidity burden). Adaptive repair → return to normal structure; maladaptive repair (fibrosis, tubular loss, persistent inflammation) drives AKI → CKD (older age, AKI phenotype, severity, duration, frequency, baseline kidney health). Intense renal vasoconstriction and inflammation in HRS-AKI may cause sustained hypoxia and concomitant ATI (kidney biopsy findings; biomarker overlap).

### Q6 — Prevention of AKI (pp. 168–170; Figure 3; Table 1)
- **Q6.1** — **Recommend** strategies to mitigate AKI risk that include a personalised **kidney-liver health (KLH) assessment** to inform susceptibility, **nephrotoxin stewardship**, and liver-specific recommendations for anticipated and unanticipated exposures. *(best practice statement)*
- **Q6.2** — **Recommend 20–25% albumin** to prevent AKI **after LVP** and in patients **with SBP**. *(strong recommendation, grade B)* Dose and duration guided by haemodynamic and volume status. *(best practice statement)*
- **Q6.3** — **Recommend against** systematic albumin in decompensated cirrhosis (a) to prevent AKI in **non-SBP infections**, and (b) solely to maintain serum albumin **>3.0 g/dL**. *(strong recommendation, grade A)*
- Rationale:
  - ~**30%** of patients with cirrhosis have a potentially avoidable adverse drug event. Lower SCr may overestimate GFR → drug-dosing errors. Nephrotoxin stewardship = assess exposure, surveil for drug events, ensure safe use.
  - **SBP:** antibiotics + 20% albumin (arbitrary dose **1.5 g/kg day 1, 1.0 g/kg day 3**) lowered AKI and mortality vs antibiotics alone — benefit shown only with **serum bilirubin >4 mg/dL or SCr >1.0 mg/dL**. Whether all SBP needs albumin, and optimal dose/duration, undetermined.
  - Systematic albumin in non-SBP infection, or daily albumin to target >3.0 g/dL → more pulmonary oedema, no effect on AKI or survival.
  - Long-term outpatient 20–25% albumin in uncomplicated ascites: conflicting randomized controlled trials (RCTs) (ANSWER benefit possibly from more frequent visits; MACHT few completed 12 months — 10% albumin, 20% placebo — many transplanted) → **insufficient evidence** to recommend it to prevent AKI.
  - **LVP:** 20–25% albumin **6–8 g per litre over 5 L removed** lowers post-paracentesis circulatory dysfunction (trigger for HRS-AKI).
  - Transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS) controls refractory ascites and may thereby prevent HRS-AKI. The alfapump (ascites-to-bladder pump) reduces LVP frequency but is not widely available and is associated with AKI if early removal volumes are high; regular albumin may be needed.

**Table 1 (p. 170) — Strategies to prevent AKI in cirrhosis**

| Exposure | Preventive interventions |
|---|---|
| Iodinated contrast | Optimise fluid status to maintain euvolemia (no clear evidence on rate/duration of fluids) · hold diuretics **only if hypovolemic** (may continue with volume overload) · **no role** for N-acetylcysteine or urinary alkalinization with intravenous (IV) bicarbonate · contrast-induced nephrotoxicity (CIN) predisposed by female sex, ascites, advanced liver disease, infection, underlying kidney dysfunction · CIN incidence very low → **do not withhold IV contrast studies** over AKI concerns when results could change therapy |

| SBP | Systematic albumin with antibiotics · titrate albumin dose/duration daily to volume and haemodynamic status (avoid under- and over-resuscitation) · maintain mean arterial pressure (MAP) **>60–65 mmHg** in septic shock |
| Bacterial infections other than SBP | Crystalloids, preferably balanced (lactated Ringer's, PlasmaLyte), for sepsis-induced hypotension · caution with albumin (volume overload, pulmonary oedema) · fluid dose by volume/haemodynamic status · MAP **>60–65 mmHg** in septic shock |
| Nephrotoxic medications | Lessen drug-associated AKI by assessing nephrotoxic burden (number of nephrotoxins × days of exposure) · surveil for drug events; avoid over- and under-dosing of renally eliminated drugs (stop, adjust, substitute) · SCr may misrepresent function → check cystatin C (when available) |
| Major abdominal surgery | Monitor for postoperative ascites · optimise intravascular volume, avoid excess sodium · avoid NSAIDs for pain |
| alfapump | Caution with initial daily volume removed (may increase progressively; **<1 L/day** recommended) · not widely available |

### Q7 — Diagnostic work-up of AKI in cirrhosis (pp. 170–172; Figure 4)
- **Q7.1** — **Recommend** using similar diagnostic tools for AKI in cirrhosis as in patients without cirrhosis. *(best practice statement)*
- **Q7.2** — **Suggest** the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration (CKD-EPI) eGFR equation **without the race variable**, preferably **with cystatin C (CysC)**, to assess kidney function — performance at low GFR and with ascites may be suboptimal. *(weak recommendation, grade B)*
- **Q7.3** — In addition to SCr, **suggest** complementary use of **functional and damage-related markers** for timely AKI detection, AKI phenotyping and treatment guidance. *(weak recommendation, grade B)*
- Rationale — work-up and volume assessment:
  - History, intravascular volume, precipitants. Most haemodynamic monitoring tools are unstudied in cirrhosis; point-of-care ultrasound is operator-variable and hard with significant ascites.
  - Urine sediment hard to read with high bilirubin (stained cells and casts); interobserver variability and discordance with biopsy.
  - Percutaneous renal biopsy complications up to **30%** (vs 0.9% general population); transvenous route has low complication rates even with coagulopathy.
- Rationale — kidney function measures:
  - SCr misleads in cirrhosis: low muscle mass, volume of distribution in fluid overload, bilirubin interference, large-volume transfusion (falsely low).
  - **CysC** rises often **~48 h before** SCr; prognostic for renal outcome and mortality; CysC added to MELD components beat MELD for mortality prediction; helps drug dosing.
  - Modification of Diet in Renal Disease (MDRD)/CKD-EPI equations need steady-state SCr; overestimate true GFR by **10–20 mL/min/1.73 m²**, especially with GFR **<40 mL/min/1.73 m²**, ascites, or both. With GFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m², **CKD-EPI-CysC** had least bias (overestimated by 10.3 mL/min/1.73 m²). Routine CysC use encouraged, also to identify simultaneous liver-kidney transplant (SLKT) candidates.
  - 2021 race-free CKD-EPI equation: widely used in the US, acceptable preliminary performance in cirrhosis; low GFR and ascites unstudied.
  - Timed-urine creatinine clearance overestimates GFR in cirrhosis, especially GFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m².
- Rationale — biomarkers (pp. 171–172):
  - **Fractional excretion of sodium (FENa):** FENa **<1%** is common in cirrhosis even without AKI → unhelpful for ATI vs HRS-AKI. A lower threshold of **<0.1–0.2%** (many labs do not report urine sodium <20 mEq/L), combined with other urinary biomarkers and clinical judgment, may improve specificity for HRS-AKI.
  - **Urinary neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (uNGAL):** rises stepwise from HRS-AKI to ATI. A value of **~220–250 µg/g creatinine** (Bioporto assay) distinguishes ATI from other phenotypes. Terlipressin response **70%** with uNGAL **<220 µg/g creatinine** vs **33%** with **>220 µg/g creatinine**. Phenotypes overlap (heterogeneity, underlying CKD, assays, adjudicated rather than biopsy standards, functional→structural continuum).
  - Combining urinary kidney injury molecule-1, uNGAL and CysC beat any single marker for HRS-AKI, especially with clinical parameters.
  - Whether uNGAL cut-offs or terlipressin-response associations hold under the **new** HRS-AKI criteria is undetermined. Context-specific damage-marker thresholds for subclinical AKI need more data.

### Q8 — General management of AKI in cirrhosis (pp. 172–173)
- **Q8.1** — **Recommend** personalised AKI management based on the patient's kidney-liver health profile and AKI phenotype. *(best practice statement)*
- **Q8.2** — **Recommend** combining physical examination, imaging, and static and dynamic measurements to guide fluid management, with frequent reassessment through all phases of treatment to avoid volume overload. *(best practice statement)*
- **Q8.3** — **Recommend crystalloids, preferentially balanced solutions,** as **first-line** therapy for AKI needing fluid resuscitation, unless a specific indication exists for other fluids. *(strong recommendation, grade B)*
- **Q8.4** — **Recommend** stopping all fluids and starting **diuretics or RRT** in patients with AKI who show signs or symptoms of **volume overload**. *(best practice statement)*
- **Q8.5** — **Insufficient evidence** to support routine measurement of intra-abdominal pressure (IAP) in tense ascites with AKI. *(not graded)*
- **Q8.6** — **Recommend** individualised RRT initiation, considering clinical context and anticipated or observed life-threatening AKI-related complications. *(best practice statement)*
- **Q8.7** — **Recommend expedited evaluation for LT** in patients with **decompensated cirrhosis after an episode of AKI**. *(best practice statement)*
- **Q8.8** — **Insufficient evidence** to recommend TIPS or extracorporeal liver support for treating AKI. *(not graded)*
- Rationale — fluids:
  - Follow KDIGO AKI recommendations plus cirrhosis guidance: stop/avoid nephrotoxins, optimise haemodynamics and volume. No method of assessing fluid responsiveness proven superior; choice by setting (ICU vs ward). Repeated volume assessment and close UO monitoring to prevent iatrogenic overload.
  - **Fluid choice by condition:** blood products for gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding; crystalloids (preferably balanced — lactated Ringer's, PlasmaLyte) for volume depletion; **20–25% albumin for SBP or HRS-AKI**, with close attention to haemodynamics and volume.
  - RCTs in critically ill patients: albumin vs crystalloid, no difference in 30- or 90-day mortality or RRT. Two RCTs in cirrhosis with sepsis-induced hypotension conflicted (5% vs 20% albumin; 0.9% saline vs PlasmaLyte; 7 vs 28 days); albumin improved short-term haemodynamics but not renal outcomes or RRT need.
  - Tense ascites: paracentesis + albumin raised creatinine clearance in critically ill HRS (correlated with IAP fall), but LVP without albumin can impair kidney function; **no evidence** for systematic LVP in raised IAP.
- Rationale — RRT (p. 173):
  - Accelerated RRT showed no benefit in critically ill RCTs (cirrhosis excluded or underrepresented). Individualise timing to kidney and liver trajectory; consider **before** overt complications.
  - Consider **early RRT** for volume overload not responding to diuretics (even without AKI) or overload that cannot be corrected without serious adverse effects. Uraemic symptoms overlap with hepatic encephalopathy → consider earlier RRT, especially if encephalopathy persists despite treatment. Modality by availability, resources, risk.
  - Listed or under LT evaluation → RRT is a tool to optimise and **bridge to LT**. **Not LT candidates → "we recommend" discussion** with patient/caregivers of goals of therapy and poor prognosis (transplant-free survival extremely low, especially at very high MELD) — text recommendation, not a graded statement.
- Rationale — transplantation (p. 173):
  - AKI → high short-term mortality, especially at high MELD → expedited inpatient transplant evaluation may help.
  - Current **US SLKT policy**: AKI duration (**eGFR ≤25 mL/min for ≥6 weeks**, with or without dialysis) and CKD at transplant; **safety net** prioritises kidney transplant if **eGFR ≤20 mL/min within 1 year** after LT. Policy omits AKI aetiology, older age, comorbidity (diabetes). Biomarkers of post-LT renal recovery could refine SLKT decisions.
  - **Kidney transplant alone** may be feasible for selected patients on chronic dialysis with compensated cirrhosis **without clinically significant portal hypertension**, especially with a treatable liver-disease aetiology.
- Rationale — TIPS and extracorporeal support: TIPS improves GFR over time in refractory ascites but is barely studied for HRS-AKI (an RCT is under way). Adsorbent columns, albumin dialysis and plasma exchange studied for ACLF and hepatic encephalopathy, not as AKI therapies.

### Q9 — Management specific to HRS-AKI (pp. 173–174; Table 2; Figure 5)
- **Q9.1** — **Recommend** starting **vasoconstrictor therapy (terlipressin first-line) with 20–25% albumin immediately** upon establishing an HRS-AKI diagnosis. *(strong recommendation, grade A)*
- **Q9.2** — **Recommend** close monitoring of volume status during HRS-AKI treatment; adjust the albumin dose **daily** to volume status and **stop albumin immediately** if there is volume overload. *(best practice statement)*
- **Q9.3** — **Recommend** increasing the **terlipressin** dose **every 24 h** if SCr has **not decreased by 25% from baseline** *(strong recommendation, grade D)*, and increasing the **norepinephrine** dose **every 4 h** if MAP has **not increased by ≥10 mmHg** from baseline *(strong recommendation, grade B)*.
- **Q9.4** — **Recommend** stopping vasoconstrictors for HRS-AKI if: (a) SCr returns to **within 0.3 mg/dL of baseline**; (b) a severe adverse reaction develops; (c) kidney function does **not improve after 48 h on maximum tolerated doses**; (d) RRT is indicated; or (e) **maximum of 14 days** of therapy. *(strong recommendation, grade B)*
- **Q9.5** — **Recommend LT, in select patients,** as the **definitive treatment** for HRS-AKI **regardless of response** to vasoconstrictor therapy. *(strong recommendation, grade A)*

**Table 2 (p. 174) — Vasoconstrictors for HRS-AKI**

| Vasoconstrictor | Route/dose | Comments |
|---|---|---|
| Terlipressin | Continuous infusion: **2–12 mg/day**, or IV bolus: **1–2 mg every 6 h** | Ischaemic events (cardiac, peripheral, mesenteric) and pulmonary oedema · cautious use with intravascular volume overload |
| Norepinephrine | Continuous infusion: **0.5–3 mg/h** | **Second-line** if terlipressin contraindicated or unavailable · needs ICU care and central line · ischaemic events and cardiac arrhythmias |
| Midodrine + octreotide | Midodrine oral **7.5–15 mg every 8 h**; octreotide subcutaneous **100–200 µg every 8 h** | **Only** if terlipressin contraindicated **or** ICU transfer for norepinephrine not possible · midodrine may cause bradyarrhythmias |

| Table 2 section | Criteria |
|---|---|
| Discontinuation | SCr within 0.3 mg/dL of baseline · no improvement in SCr after **48–72 h** with maximal tolerated doses · serious adverse reaction · initiation of RRT · liver transplantation · total duration of **14 days** |
| Titration — terlipressin | Continuous infusion: increase by **at least 2 mg/day every 24 h** up to **12 mg/day**; IV bolus: increase from **1 to 2 mg every 6 h** — if SCr has not improved by **25%** |
| Titration — norepinephrine | Increase every **4 h** in steps of **0.5 mg/h**, up to **3 mg/h**, if no MAP increase **>10 mmHg** or UO **50 mL/h × 4 h** |
| Albumin | Daily **20–25% albumin 20–40 g/day**; adjust amount daily to volume status; **withhold** with fluid overload and/or pulmonary oedema |

- Table 2 footnotes: all vasoconstrictors given with albumin. Continuous terlipressin infusion may cause fewer side effects than IV bolus (lower cumulative daily dose). **1 vial = 0.85 mg terlipressin (North American FDA [Food and Drug Administration] label) = 1 mg terlipressin acetate.**

**Rationale (pp. 173–174):**
- Terlipressin = most studied, consistently effective; preferably by **continuous infusion**; first-line. Norepinephrine comparable to terlipressin for HRS-AKI reversal in meta-analyses, except one ACLF study where terlipressin was superior. If terlipressin unavailable or contraindicated, **norepinephrine may be more appropriate than an initial trial of midodrine + octreotide** (but needs ICU and central venous catheter).
- Albumin: current guidelines recommend daily 20–25% albumin **20–40 g/day** during HRS-AKI treatment; optimal dose and duration undefined; cautious use; stop if pulmonary oedema.
- Vasoconstrictor trials used historical (type 1) HRS definitions: better renal improvement when started at **SCr <2.25 mg/dL** and when MAP rose **≥15 mmHg**. **MAP rise ≥15 mmHg from baseline without SCr improvement → consider re-evaluation for other AKI causes.** Optimal MAP goal (absolute or rise) needs study; whether the new definitions improve reversal rates is undetermined.
- Adverse events (mainly ischaemic) usually mitigated by stopping, lowering the dose, or for terlipressin switching bolus → continuous infusion. More pulmonary oedema with terlipressin than placebo; **cautious terlipressin with volume overload**; temporarily suspending albumin + giving diuretics may avoid stopping the vasoconstrictor.
- HRS-AKI reversal lowers CKD risk and post-LT RRT need but does **not** improve transplant-free survival → vasoconstrictors are a **bridge** to LT or renal recovery, not a cure.
- **MELD effect:** treatment lowers SCr and MELD, reducing waiting-list priority (harmful where waits are long and transplant MELD is high). Some countries hold SCr at its apex after vasoconstrictor start or add points for treated HRS-AKI regardless of response; allocation policy revision needed.

**Figure 5 (p. 175) — effects of HRS-AKI drugs on vascular beds, heart, kidney and lungs** (T = terlipressin, N = norepinephrine, M = midodrine, O = octreotide)

| Site | Effect (drug) |
|---|---|
| Lungs | ↑pulmonary permeability (T) · pulmonary vasodilation (T) · pulmonary vasoconstriction (N) · pulmonary venoconstriction (T) |
| Heart | ↑preload by shunting splanchnic blood to the central circulation (mainly T) · ↓cardiac output (T) · negative chronotrope (T) · positive inotrope (N) |
| Systemic vessels | Systemic vasoconstriction (T, N >> M) |
| Liver | ↓hepatic artery flow (N) · ↓portal vein flow (T, N) |
| Splanchnic bed | Splanchnic vasoconstriction (T >> O) |
| Kidney | ↑renal perfusion pressure (T, N >> M) |

- Legend: terlipressin raises renal perfusion pressure but lowers CO; with higher preload, afterload and pulmonary vascular effects, plus large albumin doses, it may raise pulmonary oedema risk. Norepinephrine is positively inotropic and constricts pulmonary arteries without affecting pulmonary veins. Midodrine (weak systemic) and octreotide (temporary splanchnic) give only a modest rise in renal perfusion.

### Q10 — Post-AKI/AKD outpatient care bundle (pp. 174–176; Figure 6)
- **Q10.1** — **Recommend** tailoring the post-AKI/AKD outpatient care bundle to the severity of both kidney and liver disease, with close hepatology–nephrology collaboration. *(best practice statement)*
- **Q10.2** — **Recommend** personalised **palliative care evaluation** — symptom burden reduction, patient/caregiver wellbeing, goals-of-care discussions. *(best practice statement)*
- Rationale:
  - After AKI: higher risk of recurrent AKI, CKD, dialysis dependency, death. Almost **half** readmitted within **3 months**; **22%** of readmissions renal/metabolic (AKI, anasarca, hyponatremia).
  - **At minimum, a KLH assessment within 1 month of discharge**, timing individualised to risk phenotype (Figure 6). Assess cirrhosis stage and complications at the first visit. SCr may overestimate kidney function → consider serum CysC. **Routine albuminuria screening** after AKI (identifies higher CKD-progression risk).
  - KLH management domains: education, medication management (reconciliation at the first visit), disease-modifying interventions, dynamic transplant and palliative care evaluations. Assess frailty, symptom burden, health-related quality of life. More than one-third of caregivers report a major adverse impact.
  - Palliative care ≠ end-of-life care; improves advance care planning and satisfaction, lowers utilisation, no survival detriment; used in **<5%** of cirrhosis. Persistent kidney dysfunction after discharge can trigger multidisciplinary goals-of-care reassessment, especially for non-LT candidates with life expectancy under a few months.

**Figure 6 (p. 176) — post-discharge follow-up by kidney axis and liver axis**
- Six columns of increasing AKI/AKD severity; the monitoring time-line arrow runs from **"within 30 days of discharge"** (least severe, left) to **"within 7 days of discharge"** (most severe, right); per-column timing is not printed.

| Column (left → right) | Features |
|---|---|
| Short duration of stage 1 AKI | No prior CKD · no prior comorbidities · SCr fully returns to baseline |
| Short duration of stage 1 or 2 AKI | No prior CKD · stable or few comorbidities · SCr not returning to baseline |
| Prolonged stage 1 AKI or transient stage 2 AKI | No prior CKD · multiple comorbidities and advancing age · SCr persistently **>25%** above baseline |
| Prolonged stage 2 AKI (duration **≥7 days**) | Baseline CKD · multiple comorbidities (cancer, heart failure, diabetes, hypertension) · SCr persistently **>25%** above baseline |
| Stage 3 AKI and persistent other forms of AKI | Baseline CKD 4/5 · multiple comorbidities · history of prior AKI |
| Dialysis-requiring AKI with or without recovery (AKI-D) | Baseline CKD 4/5 · multiple comorbidities · history of prior AKI |

- Care providers: **non-nephrology** (primary care, hepatology) at the mild end → **nephrology-based care, transplant and palliative evaluation** at the severe end. Liver axis: compensated cirrhosis (mild end) → decompensated cirrhosis (severe end).
- Legend: "We suggest" kidney function be checked **within 1 month of discharge at a minimum**; **persistent kidney dysfunction at 90 days → formal assessment for CKD** development/progression; nephrology involvement increases with AKI/AKD duration and severity. Limited data inform this structure.

### Paediatric perspective (p. 176)
- AKI common in children with cirrhosis; cholestatic disease (biliary atresia) predominates, so adult evidence does not transfer directly.
- Reported paediatric HRS-AKI prevalence **<10%** likely a gross underestimate — no HRS-AKI definition validated in children.
- CysC superior for paediatric AKI detection; explore with damage biomarkers. Pathophysiology seems similar → paediatric HRS-AKI should respond to splanchnic vasoconstrictors such as terlipressin. Paediatric cirrhotic cardiomyopathy (partly bile-acid related) linked to continuous RRT need and post-transplant outcomes.

### Conclusion (pp. 176–177)
- Recommendations rest on data **and expert opinion**; much evidence is poor or from patients without cirrhosis, and many studies predate the new HRS-AKI definition. Integrating biomarkers that identify AKI sub-phenotypes is the priority; research questions are listed in supplementary Table S4 (not in the article).

## Contradictions / Open Questions

### Inconsistencies within the document
- **Urine output wording:** statements, Box 1 and Key points give **≤0.5 mL/kg for ≥6 h**; Figure 1 gives **<0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥6 h**.
- **AKD threshold and boundaries:** text defines AKD with SCr rise **>50%**, Figure 1 with **≥50%**. HRS-AKD is HRS for "less than 90 days" and HRS-CKD "more than 90 days" — exactly 90 days is unassigned (Figure 1 uses ≤90 / >90 days).
- **Vasoconstrictor stop rule:** Q9.4 says no improvement after **48 h** at maximum tolerated doses; Table 2 says **48–72 h**. Table 2 also lists **liver transplantation** as a stop criterion, which Q9.4 omits.
- **Norepinephrine titration:** Q9.3 escalates if MAP has not risen **≥10 mmHg**; Table 2 says no rise **>10 mmHg** and adds an alternative target, UO **50 mL/h × 4 h** (no comparison sign printed).
- **Terlipressin escalation trigger:** Q9.3 "not decreased by 25% from baseline"; Table 2 "not improved by 25%". Table 2 bolus escalation is a single step (1 → 2 mg every 6 h).
- **Second-line agent:** Table 2 makes midodrine + octreotide an option only if terlipressin is contraindicated or ICU transfer is impossible, whereas the text frames norepinephrine as the choice when terlipressin is "not available or contraindicated".
- **Force of the baseline rule:** Q2.1 "We recommend" (strong, grade D); the Key points box says the lowest stable value "may be used".
- **Graded vs ungraded:** Q3.2 is worded "We recommend the following diagnostic criteria" but labelled "not graded". Letter-grade definitions (A–D) are only in supplementary Table S1; several strong recommendations carry grade D.
- **AKI staging** is used throughout (Q2.1, Figure 1, Figure 6) but stage thresholds are never printed.

### Differences from US sources on file (as summarized on the wiki's source pages)
- **HRS-AKI criteria** — [[aga-2022-aki-cirrhosis|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2022]] (Table 1) and [[aasld-2024-aclf|American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD) 2024]] (Table 3) require no response after **2 consecutive days** of diuretic withdrawal + albumin **1 g/kg/day** (max 100 g/day), absence of shock, no current/recent nephrotoxins, and no structural injury (proteinuria >500 mg/day, >50 red blood cells per high-power field, normal ultrasound); AASLD 2024 also requires **stage 2 or higher** AKI. This document: **24 h** after adequate volume resuscitation only when clinically indicated, a **strong recommendation against** the 48-h albumin requisite (Q3.3), structural damage/proteinuria/CKD **do not exclude**, shock and nephrotoxins only as examples of an alternative primary cause, and **no stage threshold**.
- **AKI definition** — [[aga-2022-aki-cirrhosis|AGA 2022]] Best Practice Advice (BPA) 1: UO **<0.5 mL/kg/h for >6 h**; this document **≤0.5 mL/kg for ≥6 h**. [[aasld-2021-ascites-sbp-hrs]] and AASLD 2024 use the ICA SCr-only definition (no UO criterion).
- **Baseline SCr** — [[aga-2022-aki-cirrhosis|AGA 2022]]: most recent value within the prior 3 months. AASLD 2024: stable SCr within 3 months, else the closest stable value, else admission SCr. This document: **lowest** stable value in 3 months → most recent up to **12 months** → **lower of** admission SCr or eGFR-75 back-calculation.
- **When to start vasoconstrictors** — AASLD 2024 Guidance Statement (GS) 24: stage **2 or greater** HRS-AKI, no recommendation for stage 1. AGA 2022: SCr still >1.5 mg/dL (stage 1b) or ≥2× baseline after 2 days of measures. This document: **immediately** on diagnosis (Q9.1), no stage qualifier.
- **Albumin with vasoconstrictors** — AGA 2022 BPA 6: **1 g/kg day 1**, then 20–40 g/day; AASLD 2021: 1 g/kg day 1, then **40–50 g/day**. This document: **20–40 g/day**, adjusted daily, no loading dose stated.
- **Terlipressin dosing** — [[aga-2022-aki-cirrhosis|AGA 2022]] BPA 7: bolus 1 mg every 4–6 h, raised to max 2 mg every 4–6 h if SCr has not fallen ≥25% **by day 3**; infusion from 2 mg/day increased every **24–48 h** to 12 mg/day; FDA restriction SCr ≥5 mg/dL or oxygen saturation <90%. This document: bolus 1–2 mg **every 6 h**, escalation **every 24 h**; does not mention the FDA SCr/oxygen restrictions or the AASLD 2024 GS 25 exclusion of ACLF-3.
- **Second-line agent** — [[aga-2022-aki-cirrhosis|AGA 2022]] BPA 6: midodrine + octreotide **or** norepinephrine by institutional preference; [[aasld-2021-ascites-sbp-hrs|AASLD 2021]]: norepinephrine, then midodrine + octreotide if neither available. This document: norepinephrine second-line, midodrine + octreotide only if terlipressin is contraindicated or ICU transfer impossible.
- **Midodrine dose** — [[aga-2022-aki-cirrhosis|AGA 2022]] BPA 8: 7.5 mg titrated to 12.5 mg three times daily; [[aasld-2021-ascites-sbp-hrs|AASLD 2021]]: 5–15 mg every 8 h. This document: **7.5–15 mg every 8 h**.
- **Norepinephrine target** — [[aga-2022-aki-cirrhosis|AGA 2022]] BPA 9: MAP ↑ ≥10 mmHg and/or UO >50 mL/h for ≥4 h; [[aasld-2021-ascites-sbp-hrs|AASLD 2021]]: MAP ↑ ≥10 mmHg or UO >200 mL/4 h. This document: MAP ↑ ≥10 mmHg (Q9.3) or UO 50 mL/h × 4 h (Table 2).
- **Stopping / response** — [[aga-2022-aki-cirrhosis|AGA 2022]]: continue until 24 h after SCr is within ≤0.3 mg/dL of baseline for 2 consecutive days, or 14 days. [[aasld-2021-ascites-sbp-hrs|AASLD 2021]] Statement 55: response = SCr <1.5 mg/dL or within 0.3 mg/dL of baseline over ≤14 days; may stop if SCr stays at or above pretreatment over **4 days** at maximum tolerated doses. This document: stop when SCr returns within 0.3 mg/dL, or after **48 h** (Table 2: 48–72 h) without improvement, or at 14 days.
- **Fluid for AKI** — [[aga-2024-vasoactive-albumin-cirrhosis]] BPA 8: IV albumin is the volume expander of choice in hospitalized cirrhosis with ascites and AKI. This document: **crystalloids (balanced) first-line** (Q8.3, strong, grade B), albumin for SBP or HRS-AKI. Closer to AASLD 2024 GS 14 and [[aasld-easl-2026-aclf-global-consensus]] Statement 46 (balanced crystalloids for shock; albumin for established indications).
- **uNGAL cut-off** — [[aga-2022-aki-cirrhosis|AGA 2022]]: 220–244 µg/g creatinine; [[aasld-2021-ascites-sbp-hrs|AASLD 2021]]: 220 µg/g creatinine (particle-enhanced turbidimetry) or 365 ng/mL (enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay). This document: ~220–250 µg/g creatinine.
- **SLKT safety net** — [[aki-in-cirrhosis]] (from its US sources) prints GFR **<20 mL/min**; this document **eGFR ≤20 mL/min within 1 year** after LT.
- **Agreement** — expedited LT evaluation after AKI (Q8.7) matches [[aasld-2021-ascites-sbp-hrs|AASLD 2021]] Statement 57 (all AKI considered for urgent LT evaluation); TIPS not a treatment for HRS-AKI (AGA 2022 BPA 13; AASLD 2021); terlipressin first-line; 14-day maximum; RRT individualised; former type 2 HRS now classed with CKD.
- **ACLF kidney failure** — the 2026 multisociety consensus defines kidney failure as KDIGO stage 2–3 or new RRT; this document gives no ACLF organ-failure definition.
