## Summary

Comprehensive evidence-based guideline covering diagnosis, screening, surveillance, and endoscopic/medical therapy of Barrett's esophagus (BE). Key updates from the prior ACG BE guideline include: (1) broadened screening modalities to include nonendoscopic capsule sponge devices with biomarkers; (2) liberalized nondysplastic BE (NDBE) surveillance intervals stratified by segment length (<3 cm every 5 yr vs ≥3 cm every 3 yr); (3) volume criteria for endoscopic eradication therapy (EET) centers; (4) EET recommended for both high-grade dysplasia (HGD) and low-grade dysplasia (LGD); and (5) structured post-complete eradication of intestinal metaplasia (CEIM) surveillance intervals based on preablation histology.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Diagnosis

- BE requires intestinal metaplasia (IM) with goblet cells in the tubular esophagus (conditional, very low quality). IM-positive BE carries hazard ratio (HR) 3.54 for esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC) vs columnar-lined esophagus without IM in the largest population-based study (N Ireland registry, n=8,522).
- Minimum columnar length of 1 cm for BE diagnosis using Prague C&M criteria. Prague criteria: circumferential (C) and maximal (M) extent; reliability coefficients 0.95 and 0.94 respectively for segments ≥1 cm; only 0.22 for <1 cm.
- No biopsies for normal or irregular Z line (<1 cm) in absence of mucosal abnormality. No cases of HGD/EAC progression were observed in cohort studies of irregular Z line patients.
- At least 8 biopsies at screening; Seattle protocol (4-quadrant biopsies every 2 cm) for segments >4 cm. For short segments where 8 biopsies are unobtainable: at least 4 biopsies/cm circumferential BE and 1 biopsy/cm in tongues.
- All dysplasia (any grade) must be confirmed by a second gastrointestinal (GI) pathologist (strong, low quality). Community LGD is downstaged 73% on expert review; confirmed LGD progresses at 9.1%/pt-year vs 0.6%/pt-year for downstaged-to-NDBE.

### Screening

- Single screening [[upper-endoscopy|endoscopy]] for patients with chronic [[gerd|gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)]] symptoms plus ≥3 additional risk factors: male sex, age >50 yr, White race, tobacco smoking, obesity, family history of BE or EAC in a first-degree relative (conditional, very low quality).
- Nonendoscopic capsule sponge device (e.g., Cytosponge) + biomarker (TFF3 or methylated DNA markers) is an acceptable alternative screening modality (conditional, very low quality). Cytosponge-TFF3 sensitivity 80%, specificity 92% in enriched case-control populations.
- No repeat endoscopic screening after a negative index examination (conditional, low quality). Exception: esophagitis Los Angeles (LA) grade B or worse may mask BE; repeat esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD) after 8–12 wk of proton pump inhibitor (PPI) healing is appropriate.

### Surveillance

- White light endoscopy AND chromoendoscopy (acetic acid or electronic/virtual chromoendoscopy) at every surveillance examination (strong, moderate quality). Narrow-band imaging (NBI)-targeted biopsies: pooled sensitivity 94.2%, specificity 94.4% for dysplasia/EAC.
- Structured Seattle protocol biopsy: 4-quadrant biopsies every ≤2 cm throughout BE segment; community adherence only 49–51%.
- Surveillance intervals for NDBE stratified by segment length (strong, moderate quality):
  - NDBE <3 cm: EGD every 5 years
  - NDBE ≥3 cm: EGD every 3 years
- Indefinite for dysplasia (IND): confirm with expert pathologist; optimize PPI to twice daily; repeat EGD in 6 months. If persistent IND: EGD annually.
- Confirmed LGD (surveillance pathway): EGD at 6 months, 12 months, then annually. Revert to NDBE intervals if dysplasia resolves.
- Cease surveillance when patient is no longer a candidate for EET or has <5-year life expectancy. Discussion at approximately age 75 is reasonable.

### Medical Therapy

- PPI at least once daily for all BE patients without contraindications (conditional, very low quality). Meta-analysis: PPI associated with 71% reduction in HGD/EAC risk (adjusted odds ratio [OR] 0.29).
- No recommendation on aspirin (ASA) + PPI combination for chemoprevention (AspECT trial: high-dose PPI superior to low-dose for time-to-event composite; ASA benefit uncertain when nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) not excluded).
- [[antireflux-surgery|Antireflux surgery]] is NOT recommended as antineoplastic therapy in BE (conditional, low quality).

### Endoscopic Therapy (EET)

- EET recommended over esophagectomy for HGD or intramucosal cancer (IMC) (strong, moderate quality). Comparable 5-year survival; EET has markedly lower adverse event rate (relative risk [RR] 0.38 vs esophagectomy).
- EET suggested for confirmed LGD (conditional, moderate quality). SURF trial (randomized controlled trial [RCT]): radiofrequency ablation (RFA) reduced HGD/EAC progression from 26.5% to 1.5% (P<0.001) vs surveillance over median follow-up.
- Endoscopic resection (ER) of any visible lesion before ablation (conditional, very low quality). Endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR) upstages/downstages histology in 30–40% of patients; changes management in ~30%. [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]] not routinely indicated for HGD/LGD staging.
- EMR techniques: cap-assisted EMR or multiband EMR (preferred in most cases); endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) for larger lesions or post-ablation scarring.
- RFA is the primary ablative modality (widest evidence base). Cryotherapy (spray or balloon) is an acceptable alternative, especially for RFA-refractory cases. Complete eradication of dysplasia (CED)/CEIM rates for cryotherapy: 81–88%/57–61% (spray); 76%/72% (cryoballoon).
- EET at high-volume centers (conditional, very low quality). Volume-outcome effect documented; European Society of Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ESGE) threshold: ≥10 new HGD/early EAC patients/yr per endoscopist.
- Mandatory post-CEIM surveillance (strong, moderate quality). Annual IM recurrence rate after CEIM: 8.6–10.5%; dysplastic recurrence 2.0%; HGD/EAC recurrence 1.2%. Most recurrences (75%) at gastroesophageal junction (GEJ); 80% of GEJ recurrences are invisible endoscopically.

**Post-CEIM surveillance intervals (by worst pretreatment histology):**

| Pretreatment histology | Post-CEIM surveillance |
|---|---|
| LGD | 1 yr → 3 yr → every 2 yr thereafter |
| HGD | 3 mo → 6 mo → 12 mo → annually thereafter |
| IMC | 3 mo → 6 mo → 12 mo → annually thereafter |

### Biomarkers (no recommendation made)

- p53 immunohistochemistry (IHC): OR 4–17 for progression; sensitivity 62%, specificity 80% (overexpression); insufficient for routine recommendation due to methodologic limitations.
- TissueCypher: 15-biomarker tissue systems pathology assay; sensitivity/specificity for HGD/EAC within 5 yr: 30%/95% in NDBE. No routine recommendation.
- WATS-3D: Abrasive cytology brush with 3D artificial intelligence (AI) analysis; adds 2% absolute increase in dysplasia detection vs forceps biopsy alone; no recommendation pending chromoendoscopy-plus-WATS-3D data.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- IM requirement for BE diagnosis is contested: UK and Asia-Pacific guidelines do not require goblet cells; two UK cohort studies found no difference in EAC risk with or without IM. The ACG maintains the IM requirement based primarily on the N Ireland registry data.
- WATS-3D data are promising but all studies to date used white light only; no chromoendoscopy + WATS-3D data exist. The incremental yield of WATS-3D may include false positives.
- ASA + PPI chemoprevention remains unresolved: AspECT trial showed benefit driven by all-cause mortality, not cancer-specific outcomes, and was not double-blinded.
- Optimal age to cease surveillance is not defined; modeling suggests 69–81 yr range depending on sex and comorbidities.
- Subsquamous buried BE after RFA: yield of random biopsies from normal neosquamous epithelium is low (~1% any recurrence); significance of nondysplastic IM recurrence at GEJ after CEIM remains unclear.
- T1b sm1 EAC: expanding role for EET in select patients (negative deep margin, well-moderate differentiation, no lymphovascular invasion [LVI], invasion <500 µm into submucosa); not yet standard of care.
