# AGA Guideline: Endoscopic Eradication Therapy (EET) of Barrett's Esophagus (2024)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Year:** 2024
- **Journal:** Gastroenterology (AGA Clinical Practice Guideline)
- **Type:** GRADE-based guideline

---

## Summary

This guideline defines when to use **endoscopic eradication therapy (EET)** — endoscopic resection of visible lesions plus ablation (e.g., RFA) — for dysplastic [[barretts-esophagus]] and early neoplasia, and how to survey afterward. It complements the separate Barrett's surveillance guideline and sits within [[endoscopic-oncology]].

---

## Key Recommendations

- **Rec 1 (Strong):** In BE with **high-grade dysplasia (HGD)**, **EET over surveillance**.
- **Rec 2 (Conditional):** In BE with **low-grade dysplasia (LGD)**, suggests **EET over surveillance** (patients prioritizing avoidance of procedural risk may reasonably choose surveillance).
- **Rec 3 (Conditional):** In **non-dysplastic BE (NDBE)**, suggests **against routine EET**.
- **Rec 4–5:** **Endoscopic resection (EMR/ESD) of visible/neoplastic lesions** before/with ablation; post-EET surveillance after complete eradication of intestinal metaplasia (CEIM) at defined intervals (e.g., HGD: 3, 6, 12 months then annually; LGD: years 1 and 3).

---

## Clinical Relevance

EET is standard of care for HGD and favored for LGD; NDBE should not be routinely ablated. Resect visible lesions first, then ablate, then survey. Tightly linked to [[barretts-esophagus]] and dysplasia management via [[endoscopic-oncology]]; upstream control of [[gerd]] remains foundational.
