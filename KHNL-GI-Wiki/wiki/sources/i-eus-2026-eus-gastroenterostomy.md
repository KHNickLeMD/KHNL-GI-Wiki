## Summary

The first consensus devoted specifically to **endoscopic ultrasound-guided gastroenterostomy (EUS-GE)** and the related EUS-guided anastomoses. Earlier guidance addressed EUS-GE only in passing, usually inside broader interventional-EUS documents and often by extrapolation from limited observational data; indications, technical standards and periprocedural care were correspondingly heterogeneous between centres. A panel of **66 international experts** (therapeutic endoscopy and EUS, plus surgery and radiology) developed statements through a modified Delphi process, with literature searched to **30 April 2025**. Questions with a defined comparator and extractable evidence went through structured evidence synthesis and **GRADE**; procedural, technical and organizational questions were classified as **consensus-based**. Consensus was pre-defined at **≥85% agreement**, and statements below that threshold were **not endorsed but are still reported**, which makes the document unusually useful for knowing where expert opinion genuinely splits.

**Of 25 draft statements, 20 reached consensus and 5 did not. Six are GRADE-based; 14 are consensus-based.** The headline comparative findings concern malignant gastric outlet obstruction (GOO): EUS-GE is **recommended over duodenal stenting** (the only *strong* recommendation in the document) on the strength of a markedly lower reintervention rate, and **suggested over surgical bypass** on comparable efficacy with fewer overall adverse events. Both rest on low-quality evidence — the pooled comparative analyses were entirely observational, because the two multicentre randomized trials (ENDURO and the trial by Bang et al.) were published after the evidence cutoff and so could not be included; the panel notes both are consistent with the recommendation's direction but did not use them to modify its wording, strength or certainty.

The technical section standardizes the parts of the procedure that drive adverse events: **an enteric catheter for target-loop distension** (100% agreement), **saline** as the distension fluid with optional dye and contrast, **freehand LAMS deployment** rather than over-the-wire, **no routine intraprocedural balloon dilation of the stent**, and performance **in a fluoroscopy-equipped room**. Periprocedural care is deliberately lean: **same-day liquid diet**, **no routine post-procedural imaging** before resuming oral intake, and antithrombotic optimization before the procedure. Expanded indications — selected benign GOO, EDGE/EDGI in Roux-en-Y gastric bypass, and afferent limb syndrome — are all conditional and all require multidisciplinary discussion.

### The 20-mm LAMS statement carries a post-Delphi safety footnote

Statement 2.3 was voted **before** recall/removal notices were issued affecting selected **20 × 10-mm AXIOS / Hot AXIOS configurations and batches**, prompted by increased reports of deployment and expansion problems during stent delivery. The document states explicitly that the notices do **not** establish a class-wide disadvantage of the 20-mm diameter, that **affected devices must not be used**, and that **the statement should not be read as establishing the 20-mm LAMS as a currently preferred standard**. Current selection should comply with applicable regulatory notices and be individualized to device availability, patient anatomy, procedural indication and operator experience.

### Comparative outcome data

**EUS-GE vs enteral (duodenal) stenting** — pooled comparative evidence:
- Clinical success **RR 1.10 (95% CI 1.05–1.15)**.
- **Reintervention RR 0.18 (95% CI 0.11–0.29)** — the consistent, robust finding, attributed to creating a gastroenteric bypass remote from the tumour, so recurrent obstruction from ingrowth or overgrowth is far less likely.
- The first RCT showed a **6-month reintervention rate of 4% vs 29%**.
- Overall adverse events not increased: **RR 0.58 (95% CI 0.34–1.00)**, with substantial heterogeneity; complications after EUS-GE may be **more severe when they occur**.

**EUS-GE vs surgical gastroenterostomy** — meta-analysis performed for this consensus:
- Overall (benign and malignant): higher clinical success **OR 2.20 (1.04–4.62)**; significantly lower total adverse events **OR 0.20 (0.13–0.30)**. No clear difference in severe adverse events (OR 0.53, 0.23–1.19) or reintervention (OR 0.63, 0.20–1.99).
- **Malignant-only subgroup:** clinical success **comparable** (OR 1.14, 0.63–2.09); total adverse events significantly lower with EUS-GE (**OR 0.25, 0.13–0.46**); no clear difference in severe adverse events (OR 0.86, 0.12–6.06) or reintervention (OR 0.97, 0.25–3.76).

**Overall EUS-GE performance** (meta-analysis of 36 studies, 1846 patients): technical success **96.9%**, clinical success **90.6%**, overall adverse events **13.0%**, **LAMS misdeployment 4.6%** (the majority of events), severe adverse events **1.2%**, procedure-related mortality **0.3%**.

**Benign GOO** (largest meta-analysis, 376 patients, 15 studies): technical success **95.8%**, clinical success **93.4%**, adverse events **11.6%**, recurrence **11.6%** at median 14 months.

**EDGE in RYGB:** pooled technical success **96% (92%–97.6%)**, clinical success **91% (85%–95%)**, overall adverse events ~**17% (14%–24%)**. Versus comparators, technical success EDGE and LA-ERCP **96% and 93% vs EA-ERCP 77%** (P<0.01), ERCP completion **93% and 92% vs 64%** (P<0.01), with no significant difference in overall adverse events (19%, 20%, 13%; P=0.49). Most frequent adverse event is **LAMS dislodgement**, mitigated by larger-diameter (20 mm) stents and fixation techniques. **LA-ERCP remains preferable when concomitant cholecystectomy is required.**

**EDGI (non-ERCP interventions through the gastrogastrostomy):** technical success **98% (97%–100%)**, clinical success **99% (99%–100%)**, adverse events ~**10% (6%–13%)**.

**Afferent limb syndrome:** technical success **96% (93%–100%)**, clinical success **95% (91%–99%)**, pooled adverse events **7% (3%–11%)**.

### LAMS misdeployment — the Ghandour classification

Proposed from a multicentre cohort of **467 EUS-GE procedures**, categorizing misdeployment by **flange position and the presence of enterotomy**, each with a different management strategy. Misdeployment is the **main cause of technical failure and of adverse events**.

| Type | What happened | Management |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Distal flange in the peritoneal cavity, **without** bowel injury. The **most frequent** subtype. | Usually amenable to endoscopic management — LAMS removal, redo-anastomosis, or rescue enteral stenting, **with closure of the gastric defect** to prevent leakage. |
| **II** | Distal flange released in the peritoneum **despite successful bowel puncture** (i.e. with enterotomy). | Endoscopic rescue using stent-in-stent or NOTES-based approaches may be feasible in selected cases; **urgent surgery is required in a non-negligible proportion — up to 7%**. |
| **III** | **Proximal** flange deployed in the peritoneal cavity. | Typically prevented by intrachannel release technique; **most often requires surgical management**. |
| **IV** | **Gastrocolonic anastomosis** — inadvertent deployment between stomach and colon. ~**4%** of misdeployments. | Prevention relies on careful loop identification and controlled fluid instillation. Management is **usually delayed, allowing fistula tract maturation**, followed by endoscopic LAMS removal and defect closure, often with over-the-scope clips. |

**Immediate broad-spectrum systemic antibiotics are recommended in cases of LAMS misdeployment**, and early recognition with prompt multidisciplinary evaluation is critical — **delayed diagnosis is associated with significantly worse clinical outcomes**.

### Technique detail (Figures 1 and 2)

**Jejunal distension:**
- Advance an enteric catheter — nasojejunal feeding tube or nasobiliary catheter — across the stricture and **beyond the ligament of Treitz**; infuse saline continuously or intermittently until a **stable fluid-filled loop with adequate luminal apposition** is seen on EUS.
- **Direct needle puncture with saline instillation should be reserved** for cases where catheter or endoscope advancement beyond the obstruction is not feasible (tight or serrated stricture) — an over-the-wire approach after needle puncture carries **lower technical success and a higher risk of stent misdeployment** than freehand deployment.
- **Visualizing the catheter within the jejunal lumen on EUS** helps confirm the correct loop and **reduces the risk of colonic mispuncture**.
- **Saline is preferred over water** to minimize electrolyte disturbance including hyponatraemia. **Dye (e.g. methylene blue)** aids endoscopic confirmation of correct intraluminal deployment of the proximal flange; **radiopaque contrast** delineates small-bowel anatomy under fluoroscopy. **Antiperistaltic agents** — hyoscine butylbromide or glucagon — may be given intravenously to reduce bowel motility.
- No data demonstrate clinically meaningful superiority of one fluid composition over another.

**Jejunal access and stent deployment:**
- **Freehand (wireless) technique preferred** — the distended loop is punctured directly with an electrocautery-enhanced LAMS without guidewire placement. Fewer device exchanges, shorter procedure time, better target-loop stability. Three retrospective comparative studies show higher technical success and fewer adverse events, with **stent misdeployment the main driver of complications in the over-the-wire approach**.
- Over-the-wire access should be avoided routinely: **guidewire manipulation may destabilize the target loop** and increase misdeployment risk.
- Formalized as the **Wireless EUS-GE Simplified Technique (WEST)**.
- **No routine intraprocedural balloon dilation** — spontaneous gradual expansion is sufficient, and dilation has been associated with **stent dislodgement and perforation**; reported dilation balloon diameters ranged 10–18 mm across studies, with no comparative study against no dilation.

**Learning curve:** available single- and multicentre data show most adverse events and misdeployments occur in the early learning phase, with progressive reduction after approximately **40 procedures**. The panel judged this evidence **insufficient to define a universal credentialing threshold or minimum institutional case volume**, and deliberately framed Statement 1.2 around prior experience and supervised training rather than a numerical cutoff — the figure of ~25–40 procedures should be read as **an estimate of proficiency development, not a credentialing requirement**.

### Ascites definitions used

The panel's categories are explicitly **pragmatic rather than evidence-based**, because published EUS-GE studies have used no uniform ascites grading system:
- **Mild** — small-volume, asymptomatic, usually detected intraprocedurally.
- **Moderate** — clinically relevant or drainable; may benefit from preprocedural paracentesis.
- **Severe** — tense, recurrent, or extensive, mandating drainage before any EUS-GE attempt.

Reported infective complication rates in patients with ascites are low, possibly reflecting systematic periprocedural antibiotic prophylaxis per ESGE guidance. Most published cases used the **EUS-guided double-balloon-occluded gastrojejunostomy bypass (EPASS)** technique, which may stabilize the loop in the presence of intraperitoneal fluid. Ascites may also mark more advanced disease — carcinomatosis or portal vein thrombosis — which carries poorer long-term outcomes and increased risk of recurrent obstruction.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Sharpens rather than contradicts [[asge-2021-gastroduodenal-obstruction]].** ASGE 2021 made three conditional recommendations and **preferred no modality**, leaving the operative content in its qualifying comments. This consensus takes a position: **EUS-GE over duodenal stenting (strong)** and **over surgical bypass (conditional)** for malignant GOO. It is newer and specific to the procedure, so it governs; the ASGE positions are not contradicted, only left behind as less specific.
- **Does not resolve the [[aga-2021-malignant-alimentary-tract-obstruction]] vs [[asge-2021-gastroduodenal-obstruction]] life-expectancy disagreement** (>2 months vs 6 months for stent-vs-surgery). This document frames the choice around EUS-GE rather than life expectancy and issues **no life-expectancy threshold of its own**, so that open question stands as recorded on [[gastric-outlet-obstruction]].
- **The two randomized trials fall outside the evidence base.** ENDURO and Bang et al. were published after the 30 April 2025 cutoff and were **not pooled or GRADE-assessed**; the panel reports both as consistent with the recommendation's direction but did not let them modify wording, strength or certainty. The wiki already holds [[kastelijn-2023-enduro-protocol]] — so the page carries a *recommendation whose certainty rating deliberately excludes the randomized evidence the wiki also holds*.
- **Five questions have no answer and should not be given one:** whether peritoneal carcinomatosis contraindicates EUS-GE (80%), whether orotracheal intubation is required (67% — the lowest agreement in the document, reflecting interinstitutional variation in anaesthetic practice rather than conflicting evidence), whether antibiotic prophylaxis is required (80%), EDEE in non-RYGB altered anatomy (82%), and EUS-guided coloenterostomy (82%). The panel noted that **many centres do give a single dose of prophylactic broad-spectrum antibiotic**, particularly for complex procedures, higher infection risk, or ascites — but declined to recommend it.
- **Statement 2.3 is partially overtaken by events** — see the post-Delphi recall footnote above. This is a case where **the document itself tells the reader not to apply its own recommendation as written**.
- **Almost all evidence is observational.** Only 6 of 20 endorsed statements could be GRADE-assessed; the rest rest on structured expert consensus, and the GRADE-rated ones are all **low or very low quality**.
