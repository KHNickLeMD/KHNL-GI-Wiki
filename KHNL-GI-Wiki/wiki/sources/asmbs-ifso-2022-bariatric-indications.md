## Summary

A joint ASMBS–IFSO statement that **replaces the 1991 National Institutes of Health (NIH) Consensus Statement** as the eligibility standard for metabolic and bariatric surgery (MBS). The 1991 threshold — **body mass index (BMI) ≥40 kg/m², or ≥35 kg/m² with co-morbidities** — had been applied universally by providers, hospitals and insurers for three decades. The 2022 statement lowers and simplifies it.

The document is **narrative, not GRADE-rated**: it numbers nothing and assigns no strength or evidence grade to any statement. Its operative content is a **"Major updates to 1991 NIH guidelines" box** (5 bullets) at the front and an **11-bullet Conclusion** at the back; the body sections argue each one. Both lists are reproduced below with the document's own wording and thresholds.

Beyond the BMI thresholds it does three things that change referral practice: it **removes the upper age limit**, it treats several formerly-excluding conditions (very high BMI, compensated cirrhosis, heart failure, end-stage organ disease awaiting transplant) as **reasons to operate rather than reasons to decline**, and it states that **insurance-mandated preoperative weight loss has no supporting data** and should not gate the operation.

> Operators below are as printed in the document; note the asymmetry — **BMI thresholds are ≥**, while the **pediatric percentile thresholds are >**.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Major updates to the 1991 NIH guidelines (the document's own front box, 5 statements)
1. **MBS is recommended for individuals with BMI ≥35 kg/m², regardless of the presence, absence or severity of co-morbidities.**
2. **MBS should be considered for individuals with metabolic disease and BMI 30–34.9 kg/m².**
3. **BMI thresholds should be adjusted in the Asian population** — BMI **≥25 kg/m²** suggests clinical obesity, and individuals with BMI **≥27.5 kg/m²** should be offered MBS.
4. Long-term results of MBS consistently demonstrate safety and efficacy.
5. Appropriately selected **children and adolescents** should be considered for MBS.

### Conclusion statements (11, ungraded and unnumbered in the original; numbering here is the wiki's)
1. Long-term data consistently show the **safety, efficacy and durability** of MBS for clinically severe obesity and its co-morbidities, with **decreased mortality** compared with nonoperative treatment.
2. **MBS is recommended for BMI ≥35 kg/m²**, regardless of presence, absence or severity of co-morbidities.
3. **MBS is recommended in patients with type 2 diabetes (T2D) and BMI ≥30 kg/m².**
4. **MBS should be considered at BMI 30–34.9 kg/m²** in those who **do not achieve substantial or durable weight loss or co-morbidity improvement using nonsurgical methods**.
5. BMI definitions **do not apply similarly to all populations**; clinical obesity in the Asian population is recognized at **BMI >25 kg/m²**, and access to MBS **should not be denied solely on traditional BMI risk zones**.
6. **There is no upper patient-age limit.** Older individuals who could benefit should be considered after careful assessment of **co-morbidities and frailty**.
7. **Carefully selected individuals considered higher risk for general surgery may benefit from MBS.**
8. **Children and adolescents** with BMI **>120% of the 95th percentile** *and* a **major co-morbidity**, or BMI **>140% of the 95th percentile**, should be considered for MBS **after evaluation by a multidisciplinary team in a specialty center**.
9. MBS is effective in patients who **need other specialty surgery** — joint arthroplasty, abdominal wall hernia repair, or organ transplantation.
10. A **multidisciplinary team** helps manage modifiable risk factors; **the ultimate decision for surgical readiness is the surgeon's**.
11. Severe obesity is a **chronic disease requiring long-term management after primary MBS**, which may include **revisional surgery or other adjuvant therapy**.

### Supporting detail from the body sections
- **Why the BMI 30–34.9 band moved.** Medical weight loss is considered more durable below BMI 35 than at or above it, so a trial of nonsurgical therapy is recommended first in this band — but it is **not** required above BMI 35, where nonsurgical options are described as ineffective for substantial sustained loss.
- **Asian populations:** BMI does not capture visceral adiposity; cardiometabolic risk occurs at a lower BMI, so the obesity risk zones shift to **25–27.5 kg/m²**.
- **Older adults:** complications are slightly higher in septuagenarians, but **co-morbidity burden and choice of operation predict 30-day adverse outcomes better than age**; **frailty**, not age, is independently associated with complications.
- **Children/adolescents:** MBS **does not impair pubertal development or linear growth**, so **no specific Tanner stage or bone age is required**. Syndromic obesity, developmental delay, autism spectrum disorder and a history of trauma are **increasingly not** treated as contraindications.
- **BMI >60 kg/m²:** no consensus on the best operation; MBS is **safe and effective** here and is the **preferred method** of achieving clinically significant weight loss at extreme BMI. (Mortality risk rises with BMI and older studies implicated BMI >50 kg/m²; newer series found no significant difference above vs below 60, and MBS has been performed safely above 70.)
- **Cirrhosis:** obesity carries a **3-fold increase in risk of decompensation** in known cirrhosis; MBS is associated with an **88% risk reduction in progression of nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) to cirrhosis**. **Compensated cirrhosis** raises perioperative mortality but it **remains <1%**, and the benefit is significant. **Data in clinically significant portal hypertension are sparse** — patient and procedure selection are what determine outcome.
- **Bridge to other treatment:** joint arthroplasty (some orthopedic societies discourage hip/knee replacement above BMI 40); **large chronic abdominal wall hernia** — MBS first as a staged procedure before definitive repair; **organ transplantation** — >50% of patients with end-stage renal disease and severe obesity are listed for kidney transplant within 5 years of MBS, and MBS is safe and effective as a bridge to liver transplantation in selected otherwise-ineligible patients.
- **Heart failure:** MBS before transplant or left ventricular assist device (LVAD) placement is performed with low morbidity/mortality; prior MBS was associated with a **3-fold higher probability of later heart transplantation** in an LVAD cohort.
- **Patient evaluation:** multidisciplinary team with medical, surgical, psychiatric and nutritional expertise (carried forward from 1991); **nutritional assessment by a registered dietitian** with MBS expertise, before and after; mental-health evaluation, since depression, binge-eating disorder and substance use are more prevalent in MBS candidates.
- **Insurance-mandated preoperative weight loss has no supporting data** and is described as **discriminatory, arbitrary and scientifically unfounded**, causing attrition and delay of lifesaving treatment.
- **Operations:** sleeve gastrectomy and Roux-en-Y gastric bypass account for ~**90%** of operations performed worldwide; vertical banded gastroplasty is historical, adjustable gastric banding much diminished. **Perioperative mortality 0.03%–0.2%.**

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **Cirrhosis with clinically significant portal hypertension (CSPH).** [[aga-2018-surgical-risk-perioperative-cirrhosis]] states plainly that **CSPH is a contraindication** to bariatric surgery, and [[aasld-2023-nafld]] makes decompensated cirrhosis an absolute contraindication. ASMBS-IFSO 2022 does not contradict those positions outright but is markedly more permissive — it reports only a "paucity of data" in CSPH and frames careful selection, not exclusion. **The GI/hepatology sources keep [[bariatric-surgery]] and [[cirrhosis]]**: ASMBS-IFSO is a surgical-society statement, is ungraded, and the hepatology guidance is both more specific to the liver decision and (AASLD) newer.
- **BMI ≥35 with no co-morbidity.** The 1991 NIH criterion most clinicians and payers still apply (≥40, or ≥35 with co-morbidities) is **superseded by this document**, not by any guideline currently on the wiki's pages. Readers carrying the old rule should be told which one they are using.
- **No grading.** The document assigns no evidence grade or recommendation strength to any statement, so none can be cited as graded.
