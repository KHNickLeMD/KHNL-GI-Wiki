## Summary
- **Question:** does teduglutide, a glucagon-like peptide 2 (GLP-2) analogue, at **0.05 mg/kg/d** with an earlier and more aggressive weaning protocol, reduce parenteral support (PS; parenteral nutrition [PN] and/or intravenous [IV] fluids, together PN/IV) in adults with short bowel syndrome (SBS) with intestinal failure (SBS-IF)? (Introduction, p. 1474)
- **Design:** 86 adults randomized 1:1, teduglutide **0.05 mg/kg/d subcutaneously once daily** vs placebo for **24 weeks**, after a PS optimization period (up to 8 weeks) and a 4- to 8-week stabilization period; PS cut by protocol when 48-hour urine output rose over baseline (Abstract; Study Design, pp. 1474–1475).
- **Primary result:** responders (**20%–100%** reduction in weekly PS volume from baseline at week 20, maintained at week 24) **27/43 (63%) vs 13/43 (30%), P = .002** (Results, p. 1476).
- **Volume:** week 24 mean PS reduction **4.4 ± 3.8 L/wk** (from 12.9 ± 7.8) vs **2.3 ± 2.7 L/wk** (from 13.2 ± 7.4), **P < .001**; ≥1 day/week off PS **54% vs 23%** (P = .005); **no patient completely weaned from PS** at week 24 (Results, p. 1476).
- **Safety:** no deaths; adverse events (AEs) leading to discontinuation **2/42 vs 3/43**, none serious; gastrointestinal AEs most frequent; two treatment-related serious AEs on teduglutide — **acute cholecystitis** and **small intestinal stenosis**, both resolved (Safety and Tolerability, p. 1478).
- **Authors' conclusion:** 24 weeks of teduglutide was generally well tolerated and reduced volumes and number of days of PS in SBS-IF (Abstract, p. 1473).

## Key Findings / Claims

### Eligibility (Table 1, p. 1474; Patients, p. 1474; Supplementary Table 1, p. 1481.e3)

| Inclusion (Table 1) | Exclusion (Table 1) |
|---|---|
| Age **≥18 y** (Patients, p. 1474) | Cancer within last **5 y** |
| SBS from intestinal failure caused by a **major intestinal resection** (eg, injury, cancer, Crohn's disease, vascular disease, volvulus) | Body mass index **<15 kg/m²** |
| **≥12 continuous months** of PS dependency (PN and/or IV fluids) before consent | Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) on immunosuppressant therapy introduced or changed within last **3 mo**, or biologics within last **6 mo** |
| PS required **≥3 times weekly** to meet caloric, fluid or electrolyte needs | Previous teduglutide |
| Crohn's disease: clinical remission for **≥12 wk** before dosing | Native GLP-2 or human growth hormone within **6 mo** before screening |
| | **>4** SBS-related hospital admissions within **12 mo**, or any admission within **30 d** before screening |

- PS dependency = **≥12 continuous months** of PS required **≥3 times weekly** because of ongoing malabsorption; protocol did not specify whether prior weaning attempts were needed (Patients, p. 1474).
- Patients with neoplasms could be included, but ongoing **radiation enteritis** or radiation-damaged enteral tissue excluded (Patients, p. 1474).
- Supplementary Table 1 further excludes: IBD needing chronic systemic immunosuppressants for symptom control (whenever started — broader than Table 1's 3-month rule); celiac disease, refractory or tropical sprue, pseudo-obstruction; untreated premalignant or malignant change on colonoscopy biopsy or polypectomy; intestinal or other major surgery scheduled during the study; chronic pancreatitis or cholecystitis; compromised immune system (eg, acquired immunodeficiency syndrome [AIDS], severe combined immunodeficiency); teduglutide/GLP-2 hypersensitivity; alcohol or drug addiction within the previous year; major uncontrolled psychiatric illness; significant active uncontrolled untreated systemic disease (p. 1481.e3).
- Patients **with a colon** needed a **baseline colonoscopy** to rule out polyps or active intestinal disease (Efficacy and Safety, p. 1476).
- Patients were **not categorized by PN vs IV fluids alone**; habitual diet and fluids to be kept constant; no new medications or treatment changes during stabilization or treatment unless medically necessary (Patients, p. 1474).

### Design — stages and dosing (Study Design and Optimization and Stabilization, pp. 1474–1475; Figure 1, p. 1474)
- **27 sites, 10 countries**, Europe and North America (p. 1474).
- **Stage 1** — screening (1–7 days, Figure 1) → **PS optimization, if needed, up to 8 weeks** → **stabilization 4–8 weeks**.
  - **Optimization target:** stable urine output **1.0–2.0 L/d** (chosen to limit dehydration complications, eg renal calculi, without hyperhydration) (p. 1474). Visits weeks 2, 4, 6 and 8 (**±3 days**); PS adjusted in targeted increments of **≥10%** of the volume at the previous visit; 48-hour oral fluid intake and urine output measured before each visit (1 day on and 1 day off PS unless PS daily) (p. 1475).
  - **Stability definition:** actual PS usage matches prescribed PS; baseline 48-hour oral fluid intake and urine output within **±25%** of the respective 48-hour volumes; urine output **2–4 L per 48 hours**; no PS adjustments allowed during stabilization (p. 1475).
  - Not stable for **≥4 consecutive weeks** immediately before randomization → restart optimization; failure to stabilize after **2 attempts** → not randomized (p. 1475).
- **Stage 2** — randomized **1:1** to placebo or **teduglutide 0.05 mg/kg/d, subcutaneously once daily into abdomen, thigh or arm, at about the same time each day, for 24 weeks**; computer-generated interactive response system, stratified by baseline PS volume **≤6 vs >6 L/wk** (p. 1475).
  - Visits weeks 1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20 and 24; PS volume recorded daily in an electronic diary; **PS reduction attempted at every visit before week 24** (p. 1475).

### PS weaning algorithm (Table 2, p. 1475; Efficacy and Safety, p. 1475)

| 48-hour urine output (vs baseline) | PN/IV action (Table 2) |
|---|---|
| **<1.0 L/d**, or below target based on stabilized urine output | Increase PN/IV by **≥10%** (wk 2) or to previous level |
| **≥1.0 L/d** but less than baseline | Dehydrated or inadequately nourished → increase PS; if not → maintain PS |
| **0% to <10%** increase over baseline | Maintain PS |
| **≥10%** increase over baseline | Reduce PS by **≥10%** of stabilized baseline level, up to a clinically appropriate amount (**maximum 30%**) |

*Baseline urine output = urine volume during the stabilization period, before treatment (Table 2 footnote).*

- Treatment-period PS adjustments targeted **≥10% but <30%** of stabilized PS level; all adjustments based on actual PS volume infused (p. 1475).
- **How** to cut — stop a day of PS, reduce volume on all PS days, change PN/IV constituents, or wean completely — left to investigator judgment and patient preference (p. 1475).
- **Interim safety check 1 week after each PS reduction:** repeat 48-hour urine collection, signs and symptoms of dehydration, body weight change, recorded oral fluid intake, blood (hematocrit, creatinine, blood urea nitrogen) and urine sodium. Tolerated → keep new weekly volume until next visit; not tolerated → resume previous tolerated volume; rechallenge allowed at the next visit (p. 1475).
- The clinician adjusting PS was a different person from the one doing the physical examination and safety assessment, because injection-site reactions or stomal changes could unblind (p. 1475).
- Compared with the earlier phase 3 teduglutide trial, this protocol allowed earlier (**after 2 vs 4 weeks**) and larger (**10%–30% vs 10%**) PS reductions (Discussion, p. 1478).
- **Weaning failure:** **13/86 (15%)** failed a weaning attempt; only **1** (placebo) also failed a second attempt (Supplementary Materials, p. 1481.e1).

### Patients (Results — Patients, p. 1476; Table 3, pp. 1477–1478; Supplementary Figure 1, p. 1481.e3)

All AEs resolved after stopping study drug.

- Absolute PS reduction significantly greater with teduglutide from **week 8 (P = .011)** through week 24; percentage reduction from **week 12 (P = .028)** through week 24 (p. 1476).

Central line systemic infections = catheter-related infection, central line infection, catheter sepsis, infective thrombosis, bacteremia (Supplementary Table 2 footnotes).*

- **Antibodies:** non-neutralizing; no decrease in PS-volume effect, no systemic hypersensitivity or other clinically significant sequelae; all 6 positives detected at week 24 (assays at baseline, week 12 and week 24/end of treatment) (p. 1478; p. 1481.e1).
- No major laboratory, chemistry or hematology findings (p. 1478).
- **Polyps / neoplasia:** the paper names no neoplasm among its AEs; the one peristomal-polyp discontinuation was in the placebo arm (Supplementary Figure 1). Authors: from the citrulline rise and preclinical data, it **cannot be excluded** that long-term teduglutide promotes growth of existing tumors; **cancer surveillance colonoscopies are relevant in patients with a preserved colon** (Discussion, p. 1480).
- **Biliary / pancreatic:** acute cholecystitis (1 related serious AE, resolved) is the only biliary event named; no pancreatic event is reported (p. 1478).

### Authors' clinical points (Discussion, p. 1480)
- **Insufficient PS weaning** → edema and dyspnea; increased absorption could concern patients with latent or overt **cardiac decompensation** if PS is not weaned accordingly.
- **Too-aggressive PS reduction** → dehydration: headache, decreased weight, muscle spasms.
- Intestinal epithelial hyperplasia can cause small intestinal obstruction → patients with signs and symptoms of small bowel obstruction need evaluation **before** starting teduglutide.
- **Stoma nipple enlargement is an expected effect** (10 teduglutide patients had stomal changes); enlarge the hole in the stoma pad to reduce discomfort.
- Use of conventional antidiarrheal and antisecretory therapy was low even in dedicated centres; prudent to use these **before** additional therapies.
- No follow-up after stopping the drug; other evidence cited suggests some patients need more PS almost immediately after stopping while others keep reductions for up to 1 year.

### Limitations (Supplementary Materials, pp. 1481.e1–e2; Discussion, p. 1480)
- Not stratified by PN vs IV-fluids-only; constituents could change during the study; oral rehydration solution and diet not documented.
- Dose timing relative to meals not required or analysed.
- Not designed to test earlier start (eg right after resection), retreatment, maintenance, or effect of discontinuation.
- 24-week duration; quality of life not assessed (Discussion, p. 1479).

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **Responder threshold wording.** Methods and Discussion define a response as a **20%–100%** reduction (pp. 1475, 1478); the Abstract says **>20%** (p. 1473). The wiki and the label use **≥20%**, consistent with the Methods wording.
- **Weaning trigger wording.** Table 2 and the Abstract trigger a PS cut at a **≥10%** rise in 48-hour urine output over baseline (pp. 1473, 1475); the Efficacy and Safety text says **>10%** (p. 1475).
- **Infusion-day endpoint denominator.** [[glp-2-agonists]] lists "≥1 fewer infusion day per week 54% vs 23%" under a header of n = 43 per arm; the paper's denominators are **39 per arm** (21/39 vs 9/39, p. 1476).
- **Malignancy signal.** [[glp-2-agonists]] (citing [[aga-2022-short-bowel-syndrome]]) reports 1 liver cancer and 2 lung cancers among teduglutide-treated subjects in "the confirmatory phase 3 trial" report to the Food and Drug Administration; this 24-week publication names no malignancy. Whether the AGA figure includes extension-study follow-up is not stated in this paper.
- **Different AE populations, not conflicts.** Label rates in [[gattex-2025-teduglutide-label]] (n = 77 vs 59, e.g. abdominal distension 20% vs 2%, peripheral edema 10% vs 3%) differ from this trial's (n = 42 vs 43: 21% vs 2%; 17% vs 5%, Supplementary Table 2); [[glp-2-agonists]] describes the label rates as pooled from two 24-week placebo-controlled trials.
- **Printed inconsistencies in the paper:** Discussion gives the teduglutide baseline PS as a mean of **12.5 L/wk** (p. 1478) vs **12.9 ± 7.8 L/wk** in Results (p. 1476); the Discussion's "FCE at week 24 was in the same magnitude in both studies (4.4 ± 7.3 L/wk)" (p. 1479) does not say which study that figure belongs to — this trial's own FCE is **5.4 ± 6.0 L/wk** in the sentence before and in Results (pp. 1476, 1479); Results state no significant baseline differences (p. 1476) though remnant colon category differs at P = .019 (Table 3); Table 3 teduglutide body mass index range printed "17.6–9.8".
- **Open:** durability after stopping, optimal duration, retreatment, earlier initiation, dose timing with meals, and response in IV-fluid-only vs PN patients (Supplementary Materials, pp. 1481.e1–e2).
