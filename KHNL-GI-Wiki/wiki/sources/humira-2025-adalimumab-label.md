## Summary

The FDA prescribing information for **adalimumab**, fetched to close the second of the two dose cells [[anti-tnf-agents]] had flagged. That page gave adalimumab induction as **160 mg week 0 → 80 mg week 2 → 40 mg q2wk from week 4**, with a caveat beneath: the loading sequence came from [[acg-2025-uc]] — the ulcerative colitis guideline — and had been carried over to Crohn's disease without a Crohn's source. **The label confirms the same sequence for Crohn's disease in its own right**, and anchors it to days rather than weeks: **160 mg on Day 1, 80 mg on Day 15, then 40 mg every other week starting Day 29**. It adds an administration option the wiki did not have: the 160 mg dose may be **given in one day or split over two consecutive days**.

The label also supplies something no ingested guideline does — **a stopping rule**. In ulcerative colitis, **discontinue adalimumab in adults without evidence of clinical remission by eight weeks (Day 57)**. The ulcerative colitis regimen is otherwise identical to the Crohn's one, which makes the stopping rule the only thing separating them.

Adalimumab has **the broadest gastrointestinal label in the class**: both **Crohn's disease (adults and children ≥6 years)** and **ulcerative colitis (adults and children ≥5 years)**, where [[cimzia-2025-certolizumab-pegol-label|certolizumab pegol]] has Crohn's only in adults and [[simponi-2025-golimumab-label|golimumab]] has ulcerative colitis only. Against that breadth the label sets a **Limitation of Use** worth reading before a second-line start: **effectiveness has not been established in patients who have lost response to, or were intolerant of, tumor necrosis factor (TNF) blockers.**

Two further points are drug-specific rather than class boilerplate. **The label lists no contraindications** — the section reads "None". And the tuberculosis instruction is not a one-off gate: evaluate for active tuberculosis and test for latent infection **prior to initiating and periodically during therapy**.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Indications (§1) — two GI indications, the broadest in the class**

- **Crohn's disease (1.5):** **moderately to severely active** disease in **adults and pediatric patients ≥6 years**.
- **Ulcerative colitis (1.6):** **moderately to severely active** disease in **adults and pediatric patients ≥5 years**.
- ⚠ **Limitation of Use (1.6):** **effectiveness has not been established in patients who have lost response to or were intolerant to TNF blockers.**
- Non-GI: rheumatoid arthritis, polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis (≥2 y), psoriatic arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, plaque psoriasis, hidradenitis suppurativa (≥12 y), non-infectious uveitis (≥2 y).

**Dosage — Crohn's disease, adults (§2.4), subcutaneous**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Day 1** | **160 mg** — **given in one day or split over two consecutive days** |
| **Day 15** (2 weeks later) | **80 mg** |
| **Day 29** (2 weeks later) onward | **40 mg every other week** |

- **Concomitant therapy permitted:** aminosalicylates and/or corticosteroids may be continued; **azathioprine, 6-mercaptopurine or methotrexate may be continued if necessary**.
- **Pediatric Crohn's ≥6 y, by weight:** **17 kg to <40 kg** — 80 mg Day 1, 40 mg Day 15, then **40 mg every other week** from Day 29. **≥40 kg** — 160 mg Day 1 (single dose or split over two consecutive days), 80 mg Day 15, then **40 mg every other week** from Day 29.

**Dosage — ulcerative colitis, adults (§2.5), subcutaneous**

- **Identical induction and maintenance to Crohn's:** 160 mg Day 1 (one day or split over two consecutive days) → 80 mg Day 15 → **40 mg every other week from Day 29**.
- ⚠ **Stopping rule: discontinue in adults without evidence of clinical remission by eight weeks (Day 57) of therapy.**
- Aminosalicylates and/or corticosteroids may be continued; azathioprine and 6-mercaptopurine may be continued if necessary.
- **Pediatric ulcerative colitis ≥5 y, by weight:** **20 kg to <40 kg** — 80 mg Day 1, 40 mg Day 8, 40 mg Day 15, then from Day 29 **40 mg every other week or 20 mg every week**. **≥40 kg** — 160 mg Day 1 (single or split), then from Day 29 **80 mg every other week** (or the weekly alternative given in the label's table).
- Non-GI dosing for contrast (§2.2): rheumatoid, psoriatic and ankylosing disease = **40 mg every other week**, with **40 mg weekly or 80 mg every other week** as an escalation in rheumatoid arthritis patients not on concomitant methotrexate.

**Before starting, and during (§2.1)**

- **Evaluate for active tuberculosis and test for latent infection prior to initiating *and periodically during* therapy** — the label repeats the evaluation, it does not treat it as a single gate.

**Contraindications (§4)**

- **None.** The label states this outright.

**Boxed warning — serious infections and malignancy**

- Increased risk of **serious infection leading to hospitalization or death**, including tuberculosis, bacterial sepsis, invasive fungal infection and other opportunistic pathogens. **Test for latent tuberculosis before use and treat if positive.**
- **Lymphoma and other malignancies, some fatal**, reported in children and adolescents on TNF blockers.

**Warnings and precautions (§5) — adalimumab-specific points**

- **Immunizations (5.10):** patients may receive concurrent vaccines **except live vaccines**; no data on secondary transmission of infection by live vaccines. In a placebo-controlled rheumatoid arthritis trial, **anti-pneumococcal antibody response did not differ** from placebo and similar proportions reached **protective anti-influenza antibody levels**, though **aggregate influenza titers were moderately lower** on adalimumab — clinical significance unknown. Bring **pediatric** patients up to date with immunizations before starting if possible.
- ⚠ **Live vaccines in the in-utero–exposed infant (5.10, 8.1):** **the safety of live or live-attenuated vaccines in an infant exposed to adalimumab in utero is unknown** — weigh risks and benefits before vaccinating.
- Also carried, as class warnings already held by [[anti-tnf-agents]]: serious infections (5.1), malignancy incl. hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma (5.2), hypersensitivity (5.3), hepatitis B virus reactivation (5.4), neurologic/demyelinating reactions (5.5), hematologic reactions (5.6), heart failure (5.8), autoimmunity (5.9), and **increased infection risk with anakinra (5.7) or abatacept (5.11)**.

**Pregnancy and the exposed infant (§8.1)**

- **Available studies do not reliably establish an association between adalimumab and major birth defects.**
- **OTIS/MotherToBaby HUMIRA Pregnancy Registry** (prospective cohort, US and Canada, 2004–2016): **221 women** treated in the first trimester (69 rheumatoid arthritis, 152 Crohn's disease) vs **106 untreated** (74 RA, 32 CD). Major birth defects in live-born infants: **10% treated (8.7% RA, 10.5% CD) vs 7.5% untreated (6.8% RA, 9.4% CD)**. The label calls **the lack of a pattern of major birth defects reassuring** and notes between-group differences may have affected the rates.
- ⚠ **Adalimumab is actively transferred across the placenta during the third trimester** and **may affect the immune response of the in-utero–exposed infant**. Monoclonal antibodies are **increasingly transported as pregnancy progresses, the largest amount in the third trimester**.
- **Disease activity is itself a risk:** increased activity in rheumatoid arthritis or inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is associated with **preterm delivery (<37 wk), low birth weight (<2500 g) and small-for-gestational-age** birth.
- Animal: no fetal harm or malformations in cynomolgus monkeys during organogenesis and later gestation at exposures up to ~**373×** the maximum recommended human dose of 40 mg subcutaneously without methotrexate.

**Adverse reactions in IBD specifically (§6.1)**

- **Adult Crohn's disease:** safety profile in **1,478 adults** across four placebo-controlled and two open-label extension studies was **similar to that seen in rheumatoid arthritis**.
- **Pediatric Crohn's disease (6–17 y):** **192 patients** (Study PCD-I plus open-label extension), profile **similar to adults with Crohn's**. During the 4-week open-label induction phase the most common reactions were **injection site pain (6%)** and **injection site reaction (5%)**. **67% experienced an infection** (upper respiratory tract infection, nasopharyngitis) and **5% a serious infection** — viral infection, catheter-related device sepsis, gastroenteritis, H1N1 influenza, **disseminated histoplasmosis**. **Allergic reactions in 5%**, all non-serious and mostly localized.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **No contradiction with the guidelines; the label fills their silence.** [[acg-2025-uc]] prints the 160 → 80 → 40 mg sequence for ulcerative colitis and [[acg-2025-crohns]] recommends adalimumab for Crohn's without printing a schedule — which is why the wiki's Crohn's figure carried a caveat. The label's Crohn's regimen matches what the wiki had.
- ⚠ **The wiki's "40 mg weekly in CD" maintenance option is not in this label.** The label gives **40 mg every other week** for adult Crohn's with no weekly alternative; weekly 40 mg appears only as a **rheumatoid arthritis** escalation for patients not on methotrexate. The wiki's Crohn's weekly figure is guideline-sourced, and a label's silence does not override a guideline — but the two do not agree, and the page should attribute the weekly option to its guideline rather than to labeling.
- **The week-8 stopping rule applies to ulcerative colitis only.** The label sets no analogous non-response endpoint for Crohn's disease.
- **Placental transfer splits the class three ways**, and all three labels are tier-equal, so these are drug-level differences rather than conflicts: **certolizumab pegol** — infant concentrations ≥75% lower than maternal, no vaccine hold; **adalimumab** — actively transferred in the third trimester, exposed-infant live-vaccine safety **unknown**; **golimumab** — transferred, with a definite **6-month live-vaccine hold** after the mother's last dose. Only golimumab's label names an interval.
- **Open:** the label gives **no renal or hepatic dose adjustment and no therapeutic drug monitoring guidance**. Neither is answerable from any ingested source for this agent.
