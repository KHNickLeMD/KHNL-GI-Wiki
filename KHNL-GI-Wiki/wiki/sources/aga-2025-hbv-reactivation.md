## Summary

This American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) guideline frames hepatitis B virus reactivation (HBVr) risk in patients starting immunosuppressive/immunomodulatory therapy as a **risk-stratified** decision between **antiviral prophylaxis** and **monitoring alone**. Risk depends on serologic status (hepatitis B surface antigen [HBsAg]-positive vs antibody to hepatitis B core antigen [anti-HBc]–positive/HBsAg-negative) and the immunosuppressive agent (e.g., **rituximab** and B-cell–depleting agents confer the highest risk). Builds on baseline screening and links conceptually to [[chronic-hepatitis-b]] management.

---

## Key Recommendations

*Numbered 1–4 by the guideline (Table 4). Every recommendation carries **moderate certainty evidence**.*

- **Rec 1 (Strong, moderate certainty):** **High risk** (anticipated HBVr **>10%**) → antiviral prophylaxis over monitoring alone.
  - Assumes antivirals with a **high barrier to resistance**.
  - Start **before** the risk-imposing medication; continue **≥6 months** after it stops (**≥12 months** for B cell–depleting agents). When risk is high, extending to 12 months is reasonable.
- **Rec 2 (Conditional, moderate certainty):** **Moderate risk** (HBVr **1–10%**) → the AGA *suggests* antiviral prophylaxis over monitoring alone.
  - Assumes antivirals with a high barrier to resistance.
  - Patients who value avoiding long-term antivirals and their cost (particularly those who are **HBsAg-negative**) may reasonably choose monitoring, considering feasibility and adherence.
  - Monitoring = every **1–3 months**, and **must include HBV viral load** as well as alanine aminotransferase (ALT).
- **Rec 3 (Conditional, moderate certainty):** **Low risk** (HBVr **<1%**) → the AGA *suggests* monitoring alone over antiviral prophylaxis.
  - Assumes regular, sufficient follow-up. Patients who value avoiding the small risk (particularly those on more than 1 low-risk drug) may reasonably select prophylaxis.
- **Rec 4 (Strong, moderate certainty):** Individuals at potential risk of HBVr → **test for hepatitis B**.
  - Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) universal screening of adults ≥18 y (HBsAg, anti-HBs, total anti-HBc) means screening no longer needs stratifying by HBVr risk.
  - Reasonable to test serology first (at minimum HBsAg and anti-HBc), then HBV DNA if HBsAg and/or anti-HBc is positive.
  - Anyone meeting current HBV treatment criteria should receive antiviral therapy regardless of HBVr risk.
- **Anti-HBs status should not be used** to guide prophylaxis decisions in any risk group.

### Table 6 — baseline HBVr risk by exposure

| Exposure | HBsAg-positive / anti-HBc–positive | HBsAg-negative / anti-HBc–positive |
|---|---|---|
| Anthracyclines (doxorubicin, epirubicin) | High\* | Moderate\* |
| Anti-tumor necrosis factor (TNF) (infliximab, adalimumab) | High | Low |
| Anti-IL-6 (tocilizumab) | High\* | Moderate\* |
| Anti-T cell (abatacept) | Moderate\* | Moderate |
| B cell–depleting (rituximab, ofatumumab) | High | High |
| CAR-T cell therapy | High\* | Moderate |
| Cytokine/integrin inhibitors (ustekinumab, secukinumab) | High | Moderate |
| HCV co-infection undergoing direct-acting antiviral (DAA) therapy | High | Low |
| Immune checkpoint inhibitors (nivolumab, pembrolizumab, ipilimumab) | Moderate | Low |
| JAK inhibitors (tofacitinib, baricitinib) | High\* | Moderate\* |
| Transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) | High | Moderate\* |
| Tyrosine kinase inhibitors (imatinib, sunitinib) | High | Moderate\* |
| Methotrexate, 6-mercaptopurine, azathioprine | Low\* | Low\* |
| Corticosteroids ≥4 wk, moderate/high dose | High\* | Moderate\* |
| Corticosteroids ≥4 wk, low dose | Moderate\* | Low\* |
| Corticosteroids ≤1 wk, any dose | Low\* | Low\* |
| Intra-articular corticosteroid | Low\* | Low\* |

*\*Lower certainty in the baseline-risk estimate. Prednisone (or equivalent): low <10 mg, moderate 10–20 mg, high >20 mg. Risk is for a single medication and may be higher in combination; it applies for the duration of the risk-imposing state or up to 1 year; if the risk-imposing state changes, reconsider the category.*

## Clinical Relevance

Test before immunosuppression; prophylaxis in high risk, prophylaxis suggested in moderate risk (shared decision), monitoring in low risk — the stratum comes from the serology × drug cell in Table 6. Connects to monitoring via [[abnormal-liver-chemistries]] and to underlying [[chronic-hepatitis-b]].
