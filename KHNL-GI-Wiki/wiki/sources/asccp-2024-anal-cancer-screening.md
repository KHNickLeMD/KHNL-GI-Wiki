## Summary

A short ASCCP practice advisory that does not create new screening criteria of its own — it **reviews, reconciles, and endorses** the two anal cancer screening guidelines published in 2024: the International Anal Neoplasia Society (IANS) consensus guidelines (Stier et al., *Int J Cancer* 2024) and the first US guidelines for people with HIV, co-sponsored by NIH OAR / CDC / HIVMA-IDSA and published in the *Guidelines for the Prevention and Treatment of Opportunistic Infections in Adults and Adolescents with HIV* (July 2024).

The framing is explicitly parallel to cervical cancer prevention: anal cancer is HPV-mediated, and the **target of screening is anal high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion (HSIL)** — not the cancer. The justification is the ANCHOR trial (Palefsky, *NEJM* 2022), in which treating anal HSIL reduced anal cancer incidence in people with HIV by almost 60%. Anal cancer is rare in the general population (**1.7 per 100,000 person-years** in the US), so screening is confined to defined high-risk populations, which IANS sorts into two risk categories by incidence rate.

**Risk Category A** is groups with incidence **≥17/100,000 p-y** — at least ten times the general population — where screening is recommended outright, each group with its own start age. **Risk Category B** is groups with incidence **<10/100,000 p-y**, where IANS calls for shared decision-making pending outcome data. Every recommended pathway ends at **high-resolution anoscopy (HRA)** for abnormal results, and the advisory repeatedly acknowledges that HRA capacity is the rate-limiting resource: where HRA is unavailable, Category A patients should still receive symptom assessment and **digital anal rectal examination (DARE)**.

ASCCP's own position, stated at the end, is deliberately minimal and implementable: providers caring for patients with lower genital tract dysplasia or HIV should offer **routine annual DARE**, and should add anal cytology and/or hrHPV testing **only if referral to HRA for abnormal results is actually available**. The advisory closes on access and equity — advocating for funding and HRA provider capacity rather than for a broader screening net.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Epidemiology and rationale**
- General US anal cancer incidence: **1.7 per 100,000 person-years**.
- Screening target is **anal HSIL**, mirroring cervical cancer prevention.
- Treating anal HSIL reduced anal cancer rates in people with HIV by **almost 60%** (ANCHOR, Palefsky *NEJM* 2022).
- NIH/CDC/IDSA and IANS **agree** on which HIV populations to screen.

**Risk Category A — screening recommended (incidence ≥17/100,000 p-y, ≥10× general population)**

| Group | Incidence (per 100,000 p-y) | When to start |
|---|---|---|
| MSM and transgender women **with HIV** | **>70** | Age **35** |
| History of vulvar HSIL or vulvar cancer (VIN3) | **>40** | **Within 1 year of diagnosis** |
| Men who have sex with women (MSW) **with HIV** | **40** | Age **45** |
| Women **with HIV** | **25** | Age **45** |
| Solid organ transplant recipients | **>25** | **10 years post-transplant** |
| MSM and transgender women **without HIV** | **>18** | Age **45** |

**Risk Category B — shared decision-making (incidence <10/100,000 p-y)**
- Age **45 and older** with: history of cervical or vaginal cancer; cervical or vaginal HSIL; perianal warts; persistent cervical **HPV 16**.
- Other immunosuppressed individuals — named examples: rheumatoid arthritis, lupus, **[[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease]]**, **[[ulcerative-colitis|ulcerative colitis]]**.
- IANS emphasizes shared decision-making here **until more outcome data are available**.

**Screening tests (IANS)**
- Accepted modalities: **anal cytology**, **high-risk HPV (hrHPV) testing**, or **combinations** of the two.
- hrHPV testing of the anal canal is **not FDA-approved**, but is highly sensitive for anal precancer and cancer.
- **Anal cytology alone is acceptable**, with HRA referral for any abnormal cytology.
- Where HRA capacity is limited: **immediate referral prioritized for high-grade cytology**; others may have **repeat testing within 12 months**.
- **hrHPV triage of cytology results** is endorsed, particularly to reduce immediate HRA referrals.
- **Primary hrHPV testing is acceptable**: positive → immediate HRA referral; negative → repeat screening in **12–24 months**.
- **Co-testing** (cytology + hrHPV) is acceptable, with immediate HRA referral for high-risk results.
- **DARE is recommended at every screening visit** — an important tool for detecting early anal cancers.

**NIH/CDC/IDSA guidelines for people with HIV — where they differ**
- All at-risk adults with HIV: assess **at least annually** for anal abnormalities (pain, burning, masses) and perform **DARE**.
- Age **<35 and symptomatic** or with signs of anal cancer: **DARE plus at least standard anoscopy**.
- Asymptomatic **MSM and transgender women with HIV**: begin **lab-based screening** (cytology alone, or cytology + hrHPV co-testing) at age **35**.
- **All other people with HIV**: begin at age **45**.
- Any patient with symptoms or an abnormal screening result: **refer for HRA**.
- ⚠ **The NIH/CDC/IDSA panel did NOT recommend primary HPV testing for screening in persons with HIV** — a direct divergence from IANS, which accepts primary hrHPV.

**Where HRA is unavailable**
- Both guidelines recognize the access limitation.
- Category A patients (people with HIV, solid organ transplant recipients, women with a history of vulvar HSIL/cancer, MSM/TW without HIV) should be screened using **symptom assessment and DARE**.

**ASCCP's own endorsement**
- Endorses **routine annual DARE** for providers caring for patients with lower genital tract dysplasia in **both** risk categories A and B.
- Anal cytology and/or hrHPV testing **may be added if there is capacity for HRA referral** for abnormal results.
- Emphasizes building HRA provider capacity, funding, and equitable access as prerequisites to implementation.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Primary hrHPV testing in people with HIV — IANS vs NIH/CDC/IDSA.** IANS accepts primary hrHPV testing as a standalone screening strategy; the NIH/CDC/IDSA panel explicitly did not recommend it in persons with HIV. The advisory presents both without adjudicating. Both are 2024 and both are consensus/guideline tier, so neither supersedes the other by date or tier — the divergence is carried on the wiki page as a stated disagreement.
- **IANS 2024 primary source is not ingested.** `raw/GI Guidelines/Other/IANS 2024 Anal Cancer Screening Consensus.pdf` is not a PDF — it is a saved Cloudflare bot-challenge page (the upstream download from Wiley failed), containing no clinical content. All IANS criteria on the wiki therefore come **second-hand through this ASCCP advisory**. The full IANS consensus (Stier et al., *Int J Cancer* 2024;154:1694-1702, doi:10.1002/ijc.34850) would be needed for the complete recommendation set, HRA technique standards, and management-after-HRA algorithms.
- **Management after HRA is out of scope** of this advisory — what is done with a biopsy-proven anal HSIL (ablation, topical therapy, surveillance interval) is not stated in the ingested source and is not on the wiki page.
- **No evidence grades.** The advisory carries no GRADE or strength-of-recommendation labels for its own statements; it is a narrative endorsement. Risk-group incidence rates derive from Clifford et al., *Int J Cancer* 2021 (meta-analysis), and the risk categorization from Stier et al. 2024.
