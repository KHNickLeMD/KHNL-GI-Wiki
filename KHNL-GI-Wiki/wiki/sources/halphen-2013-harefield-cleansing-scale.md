## Summary
- **Why a new scale:** Aronchick Scale needs a percentage-of-mucosa estimate and gives one global grade with no segment scores; Ottawa and Boston scales total 3 segments, so a well-cleansed segment can mask an inadequate one — 2 perfect segments + 1 inadequate segment can still give "acceptable" totals of **7/9** (Boston) and **3/14** (Ottawa) (Background, p. 122).
- **Harefield Cleansing Scale (HCS):** **5 segments**, each scored **0–4**; output as segment scores, a **4-level grade A–D**, or **successful (A/B) vs unsuccessful (C/D)** (p. 122; Figure 1, p. 123).
- **Design:** 3 blinded expert gastroenterologists reviewed colonoscopy videos from a multicenter randomized controlled trial (RCT) of 2 polyethylene glycol (PEG)-based preparations and rated each on both HCS and the Aronchick Scale (criterion standard); trial investigators' live HCS scores also recorded (Methods, pp. 123–124). Test-retest in a separate 16-patient sample (p. 125).
- **Results:** expert HCS vs Aronchick Pearson r **0.833**; receiver operating characteristic (ROC) area under the curve (AUC) **0.945**; sensitivity **99%** / specificity **83%** at segment score cut-off **2**; Cronbach alpha **0.81**; test-retest generalized kappa **0.64** (Results, pp. 126–129).
- **Authors' conclusion:** "a robust, reliable, and consistent tool" for standardizing bowel preparation assessment in clinical and research practice (Abstract, p. 121; Discussion, p. 130).

## Key Findings / Claims

### The Harefield Cleansing Scale (Figure 1, p. 123; Background, p. 122)

**Segments — 5, each scored separately** (Figure 1): rectum · sigmoid colon · descending colon · transverse colon · ascending colon/cecum.

**Segmental score** (Figure 1, "Segmental score assessment"):

| Score | Descriptor (Figure 1) |
|---|---|
| **0** | Irremovable, heavy, hard stools |
| **1** | Semi-solid, only partially removable stools |
| **2** | Brown liquid/fully removable semi-solid stools |
| **3** | Clear liquid |
| **4** | Empty and clean |

**Overall grade** (Figure 1):

| Grade | Segment criterion (as printed) | Binary outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | All segments scored **3 or 4** | Successful cleansing |
| **B** | **≥1** segments scored **2** | Successful cleansing |
| **C** | **≥1** segments scored **1** | Unsuccessful cleansing |
| **D** | **≥1** segments scored **0** | Unsuccessful cleansing |

- **Sum score** of the 5 segments: **0** (very bad cleansing in all areas) to **20** (very good cleansing in all areas) (Results — Interrater reliability and internal consistency, p. 128).
- **Applied after bowel cleaning**, but **grade A only if the mucosa is fully visualized without cleaning** (p. 122).
- **Brown liquid or semisolid stool that can be cleared easily → segment score 2**, which still allows grade B (p. 122).
- **"Acceptable" global score only if washing and suctioning give 100% visualization in all 5 segments**; if cleaning cannot reveal clean mucosa throughout all segments, a "successful" grade cannot be scored (p. 122).
- Authors call it more conservative than the alternatives but less likely to give a falsely reassuring result (p. 122); it evaluates **100%** of the mucosa with explicit criteria rather than percentage estimates (Discussion, p. 130).
- Binary used in analyses: **A/B = acceptable/successful, C/D = unacceptable/unsuccessful** (Methods — Adenoma detection rates, p. 126; ROC, p. 128).

**Development** (p. 122): built in the early 1990s for Norgine PEG-preparation trials; original 5-segment version graded A excellent / B good / C failed; expanded to the current 4-grade tool after reviewers found the 3-grade output gave insufficient information; current version designed in 2000 (Choice of comparator, p. 123). Used in 6 clinical trials and a 598-patient observational cohort in 9 Italian units (pp. 122–123).

### Comparator — Aronchick Global Assessment Scale (Table 1, p. 123)

| Category | Description (Table 1) |
|---|---|
| Excellent | Small volume of clear liquid or **>95%** of surface seen |
| Good | Large volume of clear liquid covering **5% to 25%** of the surface but **>90%** of surface seen |
| Fair | Some semi-solid stool that could be suctioned or washed away but **>90%** of surface seen |
| Poor | Semi-solid stool that could not be suctioned or washed away and **<90%** of the surface seen |
| Inadequate | Repeat preparation needed |

- Binary (not part of the original Aronchick approach): **excellent, good, fair = success; poor, inadequate = failure** (Methods, p. 123).
- Chosen as criterion standard because it was the only validated scale in 2000; Ottawa not yet in general use when the 2006 protocol was written; Boston not published until 2009 (pp. 123–124).

### Study population (Methods — Patient populations, p. 124; Figure 2, p. 124)
- Source trial: multicenter RCT of 2 PEG-based preparations (the NORMO study; ethics approval March 7, 2007 — p. 126); age **18 to 85 years**, screening or diagnostic colonoscopy.
- Exclusions: ileus, intestinal occlusion or perforation, toxic megacolon with severe inflammatory bowel disease, gastroparesis, documented carcinoma, other colon disease with fragile mucosa, New York Heart Association (NYHA) class III or IV heart failure, severe renal insufficiency, PEG hypersensitivity, glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency, phenylketonuria (p. 124).
- **415** trial patients → videos for **337** colonoscopies (p. 124). Figure 2: expert HCS **n = 323**; investigator HCS **n = 328**; both expert and investigator HCS **n = 321**.
- Raters: **3 experienced gastroenterologists** ("experts") not otherwise in the trial, blinded to preparation, rating by consensus (majority decision on disagreement); trial endoscopists ("investigators") scored HCS live only, not Aronchick — investigator comparisons are **exploratory** (p. 124).

### Grade distributions (Table 3, p. 127)

| Population | Grade distribution, no. (%) |
|---|---|
| Expert Aronchick, n = 323 | Excellent 52 (16.1) · Good 182 (56.4) · Fair 66 (20.4) · Poor 18 (5.6) · Inadequate 5 (1.6) |
| Expert HCS, n = 323 | A 228 (70.6) · B 72 (22.3) · C 19 (5.9) · D 4 (1.2) |
| Investigator HCS, n = 328 | A 152 (46.3) · B 134 (40.9) · C 38 (11.6) · D 4 (1.2) |

- Abstract: successful colon cleansing **92.88%** vs unsuccessful **7.12%** on cross-tabulation (p. 121).

### Criterion validity — expert HCS vs expert Aronchick (Results, pp. 126–128; Table 4, p. 127)
- **Pearson r = 0.833**; Spearman r **0.778** (p. 126).
- Linear regression: slope **0.652**, y-intercept **0.09**, R² **0.71** (perfect match would be slope 1.0, offset 0) (pp. 125–126).
- Cross-tabulation (Table 4): Mantel-Haentzel (M-H, spelled as printed) chi-square **235.0**, P **< .0001**; Cramer V **0.86** (p. 126).
- **Binary:** each scale rated **300** patients successful (Table 4); **8 (2.5%)** discordant — 4 each way; M-H chi-square **212.7**, P **< .0001**, Cramer V **0.82** (p. 126).
- Table 4 detail: all 228 grade A colons were Aronchick 1 or 2 (51 + 177); all 4 grade D colons were Aronchick 5.

### ROC analysis (Results, p. 128; Figure 3, p. 128)
Binary reference: Aronchick excellent/good/fair vs poor/inadequate; HCS A/B vs C/D, across segment score cut-offs.

| Comparison | Optimum segment score cut-off | Sensitivity | Specificity | Youden Index | AUC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Expert HCS vs expert Aronchick (primary)** | **2** | **99%** | **83%** | **0.81** | **0.945** |
| Investigator HCS vs expert Aronchick | 2 | 0.92 | 0.63 (printed as "sensitivity") | 0.63 | 0.906 |
| Expert HCS vs investigator HCS | 2 | 0.91 | 0.59 (printed as "sensitivity") | 0.50 | 0.862 |

- Higher segment score cut-offs (**3 and 4**) raised sensitivity but lowered specificity (p. 128).
- Difference between the 2 secondary AUCs not significant: z = **1.47**, P = **.07** (p. 128).
- Figure 3 plot label: AUC **0.9448** (p. 128).

### Expert vs investigator (exploratory; Results, pp. 126–128; Tables 5–6, pp. 127–128)
- Correlation, investigator HCS vs expert Aronchick: Pearson r **0.457**, Spearman **0.485**; investigator HCS vs expert HCS: Pearson r **0.463**, Spearman **0.533** (p. 126).
- Segment-by-segment expert vs investigator HCS: Cohen kappa **0.15–0.27** (slight-to-fair agreement) (p. 126).
- Expert vs investigator HCS (Table 5): M-H chi-square **88.2**, P **< .0001**, Cramer V **0.44**; binary **36 (11.22%)** discordant — **27 (8.41%)** successful by experts but unsuccessful by investigators, **9 (2.80%)** the reverse; M-H chi-square **78.9**, Cramer V **0.49** (p. 126).
- Expert Aronchick vs investigator HCS (Table 6): M-H chi-square **94.0**, P **< .0001**, Cramer V **0.47**; binary **32 (9.76%)** discordant — text: **25 (7.62%)** successful by investigators but unsuccessful by experts, **7 (2.13%)** the reverse (Table 6 cells give the opposite direction — see Printed inconsistencies); M-H chi-square **77.9**, Cramer V **0.49** (pp. 126–127).
- Authors read the lower expert–investigator correlation as environmental effects of doing the colonoscopy on perceived cleansing; all semisubjective scales need training, familiarization and calibration (Discussion, pp. 129–130).

### Reliability (Results, pp. 128–129)
- **Interrater reliability:** Shrout-Fleiss intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) (1,1), investigators vs pooled experts — **ICC 0.457 (95% confidence interval [CI], 0.366–0.539)**, "fair" interrater reproducibility (p. 128).
- **Internal consistency** (threshold: Cronbach alpha **≥0.7** acceptable — p. 125):
  - Expert HCS: Cronbach alpha **0.81**; sum-score measurement error **2.60** over the complete colon (less than 1 point per segment) (p. 128).
  - Investigator HCS: Cronbach alpha **0.86**; sum-score measurement error **4.76** (about 1 point per segment) (p. 128).
- **Test-retest** (different RCT, ref. 14): **16** videos stratified to 25% per grade; 2 tapes each of grades B and C duplicated → **20** tapes; **4 experts** → **80** scores (p. 125).
  - Pairwise Cohen kappa **0.33–0.85** across the 6 expert pairs (fair 1 pair, moderate 2, substantial 1, near-perfect 2); **generalized (Fleiss) kappa 0.64** — substantial (p. 129). Abstract gives **0.639** (p. 121).
  - Among-rater agreement on the 4 duplicated grade B/C videos: observed **62.5%** vs **50%** by chance; kappa **0.28 (95% CI, 0.12–0.69)** — fair, wide CI (p. 129).
- Kappa interpretation used (Landis and Koch; Table 2, p. 126): **<0.00** less than chance · **0.00** no better than chance · **0.01–0.20** slight · **0.21–0.40** fair · **0.41–0.60** moderate · **0.61–0.80** substantial · **0.81–1.00** near perfect.

### Adenoma detection rate (ADR) (Results, p. 129; Table 7, p. 129)
Exploratory; study not powered for ADR (p. 126).

| HCS grade | A | B | C | D | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Patients | 250 | 110 | 24 | 3 | 387 |
| ADR (%) | 26.0 | 26.4 | 25.0 | 0.0 | 25.8 |

- Across the 4 grades: P = **.90** (Fisher exact). Acceptable (A/B) vs unacceptable (C/D): **26.1% vs 22.3%**, P = **.27** (Table 7).
- About **1650** patients would have been needed to show a significant binary difference at the observed rates (Discussion, p. 130).

### Comparison with other scales (Discussion, p. 130)
- Ottawa vs Aronchick (97 patients, ref. 10): Pearson r and kappa similar to this study; ICC **0.77** for Aronchick vs **0.47** for HCS here; no external assessors, ROC or test-retest in that study.
- Boston (983 patients, ref. 11): interrater and among-rater values similar to HCS; no reference comparator, so formal comparison not possible.

### Limitations (Abstract, p. 121)
- Test-retest used a different patient population from the other measures.
- Too few patients to assess performance by ADR.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **Adequacy definition differs from the wiki's guideline rule.** [[bowel-preparation]] (from [[usmstf-2025-bowel-prep]]) calls a preparation adequate only when **every segment** scores Boston 2 or 3. HCS "successful" = grade A or B, and any segment scored 1 or 0 gives grade C or D; a segment of brown liquid / fully removable semi-solid stool (score 2) still allows grade B (Figure 1, p. 123; p. 122). The paper gives **no conversion between HCS and Boston scores**, so whether an HCS-successful colon meets the guideline segment rule cannot be read off the HCS grade; the guideline rule takes priority.
- **The paper's critique of Boston targets totals, not the segment rule.** It says 2 perfect segments + 1 inadequate segment can total an "acceptable" **7/9** on Boston (p. 122); under the guideline segment rule on [[bowel-preparation]] any segment <2 is already inadequate. The HCS grade rule (any segment 0 or 1 → C or D) is likewise segment based.
- **"Fair" counted as success for Aronchick.** The paper's binary maps Aronchick excellent/good/**fair** to success (Methods, p. 123; ROC, p. 128); [[bowel-preparation]] (guideline) says "fair" alone is not an adequacy statement. The paper notes this binary is not part of the original Aronchick approach.
- **Retained fluid.** [[bowel-preparation]] says the Boston Bowel Preparation Scale is the only scale that does not downgrade for retained fluid. In HCS, clear liquid scores **3** vs empty and clean **4**, but both qualify for grade A (Figure 1) — the segment score downgrades for clear fluid while the grade does not; brown liquid scores 2 (grade B).
- **Timing of grading.** [[bowel-preparation]] (guideline) says grade only after all washing and suctioning. The HCS is applied after cleaning, but grade A requires full visualization **without** cleaning (p. 122) — it also encodes the pre-cleaning state.
- **Grade precedence not stated.** Figure 1 defines B, C and D each as "≥1 segments scored" 2, 1 or 0; it does not say which grade applies when segments meet more than one of these criteria (e.g. one segment 0 and another 1). The text states only that any segment not cleaned to clean mucosa rules out a successful grade (p. 122).
- **Printed inconsistencies:**
  - Abstract (p. 121) calls **0.833** a Spearman coefficient and gives the segmental sum-score Spearman as **−0.778**; Results (p. 126) give **0.833** as Pearson and **0.778** as Spearman (positive).
  - ROC secondary comparisons print "sensitivity" twice (0.92 / 0.63; 0.91 / 0.59) (p. 128); for the first, 0.92 + 0.63 − 1 does not equal the printed Youden Index 0.63.
  - Interrater ICC **0.457** in Results (p. 128) vs **0.47** in the Discussion (p. 130).
  - ADR text: 400 assessed, "370 had acceptable bowel cleansing (358 grade A + 112 grade B)" — the two printed counts do not sum to 370; Table 7 counts A 250, B 110, C 24, D 3 (387) (p. 129).
  - Discussion (p. 130) says Aronchick needs at least 90% of mucosa visible for grades 2 to 4, but Table 1 defines Poor (grade 4) as **<90%** seen (p. 123).
  - Table 6 discordance: the text (p. 127) says 25 were successful by investigators (HCS) but unsuccessful by experts (Aronchick); Table 6 (p. 128) puts 25 in investigator grade C/D with expert Aronchick 1–3 (2 + 7 + 16), i.e. the reverse, and 7 in investigator A/B with Aronchick 4–5.
  - Figure 2 shows **337** videos assessed by experts on Aronchick, while the expert Aronchick distribution in Table 3 totals **323** (pp. 124, 127).
- **Open:** ADR performance underpowered; test-retest from a different population; interrater ICC only "fair"; industry authorship (Disclosure, p. 121).
