## Summary

The Tokyo Guidelines 2018 (TG18) article on gallbladder drainage — the companion to the TG18 diagnostic-criteria and severity-grading papers — answering one question: in a patient with acute cholecystitis who is **too high-risk for cholecystectomy**, how should the gallbladder be drained? It is built on systematic reviews and new meta-analyses performed for TG18 (PRISMA-reported, Review Manager 5.3, Mantel-Haenszel random-effects, odds ratios).

Its answer is conservative and **differs from current US practice**: **percutaneous transhepatic gallbladder drainage (PTGBD) is the standard**, and the endoscopic routes — endoscopic transpapillary gallbladder drainage (ETGBD) and endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)-guided gallbladder drainage (EUS-GBD) — are positioned as options **only in high-volume institutes with skilled pancreaticobiliary endoscopists**. The stated reason is not inferior outcomes but non-establishment: systematic reviews showed no significant difference between PTGBD and EUS-GBD in technical success, clinical success or adverse events, but nearly all endoscopic reports came from expert centres, so the techniques "have not yet been established as standard procedures."

Within the transpapillary route it takes no position between **endoscopic naso-gallbladder drainage (ENGBD)** and **gallbladder stenting (EGBS)**: a TG18 meta-analysis of the two randomized trials found no significant difference in technical success, clinical success or adverse events, so the choice is left to patient background and endoscopist judgement. It also supplies procedural detail (device sizes for PTGBD, ENGBD, EGBS and EUS-GBD) and the **periprocedural antithrombotic rules** for percutaneous drainage, which it adopts from the Society of Interventional Radiology.

**Two recommendations in total**, both stated as "Recommendation 1, level B". The document gives no overall grading key in its text; the level labels below are the article's own.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Recommendations (the document's own labels)

**Q1. What are the standard gallbladder drainage methods for acute cholecystitis in surgically high-risk patients?**

> **PTGBD is recommended as a standard drainage method for surgically high-risk patients with acute cholecystitis (Recommendation 1, level B). However, ETGBD or EUS-GBD could be considered in high-volume institutes when performed by skilled endoscopists (level B).**

**Q2. What procedure for preoperative drainage should be used for endoscopic transpapillary gallbladder drainage — ENGBD or EGBS?**

> **Either ENGBD or EGBS may be considered, based on the patient's background and the endoscopist's decision (Recommendation 1, level B).**

### Percutaneous transhepatic gallbladder drainage (PTGBD)

- **First alternative to surgical intervention** in high-surgical-risk patients: less invasive with a lower adverse-event risk than cholecystectomy, and **technically easy for general clinicians**.
- **Technique:** ultrasound-guided transhepatic puncture with an **18-G needle** → guidewire → **6–10 Fr catheter** placed in the gallbladder under fluoroscopy.
- ⚠ **PTGBD in Grade III (severe) cholecystitis** — by TG13 grading — was reported to carry **higher mortality, higher readmission rates and prolonged hospital stay**.

### Percutaneous transhepatic gallbladder aspiration (PTGBA) — aspiration without a catheter

- **Not recommended as a standard procedure for all patients** with acute cholecystitis: aspiration can fail when bile has been replaced by **dense biliary sludge or pus**.
- The counter-evidence is acknowledged: in the latest international multicentre study, **clinical success within 3 days was significantly higher** with PTGBA than with PTGBD or EGBS (**no difference at 7 days**), and the **complication rate was lower**. Possible explanations given: the PTGBA groups may have contained more mild/moderate grade patients, and **saline lavage during PTGBA may be more effective than simple drainage**. Prospective randomized trials with standardized technique are called for.

### Coagulopathy and antithrombotic agents (adopted from Society of Interventional Radiology guidance)

| Situation | What to do before PTGBD |
|---|---|
| **Acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin)**, high thromboembolic risk | PTGBD **can be performed without discontinuing** it |
| **Clopidogrel** | **Discontinue 5 days before** PTGBD |
| **Anticoagulant** | Perform with **PT-INR <1.5 and heparin substitution** |
| **Both an antiplatelet and an anticoagulant** | **Avoid PTGBD** — no reliable data. Consider **ETGBD** where a skilled pancreaticobiliary endoscopist is available |

- The transpapillary route is also described as **especially suitable for severe coagulopathy, thrombocytopenia, or an anatomically inaccessible gallbladder**.

### ENGBD vs EGBS — technique and the TG18 meta-analysis

- **ENGBD:** after bile-duct cannulation, a **0.025- or 0.035-inch guidewire** is advanced into the cystic duct and then the gallbladder; the catheter is withdrawn over the wire and a **5–8.5 Fr pigtail naso-gallbladder tube** is placed. **Sphincterotomy is generally not required.**
- **EGBS:** identical steps, but a **6–10 Fr internal stent** is placed instead.
- **Meta-analysis of the two randomized trials (ENGBD vs EGBS)** — no statistically significant difference in any outcome:

| Outcome | Odds ratio (95% CI) |
|---|---|
| Technical success | 1.18 (0.36–3.89) |
| Clinical success | 1.82 (0.40–8.26) |
| Adverse events | 1.04 (0.29–3.81) |

- **Trade-off that the numbers do not capture:** ENGBD tubes are sometimes **removed by patients themselves because of discomfort**, but allow **bile flushing** through the transnasal tube; **EGBS carries a risk of stent obstruction**. TG18 judges the advantages and disadvantages approximately equal.
- **Failure modes of stent placement:** the cystic duct is frequently not visible on cholangiography; severe cystic duct stenosis or an impacted stone in the gallbladder neck blocks guidewire and stent advancement; the **tortuous valves of Heister** are difficult to traverse with standard guidewires.
- ⚠ Prolonged or unsuccessful attempts may cause **post-ERCP pancreatitis and perforation of the cystic duct or gallbladder** — selective biliary cannulation and guidewire technique are prerequisites.

### EUS-guided gallbladder drainage (EUS-GBD)

- **Technique:** puncture from the gastric body or antrum, or the duodenal bulb, under direct EUS visualization → **0.035-inch guidewire** through the outer sheath → tract dilation with a mechanical, electrocautery or balloon dilator → placement of a naso-gallbladder tube, **double-pigtail plastic stent (PS)**, or **self-expandable metal stent (SEMS)**. Lumen-apposing metal stents (LAMS), the flared end of a covered SEMS, and biflanged metal stents are all described as effective and safe.
- **Internal drainage (EGBS / EUS-GBD) causes less post-procedure pain than the external drainage of PTGBD.**
- **Pooled outcomes by stent type (198 patients):**

| Stent | Technical success | Clinical success | Adverse events |
|---|---|---|---|
| Naso-gallbladder tube | 100% (32/32) | 100% (32/32) | 12.5% (4/32) |
| Plastic stent | 100% (22/22) | 100% (22/22) | 18.2% |
| SEMS | 98.6% (72/73) | 94.5% (69/73) | 12.3% (9/73) |
| LAMS | 95.8% (68/71) | 90.1% (64/71) | 9.9% (7/71) |
| **Total** | **98.0% (194/198)** | **94.4% (187/198)** | **12.1% (24/198)** |

- No significant difference between stents; **LAMS may be ideal because it had the lowest adverse-event rate**.
- Systematic reviews show **no significant difference between PTGBD and EUS-GBD** in technical success, clinical success or adverse-event frequency. **No published study compares PTGBD with ETGBD directly.**

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **TG18 makes PTGBD the standard; [[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage|AGA 2023]] and [[asge-2024-therapeutic-eus-biliary|ASGE 2024]] default to the endoscopic routes.** Same tier (all guidelines/clinical practice updates), and **the AGA and ASGE documents are the newer publications**, so the wiki page follows them: EUS-GBD as the usual first choice in a patient who will not go to surgery, transpapillary drainage where future surgery is possible. TG18's reasoning is explicitly about **establishment, not outcome** — it reports no significant difference between PTGBD and EUS-GBD and restricts endoscopic drainage because the evidence came from expert centres. In a hospital without a skilled pancreaticobiliary endoscopist, TG18's position is still the operative one.
- **Grade III cholecystitis and percutaneous drainage.** TG18 reports **worse mortality, readmission and length of stay** with PTGBD in Grade III disease, without saying what to do instead. Read with the TG18 severity paper's own statement that the grading **does not measure surgical risk or difficulty** ([[tg18-2018-cholecystitis-criteria]]), this is a flag, not an algorithm.
- **PTGBA (aspiration without a catheter) is rejected as a standard while the newest data favour it** on 3-day clinical success and complication rate. TG18 states the conflict openly and attributes it to possible case-mix differences and to saline lavage; it is unresolved.
- The periprocedural antithrombotic rules are **imported from interventional-radiology consensus**, not derived here, and they address **percutaneous** drainage only — TG18 gives no antithrombotic thresholds for the endoscopic routes.
