## Summary

Open-label randomized controlled trial (RCT) at 18 centers in four countries (India, Italy, Mexico, Spain) comparing **goal-directed aggressive vs moderate fluid resuscitation, both with lactated Ringer's solution**, in [[acute-pancreatitis|acute pancreatitis]] (AP). Enrolled May 2020 – September 2021. Primary outcome: development of moderately severe or severe AP during the hospitalization. Main safety outcome: fluid overload.

Patients were **not** of "any severity": the trial enrolled adults diagnosed by the Revised Atlanta Classification who presented ≤24 hr after pain onset and were enrolled ≤8 hr after diagnosis, and **excluded anyone already meeting criteria for moderately severe or severe disease at baseline** (shock, respiratory failure, renal failure) as well as baseline heart failure (New York Heart Association [NYHA] class II–IV), uncontrolled hypertension, hyper-/hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, hypercalcemia, life expectancy <1 yr, chronic pancreatitis, chronic renal failure, and decompensated cirrhosis.

The trial was **halted at the first planned interim analysis** (249 patients analyzed; planned sample 744, first interim planned at 248) because safety outcomes were significantly worse in the aggressive arm with no counterbalancing efficacy signal. It is the primary-evidence basis for abandoning routine early high-volume hydration in AP.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Fluid protocol (Figure 1)

| Time point | Aggressive arm | Moderate arm |
|---|---|---|
| At 0 hr | Bolus **20 mL/kg**, then infusion **3 mL/kg/hr** | Infusion **1.5 mL/kg/hr**, preceded by a **10 mL/kg bolus only if hypovolemic** |
| At 3 hr | Safety checkpoint — suspected fluid overload → decrease or stop infusion | Same safety checkpoint |
| At 12, 24, 48, 72 hr — hypovolemia | Bolus 20 mL/kg, then 3 mL/kg/hr; extra 20 mL/kg boluses if urine output <0.5 mL/kg/hr or systolic blood pressure <90 mm Hg | Bolus 10 mL/kg, then 1.5 mL/kg/hr; extra 10 mL/kg boluses for the same triggers |
| At 12, 24, 48, 72 hr — normovolemia | 3 mL/kg/hr; stop after **48 hr** if oral feeding tolerated >8 hr | 1.5 mL/kg/hr; stop after **20 hr** if oral feeding tolerated >8 hr |
| At 12, 24, 48, 72 hr — suspected fluid overload | Decrease or stop; same 48-hr stop rule | Decrease or stop; same 20-hr stop rule |

- Oral feeding started at 12 hr if the Patient-Reported Outcome Scale in Acute Pancreatitis (PAN-PROMISE) pain intensity was <5 (scale 0–70, higher = worse); fluids stopped once oral intake was tolerated for 8 hr.
- Median lactated Ringer's delivered over the first 48 hr: **7.8 L** (interquartile range [IQR] 6.5–9.8) aggressive vs **5.5 L** (IQR 4.0–6.8) moderate; the largest between-group difference in volume was in the first 12 hr.

### Outcomes

- **No efficacy difference.** Moderately severe or severe AP: **22.1% aggressive vs 17.3% moderate**; adjusted relative risk (RR) 1.30 (95% confidence interval [CI] 0.78–2.18), P = 0.32.
- **Fluid overload roughly tripled: 20.5% vs 6.3%**; adjusted RR 2.85 (95% CI 1.36–5.94), P = 0.004. Fluid overload = ≥2 of the 3 criteria symptoms, physical signs, and imaging evidence of hypervolemia, with acute respiratory distress syndrome ruled out.
- Moderate-to-severe fluid overload 4.9% vs 0.8% (adjusted RR 3.62, 95% CI 0.37–35.22); signs of fluid overload 26.2% vs 11.0% (adjusted RR 2.36, 95% CI 1.33–4.19). Most episodes were nonsevere. Median time to fluid overload 34 hr vs 46 hr.
- Median hospital stay **6 days** (IQR 4–8) vs **5 days** (IQR 3–7). Higher symptom intensity at 12 hr in the aggressive arm (median PAN-PROMISE 23 vs 18).
- Nonsignificant trends toward harm with aggressive resuscitation: necrotizing pancreatitis 13.9% vs 7.1%, intensive care unit (ICU) admission 6.6% vs 1.6%, persistent organ failure 6.6% vs 1.6%.
- Prespecified subgroups by baseline hypovolemia and by systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) did not differ materially from the overall result.
- Bottom line: early aggressive fluid resuscitation caused more fluid overload without improving clinical outcomes.

## Relevance to Wiki

- [[acute-pancreatitis]] → Therapeutics / fluid resuscitation: primary-evidence support for the moderate regimen (1.5 mL/kg/hr, 10 mL/kg bolus only if hypovolemic) and against the 20 mL/kg + 3 mL/kg/hr aggressive regimen.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Direction of the recommendation agrees with [[acg-2024-acute-pancreatitis]]; the vocabulary does not.** The trial's *moderate* arm is numerically what ACG 2024 prints as "moderately aggressive" hydration — initial rate no more than 1.5 mL/kg/hr without hypovolemia, 10 mL/kg bolus if hypovolemic. But ACG 2024 is explicitly skeptical of **goal-directed** therapy, finding insufficient evidence that it improves organ failure, infected necrosis, or mortality, whereas both WATERFALL arms were goal-directed at fixed checkpoints.
- Older observational data and small trials favoring vigorous hydration are superseded by this RCT.
- **Underpowered for efficacy** — terminated at the first of two planned interim analyses, so the efficacy comparison is not definitive; the data and safety monitoring board judged that a much larger trial would expose many more patients to fluid overload.
- Open-label design; blinding was impractical because physicians had to titrate fluid to volume status.
- Patients with baseline organ failure were excluded, so resuscitation of **established** moderately severe or severe AP is not addressed. A mandated minimum of 48 hr (aggressive) and 20 hr (moderate) of intravenous fluid in patients already tolerating oral intake may not reflect real-world practice; the authors call for trials of still more restrictive strategies.
