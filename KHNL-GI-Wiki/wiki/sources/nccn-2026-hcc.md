## How This Document Grades and Labels Recommendations

NCCN does not use Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) and does not number its recommendations. It uses two parallel schemes, both reproduced on its own categories page:

| Category of evidence and consensus | Definition |
|---|---|
| Category 1 | High-level evidence (≥1 randomized phase 3 trial or high-quality robust meta-analysis) + uniform NCCN consensus (≥85% of Panel) that the intervention is appropriate |
| Category 2A | Lower-level evidence + uniform NCCN consensus (≥85%) |
| Category 2B | Lower-level evidence + NCCN consensus ≥50% but <85% |
| Category 3 | Any level of evidence, major NCCN disagreement |

**All recommendations are category 2A unless otherwise indicated** — stated on every algorithm page. The second scheme is the **categories of preference** — *Preferred*, *Other recommended*, *Useful in certain circumstances* — and the document states that all recommendations listed are considered appropriate. Where this page gives no category, the document gave none (i.e., 2A by default).

## Summary

The guideline covers screening, imaging diagnosis, workup, and stage-directed treatment of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). Screening is ultrasound (US) + alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) every 6 months in defined at-risk groups. Diagnosis in at-risk patients is imaging-based: multiphasic contrast computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), interpreted with the Liver Imaging Reporting and Data System (LI-RADS); biopsy is reserved for defined indications (HCC-B). Treatment allocation turns on liver function (Child-Turcotte-Pugh [CTP] class, with Model for End-Stage Liver Disease [MELD] and albumin-bilirubin [ALBI] grade as adjuncts), portal hypertension, tumor burden/location, and performance status.

Three clinical presentations drive the algorithm: potentially resectable/transplantable (HCC-4), liver-confined but unresectable and transplant-ineligible (HCC-5), and extrahepatic/metastatic (HCC-6). Curative options are resection, ablation, and transplantation under United Network for Organ Sharing (UNOS) criteria; locoregional options are ablation, arterially directed therapy, and radiation therapy (RT); systemic therapy is used for advanced disease or progression.

Version 1.2026 changes: **nivolumab + ipilimumab moved from Other recommended to Preferred and to category 1** in first-line systemic therapy (CheckMate-9DW), with a new counselling footnote on early death and immune-mediated toxicity; the Principles of Radiation Therapy (HCC-H) were rewritten (the blanket phrase "all tumors irrespective of location" was **removed**, stereotactic body radiation therapy [SBRT] dose band revised to 27.5–60 Gy with biologically effective dose [BED10] >72 Gy, palliative 8 Gy × 1 added); ablation size guidance was rewritten into explicit thresholds (HCC-G); pembrolizumab is recommended regardless of microsatellite instability (MSI) status; and subcutaneous hyaluronidase formulations of atezolizumab, nivolumab, and pembrolizumab may substitute for the intravenous (IV) agents.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Screening (HCC-1)

- **Who:** CTP class A or B cirrhosis of any etiology (hepatitis B or C, alcohol-associated, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis, other); CTP class C cirrhosis **only if a transplant candidate**; hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection **without** cirrhosis.
- **How:** US + AFP. Recommended interval **every 6 months** (HCC-A). Contrast-enhanced multiphase CT/MRI are more sensitive than US and may be used if US does not detect nodules or visualization is poor.
- **Act on the result:**

| Finding | Next step |
|---|---|
| AFP positive **or** US nodule ≥10 mm | Additional workup (diagnostic pathway, HCC-2) |
| US nodule <10 mm | Repeat US + AFP in 3–6 mo |
| US negative (no observation, or only definitely benign observations) | Repeat US + AFP in 6 mo |

- Positive or rising AFP prompts CT or MRI **regardless of US result**.
- Continue screening after sustained virologic response in treated viral hepatitis.

### Imaging diagnosis (HCC-2, HCC-A)

- Multiphasic abdominal CT (preferred) or MRI (preferred), or contrast-enhanced US. Major imaging features: nonrim arterial-phase hyperenhancement, nonperipheral washout, enhancing capsule, threshold growth.
- **Criteria apply only to high-risk patients** — cirrhosis (excluding cirrhosis from congenital hepatic fibrosis, vascular causes, or age <18 y), chronic hepatitis B, or current/prior HCC. LI-RADS criteria have **not** been proposed for nodules <10 mm.
- High specificity, modest sensitivity: a lesion meeting criteria can be treated as HCC without biopsy; failure to meet criteria does **not** exclude HCC.
- Definitely benign or no observation → return to screening in 6 mo. Not definitely HCC and not definitely benign → individualized workup (further imaging or biopsy) after multidisciplinary discussion.
- Intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (iCCA) and combined HCC–cholangiocarcinoma (cHCC-CCA) cannot be diagnosed non-invasively — biopsy or resection is needed.
- In a United Kingdom audit of 418 patients being evaluated for systemic therapy, ~7% with a radiographic diagnosis of HCC had an alternative histologic diagnosis (CCA or cHCC-CCA).

### Core needle biopsy indications (HCC-B)

- Lesion highly suspicious at CT/MRI but not meeting HCC imaging features.
- Lesion meets HCC imaging features **but** the patient is not high-risk; or has cardiac cirrhosis, congenital hepatic fibrosis, or cirrhosis from a vascular disorder (Budd-Chiari syndrome, hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia, nodular regenerative hyperplasia); or has elevated CA 19-9 or carcinoembryonic antigen with suspicion of iCCA/cHCC-CCA.
- Confirmation of metastatic disease would change management, including trial enrollment.
- **Obtain biopsy before ablation** if biopsy is being considered. Surgical resection or definitive locoregional therapy *without* biopsy should be considered with multidisciplinary review.
- Repeat biopsy if the first is non-diagnostic or discordant with imaging/biomarkers.

### Workup once HCC is confirmed (HCC-3)

History and physical; hepatitis panel; bilirubin, transaminases, alkaline phosphatase; prothrombin time or international normalized ratio (INR), albumin, blood urea nitrogen, creatinine; complete blood count and platelets; AFP; chest CT; bone scan if clinically indicated; abdomen/pelvis CT or MRI with contrast; consider hepatology referral. Assess portal hypertension (varices, splenomegaly, thrombocytopenia).

### Liver functional assessment (HCC-E)

- CTP class A = 5–6 points (good operative risk), B = 7–9 (moderate), C = 10–15 (poor). The full CTP and MELD scoring tables are on [[cirrhosis]].
- **ALBI grade** = [log₁₀ bilirubin (µmol/L) × 0.66] + [albumin (g/L) × −0.085]: grade 1 ≤ −2.60; grade 2 > −2.60 to ≤ −1.39; grade 3 > −1.39.
- Most systemic therapy data come from CTP A. CTP B use requires individualized decisions and close toxicity monitoring; CTP C or progressive decompensation should be offered systemic therapy only in select circumstances or on trial.

### Resection and transplant criteria (HCC-4, HCC-F)

| Pathway | Criteria as the document states them |
|---|---|
| **Resection** | CTP class A or B; no portal hypertension; suitable tumor location; adequate liver reserve; suitable liver remnant. HCC-F adds: generally CTP A without portal hypertension (limited resection feasible with mild portal hypertension in small series); solitary mass without major vascular invasion; future liver remnant ≥20% without cirrhosis and ≥30%–40% with CTP A cirrhosis, with adequate vascular and biliary inflow/outflow |
| **Transplant (UNOS criteria)** | AFP ≤1000 ng/mL **and** a single lesion ≥2 cm and ≤5 cm, **or** 2–3 lesions each ≥1 cm and ≤3 cm; no macrovascular involvement; no extrahepatic disease. Extended criteria/downstaging protocols are available through UNOS |

- Resection is **controversial but can be considered** for limited resectable multifocal disease and for major vascular invasion.
- Preoperative portal vein embolization should be considered before major resection in chronic liver disease.
- All patients should be evaluated for transplant candidacy with multidisciplinary review; highly selected CTP B patients may undergo limited resection.
- Treatment order on HCC-4: resection (preferred) or transplant (preferred) if transplant criteria met; locoregional therapy with **ablation preferred** among the locoregional options; bridge therapy as indicated while listed.

### Post-treatment surveillance (HCC-4, HCC-5)

- Imaging (multiphasic abdomen MRI or multiphase CT, plus chest CT and CT/MRI pelvis) **every 3–6 mo for 2 y, then every 6 mo**; AFP on the same schedule. Continue imaging and AFP **for at least 5 years**; thereafter screening depends on HCC risk factors.
- Refer carriers of hepatitis to hepatology for antiviral therapy if not already done.

### Locoregional therapy — thresholds (HCC-G)

- **Ablation** (microwave/radiofrequency or percutaneous ethanol injection): efficacy of thermal ablation is directly related to tumor size. Properly located tumors **≤3 cm** in well-selected patients — ablation may be **definitive curative-intent** treatment at experienced centers. Local control **diminishes for 3–5 cm** tumors, which may need combination or alternative approaches. **Not recommended for tumors >5 cm.** Percutaneous ethanol injection should be limited to tumors **<3 cm** and leaves no ablative margin.
- Avoid ablating lesions adjacent to major vessels, major bile ducts, diaphragm, and other intra-abdominal organs.
- **Arterially directed therapies** (bland transarterial embolization, transarterial chemoembolization [TACE], drug-eluting-bead TACE, yttrium-90 [Y-90] radioembolization): relatively **contraindicated when bilirubin >3 mg/dL** unless segmental treatment is possible. Y-90 radioembolization carries increased risk of radiation-induced liver disease when **bilirubin >2 mg/dL**.
- Y-90 dosing: delivery of **≥205 Gy** to the tumor may improve overall survival; **>400 Gy to ≤25% of the liver** is recommended in CTP A patients. Radiation segmentectomy with Y-90 or ablative-dose SBRT for anatomically limited disease.
- Arterially directed therapy can be used with limited portal vein tumor invasion — Y-90 may be appropriate for **segmental or lobar** portal vein involvement rather than main portal vein thrombosis.

### Radiation therapy (HCC-H)

- RT is an option for unresectable or medically inoperable disease. Image-guided RT is **strongly recommended** with RT, intensity-modulated RT, and SBRT.
- **SBRT (typically 3–5 fractions) can be utilized for patients with 1 to 5 tumors**, assuming sufficient uninvolved liver and respectable liver radiation tolerance; extrahepatic disease should be minimal and addressed in a comprehensive plan. SBRT can be considered as an **alternative to ablation/embolization**, or when those fail or are contraindicated.
- Most data are in CTP A, with growing experience in CTP B7; safety data are limited for CTP B8 or worse, where dose modification and lower normal-tissue doses are needed. Safety in CTP C has not been established.
- Doses: SBRT **27.5–60 Gy in 3–5 fractions (BED10 >72 Gy)** preferred if constraints are met; hypofractionation 37.5–72 Gy in 10–15 fractions; conventional fractionation 50–66 Gy in 25–33 fractions; palliative **8 Gy in 1 fraction** for pain from extensive liver burden.
- Proton beam therapy may be appropriate where reduced dose to adjacent normal tissue matters. Palliative RT for symptomatic bone or brain metastases and for painful extensive liver burden in CTP A or B.

### Systemic therapy (HCC-I)

Order within each cell does not indicate preference.

| First line | Regimens (category as printed) |
|---|---|
| **Preferred** | Atezolizumab + bevacizumab (category 1); durvalumab + tremelimumab-actl (category 1); **ipilimumab + nivolumab (category 1 — moved to Preferred and upgraded this version)** |
| **Other recommended** | Durvalumab (category 1); lenvatinib (category 1); sorafenib (category 1); tislelizumab-jsgr (category 1); **pembrolizumab (category 2B)** |

| Subsequent line (after progression) | Regimens |
|---|---|
| **Options** | Cabozantinib (category 1); regorafenib (category 1) |
| **Other recommended** | Consider the Preferred and Other recommended first-line regimens |
| **Useful in certain circumstances** | **Ramucirumab — only if AFP ≥400 ng/mL** (category 1); nivolumab; for MSI-high/mismatch repair-deficient tumors, dostarlimab-gxly (category 2B); for *RET* fusion-positive tumors, selpercatinib (category 2B); for *NTRK1/2/3* fusion-positive tumors, entrectinib, larotrectinib, or repotrectinib |

- **Before atezolizumab + bevacizumab:** patients should have adequate endoscopic evaluation and management of esophageal varices **within approximately 6 months prior to treatment**, or per institutional practice, based on assessed bleeding risk.
- **Ipilimumab + nivolumab counselling (footnote, new):** CheckMate-9DW showed improved overall survival versus lenvatinib or sorafenib, but a **higher rate of death during the first 6 months** in the combination arm and **29% of patients requiring high-dose steroids** for immune-mediated adverse events (Yau T, et al. Lancet 2025;405:1851-1864).
- Pembrolizumab is a recommended option **regardless of MSI status**; its FDA approval is for MSI-high tumors in the subsequent-line setting.
- Subcutaneous substitutions with different dosing/administration instructions: atezolizumab + hyaluronidase-tqjs for IV atezolizumab; nivolumab + hyaluronidase-nvhy for IV nivolumab (**monotherapy only — not approved for concurrent use with IV ipilimumab**); pembrolizumab + berahyaluronidase alfa-pmph for IV pembrolizumab.
- Therapies listed may have limited safety data in CTP B or C; use with extreme caution when bilirubin is elevated.

### Biomarker testing (HCC-J)

- **There is no established indication for routine molecular profiling in HCC.** Multigene panel testing may be warranted for atypical histology, cHCC-CCA histology, unusual presentations, or trial enrollment.
- **No established role for MSI, mismatch repair, tumor mutational burden, or programmed death ligand 1 testing in HCC** — checkpoint inhibition was approved without selection for these markers.
- Evidence is insufficient for definitive germline-testing criteria in hepatobiliary cancers.

### Mixed HCC-CCA (HCC-C)

- 1%–10% of primary liver tumors. Resection is standard for resectable disease; liver-directed locoregional therapy may be appropriate for limited unresectable hepatic disease.
- Advanced disease: cisplatin/gemcitabine + durvalumab or pembrolizumab is an appropriate first-line choice (active in both histologies). At progression, use a matched targeted therapy if a target is present; otherwise ipilimumab + nivolumab or regorafenib. Consider repeat biopsy at recurrence/progression to establish the dominant histology. Multigene panel testing should be considered in all advanced cases.

### Staging reproduced (ST-1, ST-2)

- American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM 8th edition for hepatocellular carcinoma (T1a solitary ≤2 cm; T1b solitary >2 cm without vascular invasion; T2 solitary >2 cm with vascular invasion or multiple tumors none >5 cm; T3 multiple with at least one >5 cm; T4 major vascular branch involvement or direct invasion of adjacent organs/visceral peritoneum), with prognostic groups, histologic grade G1–G4, and Ishak fibrosis score (F0 = 0–4, F1 = 5–6).
- Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) 2022 prognostic-group definitions are reproduced: 0 very early (single ≤2 cm, preserved liver function, performance status [PS] 0); A early (single, or ≤3 nodules each ≤3 cm, preserved function, PS 0); B intermediate (multinodular, preserved function, PS 0); C advanced (portal invasion and/or extrahepatic spread, preserved function, PS 1–2); D terminal (any burden, end-stage liver function, PS 3–4) — "preserved liver function" excepting those with tumor burden acceptable for transplant.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Grading vocabulary differs from the society guidelines on the same topics.** NCCN categories 1/2A/2B/3 plus preference tiers are consensus labels, not GRADE strength-of-recommendation ratings; they are not interchangeable with the American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD) or American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) wording cited elsewhere on the HCC page.
- **Screening population is broader than a cirrhosis-only rule:** NCCN screens CTP C cirrhosis only when the patient is a transplant candidate, and screens HBV infection without cirrhosis.
- **Transplant selection is written as UNOS criteria, not Milan**; the AFP ≤1000 ng/mL ceiling is part of the criterion and has no counterpart in the original Milan size/number rule.
- NCCN reproduces BCLC 2022 as a staging table but allocates treatment from its own three-presentation algorithm rather than from BCLC stage; the two converge on function- and burden-based allocation.
- Optimal first-line choice among the three Preferred category 1 regimens is undefined — the document states order does not indicate preference, and CheckMate-9DW's early-death signal is handled by counselling rather than by a restriction.
- The role of adjuvant or perioperative systemic therapy after resection/ablation is not addressed in the algorithm pages.
