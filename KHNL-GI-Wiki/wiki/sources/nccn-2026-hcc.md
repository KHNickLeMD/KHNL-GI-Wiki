## Summary

The NCCN Hepatocellular Carcinoma guideline covers the workup, diagnosis, staging, and stage-directed management of HCC in the at-risk (predominantly cirrhotic or chronic-HBV) population. Diagnosis follows a non-invasive imaging pathway in at-risk patients (multiphasic contrast CT/MRI, with LI-RADS used to assign HCC probability) and reserves biopsy for indeterminate lesions or when tissue would change management. Liver function (Child-Pugh class, and increasingly ALBI), performance status, tumor burden, and vascular invasion/extrahepatic spread together drive treatment allocation, broadly aligned with the BCLC framework used elsewhere in the wiki.

Management is multidisciplinary and tiered: potentially curative options (surgical resection, ablation, and liver transplantation within accepted criteria) for limited disease with adequate liver reserve; locoregional therapy (TACE, TARE/Y90, and — emphasized in this version — external-beam/stereotactic body radiation therapy) for intermediate disease; and systemic therapy for advanced disease (macrovascular invasion, extrahepatic spread, or progression after locoregional therapy). The headline change in Version 1.2026 is in first-line systemic therapy: **ipilimumab + nivolumab was elevated to a Preferred, category 1 first-line option** (CheckMate-9DW), joining atezolizumab + bevacizumab and durvalumab + tremelimumab. The version also strengthens the role of radiation therapy (new Principles of Radiation Therapy and locoregional therapy footnotes) and adds subcutaneous substitution options for several checkpoint inhibitors.

## Key Findings / Claims

- **Diagnosis in at-risk patients is imaging-based.** In patients with cirrhosis or chronic HBV, a characteristic multiphasic CT/MRI appearance (arterial-phase hyperenhancement with washout ± capsule) supports HCC diagnosis without biopsy; LI-RADS categorizes lesions and indeterminate findings are biopsied or re-imaged. Pathologic confirmation is required outside the at-risk setting.
- **Treatment is allocated by liver function + tumor burden + performance status.** Resection is favored for limited disease with preserved function and adequate remnant without clinically significant portal hypertension; ablation for small lesions in non-surgical candidates; transplantation for patients meeting accepted criteria; locoregional therapy and/or systemic therapy for larger or advanced disease.
- **First-line systemic therapy (Preferred, all category 1):** atezolizumab + bevacizumab; durvalumab + tremelimumab; **ipilimumab + nivolumab (new this version)**. **Other Recommended (category 1):** durvalumab monotherapy, lenvatinib, sorafenib. An upper endoscopy to evaluate/manage varices is expected within ~6 months before starting atezolizumab + bevacizumab because of GI-bleed risk.
- **CheckMate-9DW caveat.** Ipilimumab + nivolumab improved overall survival vs lenvatinib/sorafenib but carried a higher rate of death during the first ~6 months and a high rate of immune-mediated toxicity (≈29% required high-dose steroids); counsel patients about early immune-mediated toxicity risk (Yau T, et al. Lancet 2025;405:1851-1864).
- **Radiation therapy emphasized.** New Principles of Radiation Therapy (HCC-H) and locoregional-therapy footnotes position EBRT/SBRT (with image guidance) as an option for tumors irrespective of location, as an alternative to ablation/embolization or when those fail or are contraindicated; SBRT is typically delivered in 3–5 fractions for a limited number of tumors with adequate uninvolved liver.
- **Subcutaneous checkpoint-inhibitor substitutions.** SC formulations (atezolizumab-hyaluronidase; nivolumab-hyaluronidase for monotherapy; pembrolizumab–berahyaluronidase alfa) may substitute for the corresponding IV agents, with their own dosing/administration instructions.
- **Pembrolizumab.** A recommended option in the subsequent-line setting; FDA-approved for MSI-H tumors, and per this version recommended regardless of MSI status in eligible patients.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Algorithm-based guideline (category 2A consensus by default), not a GRADE systematic review — strength-of-evidence labels differ from the AASLD/AGA guidance already cited on the HCC page. The two are concordant on the major pathways; NCCN's distinction is the explicit first-line tiering and the category 1 elevation of ipilimumab + nivolumab.
- NCCN and AASLD frame staging differently (NCCN's algorithm tables vs AASLD's BCLC 2022); both converge on function- and burden-based allocation.
