## Summary
- Update of the ASCO guideline on immune-related adverse events (irAEs) in adults with cancer treated with immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICPi) monotherapy (anti–cytotoxic T-lymphocyte–associated antigen-4 [CTLA-4], anti–programmed cell death-1 [PD-1], anti–PD ligand 1 [PD-L1]); toxicities of ICPi combined with chemotherapy (CTX) or other agents are out of scope (Introduction; Methods).
- Systematic review of literature 2017–2021 (175 studies), mostly retrospective case series/reports → **all recommendations are expert consensus–based, benefits outweigh harms, strength of recommendation: moderate** (Recommendations preamble, p 4076). No per-recommendation evidence grades in any table; this rating applies to every row below.
- Organ-based tables (Tables 1–11) give, per toxicity: workup, grade definitions (mostly Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events [CTCAE] v5.0), and grade-by-grade management; Table 12 covers prevention of corticosteroid adverse effects. The guideline numbers no individual recommendations; locators below are the printed table/section numbers (e.g., Table 2, 2.1) and journal page.
- GI-relevant core: colitis graded by stools/day over baseline; steroid 1 mg/kg/day prednisone from grade 2 (1–2 mg/kg/day at grade 3–4), early infliximab or vedolizumab for steroid-refractory disease or high-risk endoscopic features; endoscopy highly recommended at grade ≥2; fecal calprotectin ≤116 µg/g or mucosal healing as the target before resuming ICPi. Hepatitis graded by AST/ALT and bilirubin × upper limit of normal (ULN); steroids from grade 2 if no improvement after 3–5 days, mycophenolate for steroid-refractory cases; **infliximab contraindicated for immune-related hepatitis**.

## Key Findings / Claims

### General principles
*Bottom Line box (p 4074) and abstract; "recommended" that clinicians manage toxicities as follows. Rating for all: consensus based, benefits outweigh harms (strength moderate, p 4076).*
- Patients and family caregivers should receive timely, up-to-date education on immunotherapies, mechanism and possible irAEs before starting and throughout treatment and survivorship.
- High level of suspicion that new symptoms are treatment-related.
- **Grade 1:** in general continue ICPi with close monitoring — except some neurologic, hematologic and cardiac toxicities.
- **Grade 2:** consider holding ICPi for most; resume when symptoms and/or labs revert to ≤ grade 1. Corticosteroids (initial prednisone 0.5–1 mg/kg/d or equivalent) may be given.
- **Grade 3:** hold ICPi; start high-dose corticosteroids (prednisone 1–2 mg/kg/d or equivalent). Taper over **at least 4–6 weeks**. No improvement after 48–72 hours of high-dose steroid → infliximab may be offered for some toxicities.
- **Rechallenge:** once ≤ grade 1, rechallenge may be offered; caution, especially with early-onset irAEs. Dose adjustments not recommended. After toxicity from combination with a CTLA-4 antagonist, rechallenge with PD-1/PD-L1 monotherapy may be offered once recovered to ≤ grade 1.
- **Grade 4:** in general permanent discontinuation — except endocrinopathies controlled by hormone replacement.
- Grade definitions are in each table and mostly follow CTCAE v5.0.
- Section 2.0 narrative (p 4077): GI irAEs include colitis, hepatitis, gastritis and enterocolitis; median onset 6 weeks (range 1–107.5 weeks). Colitis symptoms: abdominal pain, nausea, diarrhea, blood and mucus in stool, fever. Hepatitis symptoms: jaundice, nausea/vomiting, anorexia, right-sided abdominal pain, dark (tea-colored) urine, easy bleeding or bruising.

### GI toxicities — colitis (Table 2, 2.1)
*Table 2, 2.1, pp 4081–4082. Grading "based on CTCAE for diarrhea, as most often used clinically". No row-level grades printed (all consensus, moderate).*

**Workup and evaluation — G2**
- Blood (complete blood count [CBC], comprehensive metabolic panel [CMP], thyroid-stimulating hormone [TSH]) and stool (culture, *C. diff*, parasite, cytomegalovirus [CMV] or other viral etiology, ova and parasites [O&P] if appropriate) **should** be done at initial presentation; also considered for immunosuppressant-refractory cases.
- Consider fecal lactoferrin (stratify who needs more urgent endoscopy) and calprotectin (follow disease activity).
- Screening labs (HIV, hepatitis A and B, tuberculosis [TB] testing), repeated annually in patients who require biologic treatment (eg, infliximab or vedolizumab) for >1 year until treatment is completed.
- Consider reviewing concomitant medications that could alter the gut microbiome and their indications for prolonged use (eg, proton pump inhibitors, antibiotics, probiotics).
- Imaging, eg, computed tomography (CT) abdomen/pelvis for colitis-related symptoms (abdominal pain, bleeding) to rule out complications incl. typhlitis, bowel perforation or abscess.
- GI endoscopy or colonoscopy with biopsy **should be considered** for positive stool inflammatory markers or colitis-related symptoms — colonic ulceration predicts a steroid-refractory course that may require early infliximab.
- Repeat colonoscopy may be considered for grade ≥2 to document complete remission, especially if ICPi will be resumed. **Mucosal healing on repeat endoscopy and/or fecal calprotectin ≤116 µg/g** can be considered the treatment target for stopping biologics and resuming ICPi.

**Workup and evaluation — G3–4:** all of the above; consider inpatient care.

**Grades and management**

| Grade (CTCAE diarrhea) | Management (Table 2, 2.1) |
|---|---|
| **All patients** | Counsel to report immediately: abdominal pain, nausea, cramping, blood or mucus in stool, change in bowel habits, fever, abdominal distention, constipation. For grade ≥2: consider permanently discontinuing CTLA-4 agents; may restart PD-1/PD-L1 agents if recovered to ≤G1; concurrent immunosuppressant maintenance only if clinically indicated in individual cases |
| **G1:** increase of <4 stools/day over baseline; mild increase in ostomy output vs baseline | Continue ICPi; alternatively hold temporarily and resume if toxicity does not exceed G1 or resolves. May use supportive care (eg, loperamide) if infection ruled out, diarrhea only without colitis-related symptoms, as a temporary measure. Monitor for dehydration; recommend dietary changes. Close monitoring by phone/electronic system every 3 days or more often until stable. May obtain gastroenterology consult for prolonged G1 and consider endoscopy with biopsies |
| **G2:** increase of 4–6 stools/day over baseline; moderate increase in ostomy output | Hold ICPi at least until recovery to G1. Loperamide as for G1. Consider gastroenterology consult for ≥G2. **Corticosteroids unless diarrhea is transient: prednisone 1 mg/kg/day or equivalent** until ≤G1, then taper over 4–6 weeks. Consider adding infliximab (anti–tumor necrosis factor [TNF]) or vedolizumab (anti-integrin) if corticosteroid-refractory (no decrease by one grade in 72 hours), corticosteroid-dependent, or high-risk endoscopic features on initial endoscopy. Once ≤G1, taper over 4–6 weeks; shorter tapers may be considered if also on biologics. **Endoscopy (esophagogastroduodenoscopy [EGD] or colonoscopy) highly recommended for grade ≥2** to stratify for early biologics. Resuming ICPi after ≤G1 may be considered when steroid taper completed, risks/benefits reviewed if maintained on biologics, and/or endoscopic + histologic remission achieved; fecal calprotectin ≤116 µg/g may be a surrogate for that remission. Resuming PD-1/PD-L1 carries lower flare risk; CTLA-4 inhibitor can still be considered in selected cases (no response yet or inadequate response) |
| **G3:** increase of ≥7 stools/day over baseline; incontinence; hospitalization indicated; severe increase in ostomy output; limiting self-care activities of daily living (ADL) | G2 measures plus: **corticosteroids prednisone 1–2 mg/kg/d or equivalent** until ≤G1, then taper over 4–6 weeks; consider intravenous (IV) methylprednisolone, especially if concurrent upper GI inflammation suspected. Consider **early infliximab or vedolizumab** with steroids for high-risk endoscopic features on initial endoscopy or inadequate steroid response (persistent symptoms after 3 days). Consider hospitalization for dehydration or electrolyte imbalance. Consider repeat colonoscopy if immunosuppression-refractory. Should consider permanently discontinuing CTLA-4 agents |
| **G4:** life-threatening consequences; urgent intervention indicated | G2–G3 measures plus: **permanently discontinue treatment**. Should provide inpatient care. **Methylprednisolone 1–2 mg/kg/d or equivalent** until ≤G1, then taper over 4–6 weeks. Consider early biologics (infliximab or vedolizumab) if inadequate steroid response after 3 days. Consider lower GI endoscopy if symptoms refractory despite treatment or concern for new infection |

- **High-risk endoscopic features** (Table 2 footnote a): large deep ulceration, multiple ulcers, extensive colitis beyond the left colon.
- Table 2 gives no infliximab or vedolizumab dose, interval or number of infusions.

**Additional considerations (Table 2, 2.1)**
- Refractory to previous immunosuppressants → may consider fecal microbiota transplant, the Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitor tofacitinib, or the interleukin (IL)-12–blocking antibody ustekinumab.
- Concurrent irAE hepatitis + irAE colitis (less common): management may include permanently discontinuing ICPi and other systemic immunosuppressants that treat both (eg, prednisone and mycophenolate). **Infliximab is contraindicated for hepatic irAE.**
- Enteritis and/or gastritis alone: uncommon; endoscopy with biopsy is the recommended evaluation; may be managed like colitis (steroid and/or biologics).

**Colitis discussion (section 2.0, pp 4077–4083) — condensed**
- Colitis frequency 8%–27%; diarrhea up to 54% with anti–CTLA-4, especially CTLA-4 + PD-1 combination; anti–PD-1 monotherapy diarrhea ≤19%; perforation ~1% of colitis cases (p 4077).
- Onset most often 5–10 weeks after start; can occur/recur months after stopping. PD-1/PD-L1 + CTX → possible early-onset diarrhea (1–2 weeks), so identify the causative agent (pp 4077–4080).
- Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) use reported to be associated with more ICPi enterocolitis — care with NSAIDs (p 4080).
- Resembles inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) clinically and radiologically. CT: mesenteric vessel engorgement, bowel wall thickening, fluid-filled colonic distention; descending colon most often involved. Histology: more inflammation than classic IBD — mixed lamina propria infiltrate (neutrophils, lymphocytes, plasma cells, eosinophils), chronic damage (crypt architecture distortion, Paneth cell metaplasia); diffuse in 75% (p 4080).
- Diarrhea grade ≥2 or colitis symptoms → corticosteroid 1–2 mg/kg still first line. Endoscopy adds objective severity; special consideration for endoscopy when ICPi is combined with CTX or tyrosine kinase inhibitors (also cause diarrhea). Fecal calprotectin may be an alternate or adjunct to endoscopy; it can be elevated by GI-tract cancers themselves (p 4080).
- Grade 1: conservative therapy alone advised; short-term immunosuppressants (incl. topical budesonide) with caution given lack of efficacy evidence (p 4080).
- Refractory to steroids then infliximab or vedolizumab: tofacitinib and ustekinumab reported in very small samples; fecal microbiota transplantation 75% efficacy in 15 refractory cases (pp 4080, 4083).
- GI toxicities preliminarily associated with improved survival/outcomes (p 4083).
- Mucosal healing on follow-up endoscopy or calprotectin ≤116 µg/g can guide timing of ICPi resumption to minimize colitis recurrence (pp 4083–4084).

### GI toxicities — hepatitis (Table 2, 2.2)
*Table 2, 2.2, pp 4082–4083. No row-level grades printed (all consensus, moderate).*

**Workup and evaluation**
- Monitor AST, ALT and bilirubin before each infusion and/or consider weekly if grade 1 liver function test (LFT) elevation. **No treatment recommended for grade 1 LFT abnormality.**
- Review medications and supplements that may cause hepatotoxicity; rule out development/progression of liver metastases.
- Liver biopsy should be considered if steroid-refractory or if another diagnosis that would change management is suspected.
- **Grade ≥2:** work up other causes (eg, viral hepatitis, alcohol history, iron studies, thromboembolic event, liver metastasis) with blood work and imaging (ultrasound and cross-sectional). High suspicion of primary autoimmune hepatitis → can consider antinuclear antibody (ANA) / anti–smooth muscle antibody (ASMA) / antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody (ANCA). Isolated alkaline phosphatase (ALKP) elevation → test gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT). Isolated transaminase elevation → consider creatine kinase (CK).

**Grades and management**

| Grade | Management (Table 2, 2.2) |
|---|---|
| **G1:** asymptomatic; AST or ALT >ULN to 3.0× ULN and/or total bilirubin >ULN to 1.5× ULN | Continue ICPi with close monitoring; consider alternate etiologies. Consider labs 1–2 times weekly. Supportive care for symptoms |
| **G2:** asymptomatic; AST or ALT >3.0 to ≤5× ULN and/or total bilirubin >1.5 to ≤3× ULN | Hold ICPi temporarily. Advise stopping unnecessary medications and known hepatotoxic drugs; temporarily hold other potentially hepatotoxic oncologic agents. **May give steroid (prednisone 0.5–1 mg/kg/d) or equivalent if no improvement after 3–5 days.** Increase monitoring to every 3 days. Inadequate improvement after 3 days → consider adding mycophenolate mofetil. May start steroid taper when ≤G1 and may resume ICPi when steroid ≤10 mg/d; **taper over at least 1 month**. Consider hepatology consult for G2 and above. May resume if recovered to ≤G1 on prednisone ≤10 mg/d |
| **G3:** AST or ALT 5–20× ULN and/or total bilirubin 3–10× ULN, OR symptomatic liver dysfunction; fibrosis by biopsy; compensated cirrhosis; reactivation of chronic hepatitis | G2 measures plus: **consider permanently discontinuing ICPi if asymptomatic; permanently discontinue if symptomatic.** **Immediately start methylprednisolone 1–2 mg/kg or equivalent.** Steroid-refractory → consider liver biopsy to rule out nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), tumor, cholestatic variants, other drug-related hepatic inflammation, infection or other autoimmune entity, and consider adding azathioprine or mycophenolate if infection ruled out. Labs daily or every other day; consider inpatient monitoring if AST/ALT >8× ULN and/or total bilirubin >3× ULN. No improvement on steroid, or ICPi combined with a novel agent, standard CTX or targeted therapy → refer to hepatologist for further pathologic evaluation. Steroid taper can be attempted around 4–6 weeks when ≤G1; re-escalate if needed; optimal duration unclear. Consider transfer to tertiary care if necessary |
| **G4:** AST or ALT >20× ULN and/or total bilirubin >10× ULN, OR decompensated liver function (eg, ascites, coagulopathy, encephalopathy, coma) | G3 measures plus: **methylprednisolone 2 mg/kg/d equivalents** |

- Footnote b: anecdotal experience suggests azathioprine may help in steroid-refractory immune-related hepatitis; **test for thiopurine methyltransferase deficiency** before using it.
- Footnote c: one case study reports mycophenolate mofetil with some success in steroid-refractory immune-related hepatitis.
- **Additional considerations: infliximab is contraindicated for immune-related hepatitis.**
- Table 2 gives no mycophenolate or azathioprine dose.

**Hepatitis discussion (section 2.0, pp 4083–4084) — condensed**
- Hepatotoxicity 2%–10% with ipilimumab, nivolumab or pembrolizumab monotherapy; ipilimumab + nivolumab 25%–30% all-grade, ~15% grade 3. Onset predominantly within the first 6–12 weeks.
- Liver biopsy should be considered to exclude other etiologies in persistent/refractory hepatitis when blood work or imaging is inconclusive.
- Infliximab not recommended given concern for liver toxicity; other alternative immunosuppressants need more efficacy/safety data.
- Pre-existing hepatitis with ICPi colitis: rare; options limited; should include permanent cessation of anti–CTLA-4 and possibly other ICPi.
- Hepatitis and pancreatitis can recur on resumption — usually early, low grade and manageable; ensure monitoring.

### Other GI entries — upper GI toxicity and pancreatitis (section 2.0 discussion; no Table 2 row)
- **Upper GI toxicity** (p 4083): much less common than lower GI; dysphagia, nausea/vomiting, epigastric pain; biopsies may show patchy chronic duodenitis or chronic gastritis with rare granulomas; can coexist with lower GI toxicity or be isolated. Treatment similar to colitis — corticosteroid, then infliximab or vedolizumab for refractory cases (case studies). Table 2, 2.1 additional considerations: endoscopy with biopsy is the evaluation tool.
- **Pancreatitis** (p 4083): symptomatic pancreatitis rare. **Routine amylase or lipase monitoring in asymptomatic patients is not recommended.** Standard pancreatitis workup if suggestive symptoms or suspicious findings on a scan done for other reasons (eg, tumor assessment). Role of corticosteroids for ICPi pancreatitis or enzyme elevation not clearly defined; could be considered in symptomatic disease once other etiologies are ruled out.

### Special populations / prophylaxis

**Steroid adverse-effect prevention (Table 12, p 4111; section 12.0)**
- **12.1 Pretreatment:** baseline viral hepatitis B and C serology and consideration of latent/active TB testing; pre-existing HIV → HIV viral load and CD4 count appropriate. Optimize comorbidities (diabetes mellitus [DM], hypertension, heart failure, cataract, glaucoma, infection, osteoporosis) before steroids. Dose/duration individualized; use the lowest dose for the shortest time needed (toxicity is dose- and duration-dependent).
- **12.2 Opportunistic infection:** *Pneumocystis jirovecii* pneumonia (PJP) prophylaxis may be considered once a patient has received prednisone-equivalent ≥20 mg/d for 4 or more weeks or >30 mg for 3 weeks or more (follow institutional guidelines). Prophylactic fluconazole with prolonged steroid use (>12 weeks) unclear — institutional guidelines. Herpes zoster reactivation prophylaxis may be offered to patients with prior zoster who will receive corticosteroids.
- **12.3 Monitoring:** ask routinely about steroid adverse effects. Acute: infection, insomnia, anxiety, diabetes/glucose intolerance, hypertension, cutaneous changes. Long-term: bone loss and fractures, cataract/glaucoma, myopathy, relative adrenal insufficiency, psychiatric disturbance, gastric or duodenal ulcers. **GI prophylaxis with a proton pump inhibitor (PPI) or H2 antagonist is recommended.** Bone: calcium, vitamin D, weight-bearing exercise; bone-modifying agents may be offered on steroids >3 months and are recommended with pre-existing osteoporosis; bone mineral density testing for those with/at risk of osteoporosis with long-term survival potential.
- **12.4 Tapering:** taper length by irAE type/severity, initial dose and response, not prespecified criteria. Taper slowly, generally over 4–6 weeks; clinical evaluation during taper (rebound/recurrence risk). In general oral taper over 4–6 weeks: reduce prednisone/prednisolone by 10 mg every 3–7 days (as irAE allows) to 10 mg/d, then by 5 mg every 3–7 days for quick responders; longer if steroids for several weeks. IV steroids: in general taper over 4 weeks or longer; IV methylprednisolone ≥1 mg/kg/d converts to oral prednisone 1 mg/kg/day at minimum, then taper as above. Longer tapers (>4–6 weeks) may be needed. Monitor for adrenal insufficiency after prolonged steroids; stress doses with illness, injury, surgery. Option below 5 mg prednisone or 0.5 mg dexamethasone after a long course with adrenal-insufficiency concern: hydrocortisone at physiologic dosing (10 mg morning, 5 mg afternoon). If needed for disease control, slow low-dose taper of the long-acting steroid (eg, 1 mg prednisone per week). Hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis testing 24 hours after last oral hydrocortisone (AM cortisol and adrenocorticotropic hormone [ACTH]; ACTH stimulation test if ambiguous).

**Baseline and on-steroid testing (Appendix Tables A3 and A4, p 64 of PDF)**
- Baseline before ICPi (A3): physical exam (performance status, weight, body mass index, heart rate, blood pressure, oxygen saturation); history incl. autoimmune, organ-specific, endocrine, neuropathy, infectious disease; systems review with attention to bowel habits, respiratory symptoms, rash, arthralgia, neurologic symptoms; CBC with differential; CMP incl. AST, ALT, ALKP, GGT, creatinine, CK, total bilirubin, glucose; TSH, free T4; chest X-ray, CT, electrocardiogram.
- During steroids (A4): vitals/weight; infection incl. oral *Candida*; hyperglycemia symptoms; eye exam incl. intraocular pressure with therapy >6 weeks; CBC with differential; CMP (as above); bone mineral density during prolonged therapy.

**Pre-existing autoimmune disease (AD) (Discussion, p 4113)** — no formal recommendation
- Often excluded from trials; data suggest they may be safely treated. 2016 systematic review: exacerbation in 41% (46% active at ICPi start). 2019 cohort of 112: AD flare 47%, other irAEs 42%, immunosuppression needed 43%, permanent ICPi discontinuation 21%; median progression-free survival shorter if immunosuppression was needed before ICPi (3.8 v 12 months).
- Proposed selective immunosuppression strategy (cited proposal): (1) rotation phase — stop nonselective immunosuppressants, switch to the most appropriate selective agent, confirm AD stability 2–4 weeks before ICPi if timing allows; (2) maintenance phase — selective immunosuppression throughout ICPi.
- Pre-existing IBD is not addressed separately.

**Rechallenge (Discussion, pp 4114–4115)**
- Dose reductions of ICPi should be avoided; temporary interruption or permanent discontinuation recommended instead.
- Objective response already achieved → resumption (with risk of recurrent toxicity) may not be advisable; not yet responded or inadequate response → resumption after resolution is reasonable.
- VigiBase: recurrence of initial irAE in 28.8% of rechallenged cases; colitis, hepatitis and pneumonitis associated with higher recurrence. Colitis recurs in up to one third; anti–CTLA-4 higher risk than anti–PD-1 alone; more severe index colitis → higher recurrence.
- After first-line ICPi in epidermal growth factor receptor–mutant non–small-cell lung cancer, experts advise waiting 3 months from last ICPi dose before osimertinib (grade 3–4 colitis, hepatitis, pneumonitis reported).

**Other**
- Survivorship: panel defines chronic irAEs as symptoms developing during treatment and lasting 6 months to 1 year after completion (except endocrinopathies); late irAEs = onset >6 months after discontinuation (p 4112).
- COVID-19: vaccination of patients and family is in general recommended; do not pause ICPi for vaccination; COVID-19 can mimic irAEs incl. elevated LFTs in hepatitis (p 4116).
- Organ transplant recipients: addressed only within renal toxicities (Table 6) — see below.

### Other organ systems (condensed, one line per entity per grade)
*Rating for every line: expert consensus, benefits outweigh harms, strength moderate (p 4076); tables carry no row-level ratings. Steroid doses are prednisone or equivalent unless stated. G = grade. Body surface area = BSA.*

**Cutaneous (Table 1, pp 4078–4080)** — skin grading by BSA, tolerability, morbidity, duration rather than CTCAE
- 1.1 Rash/inflammatory dermatitis — workup: history/exam incl. oral mucosa, blisters, BSA; medication review; consider dermatology referral, biopsy, serial photography.
  - G1 (<10% BSA): continue ICPi; emollients, mild–moderate topical steroid.
  - G2 (10%–30% BSA, or >30% with mild symptoms): consider holding ICPi, weekly review (not improved after 4 weeks → regrade G3); medium–high potency topical steroid, oral antihistamine; consider prednisone 0.5–1 mg/kg tapered over 4 weeks.
  - G3 (>30% BSA with moderate/severe symptoms; limiting self-care ADL): hold ICPi, dermatology consult; high-potency topical steroid; oral prednisone 1 mg/kg/d tapered over at least 4 weeks; may resume once ≤G1 and prednisone <10 mg/d in certain cases.
  - G4: immediate hold; may admit with urgent dermatology; IV methylprednisolone 1–2 mg/kg, slow taper; consider alternative therapy if not ≤G1.
- 1.2 Bullous dermatoses — workup: dermatology for biopsy and direct immunofluorescence.
  - G1 (<10% BSA, no erythema): asymptomatic noninflammatory blisters → no cessation needed; symptomatic bullae/erosions = at least G2.
  - G2 (10%–30% BSA): hold ICPi, dermatology; class 1 topical steroid, reassess every 3 days; low threshold for prednisone 0.5–1 mg/kg/d tapered over at least 4 weeks.
  - G3 (sloughing >30% BSA with pain, limiting self-care ADL): hold, consider admission; IV methylprednisolone 1–2 mg/kg, taper over at least 4 weeks; bullous pemphigoid → steroid-sparing options (IV immune globulin [IVIG], rituximab).
  - G4 (>30% BSA with fluid/electrolyte abnormalities): permanently discontinue; admit under dermatology; IV methylprednisolone 1–2 mg/kg.
- 1.3 Severe cutaneous adverse reactions (SCAR: Stevens-Johnson syndrome [SJS], toxic epidermal necrolysis [TEN], drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms [DRESS]) — all grades: suspected SJS or any mucous membrane involvement (not including isolated stomatitis) → discontinue ICPi, dermatology; no G1–G2 categories.
  - G3 (sloughing <10% BSA with mucosal involvement): hold ICPi; burn unit/wound services; IV methylprednisolone 0.5–1 mg/kg, wean over at least 4 weeks; immune suppression warranted.
  - G4 (≥10% BSA with associated signs and/or systemic symptoms): permanently discontinue; burn unit or intensive care unit; IV methylprednisolone 1–2 mg/kg; IVIG or cyclosporine may be considered if severe or steroid-unresponsive.

**Lung (Table 3, p 4085)**
- 3.1 Pneumonitis — workup: pulse oximetry, CT chest (contrast if pulmonary embolus a concern); G2+ infectious workup; COVID-19 testing per institution.
  - G1 (asymptomatic; one lobe or <25% of parenchyma): hold ICPi or proceed with close monitoring; weekly review; repeat imaging in 3–4 weeks; may resume with radiographic improvement; no improvement → treat as G2.
  - G2 (symptomatic; >1 lobe or 25%–50%; limiting instrumental ADL): hold until ≤G1; prednisone 1–2 mg/kg/d tapered over 4–6 weeks; consider bronchoscopy with bronchoalveolar lavage ± transbronchial biopsy; no improvement after 48–72 hours → treat as G3.
  - G3–4 (all lobes or >50%, oxygen, hospitalization; G4 life-threatening/intubation): permanently discontinue; IV methylprednisolone 1–2 mg/kg/d; no improvement after 48 hours → may add infliximab, mycophenolate IV, IVIG or cyclophosphamide; taper steroids over 4–6 weeks (chronic pneumonitis may need longer).

**Endocrine (Table 4, pp 4087–4090)**
- 4.1.1 Primary hypothyroidism — TSH (± free thyroxine [FT4]) every 4–6 weeks while on ICPi.
  - G1 (TSH >4.5 and <10 mIU/L, asymptomatic): continue ICPi; monitor TSH every 4–6 weeks.
  - G2 (moderate symptoms; TSH persistently >10 mIU/L): may continue or hold ICPi; thyroid hormone if symptomatic with any TSH elevation or asymptomatic TSH >10 mIU/L on two measurements 4 weeks apart; TSH every 6–8 weeks while titrating.
  - G3–4: hold ICPi until baseline with supplementation; endocrine consult; admit for myxedema; give hydrocortisone before thyroid hormone if primary vs central is uncertain.
  - Dosing: ~1.6 mcg/kg/d (ideal body weight) if <70 years, not frail, no cardiac disease; older/frail → start low and titrate.
- 4.1.2 Thyrotoxicosis
  - G1: can continue ICPi; beta-blocker; thyroid function every 2–3 weeks (transition to hypothyroidism common); >6 weeks → consider endocrine consult.
  - G2: consider holding ICPi; endocrine consult; beta-blocker, hydration; >6 weeks → endocrinology referral.
  - G3–4: hold ICPi; endocrine consult for all; consider hospitalization (steroids, potassium iodide, thionamide, surgery per endocrinology).
- 4.2 Primary adrenal insufficiency — all grades: endocrinology referral; stress-dose education, emergency injection, medical alert ID.
  - G1: consider holding ICPi until stable on replacement; hydrocortisone 15–20 mg/day in divided doses (max 30 mg/day); most need fludrocortisone 0.05–0.1 mg/d.
  - G2: consider holding; outpatient steroid at 2–3× maintenance (eg, hydrocortisone 30–50 mg or prednisone 20 mg daily), back to maintenance after 2 days; fludrocortisone.
  - G3–4: hold until stable; inpatient as needed: normal saline at least 2 L, IV hydrocortisone 50–100 mg every 6–8 hours, taper to oral maintenance over 5–7 days.
- 4.3 Hypophysitis — workup: AM ACTH and cortisol, TSH, free T4, electrolytes; MRI pituitary for new deficiencies (± headache/vision change) and for all with diabetes insipidus.
  - G1: consider holding until stable on replacement; hydrocortisone first (15–20 mg divided), then other hormones; FT4 target upper half of range (TSH unreliable).
  - G2: consider holding; prednisone 1 mg/kg/d pulse if MRI swelling or threatened chiasm, taper over 1–2 weeks to physiologic dose.
  - G3–4: hold until stable; hospitalize/emergency department: saline at least 2 L (or monitored free water if diabetes insipidus), IV hydrocortisone 50–100 mg every 6–8 hours; prednisone 1–2 mg/kg daily pulse for significant swelling, chiasm compression, severe headache or visual change.
  - Mild/moderate symptoms treated with prednisone → consider lower doses (average daily dose <7.5 mg over two months; report of reduced survival on higher doses).
- 4.4 Diabetes — glucose at baseline, each cycle, and at follow-up for at least 6 months; suspected checkpoint inhibitor–associated diabetes mellitus (CIADM): ketones, anion gap, anti–glutamic acid decarboxylase/islet antibodies, C-peptide.
  - G1 (fasting glucose >ULN to 160 mg/dL; no CIADM): continue ICPi; oral therapy for type 2 DM.
  - G2 (fasting >160 to 250 mg/dL; or CIADM evidence at any glucose without ketoacidosis): may hold ICPi; urgent endocrine consult for new CIADM; insulin for CIADM (or as default therapy if any question about the diagnosis).
  - G3–4 (G3 >250 to 500 mg/dL; G4 >500 mg/dL; ketoacidosis): hold until control and ≤G1; admit for diabetic ketoacidosis; endocrine consult; insulin for all (start 0.3–0.4 units/kg/d total).

**Musculoskeletal (Table 5, pp 4092–4093)**
- 5.1 Inflammatory arthritis (IA) — G3–4 workup: hepatitis B, C and TB testing before disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (DMARD); repeat annually if biologic >1 year.
  - G1: continue ICPi; acetaminophen and/or NSAIDs.
  - G2: consider holding ICPi; escalate NSAIDs; prednisone 10–20 mg/d if inadequate, taper over 4–6 weeks; no improvement after 4 weeks → G3; cannot get below 10 mg/d after 6–8 weeks → consider DMARD; intra-articular steroid for large joints; rheumatology.
  - G3–4: hold temporarily, may resume with rheumatology if ≤G1; prednisone 0.5–1 mg/kg; no improvement after 2 weeks → synthetic or biologic DMARD (TNF-α or IL-6 antagonists). **IL-6 inhibition can cause intestinal perforation (extremely rare) — should not be used with concomitant immune-related colitis.**
- 5.2 Myositis — workup: CK, aldolase, transaminases, LDH, troponin, acetylcholine receptor antibodies; elevated transaminases → consider immune-mediated hepatitis in the differential.
  - G1: continue ICPi; prednisone 0.5 mg/kg/day if CK/aldolase elevated with weakness; consider holding statins.
  - G2: hold temporarily, resume when controlled, CK normal and prednisone <10 mg; rheumatology/neurology; CK ≥3× ULN → prednisone 0.5–1 mg/kg/d; severe objective involvement may require permanent discontinuation.
  - G3–4: hold ICPi; consider hospitalization; prednisone 1 mg/kg/d (IV methylprednisolone 1–2 mg/kg or higher bolus if severe); consider plasmapheresis, IVIG; worsening or no improvement after 2 weeks → rituximab, TNF-α or IL-6 antagonists; consider permanent discontinuation.
- 5.3 Polymyalgia-like syndrome — screen for giant cell arteritis symptoms (urgent ophthalmology).
  - G1: continue ICPi; acetaminophen/NSAIDs.
  - G2: consider holding, resume when controlled on prednisone <10 mg; prednisone 20 mg/d, taper after 3–4 weeks.
  - G3–4: hold, may resume with rheumatology if ≤G2; prednisone 40 mg/d; steroid-sparing (methotrexate, IL-6 antagonists — not with immune-related colitis).

**Renal (Table 6, pp 4095–4096)**
- Diagnosis: definite = biopsy-confirmed; probable = sustained creatinine rise ≥50% on two values (or renal replacement therapy) with no alternative cause AND sterile pyuria (≥5 WBC/hpf) or extrarenal irAE/eosinophilia (≥500 cells/µL); possible = creatinine rise ≥50% not attributable to other causes. Creatinine before every dose; no alternative cause → assume ICPi-related and forego biopsy.
- 6.1 Nephritis or acute kidney injury (AKI)
  - G1 (creatinine increase >0.3 mg/dL; 1.5–2.0× baseline): consider temporarily holding ICPi pending alternative causes.
  - G2 (2–3× baseline): hold ICPi; nephrology; prednisone 0.5–1 mg/kg/day if other causes excluded; worse or no better after 1 week → 1–2 mg/kg/day and permanently discontinue; taper over at least 4 weeks when ≤G1; resumption considered once steroids ≤10 mg/d or stopped.
  - G3–4 (G3 >3× baseline or >4.0 mg/dL, hospitalization; G4 dialysis, 6× baseline): permanently discontinue if ICPi directly implicated; nephrology; prednisone 1–2 mg/kg/d. Reflex biopsy discouraged until steroids tried.
- 6.2 Follow-up: G2 persisting >7 days → treat as G3; G3 persisting >3–5 days or G4 >2–3 days → consider infliximab, azathioprine, cyclophosphamide, cyclosporine or mycophenolate.
- Renal allograft recipients (section 6.0 discussion, p 4094): high risk of rejection and dialysis (near-universal with PD-1 pathway blockers; possibly less with anti–CTLA-4); treatment with immunosuppression adjustment only with renal transplant nephrology input.

**Nervous system (Table 7, pp 4097–4100)**
- 7.1 Myasthenia gravis (MG) — all grades need workup/intervention; admission may be appropriate at any grade; no G1. Stop drugs that worsen MG (beta-blockers, IV magnesium, fluoroquinolones, aminoglycosides, macrolides).
  - G2 (Myasthenia Gravis Foundation of America [MGFA] class I–II): hold ICPi, may resume only if symptoms resolve and taper completed; neurology; strongly consider inpatient care; pyridostigmine 30 mg three times a day up to 120 mg four times a day; prednisone 0.5 mg/kg daily (lower than 1 mg/kg because high-dose steroid can transiently worsen MG).
  - G3–4 (MGFA III–V; any dysphagia, facial or respiratory weakness): permanently discontinue; admit (possibly ICU); continue steroids (taper from 3–4 weeks); IVIG 2 g/kg over 5 days (0.4 g/kg/d) or plasmapheresis 5 days; rituximab if refractory.
- 7.2 Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS) — no G1.
  - G2–4: discontinue ICPi; neurology; admit with ICU transfer capability; IVIG (0.4 g/kg/d × 5 days) or plasmapheresis; trial of methylprednisolone 2–4 mg/kg/d reasonable (pulse 1 g daily × 5 days may be considered for G3–4), taper over 4–6 weeks; monitor autonomic dysfunction; nonopioid neuropathic pain treatment; **treat constipation/ileus**. Extreme caution with rechallenge.
- 7.3 Peripheral neuropathy
  - G1: low threshold to hold ICPi and monitor a week (cranial nerve involvement = manage as G2).
  - G2: hold, resume at ≤G1; observation or prednisone 0.5–1 mg/kg/d; gabapentin/pregabalin/duloxetine.
  - G3–4: permanently discontinue; admit; IV methylprednisolone 2–4 mg/kg/d; manage as GBS.
- 7.4 Autonomic neuropathy (can present as GI dysfunction such as new severe constipation or nausea — section 7.0, p 4094)
  - G1: low threshold to hold and monitor a week.
  - G2: hold, resume at ≤G1 and off prednisone; observation or prednisone 0.5–1 mg/kg/d; neurology.
  - G3–4: permanently discontinue; admit; methylprednisolone 1 g daily × 3 days then oral taper.
- 7.5 Aseptic meningitis — G1–4 (single management cell): hold ICPi, discuss resumption weighing risks/benefits; consider empiric IV acyclovir and antibacterials until cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) results; then monitor off steroids or prednisone 0.5–1 mg/kg/day or IV methylprednisolone 1 mg/kg/day if moderate/severe; taper after 2–4 weeks; consider hospitalization for G3–4.
- 7.6 Encephalitis — workup includes celiac antibody panel among serum studies. G1–4: hold ICPi; IV acyclovir until PCR negative; methylprednisolone 1–2 mg/kg/d trial; severe/progressing or oligoclonal bands → pulse methylprednisolone 1 g IV daily × 3–5 days plus IVIG 2 g/kg over 5 days or plasmapheresis; taper over at least 4–6 weeks; rituximab if antibody-positive or poor response; admit G3–4.
- 7.7 Demyelinating diseases (multiple sclerosis, transverse myelitis, acute disseminated encephalomyelitis, optic neuritis, neuromyelitis optica)
  - G1: continue unless symptoms worsen.
  - G2: stop ICPi; neurology; prednisone 1 mg/kg daily, taper over 1 month.
  - G3: permanently discontinue; admit for methylprednisolone 1 g/d; IVIG or plasmapheresis if no improvement after 3 days.
  - G4: permanently discontinue; ICU; same as G3.

**Hematologic (Table 8, pp 4101–4105)** — platelet and cell-count cut-offs are in the table; only those confirmed here are quoted
- 8.1 Hemolytic anemia
  - G1 (hemoglobin <lower limit of normal to 10.0 g/dL): continue ICPi with close follow-up.
  - G2 (<10.0 to 8.0 g/dL): hold and strongly consider permanent discontinuation; prednisone 0.5–1 mg/kg/d.
  - G3 (<8.0 g/dL; transfusion indicated): permanently discontinue; consider admission; hematology; prednisone 1–2 mg/kg/d; minimal transfusion (7–8 g/dL target in stable noncardiac inpatients); folic acid 1 mg daily.
  - G4: permanently discontinue; admit; IV steroid 1–2 mg/kg/d; refractory → rituximab, IVIG, cyclosporine, infliximab, MMF or antithymocyte globulin (ATG).
- 8.2 Acquired thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP) — all grades: immediate hematology.
  - G1–2: hold ICPi (no data support restarting); prednisone 0.5–1 mg/kg/d.
  - G3–4: hold; plasma exchange (PEX) with hematology; methylprednisolone 1 g IV daily × 3 days; may offer rituximab; consider caplacizumab if ADAMTS13 activity <10 IU/dL or <10% of normal, with an inhibitor or elevated anti-ADAMTS13 IgG.
- 8.3 Hemolytic uremic syndrome (HUS) — workup includes pancreatic enzymes and diarrheal causes (Shiga toxin, *E. coli* O157).
  - G1–2: continue ICPi; supportive care.
  - G3–4: permanently discontinue; hematology; eculizumab 900 mg weekly × 4, 1,200 mg week 5, then 1,200 mg every 2 weeks; transfusion per guidelines.
- 8.4 Aplastic anemia
  - G1: hold ICPi; growth factor support; transfusion per local guidelines.
  - G2: hold; hematology; horse ATG plus cyclosporine; irradiated, filtered blood products; HLA typing for transplant if candidate.
  - G3–4: as G2; no response → rabbit ATG + cyclosporine + cyclophosphamide; refractory → eltrombopag.
- 8.5 Lymphopenia — G1–3: no action, continue ICPi. G4 (<250 peripheral blood lymphocytes): continue ICPi; *Mycobacterium avium* complex and PJP prophylaxis, CMV screening, HIV/hepatitis screening if not done.
- 8.6 Immune thrombocytopenia (ITP) — workup of new ITP includes HIV, hepatitis C, hepatitis B and *H. pylori* testing.
  - G1: continue ICPi.
  - G2: hold until G1; prednisone 1 mg/kg/day (range 0.5–2) for 4 weeks, taper over 4–6 weeks; IVIG with steroids if rapid rise needed.
  - G3–4: as G2; hematology; dexamethasone 40 mg daily × 4 days as alternative; IVIG 1 g/kg once; refractory → rituximab, thrombopoietin receptor agonists, stronger immunosuppression.
- 8.7 Acquired hemophilia A
  - G1 (5%–40% factor activity): hold ICPi; prednisone 0.5–1 mg/kg/d; hematology.
  - G2 (1%–5%): hold; factor replacement by inhibitor titer; prednisone 1 mg/kg/d ± rituximab 375 mg/m² weekly × 4 and/or cyclophosphamide 1–2 mg/kg/d, for at least 5 weeks.
  - G3–4 (<1%): permanently discontinue; admit; factor replacement or bypassing agents; prednisone 1–2 mg/kg/d ± rituximab and/or cyclophosphamide.

**Cardiovascular (Table 9, pp 4107–4108)** — qualifying statement: based on anecdotal evidence; holding ICPi recommended for all grades; rechallenge appropriateness unknown
- 9.1 Myocarditis, pericarditis, arrhythmias, impaired ventricular function with heart failure, vasculitis — workup: electrocardiogram, troponin, CK (watch for myositis–myasthenia–myocarditis overlap), brain natriuretic peptide (BNP), echocardiogram, chest X-ray.
  - G1 (abnormal biomarker, no symptoms or ECG change): hold ICPi, recheck troponin 6 hours later; may resume once normalized or if not ICPi-related.
  - G2–4: hold and discontinue ICPi; early (within 24 hours) prednisone 1–2 mg/kg/d should be considered; admit for cardiology; consider coronary care unit if troponin elevated or conduction abnormality; no immediate steroid response → methylprednisolone 1 g daily plus mycophenolate, infliximab or ATG; abatacept or alemtuzumab in life-threatening cases. Infliximab contraindicated at >5 mg/kg in moderate–severe heart failure.
- 9.2 Venous thromboembolism (VTE)
  - G1 (eg, superficial thrombosis): continue ICPi; warm compress; surveillance.
  - G2 (eg, uncomplicated deep vein thrombosis): continue ICPi; anticoagulation (low-molecular-weight heparin [LMWH], direct oral anticoagulants or vitamin K antagonist initially; LMWH, edoxaban, rivaroxaban or apixaban for at least 6 months preferred long term).
  - G3 (eg, uncomplicated pulmonary embolism): hold ICPi, may reintroduce after risk–benefit review; anticoagulate as G2.
  - G4: hold; admit; respiratory/hemodynamic support; anticoagulate. Anticoagulation continued while on immunotherapy, consider 6 more months after completion.

**Ocular (Table 10, pp 4109–4110)** — all grades: refer to ophthalmology; rule out MG; exclude patients with active uveitis
- 10.1 Uveitis or iritis
  - G1 (anterior, trace cells): continue ICPi; ophthalmology within ~1 week; artificial tears.
  - G2 (anterior, 1+ or 2+ cells): hold until ophthalmology; topical steroid, cycloplegic, systemic steroid; may resume once systemic steroid ≤10 mg prednisone if ocular-only irAE.
  - G3 (anterior 3+ or more; intermediate, posterior or panuveitis): permanently discontinue; systemic plus intravitreal/periocular/topical steroids; methotrexate if poor steroid response.
  - G4 (best-corrected acuity 20/200 or worse): permanently discontinue; emergent ophthalmology; prednisone 1–2 mg/kg/d or methylprednisolone 0.8–1.6 mg/kg/d plus local steroids. Refractory → infliximab, other TNF-α blockers or IVIG.
- 10.2 Episcleritis
  - G1: continue ICPi; ophthalmology within ~1 week; artificial tears.
  - G2 (vision 20/40 or better): hold until ophthalmology; topical/cycloplegic/systemic steroids.
  - G3 (symptomatic, vision "worse than 2/40" as printed): permanently discontinue; systemic and topical steroids with cycloplegic.
  - G4 (20/200 or worse): permanently discontinue; emergent referral; same treatment. Refractory → infliximab or other TNF-α blockers.

**Systemic (Table 11, p 4110)**
- 11.1 Infusion-related reactions (IRRs)
  - G1: continue ICPi; consider acetaminophen + antihistamine premedication.
  - G2: consider holding and/or slowing infusion to 50%; symptomatic treatment; premedicate subsequent infusions.
  - G3: hold, consider resuming at 50% rate once ≤G1; consider IV antihistamine and corticosteroid.
  - G4: permanently discontinue; ICU care. Rechallenge: alternate agent in class or desensitization under an allergist.

  - Grade-by-grade ICPi action (G1 continue; G2 hold to G1; G2+ consider permanent stop of CTLA-4 agents with PD-1/PD-L1 restart allowed; G4 permanently discontinue) and when to admit (G3–4 consider inpatient care; G4 should provide inpatient care).
  - Grade-specific steroid doses (G2 prednisone 1 mg/kg/day; G3 1–2 mg/kg/d, IV methylprednisolone if upper GI inflammation; G4 methylprednisolone 1–2 mg/kg/d) and taper 4–6 weeks (shorter possible on biologics).

  - Treatment target before resuming ICPi: mucosal healing and/or fecal calprotectin ≤116 µg/g.
  - Doses from Table A2: infliximab 5 mg/kg IV with second dose 14 days later; vedolizumab 300 mg IV weeks 0, 2, 6 then every 8 weeks; ustekinumab weight-based IV induction then 90 mg subcutaneous every 8 weeks; tofacitinib 10 mg twice daily for 30 days.

  - Upper GI toxicity (gastritis/duodenitis): biopsy-based diagnosis, treated like colitis.
- [[immune-checkpoint-inhibitor-hepatitis]] (`wiki/1-disease-scripts/hepatology-diseases/immune-checkpoint-inhibitor-hepatitis.md`) — adds:

  - G3: discontinuation conditional on symptoms (consider if asymptomatic; permanent if symptomatic); methylprednisolone 1–2 mg/kg immediately; inpatient monitoring threshold (AST/ALT >8× ULN and/or bilirubin >3× ULN); hepatology referral if no steroid response or ICPi combined with other agents.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
**Internal to the PDF**
- **Vedolizumab position:** Table 2 (2.1, G2–G4) offers infliximab *or* vedolizumab as parallel options; Appendix Table A2 lists the vedolizumab indication as "colitis refractory to infliximab or infliximab contraindicated" (PDF p 60).
- **Colitis steroid dose:** colitis discussion (p 4080) says corticosteroid 1–2 mg/kg is first line once diarrhea is grade ≥2; Table 2 G2 row starts at 1 mg/kg/day (1–2 mg/kg/d only from G3). Bottom Line (p 4074) general grade 2 dose is 0.5–1 mg/kg/d.
- **Grade 3 ICPi action:** Bottom Line says hold ICPi for grade 3; Table 2 hepatitis G3 says consider permanent discontinuation if asymptomatic and permanently discontinue if symptomatic; colitis G3 says consider permanently discontinuing CTLA-4 agents.
- **Infliximab in hepatitis — force differs:** Table 2 says "contraindicated" (2.1 additional considerations and 2.2); hepatitis discussion (p 4083) says "not recommended, given the concern of liver toxicity".
- **Hepatitis G2 sequencing:** steroid only "if no improvement is seen after 3–5 days", but mycophenolate considered "if inadequate improvement after 3 days" — order of steroid vs mycophenolate is unclear (Table 2, 2.2).
- **Hepatitis G3 inpatient threshold** printed as "elevated total bilirubin 3× > ULN" (garbled as printed, p 4083).
- **Ustekinumab naming:** "IL-12–blocking antibody" in Table 2 vs "anti–IL-12/IL-23" in Table A2.
- **Episcleritis G3** printed "vision worse than 2/40" (likely 20/40; G2 is 20/40 or better) (Table 10, p 4110).

**Differences from current wiki pages (AGA 2021 CPU is the source there; ASCO is a non-GI US society guideline)**
- *ICI colitis page:* first-line steroid there is 0.5–2 mg/kg (AGA); ASCO grades it (G2 1 mg/kg/day; G3 1–2 mg/kg/d; G4 methylprednisolone 1–2 mg/kg/d).
- *ICI colitis page:* endoscopy **before** high-dose steroids (AGA BPA 3); ASCO says endoscopy is highly recommended for grade ≥2 to stratify for early biologics, without stating it must precede steroids.
- *ICI colitis page:* escalation triggers there = no response in 72 hours, no complete response in 1 week, or recurrence on taper; ASCO = no decrease by one grade in 72 hours, steroid dependence, high-risk endoscopic features, or (G3–4) persistent symptoms after 3 days.
- *ICI colitis page:* infliximab schedule weeks 0, 2, 6 (AGA); ASCO Table A2 gives 5 mg/kg with a second dose that may be repeated 14 days later. Vedolizumab: same induction, ASCO adds every-8-week maintenance.
- *ICI colitis page:* infliximab with concurrent ICI hepatitis is case-by-case (AGA); ASCO says infliximab is contraindicated for hepatic irAE and suggests systemic agents covering both (prednisone, mycophenolate), possibly with permanent ICPi discontinuation.
- *ICI colitis page:* budesonide effective for ICI microscopic colitis (AGA); ASCO advises caution with short-term immunosuppressants incl. topical budesonide for grade 1 diarrhea given lack of efficacy evidence (different populations — note both).
- *ICI colitis page:* recurrence higher when switching PD-1/PD-L1 → CTLA-4 (AGA); ASCO: resuming PD-1/PD-L1 has lower flare risk, CTLA-4 can still be considered in selected cases (no response or inadequate response). Compatible; ASCO adds the ≤116 µg/g calprotectin target.
- *ICI hepatitis page:* grade 3 = "discontinue permanently" there; ASCO makes it conditional on symptoms (consider if asymptomatic).
- *ICI hepatitis page:* grade 2 steroids for symptomatic patients or after a 1–2 week ICI delay (AGA); ASCO steroid after 3–5 days without improvement.
- *ICI hepatitis page:* AASLD 2022 — consider hospitalization for jaundice at grade ≥3 (AGA: case by case); ASCO consider inpatient monitoring at AST/ALT >8× ULN and/or bilirubin >3× ULN.
- *ICI hepatitis page:* infliximab "caution" (AGA); ASCO "contraindicated".
- *ICI hepatitis page:* states no ingested guideline doses mycophenolate/azathioprine; ASCO Table A2 now does (see above).
- *ICI hepatitis page:* biopsy at grade ≥2 before steroids (AGA) vs persistent grade 3 (AASLD); ASCO: consider if steroid-refractory or another diagnosis would change management.
- Grade bands for hepatitis match the wiki table (G1 >ULN–3× / >ULN–1.5×; G2 >3–5× / >1.5–3×; G3 5–20× / 3–10×; G4 >20× / >10× or decompensation).
- Pre-existing IBD: ASCO has no IBD-specific recommendation (only general AD discussion); the AGA BPA 9 data on the wiki stand alone.
