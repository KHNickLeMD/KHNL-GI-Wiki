## Summary

- Third ACG/ASGE quality indicator document for esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD): **29 EGD-specific indicators** (Table 1) across preprocedure (1–2), intraprocedure (3–21), and postprocedure (22–29) periods.
- Each indicator carries a **performance target**, a **type of measure** (process vs outcome), and a **strength of recommendation** on a Guyatt-derived scale from **1A to 3** (Table 3). Grades were set by author consensus after a PubMed search from January 2014 through May 2023.
- **Targets** come from published benchmarking data; where none existed and failure was a "never event," the target was set at **>98%**.
- **Priority indicators** (9): chosen for clinical relevance and importance, evidence of significant variation in practice, and feasibility of measurement. Units should focus on these first.
- Indicators **common to all endoscopy** (consent, history and exam, sedation plan, prophylactic antibiotics, antithrombotic management, team pause, monitoring, discharge, adverse events; Table 2) live in a **separate document**, repeated here only for completeness.
- The authors stress the indicators are **not** the standard of care, credentialing requirements, or training standards; they are a framework for quality improvement.
- **New or emphasized areas:** thorough photodocumentation (common to every EGD); Eosinophilic Esophagitis Endoscopic Reference Score (EREFS) for [[eosinophilic-esophagitis|eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE)]]; [[gastric-premalignant-conditions|gastric premalignant conditions (GPMCs)]] (Sydney biopsies, high-definition white-light endoscopy [HDWLE] + virtual chromoendoscopy, surveillance plan); [[celiac-disease|celiac disease]] duodenal biopsies.
- **Dropped from the prior iteration** (compliance already high, so less useful to measure): prophylactic antibiotics before percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy (PEG), variceal ligation as first-line endoscopic therapy for esophageal varices, and acid suppression after endoscopic diagnosis of peptic ulcer. Antibiotics in [[cirrhosis]] with upper gastrointestinal bleeding (UGIB) and vasoactive drugs for suspected variceal bleeding were also left out as medical management not specific to endoscopy.
- **Not yet indicators, flagged for the future:** procedure or inspection time (Barrett's esophagus [BE] segment, gastric intestinal metaplasia [GIM]), neoplasia detection rate in BE surveillance, GIM detection rate, artificial intelligence.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Indications (Table 4)

| EGD generally indicated | EGD generally not indicated |
|---|---|
| Upper abdominal symptoms persisting despite an appropriate trial of therapy, or with signs of serious organic disease (e.g., anorexia, weight loss), or age >45 yr | Symptoms considered functional (exception: one exam to rule out organic disease, especially if unresponsive to therapy) |
| Dysphagia, odynophagia, food impaction | Metastatic adenocarcinoma of unknown primary when results will not alter management |
| Reflux symptoms (heartburn, regurgitation, noncardiac chest pain) persistent or recurrent despite appropriate therapy | Radiographic asymptomatic or uncomplicated sliding hiatal hernia |
| Esophageal physiology by impedance planimetry; wireless pH capsule placement for esophageal or extraesophageal reflux symptoms without severe erosive disease | Radiographic uncomplicated duodenal ulcer that responded to therapy |
| Screening for BE; persistent vomiting of unknown cause; familial adenomatous polyposis syndromes | Deformed duodenal bulb when symptoms are absent or respond to ulcer therapy |
| Upper gastrointestinal (GI) pathology that might change other planned management (e.g., ulcer or GI bleeding history before transplant, long-term anticoagulation, chronic nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) therapy; head and neck cancer) | Surveillance of healed benign disease (esophagitis, gastric or duodenal ulcer) |
| Confirmation and histology of radiologic lesions: suspected neoplasm, gastric or esophageal ulcer, upper tract stricture or obstruction | Surveillance during repeated dilation of benign strictures unless status changes |
| GI bleeding: active or recent; presumed chronic blood loss or iron deficiency anemia when an upper source is suspected or colonoscopy is negative | |
| Tissue or fluid sampling; suspected portal hypertension (document or treat varices); caustic ingestion | |
| Therapy: bleeding lesions, variceal banding or sclerotherapy, foreign bodies, selected polypoid lesions, feeding or drainage tubes, stenosis dilation, achalasia, palliation of stenosing neoplasms, intestinal metaplasia, diverticula, endoscopic bariatric therapy, foregut-surgery intraoperative evaluation and operative adverse events | |
| Sequential or periodic EGD: surveillance of premalignant conditions (BE, gastric intestinal metaplasia); disease activity in EoE or eosinophilic GI diseases; mucosal healing and BE screening after severe erosive esophagitis | |

### Upper Gastrointestinal Bleeding Timing (Indicator 2)

- Applies to admitted or observed overt UGIB; excludes low-risk patients (Glasgow-Blatchford score ≤1) and those discharged early from the emergency department.
- Rationale: shorter stay, less surgery, lower mortality in observational studies.
- EGD at **6–24 hr** had a mortality benefit over both urgent (<6 hr) and delayed (24–48 hr) EGD in a cohort of 6,474 patients.
- American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) class III–IV ulcer bleeding: higher mortality with very early (<12 hr) **or** very late (>36 hr) endoscopy.
- Randomized controlled trial (RCT) of high-risk UGIB (Glasgow-Blatchford ≥12): urgent (<6 hr) vs 6–24 hr — no difference in 30-day mortality or further bleeding.

### Photodocumentation (Indicator 3; Figure 1)

- Stations: esophagus; antegrade GEJ; retroflexed cardia/fundus; corpus; antegrade incisura; antegrade antrum/pylorus; second portion of duodenum.
- GEJ = top of the gastric folds or distal end of the lower esophageal palisade vessels.
- Little direct evidence; serves as a surrogate for cleaning, blind-spot inspection, and complete examination. Regions with high gastric cancer incidence recommend up to 22 gastric images.

### Esophagus

- **EoE biopsies (Indicator 4):** diagnostic standard ≥15 eosinophils per high-power field. Eosinophilia is patchy, proximal to distal; distal biopsies alone cannot exclude reflux as the cause.
  - Diagnostic sensitivity by total biopsies (pediatric study): 73% (1), 84% (2), 97% (3), 100% (6).
  - Only 67% of 240 surveyed gastroenterologists biopsied 2 levels.
- **EREFS (Indicator 5):** Edema 0–1, Rings 0–3, Exudate 0–2, Furrows 0–2, Stricture 0–1. Correlates with histologic activity and treatment response; separates inflammatory, fibrostenotic, and mixed phenotypes.
- **LA classification (Indicator 6; Figure 2):**

| LA grade | Mucosal break |
|---|---|
| A | ≥1 break ≤5 mm, not extending between the tops of 2 mucosal folds |
| B | ≥1 break >5 mm, not extending between the tops of 2 mucosal folds |
| C | ≥1 break continuous between the tops of 2 mucosal folds, <75% of circumference |
| D | ≥1 break involving ≥75% of circumference |

  - LA A is **not** sufficient for a definitive diagnosis of gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD); seen in 5%–7.5% of healthy subjects.
  - LA B, C, or D is diagnostic of GERD; no reflux monitoring needed to confirm.
  - LA D has the highest acid exposure time; LA C/D carries more BE on later endoscopy than A/B.
- **Landmarks (Indicator 7):** SCJ = squamous–columnar mucosal junction (forward view); GEJ = tubular esophagus–proximal gastric folds; hiatus = crural pinch. SCJ and GEJ may not coincide in columnar metaplasia.
- **≥1 cm columnar mucosa (Indicator 8):** one of the 2 criteria for BE (the other is intestinal metaplasia on biopsy).
  - Irregular Z-line (<1 cm tongues): 10%–15% of EGDs; progression to high-grade dysplasia or esophageal adenocarcinoma exceedingly low; interobserver agreement extremely poor → no regular surveillance.
- **Prague C & M (Indicator 9):** C = circumferential extent, M = maximal extent above the GEJ, in cm.
- **BE surveillance imaging (Indicator 10):** high-resolution white-light endoscopy after cleaning; ~1 min/cm of circumferential BE suggested for inspection. Dye or virtual chromoendoscopy raised dysplasia/cancer yield by 34%–35% vs white light.
- **Seattle protocol (Indicator 11):** 4-quadrant biopsies every **2 cm without dysplasia**, every **1 cm with dysplasia** in those electing surveillance; each level in a separate jar. Noncircumferential BE: every 2 cm from the tongues, minimum 4 biopsies. Seattle compliance roughly doubled dysplasia detection.
- **Visible lesions (Indicator 12):** describe with the Paris classification; if not resected at that exam, sample into separate labelled jars (distance from incisors, clock position) **before** the Seattle biopsies. If resection is anticipated, keep lesion biopsies targeted and minimal to avoid fibrosis.

### Peptic Ulcer and Nonvariceal Bleeding (Indicators 13–16, 26)

- Ulcers should be classified with the **Forrest classification**, because the class sets the need for endoscopic therapy and the post-intervention PPI dose.

| Forrest class | Stigma | Recurrent bleeding after successful endoscopic hemostasis (post-hoc RCT analysis) |
|---|---|---|
| Ia | Spurting hemorrhage | 22.5% |
| Ib | Oozing hemorrhage | 4.9% |
| IIa | Nonbleeding visible vessel | 11.3% |
| IIb | Adherent clot | 17.7% |
| IIc | Flat pigmented spot | Low risk; no endoscopic therapy needed |
| III | Clean based | Low risk; no endoscopic therapy needed |

- Active bleeding at initial endoscopy (Ia or Ib) is a risk factor for further bleeding; further bleeding after intervention is significantly higher with spurting than with oozing.
- **Endoscopic therapy (Indicator 14):** for active spurting, active oozing, and NBVV. ACG UGIB guidance reached no recommendation on endoscopic therapy for adherent clots.
  - Moderate-quality options: bipolar coagulation, heater probe, sclerosant (absolute alcohol).
  - Clips: evidence less strong (no direct RCT vs no therapy), but superior to epinephrine monotherapy in 2 RCTs.
  - Lower-quality options: argon plasma coagulation, soft monopolar coagulation, hemostatic powder (TC-325), over-the-scope clips (OTSCs). OTSCs may be a viable first-line option in high-risk ulcers.
- **Epinephrine (Indicator 15):** only in combination with a second modality; inferior as monotherapy.
- **Hemostasis documentation (Indicator 16):** measures documentation of primary hemostasis, not its achievement (patient mix and lesion characteristics vary too widely for a benchmark). Failure of initial hemostasis is linked to higher mortality, rebleeding, and cost.
- **High-dose PPI (Indicator 26):** defined as **≥80 mg pantoprazole or equivalent daily for ≥3 days**, intermittent or continuous, after successful endoscopic therapy of a high-risk ulcer. Bolus plus continuous infusion and intermittent regimens gave similar further-bleeding rates.

### Stomach (Indicators 17–20, 27–29)

- **Gastric ulcer (Indicator 17):** endoscopic appearance alone cannot exclude malignancy; biopsy at index or follow-up endoscopy. Biopsy may be deferred to a later endoscopy when the ulcer is bleeding or has high-risk stigmata; may be unnecessary when cancer risk is negligible (e.g., young healthy patient, small superficial ulcer, heavy NSAID use).
- **Systematic biopsy (Indicator 18; Figure 3):** 3 groups —
  1. Known GPMC, or prior gastric cancer with indications for surveillance
  2. Increased gastric cancer or GPMC risk (e.g., first-degree relative with gastric cancer; foreign-born immigrants from high-incidence regions)
  3. Endoscopic appearance concerning for GPMC

| Updated Sydney protocol | Sites (1–2 biopsies each) |
|---|---|
| Jar A — antrum | Antrum lesser curvature (A1), antrum greater curvature (A2), incisura (A3) |
| Jar B — corpus | Corpus lesser curvature (B1), corpus greater curvature (B2) |

  - Minimum 2 containers; extra targeted biopsies of abnormalities go in a separate container.
  - Pathology report should give *H. pylori* status, severity of atrophy and GIM (mild, moderate, severe), GIM subtype (complete vs incomplete), and extent (limited = antrum and incisura; extensive = also corpus).
  - Operative Link on Gastritis Assessment (OLGA) and Operative Link on Gastric Intestinal Metaplasia Assessment (OLGIM) stage atrophy or GIM from none (0) to severe (III) in antrum (incl. incisura) and corpus; stages III–IV carry higher progression risk.
- **Imaging (Indicator 19):** HDWLE plus image-enhanced endoscopy (e.g., narrow-band imaging [NBI]). GIM: mildly nodular, ridged or tubulovillous pattern, "light blue crest" on near-focus NBI (Figure 4). Atrophy: pale mucosa, visible vasculature, loss of folds.
- **Gastric polyps (Indicator 20; Figure 5):**
  - Hyperplastic and adenomatous polyps should be resected when possible (biopsy alone may miss high-grade dysplasia or cancer); **all adenomas resected**.
  - Hyperplastic polyps: neoplasia risk rises with size; typically only those **>0.5 cm** need resection.
  - Adenomas or hyperplastic polyps → detailed HDWLE and image-enhanced inspection plus Sydney biopsies for GPMC and *H. pylori*.
  - Familial adenomatous polyposis: sample representative fundic gland polyps at initial diagnosis.
  - Polyps >1 cm without typical fundic gland polyp appearance → biopsy or complete resection.
- ***H. pylori* (Indicators 27–28):** all with a positive test of active infection should be offered treatment; test those with PUD, GPMC, or other associated conditions. Eradication assessment for **all** treated patients, **at least 1–2 months** after completing therapy. The plan may be documented in the record once pathology returns rather than in the endoscopy report.
- **GPMC surveillance plan (Indicator 29):**
  - **Surveillance every 3 yr** for GIM with ≥1 of 5 high-risk factors: first-degree relative with gastric cancer; foreign-born from a high-incidence nation; high-risk race or ethnicity; incomplete GIM subtype; corpus extension (corpus plus antrum or incisura).
  - Also suggested every 3 yr: severe histologic GIM or atrophic gastritis (with or without GIM of antrum and/or corpus).
  - **Document a no-surveillance plan** for low-risk GIM or atrophy: no high-risk clinical factors and low-risk histology (complete GIM, limited extent — antrum and/or incisura).
  - Multiple risk factors → shorter interval by shared decision (e.g., incomplete GIM plus family history → 2 yr).
  - Natural history: OLGIM II → early gastric neoplasia at median 50.7 mo; OLGIM III/IV at median 22.7 mo.

### Duodenum (Indicator 21)

- Minimum: **4 biopsies including 1 from the duodenal bulb** (9 or 12 o'clock), achievable with 2 forceps passes.
- Optimal detailed exam: 4 biopsies distal to the bulb plus 1–2 from the bulb, in 2 separate jars. Bulb biopsies may catch the most severe villous atrophy. Consider 1 bite per pass for orientation.

### Postprocedure (Indicators 22–25)

- **LA C/D esophagitis (Indicator 22):** up to 20% of erosive esophagitis is unhealed at 8 weeks of antisecretory therapy, up to 30% of LA C/D; BE found in 17.4% of LA C/D in one study. Repeat EGD after potent acid suppression — **double-dose PPI or potassium-competitive acid blocker for ≥8 weeks**.
- **Peptic stricture (Indicator 23):** PPIs reduced persistent stricture and redilation vs H2 receptor antagonists in an RCT; acid suppression, **preferably PPI or another potent agent**, prevents recurrence.
- **Nondysplastic BE (Indicator 24):** progression 0.3/100 patient-years → surveillance every **3–5 yr**; short-segment BE (<3 cm) every **5 yr**. Surveillance is overused (30% recommended at 1–2 yr in one registry). An interval of ≥3 yr in patients without dysplasia has been proposed as a quality metric.
- **CE-IM (Indicator 25):** reported CE-IM 77%–90% across RCTs and registries; the 18-month window follows the TREAT-BE quality indicators (2017).
