## Summary

ASGE guideline on the **treatment** of [[achalasia]], built on updated systematic reviews and meta-analyses of the effectiveness, adverse effects and cost of botulinum toxin injection, pneumatic dilation (PD), laparoscopic Heller myotomy (LHM) and [[poem|peroral endoscopic myotomy (POEM)]]. It makes 8 recommendations. Evidence quality is rated High / Moderate / Low / Very low (⊕⊕⊕⊕ to ⊕○○○); strength is carried by wording — "we recommend" (stronger) vs "we suggest" (weaker).

[[high-resolution-manometry|High-resolution manometry (HRM)]] is the criterion standard for diagnosis; endoscopy is mandatory to exclude pseudoachalasia or other mechanical obstruction at the esophagogastric junction (EGJ). LHM, PD and POEM are all effective; the choice depends on achalasia type, local expertise and patient preference. Botulinum toxin is not recommended as definitive therapy. POEM is suggested as the preferred treatment for type III, and patients having POEM should be counseled on the higher risk of post-procedure reflux.

## Key Findings / Claims

*CI = confidence interval · RR = risk ratio · OR = odds ratio · RCT = randomized controlled trial · GERD = gastroesophageal reflux disease · LES = lower esophageal sphincter · ESD = endoscopic submucosal dissection*

### Diagnosis (background, ungraded)

- Symptoms: dysphagia to solids and liquids ~90%, regurgitation 75%, weight loss 60%, chest pain 50%, heartburn 40%
- Endoscopy findings (dilation, retained food and secretions, puckered EGJ) are not diagnostic; endoscopy may be normal early
- Timed barium esophagram preferred — aids diagnosis, documents outcomes objectively, predicts symptom recurrence
- HRM subtypes: type I (classic, low intraesophageal pressure), type II (panesophageal pressurization), type III (high-amplitude spastic contractions)
- EndoFLIP: EGJ distensibility reduced in achalasia; persistently low distensibility associated with symptomatic failure after treatment; role still evolving
- Up to 5% of achalasia patients may require esophagectomy for end-stage disease

### Outcome Definitions

- Clinical success = Eckardt score ≤3 (dysphagia, regurgitation, chest pain, weight loss, each graded by severity); analyses restricted to Eckardt-reporting studies where possible
- GERD definitions vary across studies (symptoms, esophagitis on endoscopy, or pH monitoring)

### Single-Therapy Meta-analyses

| Therapy | Data | Clinical success | Other outcomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Botulinum toxin | ASGE meta-analysis, 22 uncontrolled studies, 730 patients | 77% (95% CI 72%–81%) at 1–6 months | LES pressure 38.23 → 23.30 mm Hg; chest pain 11%; serious adverse events exceedingly rare; durability limited to months |
| PD | ASGE meta-analysis, 52 uncontrolled studies, 4,166 patients | 83% (95% CI 79%–85%) at 3–6 months | LES pressure 34.47 → 20.80 mm Hg; perforation 2.8% (95% CI 2.3%–3.5%); bleeding requiring intervention 2%; symptomatic GERD 9% (95% CI 5%–16%) at ~6 months |
| LHM | Meta-analysis, 53 studies (5 RCTs + 48 cohorts), 5,834 patients | Dysphagia improvement 87.7% at mean 40 months; predicted 91.0% at 12 months, 90.0% at 24 months (not Eckardt-based) | GERD symptoms 17.5%, on endoscopy 11.5%, on pH monitoring 11.1%; recurrent/persistent symptoms ~5%–15% |
| POEM | Meta-analysis, 36 studies, 2,373 patients (98% achalasia) | 98% (95% CI 97%–100%) | Mucosal injury 4.8%, perforation 0.2%, bleeding 0.2%, subcutaneous emphysema 7.5%, pneumothorax 1.2%; at mean 8 months: symptomatic reflux 8.5%, esophagitis 13%, abnormal acid exposure 47% |

- PD: conventional approach starts with the 30-mm balloon in most patients and progresses to larger balloons (35, 40 mm) if no response; most often under fluoroscopic guidance. Response to **graded** dilation is the outcome relevant to practice
- POEM adverse events (1,826 patients): 7.5% had adverse events; mucosotomy 2.8%; severe 0.5%. Risk factors: sigmoid esophagus (OR 2.28), endoscopist experience <20 cases (OR 1.98), triangular tip knife (OR 3.22), current other than spray coagulation (OR 3.09)
- POEM ≥5 years (23 patients): long-term success 83%; abnormal acid exposure 38%

### Comparative Effectiveness

- **PD vs botulinum toxin** (7 RCTs, 178 patients; confidence "high"): no difference within 4 weeks; success at 6 months 80.7% vs 51.8% (RR 1.57), at 12 months 73.3% vs 37.5% (RR 1.88); 3 perforations in 188 PD procedures, no adverse events in 151 injections
- **PD vs LHM** (Cheng meta-analysis, 5 studies, 498 patients): LHM better at 3 months (RR 1.16) and 1 year (RR 1.14); no difference at 2 years (RR 1.05) or 5 years (RR 1.17); mucosal tears requiring intervention at LHM fewer than PD perforations requiring therapy (RR 0.25); reflux, LES pressure and quality of life not different
- **POEM vs LHM** (Awaiz meta-analysis, 7 trials, 483 patients): no difference in adverse events (OR 1.25), reflux (OR 1.27), length of stay, pain or long-term reflux; short-term clinical failure higher with LHM, 13% vs 0.85% (OR 9.82). No published RCT at the time
- **POEM vs PD** (ASGE meta-analysis, 3 studies, 114 POEM vs 92 PD): success at 12 months 93% vs 72% (RR 1.28); symptomatic GERD trend 23% vs 9%; erosive esophagitis 9%–48% vs 0%–13%. RCT at 2 years: 92% vs 54%; reflux esophagitis 41% vs 7%

### Subtypes

- Success ranges: type II 90%–100%, type I 63%–90%, type III 33%–70%
- Meta-analysis (298 PD, 429 LHM patients): type II best, type III worst after both PD and LHM (type II vs III: PD OR 27.18; LHM OR 6.86)
- Type III POEM (32 patients): success 90.6% at median 24 months

### Treatment Failure and Retreatment

- Retreatment needed in 23%–35% at 5–7 years after PD and 18%–27% at a median 5.3 years after Heller myotomy
- After failed LHM (recurrent or persistent symptoms in up to 21%): PD response 50%–75%; POEM success 92%–100%; POEM success lower with vs without prior LHM, 81% vs 94%; redo LHM 73%–89%, with perforation 1.5%–20%
- Redo POEM: 100% success in 21 patients (mean 11 months); 85% at 3 months in 46 procedures, adverse events 17%
- Prior PD or botulinum toxin may cause submucosal fibrosis and longer POEM procedure time, but long-term POEM outcomes are not affected

### Cost

- Cost per cure, year 1 → year 4: botulinum toxin $7,862 → $14,986; PD $7,175 → $2,393; LHM $11,582 → $2,896; POEM $12,120 → $3,030
- PD most cost-effective over 2 years; myotomy most cost-effective long term (PD efficacy 67% vs myotomy 90% at year 3)

### POEM Training

- Learning curve varies widely: ~100 cases to reduce technical failure, adverse events and clinical failure (one study); plateau after ~25 cases with prior esophageal ESD experience; 13 cases in another study. No standardized training curriculum

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- PD perforation: ASGE pools 2.8% across 52 uncontrolled studies; [[acg-2020-achalasia|ACG 2020]] gives a median 1.9% in experienced hands
- PD after failed LHM: ASGE 50%–75% response; ACG 2020 89% (systematic review, 87 patients)
- Post-POEM vs post-LHM reflux: the Awaiz meta-analysis cited here found no difference (OR 1.27); Rec 5 still asks for counseling on an increased reflux risk after POEM compared with PD and LHM
- Newer [[sages-2024-poem|SAGES 2024]] guidance suggests POEM over PD (conditional), and for types I/II either POEM with appropriate PPI use or LHM with fundoplication (conditional)
