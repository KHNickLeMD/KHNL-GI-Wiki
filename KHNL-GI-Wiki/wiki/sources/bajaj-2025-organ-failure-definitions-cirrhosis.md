## Summary
- ACLF diagnosis varies because the regional definitions of each OF differ (Table 1). Because these patients are largely managed by intensive care physicians, the panel aimed for OF definitions consistent with the intensive care literature — to identify at-risk patients, standardize management, set intervention criteria, define trial entry/end points, and inform transplant priority.
- For each organ (liver, immune system/infections, brain, kidney, circulatory, respiratory, gastrointestinal [GI]) the paper proposes **dysfunction vs failure criteria, dynamic criteria (worsening/improvement), and the impact of pre-existing chronic conditions** (Table 2), plus 36 consensus statements (Table 3; 34 reached ≥70% agreement, 2 are footnoted as not reaching it), 20 statements that did not reach agreement (Table 4), and a research agenda (Table 5).
- Headline definitions ("Summary" lines in the text): liver failure = total bilirubin ≥7.5 mg/dL **and** international normalized ratio (INR) ≥1.5; immune failure = ≥1 of invasive fungal infection, spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP), spontaneous bacteremia, *Vibrio* infection, severe sepsis/septic shock; acute encephalopathy = West Haven grade ≥2; kidney failure = acute kidney injury (AKI) stage ≥2 ± renal replacement therapy (RRT); circulatory failure = mean arterial pressure (MAP) <65 mm Hg ± vasopressor use; respiratory failure = nonintubated acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) oxygenation thresholds on high-flow oxygen or noninvasive ventilation; GI failure = inability to meet nutritional needs by mouth or the enteral route.
- Conclusion: on this basis ACLF "may be defined" as liver failure plus one of the extrahepatic failures above (see *Conclusions*). The panel overlaps heavily with the later multisociety ACLF definition ([[aasld-easl-2026-aclf-global-consensus]]), which uses different liver, brain and respiratory cut-offs (see *Contradictions*).

### Liver (text pp. 1043–1044, 1048; Table 2; Figure 1)
- **Background:** in the CANONIC study (basis of the EASL-CLIF definition) mean bilirubin in ACLF grades 1, 2 and 3 was 7.7, 15.2 and 23.2 mg/dL; in a recent multicenter study, EASL-CLIF ACLF patients had median bilirubin 5.6 mg/dL at enrollment. APASL liver-failure INR threshold >1.5; EASL-CLIF lists INR ≥2.5 as coagulation failure. Separating the effect of liver dysfunction, malnutrition and sepsis on INR may be difficult.
- **Cut-off derivation:** in a large inpatient global cirrhosis cohort (externally validated), the cut-offs with the highest negative predictive value for inpatient death were admission bilirubin ≤7.5 mg/dL, INR ≤1.5 and creatinine ≤1.5 mg/dL.
- **Proposed definition (p. 1044; Summary p. 1048):** liver failure = serum **total** bilirubin **≥7.5 mg/dL and INR ≥1.5**. Total rather than direct bilirubin is suggested as the marker of hepatic function.
- **Baseline:** changes from baseline bilirubin and INR in the previous 3 months may be considered in future; without baseline values, admission bilirubin and INR may be used.
- Bilirubin and INR above normal but below failure criteria → "liver dysfunction".
- Open questions (text): whether INR ≥1.5 should replace INR ≥1.7 (Child-Turcotte-Pugh score) as the dysfunction/failure cut-off; validation of a dynamic bilirubin change (eg, >3 mg/dL rise over 3 days); cut-offs and Model for End-Stage Liver Disease (MELD)-based grading need validation.
- **Other biomarkers (p. 1044, 1048):** thrombocytopenia may not reflect liver failure (portal hypertension); lactate is part of the APASL ACLF Research Consortium (AARC)-ACLF score, but whether lactate >2.0 mmol/L reflects hepatic or extrahepatic dysfunction is unclear; MELD-lactate adds prognostic information and lactate clearance may serve as an end point; elevated ferritin is associated with unfavorable prognosis but is not specific to liver failure; ammonia is a promising prognostic biomarker, independently associated with mortality but not future liver-related complications; lactate dehydrogenase, albumin and bile acids may also serve as surrogate markers of hepatic dysfunction; interleukin 6, urinary neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (NGAL) and copeptin are associated with 90-day mortality but are not specific to liver dysfunction. **Agreed: only serum bilirubin and INR are used to define liver dysfunction and failure.**

### Immune system and infections (text pp. 1048–1051; Table 2; Figure 2)
- **Infections:** typical sites SBP, urinary tract, respiratory tract, skin and soft tissue, bacteremia. Pathogens: Gram-negative (eg, *Escherichia coli*, *Klebsiella pneumoniae*), Gram-positive (eg, *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Enterococcus faecium*), fungi (mainly *Candida albicans*, non-albicans *Candida*, mucormycosis, *Aspergillus* spp.). Hepatitis viruses are recognized ACLF causes; Epstein-Barr virus, cytomegalovirus (CMV), herpes simplex virus (HSV) and SARS-CoV-2 may precipitate ACLF, mainly in immunocompromised patients (data scarce).
- Infections count as ACLF precipitants when detected before or at ACLF diagnosis: **44%** of clinically identifiable ACLF precipitants vs infection in only **22%** of decompensated cirrhosis without ACLF.
- Nosocomial infection: present in **20%** of ACLF patients with infection at admission; develops in **35%** after admission for ACLF not precipitated by infection.
- Higher 90-day transplant-free mortality in ACLF with infection (precipitant or acquired); second infections independently associated with ACLF development and higher 30-day mortality.
- Multidrug-resistant organism (MDRO) infections rising, with poor outcomes; prophylactic antibiotic strategies linked with MDRO emergence → risk stratification, antibiotic stewardship and nonantibiotic treatments proposed. Untimely diagnosis of fungal infection → high mortality.
- **Immune dysfunction:** "cirrhosis-associated immune dysfunction"; immune failure is not clearly defined but is the inability to eliminate invading pathogens. ACLF shows immature neutrophilia (impaired effector functions), altered monocytes (incl. monocytic myeloid-derived suppressor cells) and an increased neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR). **NLR >9** was independently associated with higher short-term mortality in acutely decompensated cirrhosis; NLR as a marker of immune deficiency needs study.
- **Summary (p. 1051): immune failure = ≥1 of (1) invasive fungal infection, (2) SBP, (3) spontaneous bacteremia, (4) *Vibrio* infections, (5) severe sepsis or septic shock.**

- Clinically apparent precipitants (infection, alcohol-related hepatitis, GI hemorrhage) **or** none → ↑ pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) and ↑ cytokines.

### Brain (text pp. 1051–1054; Table 2; Figures 3–4)
- **Terminology:** "brain failure" does not exist in standard neurological terminology; consensus to use **"acute encephalopathy"** for acute delirium in critical care. "Hepatic encephalopathy" and "acute encephalopathy" are **not interchangeable**; treat each form by its cause.
- West Haven and Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) are used to grade HE; the Hepatic Encephalopathy Staging Tool and Hepatic Encephalopathy Grading Index have been used in multicenter trials to reduce subjectivity.
- **Brain dysfunction** = cognitive impairment without overt HE, incl. covert HE; **grade 2–4 HE = "acute encephalopathy"** needing inpatient or critical care.
- Factors worsening cognition: age, alcohol use, sleep apnea, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease, diabetes.
- **Hospital setting:** metabolic changes, alcohol intoxication/withdrawal, medication use/withdrawal, advanced age and prolonged intensive care unit (ICU) stay precipitate delirium. Opioid toxicity → bradypnea, pinpoint pupils, altered consciousness; benzodiazepines → reduced consciousness; psychiatric drugs → cholinergic, anticholinergic, adrenergic or serotonin syndromes. Look for cerebrovascular disease (coagulopathy → intracranial bleeding).
- **Ammonia:** levels **>1.4 times the upper limit of normal** identify the highest-risk group; **normal ammonia with altered mental status → re-evaluate the HE diagnosis**; hyperammonemia can occur without acute encephalopathy; prognostic value inconsistent.
- Specialized neuroimaging (magnetic resonance, diffusion-weighted imaging, magnetic resonance spectroscopy) can help when cognitive dysfunction persists despite HE therapy — not ready for clinical deployment.
- **Preventing progression:** grade 3–4 HE is a major sentinel event (aspiration pneumonia risk; harder advanced-care and transplant planning). Manage by drug withdrawal, precipitant recognition, fall and aspiration prevention. Inflammatory cytokines, metabolomics and thyroxine may predict progression but are not routinely measured. Prophylactic lactulose to prevent HE in variceal bleeding succeeds in selected cases; rifaximin is not useful in this acute setting.
- **Pre-hospital/post-discharge:** differentiating HE from dementia, parkinsonism, depression, sleep-disordered breathing and neurologic disorders depends on onset, course, duration, sleep-cycle effect, attention span and neuromotor features; detailed cognitive testing is better done as an outpatient.
- **Summary (p. 1054): acute encephalopathy = grade ≥2 on West Haven criteria.**

### Kidney (text pp. 1054–1056; Table 2; Figure 5)
- Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) definitions of acute kidney disease (AKD), AKI and chronic kidney disease (CKD) are largely accepted in hepatology.
- **AKI (text p. 1054):** sCr rise by 50% from baseline known/presumed within the prior 7 days, **or** sCr rise ≥0.3 mg/dL in <48 hours, **or** oliguria <0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥6 hours.
- **AKI phenotypes:** prerenal azotemia (responds to volume challenge), hepatorenal syndrome (HRS)-AKI (does not respond to volume) — both functional; acute tubular injury (ATI, formerly "acute tubular necrosis") — structural, usually with renal tubular epithelial cells and coarse granular casts in urine. Response to treatment = regression to a lower AKI stage.
- **AKD:** AKI persisting beyond 7 days and up to 3 months; ~**30%** of hospitalized cirrhosis. Risk factors: older age, stage 2/3 AKI, CKD, diabetes, ascites, infection, community-acquired AKI. Defined by sCr rise of 50% from baseline for <3 months, **or** glomerular filtration rate (GFR) <60 mL/min/1.73 m² for <3 months, **or** GFR fall (CKD-Epidemiology Collaboration equation) by >35% for <3 months. May follow unresolved/partially resolved AKI (AKI-AKD) or arise without prior AKI (nonacute kidney injury [NAKI]-AKD).
- **CKD:** diabetes/hypertension-related CKD usually shows structural damage (albuminuria, urinary sediment, small kidneys); CKD from cirrhotic hemodynamics (previously type 2 HRS) shows no urinary findings other than severe sodium retention. AKI and AKD progress to CKD in up to **25%** and **60%** respectively.
- **Acute on chronic kidney disease (AoCKD) — area of uncertainty:** no accepted definition or staging in cirrhosis; proposed to apply the AKI definition using the elevated sCr as baseline. Risk ~70%; most AoCKD is stage 1. Clinical impact of a ≥0.3 mg/dL rise over an already elevated sCr is unclear; stage 2 AoCKD (doubling) implies severe dysfunction; stage 3 AoCKD likely only sCr ≥4 mg/dL or tripled sCr (RRT candidates). Vasoconstrictor treatment of AoCKD not adequately studied; retrospective data suggest better prognosis than AKI alone.
- **Kidney failure:** no consensus on an sCr level defining kidney failure in cirrhosis; the panel **proposes AKI stage ≥2 ± RRT**. HRS was historically diagnosed at sCr >1.5 mg/dL; some data support sCr >1.5 mg/dL as a cut-off for poor renal outcomes, but it has not been used to define kidney failure. Accepted terms now: AKI, AKD, CKD.
- **Biomarkers:** urine NGAL rises stepwise from HRS-AKI to ATI; **urine NGAL >220 µg/g creatinine identifies ATI**. Favorable terlipressin response **70%** with NGAL <220 µg/g vs **33%** with NGAL >220 µg/g; role in specific scenarios needs definition. Cystatin C is more accurate than creatinine for AKI (less affected by demographics and muscle mass) but testing is limited.
- **Summary (p. 1056): kidney failure = AKI stage ≥2 ± renal replacement therapy.**

### Circulatory (text pp. 1056–1057; Table 2)
- Hyperdynamic circulation (low systemic vascular resistance, high cardiac output) = circulatory dysfunction; precipitants → relative arterial hypovolemia, hypotension, lactic acidosis.
- **Definition (text p. 1056):** irrespective of cardiovascular comorbidity, circulatory failure = hypotension (MAP <65 mm Hg requiring vasopressor support) with evidence of hypoperfusion (ie, lactic acidosis).
- Anticipated revision of the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) 2.0 score: no vasopressor / low vasopressor (norepinephrine <0.1 µg/kg/min) / high vasopressor (norepinephrine ≥0.1 µg/kg/min).
- **Monitoring:** lactate (may be affected by reduced hepatic clearance), dynamic echocardiography (cardiac index, systemic vascular resistance), inferior vena cava (IVC) diameter and collapsibility on point-of-care ultrasound (POCUS). IVC variation **>12%–18%** on mechanical ventilation and **>40%** when breathing spontaneously may separate fluid responders from nonresponders — limited validation in cirrhosis; ascites/abdominal hypertension may limit IVC and central venous pressure use.
- **MAP targets:** Surviving Sepsis Campaign prefers MAP ≥65–70 mm Hg over >70 mm Hg. Meta-analysis: higher target (mean difference 15 mm Hg) did not change mortality, more new arrhythmias. TARGET-C randomized trial (cirrhosis + septic shock), high 80–85 vs low 60–65 mm Hg: no survival benefit, more adverse events with high MAP. Several studies incl. TARGET-C suggest higher MAP improves renal outcomes. In 1542 patients with cirrhosis/ACLF, time-weighted MAP increase from <60 to 75 mm Hg was associated with a 53% reduction in 28-day mortality, with no further benefit beyond 75 mm Hg (similar for AKI nonrecovery). → Evidence does not support different MAP targets in heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF)/cirrhotic cardiomyopathy; individualize.
- **Fluids and vasopressors:** in ACLF with sepsis-induced hypotension, calcium-free balanced crystalloid appears safer and better tolerated than 20% human albumin (more pulmonary complications with albumin). In SBP and HRS-AKI, 20%–25% albumin is recommended, though as a conditional recommendation based on very low certainty of evidence (cited, not the panel's own). ACLF may respond less to fluids → **early vasopressors recommended; norepinephrine recommended** to reach target MAP and not precluded by pre-existing heart failure.
- **Summary (p. 1057): circulatory failure = MAP <65 mm Hg ± vasopressor use.**

### Respiratory (text p. 1057; Table 2)
- Respiratory failure and its severity in cirrhosis should follow the **revised Berlin criteria**, as for other critically ill patients — text gives this as arterial partial pressure of oxygen (PaO₂)/FiO₂ <200.
- Pulmonary disease other than hepatopulmonary syndrome (eg, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease [COPD], restrictive lung disease) seldom needs cirrhosis-specific management and is not part of the Berlin criteria.
- Noninvasive positive pressure ventilation (NIV) and high-flow nasal cannula (HFNC) criteria from critical care should be accepted for cirrhosis. NIV prevents intubation vs standard oxygen in mild–moderate hypoxemia; FLORALI trial: with PaO₂/FiO₂ <200, HFNC had lower intubation and 90-day mortality than standard oxygen or NIV. Use general critical care guidelines for NIV/HFNC in nonintubated cirrhosis.
- Consider ascites/abdominal hypertension: therapeutic paracentesis may be a simple adjunct to improve ventilation and oxygenation.
- **Moderate/severe ARDS:** lung-protective ventilation — low tidal volume **6 mL/kg predicted body weight**, plateau pressure **<30 cm H₂O** recommended. High positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP) can impede venous return and worsen hypotension but may improve oxygenation (PaO₂/FiO₂ <200 mm Hg). Chronic lung disease → adjust ventilation case by case.
- **Summary (p. 1057): respiratory failure = PaO₂:FiO₂ ≤300 mm Hg or peripheral oxygen saturation (SpO₂):FiO₂ ≤315 (if SpO₂ ≤97%) on HFNC with flow ≥30 L/min or NIV/continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) with at least 5 cm H₂O end-expiratory pressure.**

### Gastrointestinal (text p. 1057; Table 2)
- GI dysfunction in cirrhosis = dysmotility with delayed intestinal transit and altered permeability → abdominal discomfort, nausea, diarrhea, constipation; promotes bacterial translocation.
- GI failure = reduction of functioning gut mass below the minimum needed for adequate digestion and absorption. Precipitants: medications such as lactulose, inflammation, sepsis, electrolyte abnormalities, acidosis.
- **Management:** prompt recognition, avoid precipitants, early enteral nutrition; if unsuccessful, parenteral nutrition.

| Item (Table 2, p. 1048) | Criteria |
|---|---|
| Dysfunction | GI symptoms (nausea, vomiting, constipation, diarrhea, dysmotility, altered intestinal permeability) directly related to cirrhosis and hospitalization that impair digestion or absorption, without systemic consequences |
| Failure | Inability to meet nutritional needs by mouth or the enteral route, not restored despite intervention to use the enteral route |

- **T3-GI-1** (Table 3, p. 1050) — Inability to use the gut as a viable alternative for nutrition is gut failure. 34 (97%)

### Conclusions — proposed ACLF definition (p. 1058)
- "Based on this consensus, ACLF may be defined as" **liver failure (serum bilirubin >7.5 mg/dL and INR ≥1.5)** with: HE ≥grade 2; AKI stage ≥2; MAP ≤65 mm Hg ± vasopressors; PaO₂:FiO₂ ≤300 mm Hg; or SpO₂:FiO₂ ≤315 (if SpO₂ ≤97%); or inability to sustain adequate nutrients enterally due to cirrhosis.

### Agenda for further research (Table 5, p. 1058)
- **Liver:** validate bilirubin and INR cut-offs for diagnosing and grading liver failure; biomarkers as surrogates of improvement in trials; metabolomic signatures.
- **Immune system and infections:** rapid diagnostics for MDRO and systemic fungal infection; nonantibiotic prevention; longitudinal immune-cell dynamics toward ACLF; roles of circulating immune cells in failure to kill bloodstream pathogens; immune-deficiency biomarkers; gut mucosal immunity and microbiome by stage; local immunity/microbiome at extraintestinal barriers.
- **Brain:** biomarkers separating acute HE from other encephalopathy; ammonia as diagnostic/prognostic marker; thyroxine; specialized neuroimaging; whether persistent acute encephalopathy makes transplant futile.
- **Kidney:** biomarkers for AoCKD phenotypes; AoCKD definition, staging, prognosis; AoCKD response to drugs and definition of response; AKD prognosis and management; predicting AKI/AKD progression to CKD.
- **Cardiorespiratory:** perfusion markers in ACLF.
- **GI failure:** markers of GI permeability and nutritional failure; gut microbiome–intestinal failure interaction.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

### Inconsistencies within the document
- **Liver-failure bilirubin sign:** ≥7.5 mg/dL in Table 2, Figure 1 and the liver Summary, but **>7.5 mg/dL** in the Conclusions ACLF definition (p. 1058).
- **Liver-dysfunction INR:** "INR ≥1.3 and <1.5" in Table 2 vs ">1.3 but <1.5" in Figure 1A/B; Figure 1A also places INR ≤1.3 under "no liver dysfunction" — INR exactly 1.3 falls in both.
- **Figure 1A adds categories Table 2 lacks:** "possible liver dysfunction" and "possible liver failure" (INR ≥1.5 with bilirubin ≥3 but <7.5 mg/dL). The text does not say how "possible" categories should be used.
- **Dynamic bilirubin criterion:** Table 2 says a 3.0 mg/dL rise in 3 d "may be considered as failure, but requires validation"; the text says it "may be useful to validate"; Figure 1B shows ">3 mg/dL increase over 3 days" as a liver-failure criterion without that caveat.
- **Circulatory failure — four wordings:** text definition = MAP <65 mm Hg **requiring vasopressor support with hypoperfusion (lactic acidosis)**; Table 2 severity = MAP <65 mm Hg, then vasopressor tiers; Summary = MAP <65 mm Hg ± vasopressor use; Conclusions = **MAP ≤65** mm Hg ± vasopressors; T3-Circ-2 = "MAP <65 mm Hg or requirement for vasopressors".
- **Respiratory failure threshold:** text says the revised Berlin definition is PaO₂/FiO₂ <200, but the Summary, Table 2 and Conclusions set failure at PaO₂:FiO₂ ≤300 / SpO₂:FiO₂ ≤315 on high-flow oxygen or NIV/CPAP (which also includes Berlin "mild" ARDS).
- **Kidney failure was not agreed:** Table 2 and the Summary propose AKI stage ≥2 ± RRT, but T4-Kidney-1 (stage 2 AKI vs sCr 2 or 1.5 mg/dL) reached only 37%. The text cites "(Table 1)" for this proposal, although Table 1 lists the legacy definitions.
- **Transplant referral:** Table 2 (FAKI clinical features) states "liver transplant assessment is recommended", while T3-Kidney-3 (refer all stage 2–3 AKI for transplant evaluation) failed agreement at 57%.
- **AKI wording:** Table 2 FAKI row uses ↑sCr ">1.5× from baseline", the stage 1 row "≥1.5–2.0 times", and the text "by 50% … within the prior 7 days"; baseline sCr is "stable sCr in ≤3 mo" (Table 2).
- **Immune failure:** Table 2 requires CSPH plus a qualifying infection; the Summary omits CSPH; the Conclusions ACLF definition does not include immune failure at all.
- **Brain:** GCS bands in Table 2 overlap at their edges (grade 2 "12, can be up to 15"; grade 3 "6–12"; grade 4 "3–6"). Whether grade 3 and 4 should be separated within OF did not reach agreement (T4-Brain-9, 20%).
- Method details (selection process, rounds) are only in the online supplement.

### Differences from other wiki sources (as summarized on those wiki pages)
- **[[aasld-easl-2026-aclf-global-consensus]]** (newer, multisociety; same chairs Bajaj and Shawcross) — per the wiki's summary of it:
  - **Liver failure:** INR ≥1.5 and bilirubin **≥5 mg/dL** (Statement 3) vs **≥7.5 mg/dL** here.
  - **Brain:** West Haven 2 = dysfunction, **3–4 = organ failure** (Statement 11, Table 2) vs grade **≥2** = OF ("acute encephalopathy") here. Severe impairment there = West Haven 3–4 or GCS <12.
  - **Respiratory failure:** mechanical ventilation, PaO₂/FiO₂ **≤225**, or SpO₂/FiO₂ **≤244** (Statement 13); 225 < PaO₂/FiO₂ ≤300 = dysfunction. Here, failure = PaO₂:FiO₂ **≤300** / SpO₂:FiO₂ **≤315** on HFNC ≥30 L/min or NIV/CPAP.
  - **Circulatory failure:** need for vasopressors (not for HRS-AKI alone); MAP <65 mm Hg = dysfunction (Statement 14, Table 2). Here MAP <65 mm Hg ± vasopressors = failure.
  - **Kidney:** AKI stage 2–3 (KDIGO) or new RRT = failure — **agrees** with this document's proposal.
  - **Immune and gut failure:** "need further definition" (Statement 16) — this document gives provisional criteria for both.
  - Under source priority the newer multisociety document wins where they conflict; this document's immune and GI criteria remain the only definitions on file.
- **[[aasld-2024-aclf]]** — per the wiki's summary: its minimum ACLF definition requires liver failure as elevated bilirubin **and** INR without numeric cut-offs plus ≥1 extrahepatic OF (GS 1); brain severity = West Haven 3–4 and GCS <8. Its table of EASL-CLIF cut-offs lists liver failure as total bilirubin **≥12 mg/dL** and respiratory **≤200 / ≤214**, whereas this document's Table 1 prints EASL-CLIF liver as "bilirubin >6 mg/dL or >12 mg/dL" and respiratory as "<200 / <214".
- **[[moreau-2013-canonic-aclf]]** — per the wiki's summary: CLIF-SOFA organ failure = bilirubin **≥12.0** mg/dL (6.0 to <12.0 is a sub-failure score), creatinine ≥2.0 mg/dL or RRT, INR ≥2.5 **or platelets ≤20×10⁹/L**, PaO₂/FiO₂ ≤200 or SpO₂/FiO₂ ≤214. This document's Table 1 omits the platelet alternative and lists liver ">6 or >12 mg/dL". Mean bilirubin by ACLF grade (7.7 / 15.2 / 23.2 mg/dL) is quoted here from CANONIC.
- **[[aasld-2021-ascites-sbp-hrs]]** — per the wiki's summary:
  - AKI stage 1 = rise ≥0.3 mg/dL up to 2-fold of baseline; stage 3 creatinine >4 mg/dL is given as 353.6 µmol/L with an acute rise ≥0.3 mg/dL (26.5 µmol/L). Here: stage 1 adds "≥1.5–2.0 times baseline"; conversions printed as 352 and 26.4 µmol/L.
  - Treatment response = creatinine <1.5 mg/dL or return to within 0.3 mg/dL of baseline over ≤14 days (Statement 55). Here: complete response = AKI-stage regression with final sCr <0.3 mg/dL above baseline; partial = regression with final sCr ≥0.3 mg/dL above baseline; no absolute 1.5 mg/dL criterion and no time window.
  - "All patients with cirrhosis and AKI should be considered for urgent LT [liver transplantation] evaluation" (Statement 57) vs T3-Kidney-3 here (stage 2–3 AKI only), which failed agreement (57%).
  - Agrees that former type 2 HRS now falls under CKD.
