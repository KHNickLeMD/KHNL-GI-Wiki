## Summary

The **Hill grade** has been, since 1996, the endoscopic classification of the **antireflux barrier (ARB)**, scoring the **gastroesophageal flap valve (GEFV)** on a 4-point scale from the retroflexed view. Its uptake has been limited by subjectivity and by the perception that it is not clinically relevant. With endoscopic antireflux therapy now a live option, the AFS convened a working group to revise it.

The group's central conclusion is that the Hill grade **measures the wrong thing, or at least too little of it**: all components of the ARB — the **lower esophageal sphincter (LES) and its sling fibers, the crural diaphragm, and the flap valve** — contribute to **esophagogastric junction (EGJ) integrity**, and they are anatomically interdependent. A sliding hiatal hernia necessarily implies loss of intra-abdominal esophageal length and loss of a functioning flap valve, so a classification that grades the valve while treating the hiatus as an afterthought cannot be reproducible.

The proposed **AFS endoscopic classification of EGJ integrity** replaces visual impression with three measured parameters, the **LDF components** — hiatal hernia axial **L**ength in cm, hiatal aperture **D**iameter in cm, and **F**lap valve present or absent. These stratify EGJ integrity from **grade 1 (intact)** through **grades 2–4 (partial, moderate and complete disruption)**, each of grades 2–4 carrying loss of a functioning flap valve. Hill grades 1 and 2 — both "normal" — collapse into a single AFS grade 1, freeing three grades to describe degrees of disruption.

The paper also stipulates the **endoscopic methodology** the grade depends on, which the Hill classification never specified: without adequate insufflation and retroflexed provocative maneuvers, the EGJ is **systematically under-graded**. The classification is explicitly **unvalidated** — the stated future directions are validation studies against the presence and severity of gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), and extension to the post-intervention valve.

## Key Findings / Claims

### AFS position on the components of the antireflux barrier

Working-group survey identified 6 contributing factors; **3 rose to the top** (Figure 2):

| Component | Barrier type |
|---|---|
| **Crural diaphragm** | Physiologic **and** mechanical |
| **Gastroesophageal flap valve (GEFV)** | Mechanical |
| **LES and its sling fibers** | Physiologic |

- Intra-abdominal esophageal length and an acute angle of His are **components of having an intact GEFV**, not independent factors — they belong under the flap-valve heading.
- The **phrenoesophageal ligament** is a fascial extension of the crural diaphragm, commonly considered an extension of it rather than an independent contributor.
- **Any process that mechanically disrupts the ARB can potentially induce GERD.**

### The Hill grade, as originally described (Figure 1)

| Hill grade | Original description |
|---|---|
| **I** | Ridge of tissue closely approximated to shaft of endoscope |
| **II** | Ridge slightly less well defined, opens with respiration |
| **III** | Ridge barely present, a hiatal hernia is nearly always present |
| **IV** | Gastroesophageal area space open all the time, a hiatal hernia is always present |

- Grades **I and II are normal**; **III and IV abnormal** and observed in patients with reflux. Hill grades III and IV are associated with symptomatic and pathologic GERD and erosive esophagitis.
- Derived from a 1996 in-vitro cadaver model (no LES function; the pressure gradient was attributed to a competent "flap valve") plus a small in-vivo study — **12 controls and 12 patients with reflux**.

### Limitations of the Hill grade (the paper's own list)

- Focused **on the flap valve only**.
- Hiatal hernia is mentioned but its parameters are **vague and subjective**; hernia **measurement is not used to differentiate the grades**.
- Vague on what characteristics define a **competent** flap valve — "a ridge of tissue closely approximated to the shaft of the endoscope" is open to interpretation.
- Grades **I and II are both normal** with no explanation of why the distinction matters — both are subtle variations of a normal valve.
- **Does not describe the optimal endoscopic technique** (insufflation time, provocative maneuvers) — a major contributor to interobserver variability.
- **No endoscopic nomenclature** for describing EGJ anatomy, so no basis for standardized communication.
- Developed by a thoracic surgeon and **not widely adopted** by medical or surgical societies.

### The AFS endoscopic classification of EGJ integrity — the LDF components

- **L** = hiatal hernia **axial length in cm**, measured between the **Z line and the diaphragmatic imprint**.
- **D** = **largest aperture of the hiatus in cm**, by comparison with the endoscope shaft — **1 conventional scope diameter ≈ 1 cm**. ⚠ Because D is a measurement against the scope, **you must know your scope diameter** to measure accurately.
- **F** = **flap valve present (F+) or absent (F−)**.

**Figure 5 — the classification itself.** Recreated as a table (the published figure pairs each column with a retroflexed endoscopic photograph and a line schematic):

| | **Grade 1**<br/>Intact | **Grade 2**<br/>Partial disruption | **Grade 3**<br/>Moderate disruption | **Grade 4**<br/>Complete disruption |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hiatal axial length, cm (L)** | None (0 cm) | None (0 cm) | 0–2 cm | **>2 cm** |
| **Hiatal aperture, cm (D)** | Snug to scope, 1 cm | Loose, 1–2 cm | Open, 2–3 cm | Wide open, **>3 cm** |
| **Flap valve (F)** | Present, full lip with omega shape (F+) | **Absent**, thinning and flattening of the valve lip (F−) | Absent (F−) | Absent (F−) |
| **LDF components** | **L0, D1, F+** | **L0, D1-2, F−** | **L0-2, D2-3, F−** | **L>2, D>3, F−** |
| **Color code** | Green | Yellow | Orange | Red |

### The two grading rules that decide the grade

- **The final grade reflects the parameter showing the *greatest* degree of disruption.** The paper's own worked example: axial length **1.5 cm** (grade 3) with a hiatal aperture of **4 cm** (grade 4) → **the final grade is 4**.
- **Any axial hiatal hernia length at all makes the lowest possible grade a 3.** Once there is enough gap for a sliding hiatal hernia, the intra-abdominal esophagus and the flap valve are lost — so "hiatal hernia present with a normal-looking flap valve" is **not a possible AFS grade**, though it is routinely documented under the Hill system.

### Grade-by-grade detail

- **Grade 1** — adequate intra-abdominal esophageal length, acute angle of His, hiatus **snug against the endoscope**. The valve lip is judged normal by being **full or thick and taking an omega configuration (approximately 270°)**. **Hill grade 2 is now encompassed within AFS grade 1.**
- **Grade 2** — **no Hill equivalent.** The **most distinctive difference between AFS grades 1 and 2 is loss of the flap valve**, which is where hiatal disruption and crural separation begin: a gap of **1–2 cm** in the hiatus, progressive mediastinal migration of the intra-abdominal esophagus, widening of the angle of His — but still **no axial herniation**.
- **Grade 3** — similar to Hill grade 3, but with objective measurement of crural separation and axial hernia length.
- **Grade 4** — complete disruption: axial length **>2 cm**, aperture **>3 cm**, flap valve absent.

### Optimal endoscopic methodology (the paper's stipulated technique)

1. **Insufflate adequately** — approximately **30 to 45 seconds**, or until there is **flattening of the gastric fundus rugal folds**.
2. **Retroflex and perform rotational provocative maneuvers** to induce potential sliding hiatal herniation: **gently pull the tip of the endoscope in the retroflexed position along the lesser curvature**, inducing potential sliding herniation and widening of the hiatal aperture.
3. From this view, assess the **hiatal opening defect in cm (D)** and the **extent of hiatal herniation**, shown by **real-time involution of the Z line**.
4. **Revert to the forward view** to measure the **axial length of herniation (L)** — the distance between the Z line and the diaphragmatic indentation.
5. **Document the measurements and capture endoscopic images of the highest degree of EGJ disruption**, as the means of communicating between endoscopists.

> ⚠ **Without these maneuvers the EGJ is often "under-graded"** — the paper names this as a major contributor to interobserver variability. The accuracy of any visual classification "begins with a systematic methodology to image the hiatus."

### Basic endoscopic nomenclature of the EGJ (Figure 4)

- Orientation in the **retroflexed view**: greater and lesser curvature, with the corresponding positions of the **anterior and posterior gastric walls**.
- The flap valve is described as having a **lip** and a **body**.
- **Length of the valve** = the distance between the **diaphragmatic impression in the distal esophagus and the Z line** on the forward endoscopic view; it can also be measured in retroflexion.
- Attributed to Jobe et al. (2004), who established the characteristics in patients after antireflux surgery.

### Table 1 — Hill versus AFS, as the paper compares them

| | Hill classification | AFS classification |
|---|---|---|
| **Emphasis** | The flap valve | The flap valve **and degree of hiatal disruption** |
| **Endoscopic methodology** | Not addressed | Maximal insufflation for **30–45 s**; retroflex provocative maneuvers |
| **Nomenclature** | Not addressed | Basic endoscopic nomenclature of the EGJ provided |
| **Grade** | 1, 2, 3, 4 | 1, 2, 3, 4 |
| **Flap valve** | Normal (grades 1 & 2); abnormal (grades 3 & 4) | **Presence (F+) in grade 1**; absence (F−) in grades 2, 3 and 4 |
| **Hiatal disruption** | Cursory: occasional (grades 1 & 2), nearly always (grade 3), always present (grade 4) | **Objective criteria**: axial length (L), crural defect in scope diameters (D) |

### Stated future directions

1. **Validation studies** of the AFS classification's ability to accurately **predict the presence and severity of GERD**.
2. **Extending the work** to grading the hiatal appearance in patients who have undergone **antireflux interventions**.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **No contradiction with [[afs-2024-ge-valve-after-antireflux-surgery]]** — the 2024 white paper describes the **post-surgical** valve and explicitly defers to this paper for the native-valve hiatus grade. The two are complementary halves of one scheme by an overlapping author group.
- **Grade notation differs between the two AFS papers.** This 2022 paper uses **Arabic numerals** throughout ("AFS hiatus grade 1"); the 2024 paper writes **"AFS hiatus grades II, III, or IV"** in places. Same four strata — a typographic inconsistency within the society's own output, not a disagreement.
- **Relationship to the Hill grade is a revision, not a contradiction.** Hill grades 1 and 2 both map to AFS grade 1; AFS grade 2 has no Hill equivalent; Hill 3 ≈ AFS 3. ⚠ The two scales are **not interconvertible at the top end** — a Hill grade documented alongside an axial hernia length is a combination the AFS scheme forbids.
- **The classification is unvalidated.** The paper states this itself and makes validation its first future direction; [[afs-2023-transoral-incisionless-fundoplication]] independently says it should not drive procedure selection until validated in independent studies. The wiki therefore carries it as **reporting nomenclature, not a selection criterion**.
- **Decision thresholds on the wiki still belong to the Hill grade.** The TIF/surgery routing rule on [[antireflux-surgery]] is stated as *hiatal hernia ≤2 cm with Hill I/II versus >2 cm with Hill III/IV* ([[asge-2024-gerd]]). **This paper supplies no AFS-based procedure-selection threshold**, so none was created.
- **Figures.** Figure 5 (the grade-by-grade photographs and schematics) and Figure 3 (the insufflation/provocative-maneuver sequence) are embedded on [[reflux-testing]]. Figures 1 (Hill grades) and 4 (flap-valve nomenclature) are carried as tables and text only.
