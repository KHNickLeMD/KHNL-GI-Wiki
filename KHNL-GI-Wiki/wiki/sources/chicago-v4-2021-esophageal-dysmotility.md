## Summary

- **What it is:** fourth version of the [[chicago-classification-v4|Chicago Classification]] for [[high-resolution-manometry|high-resolution manometry (HRM)]]; replaces version 3.0 (2015).
- **Panel:** 52 experts from 6 international motility societies, 20 countries; 7 subgroups (standard protocol, achalasia, esophagogastric junction [EGJ] outflow obstruction [EGJOO], distal esophageal spasm [DES], hypercontractile esophagus, ineffective esophageal motility [IEM], EGJ metrics); November 2018–October 2020.
- **Grading vocabulary:** statements accepted at **≥80% agreement**; **≥85% = Strong recommendation**, **80–85% = Conditional recommendation**. Evidence then rated by Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) — High / Moderate / Low / Very Low — when possible; some statements carry no GRADE level or are labelled **Accepted Clinical Observation**. Statements are **unnumbered**.
- **Key updates:** standardized protocol with supine **and** upright positions plus provocative testing; stricter EGJOO definition; higher IEM threshold; fragmented peristalsis folded into IEM; no major vs minor distinction; baseline EGJ metrics; diagnoses labelled **conclusive vs inconclusive**; supportive testing with timed barium esophagram (TBE, preferably with tablet) and/or [[flip-panometry|functional lumen imaging probe (FLIP)]].
- **Scope:** normal foregut anatomy only — not after foregut surgery/intervention or with large hiatal or paraesophageal hernias; a careful index endoscopy first, since mechanical obstruction precludes CCv4.0 (Chicago Classification version 4.0).
- **Classification uses the primary position** (where 10 wet swallows are done, supine or upright); secondary position and provocation are supportive — except for EGJOO and absent contractility, which need both positions.

## Key Findings / Claims

*Statements are unnumbered in the source; grouped by section. "Grade" = GRADE evidence level + recommendation strength as printed.*

### Standard HRM Protocol (Figures 1–2)

- **Before:** fast ≥4 h (small amounts of clear fluid allowed); informed consent.
- **Supine (start):** ≥60 s adaptation → ≥3 deep inspirations to confirm position → ≥30 s baseline (upper esophageal sphincter [UES], lower esophageal sphincter [LES], respiratory inversion point [RIP], basal EGJ pressure) → **10 × 5 mL wet swallows** (ambient water, or saline with impedance), ≥30 s apart → **1 multiple rapid swallow (MRS)** sequence (5 × 2 mL, 2–3 s apart, 10-mL syringe; repeat up to 3× if failed or abnormal).
- **Upright** (sitting ≥80°): ≥60 s adaptation → ≥3 deep inspirations → ≥30 s baseline → **≥5 × 5 mL wet swallows**, ≥30 s apart → **1 rapid drink challenge (RDC)**: 200 mL water through a straw as fast as possible.
- **No conclusive major disorder, or findings don't fit symptoms:** solid test swallows, solid test meal, and/or pharmacologic provocation (high probability of missed EGJ obstruction); post-prandial high-resolution impedance observation (suspected rumination/belching).
- **Equivocal or suspected obstruction not meeting achalasia criteria:** TBE (preferably with tablet) and/or FLIP as independent supportive test.
- **Equipment:** solid-state catheter, sensor spacing <2 cm; water-perfused acceptable with appropriate normative values (supine only); impedance recommended, not required.
- Labs starting upright should perform 10 upright swallows. Report should include baseline EGJ measures and symptoms during the study (within 15 s of a motility dysfunction).

### Thresholds and Metric Statements

| Statement | Grade |
|---|---|
| Supine median integrated relaxation pressure (IRP) threshold **15 mmHg (Medtronic)**, **22 mmHg (Laborie/Diversatek)** | Moderate, Strong |
| Upright median IRP threshold **12 mmHg (Medtronic)**, **15 mmHg (Laborie/Diversatek)** | Low, Strong |
| Distinguish esophageal contractile activity from other causes of distal pressure rise (intrabolus pressure, artifact) | Very Low, Strong |
| Panesophageal pressurization cut-off **30 mmHg** on the isobaric contour tool | Very Low, Strong |
| Intrabolus pressure threshold, supine wet swallows (Medtronic) **20 mmHg** on the isobaric contour tool | Moderate, Strong |
| RDC: IRP **>12 mmHg** (Medtronic) over the first 30 s and panesophageal pressurization **>20 mmHg** are criteria for outflow obstruction | Low, Conditional |

### HRM Metrics and Thresholds (Table 1)

| Metric | Definition | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| **IRP** | Mean of 4 s of maximal deglutitive relaxation in the 10-s window after UES relaxation, referenced to gastric pressure | Abnormal: supine median **≥15 mmHg** (Medtronic) / **≥22 mmHg** (Laborie/Diversatek); upright median **≥12 mmHg** (Medtronic) / **≥15 mmHg** (Laborie/Diversatek). Supportive of outflow obstruction: IRP >12 mmHg on RDC or >25 mmHg on solid test meal (Medtronic) |
| **Distal contractile integral (DCI)** | Amplitude × duration × length (mmHg·s·cm) of distal contraction >20 mmHg, transition zone to proximal LES | Normal **450–8,000**; weak **≥100 and <450**; failed **<100**; hypercontractile **>8,000** (suitable across systems); ineffective = weak or failed |
| **Contractile wavefront integrity** | Contiguity of peristalsis at the 20-mmHg isobaric contour | Fragmented (ineffective) swallow: break **>5 cm** with DCI ≥450 |
| **Distal latency (DL)** | UES relaxation to contractile deceleration point (CDP) | Premature/spastic: **DL <4.5 s** with DCI ≥450 |
| **Pressurization** | Isobaric contour | Panesophageal **≥30 mmHg**; intrabolus **≥20 mmHg** supine (Medtronic); panesophageal >20 mmHg on RDC or solid test meal supports outflow obstruction |

- IRP thresholds are higher supine than upright; DCI and DL thresholds are the same in both positions.
- CDP: inflection between rapid proximal and slow distal phases, within 3 cm of the proximal pre-swallow EGJ high-pressure zone. If hard to find, draw a line 2–3 cm above the EGJ zone and measure to where it meets the contractile front (not the pressurization front).

### Supportive Manometric Measures (Table 2)

| Measure | Protocol | Normal response |
|---|---|---|
| **MRS** | 5 × 2 mL at 2–3 s intervals | No body contractility (DCI <100) with complete LES deglutitive inhibition during MRS, then post-MRS augmentation (post-MRS DCI > mean single-swallow DCI); must be true peristalsis, not artifact/pressurization |
| **RDC** | 200 mL rapid drink | No body contractility (DCI <100) with complete LES inhibition during RDC; no major motility disorder post-RDC. A normal post-RDC contraction is specific for normal contractility but absent in some healthy controls |
| **Solid test swallow** | 10 × ~1 cm³ soft solid (bread, soft boiled rice, marshmallow) | >20% of pharyngeal swallows followed by effective contraction (DCI >1,000) without a large (>5 cm) break; upper normal IRP with solids 25 mmHg (Medtronic) |
| **Solid test meal** | 200 g soft solid meal at normal rate; stop if not done in 8 min | As solid swallow; no symptoms; eating <200 g in 8 min also abnormal |
| **Post-prandial high-resolution impedance manometry** | Solid meal or symptom meal, ≥10 min monitoring | No symptoms or abnormal motility; ≤4 transient LES relaxations with belching in first 10 min; no volume regurgitation, rumination, or supragastric belching |
| **Amyl nitrite** | Inhaler, 4–5 sniffs, recumbent | Healthy: EGJ IRP similar to deglutitive IRP. Achalasia/true functional EGJOO: amyl nitrite drives EGJ pressure markedly below the impaired deglutitive IRP (≥10 mmHg, "relaxation gain"). EGJOO not due to LES dysfunction: little change (<10 mmHg) |
| **Cholecystokinin** | 40 ng/kg IV, recumbent | Healthy: biphasic response, mild shortening (≤2 cm) with incomplete relaxation. Inhibitory dysfunction (e.g., achalasia): paradoxical EGJ contraction >50 mmHg |

- Temporal association of ineffective contractions with symptoms (e.g., dysphagia) supports clinically relevant abnormal peristalsis.

### Classification and Definitions (Table 3, Figure 3)

| Class | Disorder | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| EGJ outflow | Type I [[achalasia]] | Abnormal median IRP **and** 100% failed peristalsis |
| | Type II achalasia | Abnormal median IRP, 100% failed peristalsis, **and ≥20%** swallows with panesophageal pressurization |
| | Type III achalasia | Abnormal median IRP **and ≥20%** swallows with premature/spastic contraction, **no evidence of peristalsis** |
| | [[esophagogastric-junction-outflow-obstruction\|EGJOO]]\* | Abnormal median IRP **supine and upright**, **≥20%** elevated intrabolus pressure (supine), not meeting achalasia criteria |
| Peristalsis | Absent contractility | Normal median IRP (supine and upright) and 100% failed peristalsis |
| | [[distal-esophageal-spasm\|DES]]\* | Normal median IRP and **≥20%** swallows with premature/spastic contraction |
| | [[hypercontractile-esophagus\|Hypercontractile esophagus]]\* | Normal median IRP and **≥20%** hypercontractile swallows |
| | [[ineffective-esophageal-motility\|IEM]] | Normal median IRP with **>70%** ineffective swallows **or ≥50%** failed peristalsis |

\* Manometric patterns of unclear clinical relevance — a clinically relevant conclusive diagnosis needs symptoms and/or supportive testing.

- Achalasia = 100% absent peristalsis, counting both failed and premature swallows; type III should have no normal or ineffective swallows. Type III vs conclusive EGJOO was vague in v3.0.
- EGJOO with peristalsis may be subtyped by the peristaltic pattern (spastic features = ≥20% premature swallows; hypercontractile features; ineffective motility; no disordered peristalsis).
- Disorders of peristalsis are considered only after EGJ outflow disorders are excluded; overlapping features → classify **DES first, then hypercontractile esophagus, then IEM**, noting overlap.
- Discordant findings between positions or on provocation → reconsider the classification; concordance strengthens it.

### Achalasia Statements

| Statement | Grade |
|---|---|
| **Type I:** conclusive = abnormal median IRP and absent contractility (100% failed peristalsis) | Very Low, Strong |
| **Type II:** conclusive = abnormal median IRP and absent contractility (100% failed peristalsis) with panesophageal pressurization in ≥20% of swallows | Very Low, Strong |
| **Type III:** conclusive = abnormal IRP and spasm (≥20% swallows with premature contraction) with no evidence of peristalsis | Very Low, Strong |
| **Inconclusive type I/II:** absent contractility, no appreciable peristalsis, IRP at upper limit of normal in both positions, with or without panesophageal pressurization in ≥20% | Strong (no GRADE level) |
| Appreciable peristalsis on changing position in a type I/II pattern shifts toward an inconclusive diagnosis needing supportive testing | Accepted Clinical Observation |
| **Inconclusive type III:** abnormal IRP with spasm and evidence of peristalsis; if strict EGJOO criteria met → EGJOO with spastic features (possible achalasia variant) | Not graded |
| Supportive TBE (preferably with tablet) and/or FLIP for inconclusive achalasia with dysphagia as a presenting symptom | Very Low, Strong |
| Opioids are associated with type III achalasia → study off opioids if possible (stop according to half-life) | Low, Conditional |

- Abnormal median IRP in **either** the primary supine or primary upright position (if 10 wet swallows done) suffices for achalasia — both positions are not required.
- 20% spasm cut-off is arbitrary; more premature swallows raise confidence in a type III variant.
- Did not reach agreement (guidance only): panesophageal pressurization cut-off is a guide, not a hard threshold; type I vs II distinction somewhat arbitrary beyond very low pressures (typically <15 mmHg) with moderate–severe dilatation; pressurization >70 mmHg may mean embedded spasm, possibly affecting outcome.

### EGJOO Statements

| Statement | Grade |
|---|---|
| A manometric diagnosis of EGJOO is always clinically inconclusive | Strong (no GRADE level) |
| **Manometric EGJOO** = elevated median IRP in the primary **and** secondary position **and** ≥20% swallows with elevated intrabolus pressure supine, with evidence of peristalsis | Low, Conditional |
| **Clinically relevant conclusive EGJOO** = manometric EGJOO + clinically relevant symptoms + ≥1 supportive test showing obstruction (TBE, preferably with tablet, and/or FLIP) | Moderate, Conditional |
| Clinically relevant EGJOO symptoms: dysphagia and/or non-cardiac chest pain | Low, Conditional |
| **Isolated** elevated supine IRP, isolated elevated upright IRP, or isolated elevated supine intrabolus pressure are **inconclusive** for manometric EGJOO | Low, Strong |
| Supportive (not required): outflow obstruction and esophageal pressurization on RDC | Very Low, Conditional |
| Supportive: outflow obstruction on solid test meal, especially if temporally linked to symptoms | Conditional (no GRADE level) |
| Supportive: abnormal EGJ function after pharmacologic provocation | Conditional (no GRADE level) |
| Describe EGJOO by peristaltic pattern: spastic features (type III features), hypercontractile features, ineffective motility, or no disordered peristalsis | Accepted Clinical Observation |

- After EGJOO entered the classification, nearly 10% of HRM studies showed the pattern; >1/3 may be clinically irrelevant (mechanical effects, opioids, artifact); some evolve toward or are variants of achalasia.

### Disorders of Peristalsis Statements

| Disorder | Statement | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| General | Fragmented peristalsis removed as a disorder; incorporated into IEM | Very Low, Strong |
| **Absent contractility** | Conclusive = normal median IRP supine **and** upright + 100% failed peristalsis (DCI <100) — criteria unchanged from v3.0 | Accepted Clinical Observation |
| **DES** | Clinically relevant = clinically relevant symptoms + conclusive manometric DES | Low, Conditional |
| | Conclusive manometric DES = **≥20% premature contractions** (DL <4.5 s, DCI ≥450) | Low, Strong |
| | Clinically relevant symptoms: dysphagia and non-cardiac chest pain | Accepted Clinical Observation |
| | **≥20%** contractions with DL <4.5 s but **DCI <450** → inconclusive for DES | Low, Conditional |
| | If the CDP is hard to identify, use alternative methods | Strong (no GRADE level) |
| **Hypercontractile esophagus** | Excess peristaltic vigor (may include excessive LES after-contraction), not associated with mechanical obstruction | Very Low, Conditional |
| | Clinically relevant = clinically relevant symptoms + conclusive manometric diagnosis | Very Low, Strong |
| | Conclusive manometric = **≥20% hypercontractile supine swallows** | Very Low, Conditional |
| | Clinically relevant symptoms: dysphagia and non-cardiac chest pain | Very Low, Strong |
| | Diagnose only when achalasia and DES criteria are not met and mechanical obstruction is carefully excluded | Very Low, Strong |
| **IEM** | Conclusive = **>70% ineffective swallows or ≥50% failed peristalsis** (ineffective = weak DCI ≥100 to <450, failed DCI <100, or fragmented) | Very Low, Strong |
| | **50–70%** ineffective swallows → inconclusive; supportive testing strengthens confidence | Very Low, Conditional |
| | Supportive: poor bolus transit on impedance or barium esophagram | Very Low, Conditional |
| | Supportive: lack of contraction reserve on MRS | Very Low, Conditional |

- **Absent contractility with borderline IRP** (supine median 10–15 mmHg, Medtronic) → consider type I achalasia; TBE (preferably tablet) and FLIP if dysphagia dominates.
- DES and hypercontractile esophagus are manometric patterns that do not always equal disease — clinically relevant only with context and supportive testing.
- Obstruction at the EGJ or distal esophagus can induce hypercontractility → exclude obstruction first.
- Hypercontractile subgroups: single-peaked hypercontractile swallows; **jackhammer** (repetitive prolonged contractions, especially post-peak — higher DCI, worse symptoms); hypercontractile with vigorous LES after-contraction.
- Hypercontractile esophagus: caution in treating contractile vigor as an endpoint; conservative medical therapy before endoscopic or surgical intervention.
- No major vs minor disorder distinction in v4.0.

### EGJ Metric Statements

| Statement | Grade |
|---|---|
| Measure the EGJ complex during quiet respiration in an artifact-free baseline segment; measure intragastric pressure below the crural diaphragm (CD) over 3 complete respiratory cycles, ideally in the same segment as EGJ contractile integral (EGJ-CI) | Strong (no GRADE level) |
| RIP = axial point where the inspiratory pressure change flips from an increase (intra-abdominal) to a decrease (intrathoracic) | Strong (no GRADE level) |
| EGJ-CI referenced to intragastric pressure, expressed in mmHg·cm | Strong (no GRADE level) |
| LES–CD separation scored as distance between the center of the CD and LES signal **during inspiration** (expiration if the LES is obscured) | Strong (no GRADE level) |
| Define the EGJ complex by LES–CD separation and RIP location | Low, Conditional |

- **EGJ-CI <25 mmHg·cm** as hypotensive EGJ was suggested but **did not reach agreement**.
- EGJ morphology, 3 subtypes: (1) CD superimposed on LES, RIP proximal to the complex; (2) LES–CD separation, RIP proximal to the CD; (3) LES–CD separation, RIP proximal to the LES. No consensus on naming (I/II/III vs A/C/B); both acceptable. No numeric separation cutoff is given.

### Future Directions (authors)

- Role of impedance topography for intrabolus pressure and bolus flow; reliability of solid swallows/meals; natural history and treatment outcome of EGJOO; spastic mechanisms and opioid overlap; whether jackhammer is a distinct subtype; manometric criteria for therapy selection (e.g., peroral endoscopic myotomy for spastic disorders; tailoring fundoplication to prevent dysphagia).

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Older guidance:** [[acg-2020-esophageal-physiologic-testing|ACG 2020 esophageal physiologic testing]] predates CCv4.0 and cites version 3.0; where criteria differ, CCv4.0 (newer, same tier) governs.
- **FLIP thresholds for "supportive" testing** are not defined here; [[dallas-2025-flip-panometry|Dallas 2025]] supplies them (reduced EGJ opening = EGJ distensibility index <2.0 mm²/mmHg and maximum diameter <12 mm).
- **After antireflux surgery** CCv4.0 does not apply; [[padova-2025-hrm-antireflux|Padova 2025]] notes post-fundoplication IRP runs higher (<15 mmHg Medtronic consistent with normal, higher may also be normal).
- **Non-agreed items:** EGJ-CI hypotensive cutoff; achalasia pressurization cut-off as a hard threshold; EGJ morphology naming.
