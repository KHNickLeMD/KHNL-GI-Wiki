## Summary

The GRADE (Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation) evidence document that underpins the AGA's 2017 therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) guideline. Where the guideline states the recommendations, this review supplies the **numbers behind them**: the trough-concentration performance data for each anti–tumor necrosis factor (TNF) agent, the antidrug-antibody (ADAb) literature, and the thiopurine metabolite thresholds and interpretation patterns.

Five focused clinical questions were finalised in PICO format and approved by the AGA Governing Board in December 2015 — two on anti-TNF agents (reactive TDM in active disease; routine proactive TDM in quiescent disease) and three on thiopurines (pre-treatment thiopurine methyltransferase [TPMT] measurement; reactive metabolite TDM; routine proactive metabolite TDM). **Every one of the five returned low- or very-low-quality evidence**, which is why the accompanying guideline's recommendations are conditional and why it makes no recommendation at all on proactive anti-TNF TDM.

The review's conceptual contribution is the **failure taxonomy** that TDM is meant to assign: non-immune-mediated pharmacokinetic failure (subtherapeutic trough, no ADAb — rapid clearance from high inflammatory burden, faecal loss, low albumin), immune-mediated pharmacokinetic failure (low trough with ADAb), and mechanistic failure (adequate trough — the disease is not driven by the targeted cytokine). Table 2 turns this into a 2 × 2 grid with the proposed action and the observed share of patients in each cell.

For thiopurines, the review supplies the metabolite interpretation framework the guideline does not print, including the **6-methylmercaptopurine (6-MMP) "shunter"** phenotype defined by a **6-MMP/6-thioguanine (6-TGN) ratio >11**, which is the pattern that predicts hepatotoxicity on dose escalation and calls for a different strategy rather than more drug.

Throughout, the review is candid that **thresholds are a spectrum, not absolutes** — they shift with treatment target (clinical vs endoscopic remission), disease activity, disease phenotype, and phase of therapy (induction vs maintenance).

## Scope and What It Deliberately Excludes

- **Anti-TNF agents and thiopurines only.** The panel, with AGA Governing Board approval, **opted not to synthesise evidence on TDM for methotrexate, cyclosporine or tacrolimus** because of limited data.
- **Non–anti-TNF biologics are not discussed** — the mechanism of [[vedolizumab]] and [[il-23-and-il-12-23-inhibitors|ustekinumab]] is distinct from anti-TNF and published TDM data were very limited at the time.
- **No distinction was made between monotherapy and combination therapy** when reviewing TDM for biologics.
- **Induction is largely unaddressed.** The threshold data are maintenance-phase; applying maintenance thresholds during induction risks misclassifying patients as mechanistic failures.

## Key Messages by Clinical Question

*The review states a "Key message" with an evidence grade for each question rather than numbered recommendations; the numbering below is the wiki's.*

| # | Question | Key message | Quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Active IBD on an anti-TNF** — is **reactive** TDM superior to empiric dose escalation or switching? | There **may be a benefit** of reactive TDM to guide treatment changes over empirically escalating the dose or switching therapy | **Very low** |
| 2 | **Quiescent IBD on an anti-TNF** — is **routine proactive** TDM superior to no monitoring? | The benefit of routine proactive TDM over no monitoring is **uncertain** | **Very low** |
| 3 | **Starting a thiopurine** — is routine **TPMT** measurement superior to empiric weight-based dosing? | The benefit over no TPMT testing is **uncertain for most patients**, but testing **may avoid serious harm in a small fraction** | **Low** |
| 4 | **Active IBD on a thiopurine, or suspected thiopurine toxicity** — is **reactive** metabolite TDM superior to empiric treatment change? | There **may be a benefit** of reactive TDM to guide treatment changes | **Very low** |
| 5 | **IBD on standard weight-based thiopurine** — is **routine proactive** metabolite TDM superior to weight-based dosing? | The benefit over empiric weight-based dosing is **uncertain** | **Very low** |

Supporting detail that explains the grades:

- **Q1** rests on one small Danish randomised controlled trial (RCT) in Crohn's disease (69 patients) at high risk of bias with poor protocol adherence, plus three observational studies. In the RCT, clinical remission at 12 weeks was 30.3% with TDM-guided change vs 38.9% with empiric escalation (relative risk [RR] 0.78; 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.40–1.51) — but **accumulated 12-month costs were 34% lower** in the TDM arm (€6,038 vs €9,178, *P* < .001). Observational data show the benefit is **concentrated in the right stratum**: 82% of patients with non-immune-mediated pharmacokinetic failure responded to dose escalation, vs only 8% of those with immune-mediated failure (RR 0.26; 0.08–0.86).
- **Q2** rests on indirect evidence from the **TAXIT** trial only. All 263 patients were first trough-optimised to 3–7 µg/mL; after that, continued proactive TDM gave remission at 1 year in 69% vs 66% with clinically guided dosing (RR 1.04; 0.88–1.24). **The optimisation phase itself worked** (Crohn's remission 65% → 88% in the subgroup with initially low troughs) and **cut drug costs 28%** in those above 7 µg/mL; the randomised phase shows only that *repeating* it before every infusion adds nothing at 1 year. The trial cannot inform one-time optimisation, infrequent optimisation, or early optimisation in patients at high clearance risk.
- **Q3** rests on 3 RCTs (1,145 patients). TPMT testing changed nothing at population level — hematologic adverse events RR 0.94 (0.59–1.50), treatment discontinuation RR 1.09 (0.94–1.27) — because **homozygotes are rare: 0.17% (2 of 1,145)**, with 9.7% heterozygous and 90.1% wild-type. A post-hoc subgroup analysis found an **89% risk reduction in hematologic adverse events** (RR 0.11; 0.01–0.85) in heterozygous carriers, and the authors calculated that a prospective trial would need **more than 42,000 participants** to show benefit for the whole patient population.
- **Q4** rests on a **single retrospective single-group study** (63 patients) comparing algorithm-concordant with algorithm-discordant decisions: clinical response in 36 of 42 (85.7%) vs 3 of 18 (16.7%), RR 5.15 (1.82–14.56).
- **Q5** rests on 2 small RCTs (107 patients), **both terminated early** for slow recruitment with 33–46% attrition. Clinical remission 42% with TDM-guided dose adaptation vs 31.6% with weight-based dosing (RR 1.44; 0.59–3.52). Notably, **the intervention largely failed on its own terms**: in the US trial, despite significantly higher azathioprine doses, median 6-TGN was 197 vs 150 pmol/8 × 10⁸ red blood cells (RBCs) — no significant difference — because the extra dose was shunted to 6-MMP. The trials capped azathioprine at 3 mg/kg and **did not permit adding allopurinol**.

## Anti-TNF Trough Concentration Thresholds

Maintenance-phase thresholds, derived from cross-sectional and cohort data (no RCT compared target thresholds). The summary position: **infliximab ≥5 µg/mL, adalimumab ≥7.5 µg/mL, certolizumab pegol ≥20 µg/mL** identify most patients unlikely to respond to further escalation.

**Table 4 — infliximab** (6 studies; proportion *not* in remission at or above each cutoff; *P* for trend < .001):

| Infliximab cutoff (µg/mL) | Patients (n) | In remission above cutoff | **Not** in remission above cutoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 929 | 49.2% | **24.8%** |
| 3 | 929 | 36.7% | **14.7%** |
| 5 | 929 | 27.3% | **7.9%** |
| 7 | 726 | 21.4% | **3.9%** |
| 10 | 772 | 17.2% | **4.3%** |

- Above **≥7** and **≥10 µg/mL** the proportion not in remission is no longer significantly different — the curve flattens, which is why ≥7 offers only marginal extra benefit for clinical remission.
- **Ulcerative colitis may need more than Crohn's disease:** at infliximab troughs **≥3 µg/mL**, significantly more patients with UC than CD were still not in remission.
- Thresholds rise with an endoscopic rather than clinical target: Adedokun et al. proposed **22.0 µg/mL at week 6** for week-8 clinical response, vs 5.1 and 3.7 µg/mL for response at weeks 14 and 30.

**Table 5 — adalimumab** (proportion not in remission): **17.0%** above 5.0 ± 1 µg/mL (4 studies, 232 patients) → **10.3%** above 7.5 ± 1 µg/mL (3 studies, 131 patients).

**Table 6 — certolizumab pegol** (pooled individual participant data, 9 trials, 2,157 patients with Crohn's disease; proportion not in remission): **41.6%** above 10 µg/mL → **32.2%** above 15 → **25.5%** above 20.

**Golimumab** — data are sparse; no table. Post-hoc PURSUIT analysis identified **2.5 µg/mL at week 6** (induction; sensitivity 59.0%, specificity 67.2%) and **1.4 µg/mL at week 44** (maintenance; sensitivity 54.1%, specificity 77.6%) as minimal thresholds for clinically important outcomes. A prospective observational study of 21 patients with UC found **2.6 µg/mL at week 6** predicted week-14 improvement (specificity 90%, sensitivity 56%, AUROC 0.79).

⚠ **Assay caveat:** infliximab assays agree well (intra-class correlation 0.960–0.971 across five assays; recovery −7% to +20%). **Adalimumab does not** — a homogeneous mobility shift assay measured consistently lower concentrations than enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), producing a **2-fold difference in the adalimumab cutoff** correlating with remission. Data comparing certolizumab pegol assays are lacking.

## Antidrug Antibody Thresholds

**Quantitative ADAb results cannot be compared between assays** — there is no international standard, and assays report in µg/mL, µg/mL equivalents, U/mL and arbitrary units. Interpret them in clinical context, with the drug concentration.

- **Order of interpretation:** evaluate the **drug concentration first**. With a therapeutic trough, ADAbs are generally undetectable or low-concentration and have limited pharmacokinetic effect; detecting both drug and ADAb in the same sample occurred in only **7.3%** of samples.
- Proposed thresholds, each assay-specific: ADAb to infliximab **>8 µg/mL** was "high" and correlated with shorter response duration and higher infusion-reaction risk (RR 2.40; 1.65–3.66); **>9.1 U/mL** predicted unsuccessful intervention (likelihood ratio 3.6; specificity 82%, sensitivity 65%); **>9 µg/mL equivalents** (infliximab) and **>4 µg/mL** (adalimumab) identified patients who failed empiric dose escalation with 90% specificity.
- **Transient vs sustained ADAbs matter more than the titre.** Of patients losing response, **69% with transient ADAbs recaptured response to empiric infliximab dose escalation vs 16% with sustained ADAbs** (*P* = .0028); only 13% with transient ADAbs had to discontinue, vs 68% with sustained (RR 5.1; 1.4–19.0).
- **ADAb-negative but subtherapeutic** means non-immune clearance — high inflammatory burden, excessive faecal loss (elevated C-reactive protein, faecal calprotectin, low albumin), not immunogenicity.

## Thiopurine Metabolite Patterns

**Metabolism.** Azathioprine is a prodrug converted to 6-mercaptopurine (6-MP), which is modified into the **active 6-TGN** and the **inactive 6-MMP / 6-methylmercaptopurine ribonucleotides**. Immunosuppression comes from incorporating 6-TGN into nucleic acids in place of guanine nucleotides. **TPMT** is one of the key enzymes; low or absent activity pushes exposure toward 6-TGN and severe bone-marrow toxicity.

- **TPMT genetics:** controlled by >25 polymorphisms, but 4 alleles cover 80–95% of those tested. Roughly **4%–11% are heterozygous** (intermediate activity) and **~0.3% homozygous** (very low or absent). Myelosuppression odds vs wild-type: **4.3 (2.7–6.9)** heterozygous, **20.8 (3.4–126.9)** homozygous. **Most leukopenia occurs within 8 weeks** of starting, but it can appear many months in.
- **TPMT-guided starting doses used in the supporting trials:** wild-type / normal activity **azathioprine 2–2.5 mg/kg/day or 6-MP 1–1.5 mg/kg/day** · heterozygous / intermediate **50% dose reduction** · homozygous / low-absent **avoid, or 0–10% of standard dose**.
- ⚠ **TPMT testing does not replace blood-count monitoring.** A significant proportion of thiopurine-related hematologic events occur **despite normal TPMT status**. The azathioprine package insert directs complete blood count **weekly for the first month, twice monthly for months 2 and 3, then monthly** (or more often with dose changes). Real-world adherence is poor — median 3 counts in the first 8 weeks and 1 between weeks 9 and 26.
- **TPMT activity is unreliable after transfusion** — allow a 3-month interval before phenotyping.

**The five metabolite patterns** (from the reactive-TDM algorithm of the supporting study, 63 patients):

| Pattern | 6-TGN (pmol/8 × 10⁸ RBCs) | 6-MMP | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Underdosed** | **<230** | — | Escalate the thiopurine — 13 of 15 (86.7%) responded to algorithm-concordant escalation |
| **Noncompliant** | absent / very low | absent / very low | Both metabolites low together; addressing adherence, not dose |
| **Therapeutically refractory** | **230–450** (therapeutic) | normal or high | Adequate active drug, no response → switch therapy; **12 of 12 (100%) improved** when the change was algorithm-concordant |
| **Overdosed** | **>450** | — | Risk of leukopenia; reduce |
| **Shunter (preferential 6-MMP producer)** | **low** | **high** | **6-MMP/6-TGN ratio >11.** Escalating the dose raises 6-MMP, not 6-TGN → **hepatotoxicity risk**. Needs a different strategy (e.g. adding allopurinol), not more thiopurine — 4 of 4 (100%) responded to an algorithm-concordant change, whereas dose reduction alone worked in 0 of 2 |

- ⚠ **This is why "just increase the dose" fails in a subset.** High 6-MMP is associated with **hepatotoxicity**, and shunters are the group susceptible to it, because dose escalation leads to 6-MMP accumulation.
- **The 6-TGN >230 threshold performs only moderately.** In a meta-analysis of 17 studies (2,052 patients), sensitivity **63%** and specificity **53%** for identifying remission (AUROC 0.63). Patients above 230 were **1.4 times** as likely to be in remission (RR 1.36; 1.16–1.61). But **34% of patients with active disease were above the threshold and 50% of patients in remission were below it.**
- **In combination with an anti-TNF the target is different and unsettled.** A cross-sectional study found **6-TGN >125 pmol/8 × 10⁸ RBCs** associated with high anti-TNF concentrations and mucosal healing; another found no correlation between 6-TGN and anti-TNF levels at all. When a thiopurine is used mainly to reduce immunogenicity rather than to drive remission, the monotherapy target does not apply.
- **Noncompliance ran 6%–11%** in the retrospective reactive-TDM studies.

## Key Findings / Claims

- **All five questions returned low or very-low-quality evidence.** Every TDM recommendation in the companion guideline is built on indirect, imprecise or high-risk-of-bias data.
- **Maintenance trough thresholds:** infliximab **≥5 µg/mL**, adalimumab **≥7.5 µg/mL**, certolizumab pegol **≥20 µg/mL**; golimumab **2.5 µg/mL (week 6)** and **1.4 µg/mL (week 44)**, from sparse data.
- **Thresholds are a spectrum, not absolutes** — they move with treatment target, disease activity, phenotype (UC may need more than CD; perianal disease more again) and phase of therapy.
- **The reactive-TDM grid assigns the failure mechanism** and is where the benefit sits: 82% of non-immune pharmacokinetic failures respond to escalation vs 8% of immune-mediated failures.
- **ADAb titres are not comparable across assays**; transient vs sustained antibodies predict recapture better than the number does.
- **TPMT testing protects a very small group** (~0.3% homozygous) and **never replaces blood-count monitoring**.
- **The 6-MMP/6-TGN ratio >11 defines the shunter** — the pattern in which dose escalation produces hepatotoxicity rather than efficacy.
- **The 6-TGN >230 threshold is only moderately discriminating** (sensitivity 63%, specificity 53%).
- **Proactive TDM's evidence gap is specific:** TAXIT shows that *repeating* optimisation at every infusion adds nothing after initial optimisation — it does not show that one-time or early optimisation is useless.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **No contradiction with [[aga-2017-tdm-ibd]]** — this is that guideline's own evidence document, and the two were developed together. Where the guideline states a recommendation, this review states the key message and grade behind it.
- **The 6-TGN >230 pmol/8 × 10⁸ RBCs target is widely cited but poorly performing** — the review says so explicitly (sensitivity 63%, specificity 53%), and notes it may differ between Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis, and between monotherapy and combination therapy. No better threshold is offered.
- **The optimal 6-TGN target in combination with an anti-TNF is unresolved** — one study supports >125, another found no correlation between 6-TGN and anti-TNF concentration at all.
- **Whether proactive TDM helps at induction, or as a one-time optimisation, is unanswered** — TAXIT's design cannot address it, and the review flags this as the specific gap.
- **Table 2's interventions are "proposed but not yet prospectively validated"** — the review labels the grid as such in its own title.
- ⚠ **Supplementary Tables 1–5 are not in the ingested file**, so the per-study assay characteristics and the full thiopurine algorithm as originally printed are not available; the algorithm above is reconstructed from the review's own running text, which states each stratum and its cut-points.
