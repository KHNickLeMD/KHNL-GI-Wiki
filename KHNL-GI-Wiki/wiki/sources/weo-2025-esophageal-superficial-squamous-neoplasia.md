## Summary
- WEO consensus on endoscopy across the pathway of **superficial ESCC (SESCC) and squamous dysplasia**: who to screen, how to detect, how to characterize (optical diagnosis, biopsy site, lateral extent, invasion depth), staging, endoscopic resection (ER) and ablation, management after noncurative resection, and surveillance. Framed for worldwide use, tailored to regional ESCC burden (incidence from 125/100,000 per year in northern China to 1–1.5/100,000 in low-incidence countries such as the United States, p. 471).
- **23 numbered statements** (Statement 1–23). By Table 1: **16 Strong, 6 Weak, 1 No recommendation** (Statement 4).
- **Core positions:**
  - Screening: population-based screening may be considered in **high-incidence countries** (weak); endoscopy (oral or transnasal) is the standard; add **advanced endoscopic imaging (AEI) or Lugol chromoendoscopy (LCE)** to white-light endoscopy (WLE) in patients at increased risk; screen the **oral cavity, oropharynx and hypopharynx** for second primary tumors, with AEI examination and photodocumentation of the hypopharynx.
  - Characterization: magnifying classifications (Inoue, Arima, Japan Esophageal Society [JES]); AEI with magnification to pick the biopsy site; LCE or magnifying AEI for lateral extent; magnifying AEI or high-frequency endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) for invasion depth.
  - Staging: tumor–node–metastasis (TNM) staging before treatment; computed tomography (CT) first, fluorine-18 fluoro-2-deoxy-D-glucose positron emission tomography (FDG-PET)/CT in potential surgical candidates, EUS for clinically deep submucosal (SM) invasion.
  - Resection: endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR) only when **en bloc** is achievable; endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) **first-line for clinical T1a epithelium/lamina propria (EP/LPM)** ESCC, **relative indication for T1a muscularis mucosae (MM) / T1b-SM1**; ESD for lesions **>15 mm** or not amenable to EMR. Ablation only for flat-type intraepithelial neoplasia or as salvage.
  - After ER: **pT1 SM, or pT1a-MM with vascular invasion → salvage** (chemoradiation or surgery); pathological mucosal cancer with high-risk features → individualize; isolated lateral margin involvement → repeat endoscopic treatment or close surveillance; stop alcohol and smoking; **annual** AEI surveillance endoscopy (every 6 months suggested with multiple Lugol-voiding lesions [LVL]).
- No algorithm or flowchart in the document.

**Supporting points (condensed):**
- **High-incidence regions** (Statement 1, p. 472): Eastern–Central Asia (China nationwide age-standardized incidence rate [ASR] 25/100,000; Iran ASR 10/100,000), South-Eastern Africa (Kenya, Tanzania, Malawi, South Africa), parts of South America (Brazil, Uruguay). 5-year survival after ESCC diagnosis **<20%**; precursor lesions are asymptomatic, dysphagia usually means advanced disease.
- **Screening evidence** (p. 472–474; cohort / case–control, no controlled trials): Hua County chromoendoscopy cohort — incidence −47%, mortality −66%; Linzhou case–control — mortality risk −47%; Taihang one-time screening — cumulative mortality 3.3% vs 5.5% over 10 years.
- **Cost-effectiveness** (p. 474): in the United States (low prevalence), ESCC prevalence would need to rise by **1948%** for an incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) below USD 50,000 per quality-adjusted life year (QALY); a Lugol screening program for high-risk populations in China — ICER USD 1027/QALY (cost-effective).
- **Risk factors for ESCC and dysplasia** (p. 471, 474): ≥3 alcoholic units/day (three- to fivefold risk, synergistic with smoking); age **>60 years**, male sex, low socioeconomic status, smoking, heavy alcohol, hot beverages, low fruit/vegetable intake, polycyclic hydrocarbon and nitrosamine exposure, family history, poor oral hygiene. **Nonmodifiable:** prior head and neck (H&N) squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), achalasia, radiotherapy, caustic injury, tylosis. Average-risk (non-high-incidence) populations: interim cluster-RCT data show a relatively low yield.
- **Examination technique** (Statement 2, pp. 474–475):
  - Wash and aspirate saliva and mucus for a clear view.
  - On high-resolution WLE, superficial neoplasia shows **slight reddening** and surface irregularity (depression or granular elevation); mucus or fine slough on the surface is a clue.
  - Protruded **0-I** (rough surface) and ulcerated **0-III** lesions are easy to see but most likely deep submucosal and usually **unsuitable for ER**; flat **0-IIb** lesions are subtle (vaguely erythematous, discrete vascular changes) and easily missed.
  - Mixed whitish nodular lesions: inspect the surrounding flat reddened component so extent is not underestimated.
  - Transnasal endoscopy is an alternative, particularly after H&N cancer with altered postsurgical anatomy.
- **Biopsy** (Statement 3 discussion, p. 475): biopsy areas suspicious for ESCC or dysplasia, but only **one to two carefully targeted biopsies** — more causes inflammation/scarring that makes later ER harder. In expert centers, lesions may be resected directly. Expert pathologist interpretation should be considered.
- **Detection data** (p. 475): esophageal cancer miss rate at upper endoscopy **6.4%** (95% confidence interval [CI] 4.4–9.3%); higher with endoscopists of **<5 years'** experience and with proton pump inhibitor therapy. Narrow-band imaging (NBI) with magnifying endoscopy (ME) detected SESCC more often than WLE (97% vs 55%, RCT). Meta-analysis (12 studies, 1911 patients): AEI vs LCE sensitivity **88% vs 92%**, specificity **88% vs 82%**. Dual-focus NBI vs high-definition WLE sensitivity 91% vs 52%.
- **Intrapapillary capillary loops (IPCLs)** change with invasion depth (accuracy 78.6–90%); the JES NBI-based IPCL classification is widely used because it is easy to understand (p. 475).
- **Nonendoscopic tests** (Statement 4, p. 475): peptide panels, plasma cell-free DNA (cfDNA) mutation panels (78% sensitivity, 100% specificity in one study), sponge cytology (feasible in Iran and Tanzania). No technique yet has accuracy suitable for population use; restricted to clinical studies.
- **H&N second primaries** (Statements 5–6, p. 476): pooled prevalence of H&N second primary tumors in ESCC **6.7%**; site — hypopharynx 60%, oropharynx 18%, oral cavity 11%, larynx 9%, other 2%; 85% diagnosed at stage 0–II. Hypopharynx = 72–87% of early oro-/hypopharyngeal cancers; lesions in pyriform sinuses 78.3%, posterior pharyngeal wall 14%, postcricoid area 8%. NBI markedly increases H&N cancer detection.
  - **Hypopharynx examination:** inspect posterior pharyngeal wall, both pyriform sinuses and larynx; inspiration abducts the vocal cords. Needs dedicated training; when an optimal examination is not feasible, consider otorhinolaryngology referral.
- **AI** (Statement 7, p. 476): meta-analysis of computer-aided detection — sensitivity 93% (CI 73–99%), specificity 89% (CI 77–95%). Multicenter tandem double-blind Chinese RCT (>12,000 sedated esophagogastroduodenoscopies [EGDs]): over 4-fold reduction in per-patient lesion miss rate (1.7% vs 6.7%) and over 2-fold per lesion (1.9% vs 5.1%); no adverse events.

**Supporting points (condensed):**
- **Training** (Statement 8, p. 477): standardized courses are rare and variable; the only validated classification is JES. Use an e-learning library with many images; onsite training at a high-volume ESCC center when available. The discussion states the proficiency threshold as **>80% accuracy** in characterizing 20 neoplastic lesions in high-risk patients (the statement says "at least 80%"). Low prevalence limits in vivo practice → e-libraries with feedback to maintain competence.
- **Classifications** (Statement 9, p. 477): ME diagnosis rests on IPCL morphology; AEI modalities named — NBI, FICE, BLI, i-SCAN. Inoue and Arima were built for detection and depth; JES integrates them into one simplified system.
  - Inoue vs Arima (one trial): sensitivity for invasion deeper than the muscularis mucosae 78.3% vs 50.0% (P = 0.144); specificity 61.9% vs 97.6% (P = 0.043); overall accuracy 70.5% vs 72.8%; interobserver κ 0.81 vs 0.66.
  - Meta-analysis, EP/LPM staging accuracy: Inoue 87.2%, Arima 98.7%, JES 86.7%. MM/SM1: JES higher than Inoue (75.5% vs 58.7%, P < 0.05). Arima most accurate for EP/LPM and SM2 or deeper (P < 0.01) but most complex (includes avascular area) and less reproducible than JES. JES is now the most used and the one applied in AI.
  - The criteria of the Inoue, Arima and JES classifications (IPCL types and their depth correlates) are not printed in this document.
- **Biopsy site** (Statement 10, pp. 477–478): preoperative biopsy histology often differs from the resected specimen; more biopsies raise bleeding risk, cost, time and make resection harder. NBI-ME more accurate than biopsy histology in one comparison (91.0% vs 85.6%). ME-AEI delineates lesions about as well as LCE and predicts depth, but interpretation needs experience (sensitivity higher in experienced endoscopists).
- **Post-ER surveillance imaging** (Statement 11, p. 478): ESCC is often multiple (synchronous/metachronous) in the upper aerodigestive tract; annual metachronous cancer incidence after ER 5.2% and 9.0% in two studies. WLE missed **at least 45%** of ESCC (including high-grade dysplasia) in a high-risk screening setting. LCE = historical gold standard for detection but **low specificity** (inflammation also shows as Lugol-voiding, i.e. unstained, areas). AEI shows dysplasia/ESCC as **brownish areas** without staining; NBI-ME sensitivity 85–100%, comparable with LCE, with higher specificity; no adverse effects reported. **LCE cannot be used in the laryngopharynx** (high aspiration risk).
- **Lateral extent** (Statement 12, pp. 478–479): LCE has been the gold standard immediately before ER. One retrospective comparison: complete lateral resection rate for invasive carcinoma LCE 90% vs NBI 94% (P = 0.715); for dysplasia 65% vs 67% (P = 0.813). Panel suggests **AEI for trained expert endoscopists, LCE for less experienced endoscopists**.
- **Invasion depth** (Statement 13, p. 479): depth correlates with lymph node metastasis (LNM) risk and curability by ER; also weigh size, location and performance status.
  - ME: high sensitivity, very low negative likelihood ratio → reliable for **confirming deep invasion**; EUS: high specificity, very high positive likelihood ratio → reliable for **confirming the cancer is limited to the surface** (m1/m2).
  - NBI-ME: sensitivity 0.90, specificity 0.89 (positive likelihood ratio 5.42, negative 0.23). High-frequency EUS: sensitivity 0.83, specificity 0.88 (positive 7.30, negative 0.19). Combined NBI-ME + EUS increased accuracy in a later meta-analysis.
  - Caveats: one study found EUS **may overstage** depth; another found EUS added no accuracy to ME + AEI in T1 ESCC.

| # | Statement (pp. 479–480) | Evidence / strength | Vote (%) A+ · A · U · D · D+ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14 | ESCC should be staged according to the **TNM system** before treatment planning | High / Strong | 75 · 21.4 · 3.6 · 0 · 0 |
| 15 | Locoregional clinical staging is primarily by **CT**. **FDG-PET/CT should be considered in potential surgical candidates** to identify distant metastasis undetected on CT. **EUS is integral** in staging patients with **clinically deep SM invasive** SCC | Moderate / Strong | 39.2 · 57.1 · 0 · 3.6 · 0 |

**Supporting points (condensed):**
- **Stage reporting** (Statement 14, p. 479): clinical stage (cTNM, from endoscopy and imaging) guides treatment; pathologic stage (pTNM, from the resection specimen) gives prognosis; 8th edition adds post-neoadjuvant pathologic stage (ypTNM). Tumors involving the esophagogastric junction (EGJ) with epicenter **no more than 2 cm** into the proximal stomach are staged as esophageal; epicenter **>2 cm** into the stomach → staged as gastric even if the EGJ is involved.
- **Depth substaging** (p. 479): mucosal ESCC (pT1a) — **m1** epithelium, **m2** lamina propria, **m3** muscularis mucosae. Submucosal ESCC (pT1b) — inner (**sm1**), middle (**sm2**) and outer (**sm3**) thirds; micrometer cutoffs in Table 2. Staging and treatment choice under a multidisciplinary team.

**Table 2 — Risk of LNM by depth of invasion of ESCC, based on surgical specimens (p. 480)**

| Depth of invasion | Risk of LNM |
|---|---|
| Intraepithelial (m1) | 0% |
| Lamina propria (m2) | 0% |
| Muscularis mucosae (m3) | 8–18% |
| Submucosa, sm1 (**<200 µm**) | 8–33% |
| Submucosa, sm2 (**200–500 µm**) | 17–35% |
| Submucosa, sm3 (**>500 µm**) | 21–48% |

- **Imaging performance** (Statement 15, p. 480):
  - CT of chest and abdomen with oral and intravenous contrast = initial staging test. CT and FDG-PET/CT are limited for depth (cT) and insensitive for locoregional nodes (pooled sensitivity **50%** and **57%**); EUS more sensitive for cN. FDG-PET/CT more sensitive than CT for cM — adding it in potential esophagectomy candidates can find occult metastasis and prevent unnecessary surgery.
  - EUS more accurate than CT for T and N staging, better for advanced than early T stage. T1: sensitivity 77% (95% CI 73–80), specificity 95% (94–96). T4: sensitivity 84% (79–89), specificity 96% (95–97). Nodes overall: sensitivity 81% (79–82), specificity 76% (73–78).
  - **EUS-guided fine-needle aspiration (FNA)** of non-peritumoral lymph nodes is recommended **if the result may alter management** (more accurate N staging than EUS alone).

**Supporting points (condensed):**
- **Pre-resection assessment** (Statements 16–17, pp. 480–481): lateral extent by LCE and/or image-enhanced magnifying endoscopy; invasion depth by non-magnifying endoscopy, ME with AEI and/or EUS. **En bloc resection is essential for curability** — piecemeal raises local recurrence; en bloc specimens allow accurate histology.
  - EP/LPM → ESD first-line. Clinically suspected MM/SM1 → relative indication (limited evidence), but **ESD should be considered as primary treatment** to aim for en bloc resection and accurate pathologic staging.
  - EMR vs ESD meta-analysis: ESD superior efficacy, similar safety; size steers the choice — **EMR for lesions ≤10 mm, ESD preferable for >20 mm, either for 10–20 mm**. (Statement 17 itself uses **>15 mm** for ESD.)
- **Ablation** (Statement 18, p. 481): argon plasma coagulation, photodynamic therapy, radiofrequency ablation (RFA), cryoablation — good short-term results, fewer adverse events and shorter learning curve than ESD; also salvage when ER or esophagectomy is not feasible, or for residual/recurrent cancer after esophagectomy, chemoradiation or ER.
  - Long-term RFA follow-up: **15–20% recurrence**; restrict RFA to **flat-type (0-IIb)** precancerous lesions or lesions **without a pink-color sign on LCE**.
  - **30%** histologically upstaged vs pretreatment assessment; incomplete eradication, delayed therapy, progression or buried cancer possible. Core problem: **no specimen to judge curability**.
- **After noncurative ER** (Statements 19–20, pp. 481–482):
  - LNM in surgically treated **pSM1** 8.3–53.1%, **pSM2** 18.5–30.0%; also high in **T1MM with vascular invasion** → ER generally **noncurative** for pT1sm.
  - No RCTs comparing surveillance, chemoradiation or surgery after noncurative ER of pT1sm; small retrospective series suggest more metastasis with surveillance alone. JCOG 0502 (Japanese multicenter prospective study, stage I ESCC): 5-year overall survival esophagectomy 86.5% vs chemoradiation 85.5%. Retrospective cohorts after noncurative ER: no survival difference surgery vs chemoradiation, but recurrence seemed commoner after chemoradiation. Single-arm confirmatory study (pT1a-MM with lymphovascular invasion n = 15; pT1b after noncurative ER n = 72) with chemoradiation: 3-year overall survival 90.7%.
  - Surgery vs chemoradiation → patient's functional status, comorbidities and preference.
  - **pT1MM:** LNM 0–26.7% in surgical specimens, considerably lower without vascular invasion; after ER of T1MM **without** vascular invasion, metastasis during observation 0–16.7% (small studies) → no definite conclusion on added surgery or chemoradiation; balance against the risks of esophagectomy or chemoradiation.
- **Lateral margin** (Statement 21, p. 482): risk factors for horizontal margin involvement — **large size, cervical esophagus**. For lesions involving a large share of the circumference, some recommend ER with a minimal horizontal margin to reduce **stricture** risk, with acceptable survival. Local recurrence with lateral margin involvement **26.7%** (median follow-up 22 months). **Visible residual lesion → additional endoscopic treatment (resection or ablation); no visible residual → close surveillance.**
- **Alcohol and smoking** (Statement 22, p. 482): abstinence significantly reduced cumulative incidence of metachronous esophageal cancer; in 29,795 smokers with a first cancer, quitting reduced all second cancers and metachronous esophageal cancers.
- **Surveillance** (Statement 23, pp. 482–483):
  - Metachronous esophageal cancer after ER: **2.2–9.0% per year**. **Multiple LVL** = strongest predictor of metachronous multiple ESCC (2-year cumulative incidence **24.7%**).
  - Most studies surveyed every **6–12 months**; most metachronous lesions were treatable endoscopically → endoscopy **at least once a year**; **every 6 months suggested for patients with multiple LVL**.
  - Other organs: standardized incidence ratios of H&N cancer 6.7–20.1; cumulative H&N cancer incidence 3.2–12.1%; 5-year cumulative gastric cancer detection 4.1% after ER for ESCC → surveillance endoscopy also looks for gastric and H&N cancer. NBI more accurate than WLE in the H&N and esophagus (multicenter RCT) → **AEI recommended for synchronous or metachronous cancers in the H&N and esophagus after ER**.
- The document does not define "multiple" LVL (number or grade), does not give a regimen for stricture prevention after circumferential resection, and does not list the "high-risk features" of Statement 20 beyond vascular invasion.

## Relevance to Wiki
Non-US consensus: under the wiki's source rules it **fills gaps** that the US guidelines on file leave, and never restates or overrides a decision a US guideline already makes (resection technique by size, curability, post-ESD surveillance intervals).
- [[esophageal-cancer]] — gaps it fills for squamous disease: who is at increased risk (risk factors; high-incidence regions); WLE appearance of superficial squamous neoplasia (Paris 0-I/0-III usually deep, 0-IIb subtle); **one to two targeted biopsies only** before planned resection; AEI or LCE added to WLE in at-risk patients (Statement 3); screening for H&N second primaries with hypopharynx examination (Statements 5–6); TNM staging, FDG-PET/CT in surgical candidates, EUS for clinically deep SM invasion, EUS-FNA when nodes would change management (Statements 14–15); Table 2 depth-specific LNM ranges (a second series beside the AGA 2019 figures); alcohol and smoking abstinence after resection (Statement 22); multiple LVL as the strongest predictor of metachronous ESCC.

- [[endoscopic-mucosal-resection]] — esophageal squamous EMR limited to lesions that can be removed en bloc (Statement 16).
- [[radiofrequency-ablation]] — squamous ablation restricted to flat-type (0-IIb) intraepithelial neoplasia or salvage; 15–20% long-term recurrence; 30% upstaging (Statement 18).
- [[upper-endoscopy]] — mucosal cleaning before inspection; hypopharynx examination and photodocumentation with AEI in patients with or at risk of ESCC (Statement 6); esophageal cancer miss rate 6.4%.
- [[endoscopic-ultrasound]] — high-frequency EUS for depth (confirms surface-limited disease; may overstage), EUS nodal accuracy, EUS-FNA indication (Statements 13, 15).
- [[artificial-intelligence-endoscopy]] — weak suggestion for computer-aided detection of ESCC pending RCT confirmation; first in vivo multicenter tandem RCT result (Statement 7).

## Contradictions / Open Questions
**Against US source pages (US guidelines govern the wiki where they address the same decision):**
- **Curability of pT1a-MM without vascular invasion.** WEO sends pT1SM and **pT1MM with vascular invasion** to salvage (Statement 19) and calls further treatment for other high-risk mucosal cancer undefinable → individualize (Statement 20). [[aga-2021-post-esd-surveillance]] (US) holds that in ESCC **any m3 (muscularis mucosae) or submucosal invasion is a noncurative resection**, without a vascular-invasion qualifier. **AGA 2021 governs**; WEO adds only that the nodal data for T1MM without vascular invasion are small and inconclusive.
- **Post-resection surveillance interval.** WEO: endoscopy with AEI **at least annually**, every 6 months with multiple LVL (Statement 23). AGA 2021 (Table 1) after curative ESD of T1a m1–m2 ESCC: first and second endoscopy at **3–6 months**, then every **6–12 months**, annually only after 2 years (HGD: 6–12 months, then annually after 2 years). **AGA 2021 governs**; WEO's 6-month interval for multiple LVL is a gap-filler for that subgroup.
- **ESD vs EMR by size.** Concordant on the threshold: WEO uses ESD for lesions **>15 mm** (Statement 17), as [[asge-2023-esd]] suggests ESD over EMR for ESCC **>15 mm**. For **≤15 mm** ASGE 2023 words it two ways — "suggest either ESD or EMR" (Question 1a box) and "no recommendation for or against either" (Table 2), conditional/low — both leaving the choice open; WEO adds the condition that EMR is acceptable only if en bloc is achievable (Statement 16). WEO's discussion also cites a meta-analysis cut-point of ≤10 mm EMR / >20 mm ESD / either between, which is not its statement.
- **SM1 squamous ceiling.** Concordant: Table 2 sm1 = **<200 µm**, matching [[aga-2019-esd-t1b-esophageal-cancer]] and [[aga-2018-esd-united-states]] (m3 or sm <200 µm as the expanded ESD indication). WEO calls MM/SM1 a **relative** indication for ESD; AGA 2018 calls it **expanded**; AGA 2021 calls the resulting pathology noncurative — these are compatible (resect for staging, treat as noncurative).
- **Nodal-risk figures differ by series.** WEO Table 2: m3 8–18%, sm1 8–33%, sm2 17–35%, sm3 21–48%. AGA 2019: m3 ≈10%, SM1 27% (table) or 8–26.5% (text), SM2 up to 60%. ASGE 2023 (as carried on [[endoscopic-submucosal-dissection]]): SM1 ESCC ≈5%. All agree that m1–m2 carries negligible risk and that risk rises from m3; the ranges come from different surgical series.
- **PET/CT and EUS in early disease.** WEO recommends considering FDG-PET/CT in potential surgical candidates and calls EUS integral for clinically deep SM invasion (Statement 15), and lists high-frequency EUS as an option for depth (Statement 13). AGA 2019 judges PET/CT unnecessary in early-stage disease and stresses EUS overstaging. Different populations (surgical candidates / deep SM vs early T1); WEO's own discussion also notes EUS may overstage.

**Internal inconsistencies in the WEO document:**
- **Statement 7** evidence: "Moderate" under the statement vs "Very low" in Table 1.
- **Statement 12** evidence: "Weak" under the statement (not a GRADE quality level) vs "Moderate" in Table 1.
- **Statement 8** threshold: "at least 80% accuracy" in the statement vs ">80% accuracy" in the discussion.
- **"13 (56.5%) received a strong agreement from more than half of the panel"**: by the printed votes, A+ exceeds 50% for 11 statements; two more (Statements 20 and 21) sit at exactly 50%.
- **Statements 11 and 23** overlap (both recommend AEI for surveillance after ER; 23 adds the annual interval).
- **JES** is expanded as "Japanese Endoscopic Society" in the Statement 3 discussion and "Japan Esophageal Society" in the Table 1 abbreviation list.
- Statement 4 vote percentages as printed total slightly more than 100%.
