Update to the 2013 ACG celiac disease guidelines. Uses GRADE methodology. Seven authors with no declared competing interests.

## Summary

Celiac disease (CD) affects ~1% of the US population and is defined as a permanent immune-mediated response to gluten in wheat, barley, and rye, characterized by small bowel injury and CD-specific antibodies. The 2022 update covers eight PICO-format clinical questions, with recommendations graded by strength (strong/conditional) and quality of evidence (high/moderate/low/very low).

**Key recommendations (8 questions):**

1. **Diagnosis — biopsy vs. serology:** [[upper-endoscopy|EGD]] with multiple duodenal biopsies remains the standard for confirmation in children and adults (strong/moderate). A nonbiopsy approach — TTG-IgA >10× ULN plus confirmatory EMA in a second sample — is conditionally acceptable in children and may be applied to symptomatic adults who cannot undergo endoscopy (conditional/moderate).

2. **Goal of GFD therapy:** Mucosal (histologic) healing is the preferred endpoint of GFD therapy, beyond clinical and serological remission alone (conditional/low). Follow-up biopsy after ~2 years on GFD is reasonable in adults.

3. **Gluten detection devices:** Routine use is not recommended (conditional/low). Standard of care for adherence assessment is interview with a dietitian expert in GFD.

4. **Probiotics:** Insufficient evidence to recommend for or against (evidence gap/very low).

5. **Oats in GFD:** Gluten-free oats are recommended for most patients with CD (strong/moderate). A small subset may react to the oat protein avenin; monitoring for tolerance is required.

6. **Pneumococcal vaccination:** Recommended in patients with CD due to increased risk (approximately 2-fold) from functional hyposplenism (conditional/low).

7. **Screening strategy:** Case finding (targeted testing in symptomatic or high-risk individuals) is recommended (strong/low); mass screening of the general population is not recommended (strong/low).

8. **Serology in children <2 years:** TTG-IgA alone is the preferred test in IgA-sufficient children <2 years; DGP-IgG or TTG-IgG should be used in IgA-deficient children (strong/moderate).

## Key Findings / Claims

- **Biopsy protocol:** 1–2 biopsies from duodenal bulb (9-o'clock or 12-o'clock position) + ≥4 biopsies from postbulbar (distal) duodenum. Studies show that ≥4 specimens significantly increase diagnostic yield (new CD detected in 1.8% vs 0.7% when <4 specimens, P <0.0001). Only 39% of clinically suspicious cases had ≥4 biopsies obtained in practice.

- **Serology performance:** TTG-IgA sensitivity ranges 63–93% and specificity 96–100% across laboratories. Negative TTG-IgA has high negative predictive value when pretest probability is low-moderate; EGD with biopsy should still be considered when pretest probability is high despite negative serology (seronegative CD exists).

- **IgA deficiency caveat:** If total IgA is deficient, use DGP-IgG or TTG-IgG. IgA-based tests (TTG-IgA, EMA-IgA) will be falsely negative.

- **Nonbiopsy diagnosis in children:** Requires TTG-IgA >10× ULN confirmed by a positive EMA in a second blood sample, with family agreement. HLA testing is no longer required per ESPGHAN 2020.

- **Marsh/Corazza classification:** Histological grading uses Marsh or simplified Corazza classification. Lymphocytic duodenosis (≥25 IELs per 100 epithelial cells) without villous atrophy = Marsh I (not specific for CD; other causes should be sought). Marsh II = lymphocytic duodenosis + crypt hyperplasia without atrophy. GFD may be considered in symptomatic patients with Marsh I or II who also have elevated CD antibodies, especially EMA.

- **Mucosal healing:** Median time to mucosal healing in adults is ~3 years. Failure to heal correlates with increased lymphoproliferative malignancy risk (HR 2.81) and hip fracture risk (HR 1.67). Normal follow-up histology is associated with null lymphoma risk (HR 0.97).

- **Nonresponsive CD (NRCD):** Defined as persistent symptoms/signs/labs despite 6–12 months of GFD. Most common cause is inadvertent gluten exposure. Systematic workup includes dietitian assessment, repeat serology, testing for lactose/fructose intolerance, pancreatic insufficiency, microscopic colitis, and [[small-intestinal-bacterial-overgrowth|SIBO]].

- **Refractory CD (RCD):** Villous atrophy with malabsorption despite strict GFD for >12 months in the absence of other disorders. <1% of CD patients. RCD type 1: polyclonal T-cell infiltrate — may respond to strict elemental diet, budesonide (open-capsule), prednisone, immunomodulators, mesalamine, biologics. RCD type 2: clonal T-cell infiltrate — poor prognosis, precursor to EATL; options include open-capsule budesonide, cladribine, autologous stem-cell transplant; parenteral nutrition often needed.

- **Conditions warranting CD testing (Table 4 highlights):** Diarrhea + weight loss, chronic diarrhea, iron deficiency anemia, metabolic bone disease/early osteoporosis, unexplained elevated transaminases, dermatitis herpetiformis, peripheral neuropathy, Down syndrome, Turner syndrome, type 1 DM (3–10% prevalence of CD), first-degree relatives of CD patients.

- **Case finding, not mass screening:** CD does not fulfill WHO mass-screening criteria. Active case finding using serology in symptomatic/at-risk patients is the preferred strategy.

- **Pneumococcal risk:** ~1/3 of CD patients have subclinical functional hyposplenism (pitted red cell counting). CD is associated with ~2-fold increased risk of pneumococcal infection.

- **Oats:** Gluten-free oats are safe for most; a small subset react to avenin. Finnish pure oats (Avena sativa "Irina" and "Potenza") have been validated as safe in children.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- No RCTs comparing follow-up biopsy vs no follow-up biopsy strategies; mucosal healing as a prognostic endpoint remains debated.
- Optimal TTG threshold for nonbiopsy adult diagnosis not yet established across US commercial laboratories.
- Role of DGP-IgG in IgA-deficient patients with negative TTG-IgG remains uncertain.
- Optimal gluten detection technology for clinical use not established.
- Probiotic benefit in CD not established; some probiotics may contain detectable gluten despite gluten-free labeling.
- Oat tolerance monitoring intervals not defined.
