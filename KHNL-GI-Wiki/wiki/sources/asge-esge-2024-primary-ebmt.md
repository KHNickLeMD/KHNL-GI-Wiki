## Summary

The first overarching guideline on **endoscopic bariatric and metabolic therapies (EBMTs)**, prepared jointly by the American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) and the European Society of Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ESGE) using GRADE (Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation). It groups therapies **by procedure type, not by brand**. It includes every EBMT with US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval or clearance, or a Conformité Européenne (CE) mark, between March 2016 and March 2021:
- **Intragastric balloon (IGB):** Orbera, Orbera365, Obalon, ReShape, Spatz.
- **Endoscopic gastric remodeling (EGR):** endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty (ESG) with the OverStitch; primary obesity surgery endoluminal (POSE) with the Incisionless Operating Platform (IOP); gastric plication with Endomina. All three are grouped because the implant that causes weight loss is the suture in each.
- **Aspiration therapy (AT):** AspireAssist.
- **Transpyloric shuttle (TPS).**
- **Duodenal-jejunal bypass liner (DJBL):** EndoBarrier.
- **Duodenal mucosal resurfacing (DMR):** Revita.

The panel included bariatric endoscopists, a bariatric surgeon, obesity medicine, bariatric psychology, dietitians, methodologists, librarians and a patient representative. Members with conflicts did not vote on the category concerned.

**Outcomes and thresholds.** The critical outcomes were percentage total weight loss (%TWL), change in hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c; small-bowel devices only) and serious adverse events (SAEs). The **minimal clinically important difference** was set a priori at a **mean difference (MD) of 3% TWL** over control, because lifestyle modification (LM) alone produced 3.2% TWL in the control arms of the EBMT trials. Requiring a 3% MD keeps the treated arm above the **5% TWL** responder threshold that improves obesity-related comorbidities. For HbA1c the threshold was an MD of **0.5%**. Where studies did not grade SAEs, Clavien–Dindo class III–V counted as serious.

**Why weight loss is the target.** Comorbidity endpoints begin to improve at **5% TWL** (falls in glucose, insulin, triglycerides and alanine aminotransferase). The guideline cites further thresholds: **7% TWL** reduced incident type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) in the Diabetes Prevention Program; **≥8% TWL** gave the greatest HbA1c reduction in Look AHEAD; **≥10% TWL** reduced cardiovascular and all-cause mortality. In metabolic dysfunction–associated steatohepatitis (MASH), **≥10% TWL** resolved MASH in 90% and regressed fibrosis in 45%.

**Bottom line.** **IGB and EGR are suggested with LM** (conditional, moderate certainty) and were the only EBMTs commercially available and routinely used when the guideline was written. AT and DJBL would be suggested if they return to the market. TPS and DMR should be used **only in clinical trials**. Every EBMT should be offered **with LM** (diet, physical activity, behavioral therapy) inside a multidisciplinary team. The IGB and EGR conditional recommendations are driven primarily by **moderate variability in patient values and preferences**; the Discussion adds **reduced equity** (no widespread coverage, so only patients who can pay have access) as a major factor.

## Key Findings / Claims

*All 14 recommendations as the guideline numbers them, condensed. Strength and certainty use its own GRADE wording. "Suggest" = conditional. Where Table 1 and the body text word a recommendation differently, both are given.*

### Who should be considered
1. **Adults with overweight or obesity:** suggest EBMT **plus LM over LM alone** for **BMI ≥30 kg/m²** with or without an obesity-related comorbidity, **or BMI 27.0–29.9 kg/m² with ≥1 obesity-related comorbidity**. — *Conditional, very low certainty.*
   - BMI 27.0–29.9 with a comorbidity: data exist for **IGB, EGR and DJBL**. The cutoff of 27 is conservative because most overweight studies started at 27 or 28, and half required a comorbidity (T2DM).
   - Class III obesity: data exist for **IGB, EGR, AT and DJBL**.
   - **No upper BMI limit**, because EBMTs serve as primary therapy or as a bridge to bariatric surgery. Studies enrolled BMIs up to 78 kg/m².
   - Pooled weight loss: **11.9% TWL** (95% confidence interval [CI] 7.7–16.0) at 6–12 months for BMI 27–29.9; **13.1% TWL** (10.8–15.4) for class III. In classes I–II (14 randomized controlled trials [RCTs], n = 2787), the **MD was 7.1% TWL** (5.4–8.8) over LM at 12 months, or at IGB removal (the Discussion gives 6.3% [5.3–7.3] at 12 months). The treated arms ranged from 5.0% to 18.6% TWL.
   - HbA1c: −1.0% (BMI 27–29.9) and −1.3% (class III) at 12 months; in classes I–II, the MD was 0.7% over control.
   - SAEs: **2.7%** (BMI 27–29.9) and **6.9%** (class III). In classes I–II, the risk ratio was **4.4 vs control**, or **14 extra SAEs per 1000**; the treated arms ranged from 0% to 10.6%.

### Intragastric balloon
2. **Adults with obesity:** suggest **IGB plus LM over LM alone**. — *Conditional, moderate certainty.*
   - 7 RCTs: the **MD was 6.9% TWL** (4.1–9.7) at removal (6–8 months), 10.7% vs 3.4%. At 12 months the **MD was 4.4% TWL** (2.9–6.0), 7.9% vs 3.3%. Patients should expect to **regain some weight within 6 months of removal**.
   - SAEs: **32 extra per 1000** (58/1028 vs 0/798), an SAE rate of 5.6%, mostly short-lived accommodative nausea, vomiting, dehydration and pain. Examples: esophageal injury 4/473, gastric ulcer or bleeding 5/650, severe dehydration 5/704, aspiration pneumonia 2/42, perforation 2/653, obstruction 1/802, deaths 0/741.
   - Mechanism: at least 400 mL of space occupation, plus slower gastric emptying. Both need the balloon in place, which explains regain after removal. Repeat IGB was not evaluated.
3. **Undergoing IGB placement:** suggest **antiemetics periprocedurally**. — *Conditional, very low certainty.* Expert survey (15 experts, 2587 fluid-filled IGBs): all gave antiemetics before placement, most often ondansetron (93%), aprepitant (73%), dexamethasone (60%) and a scopolamine patch (60%). Postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) occurred in 31.6% within 72 h, with an emergency department (ED) visit in 7.7%. On discharge, 93% prescribed ondansetron (86%), a scopolamine patch (64%), aprepitant (36%) or lorazepam (36%); PONV was 24.5% (Supplementary Table 23: 25.5%) and ED visits 9.2% within 30 days.
4. **Undergoing IGB placement:** suggest **pain medications periprocedurally**. — *Conditional, very low certainty.* No regimen is specified (choose by institutional policy, clinical context and availability). Survey: 80% prescribe acetaminophen (67%), hyoscyamine (58%) or opioids (42%); 27.5% have pain and 3.1% visit the ED for pain within 30 days. **Use opioids sparingly**, because about 10% of opioid-naive bariatric surgical patients become addicted after a periprocedural dose.
5. **Undergoing IGB placement:** suggest **proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) while the IGB is in place**, over no PPIs. — *Conditional, very low certainty.* There is no comparative study. Most RCT patients took PPIs and had gastric ulcer or bleeding SAEs <1%. All 15 experts prescribe a PPI for the whole dwell time.

### Endoscopic gastric remodeling (ESG, POSE, Endomina)
6. **Adults with obesity:** suggest **EGR plus LM over LM alone**. — *Conditional, moderate certainty.*
   - Any of the three devices may be used. Evidence is **insufficient to recommend one device over another**; choose by clinical context, patient values, availability and operator experience.
   - 3 RCTs (n = 585): the **MD was 8.0% TWL** (3.4–12.6) at 12 months, 10.5% vs 2.4%. Observational meta-analysis of 21 studies (n = 5250): **17.3% TWL** (16.2–18.4) at 12 months; OverStitch 18.2%, IOP 16.5%, Endomina 7.0%.
   - Older POSE trials plicated the **fundus**; the current ESG technique stitches the **gastric body**. The sham-controlled POSE trial had the lowest weight loss (4.95% ± 7.04% TWL), and its unblinded lead-in group lost 40% more. The open-label trial of the same technique reached 13.0% TWL.
   - SAEs: relative risk 5.6 (14/435 vs 1/253); RCT SAE rate 3.2%. Examples from the ESG RCT (n = 131): abdominal abscess treated endoscopically, upper GI bleeding managed conservatively, and malnutrition treated by endoscopic reversal of the ESG (1 each). From the largest POSE trial (n = 221): extraluminal bleeding needing laparoscopy (1), hepatic abscess drained percutaneously (1), and pain, nausea or vomiting prolonging admission (9).
   - Regulatory status: OverStitch has a CE mark and **FDA De Novo authorization for obesity**. IOP and Endomina have a CE mark and FDA 510(k) clearance for tissue approximation. All three procedures reduce gastric width and length and are thought to delay gastric emptying.
7. **Undergoing EGR:** suggest **antiemetics periprocedurally**. — *Conditional, very low certainty.* Survey (16 experts, 2700 EGRs): 88% gave antiemetics before the procedure, most often ondansetron (79%), dexamethasone (64%), aprepitant (57%) and a scopolamine patch (50%). PONV was 19.5% within 72 h, with hospital care in 2.5% (Appendix text says ED visit 2.9%). On discharge, 88% prescribed ondansetron (64%), a scopolamine patch (57%), aprepitant (36%) or metoclopramide (29%); PONV was 13.3% and hospital care 2.9% within 30 days. In the ESG RCT (MERIT), patients received IV ondansetron plus dexamethasone during the procedure, then oral ondansetron, promethazine and lorazepam as needed. Rates in MERIT: severe nausea 20%, severe vomiting 10%, admission for accommodative symptoms 4%.
8. **Undergoing EGR:** suggest **pain medications periprocedurally**. — *Conditional, very low certainty.* Survey: 88% prescribe acetaminophen (57%), opioids (36%) or nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (14%); 15.1% have pain and 2% return for pain within 30 days. **Opioids sparingly.** Pain likely comes from gas, stitches through the serosa, inflammation and transient ischemia. MERIT used a low-dose liquid narcotic as needed, with severe pain in 15%.
9. **Undergoing EGR:** suggest **prophylactic antibiotics periprocedurally** (Table 1); the body text says "**short-term** antibiotics periprocedurally". — *Conditional, very low certainty.*
   - Survey: 63% give antibiotics routinely, most often ciprofloxacin ± metronidazole (50%), amoxicillin-clavulanate or ampicillin-sulbactam (30%), or a cephalosporin (20%).
   - Duration: **60% give a single IV dose only**; 40% add a **3-day oral course**. Infection 0.2%; antibiotic adverse events 0.8%.
   - MERIT gave one IV dose of ciprofloxacin, ceftriaxone or piperacillin-tazobactam, with oral antibiotics at each site's discretion; infection 0.67%.
   - **One IV dose may suffice**, by analogy with bariatric surgery. Benefit of the extra oral course is unstudied.
10. **Undergoing EGR:** suggest **short-term PPIs after the procedure** over no PPIs (Table 1: "short-term proton pump inhibitors periprocedurally"). — *Conditional, very low certainty.* There is no comparative study. MERIT used **PPI 40 mg daily for 12 months** (GI bleeding 0.67%); Huberty used **40 mg daily for 3 months** (no bleeding). The guideline names no agent and no single duration.

### Other gastric devices
11. **Adults with obesity:** suggest **AT plus LM over LM alone, depending on device availability**. — *Conditional, low certainty.* Evidence comes only from class II–III obesity. **The device is no longer commercially available** (financial reasons).
    - 2 RCTs (n = 189): MD 10.4% TWL at 12 months. A meta-analysis (n = 373) found 16.6% TWL. SAEs 3.3%, or 16 extra per 1000.
    - About 15%–30% of meal calories are aspirated, which explains about half the weight loss; the eating behaviors the device requires explain the rest.
    - **<25% still had the device at 4 years.** Weight regain after removal is unknown. Patients with eating disorders were excluded, and no one was followed after removal. Patient acceptability is low.
12. **Adults with obesity:** **TPS only in the context of a clinical trial.** — *No recommendation, knowledge gap.* One sham-controlled RCT showed an MD of 6.7% TWL and an SAE rate of 2.8%, including one esophageal rupture needing surgery and several impactions. The device is FDA-approved for a 12-month dwell but has not been commercialized, so real-world experience is lacking. An earlier design caused ulcers in 50%.

### Small-bowel devices
13. **Adults with obesity and T2DM:** suggest **DJBL plus LM over LM alone**. — *Conditional, moderate certainty.* The current device is designed for a 12-month implant.
    - Results: HbA1c MD **0.73%** (0.39–1.06) and **5.4% TWL** at 12 months.
    - SAEs, defined as events forcing early explant: 8.5%, or 24 extra per 1000. US pivotal events: intolerance 8/212, hemorrhage 6/212, hepatic abscess 5/212, obstruction 3/212, pancreatitis 2/212, perforation 1/212.
    - The US trial was stopped by the company for hepatic abscess. The sponsor attributes the abscesses to high-dose PPI used for bleeding prophylaxis, which promoted a bacterial biofilm on the device. A new US RCT with infection-mitigation strategies was ongoing.
    - The CE mark was lost in 2017 for administrative reasons; the device was not commercially available when the guideline was written.
    - The panel judged that benefit outweighs risk because only about half of patients with T2DM reach glycemic control on medications.
14. **Adults with T2DM:** **DMR only in the context of a clinical trial.** — *No recommendation, knowledge gap.* One RCT (n = 108; Europe and Brazil, analysed separately) showed an HbA1c MD of 0.3% (−1.1 to 1.7) at 6 months. SAEs included one jejunal perforation needing surgery.

### General points
- **Durability:** EGR is effective to **at least 5 years**. Inadequate weight loss or regain can follow any EBMT. Options include repeat procedures, adding antiobesity medication, intensifying LM, or switching device, but the guideline did not evaluate them. **EBMTs do not prevent later bariatric surgery.**
- **Cost-effectiveness:** ESG was cost-effective vs LM alone in class II obesity in the US and the UK, and vs GLP-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs) and sleeve gastrectomy in the US.
- **Evidence gaps:** long-term effects on comorbidities and mortality; combining EBMTs with each other or with antiobesity medications; personalization; periprocedural care; cost-effectiveness.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **PPI with IGB: same direction, different strength.** [[aga-2021-intragastric-balloons|AGA 2021]] **recommends** a PPI (strong, moderate certainty). ASGE–ESGE 2024 **suggests** one (conditional, very low certainty). Both say to use it. The newer guideline wins the page; the difference is surfaced on [[intragastric-balloon]].
- **BMI eligibility is wider than AGA.** AGA 2021 selects on failed conventional weight-loss strategies; BMI 30–40 kg/m² is only the range US trials enrolled. ASGE–ESGE 2024 adds **27–29.9 with a comorbidity** and sets **no upper limit**. AGA 2017 gave no EBT BMI cut-point at all.
- **Wording mismatch inside this guideline:** Recommendation 9 says "prophylactic antibiotics" in Table 1 and "short-term antibiotics" in the text. Recommendation 10 says "short-term PPIs periprocedurally" in Table 1 and "short-term PPIs after the procedure" in the text. The EGR hospital-care rate for PONV within 72 h is 2.5% in the supplementary table and 2.9% in the appendix text.
- **Metoclopramide:** the EGR survey lists it as a discharge antiemetic (29%). The Brazilian IGB consensus advises against it after balloon placement. These are different procedures, so this is not a direct conflict.
