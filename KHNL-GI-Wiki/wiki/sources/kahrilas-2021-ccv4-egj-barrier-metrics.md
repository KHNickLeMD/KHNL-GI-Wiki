## Summary
- **Scope:** high-resolution manometry (HRM) metrics of EGJ integrity as an antireflux barrier in gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD); not formally addressed in CC v3.0; all metrics new in CC v4.0 (§1, §5).
- **Why hard:** EGJ = crural diaphragm (CD) + lower esophageal sphincter (LES), each under separate control; morphology varies with hiatus hernia and over time within one study; respiratory inversion point (RIP) can sit above the LES or between LES and CD; marked axial/radial asymmetry → no single metric captures barrier function (§2, p. 2).
- **Method:** CC v4.0 working group = 52 experts, 20 countries, six neurogastroenterology/motility societies; statements ranked in two rounds by RAND/UCLA Appropriateness Method (RAM) (§1–2, p. 2).
  - **≥85% agreement → strong recommendation; 80%–85% → conditional**; below → discussed in text only.
  - Strong/conditional statements graded by Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation (GRADE) by two experts external to the subgroup, when the statement structure and evidence allowed.
- **Four metrics considered** (Abstract, p. 1):
  - **LES-CD separation** — strong recommendations as indicative of hiatus hernia; **no agreed numeric threshold** for defining hiatal hernia.
  - **RIP** — no agreement on its significance; only that with hiatus hernia it can sit above the LES or between LES and CD.
  - **EGJ contractile integral (EGJ-CI)** — agreed method (referenced to gastric pressure, mmHg·cm); **no agreed threshold** for a hypotensive EGJ.
  - **Intragastric pressure** — endorsed as important, worth further study; **no agreed threshold** for abdominal obesity.
- **Bottom line (§5, p. 7):** each laboratory must set its own EGJ-CI normal range; EGJ-CI is ~85% driven by inspiratory CD contraction, so it poorly reflects LES contractility — a future expiratory-LES metric may be needed, especially with hiatus hernia.

## Key Findings / Claims

### LES-CD separation and hiatal hernia (§2.1, pp. 2–4)
- Type I (sliding) hiatus hernia is central to reflux pathophysiology; criteria separating a small hernia from normal are vague (physiological LES mobility, inconsistent definitions).
- HRM LES-CD separation vs endoscopy or barium X-ray: sensitivity **88%**, specificity **95%** (p. 3) — reference standard debatable.
- Surgery is the definitive reference: in one comparison with surgery, HRM (LES-CD metric) outperformed endoscopy and barium — sensitivity **94%**, specificity **92%**, kappa **0.85**; optimal threshold LES-CD separation **1.2 cm** (p. 3).
- LES-CD separation also correlates with reflux severity on pH-metry (p. 3).
- In a logistic regression (expiratory LES pressure, LES-CD separation, inspiratory EGJ augmentation; adjusted for age and body mass index [BMI]), **only inspiratory augmentation** was independently associated with GERD by pH-metry (p. 3).
- Working-group conclusions (p. 4):
  - Compelling evidence to adopt LES-CD separation as the HRM metric of EGJ morphology.
  - **No agreement** on the LES-CD threshold that defines hiatal hernia, or on handling variable separation within one study.
  - **No reason to separate type II from type III** morphology as proposed by the Lyon consensus — insufficient evidence for that distinction.
  - With LES-CD separation, **specify whether the RIP sits above or below the LES**.
- **Summary wording (§5, p. 7):** LES-CD separation **>1 cm** is **suggestive (but still inconclusive)** of hiatus hernia.
- **Lyon / CC v3.0 morphology, as described here (§2, p. 2)** — not adopted by this subgroup:

| Lyon / CC v3.0 type | LES-CD relationship |
|---|---|
| Type 1 | LES and CD superimposed |
| Type 2 | Axially separated, **less than 2 cm** apart |
| Type 3 | Separated by **≥2 cm** |

- **Scoring technique (Figure 3 legend, p. 5):** centers of the LES and CD high-pressure zones located with the isobaric contour tool; separation **rounded to the nearest cm**. Figure 3 uses the 15 mmHg isobaric contour, Figure 4 the 25 mmHg contour.

### Respiratory inversion point (§2.1, pp. 3–4)
- Points of general agreement (p. 3):
  1. RIP can **never be below the diaphragm**.
  2. CD superimposed on the LES (LES-CD separation **<1 cm**) → RIP above the EGJ pressure complex; most of the LES sits within and beneath the hiatus.
  3. CD and LES separated → RIP can sit at or above the CD (within the hernia) or above the LES.
- An earlier classification (Akimoto 2017) named LES-CD separation subtypes **"B"** (RIP above the LES) and **"C"** (RIP below the LES); subtype B was less likely to show pathological reflux on pH-metry than C (p. 3).
- No consensus on which RIP location indicates greater dysfunction (§5, p. 7).
- The PIP tool used to localize the RIP is technically flawed with larger hernias — all relevant sensors are above the diaphragm, so RIP localization becomes unreliable (p. 4).

### EGJ contractile integral (§2 p. 2; Figure 1 p. 3; §3.1 pp. 5–6)
- **Computation:** distal contractile integral (DCI) box set over the LES and CD for **exactly three respiratory cycles**, during rest; the "DCI" is **divided by the duration of the three cycles** → time-independent, in **mmHg·cm**, referenced to gastric pressure (p. 2; Figure 1).
- Figure 1 uses a DCI tool with the isobaric contour threshold just above gastric pressure. Its three worked examples (p. 3):

| LES-CD separation | 3-cycle time | EGJ "DCI" (box) | EGJ-CI |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (no hiatal hernia) | 10.5 s | 475 mmHg·s·cm | **45.2 mmHg·cm** |
| 2 cm (indeterminant for hiatal hernia) | 14 s | 380 mmHg·s·cm | **27.1 mmHg·cm** |
| 4 cm (hiatal hernia) | 10 s | 185 mmHg·s·cm (155 without CD) | **18.5 mmHg·cm** (15.5 without CD) |

*Worked examples from Figure 1 (p. 3); EGJ-CI = box value ÷ 3-cycle time. The 2 cm panel is indeterminant: the sphincter complex may instead be moving 2 cm with respiration without axial separation. Some investigators restrict the box to the LES when separation is complete (the "without CD" values).*

- **Forerunner:** Hoshino's LES pressure integral — 10-second recording, referenced to 20 mmHg; increased distal esophageal acid exposure when **<400 mmHg·cm·s** (p. 5).
- **Physiology (three-dimensional HRM [3D-HRM]):**
  - Inspiratory EGJ augmentation from the CD correlates strongly with EGJ-CI (**R = 0.84**) (p. 4).
  - ~**85%** of overall EGJ contractility across the respiratory cycle is attributable to the CD (p. 5).
  - CD signal correlated with acid exposure time on pH-metry (**r = −0.42, p < 0.01**) (p. 5).
- **Performance:** EGJ-CI separates GERD from controls; studies stratifying severity suggest a subset with very low EGJ-CI is prone to endoscopic esophagitis or clearly abnormal reflux testing (p. 6).
- **No firm threshold for abnormality** (p. 6):
  - Methods differ — referencing to intragastric pressure vs slightly above it; including vs excluding the CD with complete separation — but these are unlikely to explain the size of between-center differences.
  - Within a laboratory, lower EGJ-CI correlates with more severe reflux; the lower the value, the more severe the EGJ dysfunction.
  - EGJ-CI is the **best validated metric of EGJ contractile vigor** and should be adopted, referenced to gastric baseline pressure.
  - **No consensus** on excluding the CD from the computation when LES-CD separation is **>2 cm** and the LES can be isolated.
- **Each laboratory must establish its own normal range** (§5, p. 7).

### Intra-abdominal (intragastric) pressure (§4.1, pp. 6–7)
- Reflux is ultimately driven by intra-abdominal pressure; considered here beyond the Lyon consensus.
- Higher BMI and abdominal girth raise intragastric pressure; above-average BMI → more GERD. In end-stage lung disease, a higher inspiratory abdominal-to-thoracic pressure gradient was associated with worse distal esophageal acid exposure.
- Proposed measurement: **at expiration**, from an intragastric sensor relatively free of artifact (p. 7). Referencing to intragastric pressure also lets EGJ metrics be related to atmospheric pressure.
- On average, **8 mmHg** corresponds to waist circumference **36 inches** or BMI about **30 kg/m²** — suggested as a reasonable indicator of abdominal obesity (p. 7).
- **No consensus** on the intragastric pressure value that is conclusive evidence of abnormality (p. 7). §5: elevated intra-abdominal pressure referenced to atmospheric pressure indicates abdominal obesity and should be measured (p. 7).

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **LES-CD numeric cut-off — wiki says none; this review uses one.** [[chicago-classification-v4]] says CC v4.0 gives no numeric LES-CD cut-off. The main CC v4.0 document indeed lists the three subtypes without one ([[chicago-v4-2021-esophageal-dysmotility]], p. 13). This review's §3 #4 (Conditional, 80%, Low) defines the subtypes **by** LES-CD separation **<1 cm** vs **>1 cm**, and §5 calls >1 cm "suggestive (but still inconclusive)" of hiatus hernia. What remains un-agreed is the threshold for **defining hiatal hernia**, not the subtype cut-offs.
- **EGJ-CI <25 mmHg·cm.** The main CC v4.0 document (p. 13) states that a value of <25 mmHg·cm was suggested as a hypotensive EGJ but not met with agreement; [[chicago-classification-v4]] reflects this. This review gives **no number** — it says no threshold could be established and each laboratory must set its own normal range. Consistent in substance (no agreed cut-off); the review does not mention 25 mmHg·cm.
- **Subtype 2 wording.** Main document and [[chicago-classification-v4]]: subtype 2 = LES-CD separation with RIP **proximal to the CD**. This review: RIP **between the LES and CD** (§3 #4) or **at the CD level** (§2.1). Same anatomical position described differently.
- **Subtype naming.** Main document: no consensus on naming (I, II, III vs A, C, B), both acceptable. This review: no consensus on a classification scheme, and no reason to keep the Lyon type II / type III split. Agrees.
- **GRADE for subtypes.** Main document grades "EGJ complex defined by LES-CD separation and RIP location" Low GRADE, Conditional; this review's matching §3 #4 is Conditional, 80%, Low. Agrees.
- **Internal inconsistencies in this review:**
  - Subtype 2 is "hiatal hernia indeterminant" in the §3 text but "hiatal hernia" in Table 2; subtype 3 is "complete LES-CD separation" in the text but "LES-CD separation >1 cm" in Table 2.
  - Intragastric pressure: §3 #1 says measure over three complete respiratory cycles; §4.1 text says measure at expiration.
- **Open:** which RIP location means greater dysfunction; whether to exclude the CD from EGJ-CI when separation >2 cm; intragastric pressure threshold; a future metric of expiratory LES contractility (§5).
