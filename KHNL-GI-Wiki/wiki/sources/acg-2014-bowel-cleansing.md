> ⚠ **Historical / superseded source.** This 2014 US Multi-Society Task Force on Colorectal Cancer (USMSTF) bowel-prep guidance is superseded by [[usmstf-2025-bowel-prep|USMSTF 2025: Optimizing Bowel Preparation Quality for Colonoscopy]] (published simultaneously in three journals, so also cited as "ACG 2025 Bowel Prep" and "ASGE 2025 Bowel Preparation Quality" — same document). Split dosing, the 4–6 h / ≥2 h timing rule and intraprocedural grading of preparation quality carried forward unchanged; the adequacy benchmark, purgative volume, adjunct stance and repeat-colonoscopy timing did not. Where the two differ, the 2025 document governs — differences listed under [[#Contradictions / Open Questions]].

## Summary
USMSTF consensus document — approved by the governing bodies of the American College of Gastroenterology (ACG), the American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) and the American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) — establishing the framework for optimizing bowel preparation quality for [[colonoscopy]]. Literature search January 1980 (first United States Food and Drug Administration [FDA] approval of a polyethylene glycol–electrolyte lavage solution [PEG-ELS] preparation) to August 2013. Recommendations graded with the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) system.

Framing: up to **20–25% of all colonoscopies** are reported to have an inadequate preparation. Consequences — lower adenoma detection, longer procedural time, lower cecal intubation rates, increased electrocautery risk, shorter intervals between examinations. Because the consequences of inadequate cleansing are substantial, regimen choice should be based on **cleansing efficacy first and patient tolerability second** — though the two are interrelated, since a poorly tolerated agent is incompletely ingested.

Central contribution: **split dosing** for all elective colonoscopy (same-day dosing an acceptable alternative for afternoon examinations), with the second portion begun **4–6 h before** the procedure and completed **at least 2 h before**. Rationale: when the whole preparation is given the day before, small-intestinal chyme re-enters and coats the proximal colon, obscuring flat lesions; the chance of a good or excellent right-colon preparation falls by **up to 10% for each additional hour** between the end of ingestion and the start of colonoscopy.

**Adequate preparation** is defined operationally as cleansing that lets the colonoscopist recommend a screening or surveillance interval appropriate to the findings — in practice, confidence that lesions **>5 mm** were not obscured. Benchmark: **≥85% of all examinations on a per-physician basis**, measured routinely. The document also covers agent selection (4 L PEG-ELS as reference; low-volume, over-the-counter [OTC] and sulfate-based options), special populations (avoid sodium phosphate [NaP] in the elderly, in children <12 y, and in known or suspected [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]]; avoid magnesium-based preparations in chronic kidney disease), risk factors for inadequate preparation, and salvage of the patient who arrives inadequately prepped.

## Key Findings / Claims

### The 30 Recommendations

All **30** recommendations, none dropped. The document **numbers its recommendations only within each topic section, restarting at 1** — the section groupings and the numbers below are the document's own. Strength and evidence quality are quoted in the document's own vocabulary.

**Effect of inadequate preparation on polyp/adenoma detection and recommended follow-up intervals**

| # | Recommendation | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Make a preliminary assessment of preparation quality in the **rectosigmoid colon**. If the indication is screening or surveillance and the preparation is clearly inadequate to allow detection of polyps **>5 mm**, either terminate and reschedule, or deliver additional bowel-cleansing strategies without cancelling the procedure that day. | Strong | Low |
| 2 | If the colonoscopy is **complete to the cecum** but the preparation is ultimately deemed inadequate, repeat the examination — generally with a more aggressive regimen — **within 1 year**. Intervals **shorter than 1 year** are indicated when **advanced neoplasia is detected *and* the preparation was inadequate**. | Strong | Low |
| 3 | If the preparation is deemed adequate and the colonoscopy is completed, follow the guideline recommendations for screening or surveillance. | Strong | High |

**Dosing and timing of colon cleansing regimens**

| # | Recommendation | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A **split-dose** bowel cleansing regimen is strongly recommended for elective colonoscopy. | Strong | High |
| 2 | A **same-day** regimen is an acceptable alternative to split dosing, especially for patients undergoing an **afternoon** examination. | Strong | High |
| 3 | The **second dose** of a split preparation should ideally **begin 4–6 h before** the time of colonoscopy, with the last dose **completed at least 2 h before** the procedure time. | Strong | Moderate |

- "Splitting" means roughly **half** the cleansing dose is given on the day of the colonoscopy.
- Aspiration objection addressed: an American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) guideline states that ingestion of clear liquids up to 2 h before sedation does not affect residual gastric volume; two endoscopic studies found day-of ingestion of cleansing agents did not increase residual gastric volume. **Preoperative dehydration may be a greater safety concern than drinking clear liquids before anesthesia.**

**Diet during bowel cleansing**

| # | Recommendation | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | With a split-dose regimen, diet may include either **low-residue or full liquids until the evening on the day before** colonoscopy. | Weak | Moderate |

- Low-residue diet for part or all of the day before can be considered **for patients without other identifiable preprocedural risk factors for inadequate preparation**. Heterogeneity of the trials was the reason the task force stopped short of endorsing a regular diet.

**Usefulness of patient education and navigators for optimizing preparation results**

| # | Recommendation | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Provide **both oral and written** patient education instructions for all components of the colonoscopy preparation, and emphasize the importance of compliance. | Strong | Moderate |
| 2 | The physician performing the colonoscopy should ensure appropriate **support and process measures** are in place for patients to achieve adequate preparation quality. | Strong | Low |

**Rating the quality of bowel preparation during colonoscopy**

| # | Recommendation | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Assess adequacy of bowel preparation **only after all appropriate efforts to clear residual debris** (washing, suctioning) have been completed. | Strong | Low |
| 2 | Measure the **rate of adequate colon cleansing** routinely. | Strong | Moderate |
| 3 | Adequate preparation — cleansing that allows recommendation of a screening or surveillance interval appropriate to the findings — should be achieved in **85% or more of all examinations, on a per-physician basis**. | Strong | Low |

- If an endoscopist's adequate-preparation rate falls **below the 85% benchmark, an improvement initiative should be undertaken.**
- Endoscopists are encouraged to submit procedure reports to a **data registry** that benchmarks performance against minimally accepted thresholds and peer means.

**FDA-approved preparations**

| # | Recommendation | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Regimen selection should take into account the patient's **medical history, medications, and — when available — the adequacy of bowel preparation reported from prior colonoscopies**. | Strong | Moderate |
| 2 | A **split-dose regimen of 4 L PEG-ELS** provides high-quality bowel cleansing. | Strong | High |
| 3 | In **healthy nonconstipated** individuals, a 4-L PEG-ELS formulation produces a bowel-cleansing quality that is **not superior to a lower-volume PEG formulation**. | Strong | High |

**Over-the-counter, non–FDA-approved preparations**

| # | Recommendation | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | OTC bowel-cleansing agents have **variable efficacy — adequate to superior** — depending on agent, dose, timing of administration, and whether used alone or in combination. Regardless of agent, efficacy and tolerability are **enhanced with a split-dose regimen**. | Strong | Moderate |
| 2 | OTC purgatives are generally safe, but caution is required in certain populations: **magnesium-based preparations (both OTC and FDA-approved formulations) should be avoided in patients with chronic kidney disease.** | Weak | Very low |

**Adjuncts to colon cleansing before colonoscopy**

| # | Recommendation | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Routine use of adjunctive agents** for bowel cleansing before colonoscopy **is not recommended** — though agents may be useful in select circumstances, at the discretion of the prescribing physician. | Weak | Moderate |

**Differences in patient preference / willingness to repeat**

| # | Recommendation | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Split-dose** bowel cleansing is associated with **greater willingness to repeat** the regimen compared with the day-before regimen. | Strong | High |
| 2 | Use of **low-volume** bowel cleansing agents is associated with greater willingness to undergo a **repeat colonoscopy**. | Strong | High |

**Selection of bowel preparation in specific populations**

| # | Recommendation | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Elderly:** insufficient evidence to recommend specific regimens; however, **NaP preparations should be avoided** in this population. | Strong | Low |
| 2 | **Children and adolescents:** insufficient evidence to recommend specific regimens; however, **NaP should not be used in children younger than 12 y**, or in those with risk factors for complications from this medication. | Strong | Very low |
| 3 | **NaP should be avoided** in patients with **known or suspected IBD**. | Weak | Very low |
| 4 | Consider **additional bowel purgatives** in patients with risk factors for inadequate preparation — prior inadequate preparation, history of constipation, use of opioids or other constipating medications, prior colon resection, diabetes mellitus, or spinal cord injury. | Weak | Low |
| 5 | **After bariatric surgery:** low-volume preparations, or **extended-time delivery** of high-volume preparations, are recommended. | Weak | Very low |
| 6 | **Pregnancy:** **tap water enemas** should be used to prepare the colon for **sigmoidoscopy** in pregnant women. | Strong | Very low |
| 7 | **Spinal cord injury:** insufficient evidence to recommend specific regimens; consider additional bowel purgatives. | Weak | Very low |

**Salvage options for inadequate preparation**

Framing statement: there is **insufficient evidence to recommend a single salvage strategy**; the following options can be considered.

| # | Recommendation | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Large-volume enemas** can be attempted for patients who, presenting on the day of colonoscopy, report **brown effluent** despite compliance with the prescribed regimen. | Weak | Very low |
| 2 | **Through-the-scope enema** with completion colonoscopy the same day can be considered, especially in patients receiving **propofol** sedation. | Weak | Very low |
| 3 | **Waking the patient entirely from sedation** and continuing with further oral cathartic, with same-day or next-day colonoscopy, has been associated with **better outcomes than delayed colonoscopy**. | Weak | Low |

- Patients reporting brown liquid or solid effluent on arrival had a **54% chance** of a fair or poor preparation — identifying them **before sedation** allows salvage.
- When a second colonoscopy is performed specifically for inadequate preparation, it fails again for the same reason in **23%** (54/235). **Next-day** colonoscopy, relative to any other timing, carried a reduced risk of repeat failure (odds ratio [OR] 0.31; 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.1–0.92).

### Bowel Preparation Quality Scales (Appendix B)

For a scale to be clinically useful it must be both **valid** (measures what it intends to, as determined by experts) and **reliable** (reproducible between different observers).

**Aronchick Bowel Preparation Scale** (Table 1) — percentage of fluid or stool covering the bowel surface:

| Rating | Criteria |
|---|---|
| Excellent | Small volume of clear liquid, or **>95%** of surface seen |
| Good | Large volume of clear liquid covering **5–25%** of the surface, but **>90%** seen |
| Fair | Some semisolid stool that could be suctioned or washed away, but **>90%** of surface seen |
| Poor | Semisolid stool that could **not** be suctioned or washed away, and **<90%** of surface seen |
| Inadequate | Re-preparation needed |

- Intraclass correlation coefficients range from **0.79 (cecum) to 0.31 (distal colon)**. Because reliability is poor **and the scale downgrades quality for retained fluid, the Aronchick scale is not recommended for clinical practice.**
- **Ottawa scale** (Figure 1) — cleanliness scored **0–4 for each of right, mid, and rectosigmoid** segments, **plus** a single whole-colon fluid-quantity rating of **0–2**, summed to a total of **0 (perfect) to 14**. Validation showed higher Pearson correlation than Aronchick (0.89 vs 0.62) and higher κ / intraclass correlation (0.94 vs 0.77). **Because it scores the preparation before washing and suctioning, the Ottawa scale is also not recommended for clinical practice.** Observers need a calibration exercise before using it in a study or audit.
- **Boston Bowel Preparation Scale (BBPS)** (Table 2) — developed specifically for application **after withdrawal of the colonoscope, once all cleansing is complete**; each of right, transverse and left colon scored **0–3**, total **0–9**. If the procedure is **aborted** for inadequate preparation, **the proximal segments are assigned a score of 0**. Scoring descriptors and the current adequacy cut-point are on [[colonoscopy]]; the 2014 cut-points are under [[#Contradictions / Open Questions]].
  - Intra-observer weighted κ **0.77**; interobserver intraclass correlation **0.74**.
  - Polyp detection rate **40% with BBPS ≥5 vs 24% with <5** (*P* <0.02); repeat colonoscopy for inadequate preparation was recommended **2%** of the time at BBPS ≥5 vs **73%** at <5.
  - The developers published **4 endoscopic images** depicting preparations scoring 0–3, and a **15-minute training video**.
  - **The BBPS has the best data for a validated scoring system** and comes closest to meeting the task force's criteria, because it does not score retained fluid.

### Risk Factors for Inadequate Preparation (Appendix C)

Across 10 observational studies using multivariate analysis (25,376 participants), preparation was inadequate on average **23.8%** of the time (range 10.3–33%).

| Factor | Finding |
|---|---|
| Older age | OR 1.10 (1.00–1.02 as printed) in the large multicenter predictive model; associated in several studies |
| Male sex | OR 1.2 (1.02–1.15 as printed); occasionally associated |
| Higher body mass index | OR 1.1 (1.03–1.1); associated in 2 of the 7 studies recording it |
| Inpatient status | Significant association in 4 studies |
| ASA class III–V (vs class I) | OR 0.51 (0.32–0.73) for achieving a high-quality preparation |
| **≥8 prescription medications** | OR **6.52** (5.12–8.56) for poor preparation |
| Parkinson's disease | OR 3.2 (1.2–9.3); neurologic conditions with poor mobility (incl. stroke) frequently associated |
| Prior colorectal surgery | OR 1.6 (1.2–2.2); unsatisfactory preparation in **64%** after prior gastric resection and **59.7%** after prior colonic resection despite 4 L PEG-ELS on the morning of the procedure; prior colorectal resection OR 7.5 (3.4–17.6) in one prospective study |
| Diabetes mellitus | Higher proportion of inadequate preparation |
| Tricyclic antidepressants, narcotics | Occasionally seen as risk factors |
| Spinal cord injury | Neurogenic bowel dysfunction reduces effectiveness of traditional purgatives; ≥73% of preparations rated "unacceptable" in one 36-patient study |

- The best available multivariate predictive model (n = 2,811, 18 centers) had only **modest predictive ability when a split-dose regimen was used — area under the receiver operating characteristic curve 0.63** in validation.

### Agent-Specific Safety and Dosing Detail

- **NaP is not recommended** in renal insufficiency (**creatinine clearance <60 mL/min/1.73 m²**), pre-existing electrolyte disturbances, congestive heart failure (**New York Heart Association [NYHA] class III or IV, or ejection fraction <50%**), cirrhosis, or ascites. **Use with caution** in patients who are elderly or hypertensive, or who take angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, or diuretics.
- Risk factors for **NaP-induced acute phosphate nephropathy**: female sex, pre-existing renal insufficiency, inadequate hydration during preparation, **reduced interval between the 2 doses (<12 h)**, hypertension, older age, and the medications above. Oral NaP was withdrawn from the United States OTC market in December 2008; a prescription tablet formulation remains available with a boxed warning.
- NaP (and sodium picosulfate) can cause **mucosal lesions that mimic early IBD** — inflammation was 10-fold greater with NaP than PEG in one trial; aphthoid-like lesions in 24.5% receiving NaP vs 2.3% receiving PEG. The histologic appearance is distinctive and permits differentiation from idiopathic IBD, but the endoscopic changes may resemble [[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease]].
- **PEG-ELS is iso-osmotic** and is therefore often the preferred regimen in patients less able to tolerate fluid shifts — renal insufficiency, congestive heart failure, advanced liver disease.
- **PEG-3350 powder** (OTC, marketed for constipation): an 8.3-oz (238 g) bottle, typically mixed with 64 ounces of a sports drink to create a 2-L PEG formulation. Hyponatremia is a theoretical risk with a hypotonic lavage solution; reported with evening-before but not with split-dose regimens. The FDA has specifically recognized only **2 bowel cleansing kits** as meeting OTC monograph requirements: (i) magnesium citrate oral solution, bisacodyl tablets and bisacodyl suppositories; (ii) magnesium citrate oral solution, phenolphthalein, and sodium bicarbonate–sodium bitartrate suppositories.
- **Magnesium citrate** 300 mL ×3 added to NaP 45 mL ×2 gave good or excellent cleansing in **94% (right) and 97% (left)** colon. A transient rise in serum magnesium may occur. **Avoid magnesium-based preparations in chronic kidney disease**; a PEG-ELS–based regimen is preferred.
- **Senna**: high-dose = 24 tablets of 12 mg each (as effective as 4 L PEG-ELS, but significantly more cramps and abdominal pain); low-dose = 3–12 tablets combined with 2 L PEG-ELS.
- **Simethicone**, the best-studied adjunct: overall efficacy not improved (OR 2.06; 0.56–7.53; *P* = 0.27) despite a marked reduction in intraluminal bubbles (OR 39.3; 11.4–135.9; *P* ≤0.01). Doses studied **120–240 mg, or 45 mL of a 30% solution**.
- **Pediatric detail:** clear liquids for 24 h plus a normal saline enema **10 mL/kg** usually suffices for infants with normal or frequent bowel movements; PEG-3350 **1.5 g/kg/d for 4 days** before the procedure with clear liquids on day 4 is an alternative. Maintaining hydration during preparation is important.
- **Pregnancy:** colonoscopy is rarely indicated; if necessary, defer to the **second trimester** when possible. Both PEG-ELS and NaP are United States pregnancy category C. AGA recommends NaP be avoided; ASGE states NaP should be used with caution owing to possible fluid and electrolyte abnormalities.

### Salvage Regimens Described

- **Through-the-scope enema** (2 studies): after passing the colonoscope as proximally as possible, either a **phosphate enema (133 mL / 19 g)** followed by a **bisacodyl enema (37 mL / 10 mg)**, or **2 bisacodyl enemas**, instilled through the accessory channel — colon "well prepared" in all 21 patients. A **500 mL polyethylene glycol solution** rescue enema instilled at the level of the hepatic flexure through the biopsy channel cleansed **96% (25/26)** successfully.
- **Intensive repeat-preparation regimen** after a previously failed outpatient colonoscopy (51 patients): low-fiber diet for **72 h**, liquid diet the day before, **10 mg bisacodyl with 1.5 L PEG-ELS on the evening before**, and a **second 1.5-L dose of PEG-ELS on the day** of the colonoscopy → **90% (46/51)** adequate (BBPS **≥2 in each segment**).

## Relevance to Wiki
- **[[colonoscopy]]** — the Bowel Preparation section should continue to assert the 2025 positions. This source contributes net-new, non-conflicting material: the **NaP contraindication thresholds** (creatinine clearance, ejection fraction, NYHA class), the **acute phosphate nephropathy risk-factor list** incl. the **<12 h inter-dose interval**, the **pregnancy and pediatric** regimens, the **through-the-scope enema and intensive repeat-preparation salvage regimens with their doses**, and the **Appendix C risk-factor odds ratios**.
- **[[colonoscopy-quality-indicators]]** — historical origin of the adequate-preparation rate as a measured indicator, and of the "improvement initiative below benchmark" expectation. The **85%** figure is historical only; the current target is ≥90% at endoscopist **and** unit level.
- **[[inflammatory-bowel-disease]]** — NaP- and picosulfate-induced mucosal lesions mimicking early IBD, and the recommendation to avoid NaP in known or suspected IBD.
- **[[colonoscopy-surveillance]]** — recommendation 3 of the first section (adequate preparation → follow standard screening/surveillance intervals) and recommendation 2 (repeat within 1 year if inadequate; sooner if advanced neoplasia was also found).
- **[[colorectal-cancer]]** — the link between preparation quality and missed neoplasia: adenoma miss rates **47% / 37%** for advanced adenomas with poor preparation vs **21% / 9%** with excellent preparation (*P* = 0.024); adequate preparation predicted detection of polyps of all sizes (OR 1.73; 1.28–2.36).
- Supplies the **BBPS, Ottawa and Aronchick scoring criteria** that the 2025 document names but does not reproduce.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

Where this 2014 document and [[usmstf-2025-bowel-prep]] differ, the 2025 document is the later tier-1 source and governs the entity pages. Differences, all surfaced here:

| Topic | ACG/USMSTF 2014 | USMSTF 2025 (governs) |
|---|---|---|
| Adequacy benchmark | **≥85%**, per-physician only | **≥90%**, at individual endoscopist **and** endoscopy-unit level |
| Purgative volume | 4 L PEG-ELS split-dose gives high-quality cleansing; 4 L not superior to lower-volume PEG in healthy nonconstipated patients, but **no volume recommendation made** | **Suggests 2 L regimens instead of 4 L** for ambulatory patients at low risk; no purgative held superior to another |
| Adjuncts | **Routine use of adjunctive agents not recommended**; simethicone showed no efficacy benefit | **Suggests adjunctive oral simethicone** (≥320 mg); suggests against routine *non*simethicone adjuncts |
| Repeat after inadequate preparation | **Within 1 year**; shorter when advanced neoplasia was also detected | **Within 12 mo** for screening/surveillance; **as soon as possible (generally within 3 mo)** after an abnormal non-colonoscopic colorectal cancer (CRC) screening test |
| Same-day regimen, morning procedure | Not addressed | Explicitly **inferior** to split dosing (weak, low) |
| Diet | Low-residue **or** full liquids until the evening the day before (weak, moderate) | Low-residue/low-fiber or full liquids for the **early and midday meals** the day before, with modifications **limited to the day before** (strong, high) |
| Irrigation pumps | Not addressed | **Suggested** for routine use during colonoscopy (weak, very low) |
| BBPS adequacy cut-point | The scale's developers recommended **a priori that a total score <5 is inadequate**; the 2014 text cites BBPS ≥5 as the operating threshold | **Every segment scoring 2 or 3 = adequate**; a total **<6 caused by any segment <2 = inadequate** |

- **The BBPS cut-point is the one clinically live conflict** — a total of 5 made up of segment scores 3/2/0 is adequate by the 2014 threshold and inadequate by the 2025 rule. Use the 2025 segment-based rule.
- 2014 states that aborting for inadequate preparation assigns the unseen **proximal segments a score of 0**; 2025 instead asks the endoscopist to **photograph the segment that caused the abortion** and to count cancelled cases in the adequacy rate. Complementary rather than conflicting.
- ACG 2014 does **not** set a numeric threshold for how much residual stool makes a segment inadequate, beyond the ">5 mm lesion" visibility test.
- No conflict with [[asge-2015-bowel-preparation]] on the preparation-quality scales; both reproduce the Aronchick and Ottawa criteria and both grade preparation after washing and suctioning.
