## Summary

Practical commentary on submucosal lifting (injection) agents for endoscopic resection (endoscopic mucosal resection [EMR]/endoscopic submucosal dissection [ESD]/peroral endoscopic myotomy [POEM]). Submucosal injection creates a cushion separating the lesion from the muscularis propria (MP); adding a blue contrast agent demarcates margins and helps detect MP injury during EMR.

## Key Findings / Claims (narrative)

- **Purpose:** cushion separates lesion from MP; contrast (**indigo carmine** or **methylene blue**) demarcates lesion margins and signals MP injury.
- **Agent choice:** "homemade" normal saline (NS) ± contrast remains acceptable; saline dissipates quickly, prompting viscous solutions (VSs). **Cold snare polypectomy *without* submucosal injection** was non-inferior to resection methods that used submucosal injection, for non-pedunculated colorectal lesions (NPCRLs) **≤15 mm**.
- **Sessile serrated lesions (SSLs):** minimal submucosal fibrosis — lift adequately with **any** solution, including saline; cold EMR (EMR without electrocautery) is ideal for them. SSLs **do not always require injection**, especially **<20 mm** or when margins are clearly visible without it.
- **ESD requires submucosal injection regardless of anatomic location**; injecting into the lesion is avoided during ESD to reduce obscuring submucosal bleeding **and to minimize seeding risk if cancer is present**. **POEM** typically uses normal saline with a blue contrast agent, instilled with water.
- **Adjuncts:** dilute **epinephrine** added for cold EMR and EMR with electrocautery to reduce bleeding/improve visualization.
- **Technique:** prime the needle catheter (avoid air injection); central injection domes the lesion over submucosal fluid; **dynamic injection** (moving the needle within the submucosa) shapes the mound and maximizes separation from MP; larger lesions resected by serial inject-and-snare.
- Papillectomy generally does not require injection (saline with contrast may reassure against MP involvement). **Underwater EMR** is an alternative to conventional inject-and-resect EMR and uses **no** submucosal injection.

### The two explicit directives

Both are stated as what clinicians should do; the document assigns **no evidence grade or strength to either**, and numbers none of its statements.

- **Consider NS with a contrast agent** when performing polypectomy of intermediate-size NPCRLs or gastric polyps (**10–19 mm**) and POEM.
- **Avoid NS with a contrast medium as the *only* agent** when performing ESD anywhere in the gastrointestinal tract — NS-based agents dissipate too rapidly, so a viscous agent is important during ESD, especially for the initial lift and incision.

### Where injection is optional, preferred, or first-line (US Multi-Society Task Force positions quoted by the update)

| Lesion | Position |
|---|---|
| NPCRL, intermediate size **10–19 mm** | Submucosal injection **optional** |
| NPCRL **≥20 mm** | **EMR first-line therapy**; VSs **preferred** (resection in less time, fewer pieces) |
| NPCRL **≤15 mm** | Cold snare polypectomy **without** injection non-inferior to injection-based methods |
| Pedunculated polyps | Lifting agent **at the endoscopist's discretion** |

### Injection strategy by lesion size and visibility (three strategies)

| Situation | Strategy |
|---|---|
| Moderately sized flat lesion **10–30 mm**, entirely visible | **Central injection through the lesion**, so the lesion centers on a dome of submucosal fluid. Contraindicated **only** if features suggesting cancer are present |
| Sessile lesion, or a dominant nodule present | Start on the **most accessible side** of the lesion |
| Lesion **>30–40 mm** | Raise **a portion**, snare-resect it, then repeat systematically from one side to the other — in most cases **rectal side → cecal side**, to keep the field clear |
| Significant portion **not visible** (e.g. on the cecal side of a haustral fold) | **Proximal (cecal) edge injection** first, facilitated by retroflexion |

- Retroflexion is generally safe with a colonoscope in the **cecum, ascending colon, transverse colon and rectum**; in the **sigmoid and descending colon it is safer to use an upper endoscope**.

### ESD lifting differs from EMR

- Foregut lesions: place **thermal markings 2–5 mm** around the lesion.
- **Start the injection *outside* the thermal markings** — that is where the incision will start.
- After incision and "trimming" of the muscularis mucosa, direct the **dynamic lift** into the submucosal space before dissection; precision limits injection into vessels and the MP.
- Most Western endoscopists use VSs for ESD; NS-based solutions can be used with or without a water-jet/pump system.

### Access and needle choice

- Submucosal space is easier to access in the **small bowel** than the colorectum.
- **Submucosal fibrosis** — from prior partial resection, biopsy, or tattoo — makes access difficult and can create an **iatrogenic non-lifting sign**. A **thinner needle (25-gauge vs 23-gauge)** may facilitate access.
- With inexpensive agents (saline, hydroxyethyl starch), begin fluid injection first and advance the needle as it flows; with expensive agents, identify the submucosa first by inserting the needle into a thin portion of lesion or adjacent mucosa, lift toward the lumen until a small bleb develops, then inject the rest. Approach the lesion **tangentially, not en face**.

### Contrast agents and muscularis propria injury

- Contrast stains the submucosa **blue**; the **MP remains white**.
- **Thick parallel white bands indicate type 3 MP injury** → **reinforce with clips** to prevent delayed perforation.

### Lift duration and comparative performance

- For colonic EMR, lift duration was **shortest with NS plus epinephrine (<3 minutes)** and **longest with hyaluronic acid (22 minutes)**.
- Meta-analysis: NS produced **fewer en bloc resections and higher rates of residual lesion** than VSs for colorectal EMR, with **no difference in adverse events**.
- VSs give higher complete resection, higher en bloc resection and lower residual polyp rates, again with **no difference in adverse events**.
- Hydroxyethyl starch vs NS for lesions **≥30 mm**: less volume, longer-lasting lift, lower total procedure time.
- The first United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved agent vs NS for NPCRLs ≥20 mm (randomized, double-blind): outperformed NS on **total volume needed to complete EMR** and **volume for initial lift**; **no difference** in reinjections, time to resect, total procedure time, or adverse events.
- **An ideal lifting solution — readily available, inexpensive, pre-mixed, sustained cushion — does not currently exist.** Select by planned resection method, predicted histology, local expertise and preference, and cost.

### Device-assisted lifting

- The **ERBEJET 2** provides an adjustable high-pressure water jet; commonly used for POEM, and usable during submucosal tunneling and ESD.
- With a HybridKnife it uses hydrosurgery to create lift **and** tissue separation; may be most beneficial in **third-space endoscopy, especially with submucosal fibrosis**.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **Narrative commentary — the document states plainly that no formal rating of quality of evidence or strength of recommendations was performed**, and it numbers none of its statements. Nothing on this page should be cited as graded.
- **No clear superiority of proprietary agents over saline for routine EMR of most lesions.** VSs win on en bloc and residual-lesion rates in meta-analysis, but adverse events do not differ and the head-to-head randomized trial against NS found differences only in injected volume. Agent choice is driven by lesion size and fibrosis, predicted histology, local expertise, and cost.
- **Two of the listed agents are not usable in the United States** — succinylated gelatin is not available, and glycerol has limited availability — yet both occupy rows in the agent table.
- **Epinephrine's own trade-off is unresolved:** it gives a cleaner field and less intraprocedural oozing, but serial injections of dilute epinephrine into the submucosa **may cause post-procedure abdominal pain**, which is then hard to distinguish from perforation or post-polypectomy syndrome in the first hour.
- **Two agents were withdrawn or abandoned for harms discovered after approval**, which is the reason to read the table as current rather than permanent: 50% dextrose caused a painful inflammatory response resembling post-coagulation syndrome when injected into the pericolonic mesentery, and ORISE was removed from the world market in **December 2022** after distorted post-resection scars were misread as submucosal tumor.
