## Summary

Practical commentary on submucosal lifting (injection) agents for endoscopic resection (EMR/ESD/POEM). Submucosal injection creates a cushion separating the lesion from the muscularis propria (MP); adding a blue contrast agent demarcates margins and helps detect MP injury during EMR.

## Key Findings / Claims (narrative)

- **Purpose:** cushion separates lesion from MP; contrast (**indigo carmine** or **methylene blue**) demarcates lesion margins and signals MP injury.
- **Agent choice:** "homemade" normal saline ± contrast remains acceptable; saline dissipates quickly, prompting viscous/FDA-approved agents (the first FDA-approved agent and later proprietary gels). In a non-inferiority context, saline was non-inferior to other injection-based resection methods for relevant outcomes.
- **Sessile serrated lesions (SSLs):** minimal submucosal fibrosis — lift adequately with **any** solution, including saline.
- **ESD requires submucosal injection regardless of agent**; injecting into the lesion is avoided during ESD to reduce obscuring submucosal bleeding. **POEM** typically uses normal saline.
- **Adjuncts:** dilute **epinephrine** added for cold EMR and EMR with electrocautery to reduce bleeding/improve visualization.
- **Technique:** prime the needle catheter (avoid air injection); central injection domes the lesion over submucosal fluid; **dynamic injection** (moving the needle within the submucosa) shapes the mound and maximizes separation from MP; larger lesions resected by serial inject-and-snare.
- Papillectomy generally does not require injection (saline with contrast may reassure against MP involvement).

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- Commentary (no formal ratings). No clear superiority of proprietary agents over saline for routine EMR of most lesions; agent choice driven by lesion size/fibrosis, predicted histology, and endoscopist preference.
