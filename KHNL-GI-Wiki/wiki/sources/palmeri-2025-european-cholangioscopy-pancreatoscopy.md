Corresponding author: Pieter Hindryckx (Ghent). Study run on behalf of the European Cholangioscopy Group.
- **Year:** 2025 (received 26 May 2025; revised 13 July 2025; accepted 28 July 2025)
- **Journal/Publisher:** *United European Gastroenterology Journal* vol. 13, issue 9, pp. 1652–1667 (labelled "Guideline"); Wiley Periodicals LLC on behalf of United European Gastroenterology

- **Type:** consensus statement — European expert modified Delphi consensus (expert group, not issued by a society; non-US)
- **Method (as printed, pp. 2–3):**
  - Statements drafted from a systematic PubMed/Cochrane search (up to July 2024) by five authors; evidence for each statement scored with the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) system — high, moderate, low or very low quality.
  - Panel: European cholangioscopy experts chosen by European cholangioscopy group membership and/or scientific track record. **32 invited Europe-based experts agreed; 30/32 (96.9%) completed** the process.
  - Online survey (SurveyPlanet); each statement shown with its key references and GRADE score; **agree or disagree**, with rationale and suggested wording when disagreeing.
  - **Acceptance threshold 80%.** Below threshold → revised from anonymized comments and re-voted next round; **rejected if no consensus after three consecutive rounds**.
  - Three rounds. Round 3 modified one statement (S13, for possible misinterpretation of the original) and added two (S14, S35). Mean acceptance rate **93.27% ± 5.38%**.
  - Raw round transcripts (Supporting Information S1, Appendix A) and the background to each recommendation (Supporting Information S2, Appendix B) are online supplements; neither is on file.

## Summary
- Digital single-operator cholangioscopy (D-SOC) and pancreatoscopy (D-SOP) are now used across many pancreaticobiliary disorders. The two major indications are **direct lithotripsy of large biliary or pancreatic stones** and **diagnostic evaluation and sampling of biliary strictures**; others are selective wire cannulation (intrahepatic branches, cystic duct), retrieval of migrated stents, and preoperative assessment of cholangiocarcinoma or intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm (IPMN). Earlier consensus papers covered single indications; this one adds general and purely technical "how-to" statements.
- **Process:** 43 statements generated; **42 accepted** (Figure 1: 42/43, 97.7%), 1 rejected (peri-procedural simethicone use). Statements are numbered **S1–S42** and grouped into three tables: general (Table 1, S1–S20), biliopancreatic stones (Table 2, S21–S28), biliary strictures (Table 3, S29–S42).
- **Key positions:** basic simulator/hands-on training and endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) proficiency before cholangioscopy; **routine broad-spectrum antibiotic prophylaxis**; **rectal nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) for all** without a strict contraindication; sphincterotomy or sphincteroplasty first; **avoid intraductal CO₂**; low-pressure pancreatic duct irrigation; cholangioscopy-guided **laser or electrohydraulic lithotripsy** after failed conventional extraction; a new split between **"unexplained"** and **"indeterminate"** biliary strictures, with **D-SOC in the initial workup of unexplained strictures** where expertise exists; Mendoza criteria for visual assessment; **≥3 targeted biopsies**; re-sample if the stricture looks suspicious but biopsies are negative; Edmonton criteria and IgG4 staining in primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC); pre- or intraoperative pancreatoscopy in main-duct IPMN.
- Most statements rest on very low- or low-quality evidence; the paper names this as its main limitation, hence the cautious wording ("it is suggested", "may be considered") (p. 11).

- **Discussion points (p. 6):**
  - Cholangioscopy only by physicians experienced in ERCP or interventional radiology; simulators exist, so decent hands-on training before patients.
  - Cholangioscopy or pancreatoscopy during ERCP increases cholangitis and pancreatitis risk → strong consensus for every prophylactic measure: periprocedural antibiotics, preprocedural NSAIDs, sphincterotomy, balanced irrigation and suction, low-pressure pancreatic duct irrigation.
  - Depending on the manufacturer, locked cholangioscope steering wheels still allow steering while stabilizing the tip after each maneuver, giving a stable position in front of the target (stone, stricture).

- **Difficult stones (Discussion, p. 6, citing the American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2019 choledocholithiasis guideline):** stones **larger than 10 mm**, **impacted** stones, or stones in anatomically challenging locations such as the **cystic duct**. S21–S22 are framed as more precise guidance after unsuccessful conventional extraction or for such difficult stones.
- **Single-session cholangioscopy-assisted lithotripsy: 94.1% ductal clearance** in such cases (pooled from the cited literature; p. 6).
- **LL vs EHL (p. 7):** data, more robust for biliary stones, show **better outcomes and a better safety profile with LL than EHL**; LL needs additional resources.
- **Mirizzi syndrome (pp. 7, 9):** multicenter SEIZE study of D-SOC in **type II–IV** Mirizzi syndrome — technical success **89.8%**; severe adverse events **1.7% vs 15.8%** with surgery; less need for complex biliary reconstruction. No statement was voted on Mirizzi syndrome.

- **Not defined in the article:** the items of the **Mendoza criteria** (S37), the **Edmonton criteria** (S40), "dominant stricture" (S32, S40), and the pancreatoscopy classification systems (S35). The article names them without listing their components; the background text is in the online supplement.
- **Discussion (pp. 9–10):**
  - Unlike previous guidelines, D-SOC enters the **initial** workup of unexplained strictures, **not only after failed conventional techniques**; the term "indeterminate" had been used inconsistently.
  - RCT (Gerges): D-SOC-guided biopsy sensitivity **68.2% vs 21.4%** for ERCP-guided brushing (p < 0.01); visual impression **95.5% vs 66.7%** (p = 0.02); overall accuracy **87.1% vs 65.5%** (p = 0.05); adverse-event rate comparable to ERCP. Draganov: cholangioscopy-guided mini-forceps biopsy sensitivity **76.5%** vs **29.4%** standard forceps and **5.9%** brushings, overall accuracy **84.6%**. Almadi (prospective): complication rate **1.7%**.
  - Visual interpretation alone is limited (interobserver variability, benign–malignant overlap), especially in PSC and after stenting → combine with targeted biopsy; the Mendoza classification improves interobserver agreement and accuracy over earlier systems.
  - **Number of biopsies is contested:** a Portuguese Society of Digestive Endoscopy consensus recommends **six**; Bang et al. found **at least three** single-operator cholangioscopy-guided biopsies analyzed off-site gave **90%** accuracy in indeterminate strictures, with no difference between three and four (p = 0.999). 10% buffered formalin chosen for high cellularity and architectural preservation.
  - Suspicious appearance + negative biopsies → re-sample to minimize false negatives.

### Other indications — Discussion (pp. 10–11)
- **MD-IPMN (S11):** pre- or intraoperative pancreatoscopy defines lesion extent and skip lesions; across five retrospective studies (systematic review and meta-analysis) it **changed the surgical approach in 13%–62%** — **more extensive** resection in **13%–31%**, **less extensive** in **6%–31%**.
- **Selective cannulation, haemobilia, proximally migrated stents (S9, S13, S14):** cholangioscopy plays **only a secondary role** to standard ERCP techniques, depends on endoscopist expertise and preference, and rests on a few case reports.
- **Percutaneous cholangioscopy (S6–S8):** ancillary in altered anatomy, for stricture characterization and stone disease.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

### Inconsistencies within the document
- **Round-1 acceptance:** section 3.1 gives **39/43 (90.7%)** accepted in round 1; Figure 1 gives **41/43 (95.3%)**; the abstract gives **39 (92.9%)** [of 42].
- **Final total:** abstract **42 (97.6%)**; Figure 1 **42/43 (97.7%)**.
- **Category counts:** abstract — 12 pre/periprocedural, 8 stones, 13 strictures, 9 other indications; tables — 20 general (S1–S20), 8 stones (S21–S28), 14 strictures (S29–S42). Section 3.4 says 13 stricture statements were generated, though Table 3 holds 14 (S35 is a pancreatoscopy statement). Methods name four categories, the abstract five.
- **Rejection rule vs practice:** methods reject a statement only after three rounds without consensus; the simethicone statement is reported as rejected after round 1 (section 3.1; Figure 1 "1 statement omitted").
- **Round of the modified stricture statement:** section 3.4 places the IgG4 statement (S41) as accepted after modification in round 2; section 3.1 places S13's modification and the added S14 and S35 in round 3.
- **Mislabelled statement in the Discussion:** the unanimous LL-or-EHL statement is cited as "S22"; in Table 2 it is **S23** (S22 is pancreatoscopy-assisted lithotripsy selection).
- **Force softened vs Discussion:** the Discussion says the panel "recommended" over-the-wire cholangioscope introduction; **S12** says it "may be preferred … depending on" sphincterotomy size, CBD diameter and expertise.
- **S29 and S30 passed at exactly the 80% threshold.**
- **Biopsy number:** S38 says at least three; the Discussion cites a Portuguese consensus recommending six and calls the evidence conflicting.

### Differences from US sources on file
- **Status:** non-US (all-European) expert Delphi, not a society guideline. US guidelines win where they conflict; this source fills gaps only.
- **Who gets cholangioscopic biopsy for a stricture** — [[asge-2023-indeterminate-biliary-strictures|ASGE 2023]] Rec 2 (conditional, very low quality) suggests cholangioscopic-guided biopsy when the stricture is (a) **nondistal** with a high probability of adequate drainage of the critical liver segment **or** (b) after a previous nondiagnostic ERCP without cholangioscopy, **and** (c) at centers with expertise and easy access; otherwise ERCP with or without cholangioscopy. ASGE also notes cholangioscopy is less accurate in the very distal duct. This source (S30, 80%) puts D-SOC into the **initial** workup of **unexplained** strictures wherever expertise exists, with no distal/nondistal or drainage condition. ASGE 2023 wins on the US page.
- **Terminology** — ASGE 2023 uses "biliary strictures of undetermined etiology"; this source splits **unexplained** (before standard ERCP sampling) from **indeterminate** (after it) (S29). Not a conflict, but the populations are not interchangeable.
- **Antibiotic prophylaxis** — [[acg-asge-2026-ercp-quality-indicators|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG)/ASGE 2026]] lists cholangioscopy among the three scenarios where literature most supports prophylaxis — agrees with S3; but it also says **no data support routine prophylaxis for pancreatoscopy — individualize**. S3 covers cholangioscopy only, so no direct conflict; Figure 2's "Antibiotics" box sits before every branch, including pancreatoscopy. [[asge-2026-ercp-adverse-events|ASGE 2026]] calls the cholangioscopy prophylaxis evidence conflicting; S3 grades it moderate (with the table's own footnote that only one prospective study tested it directly).
- **Rectal NSAIDs** — [[asge-2023-post-ercp-pancreatitis|ASGE 2023]] recommends rectal NSAIDs in unselected patients undergoing ERCP; [[acg-asge-2026-ercp-quality-indicators|ACG/ASGE 2026]] measures it in patients with an **intact papilla** and treats repeat biliary intervention through a prior sphincterotomy as negligible risk. S4 says **all patients** before cholangioscopy or pancreatoscopy, without that exclusion. Direction agrees.
- **Difficult bile duct stones** — [[asge-2019-choledocholithiasis|ASGE 2019]] Rec 4 offers intraductal therapy **or** conventional therapy with papillary dilation as alternatives for large and difficult stones (as summarized on [[cholangioscopy]]); [[asge-2015-ercp-benign-biliary|ASGE 2015]] Rec 6 places cholangioscopy after failed sphincterotomy ± balloon dilation or mechanical lithotripsy. S21 (after failed conventional extraction, or when it is not considered feasible) is consistent with both.
- **Pancreatic duct stones** — [[asge-2024-chronic-pancreatitis-endoscopy|ASGE 2024]] chooses between ERCP ± pancreatoscopy and extracorporeal shock wave lithotripsy (ESWL) by stone size, location and radiopacity, and notes pancreatoscopy lithotripsy is less efficient than ESWL for stones >10 mm. S22 names no size cutoff and does not mention ESWL. No conflict; ASGE 2024 carries the decision criteria.
- **PSC** — [[aga-2019-psc-cancer-surveillance|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2019]] Best Practice Advice 4: ERCP with brush cytology ± fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) and/or cholangioscopy for worsening symptoms, worsening cholestasis or a dominant stricture. S32 agrees. This source does not define "dominant stricture"; use the definition on [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis]].
- **MD-IPMN pancreatoscopy** — [[iap-2024-kyoto-ipmn|Kyoto 2024]] (international, cited by this paper) says pancreatoscopy is not routine, risks overdiagnosis, and **intraoperative** pancreatoscopy has limited accuracy and risks peritoneal dissemination — **if needed, do it preoperatively**. S11 allows **pre- or intraoperative** pancreatoscopy. Both are non-US; no US source on file addresses pancreatoscopy in IPMN.
- **Intraductal gas** — S17 (avoid intraductal CO₂ during any cholangioscopy) is broader than the wiki's existing pitfall, which switches off air/CO₂ during direct peroral cholangioscopy (sourced to [[bsg-2017-common-bile-duct-stones|British Society of Gastroenterology (BSG) 2017]], also non-US). Same direction.
