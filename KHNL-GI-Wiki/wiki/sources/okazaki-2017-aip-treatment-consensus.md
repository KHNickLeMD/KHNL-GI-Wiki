## Summary

The treatment companion to [[shimosegawa-2011-icdc-autoimmune-pancreatitis|ICDC 2011]], produced by a modified Delphi process at the 20th International Association of Pancreatology meeting (2016) from a PubMed review spanning 1963–2016. It answers **nine clinical questions (CQ-1 to CQ-9)** with short consensus statements, each carrying a **recommendation level A, B or C** — the panel states it could not apply a system such as GRADE to some statements and used a United States Preventive Services Task Force–style level instead.

It supplies what the diagnostic criteria do not: **who to treat, what to start, how far to taper, how long, who needs maintenance, who will relapse, and what to do when they do.** The operative numbers are **prednisone 0.6–1.0 mg/kg/day** (minimum **20 mg/day**) for induction, taper **5–10 mg every 1–2 weeks to 20 mg/day, then 5 mg every 2 weeks**, **total induction ~12 weeks**, and **low-dose maintenance 2.5–7.5 mg/day for up to 3 years** in selected type 1 patients.

Two framing points run through it. **Relapse is a type 1 problem** — 31% of type 1 vs 9% of type 2 in the international cohort — and most relapses happen **after steroids stop** (67%), not during the taper (15%) or on maintenance (18%). And **the asymptomatic patient is often not treated at all**: 10%–25% of patients improve spontaneously, so watchful waiting suits most asymptomatic cases.

The panel states the recommendations are based on available evidence plus eastern and western expert opinion, and **can be tailored to local expertise and insurance context**.

## Key Findings / Claims

### CQ-1. Indications for treatment
- **A. Symptomatic patients are an indication for treatment** (level B): **pancreatic** — obstructive jaundice, abdominal pain, back pain; **other organ involvement (OOI)** — e.g. jaundice from a bile-duct stricture.
- **B. Asymptomatic patients are an indication for treatment** (level B) when there is: **a persistent pancreatic mass on imaging**, or **persistent liver test abnormalities with associated IgG4-related sclerosing cholangitis (IgG4-SC)**.
- **Watchful waiting may be appropriate in most other asymptomatic patients** — about **10%–25% improve spontaneously** without any intervention.
- Urgent indications: proximal biliary strictures causing superimposed infectious cholangitis; irreversible hepatic fibrosis/cirrhosis; pancreatic involvement heading to irreversible exocrine and endocrine failure.

### CQ-2. Best approach to induction of remission
- **Steroid is the first-line agent in all patients with active untreated AIP unless contraindicated** (level A).
- **Where steroids are contraindicated, rituximab can induce remission as a single agent** (level B).
- **Other than rituximab, steroid-sparing agents such as thiopurines are poorly effective as single agents for induction** (level C).
- Remission rates: **98%** steroid-treated vs 88% untreated (Japanese national study); **99.6% in type 1 (n = 684)** and **92.3% in type 2 (n = 52)** internationally.
- Faster treatment may give more complete remission with fewer long-term complications in obstructive jaundice or organ-threatening OOI.
- **Steroid mini-pulse** for refractory cases: **2 courses of methylprednisolone 500 mg × 3 days, 4 days apart**.
- **Reassess imaging and serology within 1–2 weeks of starting steroid.** Poor response → **re-evaluate the diagnosis, including pancreatic cancer**.

### CQ-3. Biliary drainage before treatment in obstructive jaundice
- **Biliary drainage is useful to prevent biliary infection**, and **brushing/cytology can differentiate IgG4-SC from biliary malignancy** (level B).
- **In mild jaundice without signs of infection, steroid treatment alone can be performed safely without biliary stenting** (level B).
- **71% of type 1 and 77% of type 2** patients with jaundice achieved remission **without** a stent. Initial prednisolone dose did not differ between the steroid-alone (0.60 ± 0.12 mg/kg/day) and drainage-plus-steroid (0.60 ± 0.17 mg/kg/day) groups.

### CQ-4. Minimum starting dose for induction
- **Prednisone 0.6–1.0 mg/kg/day should be started** (level A).
- **A minimum of 20 mg/day is generally necessary to induce remission** (level B) — below 20 mg/day the remission rate suffers.
- The initial dose runs **2–4 weeks** before tapering. High-dose corticosteroid (~30–40 mg/day) gives more rapid and fair induction than conservative management.

### CQ-5. How to taper
- **Usually tapered by 5–10 mg/day every 1–2 weeks until 20 mg/day, then 5 mg every 2 weeks** (level B).
- **Another acceptable regimen: 40 mg/day × 4 weeks, then taper by 5 mg/week until off** (level B).
- **Total remission treatment should generally last 12 weeks** (level A).
- **Very short induction (<4 weeks) with a high dose of ≥20 mg is not recommended** (level C).
- Taper is guided by clinical manifestations, biochemistry (liver enzymes, IgG/IgG4) and repeat imaging (ultrasound, computed tomography, magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography, endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography).

### CQ-6. Is maintenance treatment useful?
- **Type 1 with low disease activity before treatment, and all type 2, do not need maintenance** (level C).
- **After successful induction, maintenance with low-dose glucocorticoids or steroid-sparing agents may be useful in some type 1 patients** (level B).
- **Low-dose maintenance = 2.5–7.5 mg/day, for up to 3 years**; stopping is planned once there is radiological and serological improvement. Duration remains debated.
- **Stop at ≤3 months with no maintenance** when disease activity is low: pancreas-only segmental/focal lesion, no OOI, complete radiological remission with normalized IgG/IgG4, rapid steroid response.
- **Give maintenance** (low-dose steroid, immunomodulator or rituximab) in type 1 with: **diffuse pancreatic enlargement · delayed radiological remission · persistently high serum IgG4 (>2× ULN) after treatment · ≥2 OOI · proximal IgG4-SC before treatment**.
- Maintenance monotherapy data: **23% relapse on maintenance vs 34% without** (Japan); 33% relapse without maintenance (South Korea).

### CQ-7. Can we predict who will relapse?
- **"Risk factors for relapsing remain poorly understood."**
- **Predictors of relapse** (level B): **remarkably high pre-treatment serum IgG4 (e.g. >4× ULN)** · **persistently high serum IgG4 after steroid treatment** · **diffuse enlargement of the pancreas** · **proximal type of IgG4-SC** · **extensive multi-organ involvement (≥2 OOI)**.
- **Timing:** of relapses after starting steroids, **56% within 1 year, 76% within 2 years, 92% within 3 years**.
- **Type 1 vs type 2:** 31% of 978 type 1 relapsed at least once vs **8 of 90 (9%)** type 2. Type 1 relapses occur in the biliary system or pancreas; **type 2 relapses are limited to the pancreas**.
- **Relative to steroid course:** 67% of relapses after discontinuation, 15% during taper, 18% on maintenance.
- IgG4-SC is the strongest single marker in the international data: **56.1% of 171 with IgG4-related SC relapsed vs 25.7% of 551 without**. **69% of relapsed patients showed re-elevation of serum IgG4 before the relapse.**
- ⚠ The Japanese and international studies **disagree** on persistently abnormal IgG4: Japan found 30% vs 10% relapse by IgG4 status; the international study found **no difference** (32.7% vs 31.4%).

### CQ-8. How should relapse be treated?
- **No "gold standard"; steroids and steroid-sparing agents (immunomodulators or rituximab) are useful** (level B).
- **Steroids:** re-administer or increase the dose. Most relapses remit on **the same prednisolone dose as the initial course**, though the taper may need to be slower.
- **Immunomodulators** — thiopurines (azathioprine, 6-mercaptopurine), mycophenolate mofetil, methotrexate. **Thiopurines and mycophenolate require 6–8 weeks of overlap with steroids**, with the disease in remission when steroids are withdrawn. **They are not effective as single agents for induction.** Azathioprine added for relapse induced remission in **56 of 68 (85%)**, maintained in 86%.
- **Rituximab** works in IgG4-related disease including type 1 AIP that is resistant to or intolerant of steroids and immunomodulators, and **may be used as a single agent even in active disease** — but **jaundiced patients may need 4–6 weeks of steroids first**, because rituximab's onset is delayed.
- **Hepatitis B reactivation:** patients who are **anti-HBs or anti-HBc positive but HBs-antigen negative should be monitored with HBV DNA** during rituximab, immunomodulator, steroid or anti-cancer therapy.

### CQ-9. Is surgical treatment useful?
- **Steroids or alternative medication should be tried first, but surgery may be useful in some refractory cases** (level B).
- Palliative pancreatic resection or bypass has achieved remission with rare relapse; the realistic indication is **poor response to medical treatment where long-term biliary stenting would otherwise be needed for continuing obstructive jaundice**.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **Persistently elevated IgG4 after treatment as a relapse predictor is contradicted within this document.** The consensus statement lists it (level B), citing Japanese data (30% vs 10%); the international multicentre study it also cites found **no difference** (32.7% vs 31.4%). The statement stands as written, but the predictor is weaker than the list implies.
- **Maintenance therapy has no high-level evidence** — the panel says so. The low-dose band (2.5–7.5 mg/day) and the up-to-3-years ceiling are Japanese expert practice, and the duration is openly "still debating".
- **Eastern vs western practice differ on steroid-sparing agents** — more commonly used in western countries; Japanese use is constrained by insurance regulation, which the document notes affects the recommendations themselves.
- **No contradiction with [[aga-2019-autoimmune-pancreatitis]]**, which is newer and governs the wiki's management page where the two overlap; this consensus supplies the dosing detail AGA 2019 omits rather than disputing it.
