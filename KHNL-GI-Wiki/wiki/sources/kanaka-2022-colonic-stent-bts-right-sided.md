## Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis asking whether **preoperative colonic self-expanding metal stent (SEMS) decompression followed by elective resection — "bridge to surgery" (BTS)** — improves short-term surgical outcomes over **emergency one-stage resection (ER)** specifically in **right-sided** malignant large bowel obstruction (MLBO). Seven comparative studies published 2014–2020, **5136 patients** (BTS 1662 / 32.4%; ER 3474 / 67.6%). Five studies from Asia, two from the Netherlands; **six retrospective, one prospective observational — no RCTs**.

The clinical question exists because the two sides of the colon have been treated as different problems. BTS is established for **left-sided** MLBO and, as this paper's introduction reports, was recommended for left-sided obstruction in the **ESGE 2020 guideline update**. Right-sided MLBO has instead been managed by emergency resection with primary anastomosis on the traditional assumption that this is safe — an assumption resting on older data. Right-sided disease is not rare: it accounts for **32–54% of MLBO cases**, yet only **~5%** of reported BTS cases involve the right colon.

**The result favours BTS on every outcome that was significant.** Postoperative complications fell from 31.3% to 19.3% (OR 0.78, 95% CI 0.66–0.92) and postoperative mortality from 5.2% to 0.9% (OR 0.51, 95% CI 0.28–0.92). BTS patients were far more likely to get a primary anastomosis (97.8% vs 85.9%), far less likely to receive a stoma (2.0% vs 11.0%), and about three times as likely to have a laparoscopic rather than open operation (48.5% vs 15.7%).

**Read the effect sizes against the evidence grade, which the authors themselves rate as very low to low.** GRADE was **very low** for postoperative complications, primary anastomosis, anastomotic leakage, surgical site infection and ileus, and **low** for mortality, stoma construction and laparoscopic surgery. Mean MINORS score was 14.57 ± 1.72 ("fair" for non-randomized studies). Crucially, **severe complications did not differ** (Clavien–Dindo ≥III or requiring reintervention: OR 0.98, 95% CI 0.68–1.41) — so the complication benefit sits in the milder end of the distribution. The authors' own dominant concern is **selection bias toward "easy-to-stent cases"**: right-sided SEMS is technically harder (tumour site in the right colon independently predicted technical difficulty, OR 2.5, 95% CI 1.61–4.01, P<0.0001, in a cited Japanese post hoc analysis), so the patients who got a stent may be a favourable subset.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Population and definitions**

- **BTS** = preoperative SEMS decompression, then **elective** resection. **ER** = emergency one-stage tumour resection.
- **Inclusion:** (1) acute malignant proximal/right-sided colonic obstruction, and (2) a comparative study reporting morbidity and mortality for BTS vs ER.
- **"Right-sided" was defined inconsistently across the seven studies** — cecum to hepatic flexure (1 study), to proximal transverse colon (1), or **to the entire transverse colon (5)**. This qualifier travels with every number below: most of the pooled data treats the whole transverse colon as "right-sided."
- Interval from SEMS to surgery ranged across studies from **7.5 to 28.1 days** (medians).

**Primary outcomes**

| Outcome | BTS | ER | OR (95% CI) | Heterogeneity | GRADE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Postoperative complications | **19.3%** (320/1662) | 31.3% (1088/3474) | **0.78 (0.66–0.92)** | χ²=9.35, I²=36% | ⊕OOO very low |
| Postoperative mortality | **0.9%** (15/1662) | 5.2% (181/3474) | **0.51 (0.28–0.92)** | χ²=1.88, I²=0% | ⊕⊕OO low |
| **Severe complications** (Clavien–Dindo ≥III or requiring reintervention) | — | — | **0.98 (0.68–1.41) — NS** | 5 of 7 studies graded severity | — |

- No significant publication bias for either primary outcome (complications: Egger P=0.169, Begg P=0.179; mortality: Egger P=0.749, Begg P=0.497).
- Absolute effect for complications per GRADE profile: **51 fewer per 1000** (range 18–82 fewer).

**Other short-term outcomes (Table 4, verbatim)**

| Outcome | No. studies | Rates % (BTS : ER) | OR | 95% CI | P | χ² | I² (%) | P (het) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary anastomosis | 5 | **97.8 : 85.9** | 0.31 | 0.10–0.96 | 0.04 | 0.11 | 0 | 0.95 |
| Stoma construction | 6 | **2.0 : 11.0** | 0.45 | 0.25–0.83 | <0.01 | 5.09 | 21 | 0.28 |
| Laparoscopic surgery | 7 | **48.5 : 15.7** | 0.21 | 0.10–0.42 | <0.01 | 18.36 | 67 | <0.01 |
| Anastomotic leakage | 6 | **2.6 : 5.6** | 0.66 | 0.45–0.96 | 0.03 | 0.55 | 0 | 0.99 |
| Surgical site infection | 6 | **4.6 : 5.8** | 0.62 | 0.46–0.82 | <0.01 | 4.37 | 0 | 0.50 |
| Ileus | 6 | 6.7 : 3.1 | 1.28 | 0.96–1.71 | 0.09 | 4.87 | 0 | 0.43 |

- **Ileus is the one signal pointing the other way** — numerically more frequent after BTS (6.7% vs 3.1%), not statistically significant.
- Laparoscopic-surgery result carries **substantial heterogeneity (I²=67%, P<0.01)** and should be read as the weakest of the significant findings.

**Timing of surgery after stenting**

- Subgroup analysis split at the **median 20-day** interval. Reduction in postoperative complications held **regardless of interval length**:
  - **<20 days:** OR 0.45 (95% CI 0.22–0.92), P=0.03
  - **≥20 days:** OR 0.81 (95% CI 0.68–0.96), P=0.02

**Robustness**

- The largest included study (Sakamoto 2020, n=3000, Japanese population-based) used **propensity score matching and instrumental variable analysis** and independently found lower postoperative complications and less stoma creation with BTS.
- **Sensitivity analysis excluding Sakamoto** preserved the complication benefit and the direction of the mortality benefit, though **mortality lost statistical significance**.

**Stent-related complications in the included studies** (Table 1, where reported): 1 perforation (Kye 2016), 2 perforations (Amelung 2016), 1 re-obstruction (Li 2020); 0 in van den Berg 2014, Ji 2017 and Morita 2019. Technical success rates where reported: **81.3%, 87.5%, 90.9%, 96%, 100%**.

**Context the authors give for why ER is not the safe default it was assumed to be**

- Emergency right hemicolectomy carried a **30-day mortality of 6.0% vs 0.7% for elective** hemicolectomy (Japanese National Clinical Database, cited).
- Previously reported ER outcomes for right-sided MLBO: **mortality 8.8–14.5%, morbidity 32–54.3%**.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Internal inconsistency in the paper's own odds ratios — surfaced, not silently resolved.** The Methods state "An OR < 1 favored the BTS group," and for adverse outcomes (complications, mortality, leakage, SSI) the ORs are consistent with the rates. But for the three **favourable** outcomes the direction cannot be read literally: **primary anastomosis 97.8% vs 85.9% with OR 0.31**, and **laparoscopic surgery 48.5% vs 15.7% with OR 0.21** — an OR below 1 alongside a higher rate in BTS. These ORs are evidently computed on the complement (failure of primary anastomosis; open surgery), which is consistent with the "OR<1 favours BTS" convention but **not** with the Results prose, which says of the laparoscopic analysis "The OR was significantly higher in the BTS group than in the ER group (OR = 0.21…)". **The raw event rates are unambiguous and are what any clinical claim on a wiki page should rest on**; the ORs for those three rows are reproduced above verbatim but should not be quoted as "odds of achieving" the outcome.
- **The headline benefit may be selection, not treatment.** The authors state this plainly: right-sided SEMS is technically demanding (long distance from the anus, colonic tortuosity, poor preparation, lower success rates than left-sided), so the stented patients may be the "easy-to-stent cases" in all seven non-randomized studies. Propensity matching in the single largest study is the main counterweight.
- **No RCT exists and the authors doubt one is feasible** — right-sided MLBO is less common than left-sided, so an adequately powered trial is difficult. This is the ceiling on the evidence, not a gap awaiting a paper.
- **Severe complications did not differ.** Any statement that BTS "reduces complications" must carry this: the difference is in overall/milder morbidity, and Clavien–Dindo ≥III events were equivalent (OR 0.98, 0.68–1.41).
- **Heterogeneity beyond the statistics** — sample size, SEMS type, technical quality, pathological staging, study design, follow-up, and even the **definitions of morbidity and mortality** differed between papers. The authors state these "affect the results considerably."
- **The ESGE 2020 SEMS guideline is NOT in this corpus.** Its recommendation of BTS for left-sided MLBO is known here **only secondhand, through this paper's introduction**, and is recorded as such. No wiki page should assert a left-sided ESGE recommendation as a guideline claim until that guideline is ingested. **Ingesting ESGE 2020 (van Hooft et al., *Endoscopy* 2020;52:389–407) would close the left-sided half of this topic**, which the corpus currently does not cover at all.
- **Tier note:** this is a tier-2 source (meta-analysis of retrospective cohorts). Where it overlaps the tier-1 [[asge-2013-crc-staging-management]] it does not conflict — ASGE 2013 is silent on left-vs-right and on comparative outcomes, so this contributes net-new, non-conflicting information rather than overriding a guideline.
- **Long-term oncologic outcomes are out of scope.** This meta-analysis measured **short-term** outcomes only. Whether right-sided BTS is oncologically equivalent (recurrence, survival) is not answered here.
