## Summary

This American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) living guideline ranks advanced therapies for moderate-to-severe [[crohns-disease]] and pushes two practice-changing principles: use a **higher-efficacy agent first** and favor **initial advanced therapy over step-up ("step") therapy**. It positions the IL-23 inhibitors (risankizumab, mirikizumab, guselkumab) and the Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitor upadacitinib alongside anti–tumor necrosis factor (TNF) agents, ustekinumab and vedolizumab, with separate efficacy tiers for advanced-therapy–naïve and previously exposed patients. Part of the broader [[inflammatory-bowel-disease]] treatment framework.

## Key Recommendations

Numbering, wording and grades as in the guideline's Table 1 (executive summary). All apply to adult outpatients with moderate-to-severely active CD [Crohn's disease].

### Use and positioning of advanced therapies

| # | Recommendation | Strength · certainty |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Recommends** infliximab, adalimumab, ustekinumab, risankizumab, mirikizumab, guselkumab, or upadacitinib\* over no treatment | Strong · moderate to high |
| 2 | Suggests certolizumab pegol or vedolizumab over no treatment | Conditional · moderate |
| 3 | **Advanced-therapy–naïve:** suggests a HIGHER-efficacy drug (infliximab, adalimumab, vedolizumab, ustekinumab, risankizumab, mirikizumab, guselkumab) rather than a LOWER-efficacy drug (certolizumab pegol, upadacitinib) | Conditional · low to high |
| 4 | **Previously exposed to ≥1 advanced therapy, particularly TNF [tumor necrosis factor] antagonists:** suggests a HIGHER-efficacy drug (adalimumab, risankizumab, guselkumab, upadacitinib) OR an INTERMEDIATE-efficacy drug (ustekinumab, mirikizumab) rather than a LOWER-efficacy drug (vedolizumab, certolizumab pegol) | Conditional · low to moderate |

\*In the United States, the FDA recommends reserving JAK [Janus kinase] inhibitors for failure or intolerance of TNF-antagonist therapy (Table 1 footnote).

Implementation considerations (Recs 1–4, condensed):
- Biosimilars of infliximab, adalimumab, ustekinumab ≈ originator for therapy selection
- Subcutaneous infliximab and vedolizumab: efficacy comparable to FDA-approved intravenous maintenance
- Partial response to FDA-approved dosing (especially more severe disease): extended induction or dose escalation may help for most advanced therapies
- Risankizumab, guselkumab, upadacitinib have 2 maintenance doses; higher dose may be preferred with high inflammatory burden/more severe disease and after TNF-antagonist failure
- Second-line TNF antagonist may work after discontinuing the first for immunogenicity or intolerance; may not work after primary non-response or loss of response despite adequate trough → consider another mechanism
- JAK inhibitors: possibly higher major adverse cardiovascular event and cancer risk than TNF antagonists in older adults with cardiovascular risk factors; limited pregnancy data — avoid when pregnancy is contemplated in the near future

### Immunomodulators

| # | Recommendation | Strength · certainty |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Suggests **against** thiopurine monotherapy over no treatment for **inducing** remission | Conditional · very low |
| 6 | After remission is achieved: suggests thiopurine monotherapy over no treatment for **maintaining** remission | Conditional · low |
| 7 | Suggests subcutaneous or intramuscular methotrexate monotherapy over no treatment | Conditional · moderate |
| 8 | Suggests **against** oral methotrexate monotherapy over no treatment | Conditional · very low |

Implementation considerations: azathioprine 2–2.5 mg/kg/d, mercaptopurine 1–1.5 mg/kg/d as monotherapy with normal drug metabolism; in the maintenance trials remission was typically induced with corticosteroids; methotrexate 25 mg weekly for 16–24 weeks of induction, 15 mg weekly maintenance, with daily folic acid; routine complete blood count and liver function test monitoring on thiopurines and methotrexate; methotrexate contraindicated in pregnancy or when actively considering pregnancy.

### Combination of biologics and immunomodulators

| # | Recommendation | Strength · certainty |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | **Thiopurine-naïve, starting infliximab:** suggests infliximab + thiopurine rather than infliximab monotherapy | Conditional · low |
| 10 | No recommendation on infliximab + methotrexate over infliximab monotherapy | No recommendation (knowledge gap) |
| 11 | No recommendation on adalimumab + thiopurine or methotrexate over adalimumab monotherapy | No recommendation (knowledge gap) |
| 12 | No recommendation for or against non–TNF-targeting biologics (vedolizumab, ustekinumab, risankizumab, mirikizumab, guselkumab) + thiopurine or methotrexate | No recommendation (knowledge gap) |

Implementation considerations: benefit of routinely adding an immunomodulator after prior immunomodulator-monotherapy failure is uncertain; adding one when starting a TNF antagonist may help at higher immunogenicity risk — prior immunogenicity to a TNF antagonist, re-exposure after a prolonged lapse, HLA-DQA1*05 carriers, high drug clearance (more severe disease, high inflammatory burden, low albumin).

### De-escalation

| # | Recommendation | Strength · certainty |
|---|---|---|
| 13 | Corticosteroid-free clinical remission for **at least 6 months** on TNF antagonist + immunomodulator: suggests **withdrawing the immunomodulator** | Conditional · low |
| 14 | Same population: suggests **against** withdrawing the TNF antagonist | Conditional · low |

- Rec 13 comment: patients (particularly difficult-to-treat disease) who value avoiding a trivial-to-small relapse risk over a trivial-to-small long-term adverse-effect risk may reasonably continue combination therapy
- Continuing combination may benefit those at higher immunogenicity risk: prior anti-drug-antibody failure, low TNF-antagonist trough despite dose escalation, HLA-DQA1*05 carriers

### Early advanced therapy and treatment target

| # | Recommendation | Strength · certainty |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | Suggests **upfront advanced therapy** rather than step-up therapy (initial corticosteroids and/or immunomodulator monotherapy) | Conditional · very low |
| 16 | No recommendation for or against treating to endoscopic remission vs symptomatic remission | No recommendation (knowledge gap) |

- Rec 15: all informing trials used TNF antagonist + immunomodulator combination; some patients may still benefit from corticosteroids for symptoms while starting advanced therapy

## Clinical Relevance

Efficacy-tiered, early-advanced-therapy strategy for moderate-to-severe [[crohns-disease]]: the tiers differ by line of therapy — upadacitinib is lower-efficacy in advanced-therapy–naïve patients but higher-efficacy after prior exposure; vedolizumab is higher-efficacy first-line but lower-efficacy after prior exposure. Pairs with vaccination/health maintenance per [[ibd-preventive-care]]. As a living guideline, rankings update as comparative data accrue.
