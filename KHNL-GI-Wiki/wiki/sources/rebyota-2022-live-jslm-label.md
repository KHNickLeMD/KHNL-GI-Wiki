## Summary

The FDA prescribing information for **fecal microbiota, live-jslm (RBL)**, the first licensed microbiota-based product for recurrent *Clostridioides difficile* infection (CDI). It was fetched to close the gap [[fmt]] names in its own text: [[aga-2024-fmt]] gives **no dosing at all** for the FDA-approved products and directs the reader to the manufacturer's package insert.

The label answers the questions the guideline leaves open and tightens two that the wiki had only loosely. The **indication is narrow and the limitation is explicit** — prevention of *recurrence* in adults **18 years and older following antibiotic treatment for recurrent CDI**, and the label states in its own Limitation of Use that RBL **is not indicated for treatment of CDI**. The dose is a **single 150 mL rectal suspension**, given **24 to 72 hours after the last dose of CDI antibiotics** — a window, not the looser "1–3 days" the wiki carried, and the same antibiotic washout the trials required.

Most of the document's value is **administration technique**, which no ingested guideline covers: patient positioning, a **12 cm (5 inch)** insertion depth angled toward the umbilicus, delivery by **gravity flow only**, the three explicit "do nots" that cause an incomplete dose (do not squeeze the bag, do not hang it from an IV stand, do not let the tube sag or loop), and **retaining the position for up to 15 minutes** to limit cramping. There is a matching **cold chain**: ultracold freezer (−60 °C to −90 °C) or refrigerator for **up to 5 days including thaw time**, thawed in a refrigerator over ~24 hours, **never refrozen** and never thawed with heat.

Two findings deserve to be read against the wiki's existing text. The **only contraindication is a history of severe allergic reaction to a product component** — there is no immunocompromise contraindication on the label, although [[aga-2024-fmt]] independently restricts these products in immunocompromised patients. And the **8-week treatment-success advantage (70.6% vs 57.5%) did not carry through to 6 months**: sustained clinical response was 65.5% vs 56.5%, a 9.1-point difference whose confidence interval crosses zero (−3.6% to 21.7%). The label reports this without comment; it is the opposite pattern to the sustained response reported for the oral spore product in [[feuerstadt-2022-ser109-cdiff]].

## Key Findings / Claims

**Indication and limitation of use**

- Indicated for the **prevention of recurrence of CDI in individuals 18 years of age and older, following antibiotic treatment for recurrent CDI**.
- **Limitation of Use, stated on the label: not indicated for treatment of CDI.**

**Dose and timing**

- **For rectal administration only.**
- **A single dose is 150 mL.** Supplied only as a single-dose 150 mL bag; one carton = one dose.
- **Administer 24 to 72 hours after the last dose of antibiotics for CDI.**

**Contraindication (one)**

- History of a **severe allergic reaction (e.g. anaphylaxis) to any known product component**.

**Warnings and precautions**

- **Transmissible infectious agents** — manufactured from human fecal matter, so it may carry a risk of transmitting infectious agents. A physician suspecting a transmitted infection should report it to the manufacturer.
- **Acute allergic reactions** — appropriate medical treatment must be **immediately available** in case of anaphylaxis after administration.
- **Food allergens** — may contain them (human fecal origin); the potential to cause reactions from food allergens is **unknown**. Individuals with food allergies were **not excluded from any of the 5 clinical studies**.

**Preparation and cold chain**

| Step | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Storage on receipt | **Ultracold freezer −60 °C to −90 °C**, *or* refrigerator **2–8 °C for up to 5 days including thaw time** |
| Administration set storage | **10–34 °C — never in the freezer** |
| Thawing | In a **refrigerator (2–8 °C) for ~24 hours**. **Do not** thaw with a heat source (microwave, hot water) |
| After thawing | **Do not refreeze.** Condensation is normal |
| Bag handling | Do **not** remove the bag from the sealed outer bag; do **not** remove air from the administration tube before insertion (causes loss of product) |
| Disposal | All components in medical waste |

**Administration technique**

1. Patient empties bladder and bowel if possible; place a disposable underpad.
2. Position **left-side** (lying on left side, knee bent) **or knee-chest**.
3. Close the pinch clamp fully, then spike the bag through its spike port.
4. Lubricate the tube tip (water-soluble lubricant, not supplied) and insert **~12 cm (5 inches) into the rectum, angled slightly toward the umbilicus**.
5. Hold the tube in place with one hand throughout; open the pinch clamp and **gradually raise the bag — gravity flow**.
6. When the dose is delivered, close the clamp and withdraw the tube slowly, preventing residual product from leaking. **Some product remains in the tube — this is expected.**
7. Keep the patient in the same position for **up to 15 minutes** to minimize cramping. **No restriction on restroom use.**

- **Three stated "do nots" during delivery:** do **not** let the tube sag or loop (prevents the full dose being delivered); do **not** squeeze the bag (uncomfortable for the patient); do **not** hang the bag from an IV stand.

**Adverse reactions** (Study 1, within 8 weeks)

| Reaction | RBL (n=180) | Placebo (n=87) |
|---|---|---|
| Abdominal pain | 8.9% | 6.9% |
| Diarrhea | 7.2% | 3.4% |
| Abdominal distension | 3.9% | 2.3% |
| Flatulence | 3.3% | 0 |
| Nausea | 3.3% | 1.1% |

- **Most reactions occurred in the first 2 weeks**, declining in subsequent 2-week intervals; beyond 2 weeks only a few isolated reactions. Most were **mild to moderate**; **none life-threatening**.
- **Serious adverse events** (pooled, 5 studies, 6 months after last dose): **10.1% (60/595)** with RBL versus **7.2% (6/83)** placebo — **none considered related** to the product.
- Safety population: **978 adults** across 2 randomized double-blind and 3 open-label studies (US and Canada); median age 64 y, 67.2% female.

**Specific populations**

- **Pregnancy and lactation:** not absorbed systemically after rectal administration; maternal use is not expected to expose the fetus or the breastfed child.
- **Pediatric:** safety and effectiveness **not established under 18 years**.
- **Geriatric:** 48.8% of recipients were ≥65 y (n=477) and 25.7% ≥75 y (n=251), but the data are **not sufficient to determine whether they respond differently**. No separate dose.

**Composition and mechanism**

- An **opaque fecal microbiota suspension** from qualified donors, tested for a panel of transmissible pathogens; donors have **no dietary restrictions** with respect to food allergens.
- The filtrate of fecal matter processed with **polyethylene glycol (PEG) 3350 and saline**. Each 150 mL dose contains **1×10⁸ to 5×10¹⁰ colony forming units (CFU) per mL**, including **>1×10⁵ CFU/mL of *Bacteroides***, and **not more than 5.97 g of PEG 3350**.
- **Mechanism of action: not established** (the label's own words). **No nonclinical toxicology studies were conducted.**

**Efficacy and trial population**

- **Treatment success (absence of CDI diarrhea within 8 weeks): 70.6% vs 57.5% placebo** — difference **13.1 percentage points** (95% credible interval 2.3–24.0), **posterior probability of superiority 0.991** against a pre-defined threshold of 0.975 (Bayesian analysis integrating a phase 3 and a phase 2 study).
- **Sustained clinical response at 6 months: 65.5% vs 56.5% — difference 9.1%, 95% CI −3.6% to 21.7%, not statistically significant.**
- **Entry required** confirmed recurrent CDI (≥1 prior episode in Study 1, ≥2 in Study 2; or ≥2 episodes of severe CDI needing hospitalization within the last year), **≥10 consecutive days of completed antibiotic therapy**, and CDI **under control** (<3 unformed/loose — Bristol 6–7 — stools/day for 2 consecutive days). Antibiotic washout **24–72 h** (Study 1) or **24–48 h** (Study 2).
- **Studies 1 and 2 excluded** celiac disease, inflammatory bowel disease, irritable bowel syndrome and chronic diarrhea; one open-label study (NCT03931941) did not exclude them.
- Most enrolled patients had received **vancomycin** before treatment (87.4% in Study 1, 89.0% in Study 2).

**Patient counseling**

- Inform patients the product is **made from human fecal matter and may contain food allergens**.
- Report **persistent diarrhea** — defined on the label as **≥3 loose bowel movements in 24 h for 2 consecutive days** — if it returns after administration.
- **Avoid any oral antibiotic therapy for up to 8 weeks afterwards unless directed by the physician.**

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **No contradiction with the ingested guidelines, but one gap the label does not fill.** The label's **only** contraindication is severe allergy to a component — it states **no immunocompromise restriction**. [[aga-2024-fmt]] independently recommends **conventional FMT only** in mildly/moderately immunocompromised patients and finds insufficient evidence for the licensed products in any immunocompromised patient. These do not conflict: a label contraindication and a guideline recommendation are different instruments, and the guideline governs patient selection. The wiki keeps the guideline's restriction and does not weaken it on the label's silence.
- **The durability signal is weaker than the headline.** Superiority at 8 weeks (70.6% vs 57.5%, posterior probability 0.991) **did not persist** as a significant difference in sustained clinical response at 6 months (65.5% vs 56.5%, 95% CI −3.6% to 21.7%). This is the opposite pattern to the oral spore product in [[feuerstadt-2022-ser109-cdiff]], which reported sustained clinical response 88% vs 60%. The two were never compared head to head, the trials differ in design and population, and neither result licenses a claim about the other product.
- **Mechanism of action is unestablished by the label's own statement**, and no nonclinical toxicology studies were conducted. Any mechanistic account on a wiki page must come from elsewhere and be attributed there.
- **The trials excluded the comorbid populations clinicians most often ask about** — celiac disease, inflammatory bowel disease, irritable bowel syndrome and chronic diarrhea were excluded from both randomized studies. The efficacy numbers above do not describe those patients.
