## Summary

By 2007 the endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) literature on chronic pancreatitis (CP) had become uninterpretable across studies: the **number of features assessed ranged from 5 to 13** and the **threshold required for diagnosis from 1 to 5**, with different definitions for the same named feature. Equipment, technique and the region of pancreas examined also differed. The Cambridge classification was judged "arguably outdated". The conference set out to fix the vocabulary first and the diagnostic algorithm second.

The resulting **Rosemont classification** makes two structural changes to EUS diagnosis of CP. First, it abandons **"count the features past a threshold"** — the approach that made the literature incomparable — and **weights** features instead, on the premise that "not all features have the same positive predictive value or reliability". Features become **Major A**, **Major B**, or **minor**. Second, it attaches an **operational definition with a size and a count to every feature**, and specifies **where in the gland each may be assessed** — most only in the body and tail.

Only two features are **Major A**: **hyperechoic foci with shadowing** and **main pancreatic duct (MPD) calculi**. Both are defined by **acoustic shadowing**; the same foci without shadowing fall to minor. **Major B is a single feature** — lobularity with honeycombing. Combinations map to four categories: **consistent with CP**, **suggestive of CP**, **indeterminate for CP**, and **normal**, with the middle two requiring confirmation by another test.

The paper is candid about what it is not. There is **no broadly accepted reference standard** for CP, so the criteria could not be validated against one; the system is expert opinion informed by the literature. The panel also declines to adjust thresholds for sex, age, body mass index, alcohol or tobacco, despite evidence that these shift EUS findings — the diagnostic system is to be applied **independent of them**. And the criteria were built on **radial EUS imaging**, with the correlation to linear imaging left as future work.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Table 2 — consensus-based parenchymal features

| Feature | Definition | Weight | Rank |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hyperechoic foci with shadowing** | Echogenic structures **≥2 mm in length and width that shadow** | **Major A** | 1 |
| **Lobularity — with honeycombing** | **Contiguous ≥3 lobules** | **Major B** | 2 |
| **Lobularity — without honeycombing** | **Noncontiguous** lobules | Minor | 2 |
| **Hyperechoic foci without shadowing** | Echogenic foci **≥2 mm in both length and width, no shadowing** | Minor | 3 |
| **Cysts** | **Anechoic, rounded/elliptical** structures with or without septations | Minor | 4 |
| **Stranding** | **Hyperechoic lines ≥3 mm in length in at least 2 different directions** with respect to the imaged plane | Minor | 5 |

*Rank = the panel's electronic vote on predictive value, 1 = highest predictor. Lobularity is defined as **well-circumscribed, ≥5 mm structures with an enhancing rim and a relatively echo-poor centre**.*

### Table 3 — consensus-based ductal features

| Feature | Definition | Weight | Rank |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MPD calculi** | **Echogenic structure(s) within the MPD with acoustic shadowing** | **Major A** | 1 |
| **Irregular MPD contour** | **Uneven or irregular outline and ectatic course** | Minor | 2 |
| **Dilated side branches** | **3 or more tubular anechoic structures, each ≥1 mm in width**, budding from the MPD | Minor | 3 |
| **MPD dilation** | **≥3.5 mm in the body** or **>1.5 mm in the tail** | Minor | 4 |
| **Hyperechoic MPD margin** | Echogenic, distinct structure in **greater than 50% of the entire MPD in the body and tail** | Minor | 5 |

### The counting and location rules — the operational detail the guidelines omit

- **Hyperechoic foci with shadowing: at least 3 are needed** for the feature to count as a marker of CP.
- **Lobularity: at least 3 lobules in the body or tail** are necessary for the feature to be present. **≥3 contiguous** lobules = honeycombing = **Major B**; **3 or more noncontiguous** lobules = **minor**.
- **Hyperechoic foci without shadowing: at least 3** are needed.
- **Stranding: at least 3 strands** are needed. Nonspecific if present in **both** dorsal and ventral pancreas, **but not** if found in the ventral pancreas alone.
- **Cysts: ≥2 mm in short axis**, and **all cysts count regardless of location** in the pancreas.
- **Only 3 features may be assessed anywhere in the gland** — **hyperechoic foci with shadowing, cysts, and ductal calculi**. **Everything else is evaluated only in the body and tail**, because the pancreatic head is relatively hypoechoic.
- **Hyperechoic MPD margin:** when imaged in a parallel or perpendicular orientation, **both proximal and distal MPD borders must be hyperechoic** to distinguish the finding from specular-reflector artifact.
- **MPD dilation** — the paper notes that normally there is a **gradual decrease in MPD diameter from head to tail**, so **lack of tapering increases the likelihood of an abnormal MPD when the diameter is borderline**.
- **Criteria were built on radial EUS.** The panel notes many centres now image the pancreas solely with a linear instrument and that correlating these endpoints with linear images remains to be done.

### Table 4 — EUS diagnosis of CP on the basis of consensus criteria

| Category | Qualifying combinations |
|---|---|
| **I. Consistent with CP** | **A.** 1 Major A feature **+ ≥3 minor features** · **B.** 1 Major A **+** Major B · **C.** **2 Major A features** |
| **II. Suggestive of CP** † | **A.** 1 Major A **+ <3 minor** · **B.** 1 Major B **+ ≥3 minor** · **C.** **≥5 minor features (any)** |
| **III. Indeterminate for CP** † | **A.** **3 to 4 minor features, no major features** · **B.** **Major B alone**, or with **<3 minor features** |
| **IV. Normal** | **≤2 minor ‡ features, no major features** |

- \* "EUS diagnosis of CP should be made in the **appropriate clinical setting**."
- † **"Suggestive" and "indeterminate" require confirmation by an additional study** — the footnote lists **ERCP, CT, MRI, or PFT**. ⚠ The paper's own abbreviation list expands PFT as *"pulmonary function test"*, which is evidently an error in a pancreatic document; the expansion is not restated anywhere else in the paper.
- ‡ **The "normal" count excludes four features** — **cysts, dilated MPD, hyperechoic nonshadowing foci, and dilated side branches.** A patient with three of those and nothing else is still "normal" by Table 4; the exclusion is easy to miss and changes the category.

### What the panel says about reliability

- After reviewing each feature with its corresponding definition, the experts **"observed a κ value for interobserver reliability of >0.7 for each feature"**. ⚠ This was **achieved after extensive literature review and deliberation among the participants** — it describes agreement reached on **definitions during the consensus process**, not a measured interobserver study of independently scored examinations.
- Interobserver variability in the **prior** literature was poor: among 11 expert endosonographers, **side branches κ = 0.18**, and the criterion with the highest κ was **MPD dilation at 0.61** — which the panel notes was also the **least predictive** of CP, "thus raising questions as to the clinical utility of this feature".

### Why the older criteria were abandoned

- **The "3-2-1 rule" for MPD dilation leads to overdiagnosis** — in one control group the upper limit of normal MPD was **3.6 mm head / 3 mm body / 2 mm tail**.
- **Visible side branches alone are obsolete as a marker** — newer echoendoscopes visualise side branches in nearly all patients, and side-branch width overlapped considerably between controls and CP patients.
- **EUS-guided FNA and Tru-cut biopsy are not supported** for diagnosing CP — poor diagnostic accuracy and procedure-related risk (FNA specificity only **67%**; Tru-cut agreement **κ = 0.25** with 2 of 16 patients requiring hospitalisation for a complication). Histologic/cytologic features were therefore **deliberately excluded** from the criteria.
- **Test performance of EUS overall:** among studies without patient follow-up, a cutoff of **≥3 features** gave **sensitivity 80% / specificity 86%** in one series and **88% / 100%** in another; at that cutoff there was a **17% probability of an abnormal ERCP and a 13% chance of a positive secretin test**.

### The panel's explicit refusal to adjust for patient factors

- Male sex is an independent predictor of **≥5 criteria** (odds ratio 1.8, 95% CI 1.3–2.6; 2.9, 95% CI 1.2–6.8 in a second study), while **age did not correlate** with imaging findings once sex was accounted for.
- **Heavy alcohol** (OR 5.1, 95% CI 3.1–8.5) and **heavy smoking** (OR 1.7, 95% CI 1.2–2.4) are associated with more EUS features of CP.
- Despite this, **the diagnostic system is to be applied independent of sex, age, BMI, alcohol and tobacco use** — the panel recognises these raise the likelihood of pathology but did not modify thresholds by subgroup.
- "Other than mild increases in duct size with age, there is **no convincing evidence that increasing age independently leads to more criteria**."

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **⚠ The lobularity discrepancy is resolved in this paper's favour.** [[apa-2014-chronic-pancreatitis]] states the parenchymal Major A criteria were "(1) hyperechoic foci with shadowing and (2) well-circumscribed lobularity" — then assigns honeycombing lobularity to Major B and noncontiguous lobularity to minor in the same paragraph, leaving no lobularity variant for Major A. **This paper's Table 2 and its own abstract are unambiguous: the only Major A features are hyperechoic foci with shadowing and MPD calculi; lobularity with honeycombing is Major B.** APA 2014's sentence is an error, and [[chronic-pancreatitis]] already omitted lobularity from Major A pending this confirmation.
- **The reliability claim cuts against the reliability data, within this same paper.** The panel reports **κ >0.7 for each feature** after deliberation, while the literature it reviews reports **κ <0.4 for 7 of 9 conventional criteria** and only moderate agreement (**κ 0.45**) on the final diagnosis. These measure different things — agreement on a definition versus agreement on an image — and the page states the measured figures, not the consensus figure.
- **No validation, by the authors' own statement.** "The results of our deliberations do not provide validation of our recommendations." [[apa-2014-chronic-pancreatitis]] independently notes Rosemont has never been validated against a histologic gold standard, nor shown superior to conventional criteria for clinical outcomes. **No contradiction — the two agree the system is unvalidated expert opinion.**
- **Built on radial EUS, used on linear.** The criteria were derived from radial imaging and the panel flags that correlating them to linear EUS — now the dominant instrument in many centres — is unfinished. No ingested source closes this.
- **The figures were not captured.** Figures 2–11 are the EUS example images of each feature (normal parenchyma, shadowing and nonshadowing foci, honeycombing lobularity, cyst, stranding, MPD calculi, contour irregularity, side-branch ectasia, hyperechoic margins). Rosemont is a classification made by looking, so these belong on [[chronic-pancreatitis]]; the extraction tooling was unavailable this pass (see the log entry).
