## Summary

One article of the IDSA 2024 update on complicated intraabdominal infection (cIAI) — risk assessment, imaging and microbiology in adults, children and pregnant people. This part covers **imaging of suspected acute cholecystitis (calculous and acalculous) and acute cholangitis**, stratified as **initial imaging**, then **subsequent imaging if initial imaging is inconclusive**. It replaces the imaging statements of the 2010 Surgical Infection Society (SIS)/IDSA cIAI guideline. Endorsed by the European Society of Clinical Microbiology and Infectious Diseases and the Pediatric Infectious Diseases Society.

Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation (GRADE) methods; systematic review through October 2022 (observational studies after 2010 and randomized controlled trials; MRI studies from 2005 on). Reviewed modalities: ultrasound (US; performed in a controlled manner and interpreted by a radiologist), computed tomography (CT, incl. multidetector CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP) and hepatobiliary iminodiacetic acid (HIDA) scan. **Excluded:** magnetic resonance elastography, endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP; invasive, diagnostic and therapeutic) and point-of-care US (POCUS; variable interpretation).

Pathway for nonpregnant adults: **US first → CT if US equivocal/nondiagnostic → MRI/MRCP or HIDA if both are equivocal** (cholecystitis); MRI/MRCP if cholangitis is suspected after inconclusive US and CT. Pregnancy: **US or MRI**, no preference (knowledge gap). Children: not systematically reviewed; reasonable to mirror the adult pathway.

Evidence is thin: all recommendations are **conditional** or a knowledge gap; certainty very low or low. No study assessed US or CT accuracy for **cholangitis** (cholecystitis data used as indirect evidence); no MRI/MRCP study met criteria for suspected cholecystitis; no study in pregnancy.

Four recommendations, unnumbered in the document; **R1–R4 numbering is the wiki's**. Strength and certainty as printed. Strength terms (Methods, S105): **strong** = applies to most people with few exceptions; **conditional** = applies to the majority with many exceptions, shared decision-making important.

**Remarks attached to the recommendations** (p. S104–S105):

- **Acute cholangitis diagnosis** (R1–R4) should include:
  - clinical signs — jaundice, fever, chills, right upper quadrant (RUQ) abdominal pain
  - laboratory findings — indicators of inflammation and biliary stasis
  - imaging findings — biliary dilatation or evidence of an etiology (eg, stricture, stone, obstructing mass)
- **CT with intravenous (IV) contrast is preferable and usually appropriate** when CT is obtained for subsequent imaging (R2; citing the American College of Radiology [ACR] Appropriateness Criteria, right upper quadrant pain, 2022 update).
- **If US and CT are both inconclusive but acute cholangitis is suspected → MRI/MRCP is a reasonable option** (R3 remark).
- No study assessed US or CT accuracy for acute cholangitis; panel relied on indirect evidence from acute cholecystitis (R1, R2).
- **Children:** cholecystitis/cholangitis uncommon, evidence not systematically reviewed; **reasonable to mirror the adult imaging pathway** (R1–R3). Conditions such as biliary atresia and sickle cell disease may raise the risk in children; adult pathway reasonable there too (Methods, p. S106).

### Implementation considerations (ungraded; p. S107)

- US may be limited by abdominal tenderness and obesity.
- Nonpregnant adults, **high clinical suspicion for acute cholecystitis + equivocal initial US** → may be appropriate to go **directly from US to HIDA**.
- **Other diagnoses being considered for RUQ pain** → reasonable to obtain **CT before HIDA**.
- **Suspected complications → CT as subsequent imaging** (CT with IV contrast preferable):
  - of acute cholecystitis: biloma, intraabdominal abscess, bile duct injury, hepatic injury, small bowel injury, infection, retained bile duct stones, bleeding
  - of acute cholangitis: hepatic abscess, portal vein thrombosis, acute biliary pancreatitis, liver failure

### Rationale (condensed; p. S106–S107)

- **US before CT:** reasonably accurate, useful for gallstones, less costly, more portable, results timelier; no radiation (particularly important for children) and no contrast-associated side effects. Downsides — more operator-dependent; may be less accurate in obesity — outweighed.
- **CT second:** identifies complications and rules out other etiologies; weigh radiation risk-to-benefit if imaging beyond US is needed (particularly children).
- **MRI/MRCP vs HIDA after CT** (acute cholecystitis specifically suspected):
  - MRI/MRCP — clearer picture of surrounding structures, faster result, more readily available
  - HIDA — considered the **gold standard** for acute cholecystitis, far less costly; requires fasting for several hours beforehand
- **Pregnancy:** no diagnostic-accuracy studies in pregnant patients; panel used nonpregnant-adult evidence plus benefit–harm balance (eg, radiation exposure).

### Evidence — diagnostic accuracy in suspected acute cholecystitis (Summary of Evidence, p. S106)

| Modality | Studies | Sensitivity | Specificity | Certainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US | 6 observational studies | Median 73% (range 32%–83%) | Median 83% (range 46%–88%) | Very low (US vs CT) |
| CT | 1 study | 73% | 94% | Very low (US vs CT) |
| HIDA | 3 observational studies | Median 89% (range 85%–92%) | Median 67% (range 34%–86%) | Low |
| MRI/MRCP | None found (search extended to 2005 and after) | — | — | Knowledge gap |

*Population: adults with suspected acute cholecystitis; single-study CT values.*

- A further retrospective study reported **88% sensitivity for US or CT** — but included only patients with confirmed acute cholecystitis.
- Downgrading: risk of bias (QUADAS-2), indirect comparisons, inconsistency (HIDA: especially specificity). Cholangitis evidence carries an extra layer of indirectness (population).

### Research needs (p. S107)

- Contemporary data on MRI/MRCP accuracy for acute cholecystitis and acute cholangitis.
- Studies in pregnant women.
- Head-to-head comparisons of imaging modalities.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Second-line test after nondiagnostic US — CT (IDSA) vs MRI/MRCP (TG18).**
  - IDSA 2024: CT with IV contrast second (R2, conditional, very low certainty); MRI/MRCP or HIDA only after both US and CT are nondiagnostic (R3).
  - TG18 ([[tg18-2018-cholecystitis-criteria]], Q5): MRI/MRCP "recommended if abdominal ultrasound does not provide a definitive diagnosis" (Recommendation 2, level B). TG18 cholangitis ([[tg18-2018-cholangitis-criteria]], Q5): MRI/MRCP recommended (Recommendation 2, level C), used when US or CT leave the diagnosis uncertain.
  - Source priority: IDSA is a US society guideline and newer → its sequence wins the page; TG18 stays as the contrasting view. [[acute-cholecystitis]] currently states MRI/MRCP as "the second step when ultrasound is non-diagnostic" without the IDSA CT step.
- **HIDA's place.** IDSA places HIDA after US and CT (or directly after an equivocal US when suspicion is high), calls it the gold standard, and grades its evidence low certainty, with inconsistent specificity across its three studies. TG18 also rates HIDA's diagnostic yield above US's yet keeps US first ([[tg18-2018-cholecystitis-criteria]]). The two guidelines draw on different study sets; their accuracy figures are not directly comparable.
- **US performance.** Both guidelines put US first on accuracy plus practicality — IDSA: "reasonably accurate", less costly, more portable; TG18: non-invasive with comparatively high diagnostic yield. IDSA's US estimates are in the evidence table above.
- **Cholangitis imaging.** [[acute-cholangitis]] (from TG18) says at least one of US and/or CT, US first; consistent with IDSA R1–R2. IDSA adds that no direct accuracy evidence exists for cholangitis.
- **Pregnancy.** [[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]] source page: ultrasound is the imaging of choice for gallstones in pregnancy; MRCP (no contrast) for suspected choledocholithiasis, with endoscopic ultrasound if the patient cannot undergo MRCP. IDSA R4 leaves US vs MRI open (knowledge gap) for suspected cholecystitis/cholangitis. Not a direct conflict (AGA addresses gallstone disease broadly), but IDSA does not endorse US over MRI.
- **Cholangitis diagnostic description.** IDSA's remark (clinical signs + labs + imaging) is qualitative; the operative thresholds remain those of TG18 on [[acute-cholangitis]].
- **Open:** no MRI/MRCP accuracy data after 2005 met criteria; no pregnancy data; children not reviewed.
