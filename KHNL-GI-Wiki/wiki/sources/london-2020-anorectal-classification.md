## Summary

Position paper of the International Anorectal Physiology Working Group (IAPWG), a collaborative of 29 gastroenterologists, coloproctologists and physiologists from 12 countries convened to set minimum standards for the clinical measurement of anorectal function, with particular focus on high-resolution anorectal manometry (HR-ARM). It delivers two products: a **standardized testing protocol** covering HR-ARM, the rectal sensory test (RST) and the balloon expulsion test (BET), and the **London Classification**, a four-part diagnostic scheme for disorders of anorectal function built on those measurements.

Consensus was reached between 2014 and 2018 across six face-to-face meetings (Quaker-based model) and three rounds of remote Delphi voting. Each protocol statement and each element of the classification carries a consensus level: **C1 (excellent, >90% unanimity)**, **C2 (moderate, 75–90%)**, **C3 (none, <75%)**. These are *consensus* levels, not evidence grades — the paper states explicitly that the vast majority of recommendations reflect coalescence of expert opinion rather than systematic review, and that what is not normal does not necessarily constitute disease.

The London Classification follows the model of the Chicago Classification for esophageal motility and the Lyon Consensus for reflux: findings are tiered as **major** (pattern not seen in controls, likely to represent a physiological alteration associated with symptom generation), **minor** (seen in patients but also in controls, may represent such an alteration) and **inconclusive** (seen in patients and controls; relevance not yet determined). Four parts address the rectoanal inhibitory reflex, anal tone and contractility, rectoanal coordination, and rectal sensation; one study may generate a diagnosis under more than one part.

Deliberately absent: **quantitative reference limits.** Because of heterogeneity of equipment and practice the group declined to publish normal values, describing findings instead against the local **upper limit of normal (ULN)** and **lower limit of normal (LLN)**. The classification is therefore a nomenclature and a decision structure — the laboratory supplies the numbers.

## Key Findings / Claims

Agree and minor-concern votes were amalgamated for counting. The document **numbers none of its statements**; the numbering below is the wiki's, added for reference only. Per-statement agreement tables (S1, S2) are supplementary material and are not part of the file in `raw/`.

### Study indications (§3.1)

1. Anorectal function testing should be performed **after referral from a suitable specialist practitioner and after organic pathology has been appropriately excluded** (C1).
2. Indications (each C1), with what the test is for:
   - **Constipation / evacuation disorder symptoms** — abnormalities of rectoanal coordination, parameters of evacuation, rectal sensitivity (particularly hyposensitivity); assessment of megarectum/megacolon to exclude hypo-/aganglionosis.
   - **Fecal incontinence symptoms** — impaired anal sphincter function and abnormal rectal sensitivity (hyper- and hyposensitivity).
   - **Functional anorectal pain** — anal sphincter hypertension, abnormalities of rectoanal coordination and parameters of evacuation.
   - **Preoperative assessment** — anal sphincter function and parameters of evacuation, particularly where intervention risks continence (fistulotomy, lateral sphincterotomy) or evacuation (rectopexy).
   - **After obstetric injury / traumatic birth** — to quantify anal sphincter function before future deliveries, if clinician and patient wish.
3. Use of anorectal function testing **as a tool for biofeedback was acknowledged but guidance was judged beyond the scope** of this process — i.e. the document makes no biofeedback recommendation.

### Patient preparation (§3.2) and digital rectal examination (§3.3)

4. **Continue all existing medications** (C1); patients **may eat and drink up to the time of the test** (C1).
5. Clinical interview should clarify presenting symptoms, medications, allergies, and pertinent past medical, surgical and obstetric events.
6. **Bowel preparation is optional**; patients may open their bowels before the procedure if they wish (C1). A pre-procedure **water or phosphate enema is not contraindicated but must be documented**, because of its potential effect on function (C1).
7. Document any medications known to affect anorectal function, **including analgesics** (C1).
8. Explain the procedure, answer questions, obtain **verbal consent**; written-consent policy is left to local/national policy (C1).
9. **Digital rectal examination before intubation** — not expected to be fully diagnostic, but to (a) give an initial clinical assessment of pelvic floor structure, function and sensitivity; (b) exclude local pathology and fecal loading (if loading is found, consider a tap-water or phosphate enema); (c) check the patient understands the commands "squeeze" and "push".

### Equipment specifications (§3.4)

10. **HR-ARM:** sensors should ideally record **circumferential, not unidirectional** pressure (C1). Recommendations are based on **solid-state systems (C3 — the lowest consensus in the document)** but also apply to water-perfused high-resolution systems (C2). **Minimum longitudinal recording length 6 cm** (C1). Thin flexible catheters recommended; rigid "high-definition" catheters may be used but are **not standard** (C1).
11. **Position:** left lateral, hips and knees flexed, non-anaesthetizing lubricant (C1).
12. **Rectal balloon base sited 3–5 cm above the upper border of the anal canal**, so the balloon does not impinge on the anal canal during inflation (C1). **Most distal recording sensor external to the anal verge** (C1). Withdraw the probe immediately for pain; if discomfort recurs on second insertion, seek medical assessment.
13. **RST:** left lateral with hips and knees flexed (C1); integrated or separate balloon system (C2); **balloon capacity ≥400 mL** — because maximum tolerated volume in health is no greater than 350 mL (C2); all components **latex-free** (C1). **Ramp (continuous) or phasic distension** may be used (C1), but **results from the two methods are not interchangeable**. **Insufflate with air** (C1). **Ramp rate 1–5 mL/s; phasic inflation rate 10 mL/s** (C1).
14. **BET:** flexible catheter **up to 16 Fr**, non-latex compliant balloon at the tip (C2); **fixed volume 50 mL tepid water** (C1), introduced in the left lateral position, then the subject is transferred to a **sitting position, ideally on a toilet in privacy behind a curtain**. Alternative evacuation tests (barium or magnetic resonance defecography) may be used instead of BET (C1), **noting that diagnostic agreement between these tests is fair at best**.

### Study protocol and maneuvers (§4.1–4.2)

15. The standardized sequence (Figure 1) achieved **unanimity as a whole (C1)**; total study time for HR-ARM + RST + BET is expected to be **15–20 minutes**. Particular attention is owed to **maneuver durations and between-maneuver recovery intervals**.
16. **Stabilization 3 minutes** after catheter insertion, before any maneuver, to let anal tone return to baseline (C1).
17. **Rest — 60 s** (C2); patient reminded to relax and stay quiet to avoid movement artifact (C1). Ultraslow waves (0.5–2 cycles/min) may be seen during this period.
18. **Short squeeze — three × 5 s**, separated by **30-s** recovery intervals; **best of three** used for analysis (C1).
19. **Long (endurance) squeeze — one × 30 s**, followed by a **60-s** recovery interval (C1); describes fatigue over time rather than contractile ability.
20. **Cough — two**, separated by **30 s**; each must be a single (not double or multiple) cough with adequate effort; **best attempt = the one with the greatest rise in rectal pressure** (C1).
21. **Push — three × 15 s (C3)**, separated by **30-s** recovery intervals; **rectal balloon insufflation is not mandated** (C1). Because of the high false-positive rate from patient and technical factors, the **best attempt — defined as the most qualitatively normal — is used for analysis** (C1).
22. **RAIR — one**, starting volume **≥30 mL**; failure to elicit it may simply reflect a low distending volume in a large-capacity rectum, so **if megarectum is suspected, repeat with increasing balloon volumes** (C1).
23. **RST** — balloon ≥3–5 cm above the upper border of the anal canal (C1); record volume at **first constant sensation volume (FCSV)**, **desire to defecate volume (DDV)**, **maximum tolerated volume (MTV)** (C1). A fourth threshold, **sustained urgency volume, is optional** (C1).
24. **BET** — measures ability, based on time taken, to expel a balloon from the rectum (C1).
25. **Standard instructions** should be given shortly before each maneuver (C2); exact wording was **not** subject to consensus voting (significant cultural variation), but worked examples are supplied for short squeeze, long squeeze, cough, push, RST and BET.

### Description of normality (§4.5)

26. **No specific normal values are recommended** in this first iteration, given variability in equipment, protocol and practice.
27. If normal values are taken from **published data**, equipment setup and procedure **must be identical** to the referenced manuscript (C1).
28. If normal values come from a **local healthy-volunteer study**, allow for variability by **sex (C1)**, **parity (C2)** and **age (C2)**.

### The London Classification (§4.6)

29. Four parts, each with its own decision figure; **a single study may produce a diagnosis under more than one part**:
    - **Part 1** — disorder of the rectoanal inhibitory reflex
    - **Part 2** — disorders of anal tone and contractility
    - **Part 3** — disorders of rectoanal coordination
    - **Part 4** — disorders of rectal sensation
30. Findings are tiered **major / minor / inconclusive** (definitions in Summary above), after the Chicago Classification model.
31. **Cough and long squeeze do not form part of the diagnostic classification** — the group acknowledged their widespread use but did not consider inclusion justifiable at present.
32. Terminology fixed by the classification: **(hypo/hyper)tension** = anal resting tone; **(hypo)contractility** = anal squeeze; **expulsion** = ability to expel a rectal balloon; **propulsion** = generation of an adequate rise in rectal pressure during push; **dyssynergia** = failure of coordinated anal relaxation during push; **(hypo/hyper)sensitivity** = rectal sensation.

### Stated limitations

- Most recommendations are **expert opinion**, not systematic review; **"what is not normal does not necessarily constitute disease"**; outcome studies of the classification's utility for directing interventions are still needed.
- Only **three simple office-based tests** are covered. RST and BET are screening tests; an abnormal result may warrant endoanal ultrasound, barium/MR defecography or rectal barostat, which may confirm or refute findings the classification labels *inconclusive*.
- **BET is the only test shown to predict response to biofeedback.**
- No quantitative reference limits (see §4.5); female sex, advanced age and parity all degrade sphincter and rectal sensory function, and normal ranges should reflect this.
- All recommendations can be applied to **conventional (non-high-resolution) technology**.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Dyssynergia is "inconclusive" or "minor" here, but a diagnosis elsewhere.** The London Classification never promotes dyssynergia to a major finding: abnormal expulsion with dyssynergia is a **minor** finding, and an abnormal manometric coordination pattern with *normal* expulsion is **inconclusive**. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders|ACG 2021]] and [[rome-v-2026-dgbi|Rome V]] treat abnormal evacuation testing as diagnostic of a defecation disorder. Both positions rest on the same observation — that paradoxical contraction is common in healthy people — but they handle it differently; the London scheme is a description of the physiology, not a disease definition, and the paper says so.
- **No reference values.** The classification's decision points are all "> upper limit of normal" / "< lower limit of normal" with no numbers attached. A lab cannot apply it without its own normative data or a published dataset acquired with identical equipment and protocol. This is the paper's deliberate choice, not an omission to be filled from elsewhere.
- **Rao dyssynergia types I–IV are not in this document.** The paper names the Rao criteria only in passing, in its future-research section, and notes that **recent evidence questions the usefulness of quantitative metrics such as the rectoanal pressure gradient because of significant overlap between patients with dyssynergia and healthy volunteers**. The numbered subtypes are not reproduced here.
- **Solid-state systems carry C3 (no consensus)** while the water-perfused extension carries C2 — the single weakest-supported element of the protocol, and worth knowing before citing the protocol as settled.
- Not reproduced in the article file: **Supplementary Tables S1 and S2**, which hold the per-statement and per-classification-element agreement levels.
