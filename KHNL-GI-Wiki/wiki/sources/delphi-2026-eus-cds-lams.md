- **Method (as printed, pp. 2–3):**
  - Steering committee (Khoury, Lisotti, Orzan, Napoléon) — three interventional endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) experts designed the study and wrote the statements and also voted; Orzan supervised data collection and analysis and did not vote.
  - Experts chosen by center volume, guideline participation and PubMed authorship on EUS-guided drainage with lumen-apposing metal stents (LAMS). **36 invited → 32 participated** (3 declined, 1 no response): **30 interventional advanced endoscopists + 2 hepato-pancreato-biliary surgeons**; 25 Europe (78.1%), 5 Asia (15.6%), 1 North America (3.1%), 1 South America (3.1%). 100% participation in both rounds.
  - Statements drafted from a literature review, grouped into domains, each paired with a technical-review paragraph and a population–intervention–comparison–outcome (PICO) table; 30-day comment period before voting.
  - **Two anonymous rounds** (Google Forms); **5-point Likert scale** (1 = completely disagree … 5 = strongly agree) with free text.
  - **Consensus threshold (a priori):** **≥80%** rating 4 or 5, **median ≥4**, **interquartile range (IQR) ≤1**. Agreement **very strong ≥90%**, **strong 80%–89%**.
  - Round 1 statements with <80% agreement (whatever their median/IQR) were revised by the steering committee and re-voted in round 2 with the same thresholds. Responses with two Likert values ticked, or free text only, were excluded for that statement.
  - **No graded recommendations.** Evidence behind each statement rated afterwards with the Oxford Center for Evidence-Based Medicine Levels of Evidence (Table 3), to separate expert agreement from evidence strength: levels 1a–1b high, 2a–2b moderate, 3a–3b low, 4–5 very low quality; where a guideline was cited, its strength level was reported.

## Summary
- Endoscopic ultrasound-guided choledochoduodenostomy with a lumen-apposing metal stent (EUS-CDS-L) drains the bile duct transmurally through the duodenal wall. Earlier EUS-guided choledochoduodenostomy (EUS-CDS) used plastic stents or fully covered self-expandable metal stents (FC-SEMS) in a multi-step technique with higher bile leak and peritonitis risk; LAMS (introduced 2014) made it a single-step procedure. Practice varies widely, and earlier EUS-guided biliary drainage (EUS-BD) consensus papers did not address EUS-CDS-L specifically.
- **Process:** 28 statements circulated (12 preprocedural, 14 procedural/technical, 2 post-procedural); 2 removed; 20 accepted in round 1; 6 revised and re-voted, of which 4 accepted and 2 rejected → **24 final statements** (24/26 = 92.3%), **16 with very strong agreement**.
- **Key positions:** EUS-CDS-L over percutaneous transhepatic biliary drainage (PTBD) after failed endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) in distal malignant biliary obstruction (DMBO); over EUS-guided hepaticogastrostomy (EUS-HGS) when there is no gastric outlet obstruction (GOO), but EUS-HGS when GOO coexists; an option in benign obstruction only after multidisciplinary discussion; usable regardless of tumor resectability; **common bile duct (CBD) ≥15 mm** recommended, **≥12 to <15 mm** in selected cases with expertise; small-caliber LAMS, free-hand insertion, pure cutting current, intra-channel proximal flange release, fluoroscopy; no routine LAMS surveillance. **No consensus** on contraindications or on a coaxial double-pigtail plastic stent (DPPS) through the LAMS. Most statements (17/24) rest on very low- or low-quality evidence.

- **Rationale (pp. 4, 7):**
  - Statement 3 — duodenal stenosis is the most significant risk factor for LAMS dysfunction. With DMBO + GOO, a multicenter study reported **31.8% dysfunction after a mean of 166 days**, duodenal obstruction the only independent predictor; EUS-HGS gave longer stent patency than EUS-CDS with duodenal obstruction treated by duodenal stent, also in the EUS-guided gastroenterostomy setting.
  - Statement 2 — meta-analysis: EUS-CDS-L had shorter procedure time, fewer side effects, easier puncture and less recurrent biliary obstruction than EUS-HGS; recent comparative studies show largely comparable performance when both are feasible. Panel favored EUS-CDS-L for technical simplicity and wide adoption. **Not a blanket indication over EUS-HGS** — EUS-HGS stays appropriate in experienced hands and when EUS-CDS is not feasible or less suitable for anatomy or disease.
  - Statement 6 — randomized trials and a meta-analysis: primary EUS-CDS-L had clinical success similar to ERCP, higher technical success, less post-procedural pancreatitis and fewer reinterventions.
  - Statement 9 — multicenter data: EUS-CDS-L did not compromise feasibility of pancreatoduodenectomy or increase major postoperative complications.
  - Statements 7–8 — failed training statement still had 77.4% agreeing that EUS-CDS-L needs multidisciplinary support (hepato-pancreato-biliary surgery, interventional radiology).

- **Rationale (pp. 4, 9):**
  - Statement 10 — panel favored a standardized fluoroscopy-friendly position (prone or supine).
  - Statements 13–14 — EUS-CDS-L mostly suitable at ≥15 mm (judged the safest cutoff); **guidewire assistance preferred when CBD <15 mm**; ≥12 mm feasible with advanced expertise, proposed so more patients could benefit.
  - Statement 15 — smaller-caliber LAMS preferred: **"6–8 mm and 8-8 mm"** (as printed, p. 4).
  - Statement 11 — high-quality data lacking; panel questioned routine prophylaxis especially when biliary drainage is complete. The European Society of Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ESGE) suggests prophylaxis for all transmural procedures, on low-quality evidence.
  - Statement 12 — a meta-analysis found slight, non-significant superiority of LAMS over FC-SEMS (mainly reinterventions); some experts flagged relatively high EUS-CDS-L dysfunction with limited long-term patency data. Panel acknowledges agreement reflects experience and perceived ease of EC-LAMS rather than high-quality comparative evidence.
  - Statement 21 — fluoroscopy assists key procedural steps and intraprocedural adverse events.

### Rejected statements — no consensus (Table 2, p. 7)

| Statement | Votes · round | Agreement | Median · IQR |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Initial:* **relative contraindications** to EUS-CDS-L are coagulopathy, intervening vessels or ascites | 24/31 · 1 | 77.4% | 4 · 0.5 |
| *Revised:* **contraindications** to EUS-CDS-L are uncorrected coagulopathy and the absence of a window without intervening vessels or ascites | 25/32 · 2 | 78.1% | 4 · 1 |
| *Initial:* **double-pigtail plastic stent coaxial to the LAMS** should be considered based on stent-dysfunction risk | 21/32 · 1 | 65.6% | 4 · 1 |
| *Revised:* **coaxial DPPS within the LAMS** can be considered to reduce the risk of stent dysfunction | 24/31 · 2 | 77.4% | 4 · 1 |

- **Contraindications (p. 6–7):** some experts held uncorrected coagulopathy, intervening vessels and ascites to be contraindications (per the recent ESGE guideline); others called them relative — EUS-CDS-L still possible in uncorrected coagulopathy when PTBD is the alternative, and with ascites, since most EUS-CDS-L runs through a retroperitoneal anastomosis.
- **Coaxial DPPS (pp. 7, 9–10):** a multicenter randomized controlled trial (RCT; "BAMPI trial", 91 patients) found recurrent biliary obstruction lower with LAMS + DPPS (**9%**) than LAMS alone (**30%**), but **not** reintervention rate; a 41-patient multicenter retrospective study found no significant difference in clinical success, adverse events or recurrent obstruction. Some experts never use coaxial DPPS. The panel did not recommend routine DPPS — concerns about need for fluoroscopy, longer procedure, cost and complexity; named as a priority for further trials.

### Removed before voting (p. 3)
- EUS-guided gallbladder drainage (EUS-GBD) as rescue after failed ERCP and EUS-BD with a patent cystic duct — out of scope.
- "Correct coagulopathy, avoid large intervening vessels, and perform paracentesis in severe ascites before EUS-CDS-L" — removed as redundant per panelists' comments.

### Figure 2 — patient selection and decision algorithm (p. 10)
Summary infographic (CC BY-NC-ND; embed whole only). Its content:
- **Patient assessment:** clinical status and comorbidities; biliary obstruction (cause, level); imaging (cross-sectional + EUS); local expertise and available options.
- **Type of obstruction:** malignant (distal) → concomitant GOO? **No → EUS-CDS-L over EUS-HGS** (80.6%); **Yes → EUS-HGS over EUS-CDS-L** (84.4%); either way, EUS-CDS-L regardless of tumor resectability (84.4%). Benign → EUS-CDS-L among therapeutic options (EUS-guided rendezvous or antegrade stenting are alternatives) after multidisciplinary discussion (81.2%).
- **Indications box:** DMBO (93.7%) — suitable anatomy; CBD adequately dilated (preferably ≥15 mm); after failed ERCP. Benign (81.2%) — supported by expert consensus rather than evidence. Primary EUS-CDS-L (80.6%) — over ERCP in selected cases with appropriate expertise.
- **CBD diameter on imaging/EUS (90.6%):** **≥15 mm** recommended · **12–14 mm** can be considered in selected cases with appropriate expertise · **<12 mm generally not recommended**.
- **Other anatomic considerations:** ensure safe transduodenal access to the CBD; favor a straight, short access route.
- **"Contraindications (relative)" box, labelled "consensus 77.4%":** uncorrected coagulopathy; massive ascites; inability to access the bile duct endoscopically; intervening vessels in the puncture path not amenable to safe access.
- **Decision summary:** appropriate indication **AND** favorable anatomy (usually CBD ≥15 mm) **AND** no relative contraindications **AND** operator with adequate training and expertise → proceed with EUS-CDS-L.

- **17/24 (70.8%) very low or low** (Statements 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12, 15, 16, 17, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24); **4 (16.7%) low-moderate** (3, 13, 14, 18); **2 (8.3%) moderate** (1, 10); **1 (4.2%) high** (6).

## Contradictions / Open Questions

### Inconsistencies within the document
- **Statement 2 evidence level:** Table 3 rates it **2b, moderate quality**; section 3.7 lists it among the very low/low statements and names only Statements 1 and 10 as moderate.
- **Figure 2 labels a no-consensus item as consensus.** Its "Contraindications (relative)" box carries "consensus 77.4%" — the round-1 value of a statement **rejected** below the 80% threshold (round 2: 78.1%); its list (massive ascites, inability to access the bile duct endoscopically) also differs from both voted wordings.
- **Figure 2 "<12 mm: generally not recommended"** and "favor a straight, short access route" were not voted statements. Figure gives **12–14 mm**; Statement 14 says **≥12 mm and <15 mm**.
- **Threshold wording:** methods and abstract define consensus as **≥80%** and very strong as **≥90%**; results, Figure 1 and Discussion also write ">80%" and ">90%".
- **Domain counts:** 14 procedural statements circulated (p. 3) vs "among the 13 statements" in that domain (p. 4).
- **Panel invitation:** "invited 29 internationally renowned experts" (p. 2) vs "Of 36 invited experts, 32 agreed" (pp. 2–3, Figure 1).
- **Discussion:** "20/24, 83.3%" low-moderate or lower vs section 3.7's 17 + 4 = 21 statements.
- **"8-8 mm"** LAMS size as printed (p. 4) — the second size is unclear.

### Differences from US sources on file (as summarized on [[eus-guided-biliary-drainage]])
- **Non-US expert Delphi** (one North American panelist); not a society guideline; ungraded. US guidelines win where they conflict.
- **EUS-CDS vs EUS-HGS without GOO** — [[asge-2024-therapeutic-eus-biliary]] Rec 2: either EUS-HGS or EUS-CDS after failed ERCP in distal malignant obstruction (conditional, low); this source prefers EUS-CDS-L (Statement 2, 80.6%), while stating it is not an indication over EUS-HGS in all scenarios.
- **Antibiotic prophylaxis** — the wiki page cites ACG/ASGE 2025 ([[acg-2025-eus-quality]]) to strongly consider intravenous (IV) broad-spectrum prophylaxis for all interventional EUS, including EUS-BD; this source: insufficient evidence to recommend it (Statement 11).
- **Future pancreatoduodenectomy** — ASGE 2024 (as summarized on the page) warns EUS-CDS can inflame the porta hepatis and complicate a later Whipple, and asks for surgical discussion first; this source: EUS-CDS-L can be considered regardless of resectability (Statement 9, low-quality evidence).
- **Ascites** — ASGE 2024 (as summarized): drain ascites before or at the time of EUS-BD; this source removed its paracentesis statement and reached no consensus on ascites as a contraindication.
- **Primary drainage** — [[esge-2026-biliary-strictures]] (non-US) keeps ERCP first and allows primary EUS-BD only in expert centers with CBD ≥12 mm; [[jges-2026-eus-biliary-drainage-consensus]] calls EUS-BD not standard for unresectable distal obstruction. This source: primary EUS-CDS-L can be considered over ERCP in selected cases with expertise (Statement 6, 80.6%) — similar conditions, more permissive direction.
- **Coaxial DPPS** — [[esge-2026-biliary-strictures]] says LAMS dysfunction is mitigated by an FC-SEMS or a coaxial DPPS through the LAMS; this panel failed to reach consensus on coaxial DPPS (77.4%).
- **Agreement:** CBD ≥15 mm as the comfortable floor matches ESGE 2026 (CBD ≤15 mm predicts misdeployment) and JGES 2026 (technically challenging under 15 mm); EUS-HGS with GOO/duodenal obstruction matches ESGE 2026; training in both ERCP and interventional EUS matches JGES 2026.
