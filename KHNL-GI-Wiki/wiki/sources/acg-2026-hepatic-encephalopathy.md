## Summary

This guideline updates the diagnosis, treatment, and prevention of [[hepatic-encephalopathy|hepatic encephalopathy (HE)]] in [[portal-hypertension|cirrhosis]], spanning covert/minimal HE (CHE/MHE) through overt HE (OHE). It reframes HE as a [[disorders-of-gut-brain-interaction|gut–brain axis]] disorder, emphasizes that serum ammonia should **not** drive diagnosis or titration, and strengthens the role of nutrition, [[rifaximin]], and shunt/transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS)-directed strategies. It also argues that HE burden is under-weighted in transplant prioritization, supporting earlier [[liver-transplantation]] evaluation.

### Inpatient Management of HE

| # | Recommendation | Strength · certainty of evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Patients with HE: suggest **against** routine testing of serum ammonia to guide HE treatment decisions | Conditional · very low |
| 6 | Cirrhosis and confusion **without new-onset focal neurologic deficits**: suggest **against** routine brain imaging | Conditional · very low |
| 7 | OHE: recommend **lactulose** to improve patient outcomes and prevent recurrence of OHE episodes | Strong · moderate |

### Prevention of HE Recurrence

| # | Recommendation | Strength · certainty of evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | **After an initial episode of OHE**: recommend lactulose titrated to **2–3 soft bowel movements (BMs) daily** as outpatient first-line therapy for prevention of HE recurrence | Strong · high |
| 11 | Patients treated with lactulose for HE: suggest using the [[bristol-stool-form-scale\|Bristol Stool Scale]] along with BM frequency for **outpatient titration** of lactulose to reduce readmissions | Conditional · very low |
| 12 | Cirrhosis and OHE: suggest rifaximin **in the outpatient setting** to prevent HE recurrence | Conditional · low |
| 13 | OHE **on lactulose maintenance** who experience **recurrent** HE episodes: recommend **adding rifaximin** | Strong · high |
| 14 | OHE with **persistent symptoms despite lactulose and rifaximin**: suggest adding **zinc** supplementation **in those with low blood zinc levels** | Conditional · very low |
| 15 | Suggest health information technology interventions (e.g., Patient Buddy App, electronic clinical reminders), when feasible, to optimize HE management | Conditional (printed "conditional recommendations") · very low |
| 16 | Suggest **shunt embolization** in **refractory HE on optimized medical therapy** with **adequate hepatic function** and **no contraindications** | Conditional · very low |

## Key Concepts

29 key concepts (Table 3) — **ungraded** (GRADE not applied); condensed, numbered as printed.

### CHE/MHE

1. Clinical diagnosis of CHE (minimal or grade 1 HE) has low reproducibility → **define CHE by excluding OHE (grade 2 or higher)** in the appropriate clinical context.
2. **Isolated asterixis without alterations in behavior or alertness is not enough to diagnose OHE.**
3. Patients with cirrhosis and **≥1** of the following could be prioritized for MHE testing:
   - a. Hypoalbuminemia
   - b. Decompensated cirrhosis (e.g., ascites, variceal bleeding, or jaundice)
   - c. Clinically significant portal hypertension or portosystemic shunts
   - d. Nonspecific cognitive complaints, difficulty concentrating, impaired quality of life, navigation issues/traffic violations, and falls
   - e. Difficulty operating heavy machinery
   - f. Commercial drivers/pilots
4. Cognitive complaints in cirrhosis may have causes other than MHE → assess for alternatives (sleep apnea, alcohol-related conditions, mild cognitive impairment), ideally in a specialized clinic or by referral — especially with **isolated delayed memory issues or isolated sleep issues**.
5. Cognitive testing for CHE/MHE and subsequent lactulose or rifaximin therapy may improve health-related quality of life.
6. Testing options for MHE/CHE, chosen by local expertise and norms: psychometric hepatic encephalopathy score, Stroop EncephalApp, animal naming test, inhibitory control test, continuous reaction time, critical flicker frequency.
7. **Second-line agents for MHE/CHE** could be considered in certain patients: BCAA/L-ornithine L-aspartate (LOLA) if **sarcopenia/frailty**; **zinc if low serum levels**; l-carnitine if **concomitant skeletal muscle cramps**.
8. Any theragnostic lactulose trial → evaluate adherence and symptom improvement **4–8 wk after prescription** to decide whether to continue.

### Inpatient Management of OHE

9. Cirrhosis and confusion: clinical scores — **West Haven (WH) criteria, Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS)** — identify high-grade HE needing triage/transfer to a **step-down/medical intensive care unit (SDU/MICU)** to prevent complications such as aspiration pneumonia.
10. Thorough evaluation for **bleeding, infection, sedating medications, and toxic and metabolic abnormalities** is necessary to identify and treat precipitating or contributing factors.
11. **Normal blood ammonia** with altered mental status → consider **other diagnoses in addition to OHE**.
12. **No recovery after 48–72 h** of adequate HE therapy and precipitant reversal → evaluate for alternative causes of altered mental status, inadequately addressed or unrecognized precipitants, or shunts.

### Prevention of HE Recurrence

13. A multidisciplinary **medication reconciliation at hospital discharge** is necessary — prophylactic therapy against recurrent HE prescribed; potentially harmful medications dose-reduced or stopped.
14. Serum ammonia should **not** guide initiation or titration of HE therapy, or evaluation of HE recurrence, in the outpatient setting.
15. Review medication lists to identify and minimize drugs that may affect mental status assessment or HE risk — e.g., **opiates, benzodiazepines, antiepileptic pain medications (gabapentin, pregabalin), sleep medications (zolpidem, zaleplon, eszopiclone), and proton pump inhibitors**.
16. HE therapy may be **de-escalated and potentially discontinued stepwise** in patients who clinically recompensate.
17. Oral and written advice to **avoid driving** should be given to patients and caregivers (expert consensus) in patients with **recent (<3 mo) episodes of OHE**.
18. All patients with HE and their caregivers should receive HE-based educational materials (e.g., www.cirrhosiscare.ca).
19. Screen caregivers for caregiver burnout and provide support as appropriate.

### Sarcopenia and Nutrition

20. All patients with HE (including MHE/CHE) should be evaluated for impaired muscle health/physical fitness with available tools (sarcopenia/myosteatosis, skeletal muscle strength, physical function, frailty, cardiorespiratory fitness) — all important targets of HE therapy.
21. Impaired muscle health/physical fitness in any form (sarcopenia, physical frailty, deconditioning) and myosteatosis (fat infiltration into muscle) → increased HE risk.
22. **Critically ill** patients with HE should receive **up to 2 g/kg/d** protein.
23. Sarcopenia is **not by itself an indication or a contraindication for TIPS**, but may be associated with increased post-TIPS HE and post-TIPS mortality.
24. Ammonia rise after anaerobiosis has not been associated with elevated HE risk and should not discourage prescribing exercise in cirrhosis.
25. Any form of HE may increase risk of exercise-induced injury → supervision by exercise professionals, caregivers, or other surrogates is key to prevent exercise-related adverse events.

### Liver Transplant and HE

26. **Adding 4–5 MELD points**, regardless of the MELD score iteration used, more accurately reflects 90-day mortality of inpatient OHE and outpatient CHE.
27. Multiple HE episodes can cause persistent cognitive impairment that may not recover after liver transplant (LT) → early LT may additionally benefit brain function.
28. **Early LT evaluation** should be considered with **high MELD3.0 scores, severe HE (grades III–IV), or frequent HE episodes**, to improve long-term outcomes.
29. **Persistent confusion in an inpatient setting is a relative contraindication to transplant.**

## Clinical Relevance

Reinforces lactulose → add rifaximin as the backbone, formally deprioritizes ammonia monitoring, and integrates nutrition/frailty and TIPS-period prophylaxis. Relevant to inpatient decompensation management alongside [[acute-on-chronic-liver-failure]] and [[ascites]] workups.
