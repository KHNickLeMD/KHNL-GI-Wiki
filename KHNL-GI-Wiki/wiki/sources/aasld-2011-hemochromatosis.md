## Summary
The 2011 AASLD guideline is the hepatology-society companion to the colorectal/ACG view of [[hereditary-hemochromatosis|hereditary hemochromatosis]] (HH). It frames HH around documentation of increased iron stores (elevated serum ferritin reflecting hepatic iron) with genotypic confirmation by *HFE* C282Y homozygosity or C282Y/H63D compound heterozygosity. It emphasizes that most patients are now identified while still asymptomatic, before fibrosis or cirrhosis, by serologic iron markers (transferrin saturation [TS] and ferritin).

The diagnostic algorithm starts with combined TS + ferritin (not a single test) in patients with suggestive symptoms, physical findings, or family history; if either is abnormal (TS ≥45% or ferritin above ULN), proceed to *HFE* mutation analysis. First-degree relatives of a proband should be screened by both genotype and phenotype simultaneously. The guideline repositions liver biopsy as primarily a **staging/prognostic** tool rather than a diagnostic one in the *HFE* era: biopsy is reserved for C282Y homozygotes with elevated ALT/AST or ferritin >1000 µg/L (high cirrhosis risk, 20–45% prevalence), and for non–*HFE* phenotypes where hepatic iron concentration and staining help characterize the disease. C282Y homozygotes with ferritin <1000 µg/L and normal transaminases can proceed directly to phlebotomy without biopsy.

Treatment is therapeutic phlebotomy (weekly as tolerated) to a target ferritin of 50–100 µg/L, followed by lifelong maintenance phlebotomy; dietary change is unnecessary but vitamin C and iron supplements should be avoided. Iron chelation (deferoxamine or deferasirox) is reserved for secondary iron overload from dyserythropoietic syndromes or chronic hemolytic anemia where phlebotomy is not feasible. Patients with HH and cirrhosis warrant HCC surveillance (relative risk ~20; annual incidence ~3–4%). General-population genetic screening is **not** recommended given incomplete C282Y penetrance (~30% of homozygotes lack phenotypic iron excess).

## Key Findings / Claims — Recommendations
All 16 numbered recommendations, near-verbatim (GRADE class 1 = strong / 2 = weak; level A/B/C). Where a recommendation contained two graded clauses they are kept together:

1. Patients with abnormal iron studies should be evaluated as patients with hemochromatosis, even in the absence of symptoms. (Class 1, Level A)
2. All patients with evidence of liver disease should be evaluated for hemochromatosis. (1B)
3. In a patient with suggestive symptoms, physical findings, or family history, a combination of TS and ferritin should be obtained rather than relying on a single test (1B); if either is abnormal (TS ≥45% or ferritin above the upper limit of normal), then *HFE* mutation analysis should be performed. (1B)
4. Diagnostic strategies using serum iron markers should target high-risk groups such as those with a family history of HH or those with suspected organ involvement. (1B)
5. We recommend screening (iron studies and *HFE* mutation analysis) of first-degree relatives of patients with *HFE*-related HH to detect early disease and prevent complications. (1A)
6. Liver biopsy is recommended to stage the degree of liver disease in C282Y homozygotes or compound heterozygotes if liver enzymes (ALT, AST) are elevated or if ferritin is >1000 µg/L. (1B)
7. Liver biopsy is recommended for diagnosis and prognosis in patients with phenotypic markers of iron overload who are not C282Y homozygotes or compound heterozygotes. (2C)
8. In patients with non–*HFE*-related HH, data on hepatic iron concentration is useful, along with histopathologic iron staining, to determine the degree and cellular distribution of iron loading present. (2C)
9. Patients with hemochromatosis and iron overload should undergo therapeutic phlebotomy weekly as tolerated (1A); target levels of phlebotomy should be a ferritin level of 50–100 µg/L. (1B)
10. In the absence of indicators suggestive of significant liver disease (ALT, AST elevation), C282Y homozygotes who have an elevated ferritin (but <1000 µg/L) should proceed to phlebotomy without a liver biopsy. (1B)
11. Patients with end-organ damage due to iron overload should undergo regular phlebotomy to the same endpoints as indicated above. (1A)
12. During treatment for HH, dietary adjustments are unnecessary. Vitamin C supplements and iron supplements should be avoided. (1C)
13. Patients with hemochromatosis and iron overload should be monitored for reaccumulation of iron and undergo maintenance phlebotomy (1A); target levels of phlebotomy should be a ferritin level of 50–100 µg/L. (1B)
14. We recommend treatment by phlebotomy of patients with non-HFE iron overload who have an elevated hepatic iron concentration (HIC). (1B)
15. Iron chelation with either deferoxamine mesylate or deferasirox is recommended in iron-overloaded patients with dyserythropoietic syndromes or chronic hemolytic anemia. (1A)
16. Average-risk population screening for HH is not recommended. (1B)

Additional guidance (narrative): patients with HH and cirrhosis should undergo HCC surveillance per AASLD (RR ≈20; annual incidence 3–4%); screening for non–*HFE*-related HH is not recommended (rare, <5% of cases, testing largely research-only).

## Relevance to Wiki
- Adds the AASLD hepatology perspective to [[hereditary-hemochromatosis]] (previously sourced only from ACG 2019 and ACG 2017 liver chemistries). Confirms, from a liver-society vantage, the ferritin >1000 µg/L biopsy threshold, the 50–100 µg/L phlebotomy target, the relative-risk ~20 for HCC in HH cirrhosis, and the no-population-screening stance.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- No material contradiction with [[acg-2019-hereditary-hemochromatosis|ACG 2019]]; the two largely agree on TS+ferritin → *HFE* testing, biopsy reserved for staging, phlebotomy to a low-normal ferritin, and family screening. This older edition predates refinements in non-invasive fibrosis staging (e.g. elastography) — defer to newer sources for fibrosis assessment.
