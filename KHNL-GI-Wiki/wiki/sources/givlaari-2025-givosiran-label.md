## Summary

The FDA prescribing information for **givosiran**, fetched to close the gap [[givosiran]] states on its own face: [[aga-2023-acute-hepatic-porphyria|AGA 2023]] recommends givosiran for recurrent attacks of acute hepatic porphyria (AHP) and gives the route and interval — *monthly subcutaneous* — but **states no weight-based dose**, and the page told the reader to check the labeling before prescribing. The label supplies it: **2.5 mg/kg subcutaneously once monthly, dosed on actual body weight.**

It also supplies the piece that matters more in practice than the starting dose — **what to do when the transaminases rise.** AGA 2023 says to interrupt for severe or clinically significant elevations and to monitor liver enzymes monthly for the first 3–6 months, but gives no resumption dose. The label does: after interruption and improvement, **restart at 1.25 mg/kg monthly**, and if that is tolerated without recurrence, **step back up to 2.5 mg/kg**. The transaminase signal is quantified — alanine aminotransferase (ALT) ≥3 × upper limit of normal (ULN) in **15%** of treated patients in the placebo-controlled trial, occurring **principally between months 3 and 5**, which is what the monitoring window is built around.

Two safety items on the label are **not** on the wiki from the AGA update. **Acute pancreatitis** is a postmarketing adverse reaction with its own warning: consider it in a givosiran-treated patient with acute upper abdominal pain, enzyme elevation or imaging findings, and consider interruption or discontinuation for severe cases. And givosiran **raises the concentration of sensitive CYP1A2 and CYP2D6 substrates** — avoid co-administration where a small concentration change could be serious, otherwise reduce the substrate dose. The label also converts the wiki's "monitor homocysteine" into an action: if it is elevated, **assess folate, B12 and B6, and consider a vitamin B6–containing supplement.**

The only contraindication is **severe hypersensitivity to givosiran**; anaphylaxis occurred in <1% of trial patients. Renal signal: 15% of the givosiran arm had a renally-related adverse reaction, with a **median creatinine rise of 0.07 mg/dL at month 3**. The indication is **adults** only — pediatric safety and effectiveness are not established, which is narrower than the European Medicines Agency's ≥12 years.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Indication (label §1)**

- **Adults with acute hepatic porphyria.** No age-subgroup, attack-frequency or genotype restriction is printed in the indication itself.
- **Pediatric (§8.4):** safety and effectiveness **not established**.
- **Geriatric (§8.5):** too few patients ≥65 y studied to say whether response differs.

**Dosage and administration (label §2)**

| Item | Label value |
|---|---|
| Recommended dose | **2.5 mg/kg subcutaneously once monthly**, based on **actual body weight** |
| Strength supplied | **189 mg/mL**, single-dose vial, ready to use, no reconstitution or dilution |
| Missed dose | Give as soon as possible, then **resume monthly intervals from the new administration date** |
| Dose after transaminase interruption | **Reduce to 1.25 mg/kg once monthly** on resumption |
| Re-escalation | If 1.25 mg/kg is tolerated without recurrence of severe or clinically significant transaminase elevation → **increase back to 2.5 mg/kg monthly** |
| Administered by | **Healthcare professional only** |

**Administration technique (§2.2)**

- Withdraw with a **21-gauge or larger** needle; **divide any dose requiring >1.5 mL equally into multiple syringes**.
- Swap to a **25- or 27-gauge, ½″ or ⅝″** needle to inject.
- Sites: **abdomen, back or side of the upper arms, thighs**; rotate. Never into scar tissue or reddened/inflamed/swollen skin.
- Abdomen: **avoid a 5 cm circle around the navel**. Multiple injections for one dose: **≥2 cm apart** from previous sites.
- Ensure medical support for anaphylaxis is available at administration. Discard unused drug.

**Contraindication (§4)**

- **Severe hypersensitivity to givosiran** — reactions have included anaphylaxis.

**Warnings and precautions (§5)**

| Warning | Label detail and action |
|---|---|
| **Anaphylactic reaction** (5.1) | <1% of trial patients. Have medical support available; **discontinue immediately** and treat if it occurs |
| **Hepatic toxicity** (5.2) | **ALT ≥3 × ULN in 15%**, primarily **between months 3 and 5**. Liver function tests **before starting, monthly for the first 6 months**, then as clinically indicated. **Interrupt or discontinue** for severe or clinically significant elevations |
| **Renal toxicity** (5.3) | Serum creatinine rise / estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) fall. **15%** of the givosiran arm had a renally-related adverse reaction; **median creatinine increase 0.07 mg/dL at month 3**. Monitor as clinically indicated |
| **Injection site reactions** (5.4) | **25%** — erythema, pain, pruritus, rash, discoloration, swelling. Of 12 patients with reactions, **11 (92%) mild, 1 (8%) moderate**; one patient (2%) had a transient **recall reaction** at a prior site with a later dose |
| **Blood homocysteine increased** (5.5) | **15 of 93 (16%)** in the ENVISION open-label extension; clinical relevance unknown. Measure **before starting** and monitor; if elevated, **assess folate, B12 and B6** and **consider a vitamin B6–containing supplement** (monotherapy or multivitamin) |
| **Pancreatitis** (5.6) | **Acute pancreatitis, some severe**, reported. Consider the diagnosis with acute upper abdominal pain, clinically significant enzyme elevation and/or imaging findings; **consider interruption or discontinuation for severe cases**. Also listed under postmarketing experience (6.3) |

**Adverse reactions (§6.1) — pivotal placebo-controlled ENVISION, 48 givosiran vs 46 placebo, monthly for up to 6 months (median 5.5 mo)**

- Most frequent (≥20%): **nausea 27%**, **injection site reactions 25%**.
- Table 1 (≥5% more frequent than placebo): rash 17% vs 4%; serum creatinine increase 15% vs 4%; transaminase elevations 13% vs 2%; fatigue 10% vs 4%.
- **One permanent discontinuation**, for elevated transaminases.
- Immunogenicity (6.2): treatment-emergent anti-drug antibodies in **1 of 111 (0.9%)**, with no detectable effect on efficacy, safety or pharmacokinetics.

**Drug interactions (§7.1)**

- Givosiran **increases the concentration of sensitive CYP1A2 and CYP2D6 substrates**. **Avoid** concomitant use where a minimal concentration change may cause serious or life-threatening toxicity; if unavoidable, **decrease the substrate dose** per its own labeling.

**Pregnancy and lactation (§8.1–8.2)**

- **No human data.** Animal reproduction studies in rabbits showed adverse developmental outcomes at maternally toxic doses. Weigh benefits and risks.
- Label states the disease-side risk explicitly: **porphyria attacks occur in 24–95% of pregnant AHP patients, with maternal mortality 2–42%**, and pregnancy in AHP carries higher rates of spontaneous abortion, hypertension and low birth weight.
- **Lactation:** no data on presence in human milk or effects on the breastfed child.

**Mechanism (§11, 12.1)**

- Aminolevulinate synthase 1 (ALAS1)-directed small interfering RNA (siRNA) covalently linked to a **triantennary N-acetylgalactosamine (GalNAc)** ligand for hepatocyte delivery.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **No contradiction with [[aga-2023-acute-hepatic-porphyria]].** The label fills gaps the AGA update left open rather than disputing it; the monthly subcutaneous route and the interrupt-for-transaminase-elevation rule agree.
- **Scope of approval differs by region, and the label is the narrower one.** The label indicates givosiran for **adults**; the European Medicines Agency approval is **≥12 years**. The wiki already carried both facts from the AGA update — the label confirms the US side and adds that pediatric safety and effectiveness are *not established*, which is a stronger statement than "adults only".
- **The label carries no attack-frequency threshold.** AGA 2023 Best Practice Advice 7 restricts prophylactic givosiran to **≥4 attacks per year**; the labelled indication is simply "adults with AHP". The guideline is the higher-priority source and governs patient selection on the wiki; the label governs the dose. Worth knowing that prescribing below the AGA threshold is not off-label.
- **Open:** the label gives no dose adjustment for renal or hepatic impairment at baseline, and no guidance on how long to continue therapy. Neither is answerable from any ingested source.
