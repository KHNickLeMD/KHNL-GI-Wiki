## Summary

A consensus meeting of **39 experts** (São Paulo, June 2016) that voted on **76 questions** covering patient selection, indications, contraindications, preparation, technique, multidisciplinary follow-up and adverse events for the **intragastric balloon (IGB)**. **Consensus was defined as ≥70% agreement**; questions reaching <70% are reported as *no consensus* with the vote split, which the document preserves rather than hiding. Each statement below carries **the agreement percentage as the document prints it** — that figure is the only "strength" the document assigns; there are **no GRADE ratings and no evidence levels**.

The panel also pooled its own case experience: **41,863 IGBs**, mean total body weight loss (%TBWL) **18.4% ± 2.9%**, weight loss **18.3 ± 4.4 kg**, **non-responder** (%TBWL <10%) rate **8.3% ± 6.7%**. Adverse events after the adaptation period: **2.5%** overall, most often **hyperinflation (0.9%)** and **spontaneous deflation (0.8%)**; gastric ulcers in 141 cases; **no esophageal or gastric perforation at implantation**; **early removal for intolerance 2.2%**.

Its clinical value to a gastroenterology wiki is the part no society guideline prints: **the contraindication list** (absolute, relative, and explicitly *not* a contraindication), the **BMI floor of >25 kg/m²**, the **age rules**, and the **adverse-event removal triggers**.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Indications and choice of balloon (Table 3, with consensus %)

| Statement | Consensus |
|---|---|
| Minimum age **≥12 years**, with established puberty, after failure of clinical treatment and/or with co-morbidities, evaluated by the multidisciplinary team and with **written authorization from both parents** | 84.9% |
| **No maximum age limit**, if the patient is in adequate clinical condition for endoscopic procedures | 96.8% |
| **Minimum BMI >25 kg/m² (overweight)** with progressive weight gain and refractory to clinical treatment | 84.4% |
| BMI **25–30 kg/m²** — balloon type at the physician's discretion | 83.9% |
| BMI **31–40 kg/m²** — balloon type at the physician's discretion | 90.3% |
| BMI **>40 kg/m²** — balloon type at the physician's discretion | 71.0% |

### Contraindications (Table 3)

**Absolute**

| | Consensus |
|---|---|
| Active gastric ulcers in the **body or fundus**, without signs of bleeding | 83.3% |
| Active gastric ulcers in the **antrum**, without signs of bleeding | 75.0% |
| **Ulcers in any other location** without signs of bleeding | 71.9% |
| **Previous gastric surgery** | 93.8% |
| **Esophageal varices** | 84.4% |
| **Gastric varices** | 90.6% |
| **Hiatal hernia >5 cm** | 71.0% |
| **Use of anticoagulants** | 87.5% |

**Relative:** angioectasias without signs of bleeding (75.0%) · eosinophilic esophagitis (81.3%) · HIV positive, immunocompetent (96.9%) · psychiatric disorders without control or treatment (75.8%).

**Explicitly *not* a contraindication:** esophagitis **Los Angeles grade A or B** (80.7%) · gastritis (83.3%) · hyperplastic or benign polyps (87.1%) · ***H. pylori* positive** (90.7%).

- **No consensus** on: congestive gastropathy (relative 51.6% / absolute 48.4%), familial gastric polyposis (relative 62.5% / absolute 37.5%), antiplatelet agents (relative 46.9%), and **Los Angeles grade C or D esophagitis** (absolute 52.9% / relative 44.1%, on the view that it can be treated and the balloon placed afterwards).
- Ulcers are contraindications **in the absence of active bleeding**; active bleeding raises the perforation risk further.
- *H. pylori*: where endoscopy shows no lesion justifying treatment, **46.9% leave eradication to the physician's discretion** — below the consensus threshold.

### Preprocedure evaluation and the multidisciplinary team (Table 4)

| Statement | Consensus |
|---|---|
| **No mandatory endoscopy before placement** — it can be done by the implanting physician during the procedure, suspending it if necessary | 84.4% |
| **No obligation to obtain prior imaging** (other than what the anesthesiologist requires) | 84.4% |
| **A dietician is mandatory** on the multidisciplinary team | **100%** |
| The dietician should **follow the patient throughout the treatment period** | 90.0% |
| A **psychologist is not mandatory** | 70.0% |
| An **endocrinologist is not mandatory** | 93.3% |
| A **nutritionist (physician) is not mandatory** | 100% |
| A **psychiatrist is not mandatory** | 96.8% |

- **Laboratory work before placement reached no consensus:** 41.9% always, 41.9% at the physician's discretion, 16.1% only where a clinical condition justifies it.

### Preparation, placement and removal (Table 4)

| Statement | Consensus |
|---|---|
| Placement in a clinic with **advanced life support (type II or III)** and backup transfer service | 83.9% |
| **Fasting ≥8 hours** before implant | 90.3% |
| A **prior liquid diet is not necessary** for placement | 83.9% |
| **Prior proton pump inhibitor (PPI) use is not necessary** for placement | 90.3% |
| **Minimum filling volume for nonadjustable liquid balloons: 500 mL** | 87.1% |
| **Methylene blue always in liquid balloons** as a leak precaution, except in allergy | **100%** |
| Filling substance: **saline with methylene blue** | 90.0% |
| **Carbonated cola (zero/diet) intake** as preparation for removal | 78.1% |
| **Liquid diet ≥2 days** before removal | 90.9% |
| **Fasting ≥12 hours** before removal | 80.7% |
| Preferred removal accessory: **endoscopic hybrid jaw grasper (alligator + rat tooth)** | 75.0% |
| **Esophageal overtube** in selected difficult removals | 74.1% |
| **Clinical follow-up for ≥6 months after removal** | 89.7% |

- **Balloon volume beyond the minimum had no consensus.** Nonadjustable: 64.5% accept maximum filling **>700 mL** under medical criteria. Adjustable: initial **500–600 mL** (54.8%) vs **400–500 mL** (38.7%); additional volume at readjustment **200–300 mL** (42.9%) / 100–200 mL (25.0%) / 300–400 mL (14.3%). **Downward adjustment for intolerance: minimum 300–400 mL** (59.3%).
- **Sedation reached no consensus** for either implant or explant — the vote split across conscious sedation, deep/general sedation with or without intubation, and anesthesiologist's choice.
- **Prophylactic antifungal drugs to prevent hyperinflation are not recommended** (68.8%) — there is no scientific proof the cause is fungal.

### Postimplant medication and the adaptation period (Table 5)

| Statement | Consensus |
|---|---|
| **Prescribe medication to avoid reactive symptoms after implantation, for 3–5 days** | 93.8% |
| **Ondansetron** | 85.2% |
| **Hyoscine / scopolamine** | 88.9% |
| **Steroid (dexamethasone)** | 70.4% |
| **Dimenhydrinate** | 82.8% |
| **Analgesic** | 92.6% |
| **PPI** | 96.3% |
| **PPI is mandatory throughout the entire treatment** | 87.5% |
| **Metoclopramide is not recommended** (increases motility, may worsen symptoms) | 70.4% |
| **Anti-inflammatory drugs are not recommended** (gastric injury risk) | 96.3% |
| **Anxiolytics / antidepressants are not recommended** | 78.6% |
| **Physical activity may start after the adaptation period** | 87.1% |

- **Bromopride** for nausea and vomiting: 51.9% — no consensus. **Aprepitant** 51.9% — no consensus (only 7 of 32 panellists had experience with it).
- **IGB plus anti-obesity drugs** can be used at medical discretion (69.0%); 27.6% would restrict the combination to binge eaters, grazers and sweet-eaters; 3.5% reject it.
- **Air travel:** no consensus — 45.2% no restriction, 48.4% after the adaptation period, 6.5% after 30 days.
- **Alcohol:** non-carbonated alcoholic drinks not recommended during treatment (46.9%, no consensus); **carbonated drinks are not recommended** during treatment.

### Adverse events — when the balloon must come out

**Removal is recommended by consensus for:**

| Event | Consensus |
|---|---|
| **Symptomatic hyperinflation** of the device | 96.9% |
| **Moderate or severe acute pancreatitis** | 90.6% |
| **Gastric ulcer with a nonadjustable balloon** | 90.0% |
| **Recurrent antral impaction** | 86.7% |
| **Pregnancy** — remove, usually in the **second trimester**, after consulting the obstetrician | 81.3% |
| **Recurrent hydroelectrolytic disorder** | 76.7% |
| **Gastrointestinal bleeding successfully treated by endoscopic methods alone** | 76.5% |
| **Patient request, for whatever reason** | (Table 2 consensus point) |
| **Possible balloon rupture (green urine)** — removal is **mandatory within 72 hours** | (Table 2 consensus point) |

**And when it need not:**
- **Mild pancreatitis — removal is not mandatory** (76.7%).
- **Gastrointestinal bleeding that stopped spontaneously** — the balloon can stay (84.4%).
- **Severe erosive esophagitis — do *not* remove before appropriate treatment** (87.1%); removal risks esophageal tearing or perforation.
- **Mallory-Weiss syndrome — treat with the balloon in place** (bleeding risk during removal).
- **Asymptomatic spontaneous hyperinflation without gastric lesions** — 64.5% manage conservatively (19.4% remove with simultaneous substitution).
- **Antral impaction** can first be managed by **repositioning**; removal is the answer only if it recurs (86.7%).
- **Adjustable balloon with an ulcer:** 53.9% remove even if the patient disagrees; where removal is declined, **reposition the filling catheter and treat** — **except for deep ulcers with high perforation risk, which must come out**.
- **Fungal colonization of the balloon:** no consensus and, notably, **0% recommended removal for it** (36.4% do nothing; 27.3% stop the PPI; 18.2% stop the PPI and add an antifungal; 18.2% antifungal alone).
- **Thromboembolic event:** removal recommended by only 43.8% — no consensus.
- **"Premature removal" is defined as explant up to 1 month after implant** for adverse events or patient request (84.4%).

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **Consensus percentage is not evidence quality.** Every statement here is an expert vote with a ≥70% bar; the document assigns **no evidence grade**, and several 70%–75% statements (steroid in the adaptation period, hiatal hernia >5 cm as absolute, ulcer in any other location) cleared the bar narrowly. Where [[aga-2021-intragastric-balloons]] — a graded AGA guideline — speaks on the same question, **AGA keeps the wiki's pages**: it is the higher-tier, newer, US source.
- **No endoscopy before placement (84.4%)** is a markedly more permissive position than US practice and than the document's own contraindication list implies — nearly every absolute contraindication (ulcers, varices, hiatal hernia size) is an **endoscopic** finding, and the panel's answer is that the implanting endoscopist sees them during the implant procedure and aborts if necessary.
- **Brazilian practice and device availability differ from the US.** The pooled series is dominated by balloons not all marketed in the United States, and the panel's BMI >25 floor is wider than any US indication.
- **Where the panel failed to reach consensus, it is recorded as a split, not resolved** — sedation, balloon volumes beyond the minimum, laboratory testing, air travel, alcohol, thromboembolism, and fungal colonization. Those remain open questions, not recommendations.
