## Summary

Commentary to increase awareness among gastroenterologists of alpha-gal syndrome, an emerging IgE-mediated allergy to galactose-alpha-1,3-galactose (an oligosaccharide on the cells of all nonprimate mammals). Reactions occur **1 to several hours after** ingestion of mammalian meat or mammalian-derived products and can cause anaphylaxis, GI symptoms, and skin changes. Sensitization is understood to follow a **tick bite** (Lone Star tick / *Amblyomma americanum* in the US) or parasitic infection. In the US, reports cluster in the range of the Lone Star tick (New York and Iowa to Texas and Florida).

The CPU focuses on the increasingly recognized **GI phenotype**: nonspecific abdominal pain, diarrhea, nausea, and vomiting **without** predominant skin, respiratory, or circulatory symptoms. These patients often present to primary care, ERs, or GI clinics and are commonly misdiagnosed as IBS or other functional GI illness unless the specific alpha-gal IgE blood test is sent. Research on GI alpha-gal is nascent; formal ratings of evidence quality/strength were not performed because no systematic review was done.

Mechanism (Figure 1): the ingested alpha-gal antigen binds to fat in a glycolipid, is absorbed and incorporated into chylomicrons, and enters the circulation in ~2 hours; it then binds IgE on GI mast cells, which degranulate and release histamine and other mediators acting on sensory nerves (pain), smooth muscle (contraction), and mucous glands. Two GI observational studies (16 patients in North Carolina; 359 in Virginia) found ~three-quarters of alpha-gal IgE+ GI patients improved on an avoidance diet. Among 91 oral-food-challenge–proven patients, abdominal pain (71%) and vomiting (22%) were most common, and 40.7% had GI symptoms alone.

## Key Findings / Claims (narrative — no numbered Best Practice Advice)

- **Antigen / mechanism:** allergy is to galactose-alpha-1,3-galactose (alpha-gal), present on all nonprimate mammals. IgE-mediated; delayed (hours) because antigen must be absorbed on fat via chylomicrons (~2 h to circulation) before binding GI mast cell IgE.
- **Sensitization:** occurs after tick bite (US: Lone Star tick, *Amblyomma americanum*; principal host deer) or parasitic infection (e.g., *Ascaris*). Lone Star tick is the only US tick that bites humans in the larval phase.
- **Geography (US):** range of *Amblyomma americanum* — Southeast, mid-Atlantic, Midwest, East Central Census regions (NY/Iowa → TX/FL). International reports: Australia, South Africa, Western Europe, Japan (all continents except Antarctica).
- **GI presentation:** abdominal pain, diarrhea, nausea, vomiting without predominant skin/respiratory/circulatory symptoms. Symptom stats from OFC-proven cohorts: abdominal pain 71%, vomiting 22%, GI-only reactions 40.7%. Delayed reactions (hives/anaphylaxis) typically occur 3–5 hours after eating mammalian meat.
- **When to consider it:** unexplained GI symptoms (abdominal pain, diarrhea, nausea, vomiting) in patients who live/have lived in an alpha-gal–prevalent area → send **serum alpha-gal IgE**. Features raising likelihood: **nighttime awakening from sleep with GI distress**, history of tick bites, outdoor pursuits.
- **When NOT to test / red flags:** consider not testing patients with **anemia, GI bleeding, or weight loss** — the allergy does not cause these. Skin symptoms (urticaria, angioedema) may or may not be present.
- **Diagnosis (clinical + supporting labs):** requires (1) consistent symptoms, (2) **increased alpha-gal IgE titer**, and (3) **symptom resolution/improvement after an alpha-gal–avoidance diet**. A positive alpha-gal IgE **alone is not sufficient**, especially with isolated GI distress, because sensitized individuals are often asymptomatic (seroprevalence 31% in a NC colonoscopy screening cohort; 35% in German hunters/forest workers). *(No specific numeric IgE cutoff is given in this CPU.)*
- **Diagnostic trial:** alpha-gal–avoidance diet eliminating mammalian meat (beef, pork, venison) and related products (lard, dairy, ice cream) for **at least a month**, avoiding restaurants (cross-contamination) and processed foods (additives); re-evaluate after the trial. Oral food challenge is the formal allergy diagnostic but is impractical here (hours-long delay, inconsistent reactions).
- **Management cornerstone = dietary elimination.** Avoid pork, beef, venison, and any mammal ("any animal with hair") and their products: lard, butter, milk. Dairy contains smaller amounts (higher with high-fat: **ice cream, cream, cream cheese**). **Gelatin** (pig/cow bone collagen) → marshmallows, gummy bears, gelatin candies, desserts may be problematic. Processed foods and restaurant cross-contamination are risks; some react to **aerosolized alpha-gal** (frying bacon/beef). **Acceptable:** fish, seafood, turkey, chicken, other fowl. Work with dieticians.
- **Accidental exposure:** inform a reliable person; take **1–2 diphenhydramine 25 mg tablets**; ensure access to **self-injectable epinephrine**, especially if respiratory compromise. No evidence supports acupuncture.
- **Tick-bite avoidance** (further bites raise IgE titers and worsen allergy): tick checks and showering after wooded areas, tight mesh socks pulled over pant cuffs, treat clothes/boots with **permethrin**.
- **Alpha-gal in medical products:** cetuximab (severe infusion anaphylaxis — original 2007 signal), porcine **pancreatic enzymes**, and bioprosthetic (bovine/porcine) **cardiac valves/patches/veins** (in-vitro IgE affinity; ≥2 anaphylaxis reports post-valve; no proven early device failure). Revivicor makes alpha-gal–free devices and pork (FDA-approved, not yet widely available); alpha-gal–knockout pig kidneys xenotransplanted without hyperacute rejection.
- **Follow-up (dynamic condition):** if tick bites avoided, **repeat alpha-gal IgE at 6–12 months**; as levels fall/negative, may resume first dairy then mammalian meat in small portions. Self-challenge only with modest doses (**quarter serving of meat**), antihistamines + autoinjectable epinephrine available, another person present; patients with systemic symptoms (itching, hives) should work with an allergist first.
- **Refer to allergist** when skin/systemic changes occur (facial/throat swelling, voice changes, difficulty breathing, hives, fainting) — anaphylaxis risk; need epinephrine-autoinjector counseling. No established desensitization protocol exists.
- **Cofactors** increasing risk/severity of reaction: fatty cuts of meat, NSAIDs, exercise, alcohol. A sensitized person who tolerates meat once can still be allergic (reactions don't occur after every exposure).
- **Asymptomatic sensitization:** mostly asymptomatic and not associated with decreased meat intake or higher rates of abdominal pain/diarrhea; but continued mammalian-meat intake in sensitized individuals may raise **coronary artery disease** risk (noncalcified plaque, obstructive CAD).

**Take-Home Messages (verbatim):** "Gastroenterology clinicians should be aware of the diagnosis and management of alpha-gal syndrome because a subset of alpha-gal allergic patients show GI symptoms such as abdominal pain, diarrhea, nausea, or vomiting without skin changes or anaphylaxis. The diagnosis of alpha-gal syndrome can be made among patients with GI distress and increased serum alpha-gal IgE antibodies whose symptoms are relieved adequately on an alpha-gal–avoidance diet that eliminates pork, beef, and mammalian-derived products. Patients with suspected alpha-gal allergy should be counseled on an alpha-gal–avoidance diet because that is the primary management. Counseling also should include measures to avoid tick bites because further bites can drive alpha-gal IgE titers higher and worsen the allergy. Patients who also have reactions such as facial swelling, urticaria, and respiratory difficulty should be referred to allergists. GI alpha-gal syndrome recently was described, and prospective studies are needed to better understand this condition."

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **No formal evidence grades** (commentary; no systematic review). Prospective studies on GI alpha-gal do not yet exist.
- **No numeric alpha-gal IgE cutoff** is specified for diagnosis — the CPU relies on an "increased" titer plus symptom response, explicitly noting a positive IgE alone is insufficient.
- Consequences of continued mammalian-meat intake in asymptomatic sensitized individuals are unclear; cardiovascular (CAD) association is reported but causation/device-failure not established.
