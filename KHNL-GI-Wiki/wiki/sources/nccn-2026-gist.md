## Summary

The NCCN guideline on gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GISTs) covers biopsy, pathologic diagnosis, risk stratification, mutation testing, and stage-directed treatment. Diagnosis is morphologic, supported by immunohistochemistry (IHC) for CD117, DOG1, and SDHB plus molecular testing for KIT and PDGFRA. Risk of metastasis is predicted from **tumor size, mitotic rate, and primary site**, using two separate tables — gastric and non-gastric — and tumor rupture is treated as conferring much higher recurrence risk.

Management is resectability- and genotype-based. Localized resectable disease is treated with segmental or wedge resection to negative margins, preserving the pseudocapsule; lymphadenectomy is usually unnecessary. Very small gastric GISTs may be surveilled when endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) and biopsy show no high-risk features. Preoperative therapy is reserved for tumors where downsizing would reduce surgical morbidity, chosen by genotype. Adjuvant imatinib is preferred for moderate- or high-risk completely resected tumors with an imatinib-sensitive mutation. For unresectable, recurrent, or metastatic disease, systemic therapy is lifelong in tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI)-sensitive GIST and follows a genotype-defined line sequence; PDGFRA exon 18 mutations insensitive to imatinib (including D842V) are on a separate pathway led by avapritinib.

## Key Findings / Claims

### How this document labels its recommendations

- NCCN does **not** use Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE), and does **not** number its recommendations. Any numbering used on this page is the wiki's, for reference only.
- Two separate vocabularies, both defined on the document's CAT-1 page:
  - **Categories of evidence and consensus** — the strength label:

| Category | Definition |
|---|---|
| 1 | High-level evidence (≥1 randomized phase 3 trial or high-quality, robust meta-analysis) + uniform NCCN consensus (≥85% panel support) that the intervention is appropriate |
| 2A | Lower-level evidence + uniform NCCN consensus (≥85% panel support) |
| 2B | Lower-level evidence + NCCN consensus (≥50% but <85% panel support) |
| 3 | Any level of evidence, but major NCCN disagreement that the intervention is appropriate |

  - **All recommendations are category 2A unless otherwise indicated** — so only the departures from 2A are flagged below.
  - **Categories of preference** — the ranking among appropriate options, not a strength label: **Preferred** (superior efficacy, safety, and evidence; affordability where relevant) · **Other recommended** (somewhat less efficacious, more toxic, less mature data, or significantly less affordable for similar outcomes) · **Useful in certain circumstances** (for selected populations, defined with the recommendation). **All recommendations are considered appropriate.**
- Non-2A items in this document: palliative radiation therapy for symptomatic lesions progressing on imatinib or avapritinib (2B); observation as a preoperative option for SDH-deficient GIST (2B); repotrectinib for NTRK fusion-positive GIST in the neoadjuvant setting (2B); binimetinib/imatinib for SDH-deficient GIST (2B); ipilimumab + nivolumab for tumor mutational burden-high (TMB-H) disease (2B). Category 1: imatinib first-line, sunitinib second-line, regorafenib third-line, ripretinib 150 mg daily fourth-line, and adjuvant imatinib following complete resection without preoperative imatinib.

### Biopsy and Diagnosis

- **Endoscopic transmural biopsy is favored over percutaneous transperitoneal biopsy** — lower peritoneal seeding risk. Percutaneous image-guided biopsy may be appropriate to confirm locally advanced or metastatic disease.
- Biopsy is **necessary to confirm primary GIST before starting preoperative therapy**.
- **Incisional biopsy before or during the operation should be strictly avoided.**
- Morphologic diagnosis on histologic sections is the standard. Supporting studies: IHC for **SDHB, CD117, and DOG1**, plus molecular testing for KIT and PDGFRA and other potential drivers (BRAF, NF1, NTRK and FGFR fusions).
- Refer complex or unusual histopathology to a center with GIST/sarcoma expertise. All patients should be managed by a multidisciplinary team with GIST expertise.

### Risk Stratification

- Inputs: **tumor size, mitotic rate, and primary site**. Both tables assume **no prior TKI exposure** and apply best to KIT- or PDGFRA-positive GIST; SDH-deficient GIST are more unpredictable.
- **Mitotic rate is counted in the most proliferative area and reported per 50 high-power fields (HPFs) — 50 HPFs = 5 mm². On most modern microscopes 20–25 HPFs at 40× encompass 5 mm².** The single cut-point throughout is **≤5 vs >5 mitoses/50 HPFs**.
- Mitotic rate may be inaccurate in small biopsies, or after neoadjuvant therapy when there is treatment effect (necrosis, reduced cellularity); in that case stratify on clinical parameters, size, and location instead.

**Table 1 — Gastric GIST, risk of metastasis (NCCN GIST-A, after Miettinen & Lasota 2006; risk-per-College of American Pathologists [CAP] band in the right column):**

| Tumor size | Mitotic rate | Metastasis rate | Risk per CAP |
|---|---|---|---|
| ≤2 cm | ≤5/50 HPFs | 0% | None |
| ≤2 cm | >5/50 HPFs | 0% (predicted, very small numbers) | None |
| >2 to ≤5 cm | ≤5/50 HPFs | 1.9% | Very low (1.9%) |
| >2 to ≤5 cm | >5/50 HPFs | 16% | Moderate (16%) |
| >5 to ≤10 cm | ≤5/50 HPFs | 3.6% | Low (3.6%) |
| >5 to ≤10 cm | >5/50 HPFs | 55% | High (55%) |
| >10 cm | ≤5/50 HPFs | 12% | Moderate (12%) |
| >10 cm | >5/50 HPFs | 86% | High (86%) |

**Table 2 — Non-gastric GIST (small bowel and colorectal):**

| Tumor size | Mitotic rate | Metastasis rate | Risk per CAP |
|---|---|---|---|
| ≤2 cm | ≤5/50 HPFs | 0% | None |
| ≤2 cm | >5/50 HPFs | 50%–54% | Insufficient data – High (54%) |
| >2 to ≤5 cm | ≤5/50 HPFs | 1.9%–8.5% | Low (4.3%–8.5%) |
| >2 to ≤5 cm | >5/50 HPFs | 50%–73% | High (50%–73%) |
| >5 to ≤10 cm | ≤5/50 HPFs | 24% | Insufficient data – Moderate (24%) |
| >5 to ≤10 cm | >5/50 HPFs | 85% | Insufficient data – High (85%) |
| >10 cm | ≤5/50 HPFs | 34%–57% | High (34%–57%) |
| >10 cm | >5/50 HPFs | 71%–90% | High (71%–90%) |

- **Sites not in Table 2 (esophagus, mesentery, peritoneum), and cells marked "insufficient data": use the jejunum/ileum criteria.**
- Site matters at equal size/mitotic rate: small-intestinal GIST is more aggressive than gastric; colorectal GIST (most often rectal) is aggressive and **can recur and metastasize even at <2 cm if mitotically active**.
- **Tumor rupture** — included in some stratification schemes and associated with much higher recurrence risk. Preoperative or intraoperative rupture, and gross residual disease (R2), are **managed as metastatic disease**.

### Mutation Testing

- **Frequencies:** ~80% of GIST carry a KIT mutation; another 5%–10% a PDGFRA mutation; 10%–15% lack both — the vast majority of those have functional inactivation of the succinate dehydrogenase (SDH) complex, detectable as loss of SDHB on IHC.
- **Mutation type predicts drug response, not prognosis** — KIT and PDGFRA mutation presence/type are not strongly correlated with biologic potential.
- **KIT exon 9** — lower response rate and progression-free survival (PFS) than exon 11 **at imatinib 400 mg; dosing at 400 mg twice daily (BID) has been associated with better PFS**. Dose escalation of imatinib at progression may be most effective in exon 9-mutant disease.
- **PDGFRA** — most mutations respond to imatinib. **D842V responds to avapritinib and is unlikely to respond to imatinib or most other approved TKIs for GIST.**
- Test KIT/PDGFRA whenever TKIs are part of the plan. **All GIST lacking a KIT or PDGFRA mutation should be tested for SDH deficiency and alternative drivers by next-generation sequencing (NGS)** (BRAF, NF1, NTRK, FGFR fusions). Tissue biopsy preferred; circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) may be appropriate in selected cases. A negative molecular profile warrants confirming with the testing laboratory that the assay covers the aberrations of interest — re-test if not.
- **SDH-deficient GIST** — typically gastric, in younger patients, frequently metastasize, may involve lymph nodes, usually grow slowly, and are usually imatinib-resistant; may benefit from sunitinib or regorafenib. **Refer all SDH-deficient/SDH-mutant GIST to a genetic counselor.** Germline SDH carriers are at risk of paraganglioma — **24-hour urine (catecholamine/metanephrine) testing before surgery**.
- **NF1-associated GIST** — typically small bowel, may be multifocal, often indolent; test for KIT/PDGFRA anyway; refer for genetic counseling; TKI data are limited absent a KIT/PDGFRA mutation.
- **Germline KIT/PDGFRA testing** — consider for family history of GIST and/or melanoma, multifocal GIST, and NF1 or SDH-deficient GIST.
- SDH-deficient or NF1-mutated tumors lacking KIT/PDGFRA mutations **may be considered for observation**, as most (not all) behave more indolently.

### Very Small Gastric GIST (<2 cm)

- Workup: consider EUS-guided fine-needle aspiration biopsy (EUS-FNAB) or EUS-guided core needle biopsy (EUS-CNB), plus imaging — computed tomography (CT) abdomen/pelvis with contrast and/or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) abdomen/pelvis with and without contrast.
- **No high-risk EUS or biopsy features → consider periodic endoscopic or radiographic surveillance.** EUS surveillance only after a thorough risk/benefit discussion with the patient.
- **High-risk EUS features: irregular border, cystic spaces, ulceration, echogenic foci, heterogeneity.**
- **High-risk pathologic biopsy features: presence of mitoses and/or tumor necrosis.**
- **Any high-risk EUS or biopsy feature → complete surgical resection.**

### Surgery

- Goal is resection with **histologically negative margins**. Intramural extension is limited, so extended anatomic resections (e.g., total gastrectomy) are rarely indicated — **segmental or wedge resection** is often appropriate.
- **Lymphadenectomy is usually not required** (low nodal metastasis rate); resect pathologically enlarged nodes in known SDH-deficient or translocation-associated GIST.
- GIST is fragile: **do not violate the pseudocapsule** — avoid spillage, fracture, capsule laceration, and piecemeal resection.
- **Re-resection is generally not indicated for microscopically positive (R1) margins.**
- Avoid complex multivisceral resection; if one seems necessary, get multidisciplinary input on preoperative imatinib or avapritinib instead. **Rectal GIST: sphincter-sparing approach; if abdominoperineal resection would be needed for a negative margin, consider preoperative imatinib.**
- Minimally invasive resection is an option for selected favorable locations, with the same oncologic principles; **remove the specimen in a plastic bag** to prevent spillage/port-site seeding.
- Extensive morbid surgery (e.g., total gastrectomy to reduce recurrence risk) is generally **not** recommended for multifocal SDH-deficient GIST.
- **Perioperative TKI handling:** imatinib can be stopped immediately before surgery and restarted once oral intake is tolerated; **sunitinib, regorafenib, ripretinib, and avapritinib should be stopped at least 1 week preoperatively**, restarted on clinical judgment.
- Unresectable GIST — systemic therapy is primary; surgery for previously unresectable tumors after favorable response, or for symptomatic bleeding, obstruction, or perforation.
- Metastatic GIST — systemic therapy is primary; surgery (peritoneal cytoreduction and/or liver metastasectomy) in this order of priority: stage IV after favorable response when complete cytoreduction is achievable by an experienced surgeon; unifocal TKI-refractory progression while other sites respond; low-volume multifocal progression that is safely resectable; symptomatic bleeding, obstruction, or perforation.

### Neoadjuvant Therapy

- **Only if surgical morbidity could be reduced by downsizing** — locally advanced GIST at difficult sites (rectum, esophagus and esophagogastric junction, duodenum), where multivisceral resection would otherwise be required, or in patients with significant comorbidity unfit for surgery.
- **Mutation testing (NGS) + SDHB IHC first**, to confirm a genotype likely to respond. It is acceptable to start neoadjuvant imatinib while awaiting the result.
- Regimen by genotype (Preferred unless noted): **imatinib** for KIT or PDGFRA mutations other than imatinib-insensitive PDGFRA exon 18 (incl. D842V) · **avapritinib** for imatinib-insensitive PDGFRA exon 18 incl. D842V · NTRK-directed therapy for NTRK fusions (larotrectinib, entrectinib; repotrectinib category 2B) · **SDH-deficient:** sunitinib, regorafenib, observation (category 2B), or clinical trial · BRAF-directed therapy for certain BRAF mutations (dabrafenib/trametinib for BRAF V600E) · **forgo neoadjuvant therapy for other mutations.**
- Caveat: neoadjuvant therapy may prevent accurate postoperative recurrence-risk assessment.
- **Maximal response may take ≥6 months.** Imaging to assess response and check adherence; operate after maximal response, if feasible. Proceed straight to surgery if there is bleeding, a symptomatic tumor, or poor treatment tolerance.

### Adjuvant Therapy

- **Completely resected (R0/R1), no neoadjuvant therapy:**
  - **Observe** if low-risk disease or a non-imatinib-sensitive tumor.
  - **Adjuvant imatinib (category 1), preferred, for significant recurrence risk — moderate or high risk — if the tumor has an imatinib-sensitive mutation.** Note this is moderate **or** high risk, not high risk alone.
- **Completely resected after neoadjuvant imatinib:** consider continuing adjuvant imatinib (preferred) for moderate- or high-risk disease.
- **Completely resected after neoadjuvant therapy with an agent other than imatinib:** observe.
- **Duration: optimal duration is unknown. Available data support at least 3 years for high-risk disease based on an overall survival benefit, or at least 6 years based on a disease-free survival benefit.** Feasibility of 5 years has also been shown in imatinib-sensitive GIST.
- Routine adjuvant use is not supported in GIST without a KIT mutation, or with an imatinib-resistant PDGFRA mutation.

### Unresectable, Recurrent, or Metastatic Disease

- Baseline imaging, mutation testing (NGS) + SDHB IHC, then TKI therapy. **Lifelong systemic therapy is recommended for TKI-sensitive GIST.**
- **Imatinib-sensitive KIT/PDGFRA-mutant GIST — line sequence (all Preferred):**

| Line | Agent |
|---|---|
| First | Imatinib (category 1) |
| Second | Sunitinib (category 1); if intolerant of sunitinib, consider ripretinib 150 mg daily |
| Third | Regorafenib (category 1) |
| Fourth | Ripretinib 150 mg daily, if not previously received (category 1) |

- **After progression on approved therapies (Useful in certain circumstances, listed alphabetically, not in order of preference):** avapritinib · cabozantinib · everolimus combined with a TKI (imatinib, sunitinib, or regorafenib) · nilotinib · pazopanib · ponatinib · **ripretinib dose escalation to 150 mg BID if previously on 150 mg daily** · sorafenib.
- **Imatinib-insensitive PDGFRA exon 18-mutant GIST (incl. D842V):** first-line **avapritinib** (Preferred); second-line **dasatinib** (Other recommended). Ripretinib is also an option for PDGFRA exon 18 mutations insensitive to imatinib after both avapritinib and dasatinib.
- **Other mutations (Useful in certain circumstances):** NTRK fusion-positive — larotrectinib, entrectinib, or repotrectinib first-line, repotrectinib second-line if not previously given · SDH-deficient — sunitinib, regorafenib, pazopanib, or binimetinib/imatinib (category 2B) · BRAF V600E — dabrafenib/trametinib.
- **TMB-H, irrespective of mutation status:** ipilimumab + nivolumab (category 2B).
- **Dose escalation:** additional clinical benefit may be obtained with **imatinib 400 mg BID** on progression with imatinib, and with **ripretinib 150 mg BID** on progression with ripretinib.
- Sunitinib is indicated for imatinib-resistant tumors or imatinib intolerance; regorafenib after progression on imatinib and sunitinib; ripretinib after ≥3 prior kinase inhibitors including imatinib.
- **Clinical trial referral is strongly recommended** for mutations resistant to imatinib, sunitinib, regorafenib, ripretinib, and avapritinib.
- Stable disease or response → continue TKI, obtain surgical consultation, consider resection; continue TKI if resection is not feasible.

### Progressive Disease

- **Do not stop TKI therapy even when disease is progressing** — clinical experience suggests discontinuation may accelerate progression and worsen symptoms.
- **Limited progression** on imatinib or avapritinib — continue the same TKI dose and consider: resection if feasible · ablation, embolization, radioembolization, or chemoembolization · **palliative radiation therapy for symptomatic lesions (category 2B)**. If previously on standard-dose imatinib: switch to an alternate TKI, or escalate the imatinib dose as tolerated (may be most effective in **KIT exon 9**-mutant disease). If previously on avapritinib, consider switching to an alternate TKI.
- **Generalized (widespread, systemic) progression** on imatinib or avapritinib — switch to an alternate TKI, or escalate imatinib as tolerated if previously on standard dose.
- **Progression despite prior therapies** — clinical trial; other options from the systemic-therapy list (limited data); **repeat tumor biopsy to look for uncommon mutations with a corresponding targeted therapy**; or best supportive care, within which imatinib may be reintroduced or continued for symptom palliation.
- Consider resection or ablation/liver-directed therapy for hepatic metastases. Metastasectomy, especially when complete resection is achievable, may benefit patients on imatinib or sunitinib with radiographic response or limited progression.

### Imaging and Follow-Up

- CT is performed with contrast; chest CT with or without contrast as clinically indicated; MRI with and without contrast unless contraindicated.
- Workup: CT abdomen/pelvis with contrast and/or MRI abdomen/pelvis with and without contrast; consider baseline chest imaging (x-ray or CT) for unresectable or metastatic disease. Obtain a baseline fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography (FDG-PET)/CT if FDG-PET/CT will be used in follow-up; **FDG-PET/CT with a non-diagnostic CT is not a substitute for a diagnostic CT**.
- **Response assessment: CT and/or MRI every 8–12 weeks after starting therapy**; in some patients it may be appropriate to image before 2 months. FDG-PET/CT may indicate TKI efficacy after **2–4 weeks** when a rapid readout is needed. Imaging frequency may be reduced in responding disease.
- **Interpreting response: an increase in tumor size together with a decrease in tumor density is consistent with drug efficacy or benefit** — size alone is misleading. FDG-PET/CT may clarify ambiguous CT or MRI.
- **Post-resection surveillance: history, physical, and imaging every 3–6 months (every 3 months if high risk) for 5 years, then annually; after 10 years individualize.** Less frequent surveillance may be acceptable for very small tumors (<2 cm) unless associated with a high mitotic rate.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Algorithm-based guideline using NCCN categories of evidence and consensus, not a GRADE systematic review; strength labels are not comparable to those in ACG/American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) society guidelines.
- **Optimal adjuvant imatinib duration is unresolved within the document itself** — 3 years is supported by overall survival, 6 years by disease-free survival, and no single duration is recommended.
- SDH-deficient/wild-type GIST is largely imatinib-resistant and its management rests on category 2A/2B options; observation is an accepted preoperative choice for some.
- The document gives the **metastasis rates** behind each risk band but does not restate the four-tier "none/low/moderate/high" labels as independent criteria — the band follows directly from the size × mitotic-rate × site cell.
