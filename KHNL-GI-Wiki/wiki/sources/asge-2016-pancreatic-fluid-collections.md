## Summary

American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) Standards of Practice statement on endoscopic diagnosis and treatment of inflammatory pancreatic fluid collections (PFCs) — collections arising after acute and chronic pancreatitis, pancreatic trauma, and pancreatic surgery. PubMed search January 1990 – February 2015; supplements and replaces the 2005 ASGE document on cystic lesions and inflammatory fluid collections of the pancreas.

Collections are categorized by the 2012 revised Atlanta classification: acute peripancreatic fluid collection, pancreatic pseudocyst, acute necrotic collection, and walled-off necrosis (WON). *Full definitions and contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) criteria for all four: [[pancreatic-fluid-collection-drainage]].* Most acute PFCs resolve spontaneously and need no intervention; acute peripancreatic fluid collections and acute necrotic collections generally are not drained because of their recent onset (<4 weeks) and absent mature wall.

Twelve numbered recommendations govern three decisions: **whether to drain** (symptomatic or rapidly enlarging pseudocyst; any infected PFC failing conservative management; symptomatic sterile necrosis >8 weeks after onset of [[acute-pancreatitis|acute pancreatitis]]), **when** (after the wall matures), and **how** ([[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]]-guided transmural drainage considered before surgery for pseudocysts; transmural and/or percutaneous drainage before necrosectomy or surgery for WON). Before drainage, cystic neoplasm and pseudoaneurysm must be excluded. Procedures are performed only where surgical and interventional radiology support is available.

Techniques and outcomes of endoscopic necrosectomy are explicitly **outside the document's scope**.

### Operative detail behind each recommendation

- **Rec 1 — what to exclude and with what.** Contrast-enhanced CT, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)/magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP), or EUS before drainage, to confirm the collection is not a cystic neoplasm, pseudoaneurysm, duplication cyst, or other noninflammatory fluid collection (see [[pancreatic-cysts]]).
- **Rec 2 — "maturation" = after 4 weeks.** Drainage, if required, should be undertaken **after 4 weeks** to allow encapsulation, better definition of the margins, and potentially fewer adverse events. Pseudocysts and WON usually require **>4 weeks** from onset to mature.
- **Rec 3/4 — size is not the trigger.** Large pseudocyst size alone is **not** an indication for drainage, although pseudocysts **>6 cm** tend to be symptomatic. ASGE 2016 gives no rate or interval defining "rapidly enlarging."
- **Rec 5 — infected PFC.** Infected PFCs are often drained but may instead be followed clinically while the patient remains stable on antibiotics.
- **Rec 6 — sterile necrosis, two separate triggers.** Drainage of sterile WON is indicated for **gastric outlet or biliary obstruction** caused by the collection, which may occur **within 4 to 8 weeks** of onset; and for **refractory abdominal pain, ongoing systemic illness, anorexia, or weight loss lasting >8 weeks** after onset. Choice of management option should reflect local expertise and the severity of comorbidities.
- **Rec 7 — diagnosing infected necrosis without a needle.** Infected necrosis is not initially distinguishable from sterile necrosis clinically, but usually becomes apparent **2 to 4 weeks** after onset, when its incidence peaks. Signs: new-onset or persistent sepsis · clinical deterioration despite adequate support with no alternative source of infection · **gas bubbles within the PFC** on radiologic imaging. EUS-FNA carries a high false-negative rate and may contaminate a previously sterile collection; intervening on clinical suspicion alone was accurate in **>90%** of cases in one study.
- **Rec 9 — when EUS is required rather than optional.** No luminal bulge, or suspected [[portal-hypertension|portal hypertension]]. EUS is also preferred for an unusual collection location, documented intervening varices, or a prior failed direct endoscopic approach; where an endoscopically visible indentation exists and portal hypertension is absent, direct endoscopic and EUS-assisted drainage were equivalent.
- **Rec 10 — the escalation trigger.** Step up to transmural necrosectomy when there is **no clinical improvement within 48 to 72 hours** of nasocystic lavage plus transmural drainage.
- **Rec 12 and peri-procedural care.** Anticoagulants and antiplatelets other than aspirin ideally discontinued (acute and delayed bleeding risk); deep sedation or anesthesia typical; carbon dioxide insufflation to minimize gas embolism; antibiotics typically given, especially for suspected WON.

### Other points

- **Choosing transmural vs transpapillary** for a pseudocyst turns on four factors: anatomic relationship of the collection to the stomach or duodenum · ductal communication with the pseudocyst · cyst contents · size of the collection. *Route, stent, dwell-time and after-care detail: [[pancreatic-fluid-collection-drainage]].*
- [[ercp|Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]] may be considered before percutaneous, transmural, or surgical drainage of a pseudocyst to define anatomy, but is **not necessary in most patients** when high-quality cross-sectional imaging is available; when done preoperatively it should be performed shortly before surgery to limit the risk of infecting the PFC. **ERCP is not typically used for WON** — transmural drainage is the standard initial endoscopic approach.
- **Epidemiology:** ~20% of patients with acute pancreatitis develop necrosis, with secondary infection in 30% of those.
- **Disconnected duct syndrome** arises when necrosis of the pancreatic neck and body isolates a still-viable segment of parenchyma in the tail. Long-term indwelling transmural stents may be indicated in disconnected duct syndrome or duct disruption, where recurrence risk is high.
- **WON drainage underperforms pseudocyst drainage** — lower success, higher adverse-event rates. *Outcome and adverse-event figures from this document: [[pancreatic-fluid-collection-drainage]].*

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Older than [[acg-2024-acute-pancreatitis|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2024 acute pancreatitis]] and [[aga-2020-cpu-pancreatic-necrosis|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2020 pancreatic necrosis]], which govern the step-up approach and lumen-apposing metal stent (LAMS)-based necrosectomy on the entity pages; concordant overall. This source adds the explicit drainage indications, the 4-week and 8-week thresholds, and the pre-drainage diagnostic checklist.
- **Stent material unresolved:** no clear data support self-expandable metal stents over plastic stents for PFC resolution, and metal adds procedure cost. [[aga-2020-cpu-pancreatic-necrosis]] later favors LAMS for necrosis on expert opinion.
- **Adding a transpapillary stent to transmural drainage:** this document reports a single-center study showing higher clinical success, a second limiting the benefit to partial duct disruptions, and a large multicenter study showing comparable resolution rates. It does not resolve the question.
- Does not address the techniques or outcomes of endoscopic necrosectomy, which it places outside its scope.
