## Summary

The FDA prescribing information for **tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF)**, the second of the three preferred nucleos(t)ide analogues for chronic hepatitis B (CHB). It was fetched for the same narrow reason as the entecavir label before it: [[aasld-idsa-2025-chb-treatment|AASLD/IDSA 2025]] states that TDF **requires dose adjustment for renal dysfunction or dialysis** and that renal and bone disease are reasons to avoid it, but **prints no adult milligram dose, no creatinine clearance (CrCl) cut-points and no monitoring intervals** — so [[hbv-nucleoside-analogs]] could state the avoid rule without letting anyone follow it.

The adult dose is simple — **one 300 mg tablet once daily, with or without food** — and the contrast with entecavir's empty-stomach rule is itself clinically useful. Renal adjustment, by contrast, works differently from entecavir: TDF is adjusted by **lengthening the dosing interval**, not by reducing the milligram amount, and the threshold to start adjusting is **CrCl <50 mL/min**. The label is explicit that these intervals are **modelled from single-dose pharmacokinetics in non-HBV, non-HIV subjects and have never been clinically evaluated** in moderate-to-severe renal impairment — which is the strongest argument in the document for the guideline's preference to **change the drug rather than dose-adjust it** when renal function declines on TDF.

The renal and bone toxicity concerns that drive the avoid rule are specified here in actionable form. **Renal:** acute renal failure and Fanconi syndrome (proximal renal tubulopathy with severe hypophosphatemia); assess estimated CrCl in everyone before starting and as clinically appropriate on therapy, and in those at risk add **serum phosphorus, urine glucose and urine protein**; **avoid concurrent or recent nephrotoxic agents**, with high-dose or multiple non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) named specifically. **Bone:** small decreases in bone mineral density (BMD) with increased bone-turnover markers, osteomalacia from proximal renal tubulopathy, and a named trigger for imaging — **consider BMD assessment in anyone with a prior pathologic fracture or other osteoporosis risk factors**.

One symptom rule ties the two together and is easy to miss: **persistent or worsening bone pain, limb pain, fractures, arthralgia or muscle pain/weakness on TDF should prompt an evaluation of renal function**, because they may be the presenting manifestation of proximal renal tubulopathy rather than a primary bone problem.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Indications.** HIV-1 infection (in combination) and **chronic hepatitis B** in adults and pediatric patients **≥12 years of age weighing ≥35 kg**. Safety and efficacy below 12 years or 35 kg are not established for this tablet presentation. In CHB, **the optimal duration of treatment is unknown.**

**Contraindications: none listed.**

**Adult / adolescent (≥12 y, ≥35 kg) dose:**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Dose | **300 mg once daily**, one tablet |
| Food | **With or without food** — no restriction |
| Tablet strength | 300 mg tenofovir DF (= 245 mg tenofovir disoproxil) |

**Renal impairment — adjust the *interval*, starting at CrCl <50 mL/min.** No adjustment for mild impairment (CrCl 50–80 mL/min), but routine monitoring of estimated CrCl, serum phosphorus, urine glucose and urine protein is advised even there.

| CrCl (mL/min) | 300 mg dosing interval |
|---|---|
| **≥50** | every **24 h** |
| **30–49** | every **48 h** |
| **10–29** | every **72 to 96 h** |
| **Hemodialysis** | every **7 days**, or after a total of ~12 h of dialysis — generally once weekly assuming three ~4-h sessions a week. **Give after completing dialysis** |
| **<10 and not on hemodialysis** | **No dosing recommendation available** — pharmacokinetics not evaluated |

- CrCl is **calculated using ideal (lean) body weight**.
- The interval recommendations are **modelled, not clinically validated**: derived from single-dose pharmacokinetic data in subjects without HBV or HIV. Clinical response **and** renal function must be closely monitored, and the potential benefit weighed against the risk of renal toxicity.
- **No data** exist for dose recommendations in pediatric patients ≥12 y with renal impairment.

**Boxed warning: posttreatment exacerbation of hepatitis.** Stopping anti-HBV therapy, TDF included, may cause **severe acute exacerbations of hepatitis**. Monitor closely with **clinical and laboratory follow-up for at least several months** after stopping; restarting anti-hepatitis B therapy may be warranted.

**New onset or worsening renal impairment** (warning 5.2):

- Tenofovir is **principally eliminated by the kidney**. Reported: acute renal failure and **Fanconi syndrome** (renal tubular injury with severe hypophosphatemia).
- **Assess estimated CrCl in all patients before starting** and as clinically appropriate during therapy.
- **In patients at risk of renal dysfunction** — including anyone who had renal events on adefovir dipivoxil (HEPSERA) — also assess **serum phosphorus, urine glucose and urine protein** before starting and periodically on therapy.
- **Avoid TDF with concurrent or recent nephrotoxic agents**, e.g. high-dose or multiple NSAIDs. Acute renal failure after starting high-dose/multiple NSAIDs has been reported in patients who appeared stable on TDF; some required hospitalization and renal replacement therapy. **Consider alternatives to NSAIDs** in patients at risk.
- **Persistent or worsening bone pain, pain in extremities, fractures, arthralgia or muscle pain/weakness may be manifestations of proximal renal tubulopathy → evaluate renal function.**

**Bone effects** (warning 5.6):

- Slightly greater decreases in **BMD** and increases in bone-metabolism markers than comparators, suggesting increased bone turnover; higher serum parathyroid hormone and 1,25-vitamin D levels.
- **Consider BMD assessment** in adults and patients ≥12 y with a **history of pathologic fracture or other risk factors for osteoporosis or bone loss**. The long-term fracture consequences of the BMD changes are **unknown**.
- **Calcium and vitamin D supplementation** was not studied but **may be beneficial for all patients**.
- **Osteomalacia secondary to proximal renal tubulopathy** has been reported; consider it, with hypophosphatemia, in at-risk patients with persistent or worsening bone or muscle symptoms.
- In adolescents (12 to <18 y) with CHB, bone effects resembled those in adults; **skeletal growth (height) appeared unaffected** in all pediatric trials.

**Lactic acidosis / severe hepatomegaly with steatosis** (warning 5.3): reported with nucleoside analogues including TDF, **including fatal cases**. **Suspend TDF** in any patient developing clinical or laboratory findings suggestive of lactic acidosis or pronounced hepatotoxicity — which may include hepatomegaly and steatosis **even without marked transaminase elevation**.

**HIV-1 / HBV co-infection** (warning 5.5): because of the risk of selecting HIV-1 resistance, TDF should be used in co-infection **only as part of an appropriate antiretroviral combination regimen**. **Offer HIV-1 antibody testing to every HBV-infected patient before starting**, and test every patient with HIV-1 for chronic hepatitis B before starting.

**Do not combine** (warning 5.4): TDF must not be used with other tenofovir-containing products (including tenofovir alafenamide products such as VEMLIDY, DESCOVY, BIKTARVY, GENVOYA, ODEFSEY, TRUVADA, ATRIPLA, COMPLERA, STRIBILD), nor with **adefovir dipivoxil (HEPSERA)**.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **No contradiction with [[aasld-idsa-2025-chb-treatment|AASLD/IDSA 2025]]** — the label fills a gap the guideline leaves open. Source priority is unaffected: the guideline governs whom to treat and which agent to choose; the label supplies the milligram amount, the renal arithmetic and the labelled safety monitoring.
- **A label is not a guideline** and sits outside the wiki's three-tier scheme. Authoritative for dose, administration and labelled safety; not for treatment indication, agent selection or surveillance strategy where a society guideline speaks.
- **The label predates the 2025 guideline** (revised 7/2018) and does not reflect the current preference for TAF over TDF in renal or bone disease — that comparison is a guideline judgement, not a labelled one.
- **Pregnancy is not captured here.** The wiki's pregnancy stance for TDF comes from guidelines ([[aasld-idsa-2025-chb-treatment]], [[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]]), which is also the more recent source.
