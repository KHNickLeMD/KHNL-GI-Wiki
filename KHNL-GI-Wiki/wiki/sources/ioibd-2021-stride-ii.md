## Summary

STRIDE-II updates the 2015 STRIDE-I position statement of the International Organization for the Study of IBD (IOIBD) on **treat-to-target** goals in [[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease]] and [[ulcerative-colitis]], and extends it for the first time to **pediatric** IBD. Methodology: a systematic literature review picking up where STRIDE-I's search ended (end of 2013) and running to **July 2019** — 11,278 abstracts screened (6,223 CD, 5,055 UC), 1,005 full texts reviewed (413 CD, 592 UC), **435 manuscripts included** (297 CD, 138 UC) — run in parallel with a **Delphi-like process** among the 89 IOIBD members. The first IOIBD survey (concept elicitation, 39/89 = 44% participated) ranked the most important targets; statements were then drafted by a steering committee and voted on by **70 of 89 invited IOIBD members (79%)**.

**Agreement threshold:** a statement was endorsed if **≥75% of participants scored it 7 to 10 on a 10-point rating scale** (1 = do not agree at all; 10 = agree completely). Statements failing after 2 rounds were excluded. **Fifteen recommendations were identified, of which 13 were endorsed** — the paper numbers these 1 through 13 in Table 2 and reports, for each, a **"strength of recommendation"** (the *mean* score of all responders on the 1–10 agreement scale — **not** a GRADE strength) and the **% of votes scoring 7–10**. There is **no GRADE certainty-of-evidence rating anywhere in this document.**

Substantively, STRIDE-II confirms STRIDE-I's long-term targets of **clinical remission and endoscopic healing** and **adds absence of disability, restoration of quality of life, and (in children) normal growth** as long-term targets. It formalizes **symptomatic relief as an immediate target** and **normalization of serum and fecal markers (CRP, fecal calprotectin) as intermediate targets**. **Transmural healing in CD and histological healing in UC are explicitly NOT formal targets**, but should be assessed as measures of the depth of remission. Table 4 supplies drug-by-drug **time-to-target estimates in weeks** — the practical scaffold for deciding *when* to declare a therapy a failure.

The framework is explicitly for **clinical practice, not the trial setting**, and the authors stress it is a general scheme with major evidentiary gaps (Table 5) that must be adapted to the individual patient and local resources.

## Key Findings / Claims

### What is new in STRIDE-II (Table 3, verbatim bullets)

- The STRIDE group, under the auspices of the IOIBD, has updated the 2015 first reported STRIDE recommendations and has developed 13 updated and new recommendations for treating to target in CD and UC (STRIDE-II recommendations).
- Time to expected response, remission, and endoscopic healing with the different treatments have been introduced for incorporating the treatment targets.
- STRIDE-II added clinical response and remission as well as normalization of CRP as immediate and short-term targets.
- Reduction of FC to an acceptable range has been added as a formal intermediate treatment target.
- Pediatric targets, reflected by different measuring scales and the addition of restoration of normal growth as a formal treatment target have been added.
- Restoration of QoL and absence of disability have been added to EH as long-term targets.
- Transmural healing in CD and histological healing in UC have been newly recognized as important adjunctive measures but were not endorsed as formal new treatment targets.

### Definitions supplied in the supportive text (not voted statements)

- **Endoscopic response** (from the systematic review + Delphi, "the following definitions prevailed"): **>50% decrease in the SES-CD** (simple endoscopic score in CD) or **CDEIS** (endoscopic index of severity).
- **Endoscopic remission** (same paragraph): **SES-CD ≤2 points** or **CDEIS <3 and lack of ulcerations** (ie, any ulcerations, including aphthous ulcers). ⚠ Note this differs from voted recommendation 9a (SES-CD **<3** points) — see Contradictions.
- The systematic review and Delphi process **supported an FC cutoff value of 150 μg/g to identify EH** (Supplementary Table 4), while the endorsed statement 12 gives the wider **100–250 μg/g** range. FC of **100 to 250 μg/g is considered a gray zone**; even values **<600 μg/g** can still be associated with minimal inflammation.
- **UC:** "EH is commonly defined as MES ≤1, but complete endoscopic healing (ie, MES 0) is associated with superior disease outcomes."

### Supporting evidence cited for the biomarker targets

- **Fecal calprotectin** generally outperforms CRP. Meta-analysis pooled **sensitivity 82%, specificity 72%, AUC 0.84** for FC reflecting endoscopic disease activity in CD. FC predicted long-term clinical outcomes when measured **12 weeks** after initiating medical treatment. Elevated FC carried a **53%–83% probability of relapse during the subsequent 2 to 3 months**.
- At time of anti-TNF discontinuation, FC cutoffs of **50 to 150 μg/g** predicted subsequent relapse; in one small anti-TNF study, **FC <300 μg/g or a 50% decrease at week 12** was associated with corticosteroid-free remission at 1 year. A pediatric study found **FC <500 or a decrease >50%** predictive of inactive endoscopic disease. Other studies support FC at **weeks 12 to 14** predicting clinical remission and EH, with cutoff values of **82 to 168 μg/g**.
- **CRP** has higher specificity but low sensitivity. **Low CRP** is associated with reduced risk of clinical relapse (AUC **0.70 to 0.72**). CRP normalization at **8 to 14 weeks** after treatment predicts remission at 1 year and anti-TNF success at 2 years. ACCENT post hoc: **week-14 CRP decrease by ≥60%** predicted durable sustained response to infliximab (AUC **0.75**). **CRP >5 mg/dL at week 22** predicts subsequent loss of response to anti-TNF.
- **Combining targets ("the more the merrier"):** CALM post hoc — CRP + FC combined was superior to FC alone in predicting EH after **48 weeks** of adalimumab. Pediatric ImageKids MINI index performed better including CRP in addition to FC, especially in the **gray FC zone of 100 to 600 μg/g**. A small pediatric study: **FC <500 μg/g, CRP <5 mg/dL, and PCDAI <10** had an excellent negative likelihood ratio (**0.2**) for endoscopically inactive disease. Adding CDAI to FC improved AUC for detecting mucosal inflammation from **0.88 to 0.96**; adding CRP to FC improved specificity from **87% to 100%**. Combination of **FC >30 μg/g and low QoL (SIBDQ >6)** had greater specificity for active endoscopic disease than FC alone in patients in clinical remission. EXTEND post hoc: combined lack of ulcerations + clinical remission (deep remission) was associated with fewer treatment adjustments, hospitalizations, surgeries, and better QoL than achieving EH alone.

### Why histology and transmural healing were rejected as formal targets

- **Histology (CD):** the Delphi survey rated histology low. Reasons given: no well-validated, reliable, accepted measuring tool; insufficient data to justify intensified immunosuppressant medications; currently available treatments have limited effectiveness in inducing histologic remission in CD — in one study **only 13% of patients with CD treated with long-term anti-TNF regimens had achieved histologic remission**.
- **Histology (UC):** added benefit over macroscopic EH in predicting long-term remission and cancer prevention has been presented, but histologic remission is a high hurdle; **only one-third of patients with UC with EH in the ACT trials had histologic remission**; concordance between macroscopic and microscopic degree of inflammation held only for the extreme groups (remission and severe disease), alluding to poor interobserver reliability.
- **Transmural healing:** ileocolonoscopy cannot be repeated frequently and may not be feasible in proximal small-bowel disease; in the prospective **ImageKids study of 240 children with CD**, mismatch between endoscopic and transmural healing was not uncommon. Cross-sectional imaging (ultrasound, CTE, MRE) should be an **adjuvant** assessment rather than a formal target, given the limited ability of currently available treatments to achieve transmural healing.

### Gaps in knowledge / future research (Table 5, verbatim)

| Domain | Gap | Future research |
|---|---|---|
| **PROs** | PRO2 for both CD and UC have been formulated from existing measures as a temporary measure to meet the regulatory agencies' requirement in clinical trials. They were not a product of stringent psychometric development and evaluation. | PROs should be developed aiming at use in clinical practice, with high reliability, face validity, construct validity, responsiveness, and feasibility. |
| **HRQOL** | Current measuring tools were developed as research tools and are too cumbersome for using in routine clinical practice. | Development and validation of a shorter measuring tool for everyday use. |
| **Histology and transmural healing** | It is still unclear whether these are significant enough to justify the increased utilization of medical treatment to achieve these extended endpoints. This is the reason why they were not selected as formal treatment target for STRIDE-II. | More prospective studies, preferably randomized-controlled, are needed to explore the number-needed-to treat to these targets for achieving superior clinical outcomes. |
| **Endoscopic healing** | The thresholds to define remission or response remain un-validated. | More studies are needed to link the optimal thresholds with specific outcomes. |

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Internal inconsistency — where clinical remission and CRP normalization sit on the timeline.** Voted statement **3** calls clinical remission an **intermediate (medium-term)** target and statement **12** calls CRP + FC normalization an **intermediate** target. But **Table 3** ("What is new") says STRIDE-II added *"clinical response and remission as well as normalization of CRP as **immediate and short-term** targets"*, and **Figure 2** places "Symptomatic remission and normalization of CRP" under the **short-term** bracket, with only the calprotectin decrease (and normal growth in children) under **intermediate**. The wiki asserts the **voted Table 2 wording** as primary (it is the balloted text) and records the Figure 2 placement alongside it.
- **Internal inconsistency — normal growth in children.** Voted statement **6** calls restoration of normal growth a **long-term** treatment target; **Figure 2** places "normal growth in children" in the **intermediate** box. Table 2 wording is asserted.
- **Internal inconsistency — the SES-CD endoscopic remission cutoff.** Voted recommendation **9a** gives **SES-CD <3 points** or absence of ulcerations. The supportive text on the same page states that in the systematic review and Delphi group "the following definitions prevailed… for endoscopic remission **SES-CD ≤2 points**" — numerically identical for an integer score but stated as a different comparator. [[crohns-disease]] previously carried "SES-CD ≤2" without attribution; both are now recorded with the voted "<3 points" as the asserted cutoff.
- **Fecal calprotectin cutoff is a range, not a number.** Statement 12 endorses **100–250 μg/g**, footnote *b* makes the choice outcome-dependent (**<100 μg/g** for deep/histological healing, **<250 μg/g** for less stringent outcomes such as MES 0–1), while the systematic review and Delphi separately **supported 150 μg/g to identify EH**. Existing [[ulcerative-colitis]] FC thresholds (<60 deep remission, <187 mucosal healing, >321 relapse risk) come from a different ingested source and are **not** contradicted — they answer a different question (predicting a state) than STRIDE-II's treatment target.
- **UC endoscopic target: MES 0 vs MES 0–1.** STRIDE-II statement 9b sets the target at **MES = 0** or UCEIS ≤1, and the text notes EH "is commonly defined as MES ≤1, but complete endoscopic healing (ie, MES 0) is associated with superior disease outcomes." [[ulcerative-colitis]] currently states an endoscopic target of **MES 0–1** from [[acg-2025-uc]]/[[aga-2024-uc-pharm]]. Both are tier-1; **ACG 2025 / AGA 2024 are newer than STRIDE-II (2021), so the newer guideline's MES 0–1 remains the page's asserted target**, with STRIDE-II's stricter MES 0 recorded as the deeper goal.
- **No GRADE anywhere.** This document assigns **no** certainty-of-evidence grades and **no** GRADE strength-of-recommendation labels. The column headed "Strength of recommendation" is a mean 1–10 agreement score. Any downstream page quoting a GRADE rating for STRIDE-II would be fabricating it.
- **Table 4 (time to target) was never voted on** and is explicitly "a rough estimate of experts' opinion" — it is the weakest content in the paper and should be labelled as such wherever reproduced.
