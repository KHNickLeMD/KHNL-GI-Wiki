# AGA 2021 — Medical Management of Moderate to Severe Luminal and Perianal Fistulizing Crohn's Disease

## Summary

This is the foundational American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) pharmacologic guideline for **moderate to severe luminal and perianal fistulizing Crohn's disease (CD)** in adults, developed with GRADE methodology. Moderate-to-severe disease was defined as a Crohn's Disease Activity Index (CDAI) score ≥220. The predetermined critical outcomes were **induction and maintenance of remission**; in the recommendations, drug effect is expressed as risk of *failure* to induce/maintain remission (relative risk [RR] or odds ratio [OR] <1 favors the drug). The minimal clinically important difference (MCID) over placebo was set at 10%. The guideline does **not** address surgical management, and therapeutic drug monitoring is covered in a separate 2017 AGA guideline.

Across induction and maintenance, the Panel **recommends** (strong) anti–tumor necrosis factor α (anti-TNFα) agents (infliximab, adalimumab, certolizumab pegol) and ustekinumab over no treatment and **suggests** (conditional) vedolizumab, while **suggesting against** natalizumab (conditional — progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy [PML] risk with available alternatives) and **suggesting against** thiopurine monotherapy for achieving remission (conditional). 5-aminosalicylates (5-ASA) and sulfasalazine are the one drug class **recommended against** at strong force, for induction *and* maintenance. Corticosteroids are *suggested* for induction (conditional) and *recommended against* for maintenance (strong). Biologic monotherapy is recommended over thiopurine monotherapy for induction, and combination of infliximab/adalimumab with thiopurines is suggested over anti-TNF monotherapy in biologic- and immunomodulator-naïve patients. The Panel suggests **early introduction of a biologic** rather than delaying until after failure of 5-ASA/corticosteroids (an anti-"step-up" stance). Positioning recommendations are given for biologic-naïve, anti-TNF primary nonresponders, and anti-TNF secondary nonresponders. For perianal fistula, infliximab has the most robust evidence; biologic + antibiotic combination is recommended over biologic alone.

**Reading the recommendations.** The document numbers its own recommendations 1A–11 (Table 3) and gives each a strength (Strong / Conditional / No recommendation) **and** a certainty of evidence (High / Moderate / Low / Very low / Evidence gap) — numbering and grades below are the document's, not the wiki's. *Unless otherwise specified, the guideline does not give separate recommendations for induction and maintenance of remission*; drugs started for induction are generally continued for maintenance. Induction was assessed at 4–12 weeks, maintenance at 22–54 weeks. Next update was anticipated 3 years from publication (2024).

Because there were no head-to-head trials, positioning relied on **network meta-analysis (NMA)** of indirect evidence (rated down for imprecision), so most positioning statements are conditional/low certainty.

## Scope and Definitions

**Population:** adult **outpatients** with moderate to severe luminal or perianal fistulizing CD (the Panel anticipated most recommendations would also apply to inpatients). Not covered: surgical management; therapeutic drug monitoring (separate 2017 AGA guideline).

**Severity definitions used:**
- **CDAI bands:** remission <150 · mild to moderate 150–220 · moderate to severe 220–450 · severe >450. **For this guideline, moderate to severe = CDAI ≥220.**
- **International Organization for the Study of Inflammatory Bowel Diseases (IOIBD) severe disease** = high risk of adverse disease-related complications (surgery, hospitalization, disability), based on structural damage + inflammatory burden + quality-of-life impact.
- Contributors to severe disease: large or deep mucosal lesions on endoscopy/imaging · fistula and/or perianal abscess · strictures · prior intestinal resections, particularly of segments >40 cm · stoma · extensive disease (ileal involvement >40 cm or pancolitis) · anemia · elevated C-reactive protein · low albumin.
- Severe symptoms: ≥10 loose stools/day · daily abdominal pain · anorectal symptoms (pain, urgency, incontinence, discharge, tenesmus) · systemic corticosteroid use within the prior year · lack of symptomatic improvement despite prior biologic and/or immunosuppressive exposure · significant impact on activities of daily living.

**Grading vocabulary (the document's own, Tables 1–2):**

| Term | Meaning in this document |
|---|---|
| **Strong** | Wording "The AGA recommends…" — most individuals should receive it; formal decision aids not likely needed |
| **Conditional** | Wording "The AGA suggests…" — different choices appropriate for different patients; decision aids may help |
| **No recommendation** | Wording "The AGA makes no recommendation…" — confidence in the effect estimate so low any estimate is speculative |
| **High / Moderate / Low / Very low** | Certainty of evidence (confidence that the true effect lies close to the estimate) |
| **Evidence gap** | Available evidence insufficient to determine true effect (used as the certainty label alongside "No recommendation") |

**Direction of effect estimates:** effects are reported as the risk of **failure** to induce or maintain remission — a relative risk (RR) or odds ratio (OR) **<1 favors the drug** over comparator/placebo; estimates below are given with their 95% confidence interval (CI). **Minimal clinically important difference (MCID) was set at 10%**; failure to meet the MCID was treated as no clinically meaningful impact over placebo and rated certainty down for imprecision.

## Key Findings / Claims (recommendations with strength and certainty)

### Pharmacologic management of moderate to severe LUMINAL CD

**Recommendation 1A.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA recommends the use of anti-TNFα over no treatment for induction and maintenance of remission. *(Strong recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.)* Comment: Although the evidence supporting infliximab and adalimumab was moderate certainty, the evidence for certolizumab pegol was low certainty.

**Recommendation 1B.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA suggests the use of vedolizumab over no treatment for the induction and maintenance of remission. *(Conditional recommendation; low certainty for induction, moderate certainty for maintenance.)*

**Recommendation 1C.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA recommends the use of ustekinumab over no treatment for the induction and maintenance of remission. *(Strong recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 1D.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA suggests against the use of natalizumab over no treatment for the induction and maintenance of remission. *(Conditional recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.)* Comment: Given evidence of harm from post-marketing data from progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML) and the availability of other drugs, the AGA suggests against the use of natalizumab. Patients who are John Cunningham virus antibody–negative who put a high value on the potential benefits and lower value on PML risk and who will adhere to ongoing monitoring for John Cunningham virus positivity, may consider using natalizumab.

**Recommendation 2A.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD who are naïve to biologic drugs, the AGA recommends the use of infliximab, adalimumab, or ustekinumab over certolizumab pegol for the induction of remission *(Strong recommendation, moderate certainty evidence)* and suggests the use of vedolizumab over certolizumab pegol for the induction of remission. *(Conditional recommendation, low certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 2B.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD who never responded to anti-TNFα (primary nonresponse), the AGA recommends the use of ustekinumab *(Strong recommendation, moderate certainty evidence)* and suggests the use of vedolizumab over no treatment for the induction of remission. *(Conditional recommendation, low certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 2C.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD who previously responded to infliximab (secondary nonresponse), the AGA recommends the use of adalimumab or ustekinumab *(Strong recommendation, moderate certainty evidence)* and suggests the use of vedolizumab over no treatment for the induction of remission. *(Conditional recommendation, low certainty evidence.)* Comment: If adalimumab was the first-line drug used there is indirect evidence to suggest the option of using infliximab as a second-line agent.

**Recommendation 3A.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA suggests against the use of **thiopurine monotherapy** over no treatment for achieving remission. *(Conditional recommendation, very low certainty evidence.)* Note: the recommendation box says "thiopurines monotherapy"; the Table 3 summary row says "thiopurines".

**Recommendation 3B.** In adult outpatients with quiescent moderate to severe CD (or patients in corticosteroid-induced remission), the AGA suggests the use of **thiopurine monotherapy** over no treatment for the maintenance of remission. *(Conditional recommendation, low certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 3C.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA suggests the use of subcutaneous or intramuscular methotrexate monotherapy over no treatment for the induction and maintenance of remission. *(Conditional recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 3D.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA suggests against the use of oral methotrexate monotherapy over no treatment for the induction and maintenance of remission. *(Conditional recommendation, very low certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 4.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA recommends the use of biologic drug monotherapy over thiopurine monotherapy for the induction of remission. *(Strong recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 5A.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD who are naïve to biologics and immunomodulators, the AGA suggests the use of infliximab in combination with thiopurines for the induction and maintenance of remission over infliximab monotherapy. *(Conditional recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.)* Comment: Based on indirect evidence, combination infliximab with methotrexate may be more effective over infliximab monotherapy.

**Recommendation 5B.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD who are naïve to biologics and immunomodulators, the AGA suggests the use of adalimumab in combination with thiopurines for the induction and maintenance of remission over adalimumab monotherapy. *(Conditional recommendation, very low certainty evidence.)* Comment: Based on indirect evidence, combination adalimumab with methotrexate may be more effective over adalimumab monotherapy.

**Recommendation 5C.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA makes no recommendation regarding the use of ustekinumab or vedolizumab in combination with thiopurines or methotrexate over biologic drug monotherapy for the induction and maintenance of remission. *(No recommendation, knowledge gap.)*

**Recommendation 6.** In adult outpatients with quiescent CD on combination therapy, the AGA makes no recommendation for withdrawal of either the immunomodulator or the biologic over ongoing combination therapy of a biologic and an immunomodulator. *(No recommendation, knowledge gap.)*

**Recommendation 7.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA suggests early introduction with a biologic with or without an immunomodulator rather than delaying their use until after failure of 5-aminosalicylates and/or corticosteroids. *(Conditional recommendation, low certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 8A.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA suggests the use of corticosteroids over no treatment for induction of remission. *(Conditional recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 8B.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA recommends against the use of corticosteroids over no treatment for maintenance of remission. *(Strong recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 9.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA recommends against the use of 5-aminosalicylates or sulfasalazine over no treatment for the induction or maintenance of remission. *(Strong recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.)*

### Pharmacologic management of FISTULIZING (perianal) CD

**Recommendation 10A.** In adult outpatients with CD and active perianal fistula, the AGA recommends the use of infliximab over no treatment for the induction and maintenance of fistula remission. *(Strong recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 10B.** In adult outpatients with CD and active perianal fistula, the AGA suggests the use of adalimumab, ustekinumab, or vedolizumab over no treatment for the induction or maintenance of fistula remission. *(Conditional recommendation, low certainty evidence.)* Comment: Evidence suggests certolizumab pegol may not be effective for induction of fistula remission.

**Recommendation 10C.** In adult outpatients with CD and active perianal fistula without perianal abscess, the AGA suggests against the use of antibiotics alone over no treatment for the induction of fistula remission. *(Conditional recommendation, low certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 11.** In adult outpatients with CD and active perianal fistula without perianal abscess, the AGA recommends the use of biologic agents in combination with an antibiotic over a biologic drug alone for the induction of fistula remission. *(Strong recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.)*

### Supporting evidence / data points

**Biologics vs placebo** (13 RCTs for induction, 9 for maintenance; RR for *failure*, <1 favors drug):

| Drug | Induction RR (95% CI) | Maintenance RR (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|
| Infliximab | 0.54 (0.39–0.75) | 0.77 (0.65–0.92) |
| Adalimumab | 0.82 (0.75–0.89) | 0.70 (0.62–0.79) |
| Certolizumab pegol | 0.92 (0.86–0.98) | 0.88 (0.83–0.93) |
| Vedolizumab | 0.92 (0.87–0.97) | 0.78 (0.67–0.91) |
| Ustekinumab | 0.90 (0.85–0.94) | 0.75 (0.64–0.89) |
| Natalizumab | 0.88 (0.82–0.96) | 0.58 (0.48–0.70) |

- Natalizumab **did** show benefit for both induction and maintenance; the recommendation against it rests on PML risk plus availability of other drugs — not on lack of efficacy.
- Certainty moderate for infliximab, adalimumab, ustekinumab — rated down for imprecision from a low number of events (<200; low optimal information size [OIS]). Certolizumab pegol and vedolizumab rated **down to low for induction** because summary estimates did not meet the 10% MCID over placebo; maintenance certainty moderate (rated down for imprecision, low OIS).
- **Network meta-analysis (biologic-naïve, induction; 8 RCTs, 1458 patients):** infliximab > certolizumab pegol (OR 4.33; 1.83–10.27, moderate confidence); infliximab vs vedolizumab (OR 2.20; 0.79–6.07) and vs ustekinumab (OR 2.14; 0.89–5.15) — low confidence, rated down for imprecision; ustekinumab (OR 2.02; 1.09–3.75) and adalimumab (OR 2.97; 1.16–6.70) over certolizumab pegol — low confidence, very serious imprecision; vedolizumab over certolizumab pegol (OR 1.97; 0.88–4.41) — very low confidence. **No significant difference among adalimumab, ustekinumab, or vedolizumab as a first-line agent (very low certainty).**
- **After prior anti-TNFα exposure (NMA; 6 RCTs, 1606 patients):** ustekinumab superior to placebo (OR 2.58; 1.50–4.44, moderate certainty); adalimumab in prior infliximab intolerance or secondary nonresponse (OR 3.57; 1.66–7.65, moderate certainty); vedolizumab (OR 1.53; 0.77–3.06, low certainty — very serious imprecision, wide CI crossing unity).
- **SONIC** (Study of Biologic and Immunomodulator Naïve Patients in Crohn's Disease; 3-arm RCT of infliximab vs azathioprine vs infliximab + azathioprine)**:** infliximab more effective than azathioprine for induction of clinical remission (RR 0.79; 0.67–0.94) and endoscopic remission (65 of 93 vs 91 of 109; *P* < .01). Moderate certainty, rated down for imprecision (low OIS). **No RCTs compared biologic monotherapy with methotrexate monotherapy.**
- **Methotrexate — dose and route matter (the whole basis of Recs 3C vs 3D):**
  - **Subcutaneous, 25 mg/wk** — 1 trial, 141 patients: effective for **induction**, RR 0.75 (0.61–0.93).
  - **Subcutaneous, 15 mg/wk** — 1 trial, 76 patients already in remission after 16–25 weeks of 25 mg/wk subcutaneous methotrexate: effective for **maintaining corticosteroid-free remission**, RR 0.57 (0.34–0.94). Certainty moderate for induction and maintenance, rated down for imprecision (small sample).
  - **Oral, 12.5 mg/wk** — 1 RCT: **not** effective for induction, RR 1.14 (0.72–1.82); maintenance arm RR 0.30 (0.04–2.27). Very low certainty (indirectness from the low dose, very serious imprecision). The Panel noted this single trial may have used a **suboptimal dose** and that it is not clear whether a higher oral dose would be more effective.
- **Thiopurine monotherapy:** 5 trials, 380 patients — no increased efficacy over placebo for achieving **corticosteroid-free remission in corticosteroid-dependent patients** (very low certainty: serious bias, indirectness, serious imprecision). 5 RCTs did show thiopurines more effective than placebo/no treatment for **maintaining** corticosteroid-free clinical remission, RR 0.62 (0.47–0.81) — low certainty (inadequate blinding, low OIS).
- **Infliximab + thiopurine vs infliximab monotherapy** (2 trials): induction RR 0.77 (0.64–0.92), certainty moderate; maintenance RR 0.74 (0.60–0.90), certainty low (rated down for indirectness — maintenance arms enrolled patients with active disease, not specifically quiescent disease — and imprecision).
- **Infliximab + methotrexate vs infliximab monotherapy** (1 RCT, 126 patients): **no difference** — corticosteroid-free remission at week 14 RR 1.07 (0.57–2.03); failure to maintain corticosteroid-free clinical remission at week 50 RR 1.18 (0.68–2.03). Low certainty (very serious imprecision).
- **DIAMOND** (open-label RCT, adalimumab + azathioprine vs adalimumab monotherapy, 52 wk): **no difference** in induction (RR 1.31; 0.80–2.14) or maintenance (RR 1.13; 0.72–1.78) of remission. Higher **endoscopic** remission at week 26 with combination: 48 of 57 [84.2%] vs 37 of 58 [**63.2%** as printed in the source; 37/58 computes to 63.8%]; *P* = .02. Very low certainty (unblinded, high drug-discontinuation rates, indirectness of outcomes, low OIS).
- **Harms of adding a thiopurine to an anti-TNFα:** increased risk of infections and a **2- to 3-fold higher risk of lymphoma** vs anti-TNFα monotherapy.
- **Early combined immunosuppression (basis for Rec 7) — mixed, not uniformly positive:**
  - **D'Haens** (open-label): at 52 wk, **61.5%** of the early combined immunosuppression arm in corticosteroid- and surgery-free remission vs **42.2%** with conventional step-up; RR for failure to achieve remission 0.67 (0.46–0.97). Low certainty (open label, imprecision).
  - **REACT** (Randomised Evaluation of an Algorithm for Crohn's Treatment; open-label cluster RCT, 1982 patients): **no significant difference** in corticosteroid-free remission at 12 mo (66% early combination vs 62% usual care); at 24 mo, **lower major adverse disease-related complications** with early combination, hazard ratio (HR) 0.73 (0.62–0.86).
  - **Cosnes** (RCT, 122 patients, early azathioprine within 6 mo of diagnosis vs conventional): over 3 y, **no significant difference** in corticosteroid-requiring flare (58/65 [89%] vs 61/67 [91%]; *P* = .73), hospitalization (22/65 [34%] vs 26/67 [39%]; *P* = .74), or CD-related surgery (5/65 [8%] vs 4/67 [6%]; *P* = .68). Very low certainty (open label).
- **Withdrawal from combination therapy (Rec 6):** 3 RCTs, 161 patients on anti-TNFα + immunomodulator ≥6 mo — no significant difference in relapse over 12–24 mo between continuing combination and withdrawing the immunomodulator, RR 1.02 (0.71–1.46); very low certainty (unblinded, very serious imprecision). **No RCTs compared continued combination therapy with withdrawal of the *biologic*.** A single prospective cohort of 115 patients who stopped infliximab while on combination therapy: relapse **44% at 1 y, 52% at 2 y**.
- **Corticosteroids:**
  - Systemic corticosteroids, 2 RCTs, 267 patients, at a **prednisone-equivalent dose up to 60 mg/d** — more effective than placebo for **induction**, RR 0.57 (0.45–0.73). Low certainty (bias: sequence generation and allocation concealment not reported; imprecision, low OIS).
  - Controlled-release **budesonide**, 3 RCTs, 367 patients — more effective than placebo for induction, RR 0.74 (0.60–0.91), **but 2 of the 3 studies enrolled patients with mild to moderate CD** → certainty rated low for indirectness (nonmoderate to severe CD) and imprecision.
  - Budesonide vs systemic corticosteroids, 5 RCTs: budesonide **inferior** for inducing remission, RR for failure to induce 1.20 (1.01–1.44). The Panel noted that in CD involving the **distal ileum and/or ascending colon**, patients more concerned about systemic corticosteroid adverse effects and less concerned about the lower efficacy may reasonably choose budesonide.
  - **Maintenance:** 3 studies, 269 patients — corticosteroids no more effective than placebo, RR 1.02 (0.81–1.29). Low certainty (unclear randomization; wide CI). Neither systemic corticosteroids nor budesonide has a role in long-term maintenance.
- **5-ASA and sulfasalazine — the two are not the same on induction data:**
  - **5-ASA induction**, 2 RCTs (underlying CD severity unclear; no moderate-to-severe subgroup extractable): **did not reach the 10% MCID** over placebo, RR 0.90 (0.81–1.00).
  - **Sulfasalazine induction**, 3 RCTs (overall severity unclear): sulfasalazine **was more effective than placebo** over 6–17 weeks, RR 0.78 (0.65–0.93) — *but it was unclear whether these patients had moderate to severe luminal CD*.
  - **Maintenance:** 4 studies (415 patients, sulfasalazine) and 11 RCTs (2014 patients, 5-ASA) — neither more effective than placebo (sulfasalazine RR 0.98; 0.82–1.17 — very low certainty; 5-ASA RR 1.02; 0.92–1.16 — moderate certainty).
  - Basis for the **strong** recommendation against (Rec 9): lack of induction data specifically in moderate to severe CD plus clear failure to maintain remission, and the risk that starting an ineffective drug delays appropriate therapy. The Panel noted 5-ASAs have a robust safety profile, but **sulfasalazine is associated with many adverse events**.
- **Perianal fistula** (data limited to *perianal* fistula; drug therapy for other fistula types was almost entirely lacking, so the Panel restricted scope):

  | Agent | Evidence base | Fistula-closure RR (95% CI) | Certainty |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Infliximab | **Only drug with a dedicated fistula RCT** — 94 patients with symptomatic draining fistula; closure within 18 wk | **0.52 (0.34–0.78)** | Moderate |
  | Adalimumab | Subgroup analysis, 2 RCTs, 77 patients; complete closure within 4 wk | 1.08 (0.93–1.27) — not effective | Very low |
  | Certolizumab pegol | Subgroup analysis, 2 RCTs, 165 patients | 1.01 (0.80–1.27) — no benefit | Very low |
  | Vedolizumab | Subgroup analysis, 1 RCT, 165 patients; complete closure within 14 wk | 0.81 (0.63–1.04) | Very low |
  | Ustekinumab | Pooled analysis, 4 induction trials, 238 patients | 0.85 (0.73–1.99) | Low |
  | Antibiotics alone | Single 3-arm RCT, 25 patients (ciprofloxacin vs metronidazole vs placebo) | 0.94 (0.67–1.33) — no benefit | Low |
  | **Anti-TNFα + ciprofloxacin × 12 wk** vs same anti-TNFα alone | 2 RCTs (infliximab or adalimumab); closure over 12–18 wk | **0.42 (0.26–0.68)** | Moderate |

  Despite the non-significant subgroup result, adalimumab is *suggested* (Rec 10B) on indirect evidence of luminal efficacy; certolizumab pegol is singled out in the Rec 10B comment as possibly **not** effective for induction of fistula remission. Maintenance data for the biologics in fistulizing disease were limited; thiopurine data were too limited for a recommendation.
- **Equity:** conflicting data on race/ethnicity in IBD therapy — some studies show African American, Asian, and Hispanic patients less likely to receive biologics; African American and Hispanic populations are less likely to have commercial insurance and more likely to be on Medicaid or uninsured, which may drive disparities.

## Relevance to Wiki
- Foundational pharmacologic backbone for [[crohns-disease]] — original AGA positioning statements (biologic-naïve, anti-TNF primary vs secondary nonresponse), combination anti-TNF + thiopurine evidence, early-biologic (anti–step-up) stance, and perianal fistula recommendations.
- **Largely superseded for drug positioning** by [[aga-2025-crohns-pharm]] and [[acg-2025-crohns]], which incorporate interleukin-23 (IL-23) inhibitors (risankizumab, mirikizumab, guselkumab), upadacitinib, and head-to-head data (SEQUENCE). This 2021 guideline predates those agents — it has no recommendation on IL-23 p19 inhibitors or Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors.
- Still authoritative for: corticosteroids induction-only/against-maintenance, 5-ASA/sulfasalazine against, thiopurine monotherapy against for achieving remission, subcutaneous (not oral) methotrexate, and infliximab as the best-evidenced perianal fistula agent with antibiotic combination.
- **Doses this guideline supplies** (relevant to [[methotrexate]] and [[corticosteroids-ibd|corticosteroids]] pages): subcutaneous methotrexate **25 mg/wk** for induction and **15 mg/wk** for maintenance after 16–25 wk of 25 mg/wk; oral methotrexate **12.5 mg/wk** was the dose that failed; systemic corticosteroids at a **prednisone-equivalent up to 60 mg/d** for induction; ciprofloxacin added to an anti-TNFα **for 12 weeks** in perianal fistula.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- The 2021 NMA found no significant first-line difference among adalimumab, ustekinumab, vedolizumab; the newer 2025 guidelines reposition agents by efficacy tier (infliximab/risankizumab/upadacitinib higher; certolizumab/vedolizumab lower) based on expanded data — defer to 2025 for current sequencing.
- No recommendation on withdrawal of immunomodulator vs biologic on combination therapy (Rec 6) or on combining vedolizumab/ustekinumab with immunomodulators (Rec 5C) — both remain knowledge gaps.
- **Internal tension in Recs 5A/5B.** Both comments state that, on indirect evidence, combining infliximab (or adalimumab) with **methotrexate** may be more effective than monotherapy — yet the only direct RCT of infliximab + methotrexate vs infliximab (126 patients) found **no difference** at week 14 or week 50, and the Panel rated that evidence low certainty. The recommendations themselves are worded for **thiopurines**, not methotrexate.
- **Rec 5B rests on a trial whose primary outcomes were negative.** DIAMOND showed no difference in induction or maintenance of clinical remission; only the week-26 endoscopic remission secondary outcome favored combination, at very low certainty — hence the Conditional / very low grading.
- **Rec 9 is strong despite a positive sulfazalazine induction signal.** Sulfasalazine beat placebo for induction (RR 0.78; 0.65–0.93), but the severity of the enrolled population was unclear, so the Panel judged the moderate-to-severe induction evidence absent and leaned on the clear maintenance failure. Reading "5-ASA and sulfasalazine both failed the MCID" misstates the document — only 5-ASA did.
- **Rec 1D is about harm, not efficacy.** Natalizumab was effective for both induction and maintenance (and had the single best maintenance point estimate, RR 0.58); PML risk plus available alternatives drive the suggestion against it.
- The oral-methotrexate recommendation (3D) may be a **dose artifact** — the single negative RCT used 12.5 mg/wk, and the Panel explicitly said it is unclear whether a higher oral dose would be effective.
