## Summary

This is the foundational AGA pharmacologic guideline for **moderate to severe luminal and perianal fistulizing Crohn's disease (CD)** in adults, developed with GRADE methodology. Moderate-to-severe disease was defined as a Crohn's Disease Activity Index (CDAI) score ≥220. The predetermined critical outcomes were **induction and maintenance of remission**; in the recommendations, drug effect is expressed as risk of *failure* to induce/maintain remission (RR or OR <1 favors the drug). The minimal clinically important difference (MCID) over placebo was set at 10%. The guideline does **not** address surgical management, and therapeutic drug monitoring is covered in a separate 2017 AGA guideline.

Across induction and maintenance, the Panel recommends anti-TNFα agents (infliximab, adalimumab, certolizumab pegol), vedolizumab, and ustekinumab over no treatment, while recommending **against** natalizumab (PML risk with available alternatives) and **against** 5-aminosalicylates/sulfasalazine and thiopurine monotherapy for induction. Corticosteroids are endorsed for induction only (recommended against for maintenance). Biologic monotherapy is recommended over thiopurine monotherapy for induction, and combination of infliximab/adalimumab with thiopurines is suggested over anti-TNF monotherapy in biologic- and immunomodulator-naïve patients. The Panel suggests **early introduction of a biologic** rather than delaying until after failure of 5-ASA/corticosteroids (an anti-"step-up" stance). Positioning recommendations are given for biologic-naïve, anti-TNF primary nonresponders, and anti-TNF secondary nonresponders. For perianal fistula, infliximab has the most robust evidence; biologic + antibiotic combination is recommended over biologic alone.

Because there were no head-to-head trials, positioning relied on **network meta-analysis** of indirect evidence (rated down for imprecision), so most positioning statements are conditional/low certainty.

## Key Findings / Claims (verbatim recommendations with strength and certainty)

### Pharmacologic management of moderate to severe LUMINAL CD

**Recommendation 1A.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA recommends the use of anti-TNFα over no treatment for induction and maintenance of remission. *(Strong recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.)* Comment: Although the evidence supporting infliximab and adalimumab was moderate certainty, the evidence for certolizumab pegol was low certainty.

**Recommendation 1B.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA suggests the use of vedolizumab over no treatment for the induction and maintenance of remission. *(Conditional recommendation; low certainty for induction, moderate certainty for maintenance.)*

**Recommendation 1C.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA recommends the use of ustekinumab over no treatment for the induction and maintenance of remission. *(Strong recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 1D.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA suggests against the use of natalizumab over no treatment for the induction and maintenance of remission. *(Conditional recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.)* Comment: Given evidence of harm from post-marketing data from progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML) and the availability of other drugs, the AGA suggests against the use of natalizumab. Patients who are John Cunningham virus antibody–negative who put a high value on the potential benefits and lower value on PML risk and who will adhere to ongoing monitoring for John Cunningham virus positivity, may consider using natalizumab.

**Recommendation 2A.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD who are naïve to biologic drugs, the AGA recommends the use of infliximab, adalimumab, or ustekinumab over certolizumab pegol for the induction of remission *(Strong recommendation, moderate certainty evidence)* and suggests the use of vedolizumab over certolizumab pegol for the induction of remission. *(Conditional recommendation, low certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 2B.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD who never responded to anti-TNFα (primary nonresponse), the AGA recommends the use of ustekinumab *(Strong recommendation, moderate certainty evidence)* and suggests the use of vedolizumab over no treatment for the induction of remission. *(Conditional recommendation, low certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 2C.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD who previously responded to infliximab (secondary nonresponse), the AGA recommends the use of adalimumab or ustekinumab *(Strong recommendation, moderate certainty evidence)* and suggests the use of vedolizumab over no treatment for the induction of remission. *(Conditional recommendation, low certainty evidence.)* Comment: If adalimumab was the first-line drug used there is indirect evidence to suggest the option of using infliximab as a second-line agent.

**Recommendation 3A.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA suggests against the use of thiopurines over no treatment for achieving remission. *(Conditional recommendation, very low certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 3B.** In adult outpatients with quiescent moderate to severe CD (or patients in corticosteroid-induced remission), the AGA suggests the use of thiopurines over no treatment for the maintenance of remission. *(Conditional recommendation, low certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 3C.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA suggests the use of subcutaneous or intramuscular methotrexate monotherapy over no treatment for the induction and maintenance of remission. *(Conditional recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 3D.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA suggests against the use of oral methotrexate monotherapy over no treatment for the induction and maintenance of remission. *(Conditional recommendation, very low certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 4.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA recommends the use of biologic drug monotherapy over thiopurine monotherapy for the induction of remission. *(Strong recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 5A.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD who are naïve to biologics and immunomodulators, the AGA suggests the use of infliximab in combination with thiopurines for the induction and maintenance of remission over infliximab monotherapy. *(Conditional recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.)* Comment: Based on indirect evidence, combination infliximab with methotrexate may be more effective over infliximab monotherapy.

**Recommendation 5B.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD who are naïve to biologics and immunomodulators, the AGA suggests the use of adalimumab in combination with thiopurines for the induction and maintenance of remission over adalimumab monotherapy. *(Conditional recommendation, very low certainty evidence.)* Comment: Based on indirect evidence, combination adalimumab with methotrexate may be more effective over adalimumab monotherapy.

**Recommendation 5C.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA makes no recommendation regarding the use of ustekinumab or vedolizumab in combination with thiopurines or methotrexate over biologic drug monotherapy for the induction and maintenance of remission. *(No recommendation, knowledge gap.)*

**Recommendation 6.** In adult outpatients with quiescent CD on combination therapy, the AGA makes no recommendation for withdrawal of either the immunomodulator or the biologic over ongoing combination therapy of a biologic and an immunomodulator. *(No recommendation, knowledge gap.)*

**Recommendation 7.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA suggests early introduction with a biologic with or without an immunomodulator rather than delaying their use until after failure of 5-aminosalicylates and/or corticosteroids. *(Conditional recommendation, low certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 8A.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA suggests the use of corticosteroids over no treatment for induction of remission. *(Conditional recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 8B.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA recommends against the use of corticosteroids over no treatment for maintenance of remission. *(Strong recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 9.** In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA recommends against the use of 5-aminosalicylates or sulfasalazine over no treatment for the induction or maintenance of remission. *(Strong recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.)*

### Pharmacologic management of FISTULIZING (perianal) CD

**Recommendation 10A.** In adult outpatients with CD and active perianal fistula, the AGA recommends the use of infliximab over no treatment for the induction and maintenance of fistula remission. *(Strong recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 10B.** In adult outpatients with CD and active perianal fistula, the AGA suggests the use of adalimumab, ustekinumab, or vedolizumab over no treatment for the induction or maintenance of fistula remission. *(Conditional recommendation, low certainty evidence.)* Comment: Evidence suggests certolizumab pegol may not be effective for induction of fistula remission.

**Recommendation 10C.** In adult outpatients with CD and active perianal fistula without perianal abscess, the AGA suggests against the use of antibiotics alone over no treatment for the induction of fistula remission. *(Conditional recommendation, low certainty evidence.)*

**Recommendation 11.** In adult outpatients with CD and active perianal fistula without perianal abscess, the AGA recommends the use of biologic agents in combination with an antibiotic over a biologic drug alone for the induction of fistula remission. *(Strong recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.)*

### Supporting evidence / data points
- **Induction (vs placebo, RR for failure):** infliximab 0.54 (95% CI 0.39–0.75); adalimumab 0.82 (0.75–0.89); certolizumab pegol 0.92 (0.86–0.98); vedolizumab 0.92 (0.87–0.97); ustekinumab 0.90 (0.85–0.94). Natalizumab 0.88 (0.82–0.96).
- **Maintenance (vs placebo, RR for failure):** infliximab 0.77 (0.65–0.92); adalimumab 0.70 (0.62–0.79); certolizumab pegol 0.88 (0.83–0.93); vedolizumab 0.78 (0.67–0.91); ustekinumab 0.75 (0.64–0.89). Natalizumab 0.58 (0.48–0.70).
- Certolizumab pegol and vedolizumab certainty rated down to low for induction because summary effects did not meet the 10% MCID over placebo.
- **Network meta-analysis (biologic-naïve, induction):** infliximab more effective than certolizumab pegol (OR 4.33; 1.83–10.27); infliximab vs vedolizumab (OR 2.20; 0.79–6.07) and vs ustekinumab (OR 2.14; 0.89–5.15); ustekinumab (OR 2.02; 1.09–3.75) and adalimumab (OR 2.97; 1.16–6.70) over certolizumab pegol; no significant difference among adalimumab, ustekinumab, vedolizumab as first-line.
- **After prior anti-TNF failure (NMA):** ustekinumab superior to placebo (OR 2.58; 1.50–4.44); adalimumab in prior infliximab intolerance/secondary nonresponse (OR 3.57; 1.66–7.65); vedolizumab (OR 1.53; 0.77–3.06, low certainty).
- **SONIC:** infliximab more effective than azathioprine for clinical remission (RR 0.79; 0.67–0.94) and endoscopic remission.
- **Subcutaneous methotrexate:** induction RR 0.75 (0.61–0.93); maintenance of corticosteroid-free remission RR 0.57 (0.34–0.94). **Oral methotrexate** not effective for induction (RR 1.14; 0.72–1.82) — possibly suboptimal 12.5 mg/wk dose.
- **Thiopurine monotherapy:** not effective for induction (5 trials, 380 patients); maintained corticosteroid-free remission RR 0.62 (0.47–0.81).
- **Combination (infliximab + thiopurine vs infliximab) induction:** RR 0.77 (0.64–0.92); maintenance RR 0.74 (0.60–0.90). DIAMOND (adalimumab + AZA): higher endoscopic remission at wk 26 (48/57 [84.2%] vs 37/58 [63.8%]; P=.02) but no clinical remission difference.
- **Early combined immunosuppression (D'Haens, REACT, Cosnes):** REACT showed lower major adverse disease-related complications at 24 mo (HR 0.73; 0.62–0.86) with early combination/biologic; supports anti–step-up stance.
- **Withdrawal of immunomodulator on combination therapy:** no significant difference in relapse over 12–24 mo (RR 1.02; 0.71–1.46) — basis for no recommendation (Rec 6).
- **Corticosteroids:** systemic steroids effective for induction (RR 0.57; 0.45–0.73); budesonide effective for induction (RR 0.74; 0.60–0.91) but inferior to systemic steroids (RR for failure 1.20; 1.01–1.44); neither effective for maintenance (RR 1.02; 0.81–1.29).
- **5-ASA/sulfasalazine:** did not reach 10% MCID for induction; not effective for maintenance.
- **Perianal fistula:** infliximab fistula closure RR 0.52 (0.34–0.78); adalimumab (RR 1.08; 0.93–1.27) and certolizumab pegol (RR 1.01; 0.80–1.27) not significant in dedicated subgroups; vedolizumab RR 0.81 (0.63–1.04); ustekinumab RR 0.85 (0.73–1.99); antibiotics alone RR 0.94 (0.67–1.33). **Anti-TNF + ciprofloxacin** fistula closure RR 0.42 (0.26–0.68).

## Relevance to Wiki
- Foundational pharmacologic backbone for [[crohns-disease]] — original AGA positioning statements (biologic-naïve, anti-TNF primary vs secondary nonresponse), combination anti-TNF + thiopurine evidence, early-biologic (anti–step-up) stance, and perianal fistula recommendations.
- **Largely superseded for drug positioning** by [[aga-2025-crohns-pharm]] and [[acg-2025-crohns]], which incorporate IL-23 inhibitors (risankizumab, mirikizumab, guselkumab), upadacitinib, and head-to-head data (SEQUENCE). This 2021 guideline predates those agents — it has no recommendation on IL-23 p19 inhibitors or JAK inhibitors.
- Still authoritative for: corticosteroids induction-only/against-maintenance, 5-ASA/sulfasalazine against, thiopurine monotherapy against for induction, subcutaneous (not oral) methotrexate, and infliximab as the best-evidenced perianal fistula agent with antibiotic combination.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- The 2021 NMA found no significant first-line difference among adalimumab, ustekinumab, vedolizumab; the newer 2025 guidelines reposition agents by efficacy tier (infliximab/risankizumab/upadacitinib higher; certolizumab/vedolizumab lower) based on expanded data — defer to 2025 for current sequencing.
- No recommendation on withdrawal of immunomodulator vs biologic on combination therapy (Rec 6) or on combining vedolizumab/ustekinumab with immunomodulators (Rec 5C) — both remain knowledge gaps.
