## Summary

Commentary on cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome (CHS), a chronic disorder of gut–brain interaction (Rome IV) and a **subtype of cyclic vomiting syndrome (CVS)** associated with chronic (typically years), heavy (daily/near-daily) cannabis use, predominantly in males. CHS is rising in prevalence and underrecognized. Diagnosis is clinical (no confirmatory test), resting on stereotyped cyclic vomiting, a defined cannabis-use pattern, and resolution after cannabis cessation; compulsive hot-water bathing is a supportive but non-pathognomonic feature. Management pairs acute antiemetic control (notably topical capsaicin) with long-term cannabis cessation and tricyclic antidepressants.

## Key Findings / Claims

**CVS — Rome IV criteria (adults, Table 1):**
- Stereotypical episodes of vomiting regarding onset (acute) and duration (<1 week).
- ≥3 discrete episodes in the prior year **and** 2 episodes in the past 6 months, occurring ≥1 week apart.
- Absence of nausea/vomiting between episodes (milder symptoms may persist between episodes).
- Supportive: personal or family history of migraine headaches.
- Criteria fulfilled for the last 3 months with onset ≥6 months before diagnosis.

**CHS — proposed diagnostic criteria (Venkatesan et al.):**
1. **Clinical features:** stereotypical episodic vomiting resembling CVS in onset, frequency ≥3 times annually.
2. **Cannabis use pattern:** duration >1 year before symptom onset; frequency >4 times per week on average.
3. **Cannabis cessation:** resolution of symptoms after abstinence for ≥6 months, or ≥ the total duration of 3 typical vomiting cycles in that patient.

- No confirmatory test. Hot-water bathing reported in ~71% of CHS (systematic review, 271 cases: mean age 30 yr, 69% male, mean 6.6 yr of cannabis use before onset, daily use 68%); not pathognomonic (also seen in CVS).
- **4 phases of CVS (Fleisher):** interepisodic, prodromal, emetic, recovery.
- **Complications:** dehydration, acute kidney injury, electrolyte/metabolic imbalance; rarer pneumothorax/pneumomediastinum; death.
- **ED evaluation** must exclude life-threatening mimics: acute abdomen, bowel obstruction, mesenteric ischemia, pancreatitis, myocardial infarction.
- **Outpatient DDx** after excluding structural disease: rumination syndrome, gastroparesis, CVS, pregnancy, migraine, functional chronic nausea and vomiting.

**Management:**
- **Acute/short-term (case series, small trials):** topical **capsaicin 0.1% cream** to the upper abdomen (TRPV1 activation), benzodiazepines, **haloperidol**, promethazine, olanzapine, ondansetron. **Avoid opioids** (worsen nausea, addiction risk).
- **Long-term:** counseling for **cannabis cessation** + **tricyclic antidepressant (amitriptyline)** as mainstay — start 25 mg at bedtime, titrate weekly to minimal effective dose **75–100 mg at bedtime**; efficacious in ~70%.
- Abrupt ("cold turkey") cessation may trigger cannabinoid withdrawal and high recidivism; co-manage with psychology/psychiatry for refractory disease or psychiatric comorbidity.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- Commentary, no formal evidence ratings; treatment evidence limited to case series/small RCTs. Critical appraisal has questioned the etiologic role of cannabis in CHS. Many patients remain uncertain about the cannabis link, and recidivism is high.
