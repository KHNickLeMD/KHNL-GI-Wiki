## Summary

This joint ACG/CAG guideline addresses two clinical contexts: (1) management of antithrombotic drugs in patients with acute gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding, and (2) periendoscopic management in patients undergoing elective endoscopic procedures. The guideline uses the GRADE methodology and yields 19 statements (numbered 1–18, with 14A/14B): 14 conditional recommendations (all very low certainty except Statement 12, low) and 5 where the panel could not reach a recommendation (2, 14B, 16, 17, 18).

**Acute GI Bleeding (Statements 1–10):**

- Against fresh frozen plasma (FFP) for warfarin reversal (Statement 1); no recommendation for or against prothrombin complex concentrate (PCC) vs. placebo (Statement 2); PCC preferred over FFP if reversal agent is used (Statement 3); against vitamin K in acute setting (Statement 4)
- Against idarucizumab for dabigatran-related GI bleeding (Statement 5)
- Against andexanet alfa for rivaroxaban/apixaban-related GI bleeding (Statement 6)
- Against PCC for direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC)-related GI bleeding (Statement 7)
- Against platelet transfusion for antiplatelet-related GI bleeding (Statement 8)
- Against holding cardiac aspirin (ASA) (secondary prevention) during GI bleeding (Statement 9); if held, resume on the day hemostasis is endoscopically confirmed (Statement 10)

**Elective Periendoscopic Period (Statements 11–18):**

- Continue warfarin rather than temporarily interrupt (1–7 days) for elective/planned procedures (Statement 11); if held, no bridging (Statement 12; the abstract adds "unless the patient has a mechanical heart valve")
- Temporarily interrupt DOACs rather than continue (Statement 13); typical interruption ~1–2 days pre-procedure
- Interrupt P2Y12 inhibitor while continuing ASA in dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) patients (Statement 14A); no recommendation for single-agent P2Y12 inhibitor monotherapy (Statement 14B)
- Continue ASA monotherapy (secondary prevention) for elective procedures (Statement 15)
- No recommendation on timing of resumption for warfarin (Statement 16), DOAC (Statement 17), or P2Y12 inhibitor (Statement 18) after elective endoscopy

## Key Findings / Claims

### Reversal Agents — Acute GI Bleeding

**Vitamin K antagonist (VKA; warfarin):**

- FFP fails to reliably normalize international normalized ratio (INR) (range 1.6–3.8 in one cohort); associated with fluid overload and possible thrombotic risk
- PCC (panel treated 3- and 4-factor PCC as equivalent) achieves rapid INR correction (relative risk [RR] for rapid correction vs FFP: 6.99, 95% confidence interval [CI] 3.61–13.53); preferred over FFP if reversal is needed
- Vitamin K takes 24–48 hours to work and is not useful for acute hemostasis; may be considered only if intent is prolonged reversal or cessation of VKA
- Caveats: PCC or FFP can be considered in life-threatening GI hemorrhage, supratherapeutic INR substantially above range, or when massive transfusion is undesirable

**Dabigatran — idarucizumab:**

- One comparative cohort (N=1,283 GI bleed patients): no significant mortality or venous thromboembolism (VTE) difference with idarucizumab
- High cost and very low certainty evidence; selective use may be appropriate in life-threatening bleed within 24 hours of last dose

**Rivaroxaban/apixaban — andexanet alfa:**

- Single uncontrolled cohort: among 62 GI-bleeding patients in the efficacy group, 85% excellent/good hemostasis at 12 hours; no control group; thrombotic events + 30-day mortality reported only for the whole cohort (9.7% and 13.9% of 352 patients); high cost (~$49,500 for high-dose regimen)
- Selective use may be appropriate in life-threatening bleed within 24 hours of last dose

**DOACs — 4-factor PCC:**

- Very sparse data; 2 patients with GI bleed on dabigatran receiving 4-factor PCC (4F-PCC) both died within 30 days (unadjusted)
- Selective use may be justified in life-threatening bleed within 24 hours of last dose

**Antiplatelet — platelet transfusion:**

- One cohort study (N=204 matched pairs): platelet transfusion significantly associated with increased mortality (odds ratio [OR] 5.57, 95% CI 1.52–27.1) and nonsignificant increases in rebleeding and thrombosis; against routine use

**ASA:**

- Irreversible platelet inhibition persists 7–10 days; stopping ASA has little effect on acute course
- Randomized controlled trial (RCT) (N=156, high-risk ulcer bleed): ASA continuation vs. 8-week interruption — similar rebleeding at 30 days but significantly lower 8-week mortality with continued ASA (1.3% vs. 10.3%)

### Periendoscopic Antithrombotic Management

**Procedural bleeding risk stratification (Table 3):**

- High bleeding risk (30-day major bleed >2%): [[polypectomy]] ≥1 cm, percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy (PEG)/percutaneous endoscopic jejunostomy (PEJ), [[ercp|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]] with sphincterotomy, endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR)/endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD), [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)-fine-needle aspiration (FNA)]], endoscopic hemostasis (excl. argon plasma coagulation [APC]), radiofrequency ablation (RFA), [[poem|peroral endoscopic myotomy (POEM)]], variceal treatment (including band ligation), therapeutic balloon enteroscopy, ampullary resection, tumor ablation, cystogastrostomy, pneumatic/bougie dilation, laser ablation and coagulation
- Low/moderate bleeding risk (≤2%): [[upper-endoscopy|esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD)]] ± biopsy, [[colonoscopy]] ± biopsy, flexible sigmoidoscopy ± biopsy, ERCP with stent (biliary or pancreatic) placement or papillary balloon dilation without sphincterotomy, EUS without FNA, push enteroscopy, diagnostic balloon enteroscopy, enteral stent deployment, APC, balloon dilation of luminal stenoses, polypectomy <1 cm, ERCP without sphincterotomy, marking/clipping/tattooing, video capsule

**Thromboembolic risk stratification (Table 4):**

- High: any mitral valve prosthesis; caged-ball or tilting disc aortic valve; recent (within 3 mo) stroke/transient ischemic attack (TIA); CHADS2 5 or 6 or CHA2DS2-VASc ≥7; recent (within 3 mo) VTE; severe thrombophilia; rheumatic valvular heart disease. Footnote: may also include stroke/TIA >3 mo ago with CHADS2 <5, previous thromboembolism during temporary VKA interruption, or certain surgeries (e.g., cardiac valve replacement, carotid endarterectomy, major vascular surgery)
- Moderate: bileaflet aortic valve (AV) prosthesis + ≥1 of atrial fibrillation (AF), previous stroke/TIA, hypertension, diabetes, congestive heart failure, age >75 y; CHADS2 2–4 (no previous stroke or TIA); CHA2DS2-VASc 5 or 6; VTE within past 3–12 mo; non-severe thrombophilia; recurrent VTE; active cancer (treated within 6 mo or palliative)
- Low: bileaflet AV prosthesis without AF and no other risk factors for stroke; CHADS2 0 or 1; CHA2DS2-VASc 1–4; VTE more than 12 mo ago and no other risk factors

**Warfarin periendoscopic:**

- Suggest continuation for low/moderate bleeding risk procedures
- If interrupted (for high-risk procedures): 5 days of interruption, no bridging — except mechanical heart valve, CHADS2 >5, or previous thromboembolism during VKA interruption
- BRIDGE trial (N=1,813 AF patients): no bridging noninferior for thromboembolism (0.4% vs. 0.3%) and reduced major bleeding (1.3% vs. 3.2%)
- PERIOP-2 trial (N=1,471, included mechanical heart valves N=304): similar results

**DOAC periendoscopic:**

- Temporarily interrupt rather than continue
- Interruption duration: typically 1–2 days before procedure (excluding day of procedure); PAUSE study mean off-DOAC time ~3.9 days total periendoscopic period
- 30-day thromboembolic rate with DOAC interruption: 0.7%; GI bleed rate: 2.5%
- Earlier DOAC resumption (day 0–3 vs. after day 3) associated with lower bleeding (2.3% vs. 11.5%)

**DAPT:**

- Interrupt P2Y12 inhibitor (clopidogrel, prasugrel, ticagrelor), continue ASA
- Applies to elective procedures only; not emergency
- Single P2Y12 inhibitor monotherapy: no recommendation for or against interruption

**ASA monotherapy periendoscopic:**

- Continue for elective procedures (secondary prevention)
- Consider interruption only for highest-risk procedures (ESD, biliary/pancreatic sphincterotomy, ampullectomy, POEM, RFA) where bleeding risk is particularly high
- Primary prevention ASA: stop before higher-risk procedures

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- No strong recommendations: 14 conditional (Statement 12 low certainty, the rest very low); 5 could not reach a recommendation
- No recommendation achievable for PCC vs. placebo in warfarin-related GI bleeding (insufficient comparator data)
- No recommendation on optimal timing of resumption for warfarin, DOACs, or P2Y12 inhibitors after elective endoscopy — this is a major clinical gap
- No recommendation for or against P2Y12 inhibitor monotherapy interruption before elective endoscopy
- Reversal agents (idarucizumab, andexanet alfa) are recommended against for routine use despite biological plausibility, based primarily on lack of controlled data and high cost; clinicians must use judgment for life-threatening bleeds
- DOAC periendoscopic interruption timing was informed largely by the PAUSE study, which lacked a "continue DOAC" comparator arm
- Guideline does not address advanced endoscopic procedures specifically (ESD, POEM) in sufficient depth due to lack of evidence
