## Summary
ACCELERATE-AH (American Consortium of Early Liver Transplantation for Alcoholic Hepatitis) is the US multicenter cohort that established the outcomes achievable with **early liver transplantation — i.e. LT before 6 months of abstinence — for severe alcohol-associated hepatitis**. It is the reference dataset behind the modern position that the 6-month sobriety rule is not an appropriate gate in severe AH, where 75%–90% of deaths occur within 2 months of presentation.

147 consecutive patients transplanted November 2006 – March 2017 (94% from 2011 onward) with clinically diagnosed severe AH, **no prior diagnosis of liver disease and no prior AH episode**, all with Maddrey's discriminant function ≥32. Median pre-LT abstinence was **55 days**. The cohort was profoundly ill — median MELD-Na 39 and median day-7 Lille 0.82 — predicting <20% 6-month survival without transplant.

Survival was **94% at 1 year and 84% at 3 years**, comparable to LT for alcohol-associated cirrhosis and better than several accepted indications. Post-LT alcohol use occurred (25% any use at 1 y, 34% at 3 y) but **sustained** use was infrequent (10% at 1 y, 17% at 3 y) — and it was sustained use, not a slip, that carried the mortality penalty (HR 4.59).

Design caveat that governs how the numbers should be read: this is a **retrospective cohort of selected, transplanted patients**, not a trial. Every patient had strong social support, no severe comorbidity, an addiction/social-work evaluation, and (at 10 of 12 centers) unanimous committee agreement. The survival figures describe what a rigorously selected cohort achieves, not what unselected severe AH achieves.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Selection (9 of 12 centers with reliable committee data, 126 patients)**
- Of **432** patients with severe AH presented to committee: **155 (35.9%) listed**, **126 (29.1%) transplanted**; 20 died on the waitlist, 9 delisted for clinical improvement.
- Acceptance-for-listing rate ranged **13%–100%** across the 9 centers — selection practice is highly center-dependent.
- Primary reasons not to list: **psychosocial 73%**, medical contraindication 12%, clinical improvement during evaluation 8%, financial/insurance 7%.
- Contrast with the European (Mathurin) protocol, where only **7.7%** of referred patients were deemed candidates.

**Cohort characteristics**
- n = 147; 71% male, 83% Caucasian, 66% privately insured.
- Median pre-LT abstinence **55 days** (IQR 36–91).
- At initial hospitalization: median **discriminant function 78** (IQR 58–103), median **MELD-Na 35** (IQR 29–39).
- Median **day-7 Lille 0.82** (IQR 0.56–0.97); median **MELD-Na at listing 38** (IQR 34–40).
- Median listing→LT interval **7 days** (IQR 3–12); median hospitalization→listing **13 days** (IQR 6–31).
- **79% (116/147)** would have met NIAAA inclusion criteria for AH clinical trials — but the NIAAA definition **excludes very severe disease (DF >60 or MELD >30)**, which described most of this cohort.
- Median post-LT follow-up **1.6 years** (IQR 0.8–3.0) — short, and the limit on the 3-year estimates.

**Pre-LT management**
- Only **54% (80/147)** received corticosteroids (alone or with pentoxifylline) — i.e. **46% did not**, reflecting real-world US practice vs 92% in the European protocol.
- Reasons steroids withheld: infection 29, GI bleeding 10, renal injury 8, other non-hepatic 2, provider decision 18.
- **27% (40/147)** improved MELD-Na from hospitalization to listing, median improvement only **2 points**; among these, all but 3 with available scores still had **day-7 Lille >0.45** (i.e. steroid non-response).

**Survival**
- Cumulative post-LT survival **94% at 1 y** (95% CI 89–97) and **84% at 3 y** (95% CI 75–90).
- Predictors of death: **>10 drinks/day at presentation** (HR 3.17, 95% CI 1.04–9.67, P = .04); **any post-LT alcohol use** (HR 3.54, 95% CI 1.06–11.85, P = .04); **sustained post-LT alcohol use** (HR 4.59, 95% CI 1.45–14.54, P = .01).
- Of 18 deaths: **9 within 3 months** — 8 of those 9 had received pre-LT corticosteroids (P = .04 vs no steroids) and 5 died of **sepsis**. The other 9 deaths occurred >1 year out (1.1–6.0 y); **7 were alcohol-related** (6 graft failure from recurrent ALD, 1 alcohol overdose).
- Among 141 discharged home, 3-year survival **97%** (no alcohol use) vs **75%** (any post-LT alcohol use), P = .03.

**Post-LT alcohol use (141 discharged home)**
- **101 (72%) no use; 25 (18%) slips only; 15 (11%) sustained use.**
- Cumulative **any** use: **25% at 1 y** (95% CI 18–34), **34% at 3 y** (95% CI 25–44). Median time to first use **160 days** (IQR 79–346).
- Cumulative **sustained** use: **10% at 1 y** (95% CI 6–18), **17% at 3 y** (95% CI 10–27).
- Of the 40 with any use, **33 (82%)** had binge or frequent drinking — i.e. when use recurred it was usually not trivial.
- Definitions used: **sustained** = still drinking at last follow-up, minimum duration **100 days**; **slip** = use with recovered sobriety; **binge** = >6 U/d (men) or 4 U/d (women); **frequent** = ≥4 d/wk regardless of quantity.
- First detected by: clinical interview **25 (62%)**, urine ethyl glucuronide or phosphatidylethanol **11 (28%)**, liver biopsy **4 (10%)**.
- Only predictor of **any** post-LT use on multivariable analysis: **younger age** (OR 0.95 per year, 95% CI 0.92–0.99, P = .01). Only predictor of **sustained** use: **>10 drinks/day at presentation** (OR 6.5, 95% CI 1.4–30.4, P = .02).
- No center had a dedicated addiction unit in the transplant program. Offering post-LT pharmacotherapy (32% vs 27%, P = .68) or in-house psychosocial intervention (45% vs 25%, P = .07) did not reduce post-LT alcohol use in this cohort.

**Explant histology (146 with records)**
- **96% cirrhosis**; **59% steatohepatitis** — 41% had cirrhosis alone without histologic steatohepatitis, i.e. did not meet the histologic definition of AH.
- Median pre-LT abstinence was shorter in those with histologic steatohepatitis (**48 vs 72 days**, P = .01), suggesting histology fades with time off alcohol rather than the clinical diagnosis being wrong.
- Histologic steatohepatitis was **not** associated with death (HR 1.50, 95% CI 0.56–4.00, P = .42); 1-/3-year survival 92%/86% with vs 97%/83% without (P = .69).

**Sensitivity analysis excluding the 8 pre-2011 transplants (n = 139)**
- Any alcohol use 26% at 1 y / 34% at 3 y; survival 95% at 1 y / 85% at 3 y. Younger age remained the only multivariable predictor of use (OR 0.96, P = .02).

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **Tier.** This is primary research (retrospective cohort), tier 2 — it does **not** override any guideline claim (see Content Guide → Source priority). Where [[aasld-2020-ald]], [[acg-2024-ald]], or the AASLD/AST 2025 transplant documents state a position on early LT, those govern the page; this study supplies the underlying outcome data.
- **Pre-LT corticosteroids and early death.** Steroid-treated patients accounted for 8 of the 9 deaths within 3 months, mostly from sepsis. The authors call the association non-causal but suggest prompt steroid discontinuation in Lille non-responders. This is a hypothesis from a retrospective subgroup, not a graded recommendation.
- **Not all patients had histologic AH** (41% cirrhosis alone). Some may have had acute-on-chronic liver failure from alcohol-associated cirrhosis rather than true AH — but with median MELD-Na 39 they were equally unlikely to survive 6 months untransplanted.
- **Alcohol use is probably underestimated.** No standardized questionnaire, non-uniform biomarker testing (7 of 12 centers routine, 3 selective, 2 interview only), and short median follow-up (1.6 y) all bias the 3-year estimates downward.
- **Selection, not treatment effect.** 35.9% listing rate with a 13%–100% between-center range, and a denominator limited to patients formally presented to committee. The survival figures cannot be transported to unselected severe AH.
- No prospective trial with protocolled selection and post-LT addiction management existed at publication; the authors call for one.
