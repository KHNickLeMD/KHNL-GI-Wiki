## Summary

- **What it is:** first international surgeon–gastroenterologist consensus on [[high-resolution-manometry|high-resolution manometry (HRM)]] before and after [[antireflux-surgery|antireflux surgery (ARS)]], plus a post-ARS HRM interpretation algorithm — the **Padova Classification**.
- **Panel:** 29 members (14 foregut surgeons, 15 gastroenterologists) from 22 centers in North America and Europe; 3 subgroups (candidacy for ARS, HRM before ARS, HRM after ARS); 4 non-voting literature reviewers.
- **Process:** July 2020–June 2023; 2 virtual meetings (Oct 27, 2020; Sep 15, 2022); online rankings between meetings.
- **Voting:** each statement ranked 1–9 (1–3 inappropriate, 4–6 uncertain, 7–9 appropriate); **accepted when median ≥7 with ≥80% ranking it appropriate**. Statements are **unnumbered**; each is reported with median, range, and % ranked appropriate.
- **Bottom line:** HRM before ARS is to exclude motor disorders not amenable to ARS (achalasia spectrum, esophagogastric junction (EGJ) outflow obstruction [EGJOO]); hypercontractile esophagus and distal esophageal spasm without obstructive symptoms do not by themselves bar ARS when gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) is objectively documented. After ARS, no manometric finding alone mandates reoperation; HRM classifies wrap anatomy (slipped, intrathoracic, disrupted with herniation) and physiology (post-fundoplication outflow obstruction [PFOO], absent contractility).

## Key Findings / Claims

### Recommendation Statements — HRM Before ARS (Table 1)

*Unnumbered in the source; listed in Table 1 order. Median and range are on the 1–9 appropriateness scale.*

| Statement | Median | Range | Ranked appropriate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evaluating esophageal motility with HRM is essential to exclude motor disorders not amenable to ARS | 9 | 5–9 | 89% |
| Lower esophageal sphincter (LES) obstruction, as defined by the updated Chicago Classification v4.0 (CCv4.0) EGJOO criteria, must be addressed before undertaking ARS | 8 | 4–9 | 86% |
| With objective GERD, patients with hypercontractile esophagus (CCv4.0 criteria) who have typical reflux symptoms and at least partial symptom improvement on acid-suppression medication can be referred for ARS | 7 | 5–9 | 81% |
| ARS should be considered with caution in distal esophageal spasm on manometry when obstructive symptoms (dysphagia and/or chest pain) are present | 8 | 2–9 | 95% |
| With objective GERD and no obstructive symptoms, patients with distal esophageal spasm can be referred for ARS | 7 | 5–9 | 81% |
| EGJ barrier function should be assessed by HRM using LES end-expiratory pressure, LES baseline pressure or EGJ contractile integral (EGJ-CI), including the presence of a hiatal hernia (LES–crural diaphragm [CD] separation) | 8 | 3–9 | 85% |

### Recommendation Statements — HRM After ARS (Table 2)

| Statement | Median | Range | Ranked appropriate |
|---|---|---|---|
| HRM has a diagnostic role in unsuccessful fundoplication (persistent or new-onset reflux symptoms and/or dysphagia), unless a large recurrent hernia already necessitates reoperation | 8 | 1–9 | 85% |
| No manometric finding is an absolute indication for reoperative fundoplication, but HRM aids decision-making when used alongside endoscopy, pH monitoring, and barium studies | 9 | 1–9 | 96% |
| After a successful fundoplication HRM shows a single distal high-pressure zone (HPZ) — reflecting normal LES–CD configuration — with appropriate deglutitive relaxation | 8 | 2–9 | 89% |
| Nissen (360°) fundoplication tends to give higher EGJ resting pressure, higher integrated relaxation pressure (IRP), and higher esophageal contractility than partial fundoplication | 8 | 2–9 | 81% |
| High intrabolus pressure (IBP) combined with high IRP may indicate PFOO | 8 | 5–9 | 93% |
| A very prominent single HPZ with impaired or failed deglutitive relaxation can indicate a twisted or too-tight fundoplication | 8 | 6–9 | 86% |
| Slipped fundoplication: dual HPZ — upper HPZ relaxes normally on swallowing; distal HPZ (crus plus slipped wrap) is generally a uniform pressure band | 7 | 1–9 | 80% |
| Intrathoracic fundoplication: dual HPZ — lower HPZ (crus) shows the expected respiratory pattern (as in surgery-naive hiatal hernia); upper HPZ (herniated wrap) may show deglutitive relaxation | 7 | 4–9 | 81% |
| New-onset dysphagia with normal endoscopy: timed barium swallow (with tablet) and functional lumen imaging probe (FLIP) may help clarify the cause, particularly if manometry is equivocal | 8 | 6–9 | 93% |

### Supporting Data and Commentary

- **Why HRM before ARS:**
  - >1000 patients having HRM before ARS → **3%** had EGJ obstruction suspicious for [[achalasia|achalasia-spectrum disorders]], where standard ARS would have significantly worsened transit.
  - 524 achalasia patients → **29%** had been referred for ARS for incomplete response to GERD management.
  - IRP can be within normal range in achalasia (infrequently) → absent contractility with dysphagia needs barium radiography or FLIP to exclude achalasia before ARS.
- **EGJOO before ARS:** heterogeneous pattern (LES obstruction, mechanical causes such as paraesophageal hernia, artifact); CCv4.0 requires confirmatory testing (timed barium esophagram or FLIP) plus obstructive symptoms (dysphagia, chest pain) → evaluate with confirmatory testing; address only if obstruction is definitively identified.
- **Distal esophageal spasm (DES):** may be a reactive spastic pattern in GERD; with dysphagia or noncardiac chest pain may lie on the spectrum of type III (spastic) achalasia; also described with chronic opioid use → treatment other than ARS may need to be considered.
- **EGJ barrier:** intact LES tone + superimposed CD; LES–CD separation on HRM correlates well with hiatal hernia at laparoscopy.
- **Expected post-ARS physiology:**
  - Asymptomatic patients studied 3 mo after partial or complete fundoplication invariably had a single HPZ.
  - IRP typically higher after ARS than surgery-naive, especially after 360° wrap; barium passage usually normal.
  - **IRP after ARS:** minimal data in asymptomatic patients; varies with wrap type and manometry system. **Any IRP <15 mmHg on Medtronic systems is consistent with normal, but a higher value may still be within normal limits** — no post-ARS upper limit is set.
  - IRP alone may be insufficient to judge outflow obstruction → combine with IBP.
  - One cohort found no significant IRP difference between complete and partial wraps.
- **Post-ARS failure burden:** reflux symptoms and/or pathologic acid reflux recur in ~5–15% short and long term; persistent dysphagia in up to 12–25%.
- **Barium and FLIP after ARS:** neither tested specifically in failed fundoplication; suggested as adjuncts by analogy to their role in confirming EGJOO under CCv4.0. **Post-surgery normative FLIP values for distensibility index and EGJ diameter have not been established.**

### Padova Classification (Figure 1)

**Step 1 — anatomy: separation between LES (neo-LES) and CD**

| Finding | Neo-LES basal pressure | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| No separation | — | Expected anatomy after fundoplication → go to Step 2 |
| HPZ ≥1 cm **below** CD | — | **Slipped fundoplication** |
| HPZ ≥1 cm **above** CD (whole neo-LES–fundoplication complex migrated into chest) | Low (hypotensive) | **Disrupted fundoplication with herniation** |
| HPZ ≥1 cm **above** CD | Normal | **Intrathoracic (herniated) wrap**, not disrupted |

- Separation threshold: **≥1 cm** in the classification text; **>1 cm** in Figure 1 and the discussion.
- Table follows the text (classification section and discussion agree). **Figure 1 labels the two separation branches the other way round** — "HPZ below CD" leads to the basal-pressure split, "HPZ above CD" to slipped.
- Example tracings: slipped wrap (Figure 5A) — native LES (proximal HPZ) sits above the wrap (distal HPZ, at gastric-body level); distal HPZ neither relaxes nor varies with respiration; proximal HPZ relaxes; pressure inversion point (PIP) above distal HPZ. Intrathoracic wrap (Figure 5B) — distal HPZ = crus (no swallow relaxation, respiratory variation evident); proximal HPZ = wrap around native LES, relaxes; PIP at distal HPZ.

**Step 2 — physiology (no separation)**

| Sequence | Result | Interpretation / next step |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Neo-LES basal pressure | Low | **Disrupted or ineffective procedure** → ambulatory reflux monitoring if reflux-type symptoms |
| | High | **PFOO** (tight wrap or tight crural repair) |
| 2. Normal basal pressure → IRP | Elevated (median IRP) | **PFOO** (defective relaxation) |
| 3. Normal IRP → peristalsis | 100% failed swallows, present on pre-ARS HRM | **Absent contractility**, pre-existing |
| | 100% failed swallows, new | **Pseudoachalasia or missed achalasia** (particularly if IRP at upper limit of normal) |
| | Some contractility present | **Expected findings after fundoplication** → reflux monitoring if clinically indicated for recurrent GERD |

- Intrabolus pressurization increases confidence in PFOO.
- Initial evaluation of symptoms after ARS = endoscopy + barium esophagram; HRM interpreted alongside them.
- The source gives **no numeric cutoffs** for low/normal/high neo-LES basal pressure or for elevated IBP after ARS.

### Statements Not Reaching Agreement (Supplemental Table 3)

- EGJ obstruction shown only on provocative maneuvers prompting further pre-ARS evaluation.
- Contraction vigor (ineffective swallows, peristaltic breaks, multiple rapid swallow [MRS] response) to tailor total vs partial fundoplication or predict post-ARS dysphagia.
- Postprandial HRM as an adjunct in proton pump inhibitor (PPI) non-responders; impedance metrics with HRM.
- Context: absent contraction reserve on MRS is associated (retrospective data) with more dysphagia after standard ARS, higher reflux burden, and persistent ineffective motility; straight-leg-raise pressurization associated with abnormal reflux burden, but its pre-ARS value is unproven.

### Candidacy for ARS (narrative section, no voted statements)

- **Indication:** typical GERD symptoms (heartburn and/or regurgitation) + objective pathologic reflux on endoscopy and/or ambulatory reflux monitoring.
- **Best candidates:** PPI responders; objective reflux on endoscopy (Los Angeles grade B or higher; long-segment Barrett esophagus); and/or hiatal hernia; symptom–reflux correlation on monitoring. Chest pain → negative cardiac evaluation first.
- **Also offered to:** PPI non-responders and/or extraesophageal symptoms **with abnormal reflux burden** (abnormal acid exposure and/or positive symptom–reflux association for regurgitation-predominant symptoms) and a clinically significant hiatal hernia.
- **Counsel on:** persistent symptoms, dysphagia, inability to belch, flatulence, bowel symptoms.
- **Preoperative workup:** endoscopy, barium esophagogram, esophageal manometry, reflux monitoring **off PPI** (unless endoscopy shows objective reflux). Gastric emptying and FLIP may be used selectively; no evidence links them to ARS outcome.
- **Hiatal hernia:**
  - Types 2–4 (large paraesophageal, combined, or massive with other organs) with gastric volvulus → absolute indication for emergency surgery.
  - Truly asymptomatic isolated type 2 → low risk of life-threatening events; weigh elective repair (age and American Society of Anesthesiologists [ASA] score drive morbidity); high-risk minimally symptomatic → watchful waiting may be considered.
  - Small axial (type 1) without reflux symptoms → surgery not routinely indicated.
- **Obesity:** ARS safe in body mass index (BMI) 25–30 and >30 kg/m² with comparable short-term outcomes, but long-term reflux control worse; **BMI >35 kg/m² → evaluate carefully; Roux-en-Y gastric bypass currently recommended as procedure of choice for BMI >35 kg/m² with GERD**.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Figure vs text:** Figure 1 assigns "slipped" to the HPZ-above-CD branch and the basal-pressure split to HPZ-below-CD; the classification text and discussion state the reverse. Separation written ≥1 cm (text) vs >1 cm (figure, discussion).
- **Post-ARS IRP:** <15 mmHg (Medtronic) is consistent with normal, but higher values may also be normal — the source sets no post-ARS upper limit, and Chicago Classification v4.0 thresholds were built for surgery-naive patients ([[chicago-v4-2021-esophageal-dysmotility]]).
- **No numeric post-ARS cutoffs** for neo-LES basal pressure (low/normal/high) or IBP; no post-ARS FLIP normative values.
- **EGJOO before ARS** — consistent with [[chicago-v4-2021-esophageal-dysmotility]] (EGJOO conclusive only with obstructive symptoms + supportive barium or FLIP).
- **Contraction vigor and wrap tailoring:** no agreement that ineffective swallows, peristaltic breaks, or MRS response should choose total vs partial fundoplication.
- **Limitations (authors):** literature-based agreement inherits study flaws; mostly observational, small, single-center data; no outcomes data confirming the recommendations.
