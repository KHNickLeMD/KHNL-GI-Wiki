## Summary

The Padova Consensus is the first systematic international expert consensus establishing the role of high-resolution manometry (HRM) before and after antireflux surgery (ARS). Using RAND/UCLA appropriateness methodology, a 29-member international panel of surgeons and gastroenterologists developed consensus statements covering pre-ARS screening, post-ARS diagnostic interpretation, and a novel post-ARS HRM classification algorithm — the Padova Classification.

Pre-operatively, the consensus establishes HRM as essential to exclude significant motor disorders that would contraindicate or complicate ARS, particularly achalasia spectrum disorders (3% incidence in pre-ARS cohorts who present with GERD-like symptoms). EGJOO identified pre-ARS must be addressed before surgery proceeds. The EGJ barrier is assessed using multiple manometric parameters: LES end-expiratory pressure, LES baseline pressure, EGJ-contractility index (EGJ-CI), and LES–crural diaphragm (CD) separation. Hypercontractile esophagus (jackhammer pattern) is not an absolute contraindication if the patient has objective GERD evidence and partial PPI response. DES with obstructive symptoms warrants caution; DES without obstructive symptoms with objective GERD may proceed to ARS.

Post-operatively, the consensus clarifies that HRM interpretation after fundoplication requires context-specific norms. IRP values are expected to be higher than in unoperated patients; an IRP <15 mmHg (Medtronic catheter) is the proposed post-ARS upper limit of normal. A single distal high-pressure zone (HPZ) with appropriate relaxation (IRP within range) represents successful fundoplication anatomy. The Padova Classification provides a two-step algorithm — first assessing neo-LES/crural diaphragm anatomy (separations suggesting mechanical complications), then assessing physiology (basal pressure, IRP, peristalsis) in patients without anatomical separation — to identify seven post-ARS HRM patterns with distinct clinical implications.

**The Padova Classification — Post-ARS HRM Algorithm:**

*Step 1: Anatomy — Is there neo-LES/CD separation >1 cm?*

| Finding | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| HPZ below CD | Slipped fundoplication |
| HPZ above CD + low basal pressure | Disrupted / herniated wrap |
| HPZ above CD + normal/high basal pressure | Intrathoracic wrap |

*Step 2: Physiology (if no anatomical separation — single HPZ in expected position)*

| Physiology | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| Low basal pressure | Disrupted/ineffective wrap → proceed to reflux monitoring |
| High basal pressure | PFOO (tight/twisted wrap) |
| Normal basal + elevated IRP | PFOO (obstructive relaxation failure) |
| Normal basal + normal IRP + 100% failed peristalsis | Absent contractility → compare to pre-op (pre-existing vs. pseudoachalasia/missed achalasia) |
| Normal basal + normal IRP + normal/intact peristalsis | Expected post-fundoplication pattern → consider reflux monitoring if symptoms persist |

**Key IRP Reference Values:**

- Post-ARS IRP <15 mmHg (Medtronic system) = upper limit of normal
- Post-ARS IRP context: fundoplication creates intrinsic resistance; values above surgery-naive normal (typically <15 mmHg Chicago Classification) are expected after Nissen

**PFOO (Post-Fundoplication Outflow Obstruction):**

- Defined by simultaneous presence of: elevated intrabolus pressure (IBP) + elevated IRP
- Either alone insufficient for PFOO diagnosis
- May represent tight wrap, twisted wrap, or stenotic wrap

**Pseudoachalasia / Missed Achalasia:**

- If post-ARS HRM shows 100% failed peristalsis: compare to pre-operative HRM
- Pre-operative failed peristalsis = pre-existing condition; not surgery-related
- If pre-operative HRM was normal: suspect pseudoachalasia (progressive motor failure secondary to obstruction) or missed achalasia (achalasia diagnosis missed pre-ARS)

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Post-ARS IRP threshold (<15 mmHg, Medtronic) requires catheter-system-specific application; Laborie/Diversatek systems may have different reference ranges — not addressed in this consensus
- No consensus on when post-ARS HRM should be performed (timing after surgery not specified); early post-operative edema may confound manometric interpretation
- PFOO management (e.g., dilation, revision surgery) is not addressed in this consensus; a complementary consensus on post-ARS treatment is needed
- Pseudoachalasia workup after ARS: the consensus identifies the entity but does not specify diagnostic criteria to distinguish pseudoachalasia from true (missed) achalasia; EndoFLIP and timed barium esophagram correlation recommended but not formalized
- The Padova Classification has not been prospectively validated; it is based on expert consensus and retrospective case series
- Partial vs. Nissen fundoplication differences in post-ARS HRM norms: the consensus acknowledges higher pressures with Nissen but does not provide separate reference thresholds
