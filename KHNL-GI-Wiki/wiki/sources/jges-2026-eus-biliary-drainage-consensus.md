## Summary

An international Delphi consensus on **endoscopic ultrasound-guided biliary drainage (EUS-BD)**, convened at the 1st Japan Gastroenterological Endoscopy Society (JGES) International in Tokyo, September 2025. **Fourteen expert endoscopists from six countries** voted; statements needed **≥80% agreement** (strongly agree + partially agree) across at least two iterative rounds. Three topics: EUS-BD as a **primary** drainage option in unresectable **malignant distal biliary obstruction (MDBO)**, EUS-BD for biliary disease in **surgically altered anatomy (SAA)**, and a proposed **standardized terminology** for interventional EUS.

The headline is deliberately conservative. Despite five randomized controlled trials in which EUS-BD matched or beat endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography-guided biliary drainage (ERCP-BD) on technical success, with clinical success above 90% in both arms, shorter procedure times, and **post-ERCP pancreatitis virtually absent**, the panel declined to make EUS-BD a first-line standard. It stays an **established salvage option after failed ERCP**, and an **upfront option only in selected patients** — concomitant duodenal obstruction, high risk of post-ERCP pancreatitis, or failed ERCP-BD. The stated reasons are that trial endoscopists were highly experienced in both procedures, that EUS-BD carries a risk of bile peritonitis that can be fatal, and that severe and even lethal adverse events have been reported when inexperienced endoscopists perform it.

The **choledochoduodenostomy (CDS) versus hepaticogastrostomy (HGS)** choice is explicitly declared country- and operator-dependent rather than evidence-settled: a meta-analysis of nine studies (537 patients) found no difference in technical (OR 0.83) or clinical (OR 0.96) success. In the United States, CDS using lumen-apposing metal stents (LAMS) predominates and few endoscopists are familiar with HGS; in Korea, Taiwan and Japan, HGS is the main approach, supported by dedicated 0.018-inch guidewires. The anatomical fork is the usable rule: **CDS requires duodenal access, HGS does not**.

In SAA, EUS-BD showed **consistently higher technical success and shorter procedure time** than balloon enteroscopy-assisted ERCP (BE-ERCP) with comparable clinical outcomes and adverse events, and the panel allows it as a primary option where expertise exists — but this was the **least agreed statement in the document (strongly agree 61.5%, with one panelist strongly disagreeing)**, and BE-ERCP remains first line in many Japanese institutions.

The terminology section argues that interventional EUS creates a **non-physiological route connecting two organs directly** and should therefore be called an **anastomosis, not a drainage**; proposes **ESCR (endosonographically created route)** and **trans-ESCR procedures** for anything done through such a route; and sets a naming rule — **organs named in the direction of fluid flow**, as in surgical nomenclature.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Consensus statements (the document's own topic/question numbering; agreement percentages as printed)

**Topic 1 — EUS-BD as a primary drainage option in unresectable MDBO**

| # | Statement | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | **EUS-BD is not considered a standard biliary drainage method for unresectable MDBO at present.** It is proposed for use in **selected situations**: concomitant **duodenal obstruction**, patients at **high risk for post-ERCP pancreatitis**, or those in whom **ERCP-BD failed** | Strongly agree 85.7%, partially agree 14.3% |
| 1.2 | The **preference between CDS and HGS** for unresectable MDBO **varies among countries**, depending on device availability, clinical experience, the degree of EUS-BD dissemination, and anatomical constraints | Strongly agree 85.7%, partially agree 14.3% |
| 1.3 | Dedicated **devices and delivery systems**, plus **standardized techniques**, are required before EUS-BD can be a primary drainage option. **Training of expert endoscopists skilled in both ERCP and interventional EUS is essential** for adoption | **Strongly agree 100%** |

**Topic 2 — biliary disease in surgically altered anatomy**

| # | Statement | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | *Malignant* bile duct obstruction in SAA: **when safety is ensured at institutions with experienced experts, EUS-BD can be considered a primary drainage option**, taking into account patient characteristics, anatomical factors and institutional experience | Strongly agree 61.5%, partially agree 30.8%, **strongly disagree 7.7%** |
| 2.2 | **EUS-HGS using a partially covered metallic stent (PCMS) is the commonly performed procedure in SAA.** EUS-HGS with a **dedicated plastic stent** is a useful option, **available only in a limited number of countries** | Strongly agree 64.3%, partially agree 28.6% |
| 2.3 | *Benign* biliary disease in SAA: the **optimal approach is selected on the expertise of each institution, available devices, intestinal reconstruction method, and the treatment strategy for the benign disease** | Strongly agree 78.6%, partially agree 21.4% |

**Topic 3 — proposed classification and terminology for interventional EUS** *(discussion, not voted statements)*

- **Anastomosis, not drainage.** In ERCP the stent crosses a stricture along a physiological route; in interventional EUS the stent is placed **through the gastrointestinal wall to create a new route connecting two organs**. EUS-BD should therefore be termed an **anastomosis**. EUS-guided drainage of an **acquired fluid collection** (pancreatic fluid collection, abscess) makes the collection disappear and so remains a **drainage**.
- **Naming order follows fluid flow**, as in surgical nomenclature: in EUS-CDS bile flows from the common bile duct (*choledocho-*) into the duodenum (*-duodeno-*) through the anastomosis (*-stomy*).
- **ESCR = endosonographically created route**; anything performed through one is a **trans-ESCR procedure** (direct endoscopic necrosectomy after EUS-guided drainage of a pancreatic fluid collection; antegrade stone extraction after EUS-HGS).
- Under this principle, **EDGE** is more precisely described as **ERCP following EUS-guided gastrogastrostomy with LAMS**.
- Panel caveats recorded: some attendees found "ESCR" confusing; the terminology was developed primarily by Japanese experts and may lack intuitive clarity; **ETBD (endoscopic transhepatic biliary drainage)** was floated as a more intuitive parallel to percutaneous transhepatic biliary drainage (PTBD). The panel notes ERCP itself took extensive debate in the 1970s to become established.

### Numbers worth carrying

- **EUS-BD vs ERCP-BD as primary drainage for MDBO, five RCTs:** technical success **90.9%–100% vs 76.3%–94.1%**; clinical success **84.9%–97.0% vs 85.9%–100%**, uniformly high in both; **post-ERCP pancreatitis 0% in every EUS-BD arm vs 0%–14.8%**; procedure time consistently shorter for EUS-BD (e.g. 5 vs 11 min, 10 vs 25 min).
- **CDS vs HGS meta-analysis**, nine non-randomized studies + two RCTs, 537 patients (225 HGS, 312 CDS): **no significant difference in technical (OR 0.83; 95% CI 0.41–1.68; I² = 0%) or clinical (OR 0.96; 95% CI 0.51–1.81; I² = 9.94%) success**.
- **EUS-BD vs BE-ERCP in SAA:** EUS-BD gave **consistently higher technical success** (e.g. 98.0% vs 65.3%, 95% vs 74%, 100% vs 68.3%) with comparable clinical outcomes and adverse events, and **significantly shorter procedure time**. For bile duct stones, EUS-antegrade was more procedurally efficient while BE-ERCP achieved a higher rate of complete stone clearance.
- **CDS with LAMS depends on duct calibre** — stent placement as defined in the Axios trial inclusion criteria is **generally technically challenging when the bile duct diameter is under 15 mm**.
- **LAMS-specific hazard in CDS:** compared with tubular stents, LAMS carry a risk of **inadequate drainage after biliary decompression**, because the contralateral flange may contact the bile duct wall when the duct collapses. A **coaxial double-pigtail plastic stent** may help prevent adverse events including occlusion from food reflux.
- **HGS stent choice:** PCMS is used worldwide, with 1–2 cm at the hepatic end left uncovered so the uncovered part prevents branch-duct obstruction — but **early stent dislocation at the liver side has been reported, so its protective effect should be interpreted with caution**. PCMS with an anti-migration function is now produced. FCMS allows easier removability, favoured when re-intervention is anticipated, at the cost of migration and of cholangitis or hepatic abscess from side-branch obstruction.
- **Benign disease demands removability** — in benign cases needing antegrade treatment and long-term strategies, **removability is mandatory** for stents placed via the EUS-HGS route including hepaticojejunostomy in patients with gastrectomy; plastic stents or FCMS are preferred over PCMS.
- **Antegrade stone treatment timing:** small stones may be treated **sequentially and immediately** after EUS-HGS; large stones needing cholangioscopy-assisted lithotripsy via the HGS route may require **more than 2 weeks after the initial procedure**, for tract maturation.
- **United States barrier:** there is currently **no dedicated billing code for EUS-BD** in the US, so reimbursement is unavailable — named as a major barrier to adoption.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **The panel's conclusion runs against its own evidence table, deliberately.** Table 1's five RCTs favour EUS-BD on technical success, procedure time and post-ERCP pancreatitis, yet statement 1.1 declines to make it standard. The stated reasons are external validity (trial endoscopists were highly experienced in both procedures), the risk of **bile peritonitis, which can be fatal**, and reports of severe and lethal adverse events when inexperienced endoscopists perform EUS-BD. The wiki states the conclusion with that reasoning attached.
- **Statement 2.1 is the weakest in the document** — strongly agree only 61.5%, with one of thirteen voting **strongly disagree**. Recorded as such on the page rather than presented as settled.
- **No single approach is preferred for benign biliary disease in SAA** (statement 2.3) — an explicit non-recommendation, not an omission.
- **Geographic divergence is a finding, not noise.** CDS with LAMS predominates in the US; HGS predominates in Korea, Taiwan and Japan; in India CDS is generally preferred with HGS selected for duodenal obstruction; unpublished French data suggest CDS with LAMS may be associated with food impaction causing stent dysfunction.
- Open: no study has **directly compared BE-ERCP with EUS-BD** for benign anastomotic strictures; the role of EUS-guided gallbladder drainage as a first-line biliary option remains to be established; the **utility of the double-guidewire technique** is unproven and may increase tract manipulation and theoretically the risk of bile leakage; optimal stent choice in EUS-HGS needs prospective study.
- **Terminology is proposed, not adopted** — the document asks for continued international collaboration to refine and harmonize the concepts, and records that acceptance "will likely require time".
