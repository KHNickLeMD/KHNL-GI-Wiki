## Summary

AGA Expert Review giving practical, evidence-based guidance on the **role of endoscopy in recurrent acute pancreatitis (RAP) and chronic pancreatitis (CP)**, framed around **8 Best Practice Advice statements**. RAP is defined as **≥2 distinct episodes of acute pancreatitis with complete interceding resolution, separated by ≥3 months**; ~10–30% of patients develop RAP after an index AP episode, and ~35% of RAP patients progress to CP.

For **evaluation of unexplained AP/RAP** (etiology unrevealed in 16–27% of cases despite history, medication review, labs, and noninvasive imaging), **EUS is the preferred initial diagnostic test** (yields a potential etiology in 29–88%; most common finding is occult biliary lithiasis; occult ampullary/pancreatobiliary malignancy found in up to 5% after single unexplained AP and up to 12% with RAP). MRI/MRCP with contrast is a reasonable complementary/alternative test — EUS is more likely than MRI to give a probable cause (OR 3.79, driven by sensitivity for occult biliary stones), while MRCP better identifies ductal etiologies (pancreas divisum, anomalous pancreaticobiliary union). Optimal EUS timing is a short delay (2–6 weeks) after AP resolution.

The **role of ERCP to reduce AP frequency is controversial** in both pancreas divisum (PD) and standard-anatomy unexplained RAP. In PD, minor papilla endotherapy may be considered mainly with objective outflow obstruction (dilated dorsal duct and/or santorinicele); there is no role for ERCP for pain alone. In standard anatomy, ERCP is even more controversial and should follow shared decision-making about uncertain benefit and severe adverse-event risk; biliary sphincterotomy alone may be preferable to dual sphincterotomy (Coté RCT).

For **CP**, surgery is favored over endoscopy for long-term treatment of painful obstructive CP (ESCAPE and 2 prior RCTs), with endoscopy a reasonable alternative for poor operative candidates. Endoscopic techniques covered: MPD stones (conventional extraction for ≤5 mm; ESWL and/or pancreatoscopy-guided intraductal lithotripsy for larger), MPD strictures (prolonged 6–12 month plastic-stent upsizing; FCSEMS emerging), CP-related biliary strictures (FCSEMS favored over multiple plastic stents, with a cholecystitis caveat when gallbladder in situ), and EUS-guided celiac plexus block (not routine; case-by-case for debilitating refractory pain).

## Key Findings / Claims

Verbatim/near-verbatim Best Practice Advice statements:

- **Best Practice Advice 1:** After an unrevealing initial evaluation, EUS is the preferred diagnostic test for unexplained acute and recurrent pancreatitis. MRI/MRCP is a reasonable complementary or alternative test to EUS, based on local expertise and availability.

- **Best Practice Advice 2:** The role of ERCP for reducing the frequency of AP episodes in patients with PD is controversial, but minor papilla endotherapy may be considered, particularly for those with objective signs of outflow obstruction, such as a dilated dorsal pancreatic duct and/or santorinicele. There is no role for ERCP to treat pain alone in patients with PD.

- **Best Practice Advice 3:** The role of ERCP for reducing the frequency of pancreatitis episodes in patients with unexplained RAP and standard pancreatic ductal anatomy is controversial and should only be considered after a comprehensive discussion of the uncertain benefits and potentially severe procedure-related adverse events. When pursued, ERCP with biliary sphincterotomy alone may be preferable to dual sphincterotomy.

- **Best Practice Advice 4:** Surgical intervention should be considered over endoscopic therapy for long-term treatment of patients with painful obstructive CP. Endoscopic intervention is a reasonable alternative to surgery for suboptimal operative candidates or those who favor a less invasive approach, assuming they are clearly informed that the best practice advice primarily favors surgery.

- **Best Practice Advice 5:** When ERCP is pursued, small (≤5 mm) MPD stones can be treated with pancreatography and conventional stone extraction maneuvers. For larger stones, ESWL and/or pancreatoscopy with intraductal lithotripsy may be required.

- **Best Practice Advice 6:** When ERCP is pursued, prolonged stent therapy (6–12 months) is effective for treating symptoms and remodeling MPD strictures. The preferred approach is to place and sequentially add multiple plastic stents in parallel (upsizing); emerging evidence suggests that FCSEMS may have a role for this indication, but additional research is necessary.

- **Best Practice Advice 7:** ERCP with stent insertion is the preferred treatment for benign biliary stricture due to CP. FCSEMS placement is favored over multiple plastic stents whenever feasible, given similar efficacy but significantly reduced need for stent exchange procedures during the treatment course.

- **Best Practice Advice 8:** CPB should not be routinely performed for the management of pain due to CP. The decision to proceed with CPB in selected patients with debilitating pain in whom other therapeutic measures have failed can be considered on a case-by-case basis, but only after discussion of the unclear outcomes of this intervention and its procedural risks.

**Supporting data points:**
- RAP definition: ≥2 distinct AP episodes with complete interceding resolution, separated by ≥3 months. ~10–30% develop RAP after index AP; ~35% of RAP progress to CP.
- Unexplained AP: etiology remains unknown in 16–27% despite standard workup. EUS uncovers etiology in 29–88%; most common = occult biliary lithiasis. Occult ampullary/pancreatobiliary malignancy up to 5% after single unexplained AP, up to 12% with RAP.
- EUS vs MRI: EUS more likely to give probable cause (OR 3.79). EUS timing: 2–6 week delay after AP resolution.
- Pancreas divisum: most common congenital pancreatic anomaly, ~6–10% of individuals; most asymptomatic. Endotherapy reduces/eliminates RAP in ~60–80% in uncontrolled series. Only RCT (n=19): minor papilla stenting reduced future AP (10% vs 67%, P<.05). Risks: post-ERCP pancreatitis 10–15%; post-papillotomy stenosis up to 19% (1 in 5).
- Standard-anatomy RAP: Jacob RCT — PD stenting reduced RAP (11% vs 53%, P<.02). Coté RCT — pancreatic sphincterotomy no incremental benefit over biliary sphincterotomy alone.
- Painful obstructive CP: 3 RCTs favor early surgery over endotherapy. ESCAPE (n=88, dilated MPD): complete/partial pain relief 58% (surgery) vs 39% (endotherapy) at 18 months. Endotherapy typically requires serial ERCPs over up to 12 months; surgery is one-time.
- MPD stones: occur in ~60% of CP (more with alcohol >80 g/d, tobacco >20 cig/d). ESWL fragmentation >90%; complete PD clearance by ERCP achievable in >⅔; >½ pain-free at 2 years; up to 89% report QoL improvement. Pancreatoscopy-directed lithotripsy technical success 88%, adverse events 12%.
- MPD strictures: pain relief up to 85% with stent across stricture. Durable remodeling with plastic stents needs 6–12 months incremental upsizing. FCSEMS vs multiple plastic stents meta-analysis: no difference in pain improvement (88% vs 89%) or stricture recurrence (8% vs 11%); fewer ERCPs with FCSEMS but higher adverse event rate (39% vs 14%).
- CP biliary strictures: occur in ~15% of advanced CP. Plastic-stent therapy clinically successful up to 90% but requires several sessions (every 10–12 weeks). FCSEMS: similar success/safety, fewer procedures; caveat — increased cholecystitis risk if gallbladder in situ and stent covers cystic duct orifice → reasonable to avoid FCSEMS then. Stent therapy 6–12 months depending on stricture severity/type/response.
- EUS-guided celiac plexus block: local anesthetic + corticosteroid. Pain relief in 50–60% with duration ≤6 months; weak evidence. Almost all patients need additional analgesics. Diarrhea and orthostatic hypotension most common AEs; major AEs (abscess, intravascular injection, paralysis) <1%.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **Painful obstructive CP — surgery vs endoscopy:** this CPU (2022) explicitly favors **surgery over endoscopy** for long-term pain in obstructive CP (ESCAPE + 2 RCTs), while several prior guidelines (and the existing CP page's pain ladder) frame endoscopy as the initial intervention. Higher-priority resolution: newer ASGE 2024 CP-endoscopy guideline and this AGA CPU both temper the endoscopy-first stance; the CP page notes surgery superiority for long-term pain — consistent.
- **FCSEMS for MPD strictures:** benefit (fewer ERCPs) offset by higher adverse-event rate (39% vs 14%) — role remains "emerging," not standard. For CP biliary strictures FCSEMS is favored, except gallbladder-in-situ cholecystitis caveat.
- Definitive role of ERCP in unexplained RAP (± PD) awaits ongoing sham-controlled minor-papilla-sphincterotomy RCT.
