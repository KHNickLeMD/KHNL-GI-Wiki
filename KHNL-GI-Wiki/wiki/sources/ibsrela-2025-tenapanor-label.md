## Summary

The FDA prescribing information for **tenapanor**, the first-in-class inhibitor of the gastrointestinal (GI) sodium/hydrogen exchanger isoform 3 (NHE3). It was fetched to close the gap [[tenapanor]] named in its own text: the three guidelines behind that page give the adult dose but state **no contraindications, no age limits and no renal or hepatic adjustment**.

The label answers each. There are exactly **two contraindications** — age **under 6 years**, and known or suspected **mechanical GI obstruction** — and the age limit carries a **boxed warning**, because tenapanor caused deaths presumed due to dehydration in young juvenile rats. The boxed warning is wider than the contraindication: use is to be **avoided from 6 to under 12 years** (no juvenile data in that age band, no clinical data at all), and safety and effectiveness are **unestablished below 18 years**. Unlike [[linzess-2026-linaclotide-label|linaclotide]], which is *indicated* in children down to 2 years for functional constipation, tenapanor has **no pediatric indication at any age**.

**No renal or hepatic dose adjustment is given, and the label contains no renal- or hepatic-impairment subsection at all** — consistent with a drug that is minimally absorbed (plasma concentrations below the limit of quantification). Renal impairment is instead a **tolerability** signal, not a dosing one: among the 31% of trial patients with a baseline estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) <90 mL/min/1.73 m², diarrhea including severe diarrhea occurred in **20%** versus **13%** with normal renal function, and the incidence did **not** track with the severity of the impairment.

The one interaction that changes prescribing is **OATP2B1**: tenapanor inhibits this intestinal uptake transporter, so substrates lose exposure. The label's worked example is **enalapril**, whose peak exposure fell ~70% and total exposure 50–65% when co-administered — with the instruction to monitor blood pressure and increase the enalapril dose if needed. Diarrhea is the dominant adverse reaction (16% vs 4% placebo at 26 weeks; **severe in 2.5%** vs 0.2%), and the management rule is to **suspend dosing and rehydrate**, not to dose-reduce.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Indication and dose**

- Indicated for **irritable bowel syndrome with constipation (IBS-C) in adults** — the only indication on the label.
- **50 mg orally twice daily.** Take **immediately before breakfast or the first meal of the day** and **immediately before dinner**.
- Missed dose: **skip it** and take the next at the regular time; never two doses at once.
- Supplied as 50 mg tablets only.

**Contraindications (both absolute)**

- Patients **less than 6 years of age** — risk of serious dehydration.
- Patients with **known or suspected mechanical GI obstruction**.

**Boxed warning — risk of serious dehydration in pediatric patients**

- Contraindicated **<6 years**: in young juvenile rats tenapanor caused deaths presumed due to dehydration.
- **Avoid** in patients **6 to <12 years** — no data in older juvenile rats, and no clinical safety or efficacy data in children.
- Safety and effectiveness **not established in anyone under 18 years**.

**Warnings and precautions**

- **Diarrhea** — may be severe; **suspend dosing and rehydrate** if it occurs.

**Adverse reactions**

| Reaction (Trial 1, 26 wk) | Tenapanor (n=293) | Placebo (n=300) |
|---|---|---|
| Diarrhea | 16% | 4% |
| Abdominal distension | 3% | <1% |
| Flatulence | 3% | 1% |
| Dizziness | 2% | <1% |

- **Severe diarrhea: 2.5%** versus 0.2% placebo across both trials.
- **Discontinuation** for any adverse reaction **7.6%** versus 0.8%; discontinuation for diarrhea **6.5%** versus 0.7%.
- **Renal impairment subgroup** (eGFR <90 mL/min/1.73 m², 368 patients = 31% of the trial population): diarrhea including severe diarrhea **20%** (39/194) versus 0.6% placebo, compared with **13%** (53/407) versus 3.5% in those with normal renal function. The incidence did **not** correspond to the severity of renal impairment.
- Less common (<2%, above placebo): rectal bleeding, abnormal bowel sounds.
- **Hyperkalemia** — 3 serious hospitalized cases (2 tenapanor, 1 placebo) in a trial of a *different* population (chronic kidney disease with eGFR 25–70 mL/min/1.73 m² plus type 2 diabetes).
- Post-marketing: hypersensitivity reactions (pruritus, rash, urticaria).

**Drug interactions**

- Tenapanor **inhibits intestinal OATP2B1**. Substrates may have **reduced exposure** — monitor for loss of efficacy and adjust the co-administered drug as needed.
- **Enalapril:** with tenapanor 30 mg twice daily (0.6× the recommended dose), enalapril/enalaprilat peak concentration fell **~70%** and total exposure **50–65%**. **Monitor blood pressure and increase the enalapril dose if needed.**

**Specific populations**

- **Pregnancy:** minimally absorbed (plasma <0.5 ng/mL), so maternal use is not expected to result in fetal exposure; available human data have not identified a drug-associated risk. No adverse fetal effects in rabbits up to 8.8× the maximum human dose.
- **Lactation:** tenapanor and its M1 metabolite were **not detected in breast milk** (<1 ng/mL) in a 7-woman study; maternal use is not expected to expose the infant.
- **Pediatric:** see boxed warning — contraindicated <6 y, avoid 6 to <12 y, unestablished <18 y.
- **Geriatric:** 100 of 1203 trial patients (8%) were ≥65 y; **no overall difference** in safety or effectiveness, but greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out.
- **Renal and hepatic impairment: the label gives no dose adjustment and carries no such subsection.**

**Mechanism and overdose**

- Locally acting NHE3 inhibitor on the apical surface of small intestine and colon; blocking sodium absorption increases water secretion into the lumen, accelerating transit and softening stool. The major metabolite **M1 is not active** against NHE3.
- Overdose: expect exaggerated pharmacology — diarrhea, with dehydration risk if severe or prolonged.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **No contradiction with the ingested guidelines.** [[aga-2022-ibs-c]] recommends 50 mg twice daily for IBS-C, which is exactly the labelled dose; [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]] positions tenapanor off-label in refractory constipation and gives no dose, and this label gives no dose for that indication either, so the off-label gap stays open by design.
- **The boxed warning is wider than the contraindication**, and the two are easy to conflate: 6 to <12 years is "avoid", not "contraindicated". The label states both and the wiki keeps them distinct.
- **The hyperkalemia signal is from a different population** — chronic kidney disease plus type 2 diabetes, not IBS-C — and the label reports it without drawing a conclusion for IBS-C patients. Stated as reported; no inference added.
