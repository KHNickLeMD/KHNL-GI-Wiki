## Summary

AGA Institute Clinical Practice Update giving practical guidance on managing pregnant patients with GI and liver disease, organized as **13 Best Practice Advice statements**. Overarching themes: (1) most procedures, medications, and interventions to protect maternal health should **not be withheld solely because a patient is pregnant** — individualize after a risk/benefit assessment; (2) high-risk GI/liver pregnancies (complex IBD, advanced cirrhosis, liver transplant) should be co-managed by a multidisciplinary team (GI, obstetrics/MFM) in a tertiary center; and (3) preconception counseling optimizes outcomes.

Luminal topics: heartburn/GERD (lifestyle, antacids, H2RAs, PPIs are safe), **nausea and vomiting of pregnancy (NVP)** and **hyperemesis gravidarum (HG)** (stepwise antiemetics), and **constipation** (fiber, osmotic laxatives). Endoscopy is safe with pregnancy-specific precautions (defer electives to postpartum; nonemergent → 2nd trimester; left lateral tilt after 20 weeks; sedation considerations). Cirrhotic patients should be screened for varices by upper endoscopy in the 2nd trimester.

Hepatobiliary: **ERCP** for urgent indications (choledocholithiasis, cholangitis, some gallstone pancreatitis), ideally 2nd trimester, with fetal-radiation minimization; **laparoscopic cholecystectomy is safe in any trimester** (ideally 2nd) and superior to conservative management for symptomatic gallstone disease. Liver diseases: **intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP)** dx by serum bile acids >10 μmol/L + pruritus, treat with UDCA 10–15 mg/kg/d; **pre-eclampsia, HELLP, and acute fatty liver of pregnancy (AFLP)** converge on expeditious delivery + liver-transplant evaluation if progression to ALF; **chronic HBV** with DNA >200,000 IU/mL in the 3rd trimester → tenofovir; and continue immunosuppression at lowest effective dose after liver transplant, but **never mycophenolate mofetil**.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Best Practice Advice statements (verbatim, all 13)

**BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 1:** To optimize gastrointestinal and liver disease before pregnancy, preconception and contraceptive care counseling by a multidisciplinary team should be encouraged for reproductive-aged persons who desire to become pregnant.

**BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 2:** Procedures, medications, and other interventions to optimize maternal health should not be withheld solely because a patient is pregnant and should be individualized after an assessment of the risks and benefits.

**BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 3:** Coordination of birth for a pregnant patient with complex inflammatory bowel disease, advanced cirrhosis, or a liver transplant should be managed by a multidisciplinary team, preferably in a tertiary care center.

**BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 4:** Early treatment of nausea and vomiting of pregnancy may reduce progression to hyperemesis gravidarum. In addition to standard diet and lifestyle measures, stepwise treatment consists of symptom control with vitamin B6 and doxylamine, hydration, and adequate nutrition; ondansetron, metoclopramide, promethazine, and intravenous glucocorticoids may be required in moderate to severe cases.

**BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 5:** Constipation in pregnant persons may result from hormonal, medication-related, and physiological changes. Treatment options include dietary fiber, lactulose, and polyethylene glycol–based laxatives.

**BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 6:** Elective endoscopic procedures should be deferred until the postpartum period, whereas nonemergent but necessary procedures should ideally be performed in the second trimester. Pregnant patients with cirrhosis should undergo evaluation for, and treatment of, esophageal varices; upper endoscopy is suggested in the second trimester (if not performed within 1 year before conception) to guide consideration of nonselective β-blocker therapy or endoscopic variceal ligation.

**BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 7:** In patients with inflammatory bowel disease, clinical remission before conception, during pregnancy, and in the postpartum period is essential for improving outcomes of pregnancy. Biologic agents should be continued throughout pregnancy and the postpartum period; use of methotrexate, thalidomide, and ozanimod must be stopped at least 6 months before conception.

**BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 8:** Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography during pregnancy may be performed for urgent indications, such as choledocholithiasis, cholangitis, and some cases of gallstone pancreatitis. Ideally, endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography should be performed during the second trimester, but if deferring the procedure may be detrimental to the health of the patient and fetus, a multidisciplinary team should be convened to decide on the advisability of endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography.

**BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 9:** Cholecystectomy is safe during pregnancy; a laparoscopic approach is the standard of care regardless of trimester, but ideally in the second trimester.

**BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 10:** The diagnosis of intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy is based on a serum bile acid level >10 μmol/L in the setting of pruritus, typically during the second or third trimester. Treatment should be offered with oral ursodeoxycholic acid in a total daily dose of 10–15 mg/kg.

**BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 11:** Management of liver diseases unique to pregnancy, such as pre-eclampsia; hemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, and low platelets syndrome; and acute fatty liver of pregnancy requires planning for delivery and timely evaluation for possible liver transplantation. Daily aspirin prophylaxis for patients at risk for pre-eclampsia or hemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, and low platelets syndrome is advised beginning at week 12 of gestation.

**BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 12:** In patients with chronic hepatitis B virus infection, serum hepatitis B virus DNA and liver biochemical test levels should be ordered. Patients not on treatment but with a serum hepatitis B virus DNA level >200,000 IU/mL during the third trimester of pregnancy should be considered for treatment with tenofovir disoproxil fumarate.

**BEST PRACTICE ADVICE 13:** In patients on immunosuppressive therapy for chronic liver diseases or after liver transplantation, therapy should be continued at the lowest effective dose during pregnancy. Mycophenolate mofetil should not be administered during pregnancy.

*(Unit note: BPA 10's bile-acid cutoff prints as ">10 μmol/L" in the source; serum bile acids are reported in μmol/L.)*

### Supporting details by topic

**Heartburn / GERD:**
- Frequency of heartburn/nausea/vomiting in pregnancy ranges 30%–90%; heartburn from progesterone-mediated lower-esophageal-sphincter relaxation, common late pregnancy, resolves after delivery.
- Antacids and H2-receptor blockers (cimetidine, famotidine) routine; **PPIs (omeprazole, pantoprazole)** considered if lifestyle + antacids fail — meta-analysis of 26 studies found no significant association with abortion, stillbirth, neonatal death, preterm birth, or low birth weight (nonsignificant increase in congenital malformation in 1 study).

**Nausea & vomiting of pregnancy (NVP) / hyperemesis gravidarum (HG):**
- NVP begins 4–6 wk, peaks 8–12 wk, subsides by wk 20; associated with elevated hCG/estrogen and delayed gastric emptying.
- Severity graded by **Motherisk PUQE score** (max 15; ≤6 mild, 7–12 moderate, ≥13 severe).
- Diet/lifestyle first (small, frequent, bland meals; BRAT diet; high-protein/low-fat). Ginger (250 mg capsule 4×/day). **Vitamin B6 (pyridoxine) 10–25 mg every 8 h** (ACOG). **Doxylamine + pyridoxine** combinations (10 mg/10 mg, 20 mg/20 mg) FDA-approved, safe first-line.
- HG = intractable NVP → dehydration, **>5% prepregnancy weight loss**, electrolyte imbalances; affects 0.3%–2%; usually starts before wk 22; resolves in >50% by wk 16, 80% by wk 20; persists throughout pregnancy in ~10%. Elevated liver enzymes in 40%–50% of HG.
- Step-up: metoclopramide; **ondansetron second-line** (some reports of congenital heart defects with 1st-trimester use → use case-by-case before 10 wk); promethazine. **Thiamine (vitamin B1) 100 mg/day ≥7 days** to prevent Wernicke/refeeding, then 50 mg/day. **Methylprednisolone 16 mg IV q8h up to 3 days** then taper (last resort; avoid before 10 wk — cleft palate signal); enteral/parenteral nutrition if persistent.

**Constipation / hemorrhoids:**
- Affects 20%–40% of pregnant persons; hemorrhoids ~80% (3rd trimester, gravid uterus compression).
- Increase dietary fiber to ~30 g/day; adequate fluids; bulk-forming agents (psyllium, methylcellulose) safe; **osmotic laxatives (polyethylene glycol, lactulose) safe**; avoid stimulant laxatives (conflicting safety data). Hydrocortisone foam safe for hemorrhoids in 3rd trimester.

**Endoscopy:**
- Routine screening/surveillance colonoscopy deferred until after delivery; sigmoidoscopy/colonoscopy if strong suspicion of colonic mass or severe diarrhea. Contraindicated in placental abruption, imminent birth, ruptured membranes, hypertensive disease of pregnancy.
- After 20 wk, patient should **not lie supine** (supine hypotension syndrome) — keep in **left pelvic tilt/left lateral** position.
- Sedation: propofol, fentanyl, midazolam not linked to malformations; ASGE prefers **meperidine** for moderate sedation, then small doses of midazolam (limit midazolam in 1st trimester); experienced anesthesiologist for deep sedation. Cautery: grounding pad on leg or right shoulder/arm; bipolar preferred. Most sedatives safe in lactation; breastfeeding can resume once alert (no pump-and-discard).

**ERCP & fetal radiation (Table 2):**
- Pregnancy is an **independent risk factor for post-ERCP pancreatitis** (12% vs 5%, P<.001; higher in nonteaching hospitals 14.6% vs 9.6%). 1st-trimester ERCP → poorer fetal outcomes (term 73.3%, low-birth-weight 21.4%, preterm 20%) → **ASGE recommends deferral to the 2nd trimester when possible**.
- Fetal teratogenic threshold ~**50 mGy**; detrimental outcomes usually >**100 mGy**. Fetal/gonadal shielding **no longer recommended** (AAPM — minimal benefit, may penetrate lead and inadvertently increase exposure).
- Position supine/prone in 1st trimester; **left pelvic tilt/left lateral in 2nd/3rd**. Give thromboprophylaxis + antibiotic prophylaxis when indicated.
- Confirm CBD stone before ERCP with **MRCP (no contrast)** or **EUS** to obviate ERCP.
- **Radiation-minimization measures:** (1) multidisciplinary team (obstetrician/MFM/perinatologist, radiation safety officer, obstetric anesthesiologist, experienced ERCP endoscopist); (2) modern fluoroscopy unit with collimation + pulsed fluoroscopy; (3) last-image-hold + short taps of fluoroscopy; (4) avoid spot films and magnification; (5) image intensifier close to patient, patient away from radiation source; (6) low-radiation-dose protocols + low frame rates; (7) bile-aspiration technique, cholangioscopy, or EUS to reduce cannulation/clearance radiation; (8) if large stones, place a stent and plan lithotripsy/clearance after delivery; (9) avoid pelvic/gonadal shielding.

**Gallstone disease / cholecystectomy:**
- GD is the 2nd leading cause (after appendicitis) of nonobstetric acute abdominal pain in pregnancy. US is imaging of choice; MRCP (no contrast) or EUS for suspected choledocholithiasis.
- Conservative management → recurrent biliary symptoms in **60%**, more ED visits, more cesarean birth. **Laparoscopic cholecystectomy superior to conservative management** for symptomatic cholelithiasis (1st or 2nd trimester). Same-admission cholecystectomy in acute biliary pancreatitis reduces early readmission by **85%**.
- **SAGES: laparoscopy safe in any trimester**; after 1st trimester use left lateral/partial left lateral decubitus. Percutaneous cholecystostomy or gallbladder aspiration as **bridging** therapy if hemodynamically unstable/high surgical risk. Late-3rd-trimester biliary pain may be managed by postponing surgery to postpartum if no maternal/fetal risk.

**Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP):**
- Dx: pruritus (2nd/3rd trimester) + **total serum bile acids >10 μmol/L**. AST/ALT often mildly–moderately elevated (up to 10–20× ULN); total bilirubin <6 mg/dL. If bile acids normal, repeat after excluding biliary obstruction, viral hepatitis, chronic liver disease. Bile acids correlate with **intrauterine fetal demise risk (highest ≥100 μmol/L)**.
- Tx: **UDCA 10–15 mg/kg/d** (divided) — improves pruritus, bile acids, ALT; meta-analysis showed decreased preterm birth and stillbirth. Additional pruritus tx: cholestyramine, rifampicin.
- **Delivery timing by total bile acids** (SMFM): if **≥100 μmol/L → deliver at 36 wk** (or at diagnosis if after 36 wk); if **<100 μmol/L → deliver at 36 or 39 wk** (if diagnosed later). EASL: consider early delivery if bile acids >100 μmol/L (stillbirth risk rises after 35 wk). Individualize; deliver at term for total bile acids **<40 μmol/L**. Pruritus resolves after delivery; if persists >6 wk, evaluate for underlying chronic liver disease.

**Liver diseases unique to pregnancy (Table 3 — ICP vs HELLP vs AFLP):**

| Feature | ICP | HELLP | AFLP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency (%) | 0.3–0.5 | 0.2–0.6 | 0.01 |
| Onset | 2nd/3rd trimester | 3rd trimester/postpartum | 3rd trimester/postpartum |
| Family history | Often | No | Occasionally |
| Pregnancy characteristics | Multiparity, multifetal | Multiparity, aged >35 y | Multifetal, male fetus |
| Symptoms | Pruritus, jaundice | Abdominal pain, vomiting, proteinuria, headache, peripheral edema | Abdominal pain, vomiting, polydipsia/polyuria, hepatic encephalopathy, liver failure, DIC |
| Pre-eclampsia | No | Yes | ~50% |
| Imaging | Normal | Infarcts, hematoma, rupture, no ascites | Fatty infiltration, sometimes ascites |
| Labs | Bilirubin <5 mg/dL, ↑ALP & GGT, ↑bile acids | Bilirubin <5 mg/dL, hemolysis, platelets <100×10⁹/L, ↑uric acid, ±proteinuria, ALT <500 U/L, ↑LDH | Bilirubin <5 mg/dL, hypoglycemia, ↑creatinine, ±proteinuria & thrombocytopenia, ↑ammonia, ALT <500 U/L, prolonged PT, DIC >75% |
| Morbidity/mortality | Maternal: recurrence in subsequent pregnancy (45%–70%); fetal stillbirth, fetal mortality 0.4%–1% | Maternal: seizures, acute renal failure, hepatic rupture, mortality 1%–25%; fetal mortality 11% | Maternal: acute renal failure, mortality 7%–18%; fetal mortality 9%–23% |

- Management of pre-eclampsia/HELLP/AFLP converges on **appropriate, expeditious delivery**. Liver biopsy rarely needed. **Liver transplant evaluation** if progression to acute liver failure — especially suspected AFLP who have already delivered but do not improve.
- **Low-dose aspirin 81 mg/day beginning weeks 12–16 of gestation, continued until delivery**, for patients with pre-eclampsia risk factors (prior pre-eclampsia, multiple gestation, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, chronic kidney disease, or autoimmune disease).
- **AFLP dx: Swansea criteria** (source notes limitations differentiating AFLP from other pregnancy liver diseases and acute liver failure of any cause).

**Chronic viral hepatitis:**
- **HBV:** assess for antiviral treatment independent of pregnancy per standard guidelines; order serum HBV DNA + liver biochemistries. Reduce MTCT with **HBIG + HBV vaccination to newborn at birth**; further reduce by starting **tenofovir disoproxil fumarate** in the 3rd trimester if HBV DNA **>200,000 IU/mL** (continue until childbirth or up to 12 wk postpartum). TDF preferred; growing data support **TAF** as alternative. **Breastfeeding not contraindicated** (on or off antiviral). Risk of postpartum HBV flare → continue monitoring.
- **HCV:** CDC advises screening at each pregnancy. Results of ongoing trials for HCV treatment in pregnancy awaited; **IDSA/AASLD: DAA therapy may be considered during pregnancy case-by-case** after risk/benefit discussion.

**IBD in pregnancy:**
- Active disease → ↑preterm birth, low birth weight, fetal growth retardation, hypertensive disease of pregnancy, cesarean birth. Conception in remission has outcomes similar to non-pregnant IBD. J-pouch/pelvic dissection can reduce fertility (adhesions). AGA IBD Parenthood Project: **MFM consult for every pregnant IBD patient**.
- Monitoring: **fecal calprotectin** (preconception, each trimester, postpartum; cutoff 200 μg/mg, PPV 67%–74%); **intestinal ultrasound** (84% Sn, 98% Sp; TI/sigmoid visualization decreases 3rd trimester); MRI **without gadolinium**; unsedated **flexible sigmoidoscopy preferred over colonoscopy**.
- Medications: **mesalamine safe**; **sulfasalazine + folic acid 1 mg twice daily** from 3 mo preconception through pregnancy (neural tube defects); **6-MP/azathioprine safe** (60% of newborns can have anemia); **methotrexate contraindicated — stop ≥6 mo before conception**. **Anti-TNF (infliximab, adalimumab) continue** — cross placenta after 20 wk (no live vaccines to infant for first 6 months); **certolizumab does not cross placenta**; vedolizumab data limited but no increased risk reported. Metronidazole (pouchitis, perianal CD, intra-abdominal abscess) and amoxicillin-clavulanate safe. Glucocorticoids for flares (newer data: no orofacial-cleft risk, but ↑preterm/SGA/LBW/NICU — caution 1st trimester). **Thalidomide and ozanimod avoid.** Most IBD meds safe in breastfeeding.
- **Delivery:** avoid vaginal birth in active perianal CD, perianal abscess, or active rectal disease; consider cesarean for prior complex fistulizing disease + J-pouch (preserve continence).

**Immunosuppression / post-liver transplant:**
- Continue immunosuppression at **lowest effective dose**; monitor postpartum for flare/graft rejection. **Mycophenolate mofetil contraindicated** (birth defects) — discontinue ≥6–12 weeks before conception planning. AIH flare risk up to 30% during pregnancy.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **HBV antiviral start timing:** this CPU says start tenofovir "beginning in the third trimester" for HBV DNA >200,000 IU/mL; [[aasld-idsa-2025-chb-treatment]] specifies **gestational week 28**. No true conflict (week 28 ≈ early 3rd trimester); the higher-priority/newer AASLD 2025 week-28 figure is what [[chronic-hepatitis-b]] asserts.
- **HCV DAA in pregnancy:** case-by-case per IDSA/AASLD here aligns with [[aasld-idsa-2023-hcv]]; interferon/ribavirin remain contraindicated.
- **ICP delivery timing:** AGA 2024 relays SMFM/EASL thresholds (≥100 vs <100 μmol/L); the 2016 ACG page phrased delivery simply as "~37 weeks." AGA 2024 (newer) bile-acid-stratified timing supersedes on [[liver-disease-in-pregnancy]] and [[intrahepatic-cholestasis-of-pregnancy]].
