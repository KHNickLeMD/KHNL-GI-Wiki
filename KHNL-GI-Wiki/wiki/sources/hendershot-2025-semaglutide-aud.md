- **Type:** RCT (phase 2, double-blind, parallel-arm; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05520775)

## Summary

Phase 2 double-blind RCT of **once-weekly subcutaneous [[semaglutide]] vs placebo** over 9 weeks of outpatient treatment in **48 non–treatment-seeking adults with alcohol use disorder (AUD)**. Single academic medical center in the United States, enrolling September 2022 – February 2024; 504 assessed for eligibility, 48 randomized (24 per arm). Conducted under a Food and Drug Administration (FDA) investigational new drug exemption.

Primary outcome was **laboratory alcohol self-administration** — a standardized 2-hour drinking session at pretreatment and again between weeks 8 and 9, at the **0.5 mg/week** dose. Secondary and exploratory outcomes were prospective weekly drinking (calendar-based self-report) and craving. First prospective human trial of the preclinical, observational, and pharmacoepidemiology signal that glucagon-like peptide 1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs) reduce alcohol intake — see [[glp-1-receptor-agonists]].

Relevant to gastrointestinal (GI)/hepatology because alcohol-related liver disease incidence has risen since 2020, contributing to a 29% increase in United States alcohol-related mortality since 2016–2017 ([[alcohol-associated-liver-disease]]). Semaglutide for AUD is **off-label and investigational**; the trial justifies larger trials, not practice change.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Design detail that limits the result

- **Dose escalation:** 0.25 mg/week for weeks 1–4 → **0.5 mg/week for weeks 5–8** (the dose at which the primary outcome was collected) → 1.0 mg at week 9, flexible and deferrable for tolerability. Maximum tested dose is well below the doses used for obesity and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) ([[aasld-2025-semaglutide-mash]]).
- **Population:** non–treatment-seeking adults aged 21–65 recruited by advertisement, meeting past-year *DSM-5* criteria for AUD and reporting, in the past month, >7 (women) or >14 (men) standard drinks in a week with ≥2 heavy drinking episodes (≥4 drinks for women, ≥5 for men); mean AUD severity moderate, 71% female, mean age 39.9 y.
- **Key exclusions:** currently seeking treatment for alcohol problems or actively attempting to cut down; past GLP-1 receptor agonist or weight-loss medication use; body mass index (BMI) <23; history of diabetes; other substance use disorder besides tobacco or mild cannabis.

### Outcomes

| Outcome | Effect (semaglutide vs placebo) |
|---|---|
| **Laboratory self-administration — grams of alcohol consumed** (primary) | β −0.48 (95% confidence interval [CI] −0.85 to −0.11); P = .01 |
| **Laboratory self-administration — peak breath alcohol concentration** (primary) | β −0.46 (95% CI −0.87 to −0.06); P = .03 |
| Drinks per calendar day | No effect — β −0.27 (95% CI −0.63 to 0.09); P = .17 |
| Number of drinking vs abstinent days | No effect — β 0.90 (95% CI 0.73 to 1.12); P = .89 |
| **Drinks per drinking day** | β −0.41 (95% CI −0.73 to −0.09); P = .04 |
| **Weekly alcohol craving** | β −0.39 (95% CI −0.73 to −0.06); P = .01 |
| Heavy drinking days over time (treatment-by-time) | Greater relative reduction with semaglutide — β 0.84 (95% CI 0.71 to 0.99); P = .04 |
| **Cigarettes/day**, subsample with current cigarette use (n = 13) | β −0.10 (95% CI −0.16 to −0.03); P = .005 |

- Effect sizes on the laboratory task were **medium to large**; weekly-outcome effect sizes were mostly small through week 4 and grew during weeks 5–8, reaching large (Cohen *d* ≈ 0.80) for drinks per drinking day and heavy drinking days.
- Semaglutide did not change whether participants engaged in the self-administration task (abstinence) or how long they delayed the first drink.
- **Weight** fell 5.05% from baseline with semaglutide vs +0.18% with placebo (P < .001). No significant effect on hemoglobin A₁c, blood pressure, or depression scores.
- **Safety:** adverse effects were the expected ones and largely mild. **No serious adverse events, no adverse interactions with alcohol, and no treatment-related discontinuations.**
- Interpretation: initial prospective evidence that low-dose semaglutide reduces craving and some drinking outcomes; larger trials of incretin therapies for AUD are warranted.

## Relevance to Wiki

- [[semaglutide]] → investigational, off-label use for AUD — effect on craving and consumption, and the dose actually tested.
- [[glp-1-receptor-agonists]] → the class-level AUD signal, so far a single small phase 2 trial.
- [[alcohol-associated-liver-disease]] → an emerging pharmacologic angle on alcohol reduction; not a guideline-endorsed AUD pharmacotherapy.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Not practice-changing and not in conflict with any guideline on the wiki: no society guideline lists a GLP-1RA as AUD pharmacotherapy. Semaglutide's GI indication is MASH ([[aasld-2025-semaglutide-mash]]).
- **Single center, n = 48, 9 weeks, non–treatment-seeking participants excluded from trying to cut down** — the population is the opposite of the one a clinician would treat, and the primary endpoint is a laboratory surrogate rather than a clinical drinking outcome.
- Primary outcome was collected at 0.5 mg/week only; whether higher doses do more for AUD is untested.
- Confidence intervals for secondary and exploratory outcomes were **not adjusted for multiplicity**.
- The cigarette finding comes from a preplanned analysis in 13 participants — hypothesis-generating.
