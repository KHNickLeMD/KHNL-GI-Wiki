- **Article:** [Hendershot CS, et al. Once-Weekly Semaglutide in Adults With Alcohol Use Disorder: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA Psychiatry 2025;82(4):395-405.](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2024.4789)
- **Authors:** Hendershot CS, Bremmer MP, Paladino MB, et al.
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal/Publisher:** JAMA Psychiatry
- **DOI:** [10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2024.4789](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2024.4789)
- **Type:** RCT (phase 2, double-blind, parallel-arm; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05520775)

## Summary
Phase 2 double-blind RCT of **once-weekly subcutaneous semaglutide vs placebo** over 9 weeks of outpatient treatment in **48 non–treatment-seeking adults with alcohol use disorder (AUD)** (dose escalated 0.25 → 0.5 → 1.0 mg/week). Primary outcome was laboratory alcohol self-administration (pre- vs post-treatment at 0.5 mg/week); secondary/exploratory outcomes included prospective drinking and craving. First prospective human trial testing the preclinical/observational signal that GLP-1 receptor agonists reduce alcohol intake.

Relevant to GI/hepatology given the rising burden of alcohol-associated liver disease; semaglutide use for AUD is **off-label** and investigational (phase 2, small sample), justifying larger trials rather than practice change.

## Key Findings / Claims
- **Reduced laboratory alcohol self-administration** — grams consumed (β −0.48; 95% CI −0.85 to −0.11; P = .01) and peak breath alcohol concentration (β −0.46; 95% CI −0.87 to −0.06; P = .03), medium-to-large effect sizes.
- No effect on average drinks/calendar day or number of drinking days, but **reduced drinks per drinking day** (β −0.41; P = .04) and **weekly alcohol craving** (β −0.39; P = .01); predicted greater reductions in heavy drinking over time.
- Reduced **cigarettes/day** in the subgroup with current cigarette use (β −0.10; P = .005).
- Interpretation: initial prospective evidence that low-dose semaglutide reduces craving and some drinking outcomes; larger trials needed.
