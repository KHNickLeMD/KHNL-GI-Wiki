## Summary

This ACG guideline covers five distinct clinical domains involving the hepatic and mesenteric circulation: (1) hemostasis and bleeding/thrombotic risk in cirrhosis, (2) [[portal-vein-thrombosis|portal vein thrombosis]] (PVT) and mesenteric vein thrombosis (MVT) without cirrhosis, (3) PVT/MVT with cirrhosis, (4) [[budd-chiari-syndrome|Budd-Chiari syndrome]] (BCS), and (5) miscellaneous vascular disorders including [[mesenteric-artery-aneurysm|mesenteric artery aneurysms]] and hepatic vascular malformations in [[hereditary-hemorrhagic-telangiectasia|hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia]] (HHT).

A major theme is the rebalanced [[cirrhosis-hemostasis|hemostasis in cirrhosis]] — these patients are neither auto-anticoagulated nor inherently hypercoagulable, and traditional markers (PT/INR, platelet count) poorly predict bleeding risk. This principle drives recommendations against prophylactic blood products (FFP, platelets) before routine procedures and in favor of viscoelastic testing (TEG/ROTEM) to guide transfusion.

For PVT, the guideline advocates anticoagulation broadly — in noncirrhotic acute symptomatic PVT/MVT (strong recommendation) and in complete/extending PVT in cirrhosis (strong recommendation). Anticoagulation is safe in cirrhosis despite traditional reluctance and can achieve recanalization in 40–75% of noncirrhotic cases with LMWH or vitamin K antagonists.

Budd-Chiari syndrome management follows a stepwise algorithm: anticoagulation → percutaneous angioplasty/TIPS → [[liver-transplantation|liver transplantation]]. [[hepatocellular-carcinoma|HCC]] surveillance is required in all BCS patients given the high risk. For HHT, standard therapy is first-line for hepatic vascular malformations (LVMs); bevacizumab at specialized centers is reserved for refractory cases.

## Key Findings / Claims

- Cirrhosis represents a rebalanced hemostatic state: pro-hemostatic (elevated vWF, reduced protein C/S/antithrombin) and anti-hemostatic (reduced factors II/V/VII/IX/X, thrombocytopenia) factors are simultaneously reduced, creating a new equilibrium
- PT/INR does not reflect global hemostatic status in cirrhosis; thrombin generation assays and TEG/ROTEM better reflect true clotting capacity
- PVT prevalence: ~1% in compensated cirrhosis, up to 25% in patients awaiting liver transplantation
- Noncirrhotic PVT causes: thrombophilia (factor V Leiden, prothrombin gene mutation, JAK2 mutation/MPN, antiphospholipid syndrome), [[portal-hypertension|portal hypertension]] from any cause, intra-abdominal infection/inflammation, surgery, local trauma
- MVT: similar risk factors to noncirrhotic PVT; bowel ischemia is the key complication requiring urgent recognition
- Recanalization with anticoagulation in noncirrhotic acute PVT: 40–75% with LMWH; VKA or DOAC equivalently effective
- Budd-Chiari syndrome (BCS): hepatic venous outflow obstruction; underlying thrombophilia in 75%; MPN (especially PV with JAK2) most common; 5-year survival 50–90% with stepwise management
- HHT prevalence: 1:5,000–1:8,000; hepatic vascular malformations in 32–73%; symptomatic liver disease rare (1–8%)
- Mesenteric artery aneurysm/pseudoaneurysm: SMA aneurysms most common (60%); risk of rupture higher with size >2 cm
- TIPS significantly improves recanalization of acute PVT in cirrhosis and is a key bridge in BCS

## Recommendations

| Recommendation | Strength | Evidence Quality |
|---|---|---|
| **Hemostasis in Cirrhosis** | | |
| Do not use fresh frozen plasma (FFP) prophylactically before routine procedures in cirrhosis to correct INR | Conditional | Low |
| Do not administer prophylactic platelet transfusion before routine procedures in cirrhosis outside the setting of significant renal dysfunction or active bleeding | Conditional | Very Low |
| Do not use antifibrinolytic agents in cirrhosis in the absence of clinical evidence of hyperfibrinolysis | Conditional | Very Low |
| Whole-blood viscoelastic tests (TEG or ROTEM) can be used to guide transfusion decisions and avoid unnecessary blood product use in cirrhosis | Conditional | Low |
| Doppler ultrasound is recommended as the first-line test for the diagnosis of portal vein thrombosis | Strong | Very Low |
| **PVT/MVT Without Cirrhosis — Acute** | | |
| Anticoagulation is recommended for all noncirrhotic patients with acute symptomatic PVT or MVT | Strong | Low |
| Anticoagulation should be initiated promptly to achieve recanalization and prevent bowel ischemia | Strong | Low |
| LMWH or VKA are acceptable anticoagulation agents in noncirrhotic PVT; duration ≥3–6 months | Strong | Low |
| NSBBs are the preferred first-line prophylaxis for variceal bleeding in patients with noncirrhotic PVT receiving anticoagulation | Strong | Low |
| **PVT/MVT Without Cirrhosis — Chronic** | | |
| Anticoagulation is suggested for chronic noncirrhotic PVT in the setting of thrombophilia, thrombus extension/progression, or intestinal ischemia | Conditional | Very Low |
| Anticoagulation for ≥6 months is suggested when no thrombophilia is identified and a reversible etiology is present | Conditional | Very Low |
| **PVT/MVT With Cirrhosis** | | |
| Anticoagulation is recommended for cirrhotic patients with complete main portal vein thrombosis, MVT, or thrombus extending into mesenteric veins | Strong | Low |
| Anticoagulation for ≥6 months is suggested for acute PVT in cirrhosis | Conditional | Very Low |
| Anticoagulation for chronic PVT in cirrhosis should be reserved for patients with thrombophilia, thrombus progression, or intestinal ischemia | Conditional | Very Low |
| TIPS can be used for portal vein recanalization in cirrhotic patients with PVT when anticoagulation is insufficient | Conditional | Very Low |
| **Budd-Chiari Syndrome** | | |
| Doppler ultrasound is recommended as the first-line imaging for BCS diagnosis; contrast-enhanced CT or MRI for confirmation | Conditional | Low |
| Anticoagulation should be initiated in all BCS patients without contraindications | Strong | Moderate |
| Stepwise management: anticoagulation → percutaneous angioplasty or TIPS → liver transplantation | Strong | Moderate |
| HCC surveillance with US and AFP every 6 months is recommended in all BCS patients | Conditional | Low |
| Underlying thrombophilia (especially MPN/JAK2 mutation) should be sought in all BCS patients | Strong | Low |
| **Mesenteric Artery Aneurysms** | | |
| Observe asymptomatic mesenteric artery aneurysms <2 cm with imaging surveillance | Conditional | Low |
| Treat all mesenteric artery aneurysms >2 cm even if asymptomatic due to rupture risk | Strong | Low |
| **HHT — Hepatic Vascular Malformations (LVMs)** | | |
| Routine screening for hepatic LVMs is not recommended in asymptomatic HHT patients | Strong | Low |
| Contrast CT or MRI/MRCP is recommended for evaluation of symptomatic hepatic LVMs in HHT | Strong | Low |
| Standard medical therapy (diuretics, rate control, high-output heart failure management) is first-line for symptomatic hepatic LVMs | Conditional | Low |
| Bevacizumab is suggested for HHT patients with symptomatic hepatic LVMs who fail standard medical therapy; should be administered at specialized HHT centers | Conditional | Low |
| Liver transplantation can be considered in refractory HHT liver disease with severe, intractable symptoms | Conditional | Very Low |

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Whether all patients with noncirrhotic chronic PVT should receive anticoagulation remains debated; guideline reserves for thrombophilia, progression, or bowel ischemia
- DOAC safety in cirrhosis (particularly Child-Pugh B/C) is not established; most trial data with LMWH and VKA
- Optimal duration of anticoagulation in cirrhotic PVT post-transplant is unknown
- Role of TIPS vs. anticoagulation alone in acute PVT in cirrhosis requires prospective trials
