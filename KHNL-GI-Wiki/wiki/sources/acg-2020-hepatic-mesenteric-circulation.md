## Summary

- American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) guideline on vascular liver disorders; five domains: (1) bleeding and thrombotic risk in [[cirrhosis-hemostasis|cirrhosis]], (2) [[portal-vein-thrombosis|portal vein thrombosis]] (PVT) and mesenteric vein thrombosis (MVT) without cirrhosis, (3) PVT/MVT with cirrhosis, (4) [[budd-chiari-syndrome|Budd-Chiari syndrome]] (BCS), (5) [[mesenteric-artery-aneurysm|mesenteric artery aneurysms]] and liver vascular malformations (LVMs) in [[hereditary-hemorrhagic-telangiectasia|hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia]] (HHT).
- Structure: **23 numbered recommendations** from PICO questions, graded by GRADE (Table 1); **28 numbered key concepts**, ungraded, from author expert opinion and literature review (Table 2). Evidence base mostly nonrandomized/observational — 22 of 23 recommendations rest on low or very low quality evidence (only Rec 17 is moderate).
- Cirrhosis: hemostasis is rebalanced but precarious; international normalized ratio (INR) correlates poorly with thrombin generation and bleeding → recommends against prophylactic fresh frozen plasma (FFP), platelets, and antifibrinolytics in the stated settings.
- PVT/MVT: anticoagulation is the core therapy, with indications that differ by cirrhosis status, acuity, and extent (Figure 3 algorithm, embedded on [[portal-vein-thrombosis]]).
- BCS: stepwise least-to-most invasive management — anticoagulation → angioplasty or transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS) → [[liver-transplantation|liver transplantation]] (LT); [[hepatocellular-carcinoma|hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)]] surveillance in chronic BCS.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Hemostasis in cirrhosis**
- Liver-derived procoagulant deficit offset by deficit of liver-derived anticoagulants (protein C [PC], S, antithrombin) and raised endothelial factor VIII and von Willebrand factor (vWF).
- INR was developed to monitor vitamin K antagonists (VKAs); poor correlate of thrombin generation in cirrhosis. Viscoelastic tests (thromboelastography [TEG], rotational thromboelastometry [ROTEM]) give a fuller picture but lack validated cutoffs.
- FFP at 10 mL/kg lowers prothrombin time (PT) without improving thrombin generation; high-volume infusion raises portal pressure (estimated 15–20 mm Hg); paracentesis without FFP shown safe with INR as high as 8.7.
- In vitro thrombin generation adequate at platelets 50–60,000/µL. Thrombopoietin (TPO)-receptor agonists (avatrombopag, lusutrombopag) approved before elective procedures with platelets <50,000/µL; possible ↑ thromboembolism, especially PVT.
- Fibrinogen >120–150 mg/dL has been recommended before high-risk procedures (extrapolated from trauma data).
- Hyperfibrinolysis features in up to 30% of hospitalized patients with cirrhosis; clinically evident far less often.
- Direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs): successful use reported in cirrhosis but generally avoided in Child-Pugh B and C.

**PVT/MVT — etiology and diagnosis**
- Thrombophilia is the most common cause of PVT without cirrhosis; myeloproliferative neoplasm (MPN) in ~25%; *JAK2* testing ~90–95% accurate for MPN. Causes: Table 3 below.
- PVT prevalence in cirrhosis: ~1% compensated, up to **20%** among LT-listed; new PVT 4.6% at 1 y and 10.7% at 5 y in a compensated cohort.
- MVT: 10–20% of mesenteric ischemic disorders.
- Doppler ultrasound (US): sensitivity 73–93%, specificity 99%, positive predictive value 86–97%, negative predictive value 98%. Computed tomography (CT): 90/99/99/95%. CT ~90% accurate for acute or chronic MVT.
- Contrast CT or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) distinguishes bland from tumor (HCC) thrombus — arterialization of thrombus suggests tumor.

**PVT/MVT — treatment data**
- Noncirrhotic acute PVT (prospective, 95 anticoagulated): portal vein patency 13% → 33%, superior mesenteric vein 42% → 73%.
- Acute MVT: anticoagulation 3–6 mo for reversible causes; indefinite for thrombophilia. Thrombolysis considered for progression despite anticoagulation; surgery for infarction/gangrene.
- Cirrhosis: meta-analysis (observational only) — anticoagulation ↑ recanalization, ↓ variceal bleeding or hepatic worsening. TIPS in 70 patients: recanalization 57%, mainly incomplete PVT or no varices.
- LT-listed: complete PVT at LT may worsen post-transplant survival; anticoagulation → complete recanalization in 42% and 75% (two small studies) → continue until LT.
- HCC tumor thrombus: no benefit from anticoagulation.
- Portal cavernoma cholangiopathy: 0.5–1% of chronic PVT; diagnosis by magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP).

**Budd-Chiari syndrome**
- Primary BCS: incidence 0.5–2 per million (Western); ≥1 prothrombotic disorder in 79–84%, ≥2 in 25–46%; MPN ~half (*JAK2* V617F). ~20% asymptomatic.
- Doppler US, CT, MRI agree highly (173-patient prospective study).
- Major bleeding on anticoagulation up to 22.8 episodes/100 patient-years.
- Angioplasty long-term success ~1/3 of patients. Polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE)-covered stents cut TIPS dysfunction from 87% to ≤30–40%.
- European stepwise cohort (157 patients): overall survival 77%; 39.5% needed TIPS, 6.45% LT. TIPS 5-y transplant-free survival 72–78%.
- LT for BCS: 5-y survival 70–92%; recurrent thrombosis up to 20%.
- HCC: pooled prevalence 15.4% in BCS; alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) cutoff 15 ng/mL separated benign nodules from HCC (positive predictive value 100%, negative 91%).

**Mesenteric artery aneurysms**
- True aneurysm = localized dilation >1.5× expected diameter, all 3 wall layers; pseudoaneurysm = intimal/medial disruption, higher rupture risk.
- Mortality after rupture up to 70%; pancreatitis-related pseudoaneurysm rupture 30–50%.
- Splenic artery ~60% of cases (multiparous women); hepatic next.

**HHT liver vascular malformations**
- HHT prevalence 1:5,000–8,000; LVMs on imaging in 55% of definite HHT; symptomatic at diagnosis 8–14%; ~3.5%/y of asymptomatic develop symptoms.
- Three shunt types: hepatic artery → hepatic vein (high-output heart failure [HOHF], ischemic cholangiopathy), hepatic artery → portal vein (portal hypertension [PH]), portal vein → hepatic vein (encephalopathy).
- Presentations: HOHF 63%, biliary ischemia 19%, PH 17% (mostly via nodular regenerative hyperplasia, which mimics cirrhosis).
- Liver biopsy not indicated (bleeding risk; histology misleading).
- Bevacizumab 5 mg/kg IV every 14 days × 6 doses: cardiac index normalized 12%, improved 71%, no response 17% (HOHF cohort, n=24). Adverse effects: arthralgias, headache, proteinuria, hypertension, poor wound healing.
- Hepatic artery embolization: 30% mortality in one series — proscribed with biliary disease or PH.

## Recommendations

Numbered as in the guideline's Table 1 (23 recommendations). The operative statement is given with its decision qualifiers; the guideline's supporting commentary is condensed.

### Bleeding and Thrombotic Risk in Liver Disorders

| # | Recommendation | Strength / Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Do not** give FFP to improve thrombin generation in cirrhosis at conventional doses (10 mL/kg). Volume enough to lower a prolonged INR (1–2 L) raises portal pressure and may trigger variceal hemorrhage — prophylactic plasma to reduce bleeding risk is futile and potentially risky | Conditional / Low |
| 2 | **Do not** give prophylactic platelet transfusion before common procedures (e.g., variceal banding, paracentesis) outside significant renal dysfunction (**serum creatinine >2.5 mg/dL**) or sepsis. Thrombin production adequate at **≥50,000/mL**; one adult dose does not improve it. Higher platelet levels may be appropriate for high-risk procedures (large polyp removal, major surgery); if elective, **TPO agonists** may be more appropriate | Conditional / Very low |
| 3 | **Do not** use antifibrinolytics (epsilon aminocaproic acid, tranexamic acid) for persistent active bleeding in cirrhosis **in the absence of hyperfibrinolysis**; caution if pathologic clot such as PVT already present | Conditional / Very low |

### PVT/MVT With and Without Cirrhosis — Diagnosis

| # | Recommendation | Strength / Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Doppler US** is the initial noninvasive test for PVT. **Contrast CT or MRI** to assess extension into mesenteric veins and exclude tumor thrombus in patients with cirrhosis and new PVT/MVT | Strong / Very low |

### PVT/MVT Without Cirrhosis

| # | Recommendation | Strength / Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | **Anticoagulation** (not thrombolysis) for **all** noncirrhotic patients with **acute symptomatic** PVT or MVT, absent contraindication | Strong / Low |
| 6 | Chronic PVT: anticoagulate if **any** of (i) inherited or acquired thrombophilia, (ii) thrombus progression into mesenteric veins, (iii) current or previous bowel ischemia | Conditional / Very low |
| 7 | Duration: **≥6 months** if no demonstrable thrombophilia and the etiology is reversible; **indefinite** if thrombophilia | Conditional / Very low |
| 8 | Variceal prophylaxis in chronic PVT needing anticoagulation: **nonselective beta-blockers (NSBBs)** for **high-risk varices**; endoscopic variceal ligation (EVL) if NSBB contraindicated or not tolerated (anticoagulation may need interrupting periprocedurally) | Strong / Low |
| 9 | Initial agent: **unfractionated heparin or low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH)** — weigh pros and cons (Table 4) | Conditional / Very low |
| 10 | Maintenance: **LMWH or warfarin**. DOAC experience limited; absorption may be limited with intestinal edema, so monitor — normal thrombin time and activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT) for dabigatran, normal PT or anti-Xa for apixaban/rivaroxaban rule out substantial drug effect. Consider reversal-agent availability (Table 5) | Conditional / Very low |

### PVT/MVT With Cirrhosis

| # | Recommendation | Strength / Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | **Anticoagulation** for (i) **acute complete main PVT**, (ii) **MVT**, or (iii) extension of portal venous thrombosis into mesenteric veins. Weigh bleeding risk — e.g., platelets **<50,000/µL** or hepatic encephalopathy with fall risk | Strong / Low |
| 12 | Chronic PVT: anticoagulate **only if** (i) inherited thrombophilia, (ii) thrombus progression, or (iii) history of bowel ischemia from extension into mesenteric veins. May also be considered in patients awaiting LT | Conditional / Very low |
| 13 | Acute PVT/MVT without inherited thrombophilia: **6 months**; continue beyond that while on the **LT waiting list** | Conditional / Very low |
| 14 | Variceal prophylaxis in PVT needing anticoagulation: **NSBBs** for **high-risk varices**; EVL if NSBB contraindicated or not tolerated (interrupt anticoagulation periprocedurally as needed) | Strong / Low |
| 15 | Initial agent: unfractionated heparin or LMWH — **unfractionated heparin preferred with renal insufficiency, LMWH preferred with thrombocytopenia** | Conditional / Very low |

### Budd-Chiari Syndrome

| # | Recommendation | Strength / Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 16 | **Doppler US** is the initial test for BCS (e.g., new-onset ascites). **Contrast CT or MRI** to assess thrombus extension, rule out tumor thrombus, judge response to anticoagulation, evaluate indeterminate nodules, and whenever suspicion stays high despite negative/inconclusive Doppler | Conditional / Low |
| 17 | **Stepwise, least to most invasive.** Systemic anticoagulation first. If medical therapy fails — worsening liver and/or renal function, ascites, or hepatic encephalopathy — endovascular therapy (angioplasty or TIPS). **LT reserved for TIPS failure and BCS presenting as fulminant liver failure** | Strong / Moderate |
| 18 | **HCC surveillance** in chronic BCS: abdominal US + serum AFP **every 6 mo**; refer to expert centers for diagnosis | Conditional / Low |

### Mesenteric Artery Aneurysms

| # | Recommendation | Strength / Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 19 | Asymptomatic **<2 cm**: treat **regardless of diameter** only for aneurysms of the **pancreaticoduodenal or gastroduodenal arcade**, **intraparenchymal hepatic artery branches**, **women of childbearing age**, and **LT recipients**. Others: imaging at **6 mo, 1 y, then every 1–2 y**. **Treat symptomatic aneurysms** (abdominal pain without other cause) | Conditional / Low |
| 20 | Intervene on **all aneurysms >2 cm**, even if asymptomatic | Strong / Low |

### HHT — Liver Vascular Malformations

| # | Recommendation | Strength / Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 21 | **Do not** routinely screen for LVMs in HHT. **Do** evaluate those with a liver bruit, hyperdynamic circulation, or liver test abnormalities; pregnant women with LVMs need special attention (hemodynamic stress) | Strong / Low |
| 22 | **Contrast CT or MRI/MRCP** for HHT patients with symptoms or signs of heart failure, biliary ischemia, hepatic encephalopathy, mesenteric ischemia, or PH. Doppler US can diagnose with a compatible picture but is less accurate. **Angiography and liver biopsy not recommended** for diagnosis | Strong / Low |
| 23 | **Standard medical therapy** for each LVM complication first (most respond). Nonresponders → multidisciplinary specialized centers. **Bevacizumab** for HOHF and possibly other complications before invasive therapy; symptoms recur after stopping. **Hepatic artery embolization or ligation proscribed** with biliary involvement or PH; insufficient evidence for it in HOHF. LT for nonresponders or relapse after medical therapy (listing criteria unclear; high perioperative complications; LVMs may recur as early as 6 y post-LT) | Conditional / Low |

## Key Concepts (Table 2 — ungraded)

| # | Key concept (condensed) |
|---|---|
| 1 | Hemostasis in compensated cirrhosis largely intact but precarious; sepsis, hypothermia, or kidney dysfunction can tip it toward clotting or bleeding, especially when decompensated |
| 2 | Procoagulant deficit offset by deficit of anticoagulants (PC) and ↑ factor VIII and vWF |
| 3 | Anticoagulant-system changes can cause relative hypercoagulability undetected by conventional tests |
| 4 | INR correlates poorly with thrombin generation and bleeding risk in cirrhosis |
| 5 | TEG/ROTEM may avoid unnecessary blood products before procedures with prolonged INR; transfusion parameters not established |
| 6 | Pharmacologic deep vein thrombosis prophylaxis seems safe in hospitalized cirrhosis **without bleeding and with platelets ≥50,000/µL** |
| 7 | Thrombophilia work-up for PVT/MVT **without cirrhosis** absent an obvious cause (e.g., acute intra-abdominal process). **With cirrhosis**, only if (i) prior thrombosis, (ii) thrombosis at unusual sites such as hepatic veins, or (iii) family history of thrombosis |
| 8 | ***JAK2*** testing for MPN — most common thrombophilic cause of noncirrhotic PVT/MVT |
| 9 | Abdominal pain out of proportion to examination → suspect PVT/MVT |
| 10 | In PVT/MVT, suspect intestinal ischemia with fever, ascites, rebound tenderness, leukocytosis, ↑ serum lactate |
| 11 | Doppler US of hepatic vasculature at (i) new cirrhosis diagnosis, (ii) onset of PH, (iii) hepatic decompensation |
| 12 | Chronic PVT → endoscopy for esophageal/gastric varices |
| 13 | Routine prophylactic anticoagulation to prevent PVT in cirrhosis needs more data |
| 14 | Anticoagulation for PVT/MVT in cirrhosis is **not** associated with ↑ variceal bleeding risk |
| 15 | Portal hypertensive cholangiopathy: endoscopic treatment for symptomatic cholangitis; also for choledocholithiasis or biliary stricture. Surgery only if endoscopy fails |
| 16 | Work up acquired and inherited thrombophilia in **all** BCS — even after one conspicuous disorder is found (≥2 factors common) |
| 17 | Investigate the hepatic venous outflow tract in acute or chronic liver disease without obvious cause, especially new ascites and/or abdominal pain |
| 18 | Hepatic venogram and/or liver biopsy rarely needed to diagnose BCS |
| 19 | Refer BCS to a hematologist for the underlying prothrombotic disorder |
| 20 | Gastroesophageal varices do **not** contraindicate anticoagulation in BCS; give primary/secondary variceal prophylaxis as indicated |
| 21 | Hepatic vein balloon angioplasty (± stent) only for **short-segment** hepatic vein stenosis |
| 22 | PTFE-covered stents preferred over bare stents for TIPS |
| 23 | US-guided direct intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (DIPS) if TIPS impossible because of complete hepatic vein occlusion |
| 24 | Surgical portosystemic shunts only when neither TIPS nor DIPS is feasible |
| 25 | After LT for BCS, consider long-term anticoagulation, especially with persistent prothrombotic risk (e.g., MPN) |
| 26 | Prognostic scores do not guide choice of therapy in BCS |
| 27 | Triphasic contrast CT or MRI required to evaluate hepatic nodules in BCS |
| 28 | Standard LVM treatment = symptom management of each complication: HOHF, PH (ascites, variceal hemorrhage), mesenteric ischemia, bilomas |

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Agent choice (noncirrhotic PVT) — superseded.** ACG 2020: unfractionated heparin or LMWH to start, LMWH or warfarin to maintain, limited DOAC experience. [[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension|Baveno VIII]] (newer): LMWH first, DOACs as the primary oral option outside stated exclusions.
- **Variceal screening before anticoagulation — superseded.** ACG Figure 3 routes chronic PVT through upper endoscopy before anticoagulation. Baveno VIII and [[aga-2025-pvt-cirrhosis|AGA 2025]] (newer): start anticoagulation without delay; do not postpone for variceal prophylaxis.
- **Partial PVT in cirrhosis — superseded.** ACG 2020: no anticoagulation for acute partial PVT except transplant-listed patients. AGA 2025: consider anticoagulation for recent >50% occlusive or main portal/mesenteric involvement. Baveno VIII: consider it for recent partial (<50% remnant lumen) main portal vein thrombosis; anticoagulate any PVT in a transplant candidate.
- **Chronic PVT in cirrhosis.** ACG 2020 anticoagulates for thrombophilia, progression, or ischemia; AGA 2025 advises against anticoagulation for chronic complete occlusion with cavernous transformation (futile).
- **Duration in cirrhosis.** ACG 2020: 6 months (longer if transplant-listed). Baveno VIII: at least 6 months and consider long-term regardless of recanalization.
- **Intra-document:** Rec 19/20 use a **2 cm** threshold; the body text frames intervention at >2–2.5 cm. Rec 2 prints the platelet threshold as "/mL" where the rest of the document uses /µL.
