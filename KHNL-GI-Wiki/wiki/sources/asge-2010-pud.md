## Summary

ASGE Standards of Practice statement on the role of endoscopy across the spectrum of [[peptic-ulcer-disease|peptic ulcer disease]] (PUD) — diagnosis, malignancy exclusion, surveillance, bleeding, and complications. Endoscopy is central to PUD: it diagnoses and characterizes ulcers, distinguishes benign from malignant gastric ulcers, provides hemostasis for bleeding ulcers, and treats benign gastric outlet obstruction with balloon dilation. The document stratifies recommendations by GRADE.

Key practice points: routine biopsy of duodenal ulcers is unnecessary (rarely malignant), whereas most gastric ulcers warrant biopsy because malignant gastric ulcers can appear endoscopically benign. Decisions on gastric-ulcer biopsy and surveillance endoscopy should be individualized rather than applied uniformly. Endoscopy should be performed early in bleeding PUD, repeated for rebleeding before surgery/IR, and avoided in acute perforation.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Recommendations (verbatim, with GRADE quality of evidence):**

1. We recommend that testing for the presence of H. pylori be performed in all patients with PUD because it is a common etiology. (Moderate)
2. Duodenal ulcers are extremely unlikely to be malignant, and routine biopsy of these ulcers is not recommended. (Moderate)
3. Endoscopy is not recommended to evaluate benign-appearing, uncomplicated duodenal ulcers identified on radiologic imaging. (Moderate)
4. We suggest that surveillance endoscopy be considered in patients with duodenal ulceration who experience persistent symptoms despite an appropriate course of therapy, specifically to rule out refractory peptic ulcers and ulcers with nonpeptic etiologies. (Low)
5. We suggest that most gastric ulcers undergo biopsy because malignant gastric ulcers may appear endoscopically benign. However, in some clinical situations (eg, young patients taking NSAIDs with multiple benign-appearing ulcers), the risk of malignancy is very low; the decision to perform biopsy and/or surveillance endoscopy should be individualized. (Low)
6. We suggest that the decision to perform surveillance endoscopy in patients with a gastric ulcer be individualized. Surveillance endoscopy is suggested for those gastric ulcer patients who remain symptomatic despite an appropriate course of medical therapy, in patients with gastric ulcers without a clear etiology, and in those who did not undergo biopsy at the index EGD. (Very low)
7. In patients with refractory PUD, we suggest surveillance endoscopy be performed until the ulcer has healed or the etiology has been defined. (Low)
8. Because endoscopy is an effective tool in the diagnosis, prognostication, and therapy of bleeding peptic ulcers, we recommend that it be performed early in the course of hospitalization. (High)
9. In patients who rebleed after initial endoscopic hemostasis, repeat endoscopic therapy is recommended before considering surgical or radiologic intervention. (Moderate)
10. We recommend against endoscopy in patients with clinical evidence of acute perforation. (Moderate)
11. We recommend endoscopy for the evaluation of gastric outlet obstruction. (High)
12. We suggest endoscopic balloon dilation be considered for the management of benign gastric outlet obstruction. (Low)

**Other points:**
- Gastric outlet obstruction: active ulcers found in up to one third of patients endoscoped for this; endoscopic balloon dilation gives 67%-83% short-term symptom relief, but restenosis develops in up to 84%, ~51% ultimately require surgery, and pyloric dilation perforation rates are high (4%-7%). Patients needing >2 dilations are at high risk of endoscopic failure.
- Endoscopy has no therapeutic role in penetrating ulcers.
