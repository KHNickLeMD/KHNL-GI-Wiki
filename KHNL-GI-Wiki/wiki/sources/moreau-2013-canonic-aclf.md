## Summary

CANONIC is the study that created the EASL–CLIF definition of acute-on-chronic liver failure (ACLF). It prospectively enrolled **1,343 patients hospitalised with cirrhosis and acute decompensation (AD)** at **29 liver units in 8 European countries**, February–September 2011, and asked an empirical question: among patients admitted with AD, which combinations of organ failure carry a short-term mortality high enough to constitute a distinct syndrome?

Organ failure was defined **before the study started**, using a cirrhosis-adapted modification of the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment score — the **CLIF-SOFA score** (Table 1) — scoring six systems (liver, kidney, brain, coagulation, circulation, lungs) from 0 to 4, aggregate range 0–24. The authors then set a **pre-specified 28-day mortality threshold of 15%** and worked backwards: whichever organ-failure patterns exceeded it would define ACLF.

Three patterns did. **Two or more organ failures** carried 32.0% 28-day mortality and **three or more** carried 78.6%, against **14.6%** for a single organ failure. Single organ failure was not homogeneous: **single kidney failure** alone exceeded the threshold (18.6%), while single "non-kidney" failures did not — *unless* the patient also had **kidney dysfunction (creatinine 1.5–1.9 mg/dL) and/or mild-to-moderate hepatic encephalopathy (HE grade I–II)**, which pushed single liver failure from 5.9% to 30.3% and single coagulation failure from 5.3% to 22.2%. From these three risk factors the four-group classification — **no ACLF, ACLF grade 1, grade 2, grade 3** — was built.

The paper also establishes ACLF as a syndrome rather than an end-stage: **22.6% had it at enrollment** and **30.9% of the whole cohort** had it at enrollment or within 28 days; **43.6% had no identifiable precipitant**; and it was **more severe in patients with no prior history of AD**. Mortality tracked leukocyte count and C-reactive protein in parallel with grade, pointing at systemic inflammation rather than simple hepatic decompensation.

## Key Findings / Claims

### CLIF-SOFA score (Table 1) — the organ-failure instrument

Each system scored 0–4; **bold cells in the published table are the diagnostic criteria for organ failure**.

| Organ/system | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Liver** (bilirubin, mg/dL) | <1.2 | ≥1.2 to <2.0 | ≥2.0 to <6.0 | ≥6.0 to <12.0 | **≥12.0** |
| **Kidney** (creatinine, mg/dL) | <1.2 | ≥1.2 to <2.0 | **≥2.0 to <3.5** | **≥3.5 to <5.0** | **≥5.0** |
| | | | **…or use of renal replacement therapy** (spans scores 2–4) | | |
| **Cerebral** (HE grade) | No HE | I | II | **III** | **IV** |
| **Coagulation** (INR) | <1.1 | ≥1.1 to <1.25 | ≥1.25 to <1.5 | ≥1.5 to <2.5 | **≥2.5, or platelet count ≤20×10⁹/L** |
| **Circulation** (mean arterial pressure, mm Hg) | ≥70 | <70 | **Dopamine ≤5 or dobutamine or terlipressin** | **Dopamine >5 or E ≤0.1 or NE ≤0.1** | **Dopamine >15 or E >0.1 or NE >0.1** |
| **Lungs** — PaO₂/FiO₂ | >400 | >300 to ≤400 | >200 to ≤300 | **>100 to ≤200** | **≤100** |
| **Lungs** — SpO₂/FiO₂ | >512 | >357 to ≤512 | >214 to ≤357 | **>89 to ≤214** | **≤89** |

E, epinephrine; NE, norepinephrine (doses µg/kg/min). The original SOFA score is Vincent et al.

**Organ failure therefore = bilirubin ≥12.0 mg/dL · creatinine ≥2.0 mg/dL or renal replacement therapy · HE grade III–IV · INR ≥2.5 or platelets ≤20×10⁹/L · any vasopressor · PaO₂/FiO₂ ≤200 or SpO₂/FiO₂ ≤214.**

### The four groups (the diagnostic criteria for ACLF)

| Group | Definition | n (%) of 1,343 | 28-day mortality | 90-day mortality |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **No ACLF** | (1) no organ failure; **or** (2) single "non-kidney" organ failure (liver, coagulation, circulation or respiration) **with** creatinine <1.5 mg/dL **and** no HE; **or** (3) single cerebral failure **with** creatinine <1.5 mg/dL | 1,040 (77.4) | 4.7% | 14% |
| **ACLF grade 1** | (1) single **kidney** failure; **or** (2) single failure of liver, coagulation, circulation or respiration **with** creatinine 1.5–1.9 mg/dL **and/or** mild-to-moderate HE; **or** (3) single **cerebral** failure **with** creatinine 1.5–1.9 mg/dL | 148 (11.0) | 22.1% | 40.7% |
| **ACLF grade 2** | **2 organ failures** | 108 (8.0) | 32.0% | 52.3% |
| **ACLF grade 3** | **3 organ failures or more** | 47 (3.5) | 76.7% | 79.1% |

- **Mild-to-moderate HE = grade I or II** (CLIF-SOFA cerebral score 1 or 2); **kidney dysfunction = creatinine 1.5–1.9 mg/dL**. Both are *below* the thresholds that count as organ failure, which is the point — they are modifiers that upgrade a single organ failure into ACLF.
- Overall at enrollment: **303 (22.6%) had ACLF**; 28-day mortality **33.9%**, 90-day **51.2%**.

### Post-enrollment ACLF and the whole cohort

- Of the 1,040 without ACLF at enrollment, **112 (10.8%) developed it within 28 days** (median 5 days). Post-enrollment ACLF carried **29.6% vs 1.9%** 28-day and **51.1% vs 9.7%** 90-day mortality.
- Whole group (enrollment or during 28-day follow-up): **415 (30.9%)** — grade 1 **213 (15.8%)**, grade 2 **146 (10.9%)**, grade 3 **56 (4.4%)**. 28-day mortality 23.3% / 31.3% / 74.5%; 90-day 40.8% / 55.2% / 78.4%.
- Independent predictors of *developing* ACLF after enrollment: **higher CLIF-SOFA score, higher leukocyte count, and ascites**, all measured at enrollment.

### Performance and nature of the syndrome

- CLIF-SOFA was **as accurate as MELD and more accurate than Child–Pugh** for 28-day mortality. Bootstrap-validated odds ratio for a 1-point rise in CLIF-SOFA **1.557 (95% CI 1.459–1.672)**; area under the receiver operating characteristic curve **0.831**.
- **No precipitating event identified in 43.6%** of ACLF patients; among those identified, bacterial infection and active alcoholism were commonest. **Type and presence of precipitant were not related to mortality.**
- **ACLF without prior AD was more lethal** than ACLF in previously decompensated patients (28-day 42.2% vs 29.6%, P = .03). Previous AD was absent in 23.2% of ACLF patients.
- Leukocyte count and C-reactive protein rose in parallel with ACLF grade, including when analysis was restricted to non-infected patients — the evidence base for calling ACLF an inflammatory syndrome.
- Organ-failure prevalence differs by grade: **kidney failure predominates in grade 1**; in **grade 2 liver failure is commonest**, then kidney, cerebral and coagulation; in **grade 3 all organ failures are frequent**.
- Main cause of death across the whole ACLF group at 90 days: **multiple organ failure with septic or hypovolaemic shock (37%)**, then septic shock (23.4%) and hypovolaemic shock (7.2%).

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Mortality figures differ from those already on the page, and both are correct for their own endpoint.** [[aasld-2024-aclf]] prints **30-day** mortality from a CLIF-SOFA *validation* cohort (no ACLF 3%, grade 1 10%, grade 2 37%, grade 3 76%); this paper gives **28-day** mortality from the *derivation* cohort (4.7 / 22.1 / 32.0 / 76.7%). Different follow-up window, different cohort — not a disagreement. The guidance is tier 1 and keeps the page's headline table; the derivation figures sit beside the grade definitions they belong to.
- **Terminology:** this paper writes "ACLF grade 1/2/3"; the wiki and AASLD write "ACLF-1/-2/-3". Same strata.
- **Derivation only — no external validation in this paper.** The classification is built from the cohort it describes; the authors state the cut-offs follow from a pre-specified 15% mortality threshold, not from an independent test set. The CLIF-C ACLF prognostic score (a separate, later instrument) is not derived here.
- **European cirrhosis cohort.** Hepatitis B reactivation, a common ACLF precipitant in Asia, was "extremely infrequent" in this population — the grade definitions are not population-specific, but the precipitant mix is.
- **Not a management document.** CANONIC defines and grades; it recommends no treatment, and carries no GRADE ratings or numbered recommendations.
