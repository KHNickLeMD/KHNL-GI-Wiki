## Summary

AGA Institute guideline on **asymptomatic** neoplastic pancreatic cysts, developed under GRADE and accompanied by a technical review (Scheiman, Hwang, Moayyedi — *Gastroenterology* 2015;148:824–848). Its defining feature is that **every single recommendation rests on very-low-quality evidence**: nearly all data came from retrospective case series with major unexplained heterogeneity, none of which directly measured reduced mortality from pancreatic adenocarcinoma. The panel states plainly that "a reasonable argument can be advanced that no recommendations regarding the management of pancreatic cysts can be made," and that "it is unclear that the benefits of surveillance outweigh the risks for most patients."

**Scope is narrow and must be respected.** The guideline covers only *asymptomatic* cysts found on radiology in adults. It explicitly **excludes** solid papillary neoplasms, cystic degeneration of adenocarcinomas, neuroendocrine tumors, and **main-duct IPMNs without side-branch involvement** — because for these the accepted approach is surgical resection in a suitable candidate, so they are less challenging. It also did not evaluate how symptoms change management. Where a comparator is not stated in a recommendation, the implied comparator is "do nothing."

The central quantitative argument is that the **baseline risk is very low**. Pancreatic cysts are found in ~15% of patients undergoing abdominal MRI for other indications and may reach 25% in those over 70. From SEER data, an incidentally seen cyst carries a **10 in 100,000** chance of being a mucinous invasive malignancy and a **17 in 100,000** chance of being a ductal cancer; the malignant-transformation rate is **~0.24% per year**. Against that, surgery carries **2% postoperative mortality and 30% major morbidity** in the literature reviewed (SEER, reflecting all US pancreatic surgery, reports **6.6%** postoperative mortality), and 5-year survival with invasive cancer is only ~28%.

The panel drew four deliberate breaks from all previously published cyst guidelines, and says so in its own Summary: a **2-year surveillance interval for a cyst of any size**, **stopping surveillance after 5 years** if unchanged, **surgery only if more than one concerning feature** on MRI and confirmed on EUS and only at high-volume centres, and **no surveillance after surgery** if there was no invasive cancer or dysplasia. It anticipated controversy, since all four move toward less testing and a higher threshold to intervene.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Recommendations — all 10, verbatim, with the document's own strength and quality

The document numbers its recommendations 1–10 and prints each in a boxed statement. **Recommendation 1 carries no GRADE rating** — the panel calls it a "motherhood statement that does not require application of the GRADE system." Recommendation 8 is the **only Strong** recommendation in the document. Every other statement is Conditional, and **every graded statement is Very low quality**.

### The three high-risk features, and the "at least 2" combination rule

Recommendation 3 names exactly three high-risk features and requires **at least 2 of them together** before EUS-FNA:

| Feature | Effect on malignancy risk (technical review, *unselected* cysts) |
|---|---|
| Size **≥3 cm** | ~**3×** increased risk |
| Associated **solid component** | ~**8×** increased risk |
| **Dilated main pancreatic duct** | **No statistically significant association** in the AGA review — included anyway on the strength of systematic-review findings in *resected* IPMNs |

- The combination rule is the recommendation. One feature alone does not meet the Rec 3 threshold, though the panel concedes "some clinicians and patients may elect to evaluate the cyst with just one high-risk feature present, such as a solid component if this is particularly prominent."
- Evidence graded very low because of unexplained between-study variation and because the populations studied were highly selected — patients who went to pancreatic resection.
- An 8× relative increase is substantial, but "given the very low baseline risk, the absolute effect is modest."

### Test performance and outcome figures

- **EUS-FNA for malignancy:** sensitivity ~**60%**, specificity ~**90%**. Sensitivity is modest but "more than counterbalanced by the low prevalence of malignancy" — the NPV of an unremarkable EUS-FNA, while not 100%, is high.
- **Combination of high-risk features at surgery (Rec 7):** specificity likely **>95%**.
- **Malignant transformation:** **~0.24% per year** across all cysts, including those that change over time.
- **Prevalence of malignancy in resected cysts:** **15%** harboured invasive malignancy, in a highly selected surgical population; **~17%** of resected IPMNs harboured HGD.
- **Surgical risk:** postoperative mortality **2%** (centres of excellence, technical review) vs **6.6%** (SEER, all US pancreatic surgery); major morbidity **30%**.
- **5-year survival with invasive cancer:** ~**28%**, possibly inflated by lead- and length-time bias.
- **Cyst prevalence:** ~**15%** on abdominal MRI done for other indications; up to **25%** over age 70.
- **Risk an incidental cyst is malignant (SEER):** **10/100,000** mucinous invasive; **17/100,000** ductal cancer.

### Why MRI, and why these intervals

- **MRI preferred over CT** for surveillance: no radiation, and it better demonstrates the structural relationship between the pancreatic duct and the cyst. MRI is also less invasive than EUS.
- **The interval itself is not evidence-based.** The panel states outright that the 1-year-then-2-year schedule "is not based on any evidence but is believed to be reasonable given the small absolute risk of malignancy."
- **Rec 6 exit criteria, both qualifying:** no significant change after 5 years of surveillance, **or** the patient is no longer a surgical candidate. Some patients may reasonably continue longer given a strong family history of pancreatic cancer or equivocal changes in a cyst with high-risk features.
- **Rec 1 non-candidates:** surveillance is "inappropriate for patients who are not surgical candidates because of age or severe comorbidities," and patients with limited life expectancy are unlikely to benefit.
- **Rec 10 scope limit:** the no-post-op-surveillance statement "refers to those with no mixed duct IPMN and no strong family history of pancreatic cancer."

### Positions taken on other tests

- **Molecular/cyst-fluid markers:** "Molecular techniques to evaluate pancreatic cysts remain an emerging area of research, and the diagnostic utility of these tests is uncertain." No recommendation for or against.
- The document gives **no cyst-fluid CEA or amylase cutoffs**, and **no cyst-typing criteria** (IPMN vs MCN vs SCA vs pseudocyst). Those live on [[pancreatic-cysts]] from [[acg-2018-pancreatic-cysts]].

## Contradictions / Open Questions

**[[acg-2018-pancreatic-cysts]] is newer within tier 1 and governs [[pancreatic-cysts]] wherever the two differ.** AGA 2015 is recorded as the contrasting position, not as the page's operative guidance. The divergences are substantive, not cosmetic — this is the same document ACG 2018 was in part written to answer.

| Decision | AGA 2015 | [[acg-2018-pancreatic-cysts\|ACG 2018]] (governs) |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger for EUS-FNA | **At least 2** of: size ≥3 cm, dilated MPD, solid component (Rec 3) | **Any one** high-risk feature (Rec 11) |
| Surveillance interval | 1 year, then **every 2 years for any size** (Rec 2) | **Size-stratified**, from q2 years (<1 cm) to q6 months alternating MRI/EUS (>3 cm) |
| Stopping surveillance | **Stop at 5 years** if unchanged (Rec 6) | **Insufficient evidence to stop at 5 years** in a still-fit patient; stop when no longer a surgical candidate, reassess at >75 |
| Post-resection, no HGD/cancer | **Against** routine surveillance (Rec 10) | Against for MCN and benign cysts, but **all resected IPMN require surveillance** (Rec 17–20) |
| Surgery threshold | Solid component **and** dilated MPD, and/or concerning EUS-FNA (Rec 7) | Multidisciplinary referral on **any** Rec 11 feature |

- **Both guidelines agree** that surgery belongs at a high-volume centre — and this is the one point AGA graded **Strong**.
- **Dilated MPD as a risk factor is internally contested within AGA 2015 itself.** The panel found **no statistically significant association** with malignancy in unselected cysts, yet kept it as one of the three high-risk features on the basis of resected-IPMN series. Any use of this feature should carry that caveat.
- **Scope gap, not a disagreement:** AGA 2015 excludes main-duct IPMN without side-branch involvement, neuroendocrine tumours and solid pseudopapillary neoplasms. It cannot be cited for any of those; [[acg-2018-pancreatic-cysts]] covers them.
- **The guideline's own stated uncertainty is a finding.** With all evidence at very low quality and the panel conceding it is unclear whether surveillance benefits most patients, no AGA 2015 number should be presented on a wiki page as a firm threshold.
- **Figure/decision tool:** the guideline cross-references an accompanying "Clinical Decision Tool and Technical Review (pages 823–824)". That tool is a **separate document** and is not part of the four-page guideline in `raw/`; its criteria are therefore not reproduced here.
