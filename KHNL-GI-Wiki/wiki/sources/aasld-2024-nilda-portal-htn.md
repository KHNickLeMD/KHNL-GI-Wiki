# AASLD Practice Guideline on Noninvasive Liver Disease Assessment of Portal Hypertension (2024)

## Summary

American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD) guideline on using noninvasive liver disease assessment (NILDA) — blood-based markers, platelet count, liver stiffness measurement (LSM), spleen stiffness measurement (SSM), magnetic resonance elastography (MRE) — to detect **clinically significant portal hypertension (CSPH)** in chronic liver disease (CLD). Hepatic venous pressure gradient (HVPG) is the reference standard: portal hypertension = HVPG >5 mmHg; CSPH = HVPG ≥10 mmHg. **Variceal screening is explicitly outside its scope** ("intentionally not addressed here"). Imaging-based NILDAs are the suggested primary approach; blood-based markers lack sensitivity.

---

## Guideline Statements

| # | Statement | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Adults with CLD: AASLD **advises against** blood-based markers or thrombocytopenia **alone** to detect CSPH (HVPG ≥10 mmHg) | Conditional, low-quality evidence |
| 2 | Adults with CLD: AASLD **suggests** combining **LSM and platelet count** to assess for CSPH | Conditional, low-quality evidence |
| 3 | Adults with CLD: AASLD **suggests against** using LSM to quantify higher degrees of portal hypertension (HVPG ≥12 mmHg) | Conditional, low-quality evidence |
| 4 | Serial LSM and platelet count **may be followed** to monitor for progression to CSPH in adults with CLD without baseline CSPH whose underlying etiology is active/uncontrolled | Ungraded |
| 5 | Adults with cirrhosis on treatment for their liver disease or known portal hypertension: AASLD **suggests caution** using LSM or SSM to detect longitudinal change in portal hypertension (HVPG) | Conditional, low-quality evidence |
| 6 | CLD with CSPH: **insufficient evidence** to use blood- or imaging-based NILDAs to predict clinical outcomes | Ungraded |

## Key Findings / Claims

**Figure 1 algorithm (figure paywalled; thresholds from its caption and the text below it, p. 20):**
- **LSM <15 kPa + platelets >150,000/mcl** → essentially rules out CSPH (text; the caption prints platelets ≥150,000 "further suggests the absence of CSPH"; LSM <15 with platelets <150,000 → CSPH uncertain).
- **LSM ≥25 kPa** → rules in CSPH, absent confounders (right heart failure, severe hepatic inflammation).
- **LSM 10–15 kPa with platelets <150,000/mcl, or LSM 15–25 kPa with platelets >150,000/mcl** → CSPH possible; after excluding other causes of thrombocytopenia, further testing: **MRE ≥5.1 kPa**, **SSM ≥40 kPa**, esophagogastroduodenoscopy (varices, if present, indicate CSPH), or direct HVPG.
- **LSM 15–25 kPa = "gray zone"** (clinical judgement; other testing helpful): within it, CSPH more likely with **platelets ≤110,000/mcl**, as with **LSM ≥20 kPa and platelets <150,000/mcl** (caption).
- Platelets cannot be integrated in primary hematologic or splenic disorders.
- Best applied to **active** primary liver disease; after HBV suppression or HCV direct-acting antiviral therapy, thresholds likely differ.

**Other thresholds:**
- Technical remarks: LSM ≥25 kPa or ≤15 kPa can rule in or rule out CSPH (the remarks print ≤15; the Figure 1 caption prints <15). Between 15 and 25 kPa: LSM ≥15 kPa + platelets <110,000/mcl suggests CSPH but needs clinical judgement.
- Technical remarks: **SSM is rapidly evolving; SSM >40 kPa may be used to detect CSPH** (text under Figure 1: ≥40 kPa). Imaging section (p. 4): SSM is less likely than LSM to be affected by elimination of injurious agents.
- Changes in portal hypertension (pp. 15–18) — after HCV eradication (European study): post-treatment LSM <12 kPa + platelets >150×10³/mcl had 99% sensitivity for excluding CSPH; in a validation cohort, LSM <12 kPa + platelets >150×10³/mcl → no decompensation within 3 years; LSM ≥25 kPa → 3-year decompensation risk 10%; 13–24 kPa = "gray zone".

---

## Clinical Relevance

Defines the LSM + platelet thresholds for detecting CSPH (Statement 2, Figure 1) and when to escalate to MRE, SSM, endoscopy or HVPG. It does **not** address variceal screening — decisions on screening endoscopy rest on other guidance (see [[variceal-upper-gi-bleeding]]).
