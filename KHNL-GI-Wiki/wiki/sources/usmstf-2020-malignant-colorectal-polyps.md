## Summary

- **Scope:** endoscopic recognition and management of the **malignant polyp** — a colorectal lesion with cancer invading the submucosa but not the muscularis propria (pT1, 8th-edition American Joint Committee on Cancer [AJCC] staging). Synonym: *submucosally invasive polyp/lesion*. Cancer prevalence in colorectal polyps: 0.2%–5%.
- **Three tasks, 6 key questions (KQs):** (1) recognize deep submucosal invasion (SMI) → refer straight to surgery; (2) choose resection technique and specimen handling when *superficial* SMI is suspected; (3) weigh surgery vs surveillance once an endoscopically removed polyp proves invasive.
- **Excluded:** polyps in inflammatory bowel disease. Resection technique for large polyps is covered by a separate USMSTF document ([[usmstf-2020-endoscopic-removal]]).
- **Grading:** USMSTF adaptation of Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE). Evidence quality high / moderate / low / very low (Table 1 labels A–D); recommendation **strong** ("we recommend") or **weak** ("we suggest"). Nine labelled recommendations: 1a, 1b, 2a, 2b, 3, 4a, 4b, 5, 6. The grade line under each uses the words "Strong/Weak recommendation; high-/moderate-/low-quality evidence" (no A–D letters).
- **Core logic:** deep SMI (≥1 mm) → 10%–18% risk of residual cancer in bowel wall or lymph nodes after endoscopic resection → generally an indication for surgery. Superficial SMI (<1 mm) → lymph node metastasis (LNM) 0%–4% when no other adverse histology. Endoscopic features predict depth; *en bloc* resection + pinned specimen lets pathology measure it.
- **Multidisciplinary:** gastroenterologist, pathologist, surgeon, ± oncologist, and patient; the endoscopist often coordinates.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Definitions

- **Malignant polyp** = pT1 [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer (CRC)]]: neoplastic invasion of the submucosa, no extension into muscularis propria (Figure 1 of the source: Tis → T4 depth diagram).
- **Not cancer:** high-grade dysplasia confined to epithelium, lamina propria, or muscularis mucosae = **"Tis"** (AJCC; National Comprehensive Cancer Network [NCCN]).
  - Precancerous; no risk of residual neoplasia in bowel wall or lymph nodes after complete endoscopic resection → **endoscopic resection alone**.
  - Endoscopists and pathologists should avoid "carcinoma"/"cancer" for mucosa-confined lesions (causes alarm, unnecessary surgery).
- **Deep SMI** = **≥1 mm (1,000 µm)** of submucosal invasion → 10%–18% residual cancer risk after endoscopic resection; generally an indication for adjuvant surgery.
- **Superficial SMI** = **<1 mm** → LNM 0%–4%, provided other adverse histologic features are absent.
- No endoscopic sign has high sensitivity or specificity for superficial SMI; features that raise its risk: **size ≥2 cm**, depressed or sessile morphology in nongranular laterally spreading tumors (LST-NG), discrete nodules in granular laterally spreading tumors (LST-G) (see below).

**Vienna classification of gastrointestinal epithelial neoplasia (Table 2)** — malignant polyp = category **5.2**:

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| 1 | Negative for neoplasia/dysplasia |
| 2 | Indefinite for neoplasia/dysplasia |
| 3 | Noninvasive low-grade neoplasia (low-grade adenoma/dysplasia) |
| 4 | Noninvasive high-grade neoplasia: 4.1 high-grade adenoma/dysplasia · 4.2 noninvasive carcinoma (carcinoma in situ) · 4.3 suspicion of invasive carcinoma |
| 5 | Invasive neoplasia: 5.1 intramucosal carcinoma (invasion into lamina propria or muscularis mucosae) · 5.2 submucosal carcinoma or beyond |

### Endoscopic Classification Systems

- Every lesion: assess morphology, surface, and vessel pattern before resection; dye-based chromoendoscopy or electronic image enhancement — narrow-band imaging (NBI), blue light imaging, linked color imaging, i-scan — ± optical magnification.

**NBI International Colorectal Endoscopic (NICE) classification (Table 3)** — usable with or without magnification, no dye spray:

| Feature | Type 1 | Type 2 | Type 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Color | Same or lighter than background | Brown relative to background | Brown or black relative to background |
| Vessels | None or isolated lacy vessels | Brown vessels surrounding white structures | Areas of disrupted or missing vessels |
| Surface pattern | Dark or white spots of uniform size | Oval, tubular, or branched white structures | Amorphous or absence of pattern |
| Most likely histology | Hyperplastic or serrated polyp (sessile serrated polyp) | Adenoma to superficial SMI | **Deep SMI** |

- NICE 3 is specific but not sensitive for deep submucosal invasive cancer.

**Japan NBI Expert Team (JNET) classification (Table 4; Figure 3)** — requires magnification; keeps NICE types 1 and 3, splits type 2 into 2A and 2B:

| Feature | Type 1 | Type 2A | Type 2B | Type 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vessel pattern | Invisible | Regular caliber, regular distribution | Variable caliber, irregular distribution | Loose vessel areas, interruption of thick vessels |
| Surface pattern | Regular dark or white spots, similar to surrounding mucosa | Regular tubular, branched, or papillary | Irregular or obscure | Amorphous areas |
| Most likely histology | Hyperplastic polyp or sessile serrated polyp | Low-grade intramucosal neoplasia | High-grade intramucosal neoplasia / superficial SMI cancer | **Deep SMI cancer** |

- Figure 3 subdivides 2B: **2B-low** — irregular vessels of uniform thickness/distribution; pit-like network irregular with smooth margins. **2B-high** — vessel diameter/distribution heterogeneous; irregular, destroyed pit-like pattern with fluffy, unclear margins. Images embedded on [[polypectomy]].
- JNET 2B → higher risk of superficial SMI → consider *en bloc* resection where feasible. Accuracy without full optical magnification uncertain; early studies promising.

**Kudo pit pattern (Table 5; Figure 2)** — requires magnification colonoscopy with dye spray (many Western endoscopists use it without dye spray):

| Type | Features | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| I | Round, normal | Normal |
| II | Asteroid | Hyperplastic |
| IIIS | Tubular or round pit smaller than normal pit | Tubular adenoma |
| IIIL | Tubular or round pit larger than normal pit | Tubular adenoma |
| IV | Gyrus/dendritic | Tubulovillous or villous adenoma |
| VI | Irregular arrangement (and sizes of IIIL/IIIS/IV pits) | Neoplastic, invasive |
| VN | Loss or decrease of pits with amorphous structure | Neoplastic, invasive |

- Types I–II: normal, serrated, or inflammatory; III–V: dysplastic and malignant change.
- Other NBI magnifying systems (Hiroshima, Sano, Showa, Jikei; Sano IIIA/IIIB) and BASIC (blue light imaging) exist but are little used in the US.

**Paris classification (Figure 4)** — type 0 superficial lesions: polypoid **0-Ip** (pedunculated), **0-Is** (sessile); non-polypoid **0-IIa** (slightly elevated), **0-IIb** (flat), **0-IIc** (slightly depressed); excavated **0-III** (rare in colon). Interobserver agreement among experts only modest.

**LSTs** — >10 mm, flat (0-II) or sessile (0-Is), growing laterally along the wall (Figures 5–6):

| Subtype | Surface | SMI risk (source data) |
|---|---|---|
| LST-G, homogeneous (even-sized nodules) | Granular, nodular | **<2%**; can grow very large; low fibrosis risk |
| LST-G, mixed-sized nodules | Granular, one or more larger nodules | **7.1%** if <20 mm, **38%** if larger (cut-point printed as >20 mm in one passage, >30 mm in another); invasion usually under the largest nodule |
| LST-NG, flat elevated | Smooth | **6.4%** (10–19 mm), **10.4%** (20–29 mm) |
| LST-NG, pseudodepressed | Smooth, depressed area | **27.8%** (10–19 mm), **41.4%** (20–29 mm); often multifocal invasion |

- LST-NG with depression/ulceration (181 lesions): **deep** SMI 12.5% (10–19 mm), 32.4% (20–29 mm), 83.3% (≥30 mm).
- LST-NG often has submucosal fibrosis → simple snare or standard endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR) technically harder.
- Other series: LST-NG more often right-sided and invasive than LST-G (82 LSTs); SMI 7% LST-G vs 14% LST-NG (511 LSTs); LST-G nodules >10 mm → SMI 29.8% vs 2% (*P* < .0001); pseudodepressed vs flat elevated LST-NG 42.1% vs 6.1% (1,363 LSTs ≥10 mm; *P* < .01).

**Nonlifting sign** (no lift after submucosal injection): associated with deep SMI, positive predictive value (PPV) ~80% in sessile polyps. Also caused by fibrosis from prior biopsy, cautery, or tattoo → then **not** a sign of deep invasion and **not a contraindication** to endoscopic resection.

### KQ1 — Endoscopic Prediction of Deep SMI

- **NICE 3:** validation, 80 images, 5 expert endoscopists — any 1 of the 3 NICE-3 features (color, vessels, surface) → **94% accuracy, 96% negative predictive value (NPV)** for deep SMI.
- **Kudo V:** 479 patients with polyps ≥20 mm — deep SMI in **56% (14/25)** with pit pattern V vs 4%–5% with other patterns.
- Follow-up, 2,693 lesions: Kudo V strongest factor for overt SMI cancer — odds ratio (OR) printed as 1.42 with 95% confidence interval (CI) 8.57–23.4 (point estimate falls outside its CI as printed); specificity 97%, sensitivity 40%, accuracy 93%.
- **Kudo VN** meta-analysis (20 studies; sub-analysis 1,623 lesions from 4 studies): pooled sensitivity **90.4%** (95% CI 79.7–95.7), specificity **88.4%** (82.9–92.3).
- NICE 3 / Kudo VN often coincide with surface ulceration and irregularity.
- **Nonpedunculated + NICE 3 or Kudo VN** → biopsy the area of surface disruption, tattoo unless in/near cecum, direct to surgery.
- **Pedunculated (0-Ip) + deep-SMI features** → still endoscopic resection: overall histology may be favorable. Resect *en bloc* through the stalk; pathology bivalves through head and stalk.

### KQ2 — Predictors of Superficial SMI; Resection Method

- **Depressed (0-IIc):** invasive even when small; 61% of 0-IIc lesions had SMI (3,680 lesions).
- **Paris type in 2,277 lesions ≥20 mm** (vs 0-IIa):
  - 0-Is → OR 2.73 (95% CI 1.64–4.55); 0-IIa+Is → OR 2.49 (1.52–4.08) for SMI.
  - Any 0-IIc component → specificity **95.9%**, accuracy **90.3%**, sensitivity 21% for SMI.
  - Covert SMI (cancer without endoscopic invasive features): 0-Is nongranular OR **22.5** (7.07–71.6); 0-IIa+Is nongranular OR **14.4** (4.53–45.5).
- **Size** (11,188 adenomatous lesions; invasive cancer in 1,313 = 11.7%): vs ≤5 mm, OR **4.27** (3.06–5.96) for >16 mm, OR **10** (6.97–14.56) for >35 mm. Separate series of 1,354 polypectomies: 28 (2.1%) invasive; 71% of these >20 mm.
- Size or location alone does not discriminate enough; combine with endoscopic features.
- **Location:** malignant polyps mostly right colon, with predilection for rectosigmoid (e.g., 61/116 in sigmoid; 41 of the other 55 in rectum; 58/81 sigmoid; 32.9% rectum, 17.9% right colon; 103/479 [21.5%] SMI in right colon). In 2,277 lesions ≥20 mm: size per 10 mm OR 1.12 (1.01–1.23) and rectosigmoid location OR 1.91 (1.31–2.79) for overt and covert SMI.
- **Rectum favors *en bloc*:** thicker wall and part below the peritoneal reflection → *en bloc* relatively safe; rectal operations after endoscopic resection of a malignant polyp are more morbid than colonic resections.
- **En bloc + proper sectioning** → depth measurable; superficial SMI may spare surgery that piecemeal resection of the same lesion would have triggered.
- LST-NG with unfavorable morphology is often small → candidate for *en bloc* EMR. Large LST-G with dominant nodule: experts resect the nodule *en bloc*, submit separately, remove the rest piecemeal.
- *En bloc* beyond the endoscopist's skill → refer to a center with expertise. **Endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD):** highest *en bloc* rates; vs EMR, more complications (incl. perforation), cost, post-procedure hospitalization, longer learning curve, poor reimbursement. Final approach weighs size, morphology, location, local expertise.

**Algorithm (Figure 9)** — screenshot embedded on [[polypectomy]]:

- **Pedunculated** → *en bloc* resection → benign = surveillance; submucosal cancer → favorable histology = surveillance, unfavorable = surgical evaluation.
- **Large (≥1 cm) nonpedunculated, NICE III or Kudo V or VI** (as printed) → biopsy + tattoo → surgical evaluation.
- **NICE I/II, granular:** dominant nodule(s) → attempt *en bloc* resection + pin specimen; no dominant nodule → EMR (piecemeal if >20 mm).
- **NICE I/II, non-granular:** flat, non-depressed → EMR → surveillance; sessile or depressed/ulcerated → attempt *en bloc* + pin specimen.
- After attempted *en bloc*: en bloc + superficial SMI + no unfavorable criteria → surveillance; piecemeal or unfavorable criteria → surgical evaluation.
- Footnotes: tattoo not required for cecal polyps; place it near the polyp base and on the opposite lumen wall. If complete removal unlikely → tattoo near the polyp and refer to a more experienced center.

### KQ3 — Specimen Handling

- Requisition: location, size, morphology (sessile vs pedunculated).
- **Pedunculated:** retrieve via suction channel or net/snare; do **not** cut large *en bloc* specimens to fit the channel; pathology bisects through head and stalk; cautery burn marks the stalk; pin the stalk before formalin if it retracts. Head removed piecemeal → submit stalk (± attached head) separately.
- **Nonpedunculated, *en bloc* (EMR or ESD):** pin fresh specimen onto a firm surface with stainless-steel pins around the whole circumference → 10% formalin (Figure 10, embedded on [[polypectomy]]). Section perpendicular to the resection plane, usually the entire lesion at **2-mm intervals**.
- Unpinned fixation → shrinkage, curling, lost orientation → margins and depth not assessable.

### Histologic Depth Classifications

**Kikuchi (sessile lesions; Figure 7)** — submucosa divided into thirds:

| Level | Invasion | LNM risk | Management implied |
|---|---|---|---|
| SM1 | Superficial third | 1%–3% | Endoscopic resection + close surveillance |
| SM2 | Middle third | 8% | Surgical resection warranted |
| SM3 | Deepest third | 23% | Surgical resection warranted |

- Endoscopic specimens usually lack muscularis propria → SM level often indeterminable → largely replaced by **depth measured with an optical micrometer** (<1 mm superficial; ≥1 mm deep).
- Accurate depth requires pinning the specimen to a stiff material before formalin so sections are cut at right angles to the resection plane.

**Haggitt (pedunculated polyps; Figure 8):**

| Level | Extent |
|---|---|
| 0 | Dysplasia limited to mucosa |
| 1 | Invasion into submucosa, limited to the polyp head |
| 2 | Reaches the neck |
| 3 | Invades the stalk |
| 4 | Invades submucosa below the stalk, not muscularis propria |

- Every malignant **nonpedunculated** lesion is level 4 by definition.
- One study, 150 surgically treated polyps with submucosal cancer: 6% of pedunculated polyps had LNM; levels 1–3 → no LNM; level 4 → **27%**.
- Endoscopists transect through the stalk, not below it → in practice the **resection margin** matters more than Haggitt level for pedunculated polyps.

### KQ4 — Histologic Predictors of LNM and Residual/Recurrent Cancer

- **Unfavorable for both pedunculated and nonpedunculated:** poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma, lymphovascular invasion (LVI), tumor budding. **Pedunculated only:** resection margin <1 mm. **Nonpedunculated:** width of resection margin also important.
- Depth of invasion matters most for nonpedunculated polyps; resection margin for pedunculated.
- **Pooled pT1 studies (17 studies, 3,621 patients):** independent LNM predictors — lymphatic invasion relative risk (RR) 5.2 (95% CI 4.0–6.8); SMI >1 mm RR 5.2 (1.8–15.4); tumor budding RR 5.1 (CI misprinted in the document).
- **Nonpedunculated only (5 studies, 1,213 patients):** LNM **13%** (95% CI 11.5%–15.4%). Absence of LNM associated with SMI <1 mm (RR 1.15; 1.11–1.18), well-differentiated histology (RR 3.99; 1.18–13.46 vs poorly), no lymphatic or vascular invasion (RR 1.26; 1.18–1.34 and RR 1.16; 1.07–1.24), no tumor budding (RR 1.16; 1.08–1.25).
- **653 surgically resected pT1 cancers:** LNM 9.2%; LVI OR 9.84 (3.42–28.3), tumor budding OR 1.80 (1.01–3.21), poor differentiation OR 2.31 (1.25–4.27).
- **NCCN high-risk features** (cited): positive margins (<1 mm or indeterminate), histologic grade 3 or 4, LVI, tumor budding.

**Depth of SMI (nonpedunculated):**

- Requires *en bloc* resection, proper specimen handling, optical micrometer. **≥1 mm** = widely accepted cutoff for deep SMI and higher LNM.
- Meta-analysis (described as 7,376 polyps in one passage, 13 studies / 7,066 patients in another): deep SMI (>1 mm, or ≥SM2 by Kikuchi) → LNM OR **3.00** (1.36–6.62).
- 6 Japanese centers: SMI ≥1 mm → LNM OR 5.4 (1.62–17.93). Systematic review, 23 studies / 4,510 patients: deep SMI (>1 mm) → OR 3.87 (1.50–10.00).

**Resection margin:**

- Clear margin, 60 malignant polyps: no local recurrence at 5 years. Later advice: margin **≥1 mm, ideally 2 mm**.
- Proximity of tumor to margin = key factor for pedunculated polyps.
- Margin ≥1 mm → local cancer and/or LNM **0%–2%**; margin <1 mm → **21%–33%** (endoscopic then surgical resection).
- 147 patients, EMR then surgery: residual malignancy **16%** with margin <1 mm, **21%** indeterminate, **0%** ≥1 mm.
- 85 patients (mean follow-up 67 months): margin not cancer-free or indeterminate → adverse outcome OR 20.2 (2.6–998).
- 31 studies / 1,900 patients: positive margin → unfavorable outcome (residual, recurrence, LNM, hematogenous spread, death) pooled OR **22** (10.3–46.6).
- European definition of positive margin: malignant cells <1 mm from margin. Polypectomy artifact can overcall: 105 patients with surgery — 63% of margins not tumor-free, residual carcinoma at surgery only 2.8%.

**Grade of differentiation:**

- Meta-analysis, 13 studies / 7,066 patients: poorly differentiated → LNM OR **8.27** (4.6–14.6).
- 31 studies / 1,900 patients: poor differentiation → residual disease OR 2.2 (1.1–4.8); LNM (surgical subgroup) OR 3.9 (2–7.9).

**LVI:**

- Definitions vary; interobserver variability high.
- 16 case series / 351 patients with surgery: **45%** of patients with LVI had LNM.
- 8 studies (meta-analysis): LVI → LNM OR **5.47** (2.46–12.17). 865 patients, 6 Japanese centers: lymphatic invasion OR 4.7 (2.77–7.95). 31 studies / 1,900 patients: submucosal vascular invasion OR 7 (2.6–19.2).

**Tumor budding:**

- Document's definition: isolated cancer cells or a cluster of **≤5** cancer cells at the invasive margin, counted in the hotspot. International Tumor Budding Consensus Conference (ITBCC) 2016 definition: single tumor cell or cluster of **≤4** cells; independent LNM predictor, separate from grade, to be part of CRC reporting.
- Historically no consensus cutoff; likely reported as poor differentiation in the past.
- 98 malignant polyps: LNM OR 20.1 (1.6–246.5). Meta-analysis, 13 studies / 7,066 patients: OR **4.59** (3.44–6.13). 290 patients: budding in 42% with LNM vs 18% without (OR 2.3; 1.1–5.0).
- Reporting recommended by AJCC and the updated 2018 College of American Pathologists (CAP) protocol, but **not a required core element** of synoptic reporting.

**Unfavorable histologic criteria (Figure 9 footnote)** → surgical evaluation:

- Depth of SMI >1 mm (deep SMI defined in the text as ≥1 mm)
- Polypectomy margin positive for tumor cells
- Poor grade of differentiation
- Tumor budding
- LVI

**Also an indication for surgery:** any cancer in a nonpedunculated or pedunculated lesion resected **piecemeal**, or in a pedunculated polyp that could **not be oriented** for pathology. Any histologic risk factor = general indication for adjuvant surgery, weighed against surgical risk and comorbidity.

### KQ5 — Pathology Reporting

- Use CAP templates (synoptic checklists reduce omissions). Report tumor site; endoscopist supplies it on the requisition and in the procedure report.
- Specimen integrity (allows margin assessment); polyp size; morphology (pedunculated vs sessile).
- Histologic subtype per World Health Organization (WHO) classification.
- Grade, 4 tiers (well, moderately, poorly, undifferentiated); **worst area** sets the grade.
- Size of invasive component, with greatest tumor extent.
- Submucosal invasion level by optical micrometer, **particularly for nonpedunculated** polyps.
- Deep margin: distance of invasive carcinoma from the deepest resection margin (usually mm).
- LVI present/absent (processing or thermoelectric artifact can prevent assessment); tumor budding if observed (hotspot).

### KQ6 — Multidisciplinary Management

- Team: gastroenterologist, pathologist, oncologist, surgeon; ± primary care and other specialists (e.g., cardiologist) when comorbidity or life expectancy matters. Endoscopist ready to coordinate.
- Core question: adjuvant surgery or not — residual/metastatic cancer risk vs surgical risk. Overall mortality after colon cancer surgery **1%–8%**, rising with age and comorbidity.
- Individualize on patient factors (age, comorbidity, preferences) and polyp factors (size, histology). Illustrative scenarios from the document:
  - Healthy patient + any unfavorable criterion → surgery generally advisable.
  - Poor surgical candidate + no unfavorable features → avoid surgery.
  - Elderly with several comorbidities → forgoing surgery may be reasonable even with an unfavorable feature.
  - Young, very low surgical risk → may choose surgery even with all-favorable histology.
  - Healthy middle-aged patient, large sessile malignant polyp with deep SMI or other unfavorable features → surgery appropriate.
  - Residual-cancer risk ≈ surgical mortality → shared decision-making.
- Summary decision inputs: polyp shape, *en bloc* resection + adequate histologic assessment, unfavorable features, surgical mortality/morbidity, patient preference.

## Recommendations (all 9, verbatim)

### Recommendation 1a
>
> "We recommend that both pedunculated and nonpedunculated polyps with the following features be considered to have deep submucosal invasion: NICE classification type 3 or Kudo classification of type V (VN and VI)."
> **Strong recommendation; high-quality evidence**

### Recommendation 1b
>
> "Nonpedunculated lesions with these features should be biopsied (in the area of surface feature disruption), tattooed (unless in or near the cecum), and referred to surgery. Pedunculated polyps with features of deep submucosal invasion should undergo endoscopic polypectomy."
> **Weak recommendation; low-quality evidence**

### Recommendation 2a
>
> "LST-NG morphology with sessile shape or depression, and LST-G with a dominant nodule predict a higher risk of submucosally invasive cancer."
> **Weak recommendation; moderate-quality evidence**

### Recommendation 2b
>
> "We recommend that such lesions be considered for *en bloc* endoscopic resection, instead of piecemeal resection, when feasible and based on local expertise. In the case of LST-G with a dominant nodule, at least the nodular area should be considered for *en bloc* resection. All pedunculated polyps, even if large, should be resected *en bloc*."
> **Weak recommendation; low-quality evidence**

### Recommendation 3
>
> "We recommend that specimens with features associated with submucosally invasive cancer that are removed *en bloc* be handled in ways to optimize specimen orientation and pathologic assessment."
> **Weak recommendation; low-quality evidence**

### Recommendation 4a
>
> "We recommend that specimens with features associated with submucosally invasive cancer that are removed *en bloc* be handled in ways to optimize specimen orientation and pathologic assessment."
> **Weak recommendation; low-quality evidence**

*As published, 4a repeats the wording of 3 word for word, although it is placed under KQ4a (histologic features in nonpedunculated polyps).*

### Recommendation 4b
>
> "We recommend that pedunculated malignant polyps be considered at high risk of residual or recurrent cancer if they have any of the following features: poor tumor differentiation, lymphovascular invasion, tumor within 1 mm of the resection margin."
> **Strong recommendation; moderate evidence**

### Recommendation 5
>
> "We recommend that the pathology report adhere to the recommendation of the College of American Pathologists structured template and that the report contain the histologic type, grade of differentiation, tumor extension/invasion, stalk and mucosal margin status, as well as the presence or absence of lymphovascular invasion. We suggest other aspects, such as specimen integrity, polyp size, polyp morphology, and tumor budding be included."
> **Weak recommendation; low-quality evidence**

### Recommendation 6
>
> "We suggest establishing methods of communication among the gastroenterologist, pathologist, oncologist, surgeon, and the patient for the management of patients with malignant polyps."
> **Weak recommendation; Low-quality evidence**

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Deep SMI as a stand-alone surgical trigger** — this document: deep SMI (≥1 mm) generally indicates adjuvant surgery (10%–18% residual cancer). [[aga-2025-endoscopic-resection-crc|AGA 2025 expert commentary]] (newer): deep SMI as the *only* risk factor carries ~2.6% absolute LNM risk and was not an independent predictor → individualize. Newer statement shown alongside on [[polypectomy]] / [[colorectal-esd]].
- **Margin <1 mm** — this document: margin ≥1 mm (ideally 2 mm) favorable; <1 mm unfavorable, especially for pedunculated polyps (Rec 4b). AGA 2025 cites newer multicenter data showing no significant rise in local recurrence at 0.1–1 mm without tumor budding; unsettled.
- **LVI rank** — here LVI is one of five equal criteria; AGA 2025 calls LVI the strongest LNM predictor.
- **Tumor budding definition** — this document's text uses clusters of ≤5 cells; the ITBCC definition it cites (and AGA 2025) uses ≤4 cells and grades Bd1–3. This document gives no Bd cut-off.
- **Deep-SMI threshold wording** — text and Rec framing: ≥1 mm; Figure 9 footnote: >1 mm.
- **LST-G mixed-nodule size cut-point** — 38% SMI reported for lesions >20 mm in one passage, >30 mm in another.
- **0-IIc SMI rate** — 61% (3,680 lesions) here vs 27%–35.9% in [[usmstf-2020-endoscopic-removal]]; different series and denominators.
- **Pseudodepressed LST-NG** — 27.8%/41.4% by size band here vs ~31.6% overall in [[usmstf-2020-endoscopic-removal]]; consistent in range.
- **Printed statistics with internal errors** — Kudo V OR 1.42 with 95% CI 8.57–23.4; tumor-budding RR CI "3.6–1"/"3.6–2". Treat these figures with caution.
- **JNET without magnification** — accuracy uncertain; early data only.
- **LVI reporting** — interobserver variability high; definitions vary.
