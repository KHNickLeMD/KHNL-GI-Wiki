## Summary

The largest series of transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS) in Budd-Chiari syndrome (BCS) at the time: **124 consecutive patients at 6 European centres, July 1993 – March 2006**, followed to death, orthotopic liver transplantation (OLT) or last evaluation. Its two contributions are separable — an outcome result, and a score.

**The outcome result:** these were sick patients — **98% at intermediate or high risk by the Rotterdam BCS prognostic index** — yet **1- and 5-year OLT-free survival were 88% and 78%**. In the high-risk stratum specifically, 5-year OLT-free survival was **71% against the 42% the Rotterdam score predicts**, a ~30-point improvement. This is the evidence base for TIPS as the salvage step in BCS when medical therapy and recanalisation fail; medical management alone succeeds in only 18% of patients, rising to 32% with recanalisation of short-length hepatic vein stenoses.

**The score:** on multivariate Cox regression of pre-TIPS variables, only **bilirubin, age and INR** independently predicted 1-year OLT-free survival. From the natural logarithms of their regression coefficients the authors derived the **BCS-TIPS prognostic index (BCS-TIPS PI)**, with a most-specific discriminative value at **7**. The index identifies a very small, very high-risk subgroup — **8 of 122 patients** scored >7 — in whom TIPS does not rescue and early OLT might.

The authors are explicit that the index is **not externally validated** and "cannot be recommended until prospectively validated."

## Key Findings / Claims

### BCS-TIPS prognostic index — the formula

> **BCS-TIPS PI = age (years) × 0.08 + bilirubin (mg/dL) × 0.16 + INR × 0.63**

- Derived from the three independent predictors of **1-year OLT-free survival after TIPS**, weighted by the **natural logarithm of their Cox regression coefficients** (Table 4).
- Multivariate hazard ratios: **bilirubin 1.171** (95% CI 1.08–1.27, P < .001) · **age 1.086** (1.03–1.14, P = .002) · **INR 1.876** (1.13–3.1, P = .014).
- Bootstrap selection frequency across 200 samples: bilirubin **92%**, age **64%**, INR **50%**.

### The cut-off and what it predicts

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cut-off** | **>7** (the most specific discriminative value) |
| **Outcome** | **death or OLT within 1 year after TIPS** |
| Sensitivity | 58% |
| Specificity | **99%** |
| Positive predictive value | 88% |
| Negative predictive value | 96% |
| Area under the ROC curve | **0.86** (95% CI 0.72–0.99) |

- Distribution: of 122 patients analysed, **114 scored ≤7 and only 8 scored >7**. **7 of those 8 died or were transplanted**, against 5 of 114 at ≤7.
- Of the 10 deaths in the first year, **7 had a score >7**; neither of the 2 transplanted patients did.
- In bootstrapped Cox regression including MELD, Rotterdam, Child–Pugh, revised Clichy and original Clichy, the BCS-TIPS PI was selected as the best predictor of 1-year OLT-free survival in **97%** of samples, vs **MELD 18%, Rotterdam 9%, Child–Pugh 4%, revised Clichy 4%, original Clichy 3%**.
- Among the **9 patients (7.3%) meeting acute liver failure criteria before TIPS** (median MELD 28), the **4 who died — all within a median of 9 days of the procedure — had a BCS-TIPS PI >7**, and the 5 survivors all scored under 7.

### TIPS outcomes in severe BCS

- **OLT-free survival 88% at 1 year, 78% at 5 years**, despite 98% of the cohort being intermediate- or high-risk by Rotterdam.
- **High-risk stratum: 5-year OLT-free survival 71% vs 42% predicted** by the Rotterdam score.
- **TIPS technically successful in 93%** of patients in whom it was attempted; a formal contraindication (e.g. chronic complete portal vein thrombosis) existed in only **10%**.
- Where no hepatic vein stump remained, **direct puncture from the intrahepatic inferior vena cava (IVC)** was used — 47 patients (38%) had the shunt created from the IVC or a hepatic vein stump route.
- **Hepatic encephalopathy (HE) after TIPS: 21% actuarial at 1 year** (95% CI 14–29), median onset **1.2 months**. Transient and easily controlled in 22 patients; **4 had recurrent HE and all 4 were listed for OLT**.
- Only **8 patients (6.5%)** of the whole cohort ultimately required OLT. **66% of patients continued to deteriorate** on medical management before TIPS, which is what selected them.
- Comparison the authors draw: **84% 5-year survival with TIPS here vs 71.4% with OLT** in the European BCS cohort — with the point that OLT costs a graft and lifelong immunosuppression.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- ⚠ **The score's original management use has been withdrawn by the guidelines, and the page must keep it that way.** [[baveno-vii-2022-portal-hypertension|Baveno VII]] 8.24–8.25 endorsed **BCS-TIPS PI >7** as a trigger to consider liver transplantation *before* TIPS. [[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension|Baveno VIII]] **withdraws that trigger**, agreeing with [[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]] KC 26 that prognostic scores should not dictate therapy in individual patients. Tier 1 and newer governs: the formula is printed so the number can be computed and understood, **not as a treatment rule**.
- **The authors agree with the guidelines, not with Baveno VII.** The paper states the index **"has not been externally validated"** and **"cannot be recommended until prospectively validated"** — a self-imposed limit stronger than the use Baveno VII put it to.
- **Derivation only, retrospective, and tiny at the decision point.** The >7 stratum contains **8 patients**. Sensitivity is 58%: a score ≤7 does not promise a good outcome (5 of 114 still died or were transplanted), and the index misses roughly 4 in 10 of the events it is meant to flag.
- **Era and unit caveats.** Patients span 1993–2006, bridging bare and polytetrafluoroethylene-covered stents; bilirubin is in **mg/dL** here, whereas the Rotterdam score on the same page takes bilirubin in **µmol/L** — the two indices are not interchangeable in their inputs.
