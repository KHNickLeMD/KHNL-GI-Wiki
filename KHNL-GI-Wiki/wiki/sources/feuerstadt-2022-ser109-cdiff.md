## Summary

Phase 3 double-blind placebo-controlled randomized controlled trial (RCT) of **SER-109**, an orally administered microbiome therapeutic of **live purified *Firmicutes* spores**, in recurrent [[clostridioides-difficile|*Clostridioides difficile*]] infection (CDI). Conducted at 56 sites in the United States and Canada, July 2017 – September 2020; 281 screened, 182 randomized (planned 188 — enrollment stopped early because of the coronavirus disease 2019 [Covid-19] pandemic).

Rationale: antibiotics kill toxin-producing vegetative bacteria but not spores and do not repair the disrupted microbiome. Restoring spore-forming *Firmicutes* raises secondary bile acids, which inhibit *C. difficile* spore germination.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Population and regimen

- **Eligibility:** adults ≥18 y with **≥3 episodes of CDI within 12 months, inclusive of the qualifying episode**. Qualifying episode = ≥3 unformed stools over 2 consecutive days **plus** a positive *C. difficile* toxin test **plus** resolution of symptoms on **10–21 days of standard-of-care antibiotics** (vancomycin or fidaxomicin — see [[cdiff-antibiotics]]).
- **Regimen:** SER-109 ≈ **3 × 10⁷ spore colony-forming units** — 4 oral capsules once daily for **3 consecutive days**, given after the antibiotic course is complete.
- **Bowel preparation:** 10 ounces of magnesium citrate the night before dosing, to limit inactivation of the dose species by residual vancomycin/fidaxomicin (both can persist 5–7 days after discontinuation).
- Randomization 1:1, stratified by age (<65 vs ≥65 y) and antibiotic received for the qualifying episode.
- **Recurrence** = onset of ≥3 unformed stools/day over 2 consecutive days, a positive stool toxin assay, and investigator assessment that treatment was warranted.

### Efficacy and safety

- **Recurrence up to 8 weeks: 12% (SER-109) vs 40% (placebo)** — relative risk (RR) 0.32 (95% confidence interval [CI] 0.18–0.58), P < 0.001. Absolute difference −28 percentage points; **number needed to treat 3.6**.
- **Sustained clinical response at 8 weeks: 88% vs 60%.**
- Benefit held in every prespecified stratum — age <65 y RR 0.24 (95% CI 0.07–0.78) and ≥65 y RR 0.36 (95% CI 0.18–0.72); prior vancomycin RR 0.41 (95% CI 0.22–0.79) and prior fidaxomicin RR 0.09 (95% CI 0.01–0.63).
- Most recurrences occurred early — onset as early as day 4, 75% within 2 weeks and 85% within 4 weeks of dosing.
- **Safety profile similar to placebo.** Any adverse event 93% vs 91%; serious adverse events 8% vs 16%. Most common were gastrointestinal (flatulence, abdominal distension, abdominal pain), largely mild to moderate. No serious adverse event or death was judged drug-related.
- SER-109 dose species were detected as early as **week 1** and persisted through week 8, with greater increases in secondary bile acids than placebo at every time point.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Complements [[aga-2024-fmt]] — no conflict. The guideline, not this trial, is what the wiki follows for *when* to use a fecal microbiota–based therapy; this trial supplies the regimen and the effect size.
- Patients had to have symptom resolution on antibiotics before dosing, so efficacy as **primary** (non-recurrent) CDI therapy is not addressed; neither is use in immunocompromised patients, who [[aga-2024-fmt]] handles separately.
- The trial excluded anyone on prolonged vancomycin, which the authors note was deliberate — to avoid artificially inflating apparent efficacy.
- Low representation of Black and Hispanic patients; no stool specimen before the antibiotic course, so the effect on the pre-antibiotic microbiome is unknown.
- The 8-week endpoint matters: a shorter follow-up window would have missed 15% of recurrences.
