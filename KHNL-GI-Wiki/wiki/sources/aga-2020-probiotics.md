## Summary
This AGA guideline applies the GRADE framework to the use of **probiotics** across eight gastrointestinal indications, prioritizing conditions where probiotics are commonly considered: *Clostridioides difficile*–associated disease, inflammatory bowel disease ([[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease]], [[ulcerative-colitis|ulcerative colitis]], and pouchitis), [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|irritable bowel syndrome]], acute infectious gastroenteritis, and necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) in preterm infants. A recurring theme is that probiotic effects are **strain- and dose-specific**, that the industry is largely unregulated, and that pooled meta-analytic conclusions can be misleading when heterogeneous strains/populations are grouped together. The Panel emphasized strain specificity when formulating recommendations and provided the granular strain combinations (rather than generic "probiotics").

For most indications the AGA found a **knowledge gap** and recommends probiotic use **only in the context of a clinical trial** (C difficile *treatment*, [[crohns-disease|Crohn's]], [[ulcerative-colitis|UC]], and [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|IBS]]). It makes **conditional recommendations FOR** specific multi-strain formulations in three settings: **prevention of C difficile infection in adults/children on antibiotics**, **pouchitis** (maintenance/prevention with the 8-strain combination), and **prevention of NEC in preterm low-birth-weight infants**. It makes a conditional recommendation **AGAINST** probiotics for **acute infectious gastroenteritis in children**, based on two high-quality North American RCTs showing no benefit.

Evidence quality ranged from very low (pouchitis, IBS) to moderate/high (NEC). The guideline notes that prior society guidelines recommending probiotics for pediatric acute gastroenteritis predate the negative North American trials. An update was anticipated within 3–5 years.

## Key Findings / Claims — Summary of Recommendations (Table 3, near-verbatim)

**1. C difficile infection — treatment.** "In patients with *C difficile* infection, we recommend the use of probiotics only in the context of a clinical trial." — **No recommendation; knowledge gap (quality of evidence).**

**2. C difficile infection — prevention (patients on antibiotics).** "In adults and children on antibiotic treatment, we suggest the use of *S boulardii*; or the 2-strain combination of *L acidophilus* CL1285 and *L casei* LBC80R; or the 3-strain combination of *L acidophilus*, *L delbrueckii* subsp *bulgaricus*, and *B bifidum*; or the 4-strain combination of *L acidophilus*, *L delbrueckii* subsp *bulgaricus*, *B bifidum*, and *S salivarius* subsp *thermophilus* over no or other probiotics for prevention of *C difficile* infection." — **Conditional recommendation; low quality of evidence.**
  - *Comment:* Patients who place a high value on the potential harms (particularly those with severe illnesses) or a high value on avoiding the associated cost and a low value on the small risk of *C difficile* development (particularly in the outpatient setting), would reasonably select no probiotics.

**3. Crohn's disease.** "In adults and children with Crohn's disease, we recommend the use of probiotics only in the context of a clinical trial." — **No recommendation; knowledge gap.**

**4. Ulcerative colitis.** "In adults and children with ulcerative colitis, we recommend the use of probiotics only in the context of a clinical trial." — **No recommendation; knowledge gap.**

**5. Pouchitis.** "In adults and children with pouchitis, we suggest the 8-strain combination of *L paracasei* subsp *paracasei*, *L plantarum*, *L acidophilus*, *L delbrueckii* subsp *bulgaricus*, *B longum* subsp *longum*, *B breve*, *B longum* subsp *infantis*, and *S salivarius* subsp *thermophilus* over no or other probiotics." — **Conditional recommendation; very low quality of evidence.**
  - *Comment:* Patients for whom the feasibility and cost of using this combination of bacterial strain is problematic may reasonably select no probiotics.

**6. Irritable bowel syndrome.** "In symptomatic children and adults with irritable bowel syndrome, we recommend the use of probiotics only in the context of a clinical trial." — **No recommendation; knowledge gap.**

**7. Acute infectious gastroenteritis (children).** "In children with acute infectious gastroenteritis, we suggest against the use of probiotics." — **Conditional recommendation; moderate quality of evidence.**

**8. Necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) prevention in preterm infants.** "In preterm (less than 37 weeks gestational age), low-birth-weight infants, we suggest using a combination of *Lactobacillus* spp and *Bifidobacterium* spp (*L rhamnosus* ATCC 53103 and *B longum* subsp *infantis*; or *L casei* and *B breve*; or *L rhamnosus*, *L acidophilus*, *L casei*, *B longum* subsp *infantis*, *B bifidum*, and *B longum* subsp *longum*; or *L acidophilus* and *B longum* subsp *infantis*; or *L acidophilus* and *B bifidum*; or *L rhamnosus* ATCC 53103 and *B longum* Reuter ATCC BAA-999; or *L acidophilus*, *B bifidum*, *B animalis* subsp *lactis*, and *B longum* subsp *longum*); or *B animalis* subsp *lactis* (including DSM 15954), or *L reuteri* (DSM 17938 or ATCC 55730); or *L rhamnosus* (ATCC 53103 or ATC A07FA or LCR 35) for prevention of NEC over no and other probiotics." — **Conditional recommendation; moderate/high quality of evidence.**

### Supporting evidence highlights (technical review)
- **C difficile prevention:** 2017 Cochrane review of 39 studies / 9955 patients — probiotics reduced overall C difficile risk vs placebo (RR 0.40; 95% CI 0.30–0.52); benefit concentrated in patients with high baseline risk (>15%), with no significant benefit at low/baseline risk. Subgroup RRs: *S boulardii* 0.41 (0.22–0.79); 2-species *L acidophilus* CL1285 + *L casei* LBC80R 0.22 (0.11–0.42); 3-strain combination 0.35 (0.15–0.85); 4-strain combination 0.28 (0.11–0.67). Overall quality downgraded Moderate→Low (risk of bias, publication bias from registered-but-unpublished trials).
- **C difficile treatment:** 5 placebo-controlled RCTs as adjunct to antibiotics; data too heterogeneous to pool; all had uncertain/high risk of bias. *S boulardii* RR for cessation 1.33 (1.02–1.74) and recurrence 0.59 (0.35–0.98); *L rhamnosus* ATCC 53103 RR 2.63 (0.35–19.85, harm signal). Overall certainty low.
- **Crohn's disease:** 1 induction study (no benefit; *L rhamnosus* ATCC 53103, OR 0.80, CI 0.04–17.20) and 11 maintenance studies; no overall benefit; quality low.
- **Ulcerative colitis:** 11 induction and 6 maintenance studies; 8-strain combination vs mesalamine/balsalazide for induction RR 1.72 (0.78–3.32, very low certainty); *E coli* Nissle 1917 ~equivalent to mesalamine for maintenance (RR 0.82, 0.60–1.11); rectal *L reuteri* ATCC 55730 in children RR 1.83 (1.14–2.92) for clinical response. Overall quality low; pooled results did not show benefit.
- **Pouchitis:** 8-strain combination for maintenance of chronic pouchitis remission RR 20.24 (4.28–95.81) and prevention of initial acute pouchitis RR 1.29 (1.03–1.61); very low certainty (small samples, risk of bias).
- **IBS:** 76 RCTs using 44 different strains/combinations; mostly single-RCT data. *S boulardii* (3 studies, 232 adults) — no difference in abdominal pain. 8-strain combination (2 RCTs, 73 adults) — abdominal pain VAS decrease (MD −3.78; CI −4.93 to −2.62) but small/biased. Overall very low; significant publication-bias concern.
- **Acute infectious gastroenteritis:** 89 studies; pooled mean duration of diarrhea reduced ~21.91 h (16.17–27.64) but low level of evidence; *S boulardii* (21 RCTs) reduced ~28.9 h; *L rhamnosus* ATCC 53103 (19 RCTs) reduced ~23.13 h. Two high-quality North American RCTs (PECARN/PERC; *L rhamnosus* GG and a *L rhamnosus* R0011 + *L helveticus* R0052 combination) showed **no benefit** → recommendation against in US/Canada children.
- **NEC:** Network meta-analysis (Morgan et al; 63 studies). Combinations of *Lactobacillus* spp + *Bifidobacterium* spp reduced all-cause mortality (OR 0.56; 0.39–0.80); several specific combinations reduced severe NEC (e.g. OR 0.35, 0.31, 0.30, 0.44 for various combos) and shortened time to full enteral feeds and hospitalization; moderate/high quality for the favored combinations.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- AGA recommendation **against** probiotics for pediatric acute gastroenteritis conflicts with earlier ESPGHAN (2014) and IDSA (2017) positions, which predate the two negative large North American RCTs.
- Strain- and dose-specificity means society "probiotics" recommendations cannot be generalized across products; viability/manufacturing of marketed formulations was not assessable.
