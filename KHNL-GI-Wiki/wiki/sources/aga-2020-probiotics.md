## Summary
This American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) guideline applies the GRADE framework to **[[probiotics]]** across eight clinical questions, prioritizing the conditions for which probiotics are commonly considered: *[[clostridioides-difficile|Clostridioides difficile]]*–associated disease, [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease]] ([[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease]], [[ulcerative-colitis|ulcerative colitis (UC)]], and [[pouchitis]]), [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)]], [[acute-diarrhea|acute infectious gastroenteritis]], and necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) in preterm infants. Patient-important outcomes were induction and maintenance of remission, treatment of disease, prevention of sepsis, and all-cause mortality. A recurring theme is that probiotic effects are **strain- and dose-specific**, that the industry is largely unregulated, and that pooled meta-analytic conclusions can mislead when different strains and populations are grouped together — so every recommendation is framed around named strains rather than generic "probiotics".

For most indications the AGA found a **knowledge gap** and recommends probiotic use **only in the context of a clinical trial** (C difficile *treatment*, Crohn's disease, UC, and IBS). It makes **conditional recommendations FOR** specific named formulations in three settings: **prevention of C difficile infection in adults and children on antibiotic treatment**, **pouchitis** (the 8-strain combination), and **prevention of NEC in preterm, low-birth-weight infants**. It makes a conditional recommendation **AGAINST** probiotics for **acute infectious gastroenteritis in children**, on the strength of two high-quality North American randomized controlled trials (RCTs) showing no benefit.

Evidence quality ranged from very low (pouchitis, IBS) to moderate/high (NEC). Systematic reviews informing the recommendations were updated through **December 2018**. The guideline notes that prior society recommendations favouring probiotics for pediatric acute gastroenteritis predate the negative North American trials. The next update was anticipated 3 years from publication (2023); the discussion states review and consideration for update within 3–5 years, or earlier if practice-changing evidence appears.

## Scope and Grading Vocabulary

- **Probiotics defined** (Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations / World Health Organization): live microorganisms which, administered in adequate amounts, confer a health benefit on the host.
- **Scope:** probiotic formulations for prevention and treatment of gastrointestinal disease. **Prebiotic use is not covered** (prebiotics = nutrients promoting growth or beneficial function of beneficial microbes).
- Recommendations span **adults, children, and neonates**; audience is health care providers, dietitians, and patients.
- Evidence came from the accompanying technical review: existing high-quality systematic reviews updated through December 2018, with de novo review where none existed. The Guideline Panel formulated the recommendations independently of the Technical Review Panel.
- **Viability and manufacturing of marketed formulations could not be assessed** — different manufacturers use different processes, which may change the actual probiotic content of a product sold under the same name.
- **No doses, colony-forming unit counts, or treatment durations are given** with any recommendation — the named strains and combinations *are* the recommendation.
- **No recommendation in this guideline is Strong**; every actionable one is Conditional.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Summary of Recommendations (Table 3)

*Numbering, strength, and quality wording below are the guideline's own (Table 3 numbers its recommendations 1–8).*

**1. C difficile infection — treatment.** "In patients with *C difficile* infection, we recommend the use of probiotics only in the context of a clinical trial." — **No recommendation; knowledge gap (quality of evidence).**

**2. C difficile infection — prevention (patients on antibiotics).** "In adults and children on antibiotic treatment, we suggest the use of *S boulardii*; or the 2-strain combination of *L acidophilus* CL1285 and *L casei* LBC80R; or the 3-strain combination of *L acidophilus*, *L delbrueckii* subsp *bulgaricus*, and *B bifidum*; or the 4-strain combination of *L acidophilus*, *L delbrueckii* subsp *bulgaricus*, *B bifidum*, and *S salivarius* subsp *thermophilus* over no or other probiotics for prevention of *C difficile* infection." — **Conditional recommendation; low quality of evidence.**
  - *Comment:* Patients who place a high value on the potential harms (particularly those with severe illnesses) or a high value on avoiding the associated cost and a low value on the small risk of *C difficile* development (particularly in the outpatient setting), would reasonably select no probiotics.

**3. Crohn's disease.** "In adults and children with Crohn's disease, we recommend the use of probiotics only in the context of a clinical trial." — **No recommendation; knowledge gap.**

**4. Ulcerative colitis.** "In adults and children with ulcerative colitis, we recommend the use of probiotics only in the context of a clinical trial." — **No recommendation; knowledge gap.**

**5. Pouchitis.** "In adults and children with pouchitis, we suggest the 8-strain combination of *L paracasei* subsp *paracasei*, *L plantarum*, *L acidophilus*, *L delbrueckii* subsp *bulgaricus*, *B longum* subsp *longum*, *B breve*, *B longum* subsp *infantis*, and *S salivarius* subsp *thermophilus* over no or other probiotics." — **Conditional recommendation; very low quality of evidence.**
  - *Comment:* Patients for whom the feasibility and cost of using this combination of bacterial strain is problematic may reasonably select no probiotics.

**6. Irritable bowel syndrome.** "In symptomatic children and adults with irritable bowel syndrome, we recommend the use of probiotics only in the context of a clinical trial." — **No recommendation; knowledge gap.**

**7. Acute infectious gastroenteritis (children).** "In children with acute infectious gastroenteritis, we suggest against the use of probiotics." — **Conditional recommendation; moderate quality of evidence.**
  - *Scope:* the AGA frames this as applying to children in the United States and Canada; trials from other regions were judged not generalizable to the North American population because of likely differences in host genetics, diet, sanitation, and endemic enteropathogens.

**8. NEC prevention in preterm infants.** "In preterm (less than 37 weeks gestational age), low-birth-weight infants, we suggest using a combination of *Lactobacillus* spp and *Bifidobacterium* spp (*L rhamnosus* ATCC 53103 and *B longum* subsp *infantis*; or *L casei* and *B breve*; or *L rhamnosus*, *L acidophilus*, *L casei*, *B longum* subsp *infantis*, *B bifidum*, and *B longum* subsp *longum*; or *L acidophilus* and *B longum* subsp *infantis*; or *L acidophilus* and *B bifidum*; or *L rhamnosus* ATCC 53103 and *B longum* Reuter ATCC BAA-999; or *L acidophilus*, *B bifidum*, *B animalis* subsp *lactis*, and *B longum* subsp *longum*); or *B animalis* subsp *lactis* (including DSM 15954), or *L reuteri* (DSM 17938 or ATCC 55730); or *L rhamnosus* (ATCC 53103 or ATC A07FA or LCR 35) for prevention of NEC over no and other probiotics." — **Conditional recommendation; moderate/high quality of evidence.**

### Supporting evidence highlights (technical review)
- **C difficile prevention:** 2017 Cochrane review of 39 studies / 9955 patients — probiotics reduced overall C difficile risk vs placebo (relative risk [RR] 0.40; 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.30–0.52); benefit concentrated in patients with high baseline risk (>15%), with no significant benefit at low/baseline risk. Subgroup RRs: *S boulardii* 0.41 (0.22–0.79); 2-species *L acidophilus* CL1285 + *L casei* LBC80R 0.22 (0.11–0.42); 3-strain combination 0.35 (0.15–0.85); 4-strain combination 0.28 (0.11–0.67). Overall quality downgraded Moderate→Low (risk of bias, publication bias from registered-but-unpublished trials).
- **C difficile treatment:** 5 placebo-controlled RCTs of probiotics as adjunct to antibiotics, testing 4 formulations — *S boulardii*; *Lactobacillus plantarum* 299v; *L rhamnosus* ATCC 53103; and a 4-strain combination of *L acidophilus* ATCC 700396, *L paracasei* subsp *paracasei* ATCC 335, *Bifidobacterium animalis* subsp *lactis* ATCC SD5220, and *B animalis* subsp *lactis* ATCC SD5219. Populations mixed initial and recurrent infection; probiotic given alongside metronidazole or vancomycin at low or high dose. Too heterogeneous to pool; all 5 had uncertain or high risk of bias (blinding of outcome assessment, selective reporting). Largest trial (134 patients): *S boulardii* RR for cessation of diarrhea 1.33 (1.02–1.74) and for recurrence 0.59 (0.35–0.98). *L rhamnosus* ATCC 53103 was associated with **increased** risk of recurrent C difficile infection vs placebo (RR 2.63; 0.35–19.85). Overall certainty low.
- **Crohn's disease:** only 1 induction study (11 subjects) — no evidence of benefit for *L rhamnosus* ATCC 53103 (odds ratio [OR] 0.80; CI 0.04–17.20, wide CI excluding neither benefit nor harm) — plus 11 heterogeneous maintenance studies (*Escherichia coli* Nissle 1917; *S boulardii*; *L rhamnosus* ATCC 53103; *Lactobacillus johnsonii* NCC 533; the 8-strain combination), only 1 of which enrolled children. No overall benefit for induction or maintenance; quality low.
- **Ulcerative colitis:** 11 induction and 6 maintenance studies; comparators varied and some studies used or allowed co-therapy with [[mesalamine-5-asa|mesalamine]].
  - *Induction:* 8-strain combination vs mesalamine or balsalazide RR 1.72 (0.78–3.32), very low certainty; oral *E coli* Nissle 1917 vs mesalamine RR 0.86 (0.49–1.49), uncertain; rectal *E coli* Nissle 1917 no clear benefit vs placebo; rectal *L reuteri* ATCC 55730 in children — increased clinical response vs placebo (RR 1.83; 1.14–2.92).
  - *Maintenance:* 2 studies of *E coli* Nissle 1917 and 1 of *L rhamnosus* ATCC 53103 showed no clear benefit over mesalamine (RR 0.82; 0.60–1.11); 2-strain *L acidophilus* LA-5 + *B animalis* subsp *lactis* Bb12, 2-strain *B breve* Yakult + *L acidophilus*, and 3-strain *Enterococcus faecalis* T-111 + *Clostridium butyricum* TO-A + *Bacillus mesentericus* TO-A showed no benefit vs placebo (single studies each).
  - Overall quality low; pooled results did not show benefit.
- **Pouchitis:** 7 studies, all in adults with an ileal pouch–anal anastomosis (IPAA) for ulcerative colitis. 8-strain combination for maintenance of remission in chronic pouchitis — 2 studies, 76 patients, remission at 12 months RR 20.24 (4.28–95.81), low certainty; 2 further studies of the same combination for prevention of an initial episode of acute pouchitis — RR for no episodes 1.29 (1.03–1.61), very low certainty. Single trials of *L rhamnosus* ATCC 53103, *B longum* Reuter ATCC BAA-999, and *C butyricum* CBM 588 showed no clear benefit; sample sizes extremely small throughout. Overall quality very low (risk of bias, small samples, heterogeneity). Because the evidence comes from chronic pouchitis after proctocolectomy and IPAA for UC, it is unclear whether it extends to children or to pouches created for other conditions such as [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis|familial adenomatous polyposis]].
- **IBS:** 76 RCTs using 44 different strains/combinations; mostly single-RCT data. *S boulardii* (3 studies, 232 adults) — no difference in abdominal pain. 8-strain combination (2 RCTs, 73 adults) — abdominal pain visual analogue scale (VAS) decrease (mean difference [MD] −3.78; CI −4.93 to −2.62) but small/biased. Overall very low; significant publication-bias concern.
- **Acute infectious gastroenteritis:** 89 studies (58 from a 2010 Cochrane review, 31 published later). Of the 89, 58 reported duration of diarrhea — pooled reduction 21.91 h (16.17–27.64), low level of evidence. *S boulardii* was the most-studied (21 RCTs; 9 reported duration) — reduction 28.9 h (16.78–41.03), very low level of evidence. *L rhamnosus* ATCC 53103 next (19 RCTs; 14 reported duration) — reduction 23.13 h (12.33–33.94). Most trials showing benefit came from Italy, India, Poland, Turkey, and Pakistan and carried ≥1 risk-of-bias concern. Two large multicentre, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled North American trials gave **no benefit**: the Pediatric Emergency Care Applied Research Network study (943 children, 10 US emergency departments, *L rhamnosus* ATCC 53103) and the Pediatric Emergency Research Canada study (827 children, 6 Canadian emergency departments, *L rhamnosus* R0011 + *Lactobacillus helveticus* R0052), each given for **5 days**; 2 further US/Canadian studies confirmed the lack of benefit.
- **NEC:** network meta-analysis of 63 studies of single- and multiple-strain probiotics vs placebo in preterm, low-birth-weight infants; outcomes were all-cause mortality, **severe NEC (stage II or greater)**, culture-proven sepsis, and duration of hospitalization.
  - *All-cause mortality:* combinations of *Lactobacillus* spp + *Bifidobacterium* spp — OR 0.56 (0.39–0.80).
  - *Severe NEC, moderate/high quality:* *Lactobacillus* spp + *Bifidobacterium* spp combinations OR 0.35 (0.20–0.59); *B animalis* subsp *lactis* (including strain DSM 15954) OR 0.31 (0.13–0.74); *L reuteri* (DSM 17938 or ATCC 55730) OR 0.55 (0.34–0.91); *L rhamnosus* (ATCC 53103 or ATC A07FA or LCR 35) OR 0.44 (0.21–0.90).
  - *Severe NEC, low/very low quality:* combinations adding *Enterococcus* spp OR 0.28 (0.16–0.49); *Bifidobacterium* spp + *S salivarius* subsp *thermophilus* OR 0.38 (0.19–0.75); *Bacillus subtilis* + *E faecium* OR 0.23 (0.08–0.63).
  - *Days to full enteral feeds:* *Lactobacillus* spp + *Bifidobacterium* spp + *S boulardii* MD −3.30 days (−5.91 to −0.69), moderate/high quality; other combinations similar on low/very low quality evidence.
  - *Hospitalization:* shortened by *B animalis* subsp *lactis* MD −13.00 days (−22.71 to −3.29) and *L reuteri* MD −7.89 days (−11.60 to −4.17), moderate/high quality.

## Relevance to Wiki
- Anchors [[probiotics]] — AGA FOR / AGAINST / no-recommendation stance by condition, with the named strain combinations.
- Updates [[clostridioides-difficile]] (prevention on antibiotics = conditional FOR; treatment = no recommendation, with [[fmt|fecal microbiota transplantation]] noted as the effective option for recurrent infection), [[crohns-disease]], [[ulcerative-colitis]], [[irritable-bowel-syndrome]] (no recommendation, trial only), [[pouchitis]] (conditional FOR, 8-strain combination), and [[acute-diarrhea]] (against in children).

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **Superseded for C difficile prevention:** the newer AGA 2026 clinical practice update on C difficile infection in adults states probiotics are not advised to prevent an initial or recurrent infection. As the newer same-tier AGA source it carries [[probiotics]] and [[clostridioides-difficile]]; the 2020 conditional recommendation FOR (low quality, benefit concentrated at >15% baseline risk) is retained here as the prior position.
- AGA recommendation **against** probiotics for pediatric acute gastroenteritis conflicts with earlier European Society for Paediatric Gastroenterology, Hepatology and Nutrition (ESPGHAN) (2014) and Infectious Diseases Society of America (IDSA) (2017) positions, which predate the two negative large North American RCTs.
- Strain- and dose-specificity means society "probiotics" recommendations cannot be generalized across products; viability and manufacturing of marketed formulations were not assessable.
- The guideline names strains and combinations but gives no dose, colony-forming unit count, or treatment duration for any indication, so a product cannot be matched to the recommendation on potency.
- Knowledge gaps the AGA flags for future study: C difficile treatment, Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis, and IBS; lack of consistent harms reporting makes harm hard to assess.
