## Summary

Second American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) consensus document on endoscopy in eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE). The first ASGE consensus conference produced 20 accepted statements on endoscopic diagnosis, endoscopic grading and esophageal dilation, with a single statement on monitoring; eight further statements were not accepted. This document covers the topics that were left out — **pediatric considerations, disease assessment, and disease monitoring**.

Method: modified RAND/UCLA appropriateness (Delphi) process. A core group of 6 EoE experts selected by ASGE leadership built patient-centered statements from a systematic PubMed / Cochrane / Embase review covering 1 January 1993 (the first published report of EoE) to the search date. Twenty-one additional adult and pediatric international EoE experts then voted online on an acceptability scale of 1 (completely disagree) to 9 (completely agree). A statement was accepted when **at least 80% of voters scored it 7–9**. Round 1 accepted 27 of 28 statements; the one that failed was discussed on a video meeting, rewritten, and accepted on a second round. Final yield: **28 consensus statements**.

The document is explicitly a complement rather than a replacement — it avoids repeating the first ASGE consensus and does not duplicate the other recent EoE diagnosis, treatment and monitoring guidelines. Its practical centre of gravity is (1) that disease activity in EoE must be judged across symptoms, endoscopic findings and histology together, never by peak eosinophil count or symptoms alone; (2) that strictures are common and endoscopically easy to miss, so suspicion must be high and diameter measured by some deliberate technique; (3) that initial diagnostic endoscopy belongs **off treatment**, while less-invasive sampling (unsedated transnasal endoscopy, string test, sponge) belongs to **monitoring only**; and (4) a set of pediatric-specific indications, biopsy, grading and dilation rules.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Consensus statements (all 28; percent agreement as published)

Grading: this document assigns **no evidence grades and no strength-of-recommendation labels** — each statement carries only its percent agreement. The statement numbers below are the document's own (Table 1).

**Pediatric considerations**

1. In children **18 months and younger**, endoscopy for suspected EoE should be performed when there are chronic feeding difficulties, failure to advance diet in terms of varied textures, or malnutrition or poor weight gain. — *92%*
2. In children **older than 18 months**, endoscopy for suspected EoE should be considered for protracted vomiting or reflux symptoms, abdominal pain, failure of feeding therapies, dysphagia, or food impaction. — *96%*
3. In children of **any age**, endoscopy should be performed when there is a high index of suspicion for EoE based on the patient's symptoms, concomitant atopic conditions, or family history of atopy or EoE. — *96%*
4. In children with **suspected** EoE, esophageal, gastric and duodenal biopsy specimens should be obtained. Specifically, at least **6 specimens total from at least 2 esophageal levels**; the number of gastric and duodenal specimens should be sufficient to evaluate the suspected clinical conditions of interest. — *92%*
5. In children with **known** EoE, biopsy specimens at monitoring endoscopies can be **limited to the esophagus** if prior gastric and duodenal specimens were normal, the stomach and duodenum appear normal, and there are no new symptoms suggesting gastric or small intestinal pathology. — *92%*
6. Providers should document the **EoE Endoscopic Reference Score (EREFS)** in all children undergoing endoscopy with suspected or known EoE, to allow initial and longitudinal assessment of endoscopic severity. — *100%*
7. In children with EoE, **esophageal string testing and unsedated transnasal endoscopy (uTNE) should be reserved for monitoring** disease status. Providers should **not** use either modality to establish an EoE diagnosis. — *84%*
8. In children with EoE, esophageal dilation should be considered in patients with **symptomatic strictures or narrow-caliber esophagus** on esophagram or endoscopy, **regardless of disease activity**. — *88%*
9. Fluoroscopic imaging of the esophagus may be appropriate for evaluating children with dysphagia, but should be recognized to have **limited sensitivity and specificity** for identifying strictures or determining esophageal caliber. — *84%*
10. Providers should consider initiating **feeding therapy** in children with EoE who have chronic feeding difficulties, taking into account inflammatory and fibrostenotic disease activity, specific dietary needs, and the goals of feeding therapy. — *96%*
11. Providers should carefully assess readiness and address **preprocedure anxiety** in older adolescents and young adults before either sedated or unsedated endoscopy. — *88%*
12. Providers should implement a **structured transition plan** from pediatric to adult care. — *100%*

**Disease assessment**

13. In a patient with suspected EoE, the **initial EGD should be performed off treatment**. — *84%*
14. In a patient with suspected EoE, a **standard EGD** should initially be performed, to allow comprehensive assessment (including gastric and duodenal biopsy specimens when indicated) and esophageal dilation of a symptomatic stricture if needed. — *92%*
15. **All** patients with symptoms or endoscopic findings consistent with EoE should have esophageal biopsy specimens obtained. — *100%*
16. The definitive role of **functional luminal imaging planimetry** in the routine care of EoE **has yet to be determined**. — *84%*
17. When assessing disease activity, **symptoms, endoscopic findings and histologic features should all be considered**. — *100%*
18. The **peak eosinophil count** should be interpreted in the context of other histologic features, endoscopic findings, symptoms, and the mechanisms of the treatments being used. — *100%*
19. **Symptoms of dysphagia cannot be considered in isolation** — interpret them in the context of food avoidance, modified eating behaviors, and whether dilation has been performed. — *100%*
20. In **adult** patients with EoE, endoscopists should have a **high level of suspicion for a stricture**. — *96%*
21. In all patients with EoE, providers should recognize that **endoscopic detection of strictures or narrowing is not sensitive**. — *96%*
22. Assessment of esophageal diameter can be achieved with **esophagography, functional luminal impedance planimetry, or "sizing" techniques with balloon or bougie dilators**. — *84%*

**Disease monitoring**

23. Monitoring intervals to assess **initial treatment response vary by treatment**: **6–8 weeks for diet elimination, 8–12 weeks for proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) and topical steroids, and up to 24 weeks for biologics**. — *92%*
24. At monitoring endoscopies, use the **same biopsy protocol as for diagnosis** — at least **6 esophageal specimens from at least 2 different levels**. — *100%*
25. Monitoring endoscopy **after a change in medical therapy** should be performed at an interval sufficient to allow **washout of the prior treatment** and to evaluate the new treatment on its own. — *92%*
26. Less-invasive modalities for assessing disease activity — **unsedated transnasal endoscopy, a string-based collection device, and a sponge or balloon collection device** — can be considered depending on local expertise and availability. — *96%*
27. Less-invasive modalities **should not be used** for patients who **may need esophageal dilation**, or **for initial diagnosis**. — *96%*
28. **Structured clinical and endoscopic monitoring** should be implemented to avoid gaps in care that increase the risk of fibrostenosis. — *96%*

### Supporting detail that carries a decision

**Off-treatment endoscopy (Statement 13)**
- Stop medical therapy before endoscopy — for possible EoE and also for unproven gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) per Lyon 2.0.
- Time off medication needed to attenuate a prior treatment's effect is unclear for EoE, but **for PPI therapy the evidence suggests at least 1–2 months**.
- PPI or milk elimination alone produces disease resolution in **40%–50%** — a PPI response does not distinguish EoE from GERD from both.
- If empiric PPI cannot be stopped for intolerance: scope anyway; active EoE on treatment confirms PPI non-response. If EoE is not found, stop the PPI later to confirm or refute the diagnosis.
- Starting diet and/or steroid therapy before the diagnosis is confirmed is **not** recommended. A "negative" endoscopy in a strongly suspected patient should prompt a search for confounders — PPIs, inhaled or intranasal steroids, dietary avoidance, seasonality, inadequate biopsy sampling.

**Stricture detection and esophageal diameter (Statements 20–22)**
- Strictures occur in **~25% of adults** with EoE; prevalence has risen steadily over two decades.
- Strictures are **the least reliably assessed feature of the EREFS**; interrater reliability for stricture diameter is poor.
- Sensitivity of endoscopy for a stricture of **≤13 mm is only 33%** versus esophagram. In 48 adults scoped with endoluminal functional luminal impedance planimetry (EndoFLIP), **12 had a diameter <15 mm not noted at endoscopy**. In children, 12 of 22 (55%) EoE strictures were diagnosed **only** on esophagram.
- A published model predicting fibrostenosis at endoscopy uses **5 easy-to-obtain clinical factors: age >18 years, symptoms for >5 years before diagnosis, dysphagia, food impaction, and lack of abdominal pain.**
- Ways to establish diameter: barium esophagography (optionally with a barium tablet/pill to gauge retention); EndoFLIP; the point of initial mucosal disruption on balloon pull-through, sequential guidewire-based dilation, or bougie cap technique; **opening a biopsy forceps** (tip-to-tip distance is known); retroflexion at the gastroesophageal junction to compare a distal stricture against the endoscope diameter; and a **bougie-like cone-shaped plastic cap fitted to the scope tip, available in 7–18 mm sizes**.

**Why a standard scope first (Statements 7, 14, 27)**
- uTNE scopes are only **3–5 mm** in diameter, so they may miss strictures needing dilation, cannot gauge esophageal diameter, and give lower video resolution; not all allow gastric and small-bowel sampling. It is unclear how well EREFS can be scored on uTNE.
- Dilation cannot be performed through uTNE (the channel will not pass a balloon dilator; a wire can be passed, but dilation under sedation is recommended in nearly all cases). Sponge-, balloon- and string-based devices assess **inflammation only** — no therapeutics, no fibrosis assessment — and a stricture may block passage or withdrawal of a sponge device.

**Monitoring and washout (Statements 23, 25)**
- Initial-response intervals come from each treatment's mechanism and onset: **diet elimination 6–8 weeks** (not counting the time to start the diet after dietitian consultation), **PPI 8–12 weeks**, **topical steroid 8–12 weeks**, **dupilumab typically 24 weeks** — earlier in more severe disease or frequent dilation, where response has also been shown before 24 weeks. With diet, if all domains improve but histologic response is incomplete, a longer initial course can be considered.
- Washout data after stopping a treatment: EoE recurs histologically and endoscopically **within ≤4 weeks** of adding foods back; **~50% relapse at ~90–120 days** off topical steroid; dupilumab may need **4–6 months** of washout given its long steady-state half-life.
- Practical next-endoscopy timing when a *new* treatment is started after an effective one: **~6 months** after a PPI or steroid (particularly with minimal symptoms and low food-impaction concern), **3–4 months** after diet, **6–12 months** after dupilumab — all individualized. **No washout is needed if the prior treatment was ineffective**, since active EoE is already established.

**Biopsy technique at monitoring (Statement 24)**
- Standard **"turn and suction"** technique; **single bites** should be considered to improve specimen quality.
- At least 6 specimens from ≥2 levels maximizes sensitivity; **target active inflammatory findings** (especially furrows and exudates) to increase yield — but exudates can be washed or pushed off by scope advancement or dilator passage.
- Target residual findings post-treatment; if findings are only mid-esophageal, either take an extra set there or include the mid-esophagus with the proximal sampling.
- Whether to split specimens into separate pathology jars is a matter of clinical context — few data. With **systemic** therapy (diet, biologics, PPI) specimens may be combined; with **topical** therapy, effect can differ by esophageal level, so separating them may make sense. If not separated and endoscopic activity differs by level, document that in the endoscopy report.

**Pediatric detail**
- Infants and toddlers cannot self-report; parents describe difficulty advancing solids, delayed oral feeding skills, low intake volume, prolonged mealtimes.
- Gastric and duodenal biopsy yield is relatively low in EoE, but if non-EoE eosinophilic GI disease is suspected, one study suggests **up to 8 gastric and 4 duodenal specimens** maximize detection.
- Routine gastric/duodenal biopsies at monitoring endoscopies add anesthesia time, cost, risk and burden for low yield.
- EREFS total **0–9**, scored on the overall worst finding: edema present (1) or absent (0); rings mild (1), moderate (2), severe (3), absent (0); exudates mild (1), severe (2), absent (0); furrows mild (1), severe (2), absent (0); stricture present (1) or absent (0), with the esophageal diameter estimated in millimeters. Individual feature grades should be reported at each endoscopy; a visual atlas in the endoscopy suite can help.
- uTNE takes **<10 minutes** and saves patients ~2 hours in hospital versus endoscopy with anesthesia; main adverse events are vomiting/spitting up and epistaxis. Virtual-reality goggles improve satisfaction.
- Esophageal string test: capsule on a string dwells in the esophagus ~1 hour absorbing eosinophil-derived proteins (major basic protein, eosinophil-derived neurotoxin, Charcot-Leyden crystal protein/galectin-10, eotaxin-3, eosinophil peroxidase); **eotaxin-3 + major basic protein together predict histologic activity**. 98% of children and 96% of adults would use it again; 74% of children prefer it to endoscopy with anesthesia.
- Pediatric dilation safety: systematic review of 845 patients (87 children) — >95% symptom improvement; largest single-center series of 68 pediatric EoE dilations — **no perforations or significant hemorrhage, 14.7% postprocedure pain**, with no difference in adverse events by disease activity. Dilation may therefore be done at the initial endoscopy for a detected stricture, or deferred until after inflammation is controlled if symptoms persist.
- Feeding difficulties occur in up to **17%** of children with EoE; learned maladaptive behaviors in **94%** of those with EoE plus a feeding disorder. Advancing feeding therapy too aggressively during active disease may be counterproductive — address active inflammation or narrowing first. If feeding therapy plateaus, reassess treatment adherence and disease activity.
- Transition: 78% of teens and 76% of parents had no knowledge of health-care transition; one cohort showed **16% loss to follow-up**, averaging 299 days between the last pediatric and first adult visit and 790 days between procedures, with more symptoms and medication non-adherence after transfer.
- The 2016 FDA black-box warning on anesthesia in children under 3 years is part of the rationale for less-invasive monitoring.

**Disease-activity assessment**
- Eosinophil-depleting agents (lirentelimab, benralizumab) achieved high histologic response with **no** improvement in symptoms or endoscopic endpoints — the argument against peak count as a sole metric. Agents targeting mast cells or fibrosis may not change eosinophil density yet still improve disease activity.
- Validated multi-feature measures: the EoE histologic scoring system (8 features, only 1 of which is eosinophil density), EREFS, and the Index of Severity for EoE (I-SEE; symptoms, complications, endoscopic and histologic inflammation and fibrosis; available as a downloadable app).
- Distinguish **inflammatory** (exudates, furrows, edema) from **fibrotic** (rings, strictures) endoscopic features — short-term treatment should resolve the former, not the latter.
- Discordance between symptoms and biology runs both ways: adaptive eating behaviors and prior dilation mask activity; conversely symptoms persist with unrecognized or undilated narrowing, esophageal hypersensitivity/hypervigilance, superimposed dysmotility, or candidiasis complicating treatment. Overlap with avoidant/restrictive food intake disorder can look the same.
- **IMPACT** coping modifications: **I**mbibe fluids, **M**odify foods, **P**rolong mealtime, **A**void hard textures, **C**hew excessively, **T**urn away from tablets and pills.
- Assess lamina propria fibrosis and basal zone hyperplasia — both are in I-SEE and should inform initial and follow-up severity. Biopsy also distinguishes endoscopic mimics (Figure 2: esophageal candidiasis, Crohn's esophagitis, esophagitis dissecans, pseudodiverticulosis, lichen planus with edema/crepe-paper mucosa, rings, or narrowing with exudates, and lymphocytic esophagitis).
- EoE strictures are most commonly **distal**, overlapping with GERD findings — biopsy strictures to establish etiology.
- EndoFLIP's unique contribution is esophageal wall compliance and distensibility; it is more sensitive than endoscopy for strictures, can explain persistent dysphagia despite histologic response, and predicts food impaction independently. But it needs the device, added cost and procedure time, and substantial operator/staff expertise; normative esophageal diameters for young children are lacking and no EoE-specific EndoFLIP protocol consensus exists yet. **Routine use is not currently recommended in all EoE patients.**
- Gaps in care after diagnosis — untreated intervals, loss to follow-up then return — carry fibrostenotic progression risk, not just diagnostic delay.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Dupilumab response interval.** [[acg-2025-eoe|ACG 2025]] checks response at 12–24 weeks; this document says assessment "typically takes place at 24 weeks," with earlier monitoring in more severe disease or frequent dilation. Compatible, but ASGE 2026 is the newer tier-1 source and its *up to 24 weeks* wording governs the page.
- **Stricture-detection sensitivity figures differ by cutoff and comparator** across sources: 14.7% (any perceived narrowing), 25% (≤15 mm), and 33% (≤13 mm vs esophagram). All three are reported; none contradicts another — the cutoff is the qualifier and must travel with the number.
- **I-SEE remains unvalidated** — same open question as [[i-see-2022-eoe-severity-index]]; this document uses it as one of the comprehensive measures without resolving the validation gap.
- This document deliberately does **not** restate Part 1 of the ASGE consensus (endoscopic diagnosis, endoscopic grading, esophageal dilation), so its statements must be read alongside that document rather than as a stand-alone endoscopic approach to EoE.
- No evidence grading at all: percent agreement is the only quantifier the document provides, so no GRADE-style strength can be cited from it.
