## Summary

The **Revised Atlanta Classification**, and the source of the vocabulary every acute pancreatitis guideline since has used. Produced by an international, web-based, three-round consensus across 11 national and international pancreatic societies, it replaced the 1992 Atlanta definitions, whose descriptions of pancreatic and peripancreatic collections had "proved confusing and not universal".

Three things come from this document and nowhere else in `raw/`. First, the **per-domain cut-points of the modified Marshall score** (Table 1) — the instrument by which organ failure, and therefore severity, is actually assigned. [[acg-2024-acute-pancreatitis]] names the score and sets the threshold at ≥2 but does not print the bands. Second, the **four collection definitions** with their contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CECT) criteria — acute peripancreatic fluid collection (APFC), pancreatic pseudocyst, acute necrotic collection (ANC), walled-off necrosis (WON) — which exist to stop an ANC being called a pseudocyst and drained as one. Third, the **time axis** that makes the collection names meaningful: the 4-week line between APFC/ANC and pseudocyst/WON, and the 48-hour line between transient and persistent organ failure.

Severity has three grades, and the discriminator is **organ failure, not morphology**: mild (no organ failure, no complications), moderately severe (**transient** organ failure resolving within 48 h, and/or local or systemic complications), severe (**persistent** organ failure >48 h, single or multiple). Local complications alone never define severity. The document is explicit that severity in the early phase is **re-assessed daily** — the convenient points are 24 h, 48 h and 7 days — because a patient whose organ failure has not yet declared itself is treated as potentially severe until it does.

The classification also fixes two terms readers still carry. **"Pancreatic abscess" is abandoned** — the 1992 term for a localised collection of pus without significant necrosis, dropped because it is confusing and was never widely adopted. And **a pseudocyst in acute pancreatitis is extremely rare**: once solid necrotic material is present in a fluid-filled cavity, the collection is an ANC or WON, not a pseudocyst.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Diagnosis of acute pancreatitis — 2 of 3 required**

1. **Abdominal pain** consistent with acute pancreatitis (acute onset of persistent, severe, epigastric pain often radiating to the back).
2. **Serum lipase (or amylase) activity at least 3 times the upper limit of normal.**
3. **Characteristic findings on CECT**, or less commonly magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) or transabdominal ultrasonography.

- **Onset** = the time of onset of **abdominal pain**, not the time of hospital admission; record the interval between the two.
- If pain is strongly suggestive but enzymes are less than 3× upper limit of normal (as with delayed presentation), **imaging is required** to confirm. If pain and enzymes establish the diagnosis, **CECT is not usually required** in the emergency room or on admission.

**Modified Marshall scoring system for organ dysfunction (Table 1, verbatim)**

| Organ system | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Respiratory** (PaO₂/FiO₂) | >400 | 301–400 | 201–300 | 101–200 | ≤101 |
| **Renal** — serum creatinine (µmol/L) | ≤134 | 134–169 | 170–310 | 311–439 | >439 |
| **Renal** — serum creatinine (mg/dL) | <1.4 | 1.4–1.8 | 1.9–3.6 | 3.6–4.9 | >4.9 |
| **Cardiovascular** — systolic blood pressure (mm Hg)† | >90 | <90, fluid responsive | <90, **not** fluid responsive | <90, pH <7.3 | <90, pH <7.2 |

- **A score of ≥2 in any one system defines the presence of organ failure.**
- † Cardiovascular scoring is **off inotropic support**.
- **Pre-existing chronic renal failure:** the score depends on the extent of **further deterioration from baseline**; **no formal correction exists** for a baseline creatinine ≥134 µmol/L or ≥1.4 mg/dL.
- **FiO₂ estimation for non-ventilated patients** (same table): room air 21% · 2 L/min 25% · 4 L/min 30% · 6–8 L/min 40% · 9–10 L/min 50%.
- Why this score and not SOFA: modified Marshall is simple, internationally applicable, usable **on presentation and repeated daily**, and does not depend on intensive-care data. **SOFA is preferred for patients managed in critical care**, where inotropic and respiratory support are accounted for.

**Severity — three grades (Box 3)**

| Grade | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Mild** | **No organ failure** and **no local or systemic complications** |
| **Moderately severe** | **Transient organ failure** (resolves within **48 h**) **and/or** local or systemic complications **without** persistent organ failure |
| **Severe** | **Persistent organ failure (>48 h)** — single or multiple organ failure |

- **Transient = <48 h · persistent = >48 h.** Organ failure in more than one system is **multiple organ failure (MOF)**.
- Mild acute pancreatitis: usually discharged in the early phase, imaging usually unnecessary, **mortality very rare**.
- Moderately severe: mortality **far less** than severe; may resolve without intervention or need prolonged specialist care.
- Severe: persistent organ failure usually develops in the first few days; **mortality 36%–50%**; infected necrosis on top of persistent organ failure carries "extremely high mortality".
- **Severity is dynamic and must be reassessed** — on admission a patient without organ failure is mild, but if organ failure develops the grade changes; convenient re-evaluation points are **24 h, 48 h and 7 days**.
- **Persistent SIRS predicts persistent organ failure**: when SIRS is present and persistent, the patient "should be treated as if they have severe acute pancreatitis".

**SIRS (Box 2) — 2 or more of:**

- Heart rate >90 beats/min
- Core temperature <36°C or >38°C
- White blood count <4000 or >12,000/mm³
- Respirations >20/min or PCO₂ <32 mm Hg

**Two phases**

- **Early phase** — usually the first week, may extend into the second. Severity is determined by the **presence and duration of organ failure**, not by morphology; local complications may be visible but are not the determinant.
- **Late phase** — weeks to months, and **only occurs in moderately severe or severe disease**. Characterised by persisting systemic signs and by the evolution of local complications; persistent organ failure remains the main determinant of severity.

**Two types of acute pancreatitis**

- **Interstitial oedematous pancreatitis** — the majority; diffuse (occasionally localised) enlargement from inflammatory oedema, with relatively homogeneous parenchymal enhancement on CECT. Symptoms usually resolve within the first week.
- **Necrotising pancreatitis** — about **5%–10%**; most commonly involves **both pancreas and peripancreatic tissue**, less commonly peripancreatic tissue alone, and **rarely the parenchyma alone**. Perfusion changes **evolve over several days**, so an **early CECT underestimates the eventual extent** — after the first week a non-enhancing area should be considered necrosis.
- **Peripancreatic necrosis alone** carries higher morbidity and intervention rates than interstitial oedematous pancreatitis.
- **Infected necrosis** — presumed when there is **extraluminal gas** in the pancreatic or peripancreatic tissues on CECT, or when image-guided fine-needle aspiration (FNA) is positive on Gram stain or culture. **No absolute correlation** between the extent of necrosis and the risk or timing of infection; infection is **rare during the first week**. ⚠ **"Pancreatic abscess" is not used** in this classification.

**The four collections (Box 1)**

| Collection | Timing | Contents | Wall |
|---|---|---|---|
| **APFC** — acute peripancreatic fluid collection | **<4 weeks**, interstitial oedematous pancreatitis only | Homogeneous **fluid density**, no non-liquid component | **No definable wall**; confined by normal fascial planes; adjacent to pancreas, no intrapancreatic extension |
| **Pancreatic pseudocyst** | Usually **>4 weeks** | Homogeneous fluid, **no solid material**; markedly raised amylase if aspirated | **Well defined, completely encapsulated**; round or oval |
| **ANC** — acute necrotic collection | **<4 weeks**, necrotising pancreatitis only | **Fluid *and* necrosis**, heterogeneous, varying non-liquid density | **No definable wall**; intrapancreatic and/or extrapancreatic |
| **WON** — walled-off necrosis | Usually **>4 weeks** | Heterogeneous, liquid and non-liquid, often loculated | **Mature, well defined, completely encapsulated** inflammatory wall |

- **The pseudocyst/ANC distinction is the one that matters for drainage** — "when there is solid necrotic material within a largely fluid-filled cavity, the term pseudocyst should not be used".
- **Pseudocyst in acute pancreatitis is extremely rare**; a pseudocyst does **not** result from an ANC. It may also arise from **disconnected duct syndrome**, where necrosis of the neck or body isolates a viable distal remnant.
- Within the first week APFC and ANC can look alike (both fluid density); the distinction becomes clear after the first week.
- An APFC that resolves or stays asymptomatic needs **no treatment** and does **not** itself constitute severe pancreatitis.

**Local and systemic complications**

- **Local:** APFC, pancreatic pseudocyst, ANC, WON; also **gastric outlet dysfunction, splenic and portal vein thrombosis, and colonic necrosis**.
- **Suspect a local complication** when abdominal pain persists or recurs, pancreatic enzymes rise again, organ function deteriorates, or clinical signs of sepsis (fever, leucocytosis) appear.
- **Systemic complication** = **exacerbation of a pre-existing co-morbidity** (e.g. coronary artery disease, chronic lung disease) precipitated by the acute pancreatitis — distinct from persistent organ failure.

**Reporting (online appendix 2)**

- Record extent of **pancreatic parenchymal necrosis** as **<30%, 30–50%, >50%**, with the sites.
- Describe collections by **location** (intrapancreatic / extrapancreatic), **homogeneity and attenuation** (presence of non-liquid components), **presence of a well-demarcated encapsulating wall**, and **presence of extraluminal gas** (bubbles, loculated gas, gas/fluid levels).
- **CECT at 5–7 days after admission is more reliable** than earlier imaging for establishing the presence and extent of necrosis.
- ⚠ **Risk scores do not define severity.** APACHE II, CT severity index, modified CT severity index, BISAP ≥3, hematocrit, blood urea nitrogen, creatinine and C-reactive protein are listed as **markers to record and evaluate**, not as severity definitions. **C-reactive protein is most predictive at 48–72 h** (delayed onset). **Serum amylase and lipase have no role in defining severity** and need not be repeated daily.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **No contradiction with [[acg-2024-acute-pancreatitis]]** — ACG 2024 adopts this classification wholesale. This document supplies the arithmetic ACG leaves out.
- ⚠ **This is a classification, not a management guideline**, and says so in its own text. It defines severity and morphology; it prescribes no treatment, and its risk-marker list is explicitly **not** a severity definition.
- **The renal domain has no correction for baseline chronic kidney disease** — the document states plainly that "no formal correction exists" for a baseline creatinine ≥134 µmol/L (≥1.4 mg/dL), leaving the score's renal points unreliable in exactly the patients most likely to score on them.
- **Table 5, the CECT reporting framework** referenced by online appendix 2, is **not reproduced in the file in `raw/`** — the appendix text describes what to report (items A–D above), which is what reached the wiki.
- **The ≥2 threshold is per system, not a sum.** A patient scoring 1 in all three systems has **no organ failure** by this definition.
