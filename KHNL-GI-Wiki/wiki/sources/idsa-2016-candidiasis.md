## Summary

The Infectious Diseases Society of America (IDSA) 2016 update covers the whole spectrum of *Candida* disease in **17 numbered clinical questions** answered by **140 numbered recommendations**, each graded by the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) framework — **strength** *strong* or *weak*, **quality of evidence** *high*, *moderate*, *low* or *very low*. It replaces the 2009 edition and is endorsed by the American Academy of Pediatrics, the Pediatric Infectious Diseases Society and the Mycoses Study Group.

Mucosal candidiasis of the oropharynx and esophagus is explicitly in scope even though it is not classically invasive disease. For a gastroenterology reader the document matters for three questions: **VIII (intra-abdominal candidiasis)**, **XVI (oropharyngeal candidiasis)** and above all **XVII (esophageal candidiasis)** — the only ingested source that prints a **fluconazole-refractory** pathway for *Candida* esophagitis, with agents, doses and durations. Question IV covers **chronic disseminated (hepatosplenic) candidiasis**.

The central therapeutic shift from 2009 is that an **echinocandin is first-line initial therapy for candidemia and for empiric therapy in the intensive care unit (ICU)**, with fluconazole demoted to an acceptable alternative in selected, non-critically-ill, azole-naive patients. Mucosal disease runs the other way: **fluconazole is first-line**, and echinocandins and amphotericin B (AmB) are alternatives reserved for intolerance of oral therapy or refractory disease.

Numbering and grade vocabulary below are **the document's own** (its executive summary numbers every recommendation 1–140).

## Key Findings / Claims

### XVII. Treatment for esophageal candidiasis (recommendations 131–140) — the GI core
- **131.** Systemic antifungal therapy is **always required**; a **diagnostic trial of antifungal therapy is appropriate before endoscopy** (strong; high-quality).
- **132.** Oral fluconazole **200–400 mg (3–6 mg/kg) daily × 14–21 days** (strong; high-quality).
- **133.** Cannot tolerate oral therapy → IV fluconazole **400 mg (6 mg/kg) daily**, OR an echinocandin — micafungin **150 mg daily**, caspofungin **70 mg load then 50 mg daily**, anidulafungin **200 mg daily** (strong; high-quality).
- **134.** Less preferred alternative when oral therapy is not tolerated: AmB deoxycholate **0.3–0.7 mg/kg daily** (strong; moderate-quality).
- **135.** **De-escalate to oral fluconazole 200–400 mg (3–6 mg/kg) daily** once oral intake is tolerated (strong; moderate-quality).
- **136.** **Fluconazole-refractory disease:** itraconazole solution **200 mg daily**, OR voriconazole **200 mg (3 mg/kg) twice daily** IV or oral, **× 14–21 days** (strong; high-quality).
- **137.** Refractory alternatives: an echinocandin (micafungin **150 mg daily**; caspofungin **70 mg load then 50 mg daily**; anidulafungin **200 mg daily**) **× 14–21 days**, OR AmB deoxycholate **0.3–0.7 mg/kg daily × 21 days** (strong; high-quality).
- **138.** Posaconazole suspension **400 mg twice daily**, or extended-release tablets **300 mg once daily**, could be considered for refractory disease (weak; low-quality).
- **139.** **Recurrent esophagitis** → chronic suppressive fluconazole **100–200 mg three times weekly** (strong; high-quality).
- **140.** HIV-infected patients: **antiretroviral therapy is strongly recommended** to reduce recurrence (strong; high-quality).

> Note the dose asymmetry between mucosal sites: esophageal disease takes **higher doses and longer courses than oropharyngeal disease**, and unlike thrush it is never treated topically.

### XVI. Treatment for oropharyngeal candidiasis (122–130)
- **122.** Mild disease: clotrimazole troches **10 mg 5×/day**, OR miconazole mucoadhesive buccal **50 mg tablet** to the mucosa over the canine fossa once daily, **× 7–14 days** (strong; high-quality).
- **123.** Mild-disease alternatives: nystatin suspension (100 000 U/mL) **4–6 mL 4×/day**, OR 1–2 nystatin pastilles (200 000 U each) **4×/day**, × 7–14 days (strong; moderate-quality).
- **124.** **Moderate-to-severe disease: oral fluconazole 100–200 mg daily × 7–14 days** (strong; high-quality).
- **125.** Fluconazole-refractory: itraconazole solution **200 mg once daily** OR posaconazole suspension **400 mg twice daily × 3 days, then 400 mg daily**, for up to **28 days** (strong; moderate-quality).
- **126.** Refractory alternatives: voriconazole **200 mg twice daily**, OR AmB deoxycholate oral suspension **100 mg/mL 4×/day** (strong; moderate-quality).
- **127.** Other refractory alternatives: IV echinocandin (caspofungin 70 mg load → 50 mg daily; micafungin 100 mg daily; anidulafungin 200 mg load → 100 mg daily) OR IV AmB deoxycholate **0.3 mg/kg daily** (weak; moderate-quality).
- **128.** Chronic suppression is **usually unnecessary**; if needed for recurrent infection, fluconazole **100 mg three times weekly** (strong; high-quality).
- **129.** HIV-infected patients: antiretroviral therapy strongly recommended to reduce recurrence (strong; high-quality).
- **130.** Denture-related candidiasis: **disinfect the denture** in addition to antifungal therapy (strong; moderate-quality).

### VIII. Treatment for intra-abdominal candidiasis (54–57)
- **54.** Consider **empiric** antifungal therapy where there is clinical evidence of intra-abdominal infection **plus** significant candidiasis risk factors — **recent abdominal surgery, anastomotic leak, or necrotizing pancreatitis** (strong; moderate-quality).
- **55.** Treatment must include **source control** — appropriate drainage and/or debridement (strong; moderate-quality).
- **56.** Antifungal choice is **the same as for candidemia / ICU empiric therapy** (questions I and V) (strong; moderate-quality).
- **57.** **Duration is set by adequacy of source control and clinical response** — no fixed course (strong; low-quality).

### IV. Chronic disseminated (hepatosplenic) candidiasis (24–27)
- **24.** Initial lipid-formulation AmB **3–5 mg/kg daily** OR an echinocandin (micafungin 100 mg daily; caspofungin 70 mg load → 50 mg daily; anidulafungin 200 mg load → 100 mg daily) for several weeks, then oral fluconazole **400 mg (6 mg/kg) daily** if a fluconazole-resistant isolate is unlikely (strong; low-quality).
- **25.** Continue **until lesions resolve on repeat imaging — usually several months**; premature discontinuation causes relapse (strong; low-quality).
- **26.** Do **not** delay chemotherapy or hematopoietic cell transplantation for chronic disseminated candidiasis; continue antifungal therapy throughout the high-risk period (strong; low-quality).
- **27.** Debilitating persistent fevers → short-term (1–2 weeks) nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs or corticosteroids may be considered (weak; low-quality).

### IX. *Candida* in the respiratory tract (58)
- **58.** Growth of *Candida* from respiratory secretions **usually indicates colonization and rarely requires antifungal therapy** (strong; moderate-quality).

### I–III, V–VII. Candidemia, empiric ICU therapy, prophylaxis, neonates (1–53)
- **1.** Candidemia in non-neutropenic patients: **an echinocandin is initial therapy** — caspofungin 70 mg load → 50 mg daily; micafungin 100 mg daily; anidulafungin 200 mg load → 100 mg daily (strong; high-quality).
- **2.** Fluconazole **800 mg (12 mg/kg) load → 400 mg (6 mg/kg) daily** is an acceptable alternative in selected patients who are **not critically ill** and unlikely to have a fluconazole-resistant species (strong; high-quality).
- **3.** Test **all** bloodstream and clinically relevant isolates for azole susceptibility; consider echinocandin susceptibility testing after prior echinocandin therapy or with *C. glabrata* / *C. parapsilosis* (strong; low-quality).
- **4.** Step down echinocandin → fluconazole **usually within 5–7 days** if clinically stable, isolate susceptible, repeat cultures negative (strong; moderate-quality).
- **5.** *C. glabrata*: step down only to **high-dose fluconazole 800 mg (12 mg/kg) daily** or voriconazole 200–300 mg (3–4 mg/kg) twice daily, and only if the isolate is susceptible (strong; low-quality).
- **6.** Lipid-formulation AmB **3–5 mg/kg daily** is a reasonable alternative for intolerance, limited availability or resistance (strong; high-quality).
- **7.** AmB → fluconazole after **5–7 days** if susceptible, stable and cultures negative (strong; high-quality).
- **8.** Suspected azole- **and** echinocandin-resistant infection → lipid-formulation AmB 3–5 mg/kg daily (strong; low-quality).
- **9.** Voriconazole 400 mg (6 mg/kg) twice daily × 2 doses → 200 mg (3 mg/kg) twice daily is effective but **offers little advantage over fluconazole as initial therapy** (strong; moderate-quality); it **is** recommended as oral step-down for selected *C. krusei* candidemia (strong; low-quality).
- **10.** Every non-neutropenic patient with candidemia needs a **dilated ophthalmological examination within the first week** (strong; low-quality).
- **11.** Follow-up blood cultures **every day or every other day** to date clearance (strong; low-quality).
- **12.** Duration without metastatic complications: **2 weeks after documented bloodstream clearance** and resolution of attributable symptoms (strong; moderate-quality).
- **13.** Remove the central venous catheter (CVC) **as early as possible** when it is the presumed source and removal is safe; individualize (strong; moderate-quality).
- **14–20.** Neutropenic candidemia: echinocandin first-line (strong; moderate) · lipid AmB effective but less attractive for toxicity (strong; moderate) · fluconazole 800 mg load → 400 mg daily an alternative if not critically ill and azole-naive (weak; low) · fluconazole 400 mg daily for step-down during persistent neutropenia if stable, susceptible, cleared (weak; low) · voriconazole 400 mg bid × 2 → 200–300 mg bid when mold coverage is wanted, also for step-down (weak; low) · *C. krusei* → echinocandin, lipid AmB or voriconazole (strong; low) · **minimum 2 weeks after clearance, provided neutropenia and symptoms have resolved** (strong; low).
- **21.** Dilated funduscopy **within the first week after neutrophil recovery** (choroidal/vitreal findings are minimal before recovery) (strong; low-quality).
- **22.** In neutropenia, **non-CVC sources (e.g. the gastrointestinal tract) predominate** — catheter removal is an individual decision (strong; low-quality).
- **23.** Granulocyte colony-stimulating factor–mobilized granulocyte transfusions may be considered for persistent candidemia with protracted neutropenia (weak; low-quality).
- **28–33.** Empiric therapy in the non-neutropenic ICU patient: consider it for risk factors with no other cause of fever, and **start as soon as possible with septic shock** (strong; moderate) · preferred agent an echinocandin (strong; moderate) · fluconazole 800 mg load → 400 mg daily acceptable if azole-unexposed and not colonized with azole-resistant species (strong; moderate) · lipid AmB for intolerance (strong; low) · duration **2 weeks** in those who improve (weak; low) · **stop at 4–5 days** if no clinical response and no evidence of invasive candidiasis, or a negative high-negative-predictive-value non-culture assay (strong; low).
- **34–36.** ICU prophylaxis: fluconazole 800 mg load → 400 mg daily in high-risk patients in adult ICUs with **>5% invasive candidiasis** (weak; moderate) · echinocandin as an alternative (weak; low) · **daily chlorhexidine bathing** could be considered (weak; moderate).
- **37–44.** Neonatal candidiasis: AmB deoxycholate **1 mg/kg daily** (strong; moderate) · fluconazole **12 mg/kg daily** if no fluconazole prophylaxis (strong; moderate) · lipid AmB an alternative, with **caution if urinary tract involvement** (weak; low) · echinocandins **with caution, generally salvage only** (weak; low) · **lumbar puncture and dilated retinal examination** when blood/urine cultures are positive (strong; low) · computed tomography or ultrasound of genitourinary tract, liver and spleen if cultures persistently positive (strong; low) · **CVC removal strongly recommended** (strong; moderate) · duration 2 weeks after clearance (strong; low).
- **45–50.** Neonatal CNS infection: AmB deoxycholate **1 mg/kg IV daily** initially (strong; low) · liposomal AmB 5 mg/kg daily alternative (strong; low) · flucytosine 25 mg/kg 4×/day as salvage, with frequent adverse effects (weak; low) · step down to fluconazole **12 mg/kg daily** if susceptible (strong; low) · continue until all signs, symptoms, cerebrospinal fluid and radiological abnormalities resolve (strong; low) · **remove infected CNS devices** (strong; low).
- **51–53.** Neonatal ICU prophylaxis: in nurseries with **>10%** invasive candidiasis, fluconazole **3–6 mg/kg twice weekly × 6 weeks** for birth weight **<1000 g** (strong; high) · oral nystatin 100 000 U 3×/day × 6 weeks an alternative at **<1500 g** where fluconazole is unavailable or resistance precludes it (weak; moderate) · oral bovine lactoferrin 100 mg/day may be effective <1500 g but **is not available in US hospitals** (weak; moderate).

### X–XV. Intravascular, osteoarticular, ocular, CNS, urinary, vulvovaginal (59–121)
- **59–64. Endocarditis.** Native valve: lipid AmB 3–5 mg/kg daily ± flucytosine 25 mg/kg 4×/day, OR **high-dose** echinocandin (caspofungin 150 mg, micafungin 150 mg, anidulafungin 200 mg daily) (strong; low) · step down to fluconazole 400–800 mg daily when susceptible, stable and cleared (strong; low) · voriconazole 200–300 mg bid or posaconazole tablets 300 mg daily for fluconazole-non-susceptible isolates (weak; very low) · **valve replacement recommended**, with ≥6 weeks of therapy after surgery and longer with perivalvular abscess (strong; low) · no surgery possible → long-term fluconazole suppression (strong; low) · prosthetic valve: same regimens **plus chronic suppression** with fluconazole 400–800 mg daily (strong; low).
- **65–69. Cardiac devices.** Remove the entire pacemaker/defibrillator (strong; moderate) · antifungal therapy as for native valve endocarditis (strong; low) · generator-pocket infection **4 weeks** after removal (strong; low) · wire involvement **≥6 weeks** after wire removal (strong; low) · unremovable ventricular assist device → endocarditis regimen plus fluconazole suppression while the device stays in (strong; low).
- **70–73. Suppurative thrombophlebitis.** Catheter removal and incision/drainage or vein resection where feasible (strong; low) · lipid AmB, fluconazole 400–800 mg daily, or high-dose echinocandin for **≥2 weeks after candidemia clears** (strong; low) · step down to fluconazole when stable and susceptible (strong; low) · **thrombus resolution can be used as the stop signal** with supportive clinical and culture data (strong; low).
- **74–81. Osteoarticular.** Osteomyelitis: fluconazole 400 mg daily × **6–12 months**, OR echinocandin ≥2 weeks → fluconazole to complete 6–12 months (strong; low) · lipid AmB ≥2 weeks → fluconazole a less attractive alternative (weak; low) · **surgical debridement in selected cases** (strong; low). Septic arthritis: fluconazole 400 mg daily × **6 weeks**, OR echinocandin 2 weeks → fluconazole ≥4 weeks (strong; low) · lipid AmB route less attractive (weak; low) · **surgical drainage in all cases** (strong; moderate) · **remove an infected prosthesis** (strong; moderate) · if not removable, chronic fluconazole suppression (strong; low).
- **82–91. Endophthalmitis.** Dilated retinal examination in all candidemia **within the first week** (delay until neutrophil recovery if neutropenic) (strong; low) · extent graded by an ophthalmologist (strong; low) · **treatment and surgical decisions made jointly by ophthalmology and infectious diseases** (strong; low). Chorioretinitis **without** vitritis: fluconazole 800 mg load → 400–800 mg daily, or voriconazole 400 mg IV bid × 2 → 300 mg bid, if susceptible (strong; low) · resistant isolates → liposomal AmB 3–5 mg/kg ± flucytosine (strong; low) · **macular involvement adds intravitreal AmB deoxycholate 5–10 µg/0.1 mL or voriconazole 100 µg/0.1 mL** (strong; low) · duration **≥4–6 weeks**, guided by repeat examination (strong; low). **With** vitritis: the same systemic therapy **plus intravitreal injection** (strong; low) · consider **vitrectomy** (strong; low) · duration ≥4–6 weeks by examination (strong; low).
- **92–96. CNS candidiasis.** Liposomal AmB 5 mg/kg daily ± flucytosine 25 mg/kg 4×/day initially (strong; low) · step down to fluconazole 400–800 mg daily (strong; low) · continue until all signs, symptoms, cerebrospinal fluid and radiological abnormalities resolve (strong; low) · **remove infected CNS devices** (strong; low) · unremovable ventricular device → intraventricular AmB deoxycholate 0.01–0.5 mg in 2 mL 5% dextrose (weak; low).
- **97–114. Urinary tract.** Asymptomatic candiduria: **remove predisposing factors** (strong; low) · **do not treat** unless at high risk of dissemination — neutropenia, very-low-birth-weight infants (<1500 g), planned urologic manipulation (strong; low) · treat the first two groups as for candidemia (strong; low) · before/after urologic procedures, fluconazole 400 mg daily or AmB deoxycholate 0.3–0.6 mg/kg daily for several days (strong; low). Symptomatic cystitis: fluconazole **200 mg (3 mg/kg) daily × 2 weeks** if susceptible (strong; moderate) · fluconazole-resistant *C. glabrata* → AmB deoxycholate 0.3–0.6 mg/kg daily × 1–7 days OR flucytosine 25 mg/kg 4×/day × 7–10 days (strong; low) · *C. krusei* → AmB deoxycholate × 1–7 days (strong; low) · **remove the bladder catheter** (strong; low) · AmB bladder irrigation 50 mg/L daily × 5 days may help fluconazole-resistant species (weak; low). Pyelonephritis: fluconazole 200–400 mg daily × 2 weeks (strong; low) · resistant *C. glabrata* → AmB ± flucytosine (strong; low) or flucytosine monotherapy × 2 weeks (weak; low) · *C. krusei* → AmB deoxycholate × 1–7 days (strong; low) · **relieve urinary obstruction** (strong; low) · consider removing/replacing nephrostomy tubes or stents (weak; low). Fungus balls: **surgery strongly recommended in adults** (strong; low) · antifungals as for cystitis/pyelonephritis (strong; low) · nephrostomy irrigation with AmB deoxycholate 25–50 mg in 200–500 mL sterile water (strong; low).
- **115–121. Vulvovaginal.** Uncomplicated disease: topical antifungals, **no agent superior to another** (strong; high) · or a **single oral fluconazole 150 mg dose** (strong; high) · severe acute disease: fluconazole 150 mg **every 72 h × 2–3 doses** (strong; high) · azole-unresponsive *C. glabrata*: intravaginal boric acid 600 mg daily × 14 days (strong; low), nystatin suppositories 100 000 U daily × 14 days (strong; low), or topical 17% flucytosine cream ± 3% AmB cream daily × 14 days (weak; low) · recurrent disease: 10–14 days of induction then **fluconazole 150 mg weekly × 6 months** (strong; high).

## Relevance to Wiki
- **[[infectious-esophagitis]]** — the page's single open decision gap was **what to do when fluconazole fails**; recommendations 136–139 supply it (itraconazole solution or voriconazole × 14–21 days; echinocandin or AmB alternatives; posaconazole as a weak option; suppression for recurrent disease). Also adds the IV and de-escalation options for patients who cannot swallow (133–135) and the "treat empirically before endoscopy" rule (131), which sits alongside the existing [[aga-2024-esophageal-immunity-infection]] first-line dose.
- **[[acute-pancreatitis]]** — recommendation 54 names **necrotizing pancreatitis** as a risk factor that justifies empiric antifungal therapy when there is clinical evidence of intra-abdominal infection; 55 makes **source control** part of treatment and 57 ties duration to it.
- Non-GI questions (candidemia, endocarditis, ocular, CNS, urinary, vulvovaginal, neonatal) are recorded here for completeness and are not propagated to entity pages — no GI decision rests on them.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **No contradiction with [[aga-2024-esophageal-immunity-infection]].** AGA 2024 gives fluconazole 200–400 mg/day × 14–21 days for *Candida* esophagitis; IDSA 2016 recommendation 132 is the same dose and duration. The two are complementary — AGA stops at first-line, IDSA continues into the refractory and intolerant pathways.
- **Empiric treatment before endoscopy (131) vs an endoscopic diagnosis.** IDSA treats a diagnostic antifungal trial as the appropriate first step; the wiki's endoscopic criteria for *Candida* (white nummular plaques, brushings) come from AGA 2024 and apply when the trial fails or the diagnosis is in doubt. Both positions stand.
- **Species-level susceptibility is assumed, not available.** Most of the refractory-pathway choices turn on whether the isolate is azole-susceptible, and esophageal disease is usually managed without culture or susceptibility testing. The guideline does not address how to choose a refractory agent when no isolate is available.
- IDSA is **not a GI society**; it enters the wiki under the rule that a non-GI guideline is used only where a GI decision depends on it (here, the esophageal-candidiasis pathway).
