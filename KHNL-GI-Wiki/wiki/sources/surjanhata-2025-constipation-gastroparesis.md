## Summary
Post hoc analysis of prospectively collected data from a 10-center cohort of patients presenting with upper-gastrointestinal (UGI) symptoms suggestive of gastroparesis, who underwent **concurrent wireless motility capsule (WMC) and gastric emptying scintigraphy (GES)**. Fifty-six subjects were recommended a new or changed **constipation medication** based on transit-test interpretation; Gastroparesis Cardinal Symptom Index (GCSI), upper abdominal pain, and constipation scores were compared from baseline to 6 months, stratified by gastric emptying status (delayed vs non-delayed) and colonic transit (slow vs normal).

Core message: in patients with **both delayed gastric emptying and slow colonic transit**, identifying and treating the colonic delay improves global UGI symptoms — supporting whole-gut evaluation (not gastric emptying alone). Slow colonic transit *without* delayed gastric emptying did not improve significantly.

### Population and entry criteria
- Entry by **symptoms, not by scintigraphy**: age 18–80 y, **≥2 UGI symptoms** suggestive of gastroparesis (nausea, vomiting, upper abdominal pain, early satiety, postprandial fullness) for **≥12 weeks** as the primary complaint. Upper endoscopy or radiology excluded organic disorders.
- Most subjects met **Rome III functional dyspepsia** criteria (71%–100% across subgroups), and GES delay was present in only a minority (6%–47% by subgroup) — this is a symptom-defined, not scintigraphy-confirmed, gastroparesis population.
- **Excluded:** dysphagia; previous gastrointestinal (GI) surgery (except appendectomy, cholecystectomy, hysterectomy); any abdominopelvic surgery within 3 months; [[inflammatory-bowel-disease]]; chronic nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug use; [[diverticulitis]]; intestinal strictures; hemoglobin A1c >10%; implanted cardiac devices; body mass index >40 kg/m².
- 167 enrolled → management plans for 150 → 135 analyzable → **56** received a new or changed constipation therapy (36 with delayed colonic transit, 20 with normal).

### Transit cut-points used (the stratifying thresholds)
- **Slow colonic transit** = WMC colonic transit time **>59 h**.
- **Delayed gastric emptying** = WMC gastric emptying time **>5 h** (the subgroup label is WMC-defined, not GES-defined).

## Key Findings / Claims
- Subjects with **slow colonic transit** improved in GCSI (mean difference −0.58, P = 0.007) and constipation scores (mean change −0.81, P = 0.004) after new/changed constipation therapy.
- The **delayed-gastric-emptying + slow-colonic-transit subgroup drove GCSI improvement** (mean improvement −0.96, P = 0.004), with reductions in nausea/vomiting (P = 0.02) and early satiety/fullness (P = 0.002); trend for bloating/distention (P = 0.06). Upper abdominal pain unchanged (P = 0.15).
- **Slow colonic transit with non-delayed gastric emptying:** no significant GCSI change (mean −0.30, P = 0.29); only early satiety/fullness trended to improvement (P = 0.07).
- Subjects with **normal colonic transit showed no GCSI improvement** regardless of gastric emptying status (all P > 0.05), and constipation scores did not improve (mean −0.50, P = 0.29).
- Constipation agents actually used (investigator's choice): **linaclotide** (38.9% delayed / 35% normal colonic transit), **PEG-3350** (19.4% / 40%), **lubiprostone** (13.9% / 5%), **pyridostigmine** (11.1% / 10%), **misoprostol** (0 / 5%) — no significant difference in agent choice between transit groups.
- Slow colonic transit is detected in up to one-third of patients presenting with gastroparesis symptoms, alone or with other regional gut delays — delays that gastric-emptying testing alone misses.

## Relevance to Wiki
- [[gastroparesis]] → Clinical Pearls / Diagnostics: primary-evidence support for whole-gut assessment (WMC) and for treating coexisting slow colonic transit to improve upper-GI symptoms.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- Observational cohort, not randomized or case-controlled, and a secondary analysis not powered for the subgroup comparisons — hypothesis-generating; no conflict with [[acg-2022-gastroparesis]] / [[aga-2025-gastroparesis]].
- Constipation regimens were individualized and open-label, so the specific agent driving benefit is not defined; no between-agent comparison was possible.
- The normal-colonic-transit / delayed-gastric-emptying group was very small (n = 4), so the absence of benefit there is weakly supported.
- Adherence to constipation therapy at 6 months was not confirmed, and transit testing was not repeated — symptom improvement was never tied to measured transit improvement.
- **Dyssynergic defecation was not assessed or treated**, and no rectal therapy (bisacodyl suppository, enema, water irrigation) was among the management changes — the findings apply to oral constipation pharmacotherapy only.
