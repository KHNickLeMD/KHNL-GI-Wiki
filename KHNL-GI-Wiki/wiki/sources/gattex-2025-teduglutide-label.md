## Summary

The FDA prescribing information for **teduglutide**, the only glucagon-like peptide-2 (GLP-2) analogue approved in the US and the single agent behind every GLP-2 recommendation the wiki carries. It was fetched to close a dose gap the wiki had declared on its own front matter: the American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) clinical practice updates behind [[glp-2-agonists]] and [[short-bowel-syndrome]] place GLP-2 analogues in the management ladder but **give no dose, no monitoring schedule and no adverse-effect detail**.

The dose is **0.05 mg/kg once daily by subcutaneous injection**, for adults and for children ≥1 year alike, with **one adjustment: halve it to 0.025 mg/kg once daily** when the estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) is **<60 mL/min/1.73 m²** — moderate or severe renal impairment, or end-stage renal disease. Hepatic impairment needs no adjustment at Child-Pugh A or B, and the drug has **not been studied in Child-Pugh C**. There are **no contraindications**.

What makes this label worth a wiki page beyond the dose is the **endoscopic surveillance program it mandates**, which is a gastroenterologist's job and is specified nowhere else in the corpus. Because teduglutide is intestinotrophic, the label requires **colonoscopy plus upper endoscopy with polypectomy within the 6 months before starting** in adults, **repeat at the end of the first year**, and — if no polyp is found — **no less frequently than every 5 years** thereafter. **A gastrointestinal (GI) cancer diagnosed on therapy means stopping the drug.** The baseline and 6-monthly laboratory set (bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase, lipase, amylase) exists for the same reason: cholecystitis, cholelithiasis, cholangitis and pancreatitis were all seen in the trials.

Three further safety signals drive real decisions. **Intestinal or stomal obstruction** was reported in 4% at the recommended dose and in 9% at twice it, with no cases on placebo — the drug is temporarily stopped and may be restarted once the obstruction resolves. **Fluid overload and congestive heart failure** follow from the enhanced absorption the drug is prescribed for; parenteral support is adjusted rather than the drug automatically stopped. And the same enhanced absorption raises levels of **concomitant oral drugs** — the label's one worked example is a patient on prazepam who progressed to coma in the first week of therapy and recovered 5 days after both drugs were stopped.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Indication**

- Adults and pediatric patients **1 year of age and older** with **short bowel syndrome (SBS) who are dependent on parenteral support**. Safety and effectiveness below 1 year are not established.

**Dose and administration**

- **0.05 mg/kg once daily, subcutaneous** — the same dose in adults and children. Subcutaneous only; **never intravenous or intramuscular**.
- **Renal impairment: 0.025 mg/kg once daily** (half) when eGFR is **<60 mL/min/1.73 m²** — moderate, severe, or end-stage renal disease. Exposure rises 1.5-, 1.7- and 2.6-fold (area under the curve) across those three strata.
- **Hepatic impairment: no adjustment** for Child-Pugh A or B (exposure 10–15% lower in B); **not studied in Child-Pugh C**.
- **Missed dose:** take it the same day; **never 2 doses in one day**.
- Rotate injection sites — one of the 4 abdominal quadrants, or alternating thighs or arms.
- **Contraindications: none.**
- **Preparation:** reconstitute the 5 mg vial with the supplied 0.5 mL sterile water → **10 mg/mL**; stand 30 s, roll between the palms ~15 s (**do not shake**), stand ~2 min, inspect. **A maximum of 0.38 mL (3.8 mg) can be withdrawn per vial.** Use within **3 hours**; single use; do not freeze. If still undissolved after a second attempt, discard.
- The **5 mg kit is not recommended in children under 10 kg**, and self-administration has not been tested in children.

**Pre-treatment evaluation — within the 6 months before starting**

| Population | Required before starting |
|---|---|
| Adults | **Colonoscopy and upper endoscopy, with removal of any polyps**; bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase, lipase, amylase |
| Children | **Fecal occult blood testing** — if new or unexplained blood in stool, **colonoscopy/sigmoidoscopy and upper endoscopy**; same four laboratory tests |

**On-treatment surveillance**

- **Adults:** repeat colonoscopy and upper endoscopy (or alternate imaging) **at the end of year 1**. If **no polyp** is found, repeat **no less frequently than every 5 years**. If a polyp is found, follow standard polyp-surveillance intervals.
- **Children:** **fecal occult blood testing annually**; colonoscopy/sigmoidoscopy **after 1 year and every 5 years thereafter** on continuous therapy; upper endoscopy to be considered.
- **Laboratory testing every 6 months** (bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase, lipase, amylase), or more often if needed. A clinically meaningful rise → image the biliary tract, liver or pancreas and **reassess whether to continue**.
- **Diagnosis of GI cancer → discontinue.** Non-GI malignancy → continue or stop on a benefit–risk judgment.

**Warnings that change management**

- **Acceleration of neoplastic growth.** Hyperplastic and neoplastic change is the mechanism-level concern (rat and mouse carcinogenicity studies: bile-duct and jejunal adenomas, gallbladder papillary adenomas, jejunal adenocarcinoma at high dose). 14 adult trial patients were diagnosed with GI-tract polyps after starting; postmarketing reports include colorectal, gastric and small-intestinal polyps.
- **Intestinal obstruction.** **4% (3/77) at 0.05 mg/kg/day vs 9% (3/32) at twice the dose; none on placebo.** Onset 1 day to 6 months. **Temporarily discontinue**, manage clinically, and restart if appropriate. Of 8 obstruction episodes in the extension studies, **2 needed endoscopic dilation and 1 needed surgery**.
- **Biliary and pancreatic disease.** Cholecystitis, cholangitis and cholelithiasis reported; 3 adults developed cholecystitis (all with prior gallbladder disease, 1 with perforation requiring next-day cholecystectomy). Pancreatitis, chronic pancreatitis and a pancreatic pseudocyst also reported.
- **Fluid overload and congestive heart failure (CHF).** Peripheral edema 10% vs 3% on placebo; fluid overload 1% vs 0%; **2 cases of CHF (3%)**. Deemed related to the enhanced fluid absorption the drug produces. **Adjust parenteral support** and reassess, particularly with underlying cardiovascular disease.
- **Stopping the drug also destabilizes fluid balance** — monitor fluid and electrolytes after discontinuation.
- **Increased absorption of concomitant oral medications.** Monitor drugs requiring titration or with a narrow therapeutic index; **the other drug's dose may need reducing.** Worked example: a patient on prazepam plus teduglutide 0.05 mg/kg daily deteriorated to coma within the first week (prazepam level >300 mcg/L), resolving 5 days after both were stopped.

**Adverse reactions (≥10% on teduglutide and more than placebo, adults, n=77 vs 59 placebo)**

| Reaction | Teduglutide | Placebo |
|---|---|---|
| Abdominal pain | 30% | 22% |
| Nausea | 23% | 20% |
| Upper respiratory tract infection | 21% | 12% |
| **Abdominal distension** | **20%** | **2%** |
| Injection-site reaction | 13% | 12% |
| Vomiting | 12% | 10% |
| Fluid overload | 12% | 7% |
| Hypersensitivity | 10% | 7% |

- **Stoma complications in the subset with a stoma: 42% (13/31) vs 14% (3/22) on placebo.**
- Less common: colonic stenosis, pancreatic duct stenosis, small intestinal stenosis, dyspnea.
- **Anti-teduglutide antibodies rise steadily with exposure** — 3% at month 3, 17% at 6, 24% at 12, 33% at 24, **48% at month 30** in adults; 54% at month 12 in children. **No association with loss of efficacy, altered pharmacokinetics or adverse events** was observed.

**Specific populations**

- **Pregnancy:** no drug-associated risk identified in case reports; untreated SBS malnutrition itself carries preterm delivery, low birth weight, growth restriction and perinatal mortality risk.
- **Lactation: breastfeeding is not recommended**, on the basis of potential tumorigenicity.
- **Geriatric:** no overall difference in safety or efficacy in the 19 patients ≥65; experience ≥75 is limited.

**Efficacy (for context — not a basis for cards)**

- **Study 1** (24 weeks, n=86): responders — defined as **≥20% reduction in weekly parenteral support volume at both weeks 20 and 24** — **63% vs 30% placebo (p=0.002)**; mean reduction **4.4 vs 2.3 L/week**; **≥1 day/week off parenteral support in 54% vs 23%**.
- **Study 3** (24 weeks): response **46% vs 6% placebo** at the recommended dose; the higher 0.1 mg/kg/day dose **missed its primary endpoint** and is not a recommended dosage.
- **Long term (Study 2, 30 months, n=30):** 93% achieved ≥20% reduction, mean reduction **7.55 L/week (66%)**, and **10 patients were weaned off parenteral support entirely** after having needed it for 1.2–15.5 years.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **No contradiction with any ingested guideline.** The AGA clinical practice updates behind [[glp-2-agonists]] describe where GLP-2 analogues sit in management and are silent on dose, monitoring and harms; this label fills that silence rather than disputing it. Under *Source priority* a label does not outrank a guideline, and none of its content overlaps a guideline claim.
- **The label is not a guideline and no Anki card may be written from it** (cards come from tier-1 society guidelines, clinical practice updates and consensus statements only).
- **The surveillance program has no guideline counterpart in the corpus.** No ingested society document states when to scope a patient on teduglutide, so the label is the only source for an interval that a gastroenterologist is the one asked to deliver.
- **No dose-escalation, de-escalation or stopping rule for efficacy.** The label gives a fixed dose with no titration and no duration limit; trial patients were kept on the drug even after weaning off parenteral support entirely. Nothing in the corpus says when, or whether, to stop a responder.
- **Pediatric dosing below 1 year is not established**, and the 5 mg kit is not recommended under 10 kg — but no alternative presentation is named.
