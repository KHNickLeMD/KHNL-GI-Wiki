## Summary

The European Association for the Study of the Liver (EASL) update of its 2015 autoimmune hepatitis (AIH) guideline, covering adults and children, variant and specific forms, AIH-related cirrhosis and liver transplantation (LT). Twenty-eight PICO questions were set by the panel, each requiring ≥75% Delphi agreement from a 40-member international panel (including two patient representatives) before the literature search; evidence was graded by the Oxford Centre for Evidence-based Medicine (OCEBM) and recommendation strength reduced to two categories, strong or weak/open.

Three practice-changing positions distinguish it from earlier guidance. **Mycophenolate mofetil (MMF) is promoted to a first-line alternative to azathioprine** on the strength of the CAMARO randomised-controlled trial (RCT) (complete biochemical response [CBR] at 6 months 72.2% vs 32.3%; p = 0.004) and real-world cohorts. **Budesonide is removed from first-line therapy** and is contraindicated in cirrhosis, retained only as a switch option for predniso(lo)ne-dependent non-cirrhotic patients. **Response is defined by the International Autoimmune Hepatitis Group (IAIHG) endpoint set** — CBR = normalisation of aminotransferases *and* immunoglobulin class G (IgG) no later than 6–12 months; insufficient response, non-response, histological remission and intolerance each get an explicit definition.

The document also drops autoantibody subtyping of adult AIH as clinically unhelpful, makes liver biopsy a requirement for diagnosis (reported by the 2022 International AIH Pathology Group [IAHPG] categories *likely / possible / unlikely*), and offers a pragmatic compromise on biopsy before treatment withdrawal — generally desirable, mandatory in nobody, but recommended at least in patients with cirrhosis, and not justified when the patient has already decided to stop regardless of the result.

Beyond the core pathway it gives operative detail on thiopurine metabolite-guided dose optimisation, third-line rescue dosing (tacrolimus, infliximab, rituximab, belimumab), pregnancy, acute severe AIH, drug-induced autoimmune-like hepatitis (DI-ALH), decompensated cirrhosis, paediatric dosing, and post-LT recurrence and plasma cell-rich rejection hepatitis.

## Grading System

**Level of evidence (LoE), OCEBM:**

| Level | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 1 | Systematic reviews (with homogeneity) of RCTs |
| 2 | RCT or observational studies with dramatic effects; systematic review of lower quality (non-randomised, retrospective) studies |
| 3 | Non-randomised controlled cohort / follow-up study / control arm of a randomised trial |
| 4 | Case-series, case-control, or historically controlled studies |
| 5 | Expert opinion (mechanism-based reasoning) |

Levels 1–2 ≈ "further research is unlikely to change our confidence in the estimate of benefit and risk"; 4–5 ≈ "any estimate of effect is uncertain".

**Grade of recommendation:** only two categories.

| Grade | Wording used |
|---|---|
| **Strong** | shall, should, is recommended / shall not, should not, is not recommended |
| **Weak or open** | can, may, is suggested / may not, is not suggested |

**Delphi consensus labels** (neutral votes not counted): ≥75% to <90% = *consensus*; ≥90% = *strong consensus*. Statements (as opposed to recommendations) carry a LoE and a consensus label but no strength grade.

*Recommendations below are not numbered in the document; headings and bullet order are the wiki's.*

## Key Recommendations

### Clinical Spectrum and Natural History

- **Statement:** clinical manifestations of AIH vary from asymptomatic to acute liver failure (ALF) and present at any fibrosis stage regardless of age, sex and ethnicity (*LoE 2, strong consensus*).
- Subclassifying **adult** patients into subtypes by autoantibody profile **cannot be recommended** (*LoE 3, weak recommendation, consensus*).
- **Statement:** long-term complications of AIH relate to disease progression and cancer risk as in any other aetiology of liver disease (*LoE 2, strong consensus*).
- Surveillance and early recognition of disease complications recommended in **all** patients with AIH (*LoE 5, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Monitoring for complications including portal hypertension and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) recommended in AIH-related cirrhosis **as per dedicated guidelines** (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

### Variant Syndromes — When to Suspect

- Consider a variant syndrome of AIH with primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC) or primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) **whenever concomitant cholestatic features are present** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Consider underlying/associated sclerosing cholangitis in **every case of childhood AIH** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Magnetic resonance cholangiography** recommended in the initial work-up of **all childhood AIH**, independent of cholestatic enzymes, and of **young adults** with cholestasis or not achieving CBR; **repeat** when disease activity or cholestatic features remain on follow-up (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Investigate for **PBC-specific autoantibodies before any other test** in all adults with AIH and biochemical cholestasis (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Magnetic resonance cholangiography recommended in adults with cholestatic features, at diagnosis or during follow-up, **when PBC-specific autoantibody testing is negative** (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

### Diagnosis

- Diagnosis should be based on **distinct IgG elevation + autoantibodies + likely or possible liver histology** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Careful exclusion of all known causes of acute and chronic liver disease recommended, **although coexistence with metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), alcohol-related liver disease (ALD) or viral hepatitis is possible** (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Simplified diagnostic criteria** recommended in clinical practice **if rodent tissue sections are used** for antinuclear antibody (ANA) and smooth muscle antibody (SMA) detection (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- The **updated** simplified criteria can be applied if HEp-2 cells or enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISAs) are used for ANA/SMA (*LoE 3, weak recommendation, consensus*).
- The **IAHPG 2022 consensus histological criteria** can be substituted into the simplified score, as they may increase sensitivity (*LoE 3, weak recommendation, strong consensus*).
- In paediatric patients the **revised 2018 ESPGHAN scoring system** can be used (*LoE 3, weak recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Apply the diagnostic scores **with caution** in acute forms of AIH, variants, concurrent liver disease and DI-ALH (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Suspect AIH in **all** patients with elevated aminotransferases of unknown aetiology **irrespective of the level of increase**, especially with elevated IgG and circulating autoantibodies (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Suspect AIH in all patients with **cirrhosis of unknown aetiology even without aminotransferase elevation** (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Normal IgG does not exclude AIH** (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

### Autoantibody Testing

- First screening for **ANA, SMA, anti-liver kidney microsomal type 1 (anti-LKM1), anti-liver cytosol type 1 (anti-LC1) by immunofluorescence testing (IFT) on triple rodent tissue sections**, in parallel with **anti-soluble liver antigen/liver pancreas (anti-SLA/LP) by solid phase assay** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- If IFT is negative, **re-test at a lower dilution — 1:40 in adults, 1:10 in children** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Clinical laboratories should comply with AIH guidelines for **both reporting cut-offs and techniques used** (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

### Liver Biopsy

- **Liver biopsy is required to establish the diagnosis of AIH** (*LoE 1, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- The histology report should include **grading of necroinflammatory activity, staging of fibrosis, and classification as likely / possible / unlikely AIH** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

### Differential Diagnosis and DI-ALH

- Differential diagnosis should include causes of liver disease appropriate to the presentation (acute hepatitis, chronic hepatitis, cirrhosis) **as well as extrahepatic entities such as coeliac disease and systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE)** (*LoE 1, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Drug-induced liver injury (DILI) with an **autoimmune phenotype** — autoantibodies, high IgG and/or histological evidence of autoimmunity — should be considered **possible DI-ALH** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Distinguish DI-ALH from AIH **by treatment response and disease course**: resolution after withdrawal of the implicated agent, with or without a short corticosteroid course, and **no relapse in the long term**, points to DI-ALH (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

**Supporting detail:** clinical, biochemical, serological and histological features are otherwise indistinguishable; the only histological exception is advanced fibrosis/cirrhosis, less common in DI-ALH. No single serological biomarker exists. The guideline reports **Björnsson's five proposed criteria** (externally unvalidated): (1) a substance triggering liver injury with indicators of autoimmunity (elevation of any of ANA, SMA or IgG) and a biopsy compatible with AIH by the simplified criteria; (2) incomplete/no recovery or worsening of aminotransferases after drug cessation; (3) need for corticosteroids or spontaneous recovery; (4) no relapse for at least 6 months after corticosteroid withdrawal; (5) a drug potentially inducing DI-ALH with a chronic course. **The first four define probable DI-ALH; three suggest possible disease.** Fig. 2 gives the differential-diagnosis algorithm (redrawn on [[drug-induced-liver-injury]]).

### Indication to Treat

- Treatment should aim at **complete biochemical, clinical and histological remission** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Therapy recommended **to reduce morbidity and mortality and improve quality of life** (*LoE 1, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Immunosuppression recommended in **all patients with active disease, including those with advanced fibrosis and/or compensated cirrhosis** (*LoE 1, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

### Pretreatment Evaluation

- **Hepatitis A virus (HAV) and hepatitis B virus (HBV) vaccination** for all susceptible patients (*LoE 5, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- All other vaccinations (influenza, SARS-CoV-2, *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, etc.) per national guidelines (*LoE 5, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Screen for autoimmune thyroid and coeliac disease in all patients at diagnosis** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*) — thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), T4, anti-thyroid antibodies and coeliac-related antibodies.
- **Dual energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA) in all adults at treatment initiation** (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

### Induction Therapy and Tapering

- **Predniso(lo)ne ≥0.5 mg/kg/day, potentially up to 1 mg/kg/day in more severe and advanced disease**, combined with **either**:
  - **azathioprine** — only when **bilirubin <6 mg/dl**, ideally started **2 weeks after** the corticosteroid, initial **50 mg/day**, up to a final **1–2 mg/kg/day**; **or**
  - **MMF 1.5–2 g/day**
  — as **first-line treatment** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, consensus*).
- Induction and corticosteroid tapering should be **individualised according to CBR status** (*LoE 4, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **MMF is teratogenic; counsel both female and male patients** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

**Supporting detail:** rapid tapering (e.g. 5–10 mg every 1–2 weeks) is desirable but must follow response. Azathioprine must **never** be used alone as induction (high mortality), and is avoided in acute severe AIH and decompensated cirrhosis, with caution in thiopurine methyltransferase (TPMT) deficiency, pregnancy, cytopenias and malignancy. Higher predniso(lo)ne (1 mg/kg/day) gave higher response rates than 0.5 mg/kg/day in a 25-study, 3,305-patient meta-analysis, at the cost of more adverse events. MMF requires a negative pregnancy test and two methods of contraception, continued **to at least 12 weeks after** drug withdrawal.

### Monitoring on Treatment

- Treatment-related adverse events should be **pro-actively managed and anticipated** (*LoE 5, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Laboratory and clinical assessment **individualised** by disease severity, treatment response and tolerance (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Adequate calcium intake and vitamin D supplementation** considered on long-term corticosteroids (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Regular non-invasive fibrosis monitoring by transient elastography (TE)** recommended (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

**Supporting detail:** aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), prothrombin time, fasting glucose and full blood count **weekly for the first month** on azathioprine (toxicity peaks in the first 6 weeks); on MMF the same panel at **4 weeks** suffices. Then clinical/laboratory monitoring **including IgG every 2–3 months** to determine CBR at 6 and 12 months; once CBR is attained, **every 3–6 months**. Thyroid function **every 6–12 months**. Serial liver stiffness measurement (LSM) e.g. **every 12 months**; fibrosis can be assessed accurately **after 6 months** of immunosuppression; cut-offs **9 kPa** for severe fibrosis and **12.5–16 kPa** for cirrhosis. Baseline and periodic **intraocular pressure** measurement is recommended on corticosteroids. Routine cancer screening applies as for the general population (extrahepatic cancers in up to 5%).

### Budesonide

- **Budesonide is not recommended as part of first-line treatment and is contraindicated in cirrhosis** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, consensus*).
- Switching to budesonide **may be suggested** because of corticosteroid side effects in **non-cirrhotic, predniso(lo)ne-dependent** patients (*LoE 3, weak recommendation, strong consensus*).

**Supporting detail:** contraindicated in cirrhosis also because of the high risk of portal vein thrombosis. The positive RCT (budesonide 3 mg three times daily vs prednisone 40 mg tapered to 10 mg/day, both with azathioprine 1–2 mg/kg/day) was not replicated: a 381-patient retrospective series found normalisation of aminotransferases and IgG at 6 months higher with predniso(lo)ne (52%) than budesonide (37%), and the side-effect advantage disappeared once cirrhotic patients were excluded. A paediatric version of the RCT (n = 46) found no difference (16% vs 15%).

### Treatment Withdrawal

- Because of the chronic nature of AIH, **the majority should receive long-term, often lifelong, immunosuppression** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- A stopping trial should only be attempted in carefully selected patients **if low-dose monotherapy has maintained stable CBR for at least 2 years** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Immunosuppression should be **reduced stepwise** — flares during dose reduction are frequent (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Patients with **anti-SLA/LP reactivity may need permanent immunosuppression** (*LoE 3, weak recommendation, consensus*).
- Assess disease activity individually using **aminotransferases, IgG and/or liver biopsy** before a cessation trial, because residual activity predicts relapse (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Patient priorities should be included** in the decision (*LoE 5, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Monitor for relapse **at least every 3 months in the first year** after cessation, then individually — relapses may occur many years or decades later (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **After a relapse following first withdrawal, subsequent attempts are not recommended** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

**Supporting detail on biopsy:** no clear positive or negative recommendation can be given; it is uncertain whether biopsy beats AST/ALT and IgG for confirming remission. It "appears a wise compromise" to biopsy before a withdrawal trial **at least in patients with cirrhosis**, where AST and IgG can be normal while histology shows interface activity. If the patient is determined to stop regardless of the result, **biopsy is not justified**; LSM by TE is the non-invasive alternative. Favourable predictors: aminotransferases in the **lower range of normal**, **IgG below 12 g/L**, CBR duration **≥24 months**, treatment duration **≥4 years**, no prior relapses, anti-SLA/LP negative, ALT **below half the ULN** and IgG **<1,200 mg/dl** at withdrawal. Dual-drug immunosuppression at cessation doubled the relapse risk vs monotherapy. Real-world corticosteroid tapering to withdrawal takes **6–8 weeks to 3–4 months**. After stopping, check aminotransferases and IgG **every 3–4 weeks for 3 months**, then 3-monthly for the first year, **every 3–6 months for the next 3 years**, then annually indefinitely. Up to 75% report myalgia/arthralgia for up to 12 months or more after cessation.

### Maintenance Therapy

- **Statement:** in patients achieving CBR, maintenance treatment should be continued to reduce relapse risk and prevent progression (*LoE 2, strong consensus*).
- Maintenance should consist of **azathioprine or MMF as monotherapy, or combined with low-dose corticosteroid (predniso(lo)ne ≤5 mg/day)**; the dose is adapted to sustain stable CBR (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Monitor for treatment-related complications during maintenance (*LoE 5, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Low-dose predniso(lo)ne monotherapy can be suggested only** in patients with **mild disease** who achieved CBR and are **intolerant to both azathioprine and MMF** (*LoE 3, weak recommendation, consensus*).

**Supporting detail:** total immunosuppression duration **at least 4 years, with at least the last 2 years in sustained CBR**. Continuing corticosteroids beyond 3 months after ALT normalises was not associated with better outcomes in a 1,267-patient UK audit. Azathioprine continued alone at **2 mg/kg/day** reduced relapse to zero over 12 months and 17% over 5 years. Thiopurine metabolites can optimise therapy and separate true relapse from poor adherence.

### Relapse and Flares

- Monitor aminotransferases and IgG because of the **high risk of flares and relapses** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Assess treatment adherence** in case of flare or relapse (*LoE 1, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Re-biopsy can be performed** to exclude other causes of elevated aminotransferases in suspected flare/relapse (*LoE 2, weak recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Flares and relapses should be treated with **short courses of corticosteroids and adjustment of maintenance therapy** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

**Supporting detail:** precise definitions are lacking; consider relapse/flare when **ALT rises to more than 2–3× ULN and/or IgG is above ULN** (the IgG rise usually precedes the ALT rise). **Relapse** = recurrence after CBR and treatment withdrawal; **flare** = lack of CBR during corticosteroid tapering in induction or during maintenance. Recheck for spontaneous normalisation and exclude ALD, DILI, viral hepatitis, MASLD, hepatic or portal vein thrombosis and biliary disease first. Re-biopsy is reasonable in acute-on-chronic liver failure (ACLF) or when another cause is suspected.

### Non-Adherence

- **Patient-centred consultations** to assess anxiety, depression and other reasons for suspected or confirmed non-adherence, including assessment of **capability, opportunity and motivation** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Early initiation of maintenance therapy** to enable corticosteroid dose reduction and withdrawal, improving confidence in the caregiver–patient relationship (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Test thiopurine metabolites to assess adherence**; undetectable or low 6-thioguanine nucleotides (6-TGN) and 6-methylmercaptopurine (6-MMP) should trigger a medication-management/side-effect discussion and a benefit–risk conversation (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

### Intolerance to First-Line Treatment

- **MMF is the second-line treatment of choice for intolerance or side effects to thiopurines** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Mercaptopurine (MP) or thioguanine (TG) can be used** in azathioprine intolerance (*LoE 4, weak recommendation, strong consensus*).

**Supporting detail:** azathioprine discontinuation for adverse effects ≈15% in the first year retrospectively, 36.5% in the first 6 months in the prospective European Reference Network registry; myelotoxicity and hepatotoxicity <2%. MMF discontinuation rate 0–8%, response in azathioprine-intolerant patients 62–92% (pooled 82%, 95% CI 77–87%). MP start **0.5 mg/kg/day → 1–1.5 mg/kg/day** or titrated to metabolites; complete response 40% (8/20). TG **20 mg/day**; complete response 66.7% (22/33). Tacrolimus in intolerance: start **1 mg twice daily or 2 mg once daily** depending on preparation, target trough **no more than 5 ng/ml**, watching renal function and blood pressure.

### Insufficient Response

- In insufficient response to thiopurine-based treatment, **measure 6-TGN and 6-MMP (if available)** to confirm adherence or sub-therapeutic levels — **proposed cut-off 223 pmol/8×10⁸ red blood cells** (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- After excluding non-adherence, **intensify by increasing azathioprine up to 2 mg/kg/day when 6-MMP is appropriately low** (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Adding allopurinol (contraindicated in pregnancy) with reduction of azathioprine to 25% of its dose, when 6-MMP is disproportionately increased**, can be suggested as the alternative (*LoE 3, weak recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **MMF may be used before third-line therapy** after unsuccessful intensification/optimisation of azathioprine-based therapy (*LoE 2, weak recommendation, consensus*).
- **Tacrolimus, infliximab, rituximab and belimumab can be used as third-line rescue therapies** in difficult-to-treat patients **in expert centres** (*LoE 4, weak recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Patients receiving third-line therapies should be **evaluated for opportunistic infection risk** with appropriate prophylaxis or vaccination (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

**Supporting detail:** insufficient response occurs in 22–68% on first-line azathioprine and 3–28% on first-line MMF. Allopurinol dose **100 mg/day**, with azathioprine reduced a few days before. 6-TGN therapeutic range **225–450 pmol/8×10⁸ erythrocytes**; 6-MMP "appropriately low" is **<5,700 pmol/0.2 ml**. If 6-TGN is above cut-off with high 6-MMP, exclude an alternative/concurrent diagnosis (Epstein-Barr virus, cytomegalovirus, MASLD/metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis [MASH] from corticosteroids, DILI from supplements) before reducing the thiopurine. In insufficient response to **first-line MMF**, switch to thiopurines before third-line therapy. MMF rescues 32–68% of azathioprine insufficient responders.

### Pregnancy

- **Maintenance with thiopurines (± corticosteroids) should be continued during pregnancy** (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **MMF should be withdrawn at least 3 months before conception** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- In **first presentations during pregnancy**, use standard therapeutic regimens **excluding MMF** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Pregnancy in patients with cirrhosis should be closely monitored by a **multidisciplinary obstetric–hepatology team** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

**Supporting detail:** meta-analysis of 14 studies, >1,400 patients — gestational diabetes odds ratio (OR) 2.84 (95% CI 1.78–4.54), hypertensive complications (preeclampsia, eclampsia, HELLP) OR 2.22 (1.76–2.79), premature birth OR 2.20 (1.66–2.91), small for gestational age OR 2.48 (1.37–4.51), low birth weight OR 3.04 (1.85–5.01). Neonatal mortality, stillbirth and congenital malformations **not** significantly increased, independent of immunosuppression. **Flares during pregnancy 13%; postpartum flares 41%, median 11 weeks after delivery**, requiring intensification. Corticosteroids and thiopurines are safe during pregnancy and breastfeeding. CBR at conception and during pregnancy reduces flares and prematurity.

### Acute Severe AIH

- **Early corticosteroid trial — predniso(lo)ne 0.5–1 mg/kg/day, or IV methylprednisolone at an equivalent dose — in acute severe AIH without ALF or ACLF; failure to improve after 3–7 days should trigger referral to an LT centre** (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Direct evaluation (discussion with an LT centre) for LT** in acute severe AIH **with ALF or ACLF**, where corticosteroid data are very limited and outcomes poor (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- If corticosteroids are given with ALF/ACLF, **strict surveillance for infection and close monitoring of efficacy** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

**Supporting detail:** acute severe AIH = icteric, **INR ≥1.5 but <2**, no hepatic encephalopathy, no chronic lesions on biopsy. Corticosteroid response in ALF/ACLF is only **8–41%**; transplant-free survival in acute severe AIH without ALF is **52–95.2%**. Most published patients received **1 mg/kg/day IV**, but **0.5 mg/kg/day is the pragmatic starting dose**, balancing response against infection risk at 1 mg/kg/day. Predictors of corticosteroid non-response include age, ascites, hepatic encephalopathy, INR, model for end-stage liver disease (MELD), organ failure, and the day 0–3 and day 0–7 deltas; the **SURFASA score** [6.80 + 1.92×(D0-INR) + 1.94×(Δ%3-INR) + 1.64×(Δ%3-bilirubin)] identifies a **>1.75** group with an 85–100% risk of death or transplantation.

### DI-ALH Treatment

- In suspected DI-ALH the potential causative agent should be **immediately withdrawn** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- A **short course of predniso(lo)ne** is recommended for severe hepatitis, impaired liver function, or **no improvement in liver tests within 30 days** of stopping the agent (*LoE 4, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Predniso(lo)ne 0.5 mg/kg/day initially, then rapid tapering to complete withdrawal within 1–2 months** (*LoE 5, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

**Supporting detail:** stopping the agent alone brings spontaneous recovery in about half of patients after a median of 70 days. Corticosteroid benefit was clearest in severe cases meeting **nR-based Hy's law (ALT >3× ULN and total bilirubin >2× ULN)** without biochemical resolution within 30 days. DI-ALH usually appears **within 3 months** of exposure; hypersensitivity signs (eosinophilia, rash, fever) are usually absent. Drugs with "highly probable" DI-ALH include minocycline, nitrofurantoin, hydralazine, methyldopa, interferon, imatinib, adalimumab, infliximab, statins and dietary supplements.

### Elderly Patients

- Elderly patients with **at least moderate activity** should receive **standard immunosuppression** (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- A **watch-and-wait strategy** is recommended in **elderly asymptomatic patients with mild activity and without advanced fibrosis** (*LoE 4, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Rapid corticosteroid tapering** is recommended because of the risk of aggravating osteoporosis and/or cardiovascular risk factors (*LoE 5, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

**Supporting detail:** elderly (≥65 years) achieve CBR at similar rates to younger patients, with **lower relapse rates**.

### Decompensated Cirrhosis

- Patients with AIH-related **decompensated cirrhosis should be evaluated for LT** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Corticosteroid treatment should be considered** in AIH-related decompensated cirrhosis **with signs of disease activity (elevated aminotransferases and/or modified hepatic activity index [mHAI] ≥4)** (*LoE 4, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

**Supporting detail:** median predniso(lo)ne dose in the reported series was **20–30 mg/day**. An IAIHG multicentre study: **hepatic encephalopathy grade 3–4 → consider LT immediately**, treatment benefit limited; no encephalopathy or grade 1–2 → **60% LT-free survival** after starting treatment, with **baseline MELD-Na ≤28** identifying significantly better LT-free survival than MELD-Na >28. **Reduction in MELD-Na at 4 weeks** was the single most important predictor of survival. Even in the favourable subgroup one third died or required LT; infection (notably spontaneous bacterial peritonitis and pneumonia) drove mortality.

### Variant Syndromes — Treatment

- Management should be **directed at the predominant component** of the syndrome (*LoE 4, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- In **AIH/PBC**, set the initial regimen by biochemistry and histology: **standard immunosuppression + ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) 13–15 mg/kg/day** for moderate or severe hepatitis; **UDCA monotherapy** for mild hepatitis, adding immunosuppression if CBR is not achieved (*LoE 4, weak recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Immunosuppressive treatment with or without UDCA is suggested** for adult and paediatric **AIH/PSC** (*LoE 4, weak recommendation, strong consensus*).

**Supporting detail:** AIH/PSC can be treated with immunosuppression plus UDCA **if the simplified score is >5 and mHAI >3**; predniso(lo)ne **0.5 mg/kg/day tapered to 10–15 mg/day** with UDCA **13–15 mg/kg/day** improved survival and reduced LT frequency vs classic PSC. For AIH/PBC, severe interface hepatitis predicts the need for immunosuppression added to UDCA; UDCA alone induced CBR in most patients with less severe AIH activity. A liver biopsy confirming moderate and/or severe hepatitis is **mandatory** whichever diagnostic score is used. Refer to experienced centres or the ERN RARE-LIVER clinical patient management system platform.

### Concomitant Liver Disease

**MASLD**

- Patients with AIH and concomitant MASLD should receive **standard AIH treatment** (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- A **personalised multidisciplinary approach targeting the lowest effective predniso(lo)ne dose**, lifestyle modification and strict management of metabolic syndrome components (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

**Viral hepatitis**

- Patients with chronic viral hepatitis **and autoimmune features should receive antiviral therapy** (*LoE 4, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Immunosuppression is recommended if liver inflammation persists despite adequate viral control** (*LoE 4, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

**HBV reactivation**

- **Hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg)-positive** patients undergoing immunosuppression **should receive antiviral therapy** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Anti-hepatitis B core antigen (anti-HBc)-positive, HBsAg-negative patients at high risk of reactivation should receive HBV prophylaxis** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Anti-HBc-positive, HBsAg-negative patients at **low or moderate risk require HBsAg and HBV DNA monitoring every 3 months**; start antiviral therapy if reactivation occurs (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

High-risk immunosuppression here = **B cell-depleting agents or infliximab**; prophylaxis maintained **at least 18 months after stopping immunosuppression**.

**Alcohol-related liver disease**

- Treatment of alcohol use disorder aimed at **abstinence** is recommended and should follow specific guidelines (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Immunosuppression for the AIH component should follow the current AIH treatment guidelines (*LoE 5, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

### Paediatric AIH

- Treatment in children follows **the same guidance as adults except for tailored weaning of predniso(lo)ne to a maintenance dose of 2.5–5 mg/day** to avoid corticosteroid side effects including growth failure (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- In younger children or those with developmental delay, use **dispersible tablets and syrups** to avoid incorrect dosing (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **MMF and calcineurin inhibitors are recommended as second- and third-line agents** for refractory cases, with close monitoring including MMF teratogenicity (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- If not in the national programme, **HAV and HBV vaccines should be given**; **live vaccination considered with caution** and after discussion with the medical teams (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

**Paediatric dosing (Table 12):**

| Line | Drug | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | Predniso(lo)ne | 1–2 mg/kg/day, max 60 mg daily | Taper to maintenance 2.5–5 mg/day; paediatric budesonide experience limited |
| First | Azathioprine | 1 mg/kg/day, titrate up to max 2–2.5 mg/kg/day | Start after 2 weeks; caution if jaundiced (hepatotoxic); TPMT before starting; monitor 6-MMP/6-TGN ratio |
| Second | MMF | 5–10 mg/kg twice daily, increase to 20 mg/kg twice daily (max 1,000 mg twice daily) | Replaces azathioprine in refractory AIH; adverse events ≈48%; teratogenic |
| Third | Tacrolimus | 0.05 mg/kg/day in 2 divided doses; trough ≈5 ng/ml | Rarely used second-line; adverse events ≈42%; nephrotoxicity, diabetes risk |
| Third | Cyclosporine | 4 mg/kg/day in 2 divided doses; trough 200–250 ng/ml, decreasing after remission | Occasional induction use; adverse events ≈78%; cosmetic side effects, nephrotoxicity |

**Supporting detail:** screen for cholangiopathy at diagnosis and **add UDCA if AIH/PSC is diagnosed**. Cholangiography is not needed in children under 6 years if gamma-glutamyltransferase (GGT) is normal. ESPGHAN recommends extending treatment **at least 3 years** and considering withdrawal if aminotransferases and IgG are persistently normal and autoantibodies negative **for at least a year**, with **liver biopsy before withdrawal strongly advised**; a long-term cohort found 53% succeeded without biopsy under strict surveillance, with deep-normal aminotransferases and **prothrombin ratio ≥70%** the best predictors — ESPGHAN has since reaffirmed the biopsy. A TGN:MMP ratio <4 may optimise remission with allopurinol.

### Complications of Cirrhosis

- **Upper gastrointestinal (GI) endoscopy to screen for varices needing treatment (VNT)** in AIH-related cirrhosis with **LSM ≥20 kPa or platelet count ≤150 × 10⁹/L** (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Patients with **lower LSM but indirect signs of portal hypertension** (splenomegaly, increased portal diameter, collateral veins) should also undergo screening endoscopy (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Although HCC prevalence is lower than in other aetiologies, **AIH-related cirrhosis should be screened for HCC with liver ultrasound ± alpha-fetoprotein every 6 months** (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- HCC management in AIH should follow **specific HCC guidelines** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Immunotherapy for HCC can be considered in patients with well-controlled disease** (*LoE 4, weak recommendation, strong consensus*).

**Supporting detail:** Baveno VII cut-offs (LSM ≤15 kPa + platelets ≥150 × 10⁹/L rules out clinically significant portal hypertension [CSPH]; LSM ≥25 kPa rules it in) were derived in viral, alcohol and MASLD populations and **are not validated in AIH** — up to **52%** of AIH patients with clear signs of portal hypertension had LSM below the cut-offs, and immunosuppressants (thiopurines, MMF) independently lower platelets. HCC risk in AIH cirrhosis (cumulative incidence 2.6%, 4.6%, 5.6%, 6.6% at 5, 10, 15, 20 years after cirrhosis) sits **below the 0.5%/year threshold** proposed for cost-effective surveillance, so personalised strategies may be adopted. Additional independent HCC risk factors: obesity, age ≥50 years, alcohol ≥25 g/day, and AIH/PSC variant at baseline.

### Liver Transplantation

- Patients with AIH and **decompensated cirrhosis, acute severe AIH, or AIH-related ALF (including ACLF) should be managed and evaluated for LT in reference centres** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Low-dose predniso(lo)ne plus a calcineurin inhibitor (mainly tacrolimus) can be used as maintenance immunosuppression to prevent AIH recurrence** after LT (*LoE 4, weak recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Plasma cell-rich rejection hepatitis should be considered** as a cause of late graft dysfunction in patients transplanted for a **non-AIH** indication presenting with liver enzyme abnormalities and AIH-like histology, with or without IgG elevation and/or autoantibodies (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **AIH recurrence and plasma cell-rich rejection hepatitis should be treated with predniso(lo)ne at the same doses recommended for non-transplant AIH** (*LoE 4, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

**Supporting detail:** AIH was 8% of European LTs 1998–2017; recipient survival 79%, 71% and 60% at 5, 10 and 15 years; graft survival 73%, 63% and 60%. Fatal infection and rejection were more frequent than in other indications. **Recurrence 17–42% overall; 20% at 5 years and 31% at 10 years** in the IAIHG cohort — risk factors age at LT ≤42 years, MMF use in the first year after LT, donor/recipient mismatch, cytomegalovirus status mismatch, high pre-LT IgG. Diagnosis of recurrence rests mainly on **histology**, as serology is unreliable post-LT. **Plasma cell-rich rejection hepatitis** (previously "de novo AIH") occurs in **5–10% of children and 1–2% of adults** transplanted for other causes, usually **>6 months after LT**; Banff histological criteria require at least one of (1) portal and/or perivenular plasma cell-rich (>30%) infiltrates with interface hepatitis and/or perivenular necroinflammatory activity involving the majority of portal tracts and/or terminal hepatic veins, or (2) lymphocytic cholangitis. Risk factors: female graft, older donors, donor/recipient glutathione-S-transferase T1 mismatch. Treatment adds predniso(lo)ne at classic AIH doses plus adjustment of baseline immunosuppression (which may include adding azathioprine).

### Quality of Life, Support and Transition

- **Mental health and health-related quality of life (HRQL) assessments** are recommended in the routine management of all patients, with signposting to other services if needed (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- Because treatment — particularly corticosteroids — significantly impacts HRQL and adherence, **dose reduction or corticosteroid withdrawal should be considered when appropriate** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Patient involvement in daily care and research should be encouraged** (*LoE 3, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Involvement with patient support groups can be offered**, among other interventions, to improve HRQL (*LoE 5, weak recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Young people aged 16–25 years should receive specialised care** with multidisciplinary team support to address developmental needs and improve outcomes (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).
- **Transition from paediatric to adult services should not depend solely on age**; service provision should include collaboration between paediatric and adult services with inclusion of parents/carers (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus*).

## Key Findings / Claims

- **MMF enters first line.** CAMARO (multicentre, prospective, open-label superiority RCT, NCT02900443) in treatment-naive AIH: CBR at 6 months **72.2% MMF vs 32.3% azathioprine (p = 0.004)**, severe adverse events 0% vs 12.9% (p = 0.03), withdrawal 5.1% vs 25.8%.
- **CBR = normalisation of aminotransferases and IgG no later than 6–12 months.** The IAIHG 6-month definition is retained but the window is extended, because 17% of patients not in CBR at 6 months reached it at 12 months with the same prognostic benefit.
- **Non-response = <50% fall in aminotransferases from baseline at 4 weeks**; the guideline flags that this cut-off may be too early, since a ≥80% fall at 8 weeks predicted normalisation at 6 and 12 months.
- **Histological remission = mHAI <4/18**; intolerance = any adverse event leading to drug cessation.
- **Autoantibody subtyping of adult AIH is abandoned** — type 1 / type 2 / type 3 do not predict severity, response or prognosis; the largest paediatric cohort showed anti-LKM1-positive children behave like AIH-1.
- **Liver biopsy is required for diagnosis** — ≥8 portal tracts, needle ≥18G (preferably 16G or wider), cylinder ≥1.5 cm, ideally >2.5 cm, read by a specialised liver pathologist and classified by the 2022 IAHPG criteria as likely / possible / unlikely AIH.
- **Emperipolesis and hepatocyte rosettes are no longer typical features of AIH** — not specific; if present, report as surrogate markers of severity.
- Autoantibodies are detected in **>95%** when testing adheres to guideline methods; ANA/SMA account for 60–70%, anti-SLA/LP 10–20%, anti-LKM1/anti-LC1 40–50% of paediatric cohorts.
- IgG is elevated in **85–95%** of patients overall but only **25–39%** of acute presentations; patients with normal IgG at diagnosis are **more** likely to stay in remission after withdrawal.
- **HCC in AIH is uncommon** — 1.44 cases/1,000 patient-years, prevalence 1.7% across 1,428 IAIHG registry patients, but cumulative incidence after cirrhosis reaches 6.6% at 20 years.
- About **one third** of adults and 20–33% of adults and children have advanced liver disease at diagnosis.
- Untreated AIH mortality ≈55% over 30–72 months; AIH presenting as ALF has a 21-day transplant-free survival of only 14.5–20%.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

**Against [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]] (both tier 1; EASL 2025 is newer and therefore wins the wiki pages):**

| Topic | AASLD 2020 | EASL 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Budesonide | Equivalent **first-line** alternative with azathioprine in non-cirrhotic patients (meta-analysis OR 2.19 for biochemical remission) | **Not recommended first line**; contraindicated in cirrhosis; only a switch option for predniso(lo)ne-dependent non-cirrhotic patients |
| MMF | **Second-line** only, for treatment failure/intolerance | **First-line alternative to azathioprine** (1.5–2 g/day), on CAMARO |
| Induction steroid dose | Prednisone 40–60 mg/day alone, or **20–40 mg/day with azathioprine** | **≥0.5 mg/kg/day, up to 1 mg/kg/day** in severe/advanced disease, always combined |
| Remission definition | Normalisation of AST, ALT **and** IgG; no fixed deadline, speed emphasised | **CBR** = the same normalisation **no later than 6–12 months**, with explicit insufficient-response, non-response and histological-remission definitions |
| Biopsy for diagnosis | Supports diagnosis, central to scoring | **Required** to establish the diagnosis |
| Biopsy before withdrawal | Preferred, not mandatory in adults; strongly advised in children | No general recommendation either way; **advised at least in cirrhosis**, and **not justified** if the patient will stop regardless; children — ESPGHAN reaffirms biopsy |
| Withdrawal prerequisite | Normal AST/ALT/IgG for **≥2 years** on immunosuppression | Stable CBR for **≥2 years on low-dose monotherapy**, with total treatment ≥4 years |
| Autoantibody subtyping | Type 1 / type 2 framework used throughout | **Not recommended in adults** — does not change management |
| Tacrolimus | Co-equal second-line with MMF (trough 1–10 ng/mL) | **Third-line rescue** in expert centres (trough 6 ng/ml), after MMF and azathioprine optimisation |
| 6-TGN target | 100–300 pmol/8×10⁸ RBC | Therapeutic window **225–450 pmol/8×10⁸**, action cut-off **223** |
| Acute severe AIH steroid dose | Prednis(ol)one **0.5–1 mg/kg/day** monotherapy, reassess at 1–2 weeks | Same dose range but **0.5 mg/kg/day pragmatically preferred**, and **reassess at 3–7 days** before LT referral |
| Emperipolesis/rosettes | Part of "typical" histology in the simplified score | **No longer typical features**; report only as severity surrogates |
| AIH recurrence after LT | **8–12% within the first year; 36–68% after 5 years** | **17–42% overall**; IAIHG multicentre cohort **20% at 5 years, 31% at 10 years** |
| "De novo AIH" after LT | Term retained alongside the Banff proposal; **1–7% of paediatric and 1–3% of adult recipients** | Renamed **plasma cell-rich rejection hepatitis**; **5–10% of children and 1–2% of adults**, and IgG elevation/autoantibodies are **not required** for the diagnosis |
| AIH–PBC variant criteria | Paris criteria table | Paris criteria described as "quite strict"; a newer unvalidated US score is noted; **a biopsy showing moderate/severe hepatitis is mandatory whichever score is used** — consistent with [[gerussi-2026-pbc-aih-delphi]], which also declines to endorse Paris |

**Open questions raised by the guideline itself:**

- Whether **IgG normalisation belongs in the CBR definition** at all — unpublished data suggest prognosis stays excellent with normal aminotransferases and elevated IgG, but the panel keeps IgG for now.
- Whether the **4-week non-response cut-off** is too early (in a retrospective study — 370-patient discovery and 370-patient validation cohorts — an aminotransferase reduction of >80% from baseline after 8 weeks was associated with AST/ALT normalisation at 6 and 12 months and better outcomes).
- Whether **Baveno VII LSM and platelet cut-offs** can be extrapolated to AIH — up to 52% of AIH patients with clear portal hypertension fell below them.
- Whether **6-monthly HCC surveillance is cost-effective** in AIH cirrhosis, where annual risk sits below the 0.5%/year threshold.
- Whether liver **biopsy before withdrawal** is more reliable than AST/ALT and IgG — strong prospective evidence is absent.
- The HBV reactivation risk categories are printed in the article as "high (<10%), moderate (1%–10%) or low (<1%)"; the high-risk percentage as printed is inconsistent with the other two bands, so the wiki carries the **decision** (HBsAg-positive → antivirals; anti-HBc-positive on B cell-depleting agents or infliximab → prophylaxis; otherwise 3-monthly monitoring) rather than the percentages.
