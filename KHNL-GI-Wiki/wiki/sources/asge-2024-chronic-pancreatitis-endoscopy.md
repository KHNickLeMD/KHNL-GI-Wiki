## Summary

- GRADE guideline on endoscopy in [[chronic-pancreatitis|chronic pancreatitis (CP)]]; 6 questions, **all recommendations conditional** (evidence very low to moderate)
- Scope: pain (endoscopic vs surgical decompression of an obstructed main pancreatic duct (PD); celiac plexus block (CPB) route), PD stones, PD strictures, benign biliary strictures (BBSs), pseudocysts
- CPB in the included studies = local anesthetic with or without steroid injection (p.587)
- Endoscopic tools weighed: [[ercp|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]] ± pancreatoscopy with lithotripsy, extracorporeal shock wave lithotripsy (ESWL), plastic stents (PSs), fully covered self-expandable metal stents (FCSEMSs), [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]]-guided CPB and drainage

## Key Recommendations

Numbering and grades as in Table 1 (p.586) and the recommendation boxes. Every recommendation is "suggests" = conditional.

| No. | Recommendation | Strength / quality of evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1a | Painful CP + obstructed main PD, **no contraindication to surgery** → suggests **surgical evaluation** before initiation of endoscopic management | Conditional / low to moderate |
| 1b | Painful CP + obstructed main PD, **contraindication to surgery or prefers a less-invasive modality** → suggests **endoscopic management** as the initial approach (Summary: endoscopic approach first "if complete ductal clearance is likely") | Conditional / low to moderate |
| 2 | Painful CP, decision made to proceed with CPB → suggests **EUS-guided over percutaneous** approach | Conditional / low |
| 3 | Painful CP + main PD stones → base strategy on stone **size, location, radiopacity**: | Conditional / very low to low |
| 3a | Radiopaque stones **>5 mm** in the head, neck, or body → ERCP with or without pancreatoscopy, **or** ESWL alone | (as Rec 3) |
| 3b | After ESWL with no spontaneous clearance despite adequate fragmentation (fragments **<2–3 mm**) → ERCP (with or without pancreatoscopy) for stone clearance | (as Rec 3) |
| 3c | Radiopaque stones **<5 mm**, any radiolucent stone, or contraindication to ESWL → ERCP with or without pancreatoscopy | (as Rec 3) |
| 4 | Painful CP + main PD strictures: | Conditional / very low to low |
| 4a | *Number of stents:* **single over multiple** PSs for initial treatment of a dominant PD stricture | (as Rec 4) |
| 4b | *Stent diameter:* **largest-diameter PS that can be safely deployed** in initial treatment of a dominant PD stricture; avoid forceful or traumatic placement; gradual upsizing if necessary | (as Rec 4) |
| 4c | *Metal stents:* suggests **against routine FCSEMSs** for persistent or refractory PD strictures after failed initial stent placement | (as Rec 4) |
| 5 | **FCSEMSs over multiple PSs** for BBSs complicating CP | Conditional / low to moderate |
| 6 | **Endoscopic over surgical drainage** of symptomatic pseudocysts in CP | Conditional / low |

- Rec 1 rationale: endoscopy significantly inferior to surgery for any pain relief, complete pain relief and technical success in the randomized controlled trials (RCTs); no difference in mortality, adverse events, hospital stay or pancreatic function (p.587)
  - ERCP may be preferred when complete relief of obstruction is likely, e.g. uncomplicated obstruction in the head, neck, or body
  - Surgery may be considered first when endoscopy likely to fail: tail disease, dense calcification, stone burden not amenable to endoscopy
  - Expert opinion: early consideration of surgery over repeated unsuccessful ERCPs
- Rec 3 rationale: ERCP alone clears stones in <15%; ERCP alone reserved for stones <5 mm or radiolucent stones in the head/neck/body (p.588–589)
  - Pancreatoscopy lithotripsy less efficient than ESWL when stones >10 mm
  - Pancreatoscopy difficult for stones upstream of a PD stricture or in the tail
- Rec 4 definitions: persistent = symptomatic stricture not resolved after 3 months of a single PS; refractory = not resolved after 1 year (p.589)
- Rec 5 question population: BBS with jaundice and/or elevated alkaline phosphatase for >4 weeks (p.590)

### General Concepts (ungraded, Table 1)

- **Pain / Rec 1:** multidisciplinary approach strongly recommended for patients who failed medical treatment for pain; assess availability of an expert endoscopist, a pancreatic surgeon and ancillary facilities such as lithotripsy
- **CPB / Rec 2:**
  - <60% get pain relief with EUS-guided CPB, and it is nonsustained (<6 mo)
  - Consider carefully in patients nonresponsive to medical therapy or with no endoscopic or surgical option (nonobstructive CP)
  - May be considered with opioid side effects or a wish to avoid opioids
- **PD stones / Rec 3:**
  - Pancreatoscopy vs ESWL choice largely based on local expertise and availability
  - Consider ESWL for radiopaque stones >10 mm
  - Pancreatoscopy likely difficult with strictures and stones upstream of the stricture
- **PD strictures / Rec 4:**
  - Assess need for stent upsizing at 3-monthly follow-up ERCPs after initial PS placement
  - FCSEMSs have significant delayed adverse events and questionable efficacy
- **BBS / Rec 5:**
  - Treat BBSs when symptomatic or unresolved after 4 weeks, to prevent secondary biliary cirrhosis
  - FCSEMSs especially beneficial over multiple PSs with likely noncompliance or scheduling difficulty (serious adverse events with PSs left in longer)
  - Multiple PSs may be considered if the stricture is indeterminate or there is a risk of cystic duct obstruction with gallbladder in situ
- **Pseudocysts / Rec 6:**
  - Avoid a percutaneous approach as sole therapy (risk of pancreaticocutaneous fistula)
  - Consider a multidisciplinary approach with altered anatomy or pseudocysts not in proximity of the gastrointestinal lumen

## Clinical Relevance

- Painful CP with obstructed main PD: surgical evaluation first in surgical candidates; endoscopy first for those with contraindications or who prefer it
- Stone strategy by size/location/radiopacity; single largest-diameter PS for PD strictures, no routine FCSEMS; FCSEMS for BBS; endoscopic pseudocyst drainage
- Connects to [[ercp]] technique and [[endoscopic-ultrasound]]-guided interventions
