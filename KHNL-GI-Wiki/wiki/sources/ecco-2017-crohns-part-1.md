## Summary

Part 1 of the 3rd ECCO consensus on Crohn's disease — consensus methodology, agreed definitions, diagnosis, classification, and medical management of active and quiescent luminal disease. Surgical management and perianal disease are in Part 2 (Gionchetti et al.). It carries **35 numbered ECCO statements**, each tagged with an **evidence level (EL1–EL5) graded by the 2011 Oxford Centre for Evidence-Based Medicine scale**. ECCO does **not** attach a separate strength-of-recommendation rating; the EL tag is the only grade the document gives.

The most durable contribution, and the reason it is on the wiki, is **Section 1.1 — the agreed vocabulary**. ECCO supplies the operational definitions of *steroid-refractory* and *steroid-dependent* disease (with the actual doses and time windows), of *early relapse*, *relapse pattern*, *recurrence* vs *relapse*, and of *localized* vs *extensive* disease by centimetres of bowel. United States guidelines use these terms without defining them numerically, so this is where the numbers come from.

Diagnostically the consensus is conventional: no single gold standard; ileocolonoscopy with biopsies from the terminal ileum and each colonic segment is first-line; small-bowel imaging follows regardless of what ileocolonoscopy shows; serology and genetics are not for routine diagnosis. Cross-sectional imaging (magnetic resonance and computed tomography enterography, ultrasound) is complementary to endoscopy; magnetic resonance is preferred where available because of cumulative radiation in a young population. Capsule endoscopy is reserved for a high clinical suspicion surviving negative ileocolonoscopy and negative radiology.

Therapeutically it is organized **by site of disease and activity**: budesonide for mild localized ileocaecal disease, budesonide or systemic steroids for moderate, systemic steroids for severe and for colonic disease, with an anti-tumour necrosis factor (anti-TNF) strategy substituted up front in the steroid-refractory, steroid-intolerant and poor-prognosis patient, and vedolizumab positioned for failure of steroids and/or anti-TNF. The recurring theme is **minimizing steroid exposure**: no more than one in four patients given corticosteroids to induce remission is still in remission a year later, and steroids are explicitly **not** to be used for maintenance.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Agreed definitions (Section 1.1) — the operative numbers

| Term | ECCO definition |
|---|---|
| **Active disease** | Graded mild / moderate / severe; **not precisely defined entities**. Most trials recruit at Crohn's Disease Activity Index (CDAI) **>220** |
| **Remission** | **CDAI <150**. Objective data (C-reactive protein <10 mg/L, endoscopy, imaging, histology) increasingly required. Maintenance studies should run **≥12 months** |
| **Response** | **Δ CDAI ≥100 points** (some trials, including the early infliximab work, used ≥70) |
| **Relapse** | Flare of symptoms in established CD in clinical remission; for trials, **CDAI >150 with an increase of >70 points** |
| **Early relapse** | **<3 months** after achieving remission with the previous therapy (arbitrary but clinically relevant) |
| **Relapse pattern** | **Infrequent ≤1/year · frequent ≥2/year · continuous** (persistent activity with no remission period) |
| **Steroid-refractory** | **Active disease despite prednisolone up to 1 mg/kg/day for 4 weeks** |
| **Steroid-dependent** | Either **(i)** unable to reduce steroids below **prednisolone 10 mg/day (or budesonide 3 mg/day) within 3 months** of starting them without recurrent active disease, **or (ii)** relapse **within 3 months** of stopping steroids |
| **Recurrence** | Reappearance of *lesions* after surgical resection (*relapse* = reappearance of symptoms) |
| **Morphologic recurrence** | New lesions after complete resection of macroscopic disease, graded by the **Rutgeerts score** |
| **Localized disease** | Intestinal CD affecting **<30 cm** |
| **Extensive disease** | Intestinal CD affecting **>100 cm**, whatever the location, summed across discontinuous segments |

- **Assess steroid-refractoriness or -dependence only after carefully excluding disease-specific complications.** The steroid-dependence definition requires that total steroid duration not exceed 3 months before the prednisolone-10 mg/day threshold is reached. **The aim is complete withdrawal.**
- **Rutgeerts score** (ECCO's own rendering): **0** no lesions · **1** <5 aphthous lesions · **2** >5 aphthous lesions with normal mucosa between, or skip areas of larger lesions, or lesions confined to the ileocolonic anastomotic lining (<1 cm) · **3** diffuse aphthous ileitis with diffusely inflamed mucosa · **4** diffuse ileal inflammation with larger ulcers, nodules or narrowing. **Hyperaemia and oedema alone do not count as recurrence.**
- The old term **'chronic active disease' is ambiguous and is to be avoided** in favour of steroid-refractory / steroid-dependent.

### Diagnosis (statements 2A–2I, 3)

Wording condensed; direction, population and scope unchanged. Evidence level is the document's own.

| # | Statement | EL |
|---|---|---|
| 2A | Symptoms are heterogeneous but commonly **abdominal pain, weight loss, chronic diarrhoea** — these should raise suspicion of CD, **especially in young patients**. Systemic malaise, anorexia or fever are common | EL5 |
| 2B | **No single gold standard.** Diagnosis is confirmed by clinical evaluation plus a combination of endoscopic, histological, radiological and/or biochemical investigations. **Genetic or serological testing is not recommended for routine diagnosis** | EL5 |
| 2C | Full history: onset of symptoms, recent travel, food intolerances, medication (including antibiotics and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs), history of appendectomy (EL5). | 2G | **Cross-sectional imaging** (magnetic resonance and computed tomography enterography) and **trans-abdominal ultrasound** are complementary to endoscopy and detect and stage inflammatory, obstructive and fistulising disease (EL1). **Consider radiation exposure** when selecting techniques, especially for follow-up (EL4). Barium studies are less sensitive — prefer alternatives when available | mixed |
| 2H | Magnetic resonance, computed tomography and ultrasound are accurate for **small bowel stenosis** (EL2) and **penetrating complications** (EL1), and **may** assist in distinguishing predominantly inflammatory from fibrotic strictures (EL5) | mixed |
| 2I | **Small bowel capsule endoscopy** is reserved for patients in whom clinical suspicion remains high **despite negative ileocolonoscopy and negative radiology** (EL2); it has a high negative predictive value for small bowel CD (EL4). **Device-assisted enteroscopy** in expert hands when histology is needed (EL3) or for endoscopic therapy — stricture dilatation, retrieval of an impacted capsule, treatment of bleeding (EL4) | mixed |

Supporting detail worth carrying:

- **Chronic diarrhoea is the commonest presenting symptom**; a decrease in faecal consistency for **>6 weeks** separates it from self-limited infectious diarrhoea. Abdominal pain and weight loss precede diagnosis in ~80% and ~60%. Blood and/or mucus in stool in **40–50%** of Crohn's colitis. **Perianal fistulas are present in 4–10% at diagnosis** and may be the presenting complaint.
- **Infectious gastroenteritis is followed by a four-fold increased risk** of developing CD, especially in the following year, though absolute risk is low. The apparent post-appendectomy risk may be diagnostic bias.
- **Low C-reactive protein and/or low calprotectin carry a 99% negative predictive value** for irritable bowel syndrome when IBD is being considered. In CD, calprotectin has **>90% positive predictive value** for endoscopically active disease; raised faecal markers have **60–70% sensitivity** for concurrent endoscopic activity — better than C-reactive protein and clearly better than CDAI. Calprotectin's value is **diminished in small-bowel-restricted disease** and there are **no uniform thresholds**.
- **Endoscopic hallmark:** patchy inflammation with skip lesions; longitudinal ulcers, cobblestoning, fistulous orifices, stricture. **Rectal sparing is often encountered**; circumferential continuous inflammation is rare. **Anatomical criteria of severity** = deep ulceration eroding the muscle layer, or mucosal detachment/ulceration limited to submucosa but extending over more than one-third of a defined colonic segment.
- **With severe active disease, full colonoscopy carries a higher perforation risk** — do initial flexible sigmoidoscopy and postpone ileocolonoscopy until the patient improves.
- **Dysplasia or cancer complicates 3.5% of colonic strictures** — sample them.
- Capsule retention risk is **13% in established CD but only 1.6% in suspected CD**; in suspected CD without obstructive symptoms, prior resection or known stenosis, **routine small bowel imaging or a patency capsule before capsule endoscopy is not mandatory**. Over 10% of healthy subjects have small bowel mucosal breaks, so capsule findings alone do not establish CD.
- Diagnosis **changes to ulcerative colitis in about 5%** during the first year. Colonic IBD that cannot be allocated is **IBD unclassified**; *indeterminate colitis* is confined to operative specimens.

### Classification (statements 4A–4C)

| # | Statement | EL |
|---|---|---|
| 4A | **Use the Montréal classification** until a better one exists. Genetic tests and serological markers should **not** be used to classify CD in practice | EL2 |
| 4B | **The course of CD may be predicted by clinical factors at diagnosis and/or endoscopic findings** — take this into account when choosing a therapeutic strategy | EL2 |
| 4C | **Serum C-reactive protein and faecal markers** (calprotectin, lactoferrin) can guide therapy and short-term follow-up (EL2) and predict clinical relapse (EL2). **Faecal calprotectin can help distinguish CD from irritable bowel syndrome** (EL2) | EL2 |

- **Montréal classification** — age at diagnosis **A1 ≤16 y · A2 17–40 y · A3 >40 y**; location **L1 terminal ileum · L2 colon · L3 ileocolon · L4 upper gastrointestinal**; behaviour **B1 non-stricturing non-penetrating · B2 stricturing · B3 penetrating**, at any time in the course. **Perianal fistulae and abscesses are a modifier, 'p', appended to B1/B2/B3.** Location tends to stay stable; behaviour progresses over time.
- **Poor-prognosis predictors at diagnosis** (three independent cohorts): **perianal lesions, ileocolonic location, young age at diagnosis, and needing steroids for the first flare**. With **two or more predictors**, early thiopurine and/or biologic therapy could be considered. The French cohort: **two criteria present → 84–91% had 'disabling disease' by 5 years; all three → 91–93%**. An independent cohort also found **stricturing behaviour and >5 kg weight loss before diagnosis** independently associated with severe disease. ECCO notes these predictors are present in a large fraction of patients and may discriminate poorly.
- **Absence of mucosal healing can itself be used as a predictor of a complicated course.**
- Only **NOD2 variants and the 5q31 haplotype** have reproducibly been linked to ileal location, complicated disease and need for surgery — not enough to justify routine genetic testing.

### Medical management of active disease (statements 5A–5L)

| # | Statement | EL |
|---|---|---|
| 5A | **Confirm the presence of active inflammation due to CD before initiating or changing medical therapy** | *(ungraded)* |
| 5B | **Mildly active localized ileocaecal CD — oral budesonide is the preferred treatment** | EL2 |
| 5C | **Moderately active localized ileocaecal CD — budesonide** (EL1) **or systemic corticosteroids** (EL1). **An anti-TNF-based strategy is the alternative for patients previously steroid-refractory or steroid-intolerant** (EL1). | 5D | **Severely active localized ileocaecal CD — systemic corticosteroids initially** (EL1). **For those who have relapsed, an anti-TNF-based strategy** (EL1). **Surgery is a reasonable alternative** for disease refractory to conventional medical treatment and should also be discussed (EL3). Infrequently relapsing → steroids plus an immunomodulator may be appropriate (EL2). Refractory to steroids and/or anti-TNF → vedolizumab (EL1) | mixed |
| 5E | **Active colonic CD — systemic corticosteroids** (EL1). **For those who have relapsed, an anti-TNF-based strategy** (EL1). Refractory to steroids and/or anti-TNF → vedolizumab (EL1) | EL1 |
| 5F | **Extensive small bowel CD — systemic corticosteroids initially, but early anti-TNF therapy should also be evaluated** (EL5). **For severe disease that has relapsed, an anti-TNF-based strategy** (EL5) | EL5 |
| 5G | Patients with **clinical features suggesting a poor prognosis** are the most suitable for **early immunosuppressive therapy** (EL2). **Early anti-TNF therapy should be initiated in patients with high disease activity and features indicating a poor prognosis** (EL3) | mixed |
| 5H | **Mild oesophageal or gastroduodenal CD may be treated with a proton pump inhibitor only** (EL5). **More severe or refractory disease requires additional systemic corticosteroids** (EL4) **or an anti-TNF-based strategy** (EL4). **Dilatation or surgery for symptomatic strictures** (EL4) | mixed |
| 5I | **Steroid-refractory CD with objective evidence of active disease — treat with an anti-TNF-based strategy** (EL1), **although surgical options should also be considered and discussed at an early stage** (EL5) | mixed |
| 5J | **All currently available anti-TNF therapies appear to have similar efficacy in luminal CD and similar adverse-event profiles**, so choice depends on availability, route of delivery, patient preference and cost | EL5 |
| 5K | **Particular care should be taken to consider serious infections as a complication of immunosuppressive therapy, including anti-TNF** | EL3 |
| 5L | **Ask patients about complementary and alternative medicine use** (EL5). Discuss the **lack of scientific evidence, heterogeneity of the field and economic factors** with patients expressing a strong interest (EL5) | EL5 |

Therapy-specific detail:

- **Budesonide 9 mg daily** for mild localized ileocaecal disease: superior to placebo (relative risk 1.93, 95% CI 1.37–2.73), **inferior to conventional steroids** (RR 0.85, 0.75–0.97), markedly so when CDAI >300 (RR 0.52, 0.28–0.95), but with **fewer side effects** (RR 0.64, 0.54–0.76). **50–60% remit by 8 weeks.**
- **Budesonide has no role in colonic disease** unless it primarily affects the proximal colon. Budesonide MMX has not been studied in CD.
- **Mesalazine:** no clear evidence of benefit over placebo at any dose in CD; budesonide is preferred. **Sulfasalazine 4 g/day** was modestly effective in old trials of active colonic disease but **cannot be recommended** because of side effects (10–45%).
- **Systemically acting antibiotics (metronidazole, ciprofloxacin) are not recommended** for luminal CD — side effects common, effectiveness unproven. Antibiotics are appropriate for **septic complications, bacterial overgrowth and perineal disease**. **Anti-mycobacterial therapy cannot be recommended.** Rifaximin **800 mg/day** beat placebo in one trial in mild-to-moderate CD (lower and higher doses did not; no confirmatory trial).
- **Exclusive enteral nutrition has not shown efficacy in a large adult randomized trial** and is often poorly tolerated; nutritional therapy is **adjunctive, not sole, therapy** (it may be considered primary therapy if disease is mild).
- **Steroid dosing from the registration trials:** prednisone **0.5–0.75 mg/kg/day** (higher dose for more severe disease) tapered over 17 weeks gave 60% remission vs 30% placebo (number needed to treat 3); 6-methylprednisolone **1 mg/kg/day** gave 83% vs 38% (NNT 2). **No formal dose-response trial of prednisolone has ever been performed.**
- **A standard tapering strategy is recommended** for systemic steroids — it identifies the patients who relapse rapidly and therefore need thiopurines, an anti-TNF strategy or vedolizumab. **There are no trials comparing tapering regimens** and 'standard' regimens differ between centres.
- **Steroid adverse effects in three groups:** (1) **early**, from supraphysiological induction doses — acne, moon face, oedema, striae, sleep and mood disturbance, dyspepsia, glucose intolerance; (2) **prolonged use (usually >12 weeks, sometimes less)** — posterior subcapsular cataract, osteoporosis, osteonecrosis of the femoral head, myopathy, susceptibility to infection; (3) **withdrawal** — acute adrenal insufficiency from sudden cessation, pseudo-rheumatism (myalgia, malaise, arthralgia mimicking a CD flare), raised intracranial pressure.
- **Budesonide causes less bone loss than prednisolone** (mean −1.04% vs −3.84% over 2 years, p = 0.0084).
- **Steroids raise postoperative sepsis risk** (odds ratio 3.7, 95% CI 1.2–11.0), **not seen with thiopurines** (OR 1.7, 0.7–9.6). Steroids combined with other immunosuppressants increase serious infections.
- **Osteoprotection is advisable if steroid therapy is likely to exceed 6 weeks**; most advocate calcium and vitamin D supplements for all patients.
- **No more than one in four** patients given corticosteroids to induce symptomatic remission is still in remission at a year, even counting those on immunomodulators.
- **'Infrequently relapsing' is not definable from trial evidence**, but **more than one full course of systemic steroids per year may be taken as the threshold** for introducing a steroid-sparing agent.
- **18% (95% CI 14–24%) of patients enter remission on placebo alone** — for selected mild disease, starting no active treatment is one option.
- **An accelerated step-up approach is current practice** — rapid escalation if no adequate response within the expected time frame.
- **Anti-TNF should be used with care in patients with obstructive symptoms.**
- **Vedolizumab response can take 12–16 weeks or more** but is well maintained through week 52.

### Maintenance of medically induced remission (statements 6A–6K)

| # | Statement | EL |
|---|---|---|
| 6A | **After a first presentation in which remission was achieved with systemic steroids, consider a thiopurine** (EL1) **or methotrexate** (EL3). **No maintenance treatment is an option for some patients** (EL5) | mixed |
| 6B | **If a patient relapses, escalation of maintenance treatment can be considered to prevent disease progression** (EL2). **Steroids should not be used to maintain remission** (EL1). **Surgery should always be considered as an option in localized disease** (EL4) | mixed |
| 6C | **For extensive disease, thiopurines are recommended for maintenance** (EL1). **In patients with an aggressive/severe disease course or poor prognostic factors, an anti-TNF-based strategy should be considered** (EL5) | mixed |
| 6D | **Immunosuppressive-naïve patients who are dependent on corticosteroids should be treated with a thiopurine** (EL1) **or methotrexate** (EL2) **or an anti-TNF-based strategy** (EL1). **Surgical options should also be discussed** (EL4) | mixed |
| 6E | **Patients on thiopurines who relapse** should be evaluated for **adherence** and for **objective signs of inflammation** (EL5). **Dose optimisation may improve response** (EL4). Where appropriate, change to **methotrexate** (EL2) **or anti-TNF therapy** (EL1). **Surgery should always be considered in localized disease** (EL4) | mixed |
| 6F | **If remission was achieved with anti-TNF plus a thiopurine in treatment-naïve patients, maintain the same regimen** (EL1). **Thiopurine monotherapy may be an option in selected patients who achieved sustained remission on combination therapy** (EL3). **If remission was achieved with anti-TNF monotherapy, maintenance with anti-TNF monotherapy is appropriate** (EL1) | mixed |
| 6G | **For patients in long-term remission on thiopurine maintenance, cessation may be considered in the absence of objective signs of inflammation** (EL2). **No recommendation can be given for the duration of methotrexate treatment.** **Prolonged use of anti-TNF agents may be considered if needed** (EL3) | mixed |
| 6H | **Endoscopic mucosal inflammation may be assessed even if symptom control is maintained**, because mucosal healing correlates with reduced hospitalisation and surgery | EL3 |
| 6I | **Confirmed loss of response to an anti-TNF agent should first be managed by dose optimisation** (EL3). **Dose increase and interval shortening are equivalent strategies** (EL4). **If dose optimisation is ineffective, switching to a different anti-TNF agent is recommended** (EL2). **Where available, serum anti-TNF trough levels and anti-drug antibodies could be used to guide the optimisation strategy** (EL4) | mixed |
| 6K | **Thiopurines are associated with an increased risk of lymphoma** (EL1), **non-melanoma skin cancer** (EL3) **and cervical dysplasia** (EL3). **Anti-TNF agents increase the risk of melanoma** (EL3). **There is currently insufficient data to suggest anti-TNF agents alone increase the risk of lymphoproliferative disorders or solid tumours; in contrast, their combination with thiopurines significantly increases the risk of lymphoproliferative disorders** (EL3). **Absolute rates are low and the risks should always be balanced against the substantial benefits of treatment, and discussed with the patient** (EL5) | mixed |

*(The document numbers no statement 6J.)*

- **Corticosteroids, including budesonide, are not effective for maintenance**, and long-term use carries unacceptable side effects, especially osteoporosis.
- **There is no evidence mesalazine maintains medically induced remission** — meta-analyses are inconsistent.
- **Smoking cessation should be actively pursued in every patient.** Smoking increases the need for steroids, immunosuppressants and operations; cessation may improve the disease course. **Active smoking-addiction programmes should be recommended.**
- **Patients in remission should be clinically assessed on a regular basis**; C-reactive protein or faecal calprotectin may help monitor activity. Routinely repeating endoscopy or imaging may help monitor progression, but the supporting data are limited and the treatment consequences unclear.
- **Combination infliximab + azathioprine beat infliximab monotherapy** for steroid-free remission at 26 weeks (57% vs 45%, p < 0.05); azathioprine monotherapy was least effective (30%, p < 0.01 vs both infliximab arms). Infliximab **5 mg/kg at 0, 2 and 6 weeks then every 8 weeks** with azathioprine **2.5 mg/kg**.
- **Adalimumab induction 160 mg then 80 mg at 2 weeks** achieved remission in 36% at 4 weeks vs 12% placebo; as second-line after infliximab failure, 21% vs 7% at 4 weeks.
- **Early relapse (section 5.3.2 uses <6 months here, against the <3 months of the definitions section)** should prompt starting an immunomodulator. Relapse of moderate or severe activity should prompt consideration of anti-TNF.
- Continued infliximab or adalimumab is associated with **about a 30% reduction in surgery and hospitalisation at 12 months**.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **ECCO contradicts itself on "early relapse."** Section 1.1.5 defines it as **<3 months** after achieving remission; section 5.3.2 calls it "an arbitrary period of **<6 months**". Both are printed in the same document.
- **This is a 2017 European consensus.** Where it disagrees with a newer or United States guideline already on the wiki, the newer/US document governs the page — ECCO is used here mainly for the **definitions and numeric anchors that US guidelines leave unstated**.
- **Vedolizumab is positioned ahead of ustekinumab and the newer agents** simply because the consensus closed before they were available; it is silent on ustekinumab, risankizumab and the small molecules.
- **Certolizumab pegol** is discussed but was **not licensed for CD in Europe** at the time.
- **'Infrequently relapsing disease' cannot be defined from trial evidence**, and the >1 course of steroids per year threshold is explicitly offered as a may-be-considered figure, not a criterion.
- **No formal dose-response trial of prednisolone exists**, and there are **no trials comparing steroid tapering regimens** — so no taper schedule is given.
- **No uniform faecal calprotectin threshold** is endorsed.
- **Figures.** Not open access (no Creative Commons statement in the PDF), so no figure is embedded.
