## Summary

ASGE Standards of Practice guideline applying the Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) framework to three clinical questions in mechanical gastric outlet obstruction (GOO): self-expandable metal stent (SEMS) versus surgical gastrojejunostomy (GJ) for palliation of incurable malignant GOO; covered versus uncovered SEMS for malignant GOO; and endoscopic versus surgical management of benign GOO. The panel met March 2020 and the document was approved by the ASGE Governing Board in May 2020. Searches ran through December 2019.

**All three recommendations are conditional, and none establishes a preferred modality.** The evidence base is small — three published randomized controlled trials (RCTs) of SEMS versus surgical GJ totalling 84 patients, two of them single-centre — so each question resolves into a shared-decision statement with the panel's qualifying comment carrying the operative content. The comment on Question 1 is where the life-expectancy rule lives: poor surgical candidates with **short life expectancy (<6 months)**, and those who place high value on early resumption of oral diet and early discharge, are suggested SEMS; patients with life expectancy **>6 months and good performance status** are suggested surgical GJ.

On covered versus uncovered SEMS the data are directionally clear but offset: covered stents occlude far less often (4.1% vs 25.2%) and migrate far more often (11.8% vs 2.7%), with no difference in oral-diet tolerance, reintervention rate, or adverse events — which is why the panel declines to prefer either. A practical constraint is noted: **no covered enteral stent designed and FDA-approved for palliation of malignant GOO is available in the United States**, so all comparative data come from East Asia.

Endoscopic ultrasound–guided gastroenterostomy (EUS-GE) sits outside the PICO questions but is discussed: pooled technical success 92.6%, serious adverse events 5.61%, reintervention 11.43%, with lumen-apposing metal stent (LAMS) use for this indication considered **off-label at the time of drafting**. The guideline also prints relative contraindications to EUS-GE and, for benign GOO, the factors that predict failure of endoscopic balloon dilation (EBD).

## Key Findings / Claims

### Recommendations (3; the document numbers them 1–3 in its Recommendations section)

**1. SEMS or surgical GJ for palliation of incurable malignant GOO.** In patients with incurable malignant GOO undergoing palliative intervention, the panel **suggests either** SEMS placement **or** surgical GJ; the choice should rest on patient characteristics and preferences, multidisciplinary input, and local expertise. *(Conditional recommendation, low quality of evidence)*
> **Comment (decision rule).** Based on shared decision-making: in patients who are **poor surgical candidates with short life expectancy (<6 months)**, and those who **place a high value on resumption of oral diet and being discharged early**, SEMS is suggested over surgical GJ. In patients with **life expectancy >6 months and good performance status**, surgical GJ is suggested over SEMS.

**2. Covered versus uncovered SEMS.** In patients with incurable malignant GOO undergoing palliative endoscopic stent placement, there is **insufficient evidence to preferentially recommend covered over uncovered SEMS**. The final decision should be based on regional stent availability, patient characteristics, and patient preferences. *(Conditional recommendation, moderate quality of evidence)*

**3. Benign GOO — endoscopic versus surgical management.** There is **insufficient evidence to support endoscopic management over surgical management** of benign GOO. *(Conditional recommendation, low quality of evidence)*
> **Comment.** Factors to consider in choosing management: **etiology** of the benign GOO, **length of the stricture**, and **response to initial EBD**.

*The Summary and Conclusions section restates Recommendation 3 as "insufficient evidence to uniformly support either endoscopic or surgical management for mixed varieties of benign GOO."*

### Etiology (Table 1)
- **Benign:** peptic ulcer disease (PUD); nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID)–induced strictures; caustic ingestions; chronic pancreatitis; pseudocysts; walled-off pancreatic necrosis; GI polyps; lipomas; surgical anastomotic strictures; foreign bodies; radiation-induced strictures; eosinophilic enteritis; tuberculosis.
- **Malignant:** pancreatic cancer; gastric cancer; duodenal cancer; gastric carcinoids; small intestine carcinoids; pancreas neuroendocrine tumors; lymphoma; sarcomas; GI stromal tumors; ampulla of Vater cancer; metastatic cancer; cholangiocarcinoma; gallbladder cancer.
- Epidemiology: malignancy is now the leading cause of GOO (acid suppression and *H. pylori* treatment reduced PUD); GOO occurs in ~20% of advanced pancreatic cancer, >11,000 pancreatic cancer patients with GOO in 2019; PUD from NSAIDs or *H. pylori* is the most common benign cause.

### SEMS versus surgical GJ — effect estimates (Table 5)
| Outcome | Effect (SEMS vs surgical GJ) | Certainty |
|---|---|---|
| Survival (1 RCT) | MD 22 days **lower** (95% CI 53.45 lower to 9.45 higher) | Moderate |
| Survival (9 observational) | MD 42.85 days **fewer** | Very low |
| Postoperative mortality (1 RCT) | RR 0.72 (0.14–3.64) | Moderate |
| Re-establishment of oral intake (3 RCTs) | RR 0.98 (0.88–1.09) | Moderate |
| Time to oral intake (2 RCTs) | MD **3.07 days fewer** with SEMS (95% CI 4.76 fewer to 1.39 fewer) | Moderate |
| Length of hospital stay (3 RCTs) | MD **6.7 days shorter** with SEMS (9.41 to 3.98 fewer) | Moderate |
| Patency (2 RCTs) | RR 5.08 (0.96–26.74) | Moderate |
| Reintervention rate (14 observational + 1 RCT) | OR **2.95** (1.70–5.14) — higher with SEMS | Very low |
| Serious adverse events (3 RCTs) | RR 1.15 (0.33–3.98) | Moderate |
| Serious adverse events **requiring reintervention** (3 RCTs) | RR **4.71** (1.36–16.30) — higher with SEMS | Moderate |

- Cost: median 90-day cost of SEMS + readmissions **$18,500** (range $4100–$49,500) vs **$37,200** ($12,200–$67,800) for surgery in a US claims analysis; a decision-analysis model put enteral stent placement at $8213 vs laparoscopic GJ $10,340 over 1 month. A claims review suggested surgical GJ may be beneficial over SEMS for **life expectancy ≥2 months** (incremental cost-effectiveness ratio $192 per day with a Gastric Outlet Obstruction Scoring System [GOOSS] score ≥2, i.e. soft solids to regular diet, adjusted for survival).
- The panel cautions that observational studies reporting longer survival with surgical GJ are confounded by selection (better albumin, better performance status).

### Covered versus uncovered SEMS (Table 6; 5 RCTs)
| Outcome | Covered | Uncovered | Effect | Certainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tolerance of oral diet | 92.3% | 90.5% | RD 0.02 (−0.03 to 0.07) | Moderate |
| **Stent occlusion** | **4.1%** | **25.2%** | RD −0.21 (−0.27 to −0.15) | Moderate |
| **Stent migration** | **11.8%** | **2.7%** | RD 0.09 (0.04 to 0.14) | Moderate |
| Reintervention rate | 17.3% | 19.9% | RD −0.03 (−0.11 to 0.06) | Moderate |
| Adverse events | 22.3% | 30.5% | RD −0.08 (−0.17 to 0.00) | Moderate |

- All five RCTs were conducted in East Asia; three included only gastric cancer (247/443 patients, 55.8%), limiting generalizability to other etiologies. **No covered enteral stent is FDA-approved/available in the US for malignant GOO.**
- Occlusion in uncovered stents is usually manageable endoscopically (ablation, stent-in-stent); migration of covered stents may be harder to treat and may carry higher perforation risk. Anchoring covered duodenal SEMS with clips or endoscopic suturing is **not yet of proven benefit**.
- No cost comparison between covered and uncovered SEMS was identified.

### EUS-guided gastroenterostomy (outside the PICO questions)
- Systematic review/meta-analysis, 5 studies, 199 patients (benign and malignant GOO): **technical success 92.6%** (95% CI 88.26–95.79; I² = 0%); **serious adverse events 5.61%** (2.87–10.67) including stent maldeployment, peritonitis, surgical intervention, hemoperitoneum; **reintervention 11.43%** (7.29–17.46).
- A single-centre retrospective comparison (EUS-GE n = 22 vs endoscopic gastroduodenal SEMS n = 78) found 100% technical success in both and similar hospital stay, but **higher reintervention in the SEMS group (32% vs 8.3%, P = .021)**.
- Retrospective EUS-GE (n = 93) vs surgical GJ (n = 63): technical success favoured surgery (100% vs 87%, P = .009), with no difference in length of stay, serious adverse events, recurrent GOO, or mean time to reintervention.
- **Relative contraindications to EUS-GE:** tumor or blood vessels (including perigastric varices) involving the intended transgastric access site; large-volume ascites; peritoneal carcinomatosis; bowel obstruction distal to the intended gastroenterostomy site.
- LAMS use to create the gastroenterostomy was **off-label** at the time of drafting.

### Benign GOO — endoscopic balloon dilation
- Systematic review of 14 retrospective studies, 596 patients. Heterogeneity precluded pooling.
- **High response to EBD** when GOO is due to PUD **with inflammatory factors addressed** — smoking and NSAIDs discontinued, *H. pylori* treated.
- **Less responsive to EBD:** corrosive/caustic strictures; strictures from chronic pancreatitis; **long strictures (>3 cm)**; strictures that have required **multiple dilations** (need for multiple EBD predicts treatment failure and the need for surgery).
- Covered SEMS for benign GOO: Korean series of 22 patients (21/22 from PUD) showed 81.8% early improvement in GOOSS, sustained in 67%, but **migration in 62.5%** (n = 15). **Use of covered SEMS in benign disease is off-label in the US**; all dedicated enteral stents available in the US are uncovered and should not be used for benign disease because they are not removable. Off-label covered LAMS for short benign pyloroduodenal strictures is described in case series.
- Surgery is typically reserved as salvage after insufficient endoscopic therapy or a severe endoscopic adverse event (e.g. perforation). Modern pyloroplasty and laparoscopic GJ are reported with low morbidity and mortality.

### Pediatric considerations
- GOO in children is usually benign; **infantile hypertrophic pyloric stenosis** is the most common cause, typically with nonbilious emesis, and **surgery is the standard management**. Endoscopy is used only to exclude other diagnoses outside infancy; EUS has been used in some cases.
- Beyond infancy, **peptic and caustic ulceration** are the most common causes; antral webs, duplication cysts, and bezoars are reported; malignancy is rare. EBD has been used, with limited reports of needle-knife incisional therapy and SEMS placement.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **No contradiction with ingested sources identified.** The guideline makes no modality preference on any of its three questions, so it cannot conflict with a stronger claim elsewhere; where other ingested sources state a preference, this document's position is that the evidence is insufficient.
- **Open:** whether covered or uncovered SEMS is superior for severe adverse events specifically — the panel calls the available literature unclear on this critical outcome, and notes the meta-analyses did not account for adverse-event severity.
- **Open:** EUS-GE versus both surgical GJ and SEMS — no prospective RCT existed at the time of drafting; the panel calls for them.
- **Open:** benign GOO — no comparative studies of endoscopic versus surgical management exist; prior studies focused on endoscopic management to avoid surgery rather than on prospective comparison.
- **US practice constraint, not a contradiction:** the covered-versus-uncovered evidence is entirely East Asian and cannot be acted on in the US, where no covered enteral stent is approved for this indication.
