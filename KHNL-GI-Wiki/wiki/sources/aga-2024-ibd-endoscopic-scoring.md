## Summary

Endoscopic scoring systems in IBD provide objectivity, uniformity, and standardization of mucosal reporting, and are advised for assessing prognosis and treatment efficacy. This Commentary catalogs the indices in current use — **MES** and **UCEIS** for ulcerative colitis, **CDEIS** and **SES-CD** (plus **MM-SES-CD**) for Crohn's disease, and the **modified Rutgeerts score** for postoperative CD — gives each index's component scoring, and states the response/remission cutoffs used in trials and STRIDE-II.

The clinical argument for using them is dissociation: **as many as one-half of patients in clinical remission still have endoscopic evidence of active disease** (in SONIC, only half of those in clinical remission had endoscopic healing), so patients are under- or over-treated when endoscopic assessment is skipped. Severe endoscopic findings predict hospitalization and surgery in both UC and CD; mucosal healing predicts clinical remission, avoidance of surgery, and steroid-free remission.

The practical problem is uptake and reproducibility. MES and SES-CD are embraced by IBD specialists but not by general gastroenterologists; interobserver agreement for MES ranges from high to only fair/moderate, and CDEIS interobserver agreement for grading superficial vs deep ulcerations is only fair. Dedicated training programs can raise less-experienced gastroenterologists to expert central-reader performance. The Commentary then looks forward: **virtual chromoendoscopy scores (PICaSSO)** that approximate histology, endocytoscopy and probe-based confocal laser endomicroscopy, barrier-function assessment, and **artificial intelligence** for standardized scoring.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Ulcerative colitis — Table 1 reproduced

| Index | Study designs | Description and scoring | Level of validation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MES (1987)** | Prospective study | Normal or inactive disease (0); Mild disease: erythema, decreased vascular pattern, mild friability (1); Moderate disease: marked erythema, absent vascular pattern, moderate friability, erosions (2); Severe disease: spontaneous bleeding, ulcerations (3) | **Not validated** |
| **UCEIS (2012)** | Used in prospective studies | Grading system based on 3 variable subscores — **A. Vascular pattern:** normal (0), patchy obliteration (1), obliterated (2). **B. Bleeding:** none (0), mucosal (1), luminal mild (2), luminal moderate to severe (3). **C. Erosions and ulcers:** none (0), erosions (1), superficial ulcer (2), deep ulcer (3) | **Validated** |

- **MES** is scored on **vascular pattern, erythema, friability, erosions, ulcerations, and bleeding in the most inflamed part of the colon**. Strength: ease of use. Limitations: lack of validation and variability in the degree of friability. Interobserver agreement reported anywhere from high to only fair/moderate.
- **UCEIS** scores **vascular pattern, bleeding, and erosions/ulcerations in the most severely affected part of the colon**, and **excludes friability**. Developed as an **11-point score** — erosions/ulcers (1–4), vascular pattern (1–3), bleeding (1–4) — and further **simplified to an 8-point tool** — erosions/ulcers (0–2), vascular pattern (0–2), bleeding (1–4) — which had satisfactory interobserver agreement. Excellent interobserver and intraobserver agreement among 7 experienced central readers.
- **Cutoffs / definitions (UC):**
  - **Endoscopic response** = reduction of the MES by **1** or the UCEIS score by **2**.
  - **Endoscopic remission (STRIDE-II)** = **MES 0** or **UCEIS 1**.
  - **Endoscopic improvement** = **MES 0/1** — remains a commonly reported outcome in UC clinical trials.
- **ACT-1/ACT-2 (infliximab):** mucosal healing by **MES 0 or 1 at week 8** was associated with a significantly lower colectomy rate beyond 54 weeks.

### Crohn's disease — Table 2 reproduced

| Index | Descriptives | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| **CDEIS** | Deep ulcerations: 0 if absent or **12** if present. Superficial ulcerations: 0 if absent or **6** if present. Length of ulcerated mucosa (0–10 cm): score 0 to 10 according to length in centimeters. Length of diseased mucosa (0–10 cm): score 0 to 10 according to length in centimeters. Ulcerated stenosis: 0 if absent, **3** if present. Nonulcerated stenosis: 0 if absent, **3** if present. **Grading (0–44)** using the above descriptive features (ulcerations, area of involvement, stenosis characteristics) in **5 bowel segments: terminal ileum, right colon, transverse colon, left colon, rectum** | Detailed evaluation, assesses global disease, **partially validated**, no validated definition of mucosal healing. Complex (many variables, scores range 0–44) |
| **SES-CD** | Ulcers: 0 if absent, 1 if between 0.1–0.5 cm, 2 if larger than 2 cm. Ulcerated surface: 0 if none, 1 if less than 10% of the segment, 2 if between 10–20% of the segment, 3 if more than 30% of the segment. Affected surface: 0 if none, 1 if less than 50% of the segment, 2 if between 50 and 75% of the segment, 3 if more than 75% of the segment. Narrowing: 0 if none, 1 if single stenosis passable by colonoscope, 2 if multiple and passable by colonoscope, 3 if not passable. **Grading (0–56 points)** using size of ulcers, degree of ulcerated surface, presence of narrowing, in all examined segments | Simplified version of CDEIS, **partially validated**, high degree of correlation with CDEIS for grading and responsiveness to changes |

> **Fidelity flag — the published Table 2 SES-CD rows are internally incomplete.** The **Ulcers** row lists only 3 of the 4 point levels (0, 1, 2) and skips the band between 0.5 cm and 2 cm, and the **Ulcerated surface** row jumps from "10–20%" to ">30%", leaving 20%–30% unassigned. The four SES-CD variables are stated elsewhere in the text to be **scored 0 to 3 in the same 5 ileocolonic segments**, so a level is missing as printed. Reproduced here as published; the missing bands are **not** supplied from outside sources.

- **CDEIS calculation:** the numbers are added within each segment and divided by the number of segments evaluated; additional points for ulcerated stenosis and **3 points for non-ulcerated stenosis**.
- **CDEIS elementary features:** no ulcerated lesions, ulcerations, and stenosis, with the pattern of their surface, in **5 segments (rectum; sigmoid and left colon; transverse colon; right colon; ileum)**. Time-consuming, requires training, not broadly used.
- **Cutoffs / definitions (CD):**
  - **Endoscopic response** = **SES-CD reduction of 50%** or **CDEIS reduction of 50%**.
  - **Endoscopic healing** = **SES-CD <3**, absence of ulceration, or **CDEIS <4**.
  - **Partial endoscopic remission** (minor or no mucosal lesions) = **CDEIS <6**.
  - **Complete endoscopic remission** (mucosal healing — no lesions or scattered lesions only) = **CDEIS cutoff of <4 or <3, or ≤4**.
  - **CDEIS endoscopic response** was originally a decrease from baseline of at least **4 or 5** points; subsequent studies defined it as a decrease from baseline of at least **3 points or 5 points, or 75%**.
- **Reliability (central reading):** CDEIS — intraobserver ICC 0.89 (95% CI 0.86–0.93), interobserver ICC 0.71 (95% CI 0.61–0.79). SES-CD — intraobserver ICC 0.91 (95% CI 0.87–0.94), interobserver ICC 0.83 (95% CI 0.75–0.89). CDEIS interobserver agreement for grading superficial vs deep ulcerations is only **fair**.
- **MM-SES-CD (Modified Multiplier of the SES-CD):** assigns **higher weight to certain features and locations** in the bowel shown to be less likely to heal after 1 year of biologic therapy; established numerical cutoffs categorizing endoscopic disease severity that were prognostic for 1-year endoscopic and clinical remission. A study by Narula et al found significantly more disease progression with **SES-CD ≥4** than SES-CD <4, and that **MM-SES-CD <22.5** was the best threshold for predicting disease progression.
- **Novel simplified CD score — stricture localization rules:** strictures **confined to the ileocecal valve are counted in the ileum score**; **non-intubation of the ileum without stricture should be scored "not assessed"**; **every stricture should be scored before endoscopic therapeutic management**.

### Postoperative CD — Table 3, modified Rutgeerts score (reproduced)

| Endoscopic findings | Score |
|---|---|
| No aphthous ulcers | **i0** |
| ≤5 aphthous ulcers in the distal ileum | **i1** |
| >5 aphthous lesions with normal mucosa between the lesions, or skip areas of large lesions | **i2** |
| Lesions confined to the ileocolonic anastomosis (including anastomotic stenosis) | **i2a** |
| >5 aphthous lesions or larger lesions, with normal mucosa in between, in the neoterminal ileum (with or without anastomosis lesions) | **i2b** |
| Diffuse aphthous ileitis with diffusely inflamed mucosa | **i3** |
| Diffuse inflammation with large ulcers, nodules, and/or narrowing in the neoterminal ileum | **i4** |

- **Timing and technique:** severity of endoscopic activity is assessed **6 to 12 months post-surgery**; the score is applied to the **neoterminal ileum and ileocolonic anastomosis**, where inflammation typically recurs after ileocolonic resection. **At least 5 cm of neoterminal ileum should be fully evaluated.**
- The score is based on the **number of aphthous lesions, extent of ileitis, and presence of ulcers, nodules, and narrowing**, and ranks inflammation grossly **i0 to i4**. The **modified** score adds the i2a/i2b distinction — lesions confined to the ileocolonic anastomosis (i2a) vs the neoterminal ileum with or without anastomosis involvement (i2b).
- **Prognostic value:** patients in endoscopic remission with **score i1 or less** had a lower risk of clinical recurrence — **8.6% at 8 years** after ileocecal resection; **100% of patients with i4** had symptomatic recurrence **by 4 years**. Selective immunosuppression adjusted for early recurrence based on colonoscopy leads to disease control in most patients.
- **Limitation:** the score was designed for **end-to-end anastomosis 30 years ago**; ulcers in the blind limb of a **side-to-side** ileocolonic anastomosis may represent **ischemic** rather than active inflammatory change and do not necessarily require treatment. A novel adapted postoperative CD score reflecting end-to-side and side-to-end ileocolic anastomoses adds anatomic locations — **neoterminal ileum, ileal inlet, ileal body** — with the **inlet of the neoterminal ileum at higher recurrence risk** (fecal stream stasis, bacterial overgrowth), and lesions from the ileal blind loop possibly surgical rather than recurrent CD. **More prospective studies are needed.**

### Why endoscopic scoring matters clinically

- Treatment goals shifted over the last 10–15 years from symptomatic improvement to endoscopic endpoints, which are associated with clinically important outcomes.
- Endoscopic features at clinical presentation correlate with response to medical therapy and long-term prognosis; **severe endoscopic findings predict need for hospitalization and surgery in both UC and CD**.
- **Clinical–endoscopic dissociation:** as many as **one-half of patients in clinical remission still have endoscopic evidence of active disease**; in **SONIC**, only half of patients in clinical remission had endoscopic healing.
- In SONIC, patients attaining endoscopic response/mucosal healing (**SES-CD decrease of at least 50%**) had corticosteroid-free clinical remission at week 50.
- **STRIDE-II** (IOIBD) sequence: clinical response and clinical remission → normalization of biomarkers → endoscopic healing. Decreasing endoscopic inflammation matters most **early**, before irreversible injury.
- **Histologic healing is not yet a recommended endpoint** requiring adjustment of medical therapy. Other trial endpoints explored: intestinal ultrasound and MRI transmural healing via the **MaRIA score**.

### Practical challenges and the future

- SES-CD and MES are embraced by IBD-focused gastroenterologists but **not universally implemented by general gastroenterologists**, creating problems assessing severity at presentation, response to therapy, or when a patient changes provider.
- Endoscopy report writers (e.g. Provation) have built-in ability to use these scoring systems; online tutorials and a supplementary video accompany the Commentary.
- **Dedicated training programs can increase the performance of less experienced gastroenterologists to the level of expert central reviewers.**
- **Clinical trial central reading:** central readers train via a CRO module, then blindly and independently score videos from onsite endoscopists; discordance between onsite endoscopist and central reader triggers a **second central reader to adjudicate**. Improves accuracy but adds cost.
- **Virtual chromoendoscopy (VCE)** platforms named: NBI (Olympus), optical enhancement iSCAN-OE (Pentax), FICE/LASEREO with linked-color imaging and blue-laser imaging (Fujinon).
- **PICaSSO (Paddington International Virtual Chromoendoscopy Score):** assesses **mucosal changes (elongated crypts, scars, micro-erosions, ulcers) and vascular changes (sparse vessels, vessel dilation, crowding, bleeding)**, **range 0 to 15**, with **endoscopic remission defined as a value ≤3**. Showed strong correlation with 5 histological scores, reproducible across all endoscopic platforms in daily practice; **endoscopic remission by PICaSSO was not inferior to combined endoscopy with histology for predicting clinical outcomes**.
- **Endocytoscopy and probe-based confocal laser endomicroscopy** provide ultra-structural characterization and predict clinical outcomes, but interpretation is entirely subjective and requires high expertise and specialized equipment (referral centers only).
- **Artificial intelligence** has been used to grade disease activity and extent in UC; AI/deep learning reduces practice variability and improves endoscopy quality, but more studies are needed to quantify the benefit.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **No formal evidence ratings and no numbered advice statements** — commentary format.
- **Table 2 SES-CD scoring as printed is incomplete** (missing ulcer-size band; 20%–30% ulcerated-surface gap) — see the fidelity flag above.
- **UCEIS severity cutoffs are undefined:** "the specific cutoffs corresponding to remission vs mild, moderate, and severe disease have yet to be defined." The existing [[ulcerative-colitis]] page cites UCEIS ≥7 as a poor prognostic marker from [[acg-2025-uc]] — this Update does not supply or corroborate that threshold.
- **UCEIS was developed on flexible sigmoidoscopy** — the extent to which colonoscopy influences the score "needs to be determined."
- **UCEIS vs MES:** UCEIS is the validated index, but "the advantage of its use over MES has not been confirmed."
- **CDEIS has no validated definition of mucosal healing**, and several competing complete-remission cutoffs are given (<4, <3, ≤4).
- **Rutgeerts score is anastomosis-technique dependent** — ulcers at a side-to-side anastomosis may be ischemic; the adapted postoperative score awaits prospective validation.
- **Histologic healing** is not yet an actionable endpoint.
- **Pouch endoscopic indices are not covered by this Update** — see [[aga-2024-pouchitis]] for pouch-specific scoring.
