# AGA Clinical Practice Guideline: Fecal Microbiota-Based Therapies (2024)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Year:** 2024
- **Journal:** Gastroenterology (AGA Clinical Practice Guideline)
- **Type:** GRADE-based guideline

---

## Summary

This guideline defines where fecal microbiota-based therapies (conventional FMT and FDA-approved live biotherapeutics) have a role. The clearest indication is **recurrent [[clostridioides-difficile]] infection (CDI)**; use is discouraged outside trials for IBD and IBS, and contraindicated in **severely immunocompromised** patients.

---

## Key Recommendations

- **Rec 1:** In **immunocompetent adults with recurrent CDI**, suggests **fecal microbiota–based therapies** after completing standard-of-care antibiotics.
- **Rec 2:** In **mildly/moderately immunocompromised** adults with recurrent CDI, suggests **conventional FMT**; in **severely immunocompromised** adults, suggests **against** any fecal microbiota–based therapy.
- **Rec 3:** In adults **hospitalized with severe/fulminant CDI not responding** to antimicrobials, suggests **conventional FMT**.
- **Recs 4–7:** In **[[ulcerative-colitis]]**, **[[crohns-disease]]**, **pouchitis**, and **[[irritable-bowel-syndrome]]**, suggests **against conventional FMT except within a clinical trial**.

---

## Clinical Relevance

FMT's evidence-based home is **recurrent and refractory CDI** (with immune-status caveats); it is **not** recommended as routine therapy for IBD or IBS. Anchors microbiome therapeutics within [[clostridioides-difficile]] management.
