DOI: 10.1097/HEP.0000000000000323- **Type:** practice guidance (AASLD "Guidance" — actionable guidance statements, NOT GRADE recommendations; highest available evidence + expert opinion)## SummaryThe 2023 AASLD NAFLD Practice Guidance is the foundational comprehensive document on the clinical assessment and management of NAFLD (now MASLD; see [[aasld-2023-masld-nomenclature]]), updating the prior 2018 guidance to emphasize advances in noninvasive risk stratification and therapeutics. It is structured around 29 numbered **guidance statements** plus extensive "key points." A companion diabetes-focused guideline was written jointly with AACE; pediatric NAFLD is covered separately.The guidance frames fibrosis stage as the primary determinant of liver-related outcomes and mortality, and defines **"at-risk" NASH** as NASH with stage ≥2 fibrosis (F2). Prevalence of NAFLD in US adults is ~25%–30%; NASH-related cirrhosis is already the leading LT indication in women and those >65, and on par with alcohol overall, with hepatic decompensation/HCC/death projected to rise 2–3 fold by 2030. The most common causes of death across the NAFLD spectrum are cardiovascular disease and non-hepatic malignancy (with liver death predominating only in advanced fibrosis).The diagnostic core is a **two-tier noninvasive pathway**: FIB-4 as universal first-line primary risk assessment (cutoff <1.3 rules out advanced fibrosis with high NPV; >2.67 prompts referral), followed by a secondary test (VCTE, MRE, or ELF) when FIB-4 ≥1.3. Liver biopsy is reserved for diagnostic uncertainty, discordant NITs, persistently elevated aminotransferases (>6 mo), or competing diagnoses. The treatment foundation is lifestyle (weight loss thresholds 3–5% steatosis / 7–10% NASH / ≥10% fibrosis; Mediterranean diet; exercise independent of weight loss; ≥3 cups coffee/day; bariatric surgery for eligible non-cirrhotic patients). At publication there were **no FDA-approved drugs for NASH**; the guidance positions pioglitazone (in T2DM), vitamin E (non-diabetic), and semaglutide (for its approved T2DM/obesity indications) as options without proven antifibrotic benefit, and explicitly states metformin/UDCA/DPP-4i/statins/silymarin should NOT be used to treat NASH.## Key Findings / Claims- **At-risk NASH = NASH with ≥F2 fibrosis** → increased risk of cirrhosis and liver-related complications; targeted screening of high-risk populations advised.- All-cause mortality by fibrosis stage: 0.32 (F0–2) → 0.89 (bridging fibrosis) → 1.76 per 100 person-years (cirrhosis); hepatic decompensation HR 6.8 for all-cause mortality; cirrhosis regression → 6-fold reduction in liver-related events.- **True normal ALT = 29–33 U/L (men), 19–25 U/L (women)**; ALT >30 U/L should be considered abnormal. Aminotransferases are frequently normal in advanced NASH and must NOT be used alone to exclude significant fibrosis.- **Genetic modifiers:** PNPLA3 I148M, TM6SF2 (risk), MBOAT7 (risk); HSD17B13 and CIDEB loss-of-function variants are protective. Gene-based risk stratification NOT recommended clinically.- **Alcohol** classified mild (≤20 g♀/30 g♂/d), moderate (21–39♀/31–59♂), heavy (≥40♀/≥60♂); synergistic with obesity/T2DM for fibrosis, HCC, death.- **Screening high-risk populations for advanced fibrosis (Table 4):** T2DM (prevalence of advanced fibrosis 6–19%), medically complicated obesity (4–33%), NAFLD + moderate alcohol use (17%), first-degree relative of NAFLD/NASH cirrhosis patient (18%). Background population prevalence 0.9–2%.- **Statins are safe and underused** across the NAFLD spectrum including compensated cirrhosis; lead to reduced CV morbidity/mortality.- **Surrogate response markers:** ALT decrease ≥17 U/L (or to ≤40 U/L and ≥30% from baseline) and ≥30% decline in MRI-PDFF (5-fold improved odds of NASH resolution) track histologic improvement.## Table 5 — Noninvasive Assessment Cut-Points (by context of use)**"at-risk" NASH defined as NASH with stage ≥2 fibrosis.**| Purpose | Modality | Likely / Rule-in | Unlikely / Rule-out | Caveats ||---|---|---|---|---|| Steatosis | Ultrasound | "Detected" | NA | Low sensitivity for mild steatosis || Steatosis | FibroScan CAP | ≥288 dB/min | — | Limited quantification accuracy || Steatosis | MRI-PDFF | ≥5% | <5% | Most sensitive; tracks dynamic change || At-risk NASH | FAST | ≥0.67 | <0.35 | Sens 90% (<0.35), Spec 90% (≥0.67) || At-risk NASH | MAST | ≥0.242 | ≤0.165 | || At-risk NASH | MEFIB (FIB-4 ≥1.6 + MRE ≥3.3 kPa) | both above | both below | Sequential; >90% PPV for ≥F2 || At-risk NASH | cT1 | ≥875 ms | <825 ms | Needs further validation || Advanced fibrosis | FIB-4 | ≥2.67 | <1.3 | No added cost; inaccurate age <35 || Advanced fibrosis | NFS | ≥0.672 | <−1.44 | Inaccurate age <35, obesity/T2DM || Advanced fibrosis | ELF | ≥9.8 | <7.7 | Reference-lab blood test; cost || Advanced fibrosis | FIBROSpect II | ≥17 | <17 | Reference-lab; cost || Advanced fibrosis | VCTE | ≥12 kPa | <8 kPa | Point of care || Advanced fibrosis | MRE | ≥3.63 kPa | <2.55 kPa | AUROC 0.93 || Cirrhosis | FIB-4 | ≥3.48 (rule-in, 90% spec) | <1.67 (rule-out, 90% sens) | || Cirrhosis | ELF | ≥11.3 | <7.7 | ELF ≥11.3 → ↑ risk of hepatic decompensation || Cirrhosis | VCTE | ≥20 kPa | <8 kPa | LSM ≥20 associated with cirrhosis || Cirrhosis | MRE | ≥5 kPa | <3 kPa | ~95% specificity; predicts decompensation |## Guidance Statements (verbatim)1. Statins are safe and recommended for CVD risk reduction in patients with NAFLD across the disease spectrum, including compensated cirrhosis.2. Limited data exist on the safety and efficacy of statins in patients with decompensated cirrhosis, although statin use with careful monitoring could be considered in patients with high CVD risk.3. Hypertriglyceridemia can be managed through lifestyle changes and supplementation with omega-3 fatty acids, icosapent ethyl, or fibrates.4. Patients with diabetes are at higher risk for NASH and advanced fibrosis and should be screened for advanced fibrosis.5. Patients with NAFLD should be screened for the presence of T2DM.6. In patients with NAFLD, alcohol can be a cofactor for liver disease progression, and intake should be assessed on a regular basis.7. Patients with clinically significant hepatic fibrosis (≥F2) should abstain from alcohol use completely.8. NAFLD is more common in men with androgen deficiency, but current data do not support routine measurement of testosterone levels. If hypogonadism is present, as suggested by clinical signs or symptoms, this should be treated accordingly.9. General population-based screening for NAFLD is not advised.10. All patients with hepatic steatosis or clinically suspected NAFLD based on the presence of obesity and metabolic risk factors should undergo primary risk assessment with FIB-4.11. High-risk individuals, such as those with T2DM, medically complicated obesity, family history of cirrhosis, or more than mild alcohol consumption, should be screened for advanced fibrosis.12. In patients with pre-DM, T2DM, or 2 or more metabolic risk factors (or imaging evidence of hepatic steatosis), primary risk assessment with FIB-4 should be repeated every 1–2 y.13. Patients with NASH cirrhosis are at the highest risk for liver-related outcomes and require routine surveillance for HCC, esophageal varices, and monitoring for decompensation.14. Patients with suspected advanced NASH or discordant NITs should be referred to a specialist for evaluation, management, and/or further diagnostic evaluation.15. Aminotransferase levels are frequently normal in patients with advanced liver disease due to NASH and should not be used in isolation to exclude the presence of NASH with clinically significant fibrosis.16. First-degree relatives of patients with NASH cirrhosis should be counseled regarding their increased individual risk and offered screening for advanced hepatic fibrosis.17. Although standard ultrasound can detect hepatic steatosis, it is not recommended as a tool to identify hepatic steatosis due to low sensitivity across the NAFLD spectrum.18. CAP as a point-of-care technique may be used to identify steatosis. MRI-PDFF can additionally quantify steatosis.19. If FIB-4 is ≥1.3, VCTE, MRE, or ELF may be used to exclude advanced fibrosis.20. Patients with NAFLD who are overweight or obese should be prescribed a diet that leads to a caloric deficit. When possible, diets with limited carbohydrates and saturated fat and enriched with high fiber and unsaturated fats (e.g., Mediterranean diet) should be encouraged due to their additional cardiovascular benefits.21. Patients with NAFLD should be strongly encouraged to increase their activity level to the extent possible. Individualized prescriptive exercise recommendations may increase sustainability and have benefits independent of weight loss.22. Bariatric surgery should be considered as a therapeutic option in patients who meet criteria for metabolic weight loss surgery, as it effectively resolves NAFLD or NASH in the majority of patients without cirrhosis and reduces mortality from CVD and malignancy.23. There are currently no FDA-approved medications for the treatment of NAFLD, but drugs approved to treat associated comorbidities with potential benefit in NAFLD may be considered in the appropriate clinical setting.24. Semaglutide can be considered for its approved indications (T2DM/obesity) in patients with NASH, as it confers a cardiovascular benefit and improves NASH.25. Pioglitazone improves NASH and can be considered for patients with NASH in the context of patients with T2DM.26. Vitamin E can be considered in select individuals as it improves NASH in some patients without diabetes.27. Available data on semaglutide, pioglitazone, and vitamin E do not demonstrate an antifibrotic benefit, and none has been carefully studied in patients with cirrhosis.28. Metformin, ursodeoxycholic acid, dipeptidyl peptidase-4, statins, and silymarin are well studied in NASH and should not be used as a treatment for NASH as they do not offer a meaningful histological benefit.29. Improvement in ALT or reduction in liver fat content by imaging in response to an intervention can be used as a surrogate for histological improvement in disease activity.## Treatment Detail- **Weight loss dose-response:** 3–5% → improves steatosis; 7–10% → improves NASH; >10% → improves/reverses fibrosis. <10% of patients achieve effective weight loss at 1 y; multidisciplinary support needed.- **Diet:** caloric deficit; Mediterranean diet preferred (CV benefit + liver fat reduction); excess fructose/SSB harmful. **Coffee** ≥3 cups/day (caffeinated or not) associated with less advanced liver disease.- **Exercise:** ~150 min/week moderate or +60 min/week activity; benefit independent of weight loss; cirrhotics prioritize protein + recognize physical limits.- **Bariatric surgery:** resolves NASH without worsening fibrosis in ~80% at 1 y (sustained at 5 y); decompensated cirrhosis = absolute contraindication; compensated NASH cirrhosis only at high-volume centers / with LT.- **Pharmacotherapy (Table 6):** Vitamin E (rrr-α) 800 IU/day (PIVENS: ≥2-pt NAS reduction; non-diabetic, no fibrosis benefit; ?hemorrhagic stroke/prostate cancer); Pioglitazone 30–45 mg (improves NASH ± fibrosis; weight gain/HF/bone loss); Liraglutide/Semaglutide (improve steatosis/NASH, CV benefit, no proven fibrosis benefit); Tirzepatide & SGLT-2i (steatosis reduction on imaging).
