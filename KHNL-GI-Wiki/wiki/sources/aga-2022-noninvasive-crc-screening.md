## Summary

Expert commentary on **how to use the noninvasive CRC screening options** in average-risk adults — which tests work, for whom, what a positive result obliges, and what a screening program must measure. Average risk (no high-risk feature) is ~75%–80% of the US population and is the target audience; lifetime US CRC risk is ~4%.

The core argument is that within the average-risk pool, risk is graded — mostly by **age**, but also by sex, race/ethnicity, and lifestyle — and noninvasive testing should be steered toward the lower-risk end of that pool while [[colonoscopy]] is conserved for those with more risk factors and a higher likelihood of advanced neoplasia (AN). CRC incidence is 35.1/100,000 at ages 45–49 and 61.2/100,000 at 50–54; early-onset CRC is now 12% of CRCs and 7% of CRC deaths. Modeling (not trial data) underwrites the age-45 start: screening any recommended strategy from 45 through 75 in 1000 adults yields 286–337 life-years gained and avoids 42–61 CRCs and 24–28 CRC deaths; starting at 45 rather than 50 gains 6.2% more life-years at the cost of 17% more colonoscopy. One hybrid model — **annual FIT ages 45–49, then colonoscopy ages 50–70** — was the best balance of lives gained against colonoscopies performed.

The test-by-test review supplies the performance numbers. **FIT** has displaced gFOBT worldwide (better adherence, no dietary or medication restriction, fewer samples); the USMSTF positivity threshold is **≤20 µg Hb/g feces**, and the update reproduces two meta-analyses plus the pivotal trial so sensitivity/specificity can be read off against the chosen cutoff. **FIT has no utility for serrated lesion detection.** **MTsDNA (Cologuard)** is markedly more sensitive than FIT for CRC and for advanced adenoma but notably less specific, and is FDA-approved only for average-risk adults **aged 45–85**. **Septin 9** (Epi proColon) — the only FDA-approved (not Medicare-covered) serum test at the time — has CRC sensitivity 48% and advanced-adenoma sensitivity 11.2%, and is **not endorsed by any CRC screening guideline**. The **PolypDx** urine metabolomic test is not commercially available.

The final third is the operational half of the argument: a noninvasive program is only as good as the colonoscopy that follows a positive test. Delays beyond 6 months after a positive FIT are associated with higher rates of advanced adenoma, CRC, and advanced-stage CRC. The update proposes explicit, numeric program metrics (test completion, result notification, colonoscopy offer, colonoscopy completion), insists on documenting a **3-generation family cancer history** so that high-risk patients are routed to colonoscopy instead of a stool test, and states plainly that a positive noninvasive test is an *indicator of CRC risk* that must be followed by a timely high-quality colonoscopy to complete the screening spectrum.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Risk stratification (Table 1, reproduced)

| Risk category | Level of CRC risk, % | Risk factors |
|---|---|---|
| Very high | >20 | Personal or family history of a hereditary CRC syndrome (eg, adenomatous polyposis syndromes, Lynch syndrome, and hamartomatous polyposis syndromes); serrated polyposis syndrome |
| High | 10–20 | Family history of 1 or more first-degree relative with CRC younger than 60 y **or** 2 first-degree relatives at any age; personal history of AA or advanced serrated polyp; inflammatory bowel disease |
| Average risk | 4 | No symptoms and no factor above |

- Average risk ≈ **75%–80% of the US population**; lifetime US CRC risk ~4%.
- Above-average risk → **screening at a younger age, colonoscopy the preferred modality**; average risk → any endorsed option.
- Within average risk, **age is the main gradient**: 35.1/100,000 at 45–49 → 61.2/100,000 at 50–54; CRC rose in both bands over 20 years, most dramatically in non-Hispanic White men and women. Early-onset CRC (<50 y) = 12% of CRCs, 7% of CRC deaths. Observational data implicate obesity, low physical activity, and ultraprocessed food.
- **Other average-risk modifiers:** premenopausal women have lower CRC risk than same-age men of every race/ethnicity, and the sex difference persists with age. Black incidence 43.6 vs White 36.6/100,000; mortality 18.5 vs 13.6/100,000 — by ages 50–54 CRC incidence in Black men and women is significantly higher than in White or Hispanic men and women. Alaska Native and American Indian populations are second in incidence (42.3/100,000) and mortality (15.1/100,000). Smoking, alcohol, obesity, waist circumference, physical activity, diet, and aspirin/NSAID use also modulate risk.
- **Risk scores:** single-nucleotide-polymorphism panels ± clinical factors show only **mild discriminatory value (by AUC) for predicting AN**. Simple clinical scoring systems show potential; if robust, lower-risk individuals would be the noninvasive-testing candidates and higher-risk individuals — **including most by age 60** — the colonoscopy candidates.
- **Program-level rule:** noninvasive methods should be considered where the prevalence of risk factors and AN is low; colonoscopy is preferable where risk factors and likelihood of AN are greater.

### Age to start — what actually supports 45

- Guidelines offer a **conditional** (ACG; American Cancer Society 2018) or **grade B** (USPSTF) recommendation to start at 45 in asymptomatic average-risk individuals, **agnostic to screening method**.
- The real reduction in CRC incidence or mortality from screening 45–49-year-olds is **unknown**; support is mostly modeling (life-years lost to CRC mortality similar for ages 45–49 and 65–69).
- Screening any recommended strategy ages 45→75, per 1000 adults: **286–337 life-years gained, 42–61 CRCs avoided, 24–28 CRC deaths avoided** — better than starting at 50.
- Starting any option at 45 vs 50: **+6.2% life-years gained, +17% colonoscopy**, depending on modality.
- **Hybrid model:** annual FIT ages 45–49 → colonoscopy ages 50–70 may best balance lives gained against colonoscopies performed.

### Stool-based tests

- **gFOBT:** randomized trials show annual or biennial gFOBT reduces CRC mortality. High-sensitivity gFOBT outperforms older gFOBT, but **FIT has supplanted gFOBT worldwide** — greater patient adherence, no dietary or medication restrictions, fewer test samples.
- **FIT** measures human hemoglobin. Reportable **quantitatively (µg hemoglobin per g of stool)** or, as FDA-approved, **qualitatively as positive/negative against a threshold value**.
- **Positivity threshold: the USMSTF recommends ≤20 µg/g**, supported by 2 meta-analyses for detection of CRC and AA.
- **FIT has no utility for serrated colorectal lesion detection.**
- **MTsDNA (Cologuard)** = an FIT + 2 DNA methylation markers (*BMP3*, *NDRG-4*) + assessment of *KRAS* mutations + a marker of total human DNA. Positivity comes from a **validated, prespecified logistic regression algorithm**; the test result does **not** report which component was positive. **FDA-approved only in average-risk adults aged 45–85 y.**

- **Pivotal trial narrative figures:** sensitivity for CRC and for AA plus sessile serrated polyps ≥10 mm was **92.3% and 42.4% with MTsDNA** vs **73.8% and 23.8% with FIT** (*P* = .002 and *P* < .001). Specificity for CRC: **MTsDNA 86.6% vs FIT 94.9%** (*P* < .001).
- **Alaska Native population:** detection of CRC, AA, and sessile serrated polyps >10 mm was **50% with MTsDNA vs 31% with FIT** (*P* = .01). Sensitivity/specificity for CRC: MTsDNA **100%/93%**, FIT **75%/96%**.
- **Black participants (subanalysis of a comparative MTsDNA-vs-FIT study):** overall sensitivity and specificity for CRC and AA **43% and 91% for MTsDNA vs 32% and 97% for FIT**; performance **did not differ between Black and White participants**.

### Positive MTsDNA with a negative colonoscopy — do not chase the aerodigestive tract

- Subanalysis of the 13.3% of pivotal-trial participants (1216 of 9166) with a negative (normal or only non-advanced adenoma) colonoscopy and comprehensive cancer follow-up.
- At a median of **5.4 years**, incident aerodigestive cancers occurred in **2.4% of discordant** results (negative colonoscopy + positive MTsDNA) vs **1.1% of concordant** results — **no difference in the risk ratio** between groups.
- Versus SEER: aerodigestive cancer incidence was **lower than expected in the concordant group (RR 0.4; 95% CI 0.2–0.6)** and **not significantly greater than expected in the discordant group (RR 0.8; 95% CI 0.3–1.9)**.
- Those authors suggest patients with a **negative, high-quality colonoscopy should not undergo further testing**; the USMSTF recommends that, **absent symptoms or signs of upper gastrointestinal pathology, upper GI evaluation is not indicated**.

### Blood-based tests

- **Septin 9 (Epi proColon, Epigenomics)** — at the time of writing, the **only FDA-approved (but not Medicare-approved) serum test for CRC screening**. PCR-based qualitative detection of methylation of the promoter region of *Septin 9* DNA.
- **FDA-approved indication:** average-risk adults **50 years and older who have first been offered other USPSTF-endorsed CRC screening tests and refused.**
- **Accuracy:** assessed in a subset of 7941 asymptomatic average-risk adults >50 y in the US and Germany undergoing screening colonoscopy; characteristics based on 2 PCR replicates in 53 patients with CRC and 1457 without. **CRC sensitivity 48%, specificity 91.5%; AA sensitivity 11.2%.** With a third PCR replicate in available samples, **sensitivity 63.9% and specificity 88.4%**.
- **Modeling (Microsimulation Screening Analysis-Colon):** assuming perfect adherence, **annual Septin 9 gave more QALYs gained and more CRC cases and deaths averted than annual FIT — but with high rates of colonoscopy.**
- **Currently Septin 9 is not endorsed by CRC screening guidelines.** Large CRC screening studies of blood-based tests were underway (NCT04369053, NCT04136002, NCT04144751).

### Urine-based tests

- **PolypDx** (Metabolomic Technologies Inc), a metabolomic urine test for adenomas: 867 Canadian patients, validated in 661 Chinese participants undergoing colonoscopy. Unblinded training set then blinded set — adenomas identified with **89% and 83% sensitivity, 50% and 51% specificity**. Chinese study: **sensitivity 83%, specificity 42.4%**. **Not currently available commercially.**

### Quality metrics for a noninvasive screening program (proposed — "aspirational, but attainable")

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Noninvasive tests **completed** / tests ordered | **≥90% within 1 year** of ordering |
| Outreach to patients who have not completed testing | trigger at **1 month** after ordering |
| Outreach to deliver the **test result** | **100% of patients within ≤2 weeks** of the result |
| Positive test → **offered a colonoscopy date** | **≥80% within 3 months**, **100% within 6 months** |
| Colonoscopies **performed** / colonoscopies recommended | **≥95% within 6 months** of the positive noninvasive test |

- **Exclusions** from the completion metrics: patients too sick to undergo the noninvasive test or colonoscopy, who have moved, or who cannot be contacted by phone or mail.
- **All** patients with a positive result should receive a **recommendation for colonoscopy**. Patients with negative results should be told the **appropriate recall interval** and **how recall will occur** (gastroenterology practice, primary care physician, or patient calling).
- **Delays in colonoscopy longer than 6 months after a positive FIT are associated with higher risk of AA, CRC, and advanced-stage CRC.**
- Programs should assess **appropriateness of patient selection** — high-risk individuals should preferably be screened with colonoscopy — and should document and report efforts to obtain an accurate **3-generation family cancer history**, with attention to CRC and AAs **including age at diagnosis**.
- Poor adherence at any level should trigger review of protocols; root cause (patient noncompliance vs program process) should be determined. Program performance should be measured and **ideally reported publicly**.

### Summary position

- Ideal screening would use precision medicine — clinical factors (age, sex, race, family history), lifestyle factors (smoking, obesity, physical activity, medications), and genomic factors (SNPs) — to find the lower-risk individuals inside the average-risk cohort for whom noninvasive screening is appropriate.
- Evidence that CRC rose in 45–49-year-olds over 20 years is **strong**, justifying the age-45 start, **yet risk remains relatively low for most individuals before age 50** — so noninvasive screening may suit younger screenees, with colonoscopy as they age into higher-risk states.
- Conversely, risk-scoring schemes suggest **current smoking, obesity, and low physical activity** may identify younger individuals at higher AA risk who benefit from colonoscopy.
- **Patients should be informed that a positive noninvasive test is an indicator of CRC risk and must be followed by a timely, high-quality colonoscopy to complete the screening spectrum.**

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Septin 9 vs the newer cell-free DNA blood tests ([[aga-2025-crc-blood-tests]]).** This 2022 update states Septin 9 is the *only* FDA-approved serum test and that blood tests are not endorsed by any CRC screening guideline. The 2025 AGA Commentary supersedes that on blood testing: Guardant Shield was FDA-approved in May 2024 and is CMS-covered every 3 years, and Freenome is validated. **The newer source governs the blood-test section**; the 2022 Septin 9 numbers remain the best account of Septin 9 itself, which neither source endorses. Note also the divergent modeling verdicts — the 2022 update reports annual Septin 9 beating annual FIT on QALYs *assuming perfect adherence and accepting high colonoscopy rates*, whereas 2025 modeling puts triennial cfDNA tests well below annual FIT on both incidence and mortality reduction. The assumptions differ (test, interval, adherence), so this is a difference in question asked rather than a flat contradiction, but the 2025 framing is the one to quote.
- **Family-history threshold vs [[aga-2023-crc-screening-risk-stratification]].** This 2022 update keeps the classic split — high risk requires an FDR with CRC **younger than 60 y**, or 2 FDRs at any age. The 2023 AGA CPU drops the <60 cutoff and treats **any** FDR with CRC as increased risk. **The 2023 document is newer and governs initiation**; the 2022 table is retained here as the risk-percentage framework (>20% / 10–20% / 4%), which 2023 does not supply.
- **MTsDNA interval.** The interval is given as a range (1–3 y) because the societies disagree — USPSTF at the 1-year end, ACG and USMSTF at 3 years. The wiki asserts the ACG/USMSTF 3-year interval, consistent with [[acg-2021-crc-screening]].
- **Table 2's footnote defines NR as "not recommended"** where the column context is a missing specificity value. The figures are reproduced exactly as printed.
- **Open questions the update itself raises:** the true CRC incidence and mortality reduction from screening ages 45–49 is unknown (modeling only); no risk-stratification scheme has yet been shown to reduce CRC incidence or mortality; and the discriminatory value of SNP-based scores for advanced neoplasia is only mild.
