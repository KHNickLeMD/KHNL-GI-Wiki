## How NCCN Rates Its Recommendations

NCCN prints **two independent rating schemes** (document page CAT-1). They are not interchangeable and must not be collapsed into a single "strength" — an option can be *Preferred* **and** category 2A, or *Other recommended* **and** category 1.

**Categories of evidence and consensus:**

| Category | Evidence | Panel consensus |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | High-level (≥1 randomized phase 3 trial, or high-quality robust meta-analysis) | Uniform, **≥85%** panel support |
| **2A** | Lower-level | Uniform, **≥85%** panel support |
| **2B** | Lower-level | **≥50% but <85%** panel support |
| **3** | Any level | **Major NCCN disagreement** that the intervention is appropriate |

- **All recommendations are category 2A unless otherwise indicated** — so an unlabelled NCCN statement is lower-level evidence with uniform consensus, not high-level evidence.

**Categories of preference** — "all recommendations are considered appropriate":

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Preferred** | Superior efficacy, safety, and evidence; and, where appropriate, affordability |
| **Other recommended** | Somewhat less efficacious, more toxic, or based on less mature data; or significantly less affordable for similar outcomes |
| **Useful in certain circumstances** | For selected patient populations, defined with the recommendation |

- **NCCN numbers none of its recommendations.** Any numbering on a wiki page built from this document is the wiki's own, and no strength/quality pair of the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) kind exists to quote.

## Summary

The NCCN Neuroendocrine and Adrenal Tumors guideline covers the full neuroendocrine spectrum: well-differentiated grade 1/2 neuroendocrine tumors (NETs) of the gastrointestinal (GI) tract, lung, and thymus; pancreatic NETs (PanNETs, functioning and nonfunctioning); well-differentiated grade 3 NETs; neuroendocrine neoplasms of unknown primary; poorly differentiated extrapulmonary neuroendocrine carcinoma / mixed neuroendocrine–non-neuroendocrine neoplasm (MiNEN); pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma; adrenal tumors; and the multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1/type 2 (MEN1/MEN2) syndromes. The GI-relevant scope ([[gastroenteropancreatic-neuroendocrine-tumors]]) is organized by primary site — gastric (types 1/2/3), duodenal, appendiceal, jejunal/ileal/colonic, rectal, and pancreatic — each with its own evaluation, first-line treatment, and surveillance pathway.

Diagnosis rests on histology with World Health Organization (WHO) grading (Ki-67 index and mitotic count, plus differentiation), site-specific biochemical testing, and functional somatostatin-receptor (SSTR) imaging (SSTR–positron emission tomography [PET]/computed tomography [CT] or PET/magnetic resonance imaging [MRI] using ⁶⁸Ga-/⁶⁴Cu-DOTATATE or ⁶⁸Ga-DOTATOC) layered on anatomic multiphasic CT/MRI. Management is multidisciplinary and stage- and grade-dependent: endoscopic or surgical resection for localized disease; somatostatin analogs (SSAs; octreotide long-acting release [LAR], lanreotide) for symptom and tumor control in SSTR-positive metastatic disease; and an expanding set of options for progressive/advanced disease including peptide receptor radionuclide therapy (PRRT) with lutetium Lu 177 dotatate, everolimus, sunitinib (PanNET), liver-directed therapy, and cytotoxic chemotherapy. Version 1.2026 adds a management pathway for SSTR-negative disease, revised PRRT/SSA timing guidance, and updated surveillance intervals.

## Key Findings / Claims

- **Classification & grading (NE-E).** Graded by differentiation plus Ki-67 and mitotic rate per the **2019 WHO classification** (G1 <3% / <2 per 2 mm²; G2 3%–20% / 2–20; G3 >20% / >20; poorly differentiated NEC high grade by definition and not formally graded). Full table recreated on [[gastroenteropancreatic-neuroendocrine-tumors]]. Grade-assignment rules that decide: mitoses counted per **2 mm²** (= 10 high-power fields [HPF] at 40×), Ki-67 in ≥500 cells in the hot spot, and **whichever index puts the tumor in the higher grade wins** — they are not averaged. Report the exact Ki-67 percentage, never a range.
- **"High G2" is a defined cutoff, not an impression — Ki-67 >10%** (NE-C footnote). It is the split that drives the rectal NET pathway (low G2 → endoscopic/transanal; high G2 → multidisciplinary + surgical), and it exists nowhere in the WHO grade table.
- **Gastric NETs (gNETs) are subtyped by mechanism, and v1.2026 organizes them as secondary vs primary** (NE-E). *Secondary*, arising in gastric body mucosa with an identified cause: **type 1** (hypergastrinemia + background atrophy — **autoimmune *or* chronic *Helicobacter pylori*** — with enterochromaffin-like [ECL]-cell hyperplasia), **type 2** (hypergastrinemia from gastrinoma, with parietal-cell and ECL hyperplasia), and **"other"** (hypergastrinemia without gastrinoma but with parietal-cell and ECL hyperplasia → **consider proton pump inhibitor [PPI]-associated gNET**; rarely insufficient proton-pump activity from an underlying mutation). *Primary*, no cause identified and normal background mucosa: **type 3**, sporadic.
- **PPI-induced gNETs are a named fifth branch** of the NET-1 algorithm, routed to endoscopic treatment. Long-term PPI therapy may raise gNET risk, and these lesions appear to have a **much lower propensity to metastasize than sporadic type 3** tumors; the endoscopic approach is extrapolated from type 1 because direct data are limited.
- **Subtyping is a three-test protocol before any resection decision** (NE-C): serum gastrin — ideally **fasting, AM, off PPI for >1 week** (PPI use falsely elevates it; but **continue the PPI** if there are overt gastrinoma symptoms or complication risk) · **gastric pH at the time of endoscopy** · **random mapping biopsies** to exclude atrophy, *H. pylori*, and intestinal metaplasia, minimum **two antral (lesser + greater curvature), one incisura angularis, two gastric body (lesser + greater curvature)**. Subtype not apparent → multidisciplinary discussion.
- **Site- and size-based local therapy (NE-C, new in v1.2026).** The thresholds are site-specific and conditioned on grade, depth, and lymphovascular invasion (LVI) — not size alone: gastric type 1 banded at **≤1 cm / >1–≤2 cm / >2 cm**; gastric type 3 at **≤1.5 cm, G1**; non-periampullary duodenal at **≤1 cm** with technique by size (**<0.5 cm** polypectomy/endoscopic mucosal resection [EMR]; **0.5–1 cm** EMR, endoscopic submucosal dissection [ESD], or endoscopic full-thickness resection [eFTR]); rectal at **≤1 cm / >1–<2 cm / ≥2 cm**. Across sites the depth rule is the same — **EMR for mucosal lesions, ESD or eFTR once the submucosa is involved, muscle layer (T2) or concerning nodes → surgery.** Appendiceal tumors >2 cm (or incompletely resected / node-positive) warrant right hemicolectomy.
- **Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) has an explicit trigger list, not a judgment call.** For gNETs: size per subtype, **or** ulceration, **or** grade 3, **or** LVI on initial sampling. For duodenal NETs (dNETs): periampullary involvement, **size >1 cm**, extension beyond the submucosa, functional tumor, ulceration, grade 3, or LVI. Any single trigger suffices. All gastric, duodenal, and rectal NETs first get high-definition white-light endoscopy with video chromoendoscopy and biopsy for grade and Ki-67.
- **Post-resection surveillance (NET-8)** — **12 weeks to 12 months**, then **every 12–24 months out to 10 years**, then as clinically indicated. ⚠ **SSTR-based imaging and fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG)-PET/CT are explicitly *not* recommended for routine surveillance.** In type 1 gNETs, following gastrin and chromogranin A after baseline **is not recommended** (gastrin stays high by mechanism).
- **Functional imaging.** SSTR-PET (DOTATATE/DOTATOC) is central to staging, detecting occult disease, and selecting patients for SSA therapy and PRRT; a dedicated SSTR-negative pathway addresses tumors that do not express the receptor.
- **Systemic therapy for advanced disease.** Somatostatin analogs first-line for SSTR-positive disease (symptom + antiproliferative control); then PRRT with lutetium Lu 177 dotatate, everolimus (GI and pancreatic NET), sunitinib (PanNET), and chemotherapy depending on grade, site, and burden. NETTER-2 supports first-line ¹⁷⁷Lu-DOTATATE in advanced grade 2–3 gastroenteropancreatic NETs (GEP-NETs).
- **Carcinoid syndrome.** Driven by serotonin-secreting (usually metastatic midgut) tumors; managed with SSAs and telotristat for refractory diarrhea, with octreotide available intraoperatively for carcinoid crisis.
- **Hereditary context.** MEN1 should be considered with multifocal duodenopancreatic NETs, gastrinoma, and primary hyperparathyroidism; genetic counseling and syndrome-specific surveillance are recommended.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- ⚠ **The document gives two different size thresholds for the same decision — endoscopic resection of a type 3 gNET.** The Principles of Endoscopic Treatment (NE-C) allow it for lesions **≤1.5 cm, G1, not involving the muscle layer, with no concerning lymph nodes**; the gastric algorithm's own footnote (NET-1A) states endoscopic resection "should be reserved for small (**<1 cm**), superficial, low-grade tumors." Same version, same document, a 0.5 cm disagreement in the band where type 3 lesions actually present. The entity page carries **both** and resolves neither, because the source does not.
- Algorithm-based guideline (category 2A consensus), not a GRADE systematic review — strength-of-evidence labels differ from those of American College of Gastroenterology (ACG)/American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) society guidelines.
- **The Ki-67 55% split for well-differentiated G3 disease is flagged data-limited by NCCN itself** — the appropriate cutoff is uncertain, and Ki-67 is heterogeneous within a tumor and drifts across serial biopsies, so the pathologic index alone must not decide the pathway.
- **Endoscopic surveillance intervals after resection are repeatedly described as "not well established"** — for duodenal R0 (margin-negative) resection, for type 3 gNETs, and for rectal lesions — with the stated intervals offered as "consider," not as evidence-based schedules.
- Optimal timing of SSTR-PET after SSA dosing, and of SSA dosing around PRRT cycles, remains uncertain; v1.2026 relaxes the prior strict 4-week SSA washout before PRRT.
