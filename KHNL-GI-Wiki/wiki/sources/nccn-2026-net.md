## Summary

The NCCN Neuroendocrine and Adrenal Tumors guideline covers the full neuroendocrine spectrum: well-differentiated grade 1/2 neuroendocrine tumors (NETs) of the GI tract, lung, and thymus; pancreatic NETs (PanNETs, functioning and nonfunctioning); well-differentiated grade 3 NETs; neuroendocrine neoplasms of unknown primary; poorly differentiated extrapulmonary neuroendocrine carcinoma / MiNEN; pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma; adrenal tumors; and the MEN1/MEN2 syndromes. The GI-relevant scope ([[gastroenteropancreatic-neuroendocrine-tumors]]) is organized by primary site — gastric (types 1/2/3), duodenal, appendiceal, jejunal/ileal/colonic, rectal, and pancreatic — each with its own evaluation, first-line treatment, and surveillance pathway.

Diagnosis rests on histology with WHO grading (Ki-67 index and mitotic count, plus differentiation), site-specific biochemical testing, and functional somatostatin-receptor (SSTR) imaging (SSTR-PET/CT or PET/MRI using ⁶⁸Ga-/⁶⁴Cu-DOTATATE or ⁶⁸Ga-DOTATOC) layered on anatomic multiphasic CT/MRI. Management is multidisciplinary and stage- and grade-dependent: endoscopic or surgical resection for localized disease; somatostatin analogs (octreotide LAR, lanreotide) for symptom and tumor control in SSTR-positive metastatic disease; and an expanding set of options for progressive/advanced disease including peptide receptor radionuclide therapy (PRRT) with lutetium Lu 177 dotatate, everolimus, sunitinib (PanNET), liver-directed therapy, and cytotoxic chemotherapy. Version 1.2026 adds a management pathway for SSTR-negative disease, revised PRRT/SSA timing guidance, and updated surveillance intervals.

## Key Findings / Claims

- **Classification & grading.** NETs are graded by differentiation plus Ki-67 and mitotic rate (WHO): well-differentiated G1, G2, and G3, versus poorly differentiated neuroendocrine carcinoma (NEC). Grade drives prognosis and therapy selection; G3 NET and NEC are managed on separate pathways from G1/G2.
- **Gastric NETs are subtyped by mechanism.** Type 1 (atrophic gastritis / hypergastrinemia, indolent), type 2 (gastrinoma/Zollinger-Ellison, MEN1-associated), and type 3 (normal gastrin, sporadic, more aggressive). Subtype and size dictate endoscopic resection/surveillance vs. surgical resection.
- **Site- and size-based local therapy.** Small, low-grade luminal tumors (e.g., gastric type 1, small rectal/duodenal lesions) are amenable to endoscopic resection; larger or higher-risk lesions and appendiceal tumors >2 cm (or incompletely resected / node-positive) warrant formal surgical resection.
- **Functional imaging.** SSTR-PET (DOTATATE/DOTATOC) is central to staging, detecting occult disease, and selecting patients for SSA therapy and PRRT; a dedicated SSTR-negative pathway addresses tumors that do not express the receptor.
- **Systemic therapy for advanced disease.** Somatostatin analogs first-line for SSTR-positive disease (symptom + antiproliferative control); then PRRT with lutetium Lu 177 dotatate, everolimus (GI and pancreatic NET), sunitinib (PanNET), and chemotherapy depending on grade, site, and burden. NETTER-2 supports first-line ¹⁷⁷Lu-DOTATATE in advanced grade 2–3 GEP-NETs.
- **Carcinoid syndrome.** Driven by serotonin-secreting (usually metastatic midgut) tumors; managed with SSAs and telotristat for refractory diarrhea, with octreotide available intraoperatively for carcinoid crisis.
- **Hereditary context.** MEN1 should be considered with multifocal duodenopancreatic NETs, gastrinoma, and primary hyperparathyroidism; genetic counseling and syndrome-specific surveillance are recommended.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Algorithm-based guideline (category 2A consensus), not a GRADE systematic review — strength-of-evidence labels differ from ACG/AGA society guidelines elsewhere in the wiki.
- Optimal timing of SSTR-PET after SSA dosing, and of SSA dosing around PRRT cycles, remains uncertain; v1.2026 relaxes the prior strict 4-week SSA washout before PRRT.
