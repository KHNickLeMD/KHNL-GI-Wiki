## Summary

Official AGA recommendations on therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) in IBD, developed with GRADE and accompanied by a Technical Review (Vande Casteele et al., *Gastroenterology* 2017;153:835–857) that compiles the underlying evidence. Scope is limited to **anti-TNF agents and thiopurines**; because of a paucity of data at the time of publication the document **does not address TDM for vedolizumab or ustekinumab**.

The framing is that drug failure has **three possible causes**, and the trough concentration plus antidrug-antibody result assigns which one is operating: **mechanistic failure** (not responding despite optimal drug trough — disease driven by inflammatory mediators not blocked by the drug, so unlikely to respond to another drug in the same class); **non–immune-mediated pharmacokinetic failure** (subtherapeutic trough, no antidrug antibodies — rapid clearance, often with a high inflammatory burden); and **immune-mediated pharmacokinetic failure** (low or undetectable trough with high-titer neutralizing antidrug antibodies).

For **anti-TNF therapy**, the panel conditionally suggests **reactive** TDM in active IBD to guide treatment changes (very low quality), and makes **no recommendation** on **routine proactive** TDM in quiescent IBD — an explicit knowledge gap. For **thiopurines**, it suggests routine **TPMT** testing (enzymatic activity or genotype) before starting, **reactive** thiopurine metabolite monitoring in active disease or suspected thiopurine toxicity, and **against** routine metabolite monitoring in quiescent disease.

The panel also supplies practical operating detail: measure the trough **as close to the next dose as possible (within 24 hours)**; use the **same assay** for repeated trough and antibody measurements in a given patient, because antidrug-antibody reporting varies between commercial assays, there is no standardized reporting, and uniform thresholds for clinically relevant antibody titers are lacking. Optimal target troughs during **induction** are unknown — applying maintenance thresholds to induction may erroneously misclassify patients as mechanistic failures.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Recommendations (5 total).** The document **does not number its recommendations**; they are presented as boxed statements in the text and collected in Table 3. Strength and certainty are reproduced exactly as printed.

| # | Statement (verbatim) | Strength of recommendation | Quality of evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "In adults with active IBD treated with anti-TNF agents, the AGA suggests reactive therapeutic drug monitoring to guide treatment changes." | Conditional recommendation | Very low quality |
| 2 | "In adult patients with quiescent IBD treated with anti-TNF agents, the AGA makes no recommendation regarding the use of routine proactive therapeutic drug monitoring." | No recommendation | Knowledge gap |
| 3 | "In adult patients with IBD being started on thiopurines, the AGA suggests routine TPMT testing (enzymatic activity or genotype) to guide thiopurine dosing." | Conditional recommendation | Low quality |
| 4 | "In adult patients treated with thiopurines with active IBD or adverse effects thought to be due to thiopurine toxicity, the AGA suggests reactive thiopurine metabolite monitoring to guide treatment changes." | Conditional recommendation | Very low quality |
| 5 | "In adult patients with quiescent IBD treated with thiopurines, the AGA suggests against routine thiopurine metabolite monitoring." | Conditional recommendation | Very low quality |

**Comments printed with the recommendations (verbatim):**
- *Rec 1:* "Table 4 summarizes suggested trough concentration for anti-TNF therapy, for patients with active IBD on maintenance therapy. Of note, there may be a small subset of patients who may still respond by targeting higher target concentrations. Optimal trough concentrations for induction therapy are uncertain."
- *Rec 3:* "Routine laboratory monitoring, including complete blood count (CBC), should be performed, regardless of TPMT testing results."
- *Rec 4:* "When measuring thiopurine metabolite monitoring in patients with active IBD-related symptoms, we suggest a target 6-thioguanine (6-TGN) cutoff between 230 and 450 pmol/8 × 10⁸ red blood cells (RBCs) when used as monotherapy; optimal 6-TGN cutoff when thiopurines are used in combination with anti-TNF agents is uncertain."

**Table 4 — suggested target trough concentrations, reactive TDM, active IBD on anti-TNF maintenance therapy:**

| Drug | Suggested trough concentration (µg/mL) | Supporting data as printed |
|---|---|---|
| Infliximab | **≥5** | 6 studies (929 patients). Proportion not in remission fell from 25% at a threshold of ≥1 µg/mL, to 15% at ≥3 µg/mL, to ~4% at ≥7 or ≥10 µg/mL |
| Adalimumab | **≥7.5** | 4 studies. Proportion not in remission fell from 17% at ≥5.0 ± 1 µg/mL to 10% at ≥7.5 ± 1 µg/mL. Limited data on comparability of troughs across assays; unclear what proportion on 40 mg every other week or escalated 40 mg weekly achieve these |
| Certolizumab pegol | **≥20** | 1 exposure–response pooled analysis of 9 trials. Proportion not in remission fell from 42% at ≥10 µg/mL to 26% at ≥20 µg/mL |
| Golimumab | **Unknown** | "There is a lack of sufficient evidence available to establish a target trough goal" |

Table 4 footnote: the studies deriving these targets were **cross-sectional** studies of maintenance-therapy patients in various stages of remission/response, designed to identify what proportion of patients were in remission above and below specific thresholds — **not** designed to evaluate patients with secondary loss of response.

**Evidence behind reactive anti-TNF TDM (Rec 1):**
- 1 RCT (69 patients on infliximab maintenance with active CD symptoms): TDM-guided treatment change vs empiric dose escalation — **no significant difference in remission at 12 weeks (RR 0.78; 95% CI 0.40–1.51)**. Major limitation: the RCT called an infliximab trough **≥0.5 µg/mL** optimal, so 76% were classified as mechanistic failures and switched off anti-TNF — considerably lower than the ≥5 µg/mL supported by current evidence.
- 3 observational studies pooled (464 patients): **30% (139/464) mechanistic failure** (adequate trough, using higher target concentrations of 2.0–3.8 µg/mL for infliximab and 4.5–4.9 µg/mL for adalimumab); **19% (90/464) immune-mediated pharmacokinetic failure**; **51% (235/464) non–immune-mediated pharmacokinetic failure** (subtherapeutic trough, no antidrug antibodies) vs only 4% in the RCT.
- Pooling 2 studies: **45% responded to empiric dose escalation**. Retrospectively applying TDM, **82% with a subtherapeutic trough and no antidrug antibodies responded (RR 1.71; 95% CI 1.39–2.11)**, vs only **8% with a low or undetectable trough in the presence of antidrug antibodies (RR 0.26; 95% CI 0.08–0.86)**.

**Evidence behind no recommendation on proactive anti-TNF TDM (Rec 2):**
- No RCTs and no comparative observational studies compared a priori proactive TDM for achieving remission; only indirect evidence was available.
- **TAXIT** (Vande Casteele et al.): all patients were first dose-optimized to an infliximab trough of **3–7 µg/mL**, then randomized to proactive TDM vs no TDM. Uniform dose optimization raised clinical remission from **65% pre-optimization to 88% post-optimization**. After optimization, remission at 1 year did **not** differ with routine proactive TDM (**RR 1.04; 95% CI 0.88–1.24**). At 1 year, patients not receiving proactive TDM had higher rates of antidrug antibodies and undetectable infliximab troughs.
- Vaughn et al. (single-center retrospective): proactive TDM before each infliximab infusion was associated with less infliximab discontinuation and fewer disease flares or infusion reactions — very low quality, significant selection bias.
- Post hoc analyses of induction trials show an exposure–response relationship: **higher troughs between weeks 4 and 14** were more likely to achieve remission.
- Potential harms of routine proactive TDM: therapeutic dilemmas and **premature switching in patients who are in remission**, driven by unclear target troughs in asymptomatic patients and unclear significance of low-titer antidrug antibodies; cost; unclear required testing frequency.

**Evidence behind TPMT testing (Rec 3):**
- 3 RCTs comparing TPMT testing with empiric weight-based dosing. Hematologic adverse events **RR 0.94 (95% CI 0.59–1.50)**; treatment discontinuation **RR 1.09 (95% CI 0.94–1.27)**; clinical remission **RR 1.03 (95% CI 0.84–1.27)** — no significant difference at a population level.
- Of 1145 patients in these studies, only **0.17% (n = 2) were homozygous**.
- In patients with **intermediate enzymatic activity/heterozygous genotype or homozygous genotype/low enzymatic activity**, TPMT testing to guide dosing was associated with an **89% risk reduction of hematologic adverse events**.
- Considerable risk of harm in the **0.3%** who are homozygous or have low/absent TPMT enzymatic activity; mitigated by routine CBC checking, but adherence to regular monitoring in clinical practice is suboptimal — hence continue CBC and liver enzyme monitoring regardless of TPMT result.
- Dosing used in the studies: normal enzyme/genotype started full-dose thiopurine; intermediate enzymatic activity/heterozygous genotype had a **50% dose reduction**; low/absent enzyme activity or homozygous genotype were not given the drug or were given **0–10% of the initiation dose**.

**Evidence behind reactive thiopurine metabolite monitoring (Rec 4):**
- No RCTs. One retrospective observational study (60 patients with active IBD on thiopurines): care **concordant with a TDM algorithm** was significantly more likely to respond to a therapeutic change than discordant care — **RR 5.15 (95% CI 1.82–14.56)**. That algorithm optimized the thiopurine dose if 6-TGN was low (**<230 pmol/8 × 10⁸ RBCs**) and switched medication if 6-TGN was adequate.
- **6-TGN ≥230 pmol/8 × 10⁸ RBCs** was associated with **40% higher rates of remission (RR 1.4; 95% CI 1.2–1.6)** vs levels <230.
- Whether the 230–450 target applies in **combination with an anti-TNF** is unclear — in combination therapy one reason for the thiopurine is to reduce immunogenicity rather than independently target remission; lower targets have been suggested but current evidence fails to identify a threshold.
- Harms: burden of intensified laboratory monitoring with each dose adjustment, and potential for delaying alternative effective therapy in non-responders.

**Evidence behind recommending against routine metabolite monitoring in quiescent disease (Rec 5):**
- 2 RCTs (107 patients on azathioprine) of routine metabolite monitoring to achieve a 6-TGN of **250–400 pmol/8 × 10⁸ RBCs** vs standard weight-based dosing by TPMT: clinical remission **RR 1.44 (95% CI 0.59–3.52)**; serious adverse events **RR 1.20 (95% CI 0.50–2.91)**.
- Neither study met its recruitment target (high risk of bias); serious inconsistency (I² > 50%) and imprecision (wide CIs).
- These studies did not include patients on combination therapy with an anti-TNF, permitted only thiopurine dose escalation in patients with 6-TGN <230 pmol/8 × 10⁸ RBCs, and did not permit alternative strategies such as adding allopurinol — so they cannot be extrapolated to thiopurines used with an anti-TNF.

**Assay and timing detail:**
- Draw the trough **as close to the next dose as possible (i.e., within 24 hours)**.
- Quantitative infliximab concentrations from different assays were within **−7% to +20%** of each other; comparison of an ELISA and a homogeneous mobility shift assay for adalimumab showed considerable inter-assay variability.
- **Use the same assay** for repeated trough and antidrug-antibody measurements in a patient.
- Antidrug-antibody reporting is variable between commercial assays with no standardized reporting, and **uniform thresholds for clinically relevant titers are lacking**; current data do not identify optimal high- vs low-titer antibody cutoffs in commercially available assays.
- Low-titer antibodies may be transient and non-neutralizing; high-titer antibodies, especially with undetectable trough concentrations, are generally persistent and neutralizing.

**Reactive vs proactive — definitions used in this guideline:**
- **Reactive TDM** = TDM performed in patients who have **active IBD**, defined as active IBD-related symptoms confirmed by objective findings (biochemical markers, endoscopic or radiologic findings of active inflammation), **or** in patients who are clinically asymptomatic but have objective inflammation on endoscopy or radiology.
- **Proactive TDM** = TDM performed in a routine fashion while the patient is in remission.

**Anti-TNF reactive-TDM algorithm (as described by the panel):**
- Assess **trough concentration first**.
- **Trough at or above target** → may consider switching to a different drug class; escalating index therapy may be a reasonable alternative, especially in asymptomatic patients with ongoing endoscopic activity or in perianal disease, where target troughs may be higher. **In the presence of sufficient trough concentrations, antibody results should not guide treatment decisions.**
- **Trough low (below suggested threshold, active IBD) and no antidrug antibodies** → optimize the index drug: shorten the dosing interval and/or increase the dose, and/or add an immunomodulator.
- **No detectable drug (zero trough) with high-titer antidrug antibodies** → consider switching to a different drug within the class or to a different drug class.
- **No detectable drug with low-titer antibodies** → can consider optimizing the index drug (shorten interval and/or increase dose, and/or add an immunomodulator). Typically drug optimization is attempted before changing within class or switching class, although some might opt to change or switch first.

**Acknowledged knowledge gaps:** whether TDM should be performed during **induction** (vs empiric dose escalation) and what the target troughs would be; whether target troughs differ by agent, disease phenotype, disease state, and treatment target (clinical remission vs mucosal healing); how to define clinically meaningful vs insignificant antidrug antibodies and at what titers they can be suppressed before a drug change is needed; RCTs comparing routine proactive TDM vs reactive TDM vs empiric dosing on patient-relevant outcomes, including frequency and timing of proactive TDM; and evaluation of TDM for newer biologics as they are approved.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **Thiopurine metabolite targets.** [[thiopurines]] states that IBD guidelines do not give 6-TGN target ranges, on the basis of [[acg-2025-crohns]] and [[acg-2025-uc]], which discuss TDM only for anti-TNF troughs. This guideline **does** print a 6-TGN target (230–450 pmol/8 × 10⁸ RBCs as monotherapy) and a routine TPMT recommendation. It gives **no 6-MMP target** and no allopurinol co-prescribing rule.
- **Proactive TDM.** This guideline makes **no recommendation** (knowledge gap) on routine proactive anti-TNF TDM. The later [[acg-2025-crohns]] carries a meta-analysis of proactive-TDM RCTs that did not identify a clinical benefit. The two are directionally compatible; the newer guideline is the current position.
- **Trough thresholds.** This guideline prints **≥5 / ≥7.5 / ≥20 µg/mL** for infliximab / adalimumab / certolizumab pegol; [[acg-2025-crohns]] prints the same numbers as **>5 / >7.5 / >20 µg/mL**.
- **Newer agents.** Vedolizumab and ustekinumab are explicitly outside this guideline's scope. [[acg-2025-uc]] separately concludes there is insufficient evidence to measure vedolizumab serum concentrations.
- Open within the source itself: induction-phase targets, high- vs low-titer antibody cutoffs, whether targets differ for ulcerative colitis vs Crohn's disease, a golimumab target trough, and the 6-TGN target during combination therapy.
