## Summary

The fourth revision of the International Association of Pancreatology (IAP) consensus guidelines for intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm (IPMN) of the pancreas, succeeding the 2006, 2012 and 2017 ("Fukuoka") versions. The revision meeting was held in Kyoto in July 2022 during the 26th IAP meeting. What makes this version "evidence-based" rather than consensus-only is that five working groups each performed a **systematic review**, published separately, on the five topics the revision targeted: revision of high-risk stigmata (HRS) and worrisome features (WF), surveillance of non-resected IPMN, surveillance after resection, pathological aspects, and molecular markers in cyst fluid. Twenty-four clinical questions are answered with evidence levels and grades of recommendation assigned by the **Scottish Intercollegiate Guidelines Network (SIGN) 2019** system.

**Two changes matter most clinically.** First, **endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) findings and EUS-guided fine-needle aspiration (FNA) cytology are now part of the HRS/WF assessment** — suspicious or positive cytology is itself an HRS. The working group does not recommend EUS in every case and acknowledges that its quality is operator- and resource-dependent. Second, the guideline **offers an exit from lifelong surveillance**: for a branch-duct IPMN (BD-IPMN) under 20 mm that has not changed and has no worrisome features over 5 years, the two options are "stop surveillance" or "continue surveillance for possible development of concomitant pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC)". The surveillance protocol for non-resected disease is simplified and stratified into three cyst-size bands.

The HRS and WF criteria themselves are **unchanged from 2017** apart from two additions to WF (new onset or acute exacerbation of diabetes within the past year; cystic growth rate ≥2.5 mm/year, replacing the previous ≥5 mm/2 years). The panel explicitly considered and **rejected** two proposed revisions — raising the enhancing-mural-nodule HRS threshold to ≥10 mm and making any main pancreatic duct (MPD) dilation ≥5 mm a WF — for lack of supporting evidence.

Other content: IPMN classification on imaging (branch-duct, main-duct, mixed); morphological subtypes (gastric, intestinal, pancreatobiliary) and their prognostic meaning; separation of intraductal oncocytic papillary neoplasm (IOPN) as a distinct entity; terminology rulings ("carcinoma in situ" is an acceptable synonym for high-grade dysplasia, "malignant IPMN" is discouraged); frozen-section and margin management; operative principles and post-resection surveillance; multifocal disease; concomitant PDAC; family history; and cyst-fluid molecular markers.

## Key Findings / Claims

### High-risk stigmata and worrisome features (unchanged thresholds, EUS cytology added)

**High-risk stigmata (HRS)** — the four:
1. **Obstructive jaundice** in a patient with a cystic lesion of the **head** of the pancreas
2. **Enhancing mural nodule ≥5 mm**, or a **solid component**
3. **Main pancreatic duct ≥10 mm**
4. **Suspicious or positive cytology** (if performed — an option, not required)

**Worrisome features (WF)** — the ten:

| | Clinical | Imaging |
|---|---|---|
| | i. Acute pancreatitis | iv. Cyst **≥30 mm** |
| | ii. Increased serum CA19-9 | v. Enhancing mural nodule **<5 mm** |
| | iii. New onset or acute exacerbation of **diabetes within the past year** | vi. Thickened/enhancing cyst walls |
| | | vii. MPD **≥5 mm and <10 mm** |
| | | viii. Abrupt change in pancreatic duct caliber **with distal pancreatic atrophy** |
| | | ix. Lymphadenopathy |
| | | x. Cystic growth rate **≥2.5 mm/year** |

- **Mural nodule vs solid component** cannot be reliably separated on preoperative imaging; both are treated as high-risk findings (CQ1-1; evidence 2+, grade C). A mural nodule protrudes from the cyst wall and usually indicates a non-invasive lesion; a solid component is a mass in the parenchyma and suggests invasive carcinoma (IC) or concomitant PDAC.
- **How to measure a mural nodule:** by **height** on EUS, not width or maximal diameter; on multi-detector computed tomography (MDCT)/magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) it is measured as maximal diameter.
- Enhancing mural nodule ≥5 mm has sensitivity 73–100% and specificity 73–85% for high-grade dysplasia (HGD)/IC, but **nodule presence alone** gives an odds ratio of only 1.19–3.16 in recent nomogram studies. One EUS analysis found a **10 mm** nodule-height cut-off more predictive (odds ratio 7.90, 67% probability) than a 5–10 mm nodule (odds ratio 2.93, <19% probability). **The panel kept 5 mm** (CQ1-2; 2++, C).
- **MPD ≥10 mm alone** has only modest predictive power (odds ratio 1.06–1.76); kept as HRS for want of better evidence (CQ1-3; 2+, C).
- **Cyst size:** mean diameter of malignant IPMN is 30–40 mm; ≥30 mm is a WF (weak recommendation). Growth rate ≥2.5 mm/year is **recommended** as a WF — reported predictive rates range 0.96–3.5 mm/year, with 2.5 mm/year most frequently reported (CQ1-4; 2+, C).
- **CA19-9 >37 U/L** for IPMN with IC: sensitivity 41–74%, specificity 85–96%. New-onset diabetes occurs in **25%** of IPMN patients and raises the risk of HGD (risk ratio 1.27) and IC (risk ratio 1.61); "new onset" and "acute exacerbation" are not formally defined.
- **Thickened/enhancing cyst wall is an ambiguous factor** — no established measurement method or cut-off. One report gives an odds ratio of 3.51 for HGD/IC when septal thickness is ≥2.5 mm by EUS, comparable to the odds ratio of 3.36 for a ≥5 mm mural nodule.
- **Multiple WF are additive** (CQ1-5; 2−, C): risk of HGD/IC rises stepwise with the count — **22% with 1, 34% with 2, 59% with 3, and 100% with ≥4 WF**. Where multiple HRS/WF are positive, the panel weakly recommends using a **nomogram** to weigh the operative indication (reported area under the curve 0.739–0.955; CQ2-4; 2+, C). Nomogram limitations: several do not distinguish BD- from MD-IPMN, small resected-IPMN numbers in some regions, and lack of external validation.
- **"Increase in size of MPD" was considered as a WF and not included** — insufficient evidence on this dynamic measure.
- Cytology graded "suspicious" or "positive" by World Health Organization definition carries an absolute HGD/IC risk of **91–100%** and **100%** respectively, which is why cytology joined the HRS list (CQ4-8; 2+, C).
- **The guideline deliberately keeps the terms "HRS" and "WF" rather than "absolute" and "relative" indication for surgery** — operative indication rests on the degree of suspicion *plus* the patient's general condition, comorbidity, life expectancy and preference.

### Classification and risk by type

- **BD-IPMN:** a pancreatic cyst **>5 mm** in diameter communicating with the MPD (rule out pseudocyst if there is a history of acute pancreatitis or trauma). **MD-IPMN:** segmental or diffuse MPD dilation **>5 mm** without another cause of obstruction. **Mixed:** meets both.
- **HGD/IC in resected specimens: BD-IPMN 31% (15–48%), MD-IPMN 62% (36–100%).** IC alone: BD-IPMN **19% (6–38%)**, MD-IPMN **43% (11–81%)**. Because most BD-IPMNs are never resected, the true BD-IPMN rate is much lower — MD-IPMN carries a markedly higher risk.
- **Asymptomatic cysts ≤5 mm:** management remains controversial; for the time being surveillance is still recommended.
- **Mixed IPMN categories left unchanged** despite acknowledged imaging–pathology discordance in both directions.
- **Incidence:** pancreatic cysts on 1.2–2.6% of CT and 2.4–49.1% of MRI; population-based 2.6% (Germany), 2.2% (Korea), rising with age (0% in the 20s, 0.2% in the 30s, 6.6% in the 70s on ultrasound screening). **~80% of incidentally discovered cysts are BD-IPMN.**

### Surveillance of non-resected IPMN

- **Progression risk depends on size at diagnosis** (CQ2-1; 2++, grade **B**): BD-IPMN **<10 mm** progresses to a WF in **4.8%** at a median 54 months; **10–20 mm**, **10%** at 55 months; **20–30 mm**, **48.8%** at a median 23 months.
- **Interval by largest cyst size** (CQ2-2; 2+, grade **B**), after an initial short-term 6-month follow-up:

| Cyst size | Protocol |
|---|---|
| **<20 mm** | 6 months **once**, then every **18 months** if stable |
| **≥20 and <30 mm** | 6 months **twice**, then every **12 months** if stable |
| **≥30 mm** | **Every 6 months** |

- **Stopping (CQ2-3; 2+, C):** surveillance **may be discontinued** for cysts **<20 mm** with **no morphological change and no WF after 5 years**, considering the patient's condition and life expectancy. **Also discontinue** for patients unfit for surgery or with a life expectancy **<10 years**. The guideline explicitly presents **two options** — "stop" or "continue for possible concomitant PDAC" — and states the recommendation to discontinue **may not apply to younger patients or those with familial or genetic risk**, where pancreatic-cancer risk is cumulative.
- **Modality:** MRI plus physical examination, tumor marker and assessment for new-onset diabetes are preferred; MDCT and EUS when the MRI changes.
- **Cumulative transformation of indolent BD-IPMN to HGD/IC:** 0.94–3.3% at 5 years, 2.3–6.6% at 10 years, 7.6–15.0% at 15 years.

### Investigations

- **Primary cross-sectional modalities:** contrast-enhanced MRI/magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP) and contrast-enhanced MDCT. **MDCT, MRI and EUS are equivalent for diagnosing IPMN with HGD/IC.** Accuracy for diagnosing a pancreatic cystic lesion is **47–78%**, and for predicting HGD/IC **73–97%**.
- **EUS, EUS-FNA and contrast-enhanced EUS are recommended where HGD/IC is suspected and the clinical setting allows** (CQ1-6; 2++, C).
- **EUS-FNA cytology sensitivity is low — 28.7% overall (4.8–61.5%)** — but a positive result strongly influences management. **Do not perform EUS-FNA when HRS are obvious on MDCT/MRI and surgery will follow regardless.** Needle-tract seeding in pancreatic cystic lesions is reported at **0.3%**; peritoneal seeding after preoperative EUS-FNA in surgical IPMN series was **2.3% vs 4.4%** without FNA (p = 0.403), and every seeded case was HGD/IC on pathology.
- **Routine ERCP for pancreatic-juice cytology is not recommended** — sensitivity for HGD/IC only 10–50%, plus post-ERCP pancreatitis risk. ERCP's role is limited to **biliary drainage in the jaundiced patient**. Pancreatoscopy can help define the resection line in MD- and mixed IPMN but risks overdiagnosis (a low-papillary MPD lesion can be low-grade dysplasia).
- **FDG-PET/CT:** little information; sensitivity 62–82%, specificity 71–100% at maximum standardized uptake values of 1.3–3.0.
- **Blood work:** blood counts, transaminases, bilirubin, biliary and pancreatic enzymes, plus **CEA, CA19-9 and HbA1c**.

### Cyst fluid markers

- **To identify a mucinous cyst** (CQ5-1; evidence **1+**, grade **B**): **KRAS and/or GNAS mutation — sensitivity 79%, specificity 98%**; **CEA >192 ng/mL — 58% / 87%**; **glucose <50 ng/dL — 93% / 89%**. **GNAS** mutation (codon 201) is **exclusive to IPMN** and separates it from mucinous cystic neoplasm (MCN). **VHL** mutation without KRAS or GNAS gives **>99% sensitivity for a serous cystic neoplasm**.
- **To identify HGD/IC** (CQ5-2; 1+, B): **TP53, SMAD4, CDKN2A and PIK3CA** mutations — **low sensitivity (9–39%) but high specificity (92–98%)**.
- **Cyst-fluid CEA is of limited use:** up to 30% of IPMNs have low levels, it does not separate IPMN from MCN, and its concentration does not correlate with grade of dysplasia.
- Molecular markers are to be used **when the cyst diagnosis is unclear and the result will alter surveillance** — the same gate as EUS-FNA itself.

### Pathology

- **Grades:** low-grade dysplasia (LGD), HGD, IC. **"Carcinoma in situ" is an acceptable synonym for HGD; "malignant IPMN" is discouraged** for lack of clarity (CQ4-1, ungraded). HGD and IC components should be reported **separately**.
- **Three morphological subtypes** (CQ4-2; 2+, C): **gastric** (most frequent, mostly LGD BD-IPMN, best prognosis), **intestinal** (intermediate; mostly colloid carcinoma, prognostically more favourable than ordinary PDAC), **pancreatobiliary** (highest risk of neoplastic progression; tubular ductal adenocarcinoma). Where several subtypes coexist, assign the subtype of the **highest grade of dysplasia**. **Low-grade gastric IPMNs are ancestors of co-occurring high-grade components and cannot be dismissed as no-risk.**
- **IOPN is a distinct entity and should be separated from IPMN** (CQ4-3; 2+, C) — recurrent *PRKACA*/*PRKACB* fusion genes.
- **Mutations:** KRAS 60–70%, GNAS 50–70%, RNF43 15%. KRAS is a founder mutation; TP53, CDKN2A and SMAD4 are progressor mutations. **KRAS and GNAS are not reliably associated with prognosis of resected IPMN** (CQ4-4; 2+, C); genetic studies underscore multifocality and polyclonality, suggesting IPMN is a disease of the whole pancreas.
- **Mimickers to exclude pathologically:** MCN (ovarian-type stroma), intraductal tubulopapillary neoplasm (no mucin secretion, no MUC5AC), retention cyst (flat columnar lining, upstream obstruction), large-duct-type PDAC (invasive dilated ducts without ductal contour).
- **Frozen section at the margin** (CQ4-6; 2+, C): **LGD at the margin does not justify completion pancreatectomy**; HGD or IC at the margin warrants clinical consideration of additional resection; **a denuded duct is not a negative margin** and further resection should be considered. HGD and IC are often grossly indistinguishable from LGD, so a representative frozen section cannot exclude them.
- **T staging** (CQ4-7, ungraded): measure and report the **size of the invasive component separately** from the IPMN, microscopically in cm. Verona recommended sub-staging T1 as 1a ≤0.5 cm, 1b >0.5 and ≤1 cm, 1c >1 cm, and discouraged "minimally invasive"; few reports have followed this.

### Resection

- **BD-IPMN** is usually removed by **partial pancreatectomy**. **Radical pancreatectomy with lymph node dissection** where IC is suspected; **organ-preserving pancreatectomy without lymphadenectomy** (middle pancreatectomy, spleen-preserving distal pancreatectomy) where suspicion of IC is low. Laparoscopic and robotic approaches are available.
- **Intraoperative frozen section is recommended** to rule out unexpected MPD involvement at the cut margin even after macroscopically complete resection.
- For **MD- and mixed IPMN**, extension along the MPD is the concern and **the goal is a negative surgical margin**.
- **Prophylactic total pancreatectomy is not recommended**, but the possibility of total pancreatectomy must be disclosed to every patient preoperatively; the indication also depends on economic and social factors, including insulin availability.
- **Leaving HGD at the margin may be appropriate** where invasive cancer is already present in the resected specimen (prognosis is set by the invasive component), particularly in older or frail patients. Colloid-type HGD/IC at the margin progresses much more slowly and is far more curable than tubular-type PDAC at the margin.
- **Four things to tell every patient at preoperative counselling:** (1) surgical choices are usually made in the operating room without a final diagnosis; (2) resection might extend to total pancreatectomy if margins show HGD/IC; (3) LGD at the cut margin or a small indolent BD-IPMN might be left behind; (4) long-term surveillance is needed even after partial pancreatectomy for LGD with a negative margin, because of multifocality and skip progression.
- **Intraoperative pancreatoscopy** may help define the resection line but has limited accuracy for HGD/IC and risks peritoneal dissemination; if needed, do it **preoperatively**.

### Surveillance after resection of non-invasive IPMN

- **Purpose** (CQ3-1; 2+, C): patients have an increased risk of clinically significant remnant pancreatic lesions — **median 5-year risk 10% (range 0–21%)**; identify them in time for curative intervention before IC. After partial pancreatectomy for non-invasive IPMN, median incidences are **all remnant lesions 19% (8–28%), clinically significant 10% (0–21%), invasive 4% (1–10%)**.
- **Interval and duration** (CQ3-2; 2+, C): **continuous surveillance as long as the patient remains fit for further intervention**. **Yearly imaging** for patients without additional risk factors; **every 6 months** for those with a **family history of pancreatic cancer or HGD** on the initial pathology. **After total pancreatectomy for a non-invasive lesion, IPMN-specific surveillance can stop if 5 years are uneventful.**
- **Imaging predictors of HGD/IC in the remnant** (CQ3-3; 2+, grade **D** — the weakest grade in the document): **solid component, MPD dilatation, growth of a cystic lesion.** New MPD dilatation must be examined to determine whether it is MD-IPMN progression or **pancreato-enteric anastomotic stenosis** (a known mimic after pancreatoduodenectomy or middle pancreatectomy).
- **Risk factors for clinically significant remnant lesions** (CQ3-4; 2+, C): **HGD on the initial pathology** and **family history of pancreatic cancer**. HGD marks an aggressive phenotype that disseminates through the gland by intraductal spread (skip progression).
- **Three mechanisms** of remnant lesions: new or progressing multifocal IPMN; true recurrence of the resected IPMN (margin-positive local recurrence or margin-negative skip progression); and concomitant PDAC. Only the second is real recurrence.
- Outcomes after resection of IPMN with IC mirror ordinary PDAC — distant recurrence in liver, peritoneum, node or lung, with **equivalent stage-for-stage overall survival**.

### Concomitant PDAC, multifocality, family history, extra-pancreatic neoplasms

- **"Dual carcinogenesis":** IPMN can progress from LGD to HGD/IC, *and* a PDAC can arise elsewhere in the same pancreas. **Yearly incidence of concomitant PDAC 0.4–1.0%; risk about 3–5 times the age-matched population**, so IPMN is a risk factor for PDAC. The IPMNs in patients who develop concomitant PDAC are **typically small BD-IPMNs**, and **the risk continues beyond the 5-year surveillance period**.
- **Geographic caveat, stated by the panel:** most concomitant-PDAC data come from Japan; in other countries the risk in IPMN patients appears **similar to the general population**. The discrepancy may reflect Japanese pathology practice (the entire resected pancreas cut in 5 mm slices) and heightened awareness.
- **Multifocal BD-IPMN occurs in 20–40%**, mostly independent lesions ("field defect"). **Multifocality does not increase HGD/IC risk**; management and surveillance follow **the highest-risk lesion**, and where surgery is indicated only that lesion is resected — prophylactic total pancreatectomy is avoided.
- **Family history of pancreatic cancer** = pancreatic cancer in **one or more first-degree relatives** (Cancer of the Pancreas Screening definition) — to be kept distinct from **familial pancreatic cancer**, defined by germline mutations such as *ATM*, *BRCA1/2*, *MLH1*, *CDKN2A*. Family history **raises the risk of concomitant PDAC but not of LGD→HGD/IC progression**.
- **Extra-pancreatic neoplasms occur in 20–30%** of IPMN patients, but the incidence matches population-based rates, so **no additional screening for extra-pancreatic neoplasms is necessary**.

### Chemotherapy, radiotherapy, ablation

- No established role for chemotherapy or radiotherapy in unresectable IPMN with IC, nor for adjuvant/neoadjuvant therapy in resectable disease; in practice most physicians use ordinary PDAC regimens. Node-positive, MUC2-negative tubular carcinoma may benefit from adjuvant chemotherapy.
- **Cyst ablation (ethanol ± gemcitabine/paclitaxel) should only be done in a clinical trial.** Reported efficacy 9–72%, adverse events 9–23%, most commonly pancreatitis. Candidate profile in the literature: contraindication to surgery but long life expectancy, unilocular or oligolocular mucinous cyst without definite MPD communication, larger cysts without a mural nodule.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Supersedes [[acg-2018-pancreatic-cysts]] on IPMN surveillance intervals and risk criteria** (same tier, newer publication date). ACG 2018 drives surveillance from cyst size bands of its own and uses its own feature list; Kyoto 2024 uses the HRS/WF framework with 6/18-month, 6/6/12-month and 6-month protocols by size. Both remain cited: ACG 2018 still owns cyst typing, the cyst-fluid CEA and amylase cutoffs, and management of the non-IPMN entities, none of which Kyoto addresses the same way.
- **Stopping surveillance** — Kyoto 2024 permits discontinuation at 5 years for a stable BD-IPMN <20 mm, while deliberately presenting "continue" as an equally acceptable option and warning that it may not apply to younger patients or those with familial/genetic risk. This is a genuine open question the guideline declines to close, not a recommendation to stop.
- **The concomitant-PDAC risk is region-dependent and the panel says so:** 3–5× the age-matched population in Japanese series, but similar to the general population in Western series. Cited as a possible artefact of pathological sampling and awareness.
- **Internal tension on mural-nodule size:** the panel reports evidence that a **10 mm** nodule height on EUS predicts HGD/IC far better (odds ratio 7.90) than 5–10 mm (odds ratio 2.93), then **keeps 5 mm** as the HRS threshold for lack of evidence strong enough to revise. The same applies to the MPD threshold. Readers who recall a proposed ≥10 mm nodule rule should know it was considered and rejected.
- **Grade D content:** CQ3-3 (imaging predictors of HGD/IC in the remnant pancreas) carries SIGN grade D, the weakest in the document.
- **Undefined by the guideline:** "new onset" and "acute exacerbation" of diabetes; the measurement method and cut-off for a thickened/enhancing cyst wall; the clinical meaning of T1 sub-staging in IPMN.
