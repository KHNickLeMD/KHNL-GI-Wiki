## Summary

This AGA expert review provides 14 Best Practice Advice (BPA) statements on acute kidney injury (AKI) in patients with cirrhosis, covering diagnosis (Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes [KDIGO] criteria), classification of AKI subtypes, initial evaluation, pharmacological treatment of hepatorenal syndrome (HRS)-AKI (albumin plus vasoconstrictors), indications for renal replacement therapy, and [[liver-transplantation|liver transplantation]] as definitive therapy. Its Table 1 compares previous and current HRS diagnostic criteria: HRS type 1/2 → HRS-AKI and HRS–non-AKI (HRS-NAKI). The BPA statements carry no formal rating of evidence quality or strength (no systematic review was done).

## Key Findings / Claims

**Epidemiology**

- AKI occurs in ~47% of hospitalized patients with complications of cirrhosis and ~30% of outpatients with cirrhosis
- Associated with a 7-fold increase in morbidity and mortality vs. those without AKI
- ~$4 billion in total US hospitalization costs
- Repeated AKI episodes increase risk of progression to chronic kidney disease (CKD)

**Diagnosis**

- AKI defined by KDIGO criteria: serum creatinine (SCr) rise ≥0.3 mg/dL within 48 hours, OR ≥50% increase from baseline within 7 days, OR urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for >6 hours
- Baseline SCr: use most recent value within prior 3 months if no value within 7 days
- Serum creatinine underestimates true glomerular filtration rate (GFR) reduction in cirrhosis (reduced muscle mass, impaired hepatic creatine production, hyperbilirubinemia interfering with colorimetric assays)

**HRS Classification (Table 1: Previous vs Current Criteria)**

| Previous | Current | Criteria (current) |
|---|---|---|
| HRS type 1 | HRS-AKI | Meets AKI criteria + functional, no response to diuretic withdrawal and 2-day albumin challenge (criteria below) |
| HRS type 2 | HRS-NAKI: HRS–acute kidney disease (HRS-AKD) | Two criteria listed (Table 1 does not say whether both are required): estimated GFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m² for <3 months in the absence of other potential causes of kidney disease · SCr increase ≥1.5× (>50%) from baseline within 3 months (baseline = value within the previous 3 months closest to presentation) |
| HRS type 2 | HRS-NAKI: HRS-CKD | Estimated GFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m² for ≥3 months in the absence of other potential causes of kidney disease |

HRS-AKI diagnostic criteria (current, Table 1):

- SCr increase ≥0.3 mg/dL within 48h OR ≥1.5× (>50%) baseline within 7 days (baseline = value within the previous 3 months closest to presentation)
- Cirrhosis with [[ascites]]
- No response to diuretic withdrawal and 2-day volume challenge with albumin 20%–25% at 1 g/kg/d (text, Initial Evaluation: maximum 100 g/d)
- Absence of shock
- No current/recent nephrotoxic drug use
- No structural kidney injury markers (proteinuria >500 mg/d, hematuria >50 red blood cells (RBCs)/high-power field (HPF), abnormal renal ultrasound)

**Urine Studies**

- Urine Na <10 mEq/L in HRS (but may be higher post-diuretics)
- Fractional excretion of sodium (FENa) <1%: suggests prerenal/HRS; sensitivity 100%, specificity only 14% in cirrhosis
- Fractional excretion of urea (FEUrea) <28.16%: sensitivity 75%, specificity 83% for separating HRS from non-HRS in a small retrospective study — FEUrea "may" better discriminate HRS from prerenal azotemia or acute tubular necrosis (ATN) (urea reabsorption not modified by diuretics) and "may be used" in the differential diagnosis of HRS-AKI
- Urinary neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (NGAL): most studied biomarker; levels in HRS-AKI much lower than ATN; cutoff 220–244 mcg/g creatinine distinguishes ATN from HRS/prerenal; not widely available in US clinically

**Prevention**

- Avoid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), excessive diuretics, nonselective beta-blockers
- Albumin replacement with large-volume paracentesis — this update states the principle (avoid large-volume paracentesis *without* albumin) but gives no replacement dose; the per-litre dose is on [[paracentesis]]. Its albumin doses are the AKI volume challenge (BPA 5: 1 g/kg/d for 2 days) and the HRS-AKI regimen (BPA 6: 1 g/kg IV on day 1, then 20–40 g daily), not paracentesis replacement
- Antibiotics with gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding and [[spontaneous-bacterial-peritonitis|spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP)]] prophylaxis
- Counsel against alcohol use

**Initial Management (BPA 5)**

- Hold diuretics and nonselective beta-blockers
- Discontinue NSAIDs
- Treat precipitating cause
- Replace fluid losses
- If SCr doubles from baseline: albumin 1 g/kg/d for 2 days (text, Initial Evaluation: maximum 100 g/d)
- Monitor fluid status: urine output, vital signs, and when indicated echocardiography or central venous pressure (CVP) (if a central line is already in place)

**HRS-AKI Treatment (BPA 6)**

- If SCr remains >2× baseline despite initial measures: start vasoconstrictors + albumin
- Albumin: 1 g/kg intravenous (IV) on day 1, then 20–40 g/day
- Vasoconstrictor: terlipressin; if terlipressin not available, octreotide + midodrine or norepinephrine, depending on institutional preferences
- Continue until 24 hours after SCr returns to within ≤0.3 mg/dL of baseline for 2 consecutive days, OR for a total of 14 days of therapy

**Terlipressin (BPA 7)**

- Bolus: 1 mg IV q4–6h (total 4–6 mg/d)
- Escalate to 2 mg q4–6h (total 8–12 mg/d) if SCr does not decrease ≥25% by day 3
- Continuous infusion alternative: start 2 mg/d, titrate by 24–48h intervals to max 12 mg/d
- Food and Drug Administration (FDA) restrictions: not to be used if SCr ≥5 mg/dL or oxygen saturation <90%
- Predictors of response: bilirubin <10 mg/dL, SCr <5 mg/dL, lower acute-on-chronic liver failure (ACLF) grade, sustained mean arterial pressure (MAP) increase of 5–10 mmHg
- Response rate in randomized controlled trials (RCTs): 36–44% (CONFIRM: 29% reversed HRS and survived 10 days post-treatment without renal replacement therapy [RRT])
- Risk of respiratory failure: ~30% in CONFIRM terlipressin arm, especially with concomitant organ failure

**Midodrine + Octreotide (BPA 8)**

- Midodrine: start 7.5 mg orally (PO) TID, titrate to 12.5 mg TID
- Octreotide: start 100 mcg subcutaneously (SQ) TID, titrate to 200 mcg TID
- Inferior to terlipressin; works very slowly; widely used in North America because terlipressin was until recently unavailable and norepinephrine must be given in the intensive care unit (ICU)

**Norepinephrine (BPA 9)**

- Continuous IV infusion: start 0.5 mg/h, increase by 0.5 mg/h every 4 hours, max 3 mg/h
- Goal: MAP increase ≥10 mmHg and/or urine output >50 mL/h for ≥4 hours
- Must be administered in ICU (feasibility study outside ICU exists but not standard)
- Similar efficacy to terlipressin except in ACLF (bilirubin >5 mg/dL, international normalized ratio [INR] >1.5, with encephalopathy or ascites onset within 4 weeks): terlipressin superior

**Renal Replacement Therapy (BPA 12)**

- BPA 12: RRT may be used for (A) AKI from ATN; (B) HRS-AKI in potential liver transplantation (LT) candidates — not in HRS-AKI patients who are not LT candidates; (C) AKI of uncertain etiology, considered on an individual basis
- Text: continuous RRT preferred over intermittent (lower fluid shifts, less hemodynamic instability)
- ~2/3 of HRS-AKI patients on pretransplant RRT recover renal function after LT
- Duration >14 days pretransplant associated with renal non-recovery
- Each additional day of dialysis pretransplant: 3.6–6% increased risk of renal non-recovery post-LT
- Mortality with >7 days RRT: 59%

**Transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS) (BPA 13)**

- Should not be used as a specific treatment of HRS-AKI (no data supporting use)

**Liver Transplantation (BPA 14)**

- Most effective treatment for HRS-AKI (reverses underlying hepatic dysfunction)
- Treat HRS-AKI pharmacologically before LT — may improve post-LT outcomes
- Model for End-Stage Liver Disease (MELD) paradox: successful treatment lowers SCr, reducing MELD-Na score and transplant priority
- Simultaneous liver-kidney transplant criteria (Organ Procurement and Transplantation Network [OPTN]/United Network for Organ Sharing [UNOS] 2017): dialysis dependence OR GFR ≤25 mL/min for ≥6 consecutive weeks

## Recommendations

All 14 statements are Best Practice Advice (BPA), numbered by the document; none carries an evidence or strength rating. Rows marked "Text" are narrative points, not BPAs.

| Locator | Statement (condensed) |
|---|---|
| BPA 1 | Diagnose AKI when SCr rises ≥0.3 mg/dL within 48 hours, or ≥50% from baseline, or urine output falls below 0.5 mL/kg/h for >6 hours |
| BPA 2 | Prevent AKI: (1) avoid nephrotoxic drugs such as NSAIDs; (2) avoid excessive or unmonitored diuretics or nonselective beta-blockade; (3) avoid large-volume paracentesis without albumin replacement; (4) counsel against alcohol |
| BPA 3 | (A) Determine the cause: hypovolemic (volume responsive; most common in cirrhosis), ATN, HRS-AKI (functional failure persisting despite volume repletion), HRS-AKD (functional failure <3 months not meeting HRS-AKI criteria), or postrenal (rare). (B) Identify the type by history, exam, blood chemistry, urine microscopy, urine Na and urea, selected urinary biomarkers, and renal ultrasound |
| BPA 4 | Rigorous search for infection in all AKI: diagnostic paracentesis for SBP, blood and urine cultures, chest radiograph. No routine prophylactic antibiotics in AKI; start broad-spectrum antibiotics whenever infection is strongly suspected |
| BPA 5 | Hold diuretics and nonselective beta-blockers, stop NSAIDs, treat the precipitant, replace fluid losses; albumin 1 g/kg/d for 2 days if SCr doubles from baseline. Monitor fluid status by urine output, vital signs, and when indicated echocardiography or CVP (if a central line is already in place) |
| Text (not a BPA) | Albumin fluid challenge 1 g/kg body weight to a maximum of 100 g/d |
| BPA 6 | SCr still >2× baseline despite these measures → albumin 1 g/kg IV day 1, then 20–40 g daily, plus a vasoactive agent (terlipressin; if unavailable, octreotide + midodrine or norepinephrine per institutional preference); continue until 24 hours after SCr returns to within ≤0.3 mg/dL of baseline for 2 consecutive days, or for a total of 14 days |
| BPA 7 | Terlipressin bolus 1 mg every 4–6 hours (4–6 mg/d); increase to max 2 mg every 4–6 hours (8–12 mg/d) if SCr has not fallen ≥25% from baseline by day 3. Alternative: continuous IV infusion starting 2 mg/d, increased every 24–48 hours to max 12 mg/d or HRS reversal. Per FDA restrictions, not for SCr ≥5 mg/dL or oxygen saturation <90% |
| BPA 8 | Midodrine orally 7.5 mg, titrated to 12.5 mg 3 times daily, with octreotide 100 µg titrated to 200 µg subcutaneously 3 times daily |
| BPA 9 | Norepinephrine continuous IV infusion, start 0.5 mg/h, increase by 0.5 mg/h every 4 hours to max 3 mg/h; goal MAP increase ≥10 mmHg and/or urine output >50 mL/h for ≥4 hours |
| BPA 10 | Terlipressin and norepinephrine risk ischemic side effects (angina; ischemia of fingers, skin, intestine); lowered by starting at the lowest dose and titrating up gradually |
| BPA 11 | Monitor fluid status closely — risk of pulmonary edema with excessive albumin |
| BPA 12 | RRT may be used for (A) AKI from ATN; (B) HRS-AKI in potential LT candidates (not in HRS-AKI patients who are not LT candidates); (C) AKI of uncertain etiology, considered individually |
| Text (not a BPA) | Continuous RRT preferred over intermittent (lower fluid shifts, less hemodynamic instability) |
| BPA 13 | TIPS should not be used as a specific treatment of HRS-AKI |
| BPA 14 | LT is the most effective treatment for HRS-AKI; pharmacotherapy before LT may be associated with better post-LT outcomes; selected patients may need simultaneous liver-kidney transplantation per updated OPTN listing criteria |
| Text (not a BPA) | Simultaneous liver-kidney allocation requires dialysis or measured/calculated GFR ≤25 mL/min for ≥6 consecutive weeks (OPTN policy implemented by UNOS in 2017) |
| Text (not a BPA) | FEUrea <28.16% (sensitivity 75%, specificity 83%, small retrospective study) may be used in the differential diagnosis of HRS-AKI |

## Contradictions / Open Questions

1. MELD paradox: successful pharmacological reversal of HRS-AKI lowers SCr, decreasing transplant priority — not yet resolved by allocation policy
2. Long-term albumin for HRS prevention: ANSWER trial positive, but not replicated in subsequent trials
3. FENa has poor specificity (14%) in cirrhosis — limited discriminatory value despite widespread use
4. Terlipressin respiratory failure rate (30% in CONFIRM) raises safety concerns, especially in ACLF with multi-organ failure
5. Simultaneous liver-kidney vs. liver-alone transplantation: biomarkers needed to identify best candidates
6. No studies using patient-reported outcomes in AKI in cirrhosis
