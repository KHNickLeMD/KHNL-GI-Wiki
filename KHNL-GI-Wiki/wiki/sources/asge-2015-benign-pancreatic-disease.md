## Summary

ASGE Standards of Practice statement (GRADE) on the endoscopic evaluation and treatment of benign pancreatic disease — idiopathic [[acute-pancreatitis]] (including idiopathic recurrent acute pancreatitis, IRAP), sphincter of Oddi dysfunction (SOD), autoimmune pancreatitis (AIP), and [[chronic-pancreatitis]] (dominant duct strictures, pancreatolithiasis, pancreatic-duct leaks).

Key themes: ERCP is **not** a diagnostic test for a single episode of acute pancreatitis; EUS is the preferred low-risk tool to interrogate idiopathic disease, especially in older patients in whom occult malignancy must be excluded. The landmark practice point is that **ERCP must not be used to evaluate type 3 SOD** (recurrent/chronic pain alone) — a high-quality recommendation rooted in the harm/no-benefit signal later confirmed by EPISOD. Post-ERCP pancreatitis prophylaxis (rectal indomethacin and/or pancreatic-duct stenting in high-risk patients) is strongly recommended. In chronic pancreatitis, endoscopic therapy (ERP with dilation/stenting, ESWL for refractory stones, stenting for PD leaks) is appropriate within a multidisciplinary framework.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Recommendations (verbatim, with GRADE quality of evidence; 4444 = high, 444B = moderate, 44BB = low, 4BBB = very low):**

1. We suggest EUS for the evaluation of idiopathic AP for patients older than 40 years of age if history, physical examination, laboratory testing, and abdominal imaging with MRI or CT are unrevealing. (Low)
2. We recommend against diagnostic ERCP for a single episode of AP. (Moderate)
3. We suggest that ERCP with sphincter of Oddi manometry may be considered for the evaluation of idiopathic acute recurrent pancreatitis (suspected type 2 pancreatic SOD) when findings on EUS and/or MRCP are normal and without suspicion for biliary stones, sludge, or CP. Alternate strategies include ERCP with empiric biliary and/or pancreatic sphincterotomy. (Low)
4. We recommend biliary and/or pancreatic sphincterotomy in patients with type 1 pancreatic SOD or patients with type 2 pancreatic SOD confirmed by manometry. (Moderate)
5. We recommend against the use of ERCP for the evaluation of recurrent or chronic abdominal pain interpreted as type 3 SOD. (High)
6. We recommend the use of rectal indomethacin and/or PD stenting for the prevention of post-ERCP pancreatitis in high-risk patients. (High)
7. We recommend EUS-guided tissue biopsy for suspected but unproved cases of AIP. Although FNA is useful for excluding underlying malignancy in older patients, larger gauge core tissue devices may be required to confirm the diagnosis of AIP. (Moderate)
8. We suggest ePFT and/or EUS without pancreatic biopsy for the diagnosis of CP not readily evident by previous noninvasive imaging. (Low)
9. We recommend ERP with dilation and/or plastic stent placement for the treatment of symptomatic dominant PD strictures for individuals in whom multidisciplinary review considers endoscopic therapy as the preferred initial therapy. (High)
10. We recommend the adjunctive use of ESWL for patients with symptoms attributed to pancreatolithiasis refractory to standard endoscopic stone extraction techniques. (Moderate)
11. We recommend that ERP with stenting be the first-line therapy for the management of PD leaks. (High)

**Other points:**
- ePFT = endoscopic pancreatic function testing; EUS criteria can diagnose early CP not seen on cross-sectional imaging.
- For suspected AIP, core-tissue devices (not just FNA) may be needed to confirm; FNA's main role is to exclude malignancy in older patients.

## Relevance to Wiki
- Updates [[acute-pancreatitis]] — no diagnostic ERCP for a single episode; EUS for unexplained AP in patients >40 with negative workup; SOD typing (treat type 1, manometry-confirmed type 2; do NOT scope type 3); rectal indomethacin ± PD stent for post-ERCP pancreatitis prophylaxis.
- Updates [[chronic-pancreatitis]] — EUS/ePFT for imaging-occult CP; ERP dilation/stenting for dominant PD strictures; adjunctive ESWL for refractory pancreatolithiasis; ERP+stent first-line for PD leaks.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- Older (2015) than [[asge-2023-post-ercp-pancreatitis|ASGE 2023 post-ERCP pancreatitis prevention]] and [[asge-2024-chronic-pancreatitis-endoscopy|ASGE 2024 chronic pancreatitis endoscopy]]; for detailed PEP-prophylaxis algorithms and CP endotherapy the newer ASGE documents govern. This source contributes the type-3-SOD prohibition and the idiopathic-AP/IRAP evaluation framework. Its type-2 SOD manometry recommendation predates the EPISOD trial's negative findings for SOD type III — the type-3 prohibition (rec 5) aligns with EPISOD; the type-2 manometry pathway is now applied selectively.
