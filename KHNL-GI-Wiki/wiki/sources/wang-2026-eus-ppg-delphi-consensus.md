## Summary

International expert modified-Delphi consensus on **endoscopic ultrasound-guided portosystemic pressure gradient (EUS-PPG)** measurement — a technique (first described 2017) that directly and sequentially measures **hepatic vein and portal vein pressures** via EUS-guided needle puncture, with the gradient = mean portal vein pressure − mean hepatic vein pressure (mmHg). This contrasts with the transjugular **[[hepatic-venous-pressure-gradient|HVPG]]**, which measures wedged (occluded) hepatic vein pressure as an *indirect* proxy for portal pressure. Because EUS-PPG measures portal pressure directly, it is expected to be more accurate than HVPG when portal hypertension is **presinusoidal** (where wedged pressure underestimates severity) and in **MASH**.

A steering committee of 8 experts (6 advanced endoscopists, 2 hepatologists) drafted statements across **4 domains**: indications, pre-procedural protocol, intra-procedural technique, and future research. 47 experts (29 advanced endoscopists, 18 hepatologists; expertise defined as ≥15 EUS-PPG procedures in the year before 2023, or being the primary referring hepatologist) were invited; 43 (91%) agreed, 41 participated in round 1, 37 in round 2. Consensus threshold = **≥70% of participants scoring a statement ≥7 ("moderately agree" or higher) on a 1–9 Likert scale.**

**54 statements** were initially proposed; 46 (85%) reached consensus in round 1 (Table 1), 8 did not (Table 2) — of which 2 coagulation statements were merged into 1, and 7 revised statements passed on round 2. **In total, 53 statements reached consensus after 2 rounds.** Key headline conclusions: (1) indications for EUS-PPG include all indications for HVPG and it may be *preferred* for suspected presinusoidal portal hypertension or when another endoscopic indication (e.g. variceal screening, liver biopsy) is present ("one-stop-shop"); (2) EUS-PPG is safe and well tolerated (aggregate adverse events <1%, most common = post-procedural pain); (3) it should be performed under monitored anesthesia care (MAC) or general anesthesia; (4) meticulous technical detail is required for accuracy.

Correlation data cited: EUS-PPG vs HVPG ICC 0.82 (Martinez-Moreno); ENCOUNTER trial (Vanderschueren) r=0.74 overall, r=0.85 portal vein, r=0.72 hepatic vein, and r=0.91 vs direct transjugular portal pressure in post-TIPS patients. The largest multicenter series (Kolb, n=385) reported **no bleeding adverse events** despite no platelet/INR exclusion criteria, including in 226 (58.7%) who had same-session EUS liver biopsy. ENCOUNTER also observed that **propofol-based general anesthesia substantially and clinically depresses HVPG** vs no sedation — flagged as a key confounder needing study.

## Key Findings / Claims

*All 53 consensus statements, verbatim/near-verbatim, grouped by the 6 tabulated categories (which map to the 4 domains). Each shows % Agree / mean Score (1–9) / SD. Statements marked **[Round 2]** did not reach consensus initially and passed on re-vote after revision (the revised wording is what is reproduced). Consensus threshold = ≥70% scoring ≥7.*

### Domain 1 — Indications
1. Clinical indications for EUS-PPG measurements include, but are not limited to, all indications for HVPG measurements. — **88% / 8.0 / 1.8**
2. EUS-PPG measurements have an excellent correlation with clinical parameters of portal hypertension, including the presence of varices, portal hypertensive gastropathy, and thrombocytopenia. — **90% / 7.8 / 1.4**
3. EUS-PPG should be considered over HVPG measurements in scenarios in which presinusoidal causes of portal hypertension are suspected. — **95% / 8.3 / 1.3**
4. EUS-PPG may be favored over HVPG measurements from the perspective of patient convenience, particularly if the patient has another indication for either EGD or EUS (eg, liver biopsy, variceal screening/surveillance). — **88% / 8.1 / 1.8**
5. **[Round 2]** EUS-PPG may also be favored over HVPG from the perspective of patient comfort due to usage of anesthesia. — **72% / 6.8 / 2.4** *(original wording "eg, moderate sedation vs anesthesia" failed at 66%)*

### Domain 2 — General pre-procedure considerations
6. EUS-PPG should be avoided in patients with tense or massive ascites but can be considered if pre-procedural large-volume paracentesis is performed. — **80% / 7.3 / 1.9**
7. EUS-PPG is a safe procedure with minimal risk of adverse events. — **95% / 8.3 / 0.9**
8. A screening EGD should be done at the time of EUS to assess for and document sequalae of portal hypertension (eg, esophageal varices, gastric varices, portal hypertensive gastropathy). — **88% / 8.1 / 1.6**
9. Prior to PPG, an EUS survey of the liver should be performed, including assessment of vasculature, to delineate targets and to rule out vascular abnormalities (eg, portal vein thrombosis). — **100% / 8.7 / 1.0**
10. If indicated, EUS-liver biopsy can be safely performed during the same session as EUS-PPG. — **98% / 8.6 / 1.1**
11. **[Round 2]** In patients with cirrhosis, no specific platelet or INR cutoff can be recommended for EUS-PPG as these tests poorly reflect procedural bleeding risk. Where available, TEG may be a more useful tool to predict bleeding risk in these patients. — **79% / 7.5 / 1.3** *(merged from two failed statements: "avoided if platelets <50 K" [51%] and "cautioned if cirrhosis and PT-INR >2.0" [56%])*
12. **[Round 2]** EUS-PPG is reasonable to perform in patients with mild to moderate (ie, grade 1 or 2) ascites, although it may be prudent to consider therapeutic paracentesis prior to the procedure if technically feasible. — **92% / 7.6 / 1.9** *(original "EUS-PPG is safe in patients with mild to moderate ascites" failed at 66%)*

### Domain 3 — Endoscopy-specific pre-procedure considerations
13. EUS-PPG should be performed with monitored anesthesia care or general anesthesia to minimize patient discomfort and unintentional movement during the procedure. — **83% / 7.9 / 2.3**
14. Patients can be positioned either supine or left lateral decubitus. — **72% / 6.7 / 2.9**
15. When the patient is in the supine position, the patient should be flat, and the manometer should be centered at the level of the right atrium (mid-axillary line). — **100% / 8.5 / 0.8**
16. When the patient is in the left lateral decubitus position, the patient should be flat, and the manometer should be centered at the level of the right atrium (mid sternum). — **82% / 7.9 / 1.4**
17. Serial EUS-PPG measurements (ie, during different sessions) should be performed under the same anesthetic conditions and patient positioning. — **100% / 8.5 / 0.9**
18. The manometer should be zeroed to atmospheric pressure prior to EUS-PPG device setup. — **92% / 8.2 / 1.1**
19. **[Round 2]** When consenting patients for the EUS-PPG procedure, endoscopists can cite similar risks associated with an EUS-FNA procedure, such as infection and bleeding. — **96% / 7.9 / 1.5** *(original "the same risks associated with an EUS-FNA procedure" failed at 65%)*
20. **[Round 2]** Peri-procedural antibiotics should be considered. — **74% / 6.9 / 2.3** *(original "should be administered" failed at 65%)*

### Domain 4 — Intra-procedure considerations prior to measurements
21. Once zeroed, the entire device should then be flushed with heparinized or normal saline; great care must be taken to avoid any air bubbles. — **100% / 8.8 / 1.0**
22. The manometer should be fixated in a stable and consistent position during the procedure. — **100% / 8.7 / 0.9**
23. The optimal approach for EUS-PPG measurements is the transgastric approach through the liver parenchyma. However, a transduodenal approach may occasionally be required. — **77% / 7.7 / 1.8**
24. Needle access should be ideally performed through liver parenchyma to provide tamponade upon needle withdrawal. — **100% / 8.6 / 0.9**
25. Prior to needle insertion into either the hepatic or portal vein, Doppler wave forms should be used to identify branches of portal and hepatic veins when feasible. — **88% / 8.1 / 1.7**
26. For most patients, the ideal hepatic vein targets include the left and middle hepatic veins. — **92% / 8.3 / 1.1**
27. Calculation of the portosystemic pressure gradient is performed by subtracting the mean hepatic vein measurement from the mean portal vein measurement (both reported in mmHg). Therefore, measurement of both veins is necessary as part of the EUS-PPG procedure. — **88% / 8.4 / 1.6**
28. For most patients, the ideal portal vein target is the umbilical portion of the left portal vein. — **76% / 7.7 / 1.4**
29. **[Round 2]** Endoscopic suction should be cautioned during and after EUS-PPG device insertion into the endoscope instrument channel. — **77% / 7.2 / 1.6** *(original "should be avoided" failed at 44%)*
30. **[Round 2]** If the hepatic vein is not readily accessible, the intrahepatic portion of the IVC may be a reasonable alternative target in place of the hepatic vein. — **91% / 7.9 / 1.1** *(original — plain "the IVC is a reasonable alternative" — failed at 69%)*

### Domain 5 — Intra-procedure considerations during measurements
31. A minimum of 3 sequential, tightly clustered measurements should be taken for each vascular target to ensure values are reproducible. Measurements should be ≤2 mmHg of each other. The mean value should be reported. — **96% / 8.5 / 1.0**
32. After each flush, the manometer readings should peak, then drop, and eventually plateau at a stable value for at least 10 seconds (range of ≤1 mmHg). — **100% / 8.5 / 0.9**
33. After each flush, the heparin syringe plunger should not be touched during measurements to avoid inadvertent pressure and falsely elevated readings. — **100% / 8.7 / 0.9**
34. The attempt should be repeated if the manometer reading takes more than 60 seconds to stabilize. — **96% / 8.2 / 0.8**
35. Care should be taken to obtain a relaxed scope position and avoid undue elevator engagement during measurements, as these variables can affect pressure readings. — **84% / 7.7 / 1.8**
36. During measurements, it is important to maintain intravascular needle positioning and away from the vascular wall, as this may impact pressure readings. — **100% / 8.6 / 1.0**
37. Intravenous fluids should be maintained at a steady state during pressure measurements given their potential impact on accuracy of pressure readings. — **92% / 8.1 / 1.7**
38. If changes to intravenous fluid are needed during or in between measurements, repeat measurements should be considered under stable hemodynamic conditions. — **96% / 8.2 / 1.4**
39. Re-accessing hepatic and portal venous systems can be considered when there are discrepancies during initial measurements. — **100% / 8.5 / 0.9**
40. Patient movement and coughing can affect intraprocedural readings. — **92% / 8.4 / 1.1**
41. Patients ideally should be stable at a steady state of anesthesia administration prior to needle insertion for pressure measurements. Anesthetic boluses between measurements should be avoided as they can confound measurements. — **96% / 8.2 / 1.0**
42. If there are unavoidable changes to anesthesia during or in between measurements, events such as timing of boluses, increases, and decreases to anesthesia dosing should be carefully documented in the procedure report. — **84% / 7.6 / 1.2**
43. If there are unavoidable changes to anesthesia during or in between measurements, repeat measurements should be considered under steady-state anesthesia conditions. — **96% / 8.4 / 0.9**

### Domain 6 — Future research directions
44. Further research is necessary to evaluate the clinical utility of EUS-PPG measurements in predicting long-term outcomes in patients with cirrhosis and portal hypertension. — **90% / 8.3 / 1.2**
45. Particular attention should be focused on the impact of EUS-PPG in guiding management of compensated cirrhosis and subclinical portal hypertension. — **98% / 8.2 / 1.4**
46. Further research is needed to assess the impact of EUS-PPG in preoperative assessment and risk stratification of patients suspected to have portal hypertension prior to their hepatic and nonhepatic surgery. — **90% / 8.2 / 1.1**
47. Further research is needed to directly correlate EUS-PPG measurements with HVPG in human subjects, especially in areas in which EUS-PPG may be more accurate than HVPG (eg, true portal vein pressure in patients with MASH). — **90% / 8.0 / 1.8**
48. Additional research is needed to evaluate and validate the performance characteristics between EUS-PPG measurements and data from noninvasive liver disease assessments during assessment of portal hypertension. — **88% / 8.0 / 1.2**
49. Additional research is needed to understand the effects of anesthesia (both monitored anesthesia care and general anesthesia) on performance characteristics of EUS-PPG. — **98% / 8.4 / 0.9**
50. Further research would be helpful to delineate the role of EUS-PPG in diagnosis and management of noncirrhotic portal hypertension (ie, etiologies of pre- and post-sinusoidal portal hypertension). — **98% / 8.4 / 1.1**
51. Additional research would be helpful to assess the utility of serial measurements EUS-PPG in specific scenarios (eg, titration of beta blocker medications, pre and post-bariatric interventions). — **93% / 8.2 / 1.5**
52. Further research would be helpful to evaluate the patient experience (eg intra-procedure comfort, convenience) in undergoing EUS-PPG, in particular when compared with similar procedures performed by interventional radiologists. — **88% / 7.9 / 1.6**
53. Additional research would be helpful in assessing the cost utility of EUS-PPG measurements compared with HVPG. — **90% / 7.9 / 1.4**

### Supporting data / rationale (non-statement)
- **Definition/calculation:** EUS-PPG = mean portal vein pressure − mean hepatic vein pressure (both mmHg), by direct needle puncture (Statement 27). HVPG, by contrast, = wedged (occluded) hepatic vein pressure − free hepatic vein pressure, an *indirect* portal-pressure proxy.
- **Safety:** aggregate adverse events <1%; most common = post-procedural pain. Largest series (Kolb, n=385) had zero bleeding events despite no platelet/INR exclusion, including 226 (58.7%) with same-session EUS liver biopsy.
- **Correlation with HVPG:** ICC 0.82 (Martinez-Moreno); ENCOUNTER r=0.74 overall, r=0.85 (portal vein), r=0.72 (hepatic vein), r=0.91 vs direct transjugular portal pressure post-TIPS.
- **Presinusoidal example:** Santopaolo 2025 — in porto-sinusoidal vascular disorder with clinically significant PH (n=26), mean EUS-PPG 16.7 vs HVPG 5.5 mmHg; only EUS-PPG (not HVPG) discriminated hepatic decompensation.
- **Anesthesia confounder:** ENCOUNTER observed propofol-based general anesthesia substantially/clinically depressed HVPG vs no sedation — motivates studying anesthesia effects and a possible correction factor.
- **Bleeding-risk tool:** where platelets/INR are unreliable in cirrhosis, TEG/TEM may better predict bleeding risk (RCT evidence for TEG-guided transfusion in cirrhosis).
- **Coag management extension:** the group noted (though not an explicit statement) that antiplatelet/anticoagulant hold-and-restart strategies used for EUS-FNA can be applied to EUS-PPG.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **No platelet/INR cutoff (Statement 11)** deliberately departs from the typical "avoid if platelets <50 K / caution if INR >2.0" thresholds used for other EUS procedures — the consensus rejects those cutoffs for EUS-PPG in cirrhosis (they poorly predict bleeding), favoring TEG/TEM. This should be surfaced against any wiki page that still cites platelet/INR thresholds for periprocedural bleeding risk in cirrhosis (cf. AASLD 2020 vascular-liver-disorders guidance).
- **Anesthesia depresses portal/hepatic pressures** — ENCOUNTER found propofol GA lowers HVPG; the consensus mandates MAC/GA (Statement 13) yet flags that anesthesia itself may bias absolute pressure values (Statements 41–43, 49). Open question: preferred anesthesia strategy and whether a correction factor is needed when comparing EUS-PPG to HVPG.
- **Weak/borderline consensus items** (near the 70% floor, high SD): patient positioning either supine or LLD (14, 72%), patient-comfort advantage over HVPG (5, 72%), peri-procedural antibiotics "should be considered" (20, 74%), umbilical left portal vein as ideal target (28, 76%), transgastric optimal approach (23, 77%), endoscopic suction cautioned (29, 77%). These reflect genuine practice variability, not settled standards.
- **Generalizability:** 85% of participants were North American, most at liver-transplant academic centers with Cook Medical relationships; only the compact-manometer EUS-PPG system (US FDA-approved) was represented — centers using alternative pressure-transducing strategies were not invited. Expertise threshold was only ≥15 procedures.
- **No outcome data yet:** the consensus is explicit that EUS-PPG's link to long-term clinical outcomes (decompensation, mortality), its validation vs noninvasive tests, and its cost-utility vs HVPG are all unproven (future-research domain).
