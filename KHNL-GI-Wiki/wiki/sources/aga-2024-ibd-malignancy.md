## Summary

AGA CPU Commentary on the two-way relationship between IBD and cancer: (1) the malignancy risks conferred by chronic intestinal inflammation and by IBD immunosuppressive therapies, and (2) how to manage IBD medications in a patient who develops an active/recent cancer or who has a prior cancer history. Being a **Commentary**, it issues **no numbered Best Practice Advice statements**; its operative decision content is **Table 1 — what to do with each IBD drug class once a specific malignancy develops** (recreated below), plus quantified cancer-risk estimates.

Two themes dominate. First, the drugs of greatest concern are **thiopurines** (NMSC, non-Hodgkin/hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma, urinary-tract and cervical cancer) and **anti-TNF** (melanoma; lymphoma chiefly when combined with a thiopurine). Newer classes — anti-integrin (vedolizumab), anti–IL-12/23 & anti–IL-23 (ustekinumab, risankizumab, mirikizumab), S1P modulators (ozanimod, etrasimod), and JAK inhibitors — have **no demonstrated increased malignancy risk in IBD** to date, though long-term data are limited. Second, for patients with **prior cancer**, accumulating cohort/registry data (CESAME, ENEIDA, NYCCO, SAPPHIRE, and meta-analyses) show **no significant increase in new or recurrent cancer** from immunomodulators, anti-TNF, vedolizumab, or ustekinumab — reassurance that effective IBD therapy generally need not be withheld on cancer-history grounds alone.

For **active cancer**, if IBD activity must be controlled during oncologic treatment, most biologics are warranted with close oncologist–gastroenterologist collaboration. Cytotoxic chemotherapy tends to quiet IBD (most patients achieve/maintain remission), whereas **hormonal deprivation therapy** (breast/prostate cancer) is associated with IBD flare (HR ~1.9–2.0). **Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs)** can trigger an IBD-like colitis and, in patients with known IBD, provoke relapse in ~40% (CTLA-4 inhibitors > PD-1/PD-L1); ICI colitis is managed with steroids and biologics for steroid-refractory disease.

## Key Findings / Claims

**This is a Commentary — no numbered Best Practice Advice. Key quantified claims and Table 1 below.**

### Cancer risk from underlying IBD inflammation
- **CRC (colitis-associated):** declining in modern cohorts — ~**1%, 2%, 5%** cumulative CRC risk at **10, 20, >20 years** from IBD diagnosis (older estimate: 15% after 30 y). Field cancerization → multifocal dysplasia; >1/3 of UC-CRC colectomies had synchronous CRC/dysplasia elsewhere → underscores mucosal healing + dysplasia surveillance.
- **CRC risk factors in IBD:** longer disease duration, greater colonic extent, family history of CRC, concomitant **PSC** (>3-fold odds of CRC/dysplasia).
- **Small-bowel adenocarcinoma** (Crohn's small-bowel inflammation): aHR **15.8** (95% CI 11.0–22.8); **neuroendocrine tumors** aHR **5.5** — but absolute risk low (2.4/10,000 PY CD; 4.6/10,000 PY ileal CD vs 0.28 reference). RFs: tobacco, male sex, childhood-onset CD, long-standing/penetrating/stricturing disease, prior SB resection.
- **Intestinal lymphoma:** 2–3× increased (absolute 1–4/10,000 PY CD); predominantly B-cell non-Hodgkin, often EBV-associated.
- **Anal cancer** (Crohn's perianal/anal disease): anal SCC 2.6/10,000 PY (with anal/perianal lesions) vs 0.8 without; RF = HPV, long-standing perianal fistulizing disease (combined SCC+adenocarcinoma 3.8/10,000 PY).
- **Cholangiocarcinoma:** 4× more likely in IBD (7.6 vs 1.9/100,000 PY); driven by **PSC** (>150-fold vs no PSC; 5%–10% lifetime risk).
- **Extraintestinal (systematic review):** NMSC (CD IRR 2.22; UC IRR 1.38), hematologic malignancy (CD IRR 2.40), lung cancer (CD IRR 1.53).

### Cancer risk associated with IBD therapies
- **Thiopurines:** ~2× NMSC (persists after stopping → annual dermatologic exam for current/prior users); non-intestinal lymphoma SIR 2.8–9.2 (highest absolute risk age >50 [1/354 PY]; highest relative risk age <30); hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma in young males, esp. combined with anti-TNF, often fatal; possible AML/MDS signal; urinary-tract cancer 2.8× (esp. males); cervical high-grade dysplasia/cancer 1.3× (driven by cumulative immunomodulator, not biologic, exposure).
- **Methotrexate:** limited data; possible small NMSC increase.
- **Anti-TNF:** conflicting melanoma data (one claims ~2× ; others null); lymphoma risk mostly not increased with **monotherapy** but consistently increased when **combined with thiopurine** (esp. young EBV-unexposed → hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma).
- **JAK inhibitors:** no increased malignancy demonstrated in IBD; RA signal (ORAL Surveillance) of ↑ any malignancy vs anti-TNF in patients >50 (NMSC, lung, lymphoma) — extrapolation uncertain.
- **Vedolizumab, ustekinumab, risankizumab, mirikizumab, ozanimod, etrasimod:** no increased malignancy risk shown (long-term data lacking); no data on combining biologic + small molecule.

### Prior cancer — new/recurrent cancer risk on IBD therapy (reassuring)
- CESAME: prior-cancer patients have higher incident cancer (HR 1.9) but **no** association with immunomodulator exposure. Confirmed by ENEIDA registry and NYCCO cohort (no difference by anti-TNF vs antimetabolite vs combo vs none).
- Meta-analyses (11,700 and 24,328 immune-mediated patients w/ cancer history): **no** difference in recurrence with anti-TNF, immunomodulators, or no immunosuppression.
- Vedolizumab & ustekinumab: no incident-cancer signal in prior-cancer IBD patients.
- **SAPPHIRE registry** (305 IBD + prior cancer; 69% received subsequent immunosuppression; median f/u 4.8 y): 15% developed subsequent cancer — **no** significant association with immunosuppression (aHR 1.41; 95% CI 0.69–2.90).

### Active cancer — impact of cancer therapy on IBD
- Cytotoxic chemotherapy: generally induces/maintains IBD remission (durable at 5 y).
- **Hormonal deprivation therapy** (breast/prostate): associated with IBD flare (HR 2.00 alone; 1.86 with chemo) → follow closely.
- **ICIs:** RR of diarrhea 1.64, colitis 10.35; in known IBD → relapse in ~40% (≥1/3 need biologics); **CTLA-4 > PD-1/PD-L1** for relapse risk; ICI colitis Rx = steroids, biologics if steroid-refractory.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- Melanoma–anti-TNF association is inconsistent (one insurance-claims study ~2×; Danish cohort and a biologics meta-analysis null). Table 1 nonetheless advises stopping anti-TNF if melanoma develops.
- JAK-inhibitor malignancy signal derives largely from RA (ORAL Surveillance, age >50); direct IBD data do not show an increase — extrapolation uncertain.
- No wiki page yet for **ICI colitis** as a discrete entity (only `[[immune-checkpoint-inhibitor-hepatitis]]`); this CPU covers ICI colitis only tangentially — a dedicated AGA ICI colitis/hepatitis source would be needed to build it.
