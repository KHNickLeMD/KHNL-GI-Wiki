## Summary

The IAIHG's own diagnostic criteria, proposed in 1993 and revised in 1999 ([[alvarez-1999-iaihg-revised-criteria]]), were **complex and built for research comparability, not bedside use**. This study set out to replace them for routine practice with a score built from **four routinely available measurements**: autoantibodies, immunoglobulin G (IgG), liver histology, and the absence of viral hepatitis.

**Design.** Retrospective cohort, **11 centres in 10 countries** across North and South America, Europe and Asia, data assembled January 2005 to September 2006. **Training set: 250 patients with AIH** (including 8 AIH/primary biliary cirrhosis and 2 AIH/primary sclerosing cholangitis overlap) **and 193 controls**. **Validation set: 109 AIH patients and 284 controls**, collected after the training analysis — so the mix of control diagnoses differed deliberately between the two sets (the validation controls are weighted toward viral hepatitis and steatohepatitis). All AIH patients had a **definite** score on the old IAIHG system before immunosuppression, and **response to immunosuppressive therapy was mandatory** for inclusion.

**Result.** Candidate variables were screened univariately; stepwise logistic regression selected **IgG, antinuclear antibody (ANA) and smooth muscle antibody (SMA) titres, and liver histology** (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve [AUC] 0.99 in combination). Points were assigned to make bedside calculation possible, giving a maximum of 8. **AUC 0.946 in the training set and 0.91 in the validation set.** Two cut-points were chosen: **≥6 = probable AIH, ≥7 = definite AIH**.

**What the authors warn about.** Histology is a **prerequisite**, not an optional component. Autoantibody testing is **not standardised** between laboratories — the paper gives explicit instructions for ANA on HEp-2 cells and says enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) is not yet adequate for this score. And the score is weakest exactly where AIH is hardest: **overlap syndromes, drug-induced liver injury, and populations with a high prevalence of viral hepatitis or steatotic liver disease.**

## Key Findings / Claims

### Table 2 — the simplified diagnostic criteria (the score itself)

| Variable | Cutoff | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **ANA or SMA** | **≥1:40** | **1** |
| **ANA or SMA** | **≥1:80** | **2\*** |
| **or anti-LKM** | **≥1:40** | **2\*** |
| **or anti-SLA** | **Positive** | **2\*** |
| **IgG** | **> upper normal limit** | **1** |
| **IgG** | **>1.10 × upper normal limit** | **2** |
| **Liver histology** (evidence of hepatitis is a **necessary condition**) | **Compatible with AIH** | **1** |
| **Liver histology** | **Typical AIH** | **2** |
| **Absence of viral hepatitis** | **Yes** | **2** |

\* **The autoantibody block is capped at 2 points total** — the paper states "addition of points achieved for all autoantibodies (maximum, 2 points)". Stacking ANA, SMA, anti-liver-kidney microsomal (anti-LKM) and anti-soluble liver antigen (anti-SLA) beyond 2 is the scoring error.

| Total score | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| **≥6** | **Probable AIH** |
| **≥7** | **Definite AIH** |

**Maximum possible score = 8** (2 autoantibody + 2 IgG + 2 histology + 2 viral exclusion).

### The three histology categories (defined for this score by Dienes and Lohse)

- **Typical AIH (2 points)** — **all three** of: **interface hepatitis** with lymphocytic/lymphoplasmacytic infiltrates in portal tracts extending into the lobule; **emperipolesis** (active penetration by one cell into and through a larger cell); **hepatic rosette formation**. Each of the three must be present.
- **Compatible with AIH (1 point)** — a picture of chronic hepatitis with lymphocytic infiltration but **without all the features considered typical**.
- **Atypical (0 points)** — showing signs of another diagnosis, such as steatohepatitis.

### Performance (Table 3)

| Cut point | Training sensitivity | Training specificity | Validation sensitivity | Validation specificity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **≥5** | 80% | 99% | 81% | 98% |
| **≥6** | **88%** | **97%** | **88%** | **96%** |
| **≥7** | 68% | 99% | 81% | 99% |

*(rows after exclusion of viral hepatitis; the paper also tabulates the variants before exclusion, which did not differ significantly)*

- **AUC 0.946 training / 0.91 validation** for the final score; 0.987 validation for the score before exclusion of patients with viral hepatitis.
- Excluding patients with viral hepatitis **before** applying the score made **no significant difference** — which is why the viral-exclusion parameter was kept inside the score rather than made a gate.

### Comparison with the old IAIHG score

- **Direct comparison is not possible in this study** — AIH was *defined* by the descriptive IAIHG criteria, so the old score showed **definite AIH in all patients** by construction (above 15 before treatment, above 17 after).
- Against six published studies of the 1993 score: sensitivity **97%–100%**, but **8%–52% of patients with primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC), primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) or other biliary disease scored as probable AIH**, dropping overall specificity to **45%–92%**. The 1999 revision was made to improve exclusion of biliary disease; the simplified score "seems to compare well".

### Limitations the authors state

- **Autoantibody testing is not standardised** between laboratories. **ANA screening on HEp-2 cells gives higher values than tissue sections — if HEp-2 results are used, they should be halved.** ELISA for ANA and SMA is **not sufficiently standardised** for use with this score.
- **Anti-SLA/LP** requires ELISA, immunoblotting or immunoprecipitation — it does not appear on standard immunofluorescence. It was **not tested in 81%** of AIH patients here; included anyway because its specificity is "exquisite" and it is sometimes the only antibody present.
- **Anti-LKM should not be included in the score in a patient with hepatitis C** — LKM occurs in HCV, and all LKM-positive controls here were HCV patients.
- **Histology is a prerequisite.** Women with cirrhosis from steatohepatitis may have elevated gamma-globulins and autoantibodies; histology is what excludes them.
- **Patients with nonalcoholic fatty liver disease and HCV were under-represented** relative to routine practice; the paper says this "might lead to misleadingly high sensitivity and specificity".
- **Drug-induced hepatitis is the hardest differential** and may be indistinguishable — though hypergammaglobulinaemia and highly elevated autoantibodies are rare in drug-induced disease.
- **Wilson disease, hereditary haemochromatosis and alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency** were largely not included, and their biopsies can mimic AIH.
- **Type 2 AIH is systematically overlooked** by the score — those patients usually have **only LKM** antibodies.
- Viral-marker negativity was **documented completely only for hepatitis B and C**; hepatitis A and E, cytomegalovirus, Epstein-Barr virus, herpes simplex virus, parvovirus B19 and adenovirus were not systematically excluded.
- **Overlap syndromes:** distinction between PBC/AIH and PSC/AIH "was not always possible on the basis of this score".

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **The performance figures differ from those [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]] prints.** AASLD 2020 gives the simplified score **95% sensitivity / 90% specificity / 92% accuracy**; this derivation paper gives **88% / 97% (training) and 88% / 96% (validation) at ≥6**. AASLD's figures come from later independent studies, not from this paper. **Tier 1 outranks tier 2, so the guidance's numbers remain the page's headline**; the derivation numbers are recorded here as what the original cohort produced.
- **No contradiction on the score itself** — AASLD 2020 adopts these criteria and cut-points unchanged.
- **The paper does not define "low score"** as a threshold for falling back to the revised 1999 system. AASLD 2020 instructs that fallback without a number, and this paper supplies only the ≥6 and ≥7 cut-points. The band below 6 is simply "not probable AIH"; no numeric trigger for reassessment exists in either document.
- **Cut-point choice is a judgement, not a derivation.** Sensitivity at ≥7 is only **68%** in the training set — nearly a third of AIH patients miss "definite". The paper presents ≥6 and ≥7 as a sensitivity/specificity trade-off rather than deriving an optimum.
