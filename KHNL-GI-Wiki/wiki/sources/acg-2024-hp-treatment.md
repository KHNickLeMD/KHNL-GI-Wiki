## Summary

This ACG guideline covers *H. pylori* treatment in North America — who to test and treat, first-line and salvage regimens, antibiotic susceptibility testing, and test of cure. It notes substantial changes from the 2017 ACG guideline, driven by rising resistance to clarithromycin and levofloxacin and by new options (rifabutin, potassium-competitive acid blockers [PCABs]). It does not review standard diagnostic tests (breath, stool antigen, serology).

For **treatment-naive** patients, **optimized [[bismuth-quadruple-therapy|bismuth quadruple therapy (BQT)]] for 14 days** is the preferred regimen when susceptibility is unknown — the only strong recommendation. Rifabutin triple therapy and [[vonoprazan]]-amoxicillin dual therapy (14 days) are suggested empiric alternatives in patients without penicillin allergy; PCAB-clarithromycin triple therapy is suggested over PPI-clarithromycin triple therapy when clarithromycin susceptibility is unknown. Clarithromycin- and levofloxacin-containing regimens should be avoided without demonstrated susceptibility (Key Concept 3).

For **treatment-experienced** patients, optimized BQT is suggested if not previously given; rifabutin triple therapy after BQT; levofloxacin triple therapy only for a known levofloxacin-sensitive strain when optimized BQT or rifabutin triple has already been used or is unavailable. There is no recommendation for high-dose PPI/PCAB dual therapy or PCAB-clarithromycin triple therapy as salvage (evidence gap). Every treated patient needs a test of cure at least 4 weeks after therapy; probiotics are not supported.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Epidemiology

- North American prevalence **30%–40%**, falling (Key Concept 1); global prevalence >40% (58.2% in 1980–1990 → 43.1% in 2011–2020)
- US seroprevalence 24.8% (2005–2014 laboratory samples), highest (>30%) in the South and Southeast; US Veterans 1999–2018: 25.8% overall (non-Hispanic White 20.1%, Hispanic 36.7%, Black 40.2%)
- Usually acquired in childhood; World Health Organization group I carcinogen; main sequelae dyspepsia, peptic ulcer, gastric adenocarcinoma; also MALT lymphoma, iron deficiency anemia, idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura

### Who to Test and Treat (Table 4)

Treat every patient with active infection on a **non-serological** test, absent contraindications. **Serology is not recommended in low-prevalence populations** without a high pre-test probability (e.g. peptic ulcer).

- Peptic ulcer disease — prior history or active disease
- Marginal zone B-cell lymphoma, mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) type
- **Uninvestigated dyspepsia under age 60**; in populations at high risk for gastric cancer, test and treat at **age 45–50**
- Functional dyspepsia
- Adult household members of a person with a positive non-serological test
- Long-term nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), or starting long-term low-dose aspirin
- Unexplained iron deficiency anemia; idiopathic (autoimmune) thrombocytopenic purpura
- Primary and secondary prevention of gastric adenocarcinoma: current or past gastric premalignant conditions (GPMC: atrophic gastritis, intestinal metaplasia, dysplasia); current or past early gastric cancer resection; current or prior gastric adenocarcinoma; gastric adenomas or hyperplastic polyps; first-degree relative with gastric cancer*; increased gastric-cancer risk (certain non-White racial/ethnic groups, immigrants from high-incidence regions, hereditary cancer syndromes)*; autoimmune gastritis

*\*Decision to test and treat follows shared decision-making.*

**Text detail:**

- Test-and-treat for uninvestigated dyspepsia: age **<60**, no alarm features (vomiting, GI bleeding, unexplained iron deficiency, weight loss), no other endoscopy indication; a threshold of **50** may suit populations at higher gastric-cancer risk
- Functional dyspepsia: eradication beats control for symptom cure (number needed to treat [NNT] 14) and improvement (NNT 9); discuss benefits and risks of antibiotics before testing
- **Eradication suggested in all patients with GPMC and after resected early gastric cancer**; benefit greatest before GPMC develops; endoscopic surveillance still indicated for high-risk GPMC
- Broad *H. pylori* screening for primary gastric-cancer prevention is **not** recommended in the general US population; focused testing in high-risk groups is appropriate
- First-degree relatives: Korean RCT — treatment lowered gastric cancer (hazard ratio [HR] 0.45; 0.27 if eradicated)
- Gastric MALT lymphoma: eradication is primary treatment for early stage; complete remission **75.2%** (95% confidence interval [CI] 70.5–79.9) in a meta-analysis of 61 single-arm studies (2,936 patients)
- Gastric hyperplastic polyps or adenomas → systematic biopsies (e.g. Sydney protocol) for *H. pylori* and surrounding GPMC
- Autoimmune gastritis → test and treat all

### Antibiotic Resistance (Figure 2)

- US meta-analysis, 2,669 strains, 2011–2021: clarithromycin **31.5%**, levofloxacin **37.6%**, metronidazole **42.1%**, tetracycline 0.9%, amoxicillin 2.6%, rifabutin 0.2%; dual metronidazole/clarithromycin 11.7%
- 381 US patients tested December 2019–January 2021 (the US/European RCT population): clarithromycin **22.2%**, amoxicillin 1.2%, metronidazole 69.2%; clarithromycin 15.2%–24.9% and metronidazole 54.5%–73.3% by region
- Text: current US clarithromycin resistance **20%–30%**, levofloxacin approaching **40%** → predicted empiric triple-therapy eradication ≤70%; amoxicillin, tetracycline and rifabutin resistance all <5%
- Known clarithromycin resistance: PPI-clarithromycin triple eradicates fewer than a third of patients

### First-Line Regimens (Table 5, Figure 1)

All four recommended or suggested regimens are given for **14 days**. Concomitant therapy is not among them (Rec 5).

| Regimen | Drugs (doses) | Dosing frequency | FDA-approved | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Optimized bismuth quadruple** | PPI (standard dose) · bismuth subcitrate 120–300 mg or subsalicylate 300 mg · tetracycline 500 mg · metronidazole 500 mg | PPI b.i.d. · bismuth q.i.d. · tetracycline q.i.d. · metronidazole t.i.d. or q.i.d. | No (as individual components) | Strong (moderate quality of evidence) |
| **Rifabutin triple** (Talicia) | Omeprazole 10 mg + amoxicillin 250 mg + rifabutin 12.5 mg per capsule | 4 capsules t.i.d. | Yes | Conditional (low quality of evidence) |
| **PCAB dual** (Voquezna DualPak) | Vonoprazan 20 mg · amoxicillin 1,000 mg | Vonoprazan b.i.d. · amoxicillin **t.i.d.** | Yes | Conditional (moderate quality of evidence) |
| **PCAB triple** (Voquezna TriplePak) | Vonoprazan 20 mg · clarithromycin 500 mg · amoxicillin 1,000 mg | All b.i.d. | Yes | Conditional (moderate quality of evidence) |

- **Optimized** BQT = appropriately dosed PPI, bismuth, nitroimidazole **1,500–2,000 mg/day in divided doses**, and tetracycline — **doxycycline is not a substitute**. PPIs dosed 30–60 min before a meal. Salicylate allergy → no bismuth subsalicylate.
- Talicia total daily doses: omeprazole 120 mg, amoxicillin 3,000 mg, rifabutin 150 mg.
- PCAB triple is FDA-approved for empiric first-line use independent of resistance testing, but the guideline states clarithromycin-containing regimens **should be reserved for demonstrated clarithromycin-sensitive strains**.
- **Clarithromycin- and levofloxacin-containing regimens** should be avoided without demonstrated macrolide/quinolone susceptibility (Key Concept 3).
- **Figure 1 (empiric first line, no susceptibility testing):** no penicillin allergy → optimized BQT, rifabutin triple, PCAB dual, or PCAB-clarithromycin triple (avoid the last after previous macrolide exposure); **penicillin allergy → optimized BQT** only (may need formal allergy testing)
- Penicillin allergy where BQT is not appropriate → allergist referral to confirm and consider desensitization (<1% of the population has true type 1 IgE-mediated penicillin allergy)
- Histamine-2 receptor antagonists (H2RAs) are not recommended in eradication regimens

**BQT details:**

- Pylera (bismuth subcitrate + metronidazole + tetracycline) is FDA-approved with omeprazole but dispensed only as a 10-day regimen; Helidac (bismuth subsalicylate + metronidazole + tetracycline) is a 14-day pack originally approved with an H2RA
- US single-center data: 14-day BQT eradicated **87%** of 585 patients; 10 days → 77% (135 patients); doxycycline in place of tetracycline → 70% (14 days) / 67% (10 days)
- BQT recommended over PPI-clarithromycin triple therapy (network meta-analysis of Western trials); needs no pretreatment susceptibility testing; contains no amoxicillin
- Disadvantages: pill burden, frequent minor (mainly GI) adverse events, tetracycline access and cost, tetracycline relative contraindications (photosensitivity, women of childbearing potential). Taiwanese RCT: >50% had ≥1 adverse effect (dark stool, fatigue, nausea, diarrhea, dizziness each >15%), only ~5% discontinued

**Rifabutin triple details:**

- Talicia is the only FDA-approved rifabutin regimen and the only one evaluated as first-line; its trials gave rifabutin 50 mg every 8 hours × 14 days, which keeps intragastric rifabutin above the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC90) longer than 150 mg once or twice daily or 300 mg once daily
- Its two registration trials compared it with omeprazole-amoxicillin dual therapy and with placebo — **not** with PPI-clarithromycin triple therapy or BQT
- Advantages: very low rifabutin and amoxicillin resistance; no clarithromycin, so no pretreatment testing. Myelotoxicity not reported at 150 mg/day

**PCAB (vonoprazan) details:**

- PCABs block the H⁺/K⁺-ATPase by a different mechanism; antisecretory effect more rapid, robust and prolonged than standard-dose PPIs, holding intragastric pH above 6 for longer; vonoprazan can be taken fed or fasting
- High intragastric pH promotes *H. pylori* replication (more susceptible to bactericidal antibiotics) and stabilizes acid-labile clarithromycin and amoxicillin
- Vonoprazan is the only PCAB studied in the US for *H. pylori*; Voquezna DualPak and TriplePak FDA-approved in 2022
- **US/European RCT (14 days):** vonoprazan-amoxicillin dual noninferior to lansoprazole-clarithromycin triple in clarithromycin-sensitive strains (**79% vs 79%**, modified intention-to-treat [mITT]); superior in clarithromycin-resistant strains (70% vs 32%) and overall (77% vs 69%). Vonoprazan-clarithromycin triple: noninferior in sensitive strains; superior in resistant strains (66% vs 32%) and overall (81% vs 69%); diarrhea 4%, dysgeusia 4%. ~90% of participants White, under half US-enrolled
- Asian RCTs and meta-analyses: vonoprazan-based clarithromycin triple noninferior or superior to PPI-based triple; a network meta-analysis of 13 Western RCTs ranked vonoprazan-based triple most efficacious (72% probability)
- High-dose PPI dual (omeprazole 40 mg + amoxicillin 1 g, each three times daily) in treatment-naive patients: only **57.7%** (mITT)

**CYP2C19 and PPI choice:**

- Omeprazole, lansoprazole and pantoprazole are extensively metabolized by CYP2C19; rapid/ultrarapid metabolizers have **2.5–4.4-fold** higher eradication failure with those PPIs (no association with rabeprazole or esomeprazole)
- No CYP2C19 testing solely to pick an acid suppressant; known rapid/ultrarapid genotype → **esomeprazole or rabeprazole preferred**, or raise the dose of another PPI, or use a PCAB-based regimen (PCABs are not CYP2C19-metabolized)

**Concomitant therapy (Rec 5):** PPI + clarithromycin + amoxicillin + metronidazole b.i.d. for 5–14 days; meta-analysis of 6 non-US studies (1,810 patients) — no significant difference vs BQT overall (87.4% vs 85.2%), small advantage for BQT in low-bias studies (88.2% vs 84.5%) → not suggested over BQT

### Test of Cure (Key Concept 4)

- **All** treated patients: urea breath test, fecal antigen test, or biopsy-based test **at least 4 weeks after** completing therapy
- **Stop PPIs 2 weeks** before breath or stool testing; probably also PCABs (not adequately studied). Standard-dose H2RAs and antacids do not affect accuracy. **Avoid bismuth and antibiotics for at least 4 weeks** before testing
- Serology should not be used to establish post-treatment status (antibodies persist months to years)
- Endoscopy needed shortly after treatment (e.g. large gastric ulcer, MALT lymphoma) → histology and/or biopsy urease test; same drug holds apply. A positive biopsy urease test may suffice to show infection, but a negative one alone is not proof of eradication
- Test everyone, including dyspeptic patients, regardless of post-treatment symptoms
- After documented eradication, US recurrence is ~**1% per year** (95% CI 0.3%–3%); Alaska Natives >8%
- Monitor local results; first-line eradication **below ~85%** → consider resistance testing and/or a different empiric regimen
- US post-treatment testing rose from <25% (2005) to 60%–80% (2019)

### Salvage Regimens (Table 6, Figures 3 and 4)

"Second-line" = after 1 failed course; "third-line" = after 2; "salvage" = any treatment after initial failure. **There are no FDA-approved regimens for persistent infection; all regimens are given for 14 days.**

| Regimen | Drugs (doses) | Frequency | Susceptibility testing required? | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Optimized BQT | PPI (standard dose) · bismuth subcitrate 120–300 mg or subsalicylate 300 mg · tetracycline 500 mg · metronidazole 500 mg | b.i.d. · q.i.d. · q.i.d. · t.i.d. or q.i.d. | No | Conditional (very low quality of evidence) |
| Rifabutin triple | PPI (standard to double dose) · amoxicillin 1,000 mg · rifabutin 50–300 mg | b.i.d. · b.i.d. or t.i.d. · q.d., b.i.d., or Talicia 50 mg t.i.d. | No | Conditional (low quality of evidence) |
| Levofloxacin triple | PPI (standard dose) · levofloxacin 500 mg · amoxicillin 1,000 mg or metronidazole 500 mg | b.i.d. · q.d. · b.i.d. | Yes | Conditional (low quality of evidence) |
| PCAB triple (Voquezna TriplePak) | Vonoprazan 20 mg · clarithromycin 500 mg · amoxicillin 1,000 mg | b.i.d. | Yes | No recommendation (evidence gap) |
| High-dose dual | Vonoprazan 20 mg or PPI (double dose) · amoxicillin 1,000 mg | b.i.d. or t.i.d. · t.i.d. | No | No recommendation (evidence gap) |

*Footnotes:* salicylate allergy → no bismuth subsalicylate; doxycycline not a substitute for tetracycline; Pylera acceptable but only a 10-day product; Helidac with a b.i.d. PPI for 14 days is appropriate. Rifabutin: optimal salvage dosing unclear, 50 mg tablets not commercially available, Talicia (50 mg t.i.d.) not evaluated as salvage — if prescribing separately, rifabutin **150 mg b.i.d. or 300 mg q.d.** Levofloxacin: black-box warning (tendonitis, tendon rupture); only for confirmed levofloxacin-sensitive infection; metronidazole replaces amoxicillin in true penicillin allergy. PCAB triple: not evaluated as salvage — not after macrolide exposure, not without a demonstrated clarithromycin-sensitive strain. High-dose dual: high-potency acid suppression + high-dose amoxicillin; insufficient North American evidence.

- **Figure 3 (empiric salvage, no susceptibility testing):** after PPI-clarithromycin triple **or** after nonoptimized BQT — no penicillin allergy → optimized BQT, rifabutin triple, or high-dose PPI/PCAB dual (the last only when optimized BQT or rifabutin triple is not an option and susceptibility testing is unavailable); penicillin allergy → optimized BQT. Lists are options, not a hierarchy
- **Figure 4 (susceptibility-guided salvage):** clarithromycin- and levofloxacin-sensitive → clarithromycin triple (PPI or PCAB), optimized BQT, rifabutin triple, levofloxacin triple; clarithromycin-resistant / levofloxacin-sensitive → optimized BQT, rifabutin triple, levofloxacin triple; clarithromycin-sensitive / levofloxacin-resistant → clarithromycin triple, optimized BQT, rifabutin triple; both resistant → optimized BQT, rifabutin triple, high-dose PPI/PCAB dual (only when optimized BQT or rifabutin triple is not an option). Levofloxacin only when other options are inappropriate; also weigh prior treatments
- **Optimized BQT (text):** bismuth **300 mg four times daily at least**, metronidazole **1.5–2 g/day in 3–4 doses**, tetracycline 500 mg four times daily, standard-dose PPI twice daily, for 10 to (preferably) 14 days. Non-optimized prior BQT (duration <10 days, metronidazole <1.5–2 g/day, doxycycline, inadequate PPI) → repeating BQT with optimization is acceptable — the only exception to not repeating a failed regimen
- BQT 10–14 days as second line: pooled 78.8% eradication (vs 67.8% for 7 days); combination-capsule BQT outperformed separately prescribed components in European data
- US claims data: >50% of patients needing second-line treatment got PPI-clarithromycin triple again, without susceptibility testing
- **Rifabutin triple after BQT (Rec 8):** one Chinese RCT (364 patients after ≥2 failures) — rifabutin 150 mg + amoxicillin 1 g + esomeprazole 20 mg, each twice daily, noninferior to BQT (ITT 89.0% vs 89.6%) with fewer adverse events; European pooled rifabutin triple 72.2% (second line) to 79.6% (third line) ITT. Text suggests rifabutin **50 mg three times daily, or 150 mg twice daily if Talicia is unavailable**; 150 mg once daily performed worse; higher doses carry dose-dependent myelosuppression risk. Rifabutin is on the World Health Organization watch/reserve lists
- **Clarithromycin triple as salvage (Key Concept 5, expert consensus):** only with confirmed clarithromycin sensitivity; the only scenario for repeating clarithromycin triple after it failed is documented clarithromycin and amoxicillin sensitivity with room to optimize acid suppression (e.g. esomeprazole or rabeprazole, or a PCAB)
- **Levofloxacin triple:** pooled 81.1% eradication with susceptible strains vs 36.3% with resistant; predicted efficacy ~72% / 68% / 63% / <59% at levofloxacin resistance of 20% / 30% / 40% / >50%; 14 days better than 10 days (87.1% vs 72.2%); high-dose (>500 mg) no better than ≤500 mg
- **High-dose dual therapy (Rec 11):** may be considered in selected scenarios — optimized BQT or rifabutin triple not an option, susceptibility testing unavailable or unhelpful, or a strain sensitive only to amoxicillin

### Antibiotic Susceptibility Testing (Key Concept 6)

- Phenotypic (culture) or genotypic (PCR, next-generation sequencing [NGS]) methods; **no universally accepted international gold standard**; culture is not widely used in the US (workflow complexity, low culture success, uncertain incremental value)
- Molecular testing suits clarithromycin and levofloxacin, where a few mutations explain almost all resistance; PCR works on gastric biopsies or stool (meta-analysis of 11 studies, 592 patients: stool PCR accurately determined clarithromycin resistance); stool NGS 92% concordant with gastric-biopsy NGS; NGS yields DNA from >90% of samples
- Tailored vs empiric first-line therapy: small advantage (risk ratio 1.15 and 1.14 in two meta-analyses) but **none when the empiric regimen is BQT** or a nonbismuth quadruple → with BQT first line and local eradication **>85%**, no testing needed before first-line treatment
- Treatment-experienced: meta-analyses show no clear benefit of testing before second- or third-line therapy (few, low-quality studies); a large third-line study found tailored therapy similar between molecular and culture testing, both better than historical empiric controls (88% vs 78%)
- Expert consensus: use testing **whenever the choice of therapy remains unclear** after considering prior *H. pylori* treatment, past antibiotic (especially macrolide and quinolone) exposure, and penicillin allergy; testing is advised **before regimens containing clarithromycin or levofloxacin** (largely expert opinion)

### Probiotics (Rec 12)

- Network meta-analysis (40 studies, 8,924 patients): modest eradication gain (RR 1.10) and fewer side effects (RR 0.47), heterogeneous, mostly Asian; no high-quality North American RCTs → hypothesis-generating only

**Key concepts (Table 3 — ungraded):**

1. North American prevalence is falling but remains 30–40%; typically acquired in childhood; higher among non-White races and ethnicities, crowded or poor sanitary conditions, and early-generation immigrants from endemic countries.
2. When to **test for** and when to **treat** *H. pylori* are a **single decision**, not two.
3. Clarithromycin- and levofloxacin-containing regimens should be **avoided without demonstrated** macrolide and quinolone susceptibility, respectively.
4. **All** treated patients should have a **test of cure** — urea breath test, fecal antigen, or biopsy-based — **at least 4 weeks after** completing therapy.
5. In treatment-experienced patients with persistent infection **confirmed clarithromycin-sensitive**, PPI- or PCAB-clarithromycin triple therapy **is suggested**.
6. Phenotypic (culture) and molecular (PCR, next-generation sequencing) susceptibility testing are increasingly available in the US; the incremental benefit of tailored over empiric therapy **remains inadequately defined**. By expert consensus, use susceptibility testing whenever the choice of therapy remains unclear after accounting for prior *H. pylori* treatments, past antibiotic exposure generally, and documented penicillin allergy.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Rifabutin salvage dose:** the Table 6 footnote suggests 150 mg b.i.d. or 300 mg q.d. when prescribed separately; the Rec 8 text suggests 50 mg t.i.d., or 150 mg b.i.d. if Talicia is unavailable
- **Bismuth dose:** Tables 5 and 6 list subcitrate 120–300 mg or subsalicylate 300 mg q.i.d.; the Rec 6 text defines optimized BQT as bismuth "300 mg 4 times daily at least"
- **PCAB-clarithromycin triple as salvage:** Table 6 makes no recommendation (evidence gap), while Key Concept 5 suggests PPI- or PCAB-clarithromycin triple when persistent infection is confirmed clarithromycin-sensitive
- Rifabutin triple: the only head-to-head comparison with BQT in treatment-experienced patients is one Chinese RCT; no RCT has compared it with BQT or PPI-clarithromycin triple as first line
- Incremental benefit of susceptibility-guided over empiric therapy remains inadequately defined (Key Concept 6); which method (culture, PCR, NGS) gives the best eradication at lowest cost is unknown
- PCAB substitution for the PPI in BQT or rifabutin triple has not been studied
- Research gaps named: who in North America benefits from opportunistic test-and-treat for gastric-cancer prevention; North American comparisons of the new regimens; CYP2C19-tailored acid suppression
