## Summary

- Update of the 2003, 2011 and 2016 multisociety position statements; drafted 2018/2019 by an ASGE Quality Assurance in Endoscopy subcommittee plus Standards of Practice committee members, then redistributed to endorsing organizations for consensus (Appendix 2, *Endorsing organizations*, p. 33.e4).
- Scope: reprocessing of flexible gastrointestinal (GI) endoscopes by high-level disinfection (HLD), plus training, unit layout, accessories, maintenance, loaner endoscopes and infection-control leadership (Aims and Objectives, p. 13).
- **Method:** 4 clinical questions by GRADE (repeat HLD, ethylene oxide (EtO) sterilization, maximum storage time, microbiologic surveillance); 18 further questions by literature review (Table 1, p. 13). Literature search 1960 to March 31, 2018; 770 citations (Appendix 1, p. 33.e1).
- **Strength vocabulary:** strong or conditional recommendation; statements sit under "Recommend." or "Suggest." headings, but the 6 conditional GRADE statements are printed under "Recommend." headings, so the heading does not mark strength — read each statement's own label; four quality-of-evidence categories, from high down to very low (Appendix 1, p. 33.e2; Supplementary Tables 1–2).
- Contains expanded detail on the critical reprocessing steps of **cleaning and drying** (Introduction, p. 11).
- Key new evidence: in nonoutbreak settings repeat HLD does not lower duodenoscope contamination versus single HLD, and limited data suggest EtO sterilization does not either; EtO has terminated duodenoscope outbreaks; storage <7 days had lower contamination with any organism, but no maximum storage time can be set.
- Table 2 (pp. 14–17) lists the full recommendation set; the body text adds 5 conditional statements ("Suggest") not repeated in Table 2.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Spaulding classification and levels of disinfection

*Figure 1 and text, pp. 11–13 (figure redrawn as a table; device photographs omitted)*

| Patient contact | Device class | Reprocessing (Figure 1) | Examples (text) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intact skin | **Non-critical** — does not ordinarily touch the patient, or touches only intact skin | Low-level disinfection; intermediate-level disinfection (text: low-level disinfection/manual cleaning) | Stethoscopes, blood pressure cuffs, mouth guards |
| Mucous membranes; non-intact skin | **Semi-critical** — contacts intact mucous membranes or nonintact skin, does not penetrate sterile tissue | **High-level disinfection** (text: should be sterilized, but HLD acceptable if sterilization not feasible; HLD at a minimum) | Flexible GI endoscope (pictured in Figure 1; text p. 20: flexible GI endoscopes get at least HLD) |
| Sterile areas of the body; vascular system | **Critical** — enters the vascular system or sterile tissue | **Sterilization** (destruction of all microbial life) | Endoscopes used in sterile settings (laparoscopic endoscopy); devices for invasive procedures such as endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) and interventional EUS; biopsy, polypectomy and sphincterotomy equipment |

| Level | What it eliminates (p. 12) |
|---|---|
| Sterilization | All forms of viable organisms |
| High-level disinfection | All vegetative microorganisms, mycobacteria, small/nonlipid and medium/lipid viruses, fungal spores; **some bacterial spores and prions may remain** |
| Intermediate-level | Vegetative organisms incl. *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, all fungi, most viruses; does not kill bacterial spores |
| Low-level | Some viruses, most vegetative bacteria, some fungi; may not remove bacterial spores or tubercle bacilli |
| Cleaning | Removes residual organic material and bacterial burden, interior and exterior |

- Some authors propose reclassifying duodenoscopes and echoendoscopes as critical ("directly or secondarily enter sterile tissue"); the guideline **does not advocate reclassification**, because the GI tract is not sterile and sterility is broken on entry (pp. 12–13).

### Duodenoscope contamination and FDA actions

*Text, p. 21*

- No transmission linked to standard (end-viewing) endoscopes when all reprocessing steps are followed; outbreaks linked to duodenoscopes despite appropriate compliance.
- FDA has advised that units using duodenoscopes add **1 or more supplemental measures**: microbiologic culturing, EtO sterilization, liquid chemical sterilant processing system, or repeat HLD. Nearly 90% of U.S. units implemented 1 or more; data on their effectiveness are scant.
- FDA postmarket surveillance: **.3% to 4.4%** of duodenoscopes contaminated with low/moderate-concern organisms; **4.1% to 6.1%** with high-concern organisms.
- Contributing factors: complex distal end, inaccessible channels, prolonged storage in noncontrolled environments, human factors, damage, waterborne pathogens from rinse water.

### Repeat HLD and EtO sterilization — evidence

*Text and Figure 2, pp. 21–22*

| Comparison (nonoutbreak) | Evidence | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Repeat HLD (1 manual cleaning + 2 AER HLD cycles) vs single HLD | 2 randomized controlled trials: Snyder (single center; stopped early for futility) and Bartles (45 duodenoscopes and linear echoendoscopes, 2925 encounters, 4 hospitals, 6 months) | Pooled odds ratio (OR) **.92** (95% confidence interval (CI), .72–1.18) — no reduction in contamination |
| EtO sterilization vs single HLD | 1 trial (Snyder; 3 arms) | No significant difference; contamination with nonpathogenic organisms numerically higher in the EtO arm |
| Repeat manual cleaning **and** repeat HLD | 1 duodenoscope study; 1 single-arm study (Rex) | 1-log reduction in culture positivity; Rex: positive cultures .3% (known pathogens), 4.9% (low-pathogenicity organisms), no transmission — does not eliminate contamination |

- **Outbreaks:** systematic review — **6 of 17** studies of duodenoscope-attributed carbapenem-resistant *Enterobacteriaceae* (CRE)/multidrug-resistant organism (MDRO) infections used EtO during the outbreak; in at least 3 and possibly 6 it produced absence of culture positivity at all sites and stopped the outbreak (p. 22).
- EtO drawbacks: costly, inefficient, potential toxicity to staff and communities, concerns about endoscope performance and durability, not widely available → **selective sterilization of duodenoscopes used in high-risk patients** (eg, colonized with CRE/MDRO or at risk of such infection after ERCP) may be more feasible (p. 22).

### Precleaning, leak testing, manual cleaning, inspection

*Text, pp. 18–20*

- Sequence after point-of-use precleaning and transport (closed, "Biohazard"-labeled container): **leak test → manual cleaning and rinsing → inspection → HLD** (p. 18).
- Endoscopes **failing a leak test**: removed from use after reprocessing per original manufacturer IFU; clinical team informed (p. 19).
- Delayed cleaning example: after-hours emergency procedures → follow manufacturers' delayed-processing directions (p. 19).
- Extra manual steps may be needed for duodenoscope elevator channels (brushing forceps elevator and elevator recess, repeated detergent rinses) and the echoendoscope balloon groove (p. 19).
- Systematic manual cleaning lowers pathogen counts by **99.9%** (p. 19).
- Visible debris on the exterior after manual cleaning → repeat manual cleaning (p. 19).
- Borescope inspection after reprocessing found channel damage (86%–100%), debris (22%–96%) and fluid droplets (8%–95%); clinical significance unclear, no microbiologic correlation, interpretation not standardized (pp. 19–20).

### Drying

*Text, pp. 22–23*

- Improper drying: **80%** endoscope contamination rate; linked to outbreaks of waterborne organisms. Nearly 10% of units report not drying; less than half use forced-air drying (p. 23).
- Sequence: exterior with lint-free cloth → high-efficiency particulate air–filtered medical or instrument air through all channels.
- **Ineffective:** vertical hanging alone without prior/concurrent filtered-air flushing; a manual forced-air pistol directed into several areas at varying time points.
- **Sufficient for internal working channels:** continuous air via dedicated drying devices for **a minimum of 10 minutes**, or via storage cabinets for **a minimum of 1 hour**.
- Some European guidelines require only removal of major water residue if reuse is immediate or within a short period (ie, within 3 hours); several U.S. guidelines require complete drying after every cycle — this guideline requires **complete drying before use**, because moisture drives bacterial growth and reuse timing is uncertain (p. 23).
- **Alcohol:** 70% to 90% ethyl or isopropyl alcohol through working channels is a common preliminary drying step (often an AER terminal cycle before a brief air purge); benefits = purging/evaporation of residual water and intrinsic antimicrobial effect; concern = protein fixation could retain organisms; no data strongly support or refute it (p. 23).

### Storage and hang time

*Text and Figures 3–4, pp. 23–25*

- Passive cabinets without airflow into all channels are **not sufficient to dry an endoscope from a wet state**; drying cabinets force air through each channel, so endoscopes can be stored vertically or horizontally (p. 24).
- Cabinets: secure location near procedure rooms; maintained per manufacturer; routinely inspected for damage and cleaned (and when soiled) with an Environmental Protection Agency–registered hospital disinfectant (p. 24).
- After automated drying, no difference in channel fluid droplets between vertical and horizontal storage → vertical storage in a conventional cabinet may not be necessary (p. 24).
- Reusable devices that withstand sterilization (removable valves, biopsy channel caps): if sterilized, co-location or tracking/labeling not needed; no evidence that disposable buttons/valves reduce infection (p. 24).

| Storage-time comparison | OR (95% CI) | Organisms counted |
|---|---|---|
| <7 days vs ≥7 days | **.15 (.06–.33)** | Any (pathogenic and nonpathogenic) |
| <7 days vs ≥14 days | **.43 (.19–.99)** | Any |
| <7 days vs ≥7 days | **.50 (.13–1.97)** — no difference | Pathogenic only |

*The guideline reviewed 8 storage-time studies (1242 procedures, storage range 1–56 days); each pooled OR comes from a subset: <7 vs ≥7 days any organism, 4 studies (Figure 3); <7 vs ≥14 days, 2 studies; pathogens only, 2 studies (Figure 4). Text and Figures 3–4, pp. 24–26.*

- Most frequent isolate: nonpathogenic coagulase-negative *Staphylococcus* (p. 25).
- Conclusion (p. 25): reuse **within 21 and perhaps even 56 days appears safe**, but data are insufficient for a maximal outer duration.

### Microbiologic surveillance and adenosine triphosphate testing

*Text, pp. 25–26*

- Surveillance culturing is not advised in current U.S. standards but is advised by several international organizations; limits = delayed feedback, cost and rigor, frequent environmental isolates, variable protocols; several outbreak sites could not culture the transmitted organism from the implicated duodenoscopes.
- FDA, CDC and American Society for Microbiology have issued detailed sampling/culturing guidance; **"culture and quarantine"** of duodenoscopes at some U.S. units yielded fewer culture-positive duodenoscopes and ended transmission during ERCP.
- Cultures remain **the most reliable indicator of residual contamination** (used in FDA-ordered postmarket studies).
- **Adenosine triphosphate (ATP)** testing (cleaning adequacy, before HLD):
  - Relative light unit (RLU) **<200** (3M Clean Trace) associated with "clean" benchmarks of <6.4 mg/cm² protein, <2.2 mg/cm² hemoglobin and <4-log10 colony-forming units/cm² bioburden (simulated duodenoscope study; validated clinically in colonoscopes and duodenoscopes); other studies suggest **<100** RLU for colonoscopes.
  - After manual cleaning of duodenoscopes vs terminal cultures: sensitivity **30%**, specificity **53%**.
  - **FDA advises against ATP testing for duodenoscope surveillance** (no sponsor has validated test strips or obtained FDA clearance for this use).
  - Role: training, competency testing, spot surveillance of cleaning steps before and after HLD.

### Unit layout, accessories, water, simethicone

*Text, Appendix 2, pp. 33.e2–33.e4*

- Separate dedicated rooms for manual cleaning ("dirty" room) and disinfection ("clean" room), in close proximity; **1.5 to 2:1 ratio of AERs to procedure rooms** advocated for efficiency; unidirectional or circular patient/staff flow (p. 33.e2).
- FDA backflow-valve logic: distal components (patient side, incl. the backflow-prevention valve) replaced/reprocessed before reuse; proximal components (water-bottle side, excl. the valve) can be used for multiple patients in 1 day. Suction valves allow 2-way flow when open, so the vacuum tubing/canister interval is unresolved (p. 33.e3).
- Water: no increased bacterial growth in water bottles or clinical adverse events with tap vs sterile water; but **sterile water in irrigation bottles is recommended for immunosuppressed patients (eg, liver transplant)**; sterile water should be considered the primary water-bottle source, especially in nonsterile areas or where mucosa is traversed or penetrated (p. 33.e3).
- Simethicone droplets may persist in working channels despite adequate HLD and in waterjets; a lower concentration (.5%) is no different from sterile water alone; droplet differences at higher concentrations disappear with dual reprocessing; more droplets when delivered via water bottle/irrigation jet than when flushed only through the working channel (p. 33.e4).
- Single-use accessories: discard immediately after the procedure, never reprocess (p. 33.e4).

### Maintenance, loaners, leadership, new technology

*Text, pp. 26–29*

- Endoscope with suboptimal performance or overt defect: **immediately removed from service** and tagged until cleared by clinical/bioengineering staff; repair and maintenance log entry every time equipment goes out (model, serial number, service company, date, reason) or returns (date, visual inspection, function test, reprocessed per policy) (pp. 26–27).
- Leadership team: endoscopists, endoscopy nurses and infection preventionists, possibly reprocessing staff, risk management, frontline staff and hospital leaders; an infection control champion runs education, change management and outbreak management (pp. 27–28).
- **Single-use endoscopes:** FDA-cleared for colonoscopy and ERCP; single-use duodenoscopes performed similarly to reusable ones in anatomic models but with lower image quality; FDA recommended transition to duodenoscopes with **disposable components (incl. distal endcaps) or fully disposable duodenoscopes** when available (p. 29).
- **Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19):** manual cleaning and HLD eliminate nearly all microorganisms and detergents inactivate coronaviruses incl. severe acute respiratory syndrome–coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2); **no specific reprocessing changes needed** during the pandemic (p. 29).
- Table 3 (p. 28) lists research gaps, incl. maximum storage time, required air-drying duration, alcohol flushes, ATP accuracy, liquid chemical sterilant systems vs HLD AERs, safe simethicone concentration, disposable endoscopes, and repeat culturing after EtO sterilization in MDRO patients.

- **[[endoscope-reprocessing]]** — supersedes the 2016 multisociety guideline ([[mstf-2016-endoscope-reprocessing]]) as the current U.S. standard: full 2021 recommendation set with GRADE strength/quality, the drying parameters (≥10 minutes medical air; 1 hour in a drying cabinet), vertical-or-horizontal storage, the storage-time meta-analysis, repeat HLD and EtO conclusions, water-bottle/tubing/valve intervals, simethicone limits, loaner and leadership requirements, and the expanded reporting chain (patient first).

## Contradictions / Open Questions

**What changed from the 2016 update** ([[mstf-2016-endoscope-reprocessing]]; Petersen et al, *Gastrointest Endosc* 2017;85:282–294). This 2021 guideline is the newer U.S. guideline and wins wherever they differ.

| Topic | 2016 update (recommendation no., category) | 2021 guideline |
|---|---|---|
| Grading | 41 numbered recommendations, CDC categories (IA, IB, IC, II, NR) | Unnumbered; GRADE (strong/conditional × quality of evidence) |
| Manual cleaning timing | Within the manufacturer's recommended time frame; no number (#5, IB) | Same, plus **"usually within 60 minutes"** after release from the procedure (Strong, Low) |
| AER use | AER "advisable and should be adopted when feasible"; elevator wire channel "not effectively disinfected by **most** AERs" (#16, IB) | **HLD should be performed in an AER** (Strong, Moderate); elevator wire channel "may not be effectively disinfected by **some** AERs" |
| Alcohol flush | Flush channels with 70% to 90% ethyl or isopropyl alcohol, then filtered forced air (#20, IA) | **Follow manufacturers' IFU** on alcohol (Strong, Moderate); no data strongly support or refute it |
| Transport of soiled endoscopes | Enclosed, labeled containers through corridors; **an open container can suffice for transport to immediately adjacent reprocessing rooms** (#3, II) | **Fully enclosed**, puncture-resistant, leak-proof, labeled containers — no adjacent-room exception (Strong, Low) |
| Disinfectant concentration testing and reuse life | Stand-alone: test liquid HLD for minimum effective concentration at the start of each day of use (#28, IA); discard at end of reuse life (#29, IB) | Placed under **"If manual HLD is performed (ie, the AER is undergoing repairs)"** (row 37); the guideline does not explain the change |
| Drying duration | Not quantified | Medical air through all channels, ideally simultaneously, **≥10 minutes**; complete drying before use |
| Storage position | Hang vertically to facilitate drying (#22, II) | **Vertical or horizontal** per validated cabinet design (Strong, Moderate); if vertical, not coiled, not touching cabinet bottom |
| Hang time | NR: reuse within 21 days appears safe; no maximum (#25) | Conditional, Moderate: still no maximum; unit sets its own policy; new pooled data favor <7 days for any-organism contamination; 21 and perhaps 56 days appear safe |
| Duodenoscope supplemental measures | Consider 1 or more FDA-suggested modalities (culture, EtO, repeat HLD, liquid chemical germicide) (#24, II) | Repeat HLD does not reduce, and limited data suggest EtO does not reduce, duodenoscope contamination in nonoutbreak settings; routine EtO for all endoscopes not recommended; EtO considered in outbreaks (all Conditional) |
| Microbiologic testing | NR for routine testing (#36); if done, per **CDC** guidance (#37, II) | Conditional, Moderate: not established but most recognized technique; if done, per **FDA and CDC** guidance |
| Water bottle | HLD or sterilize bottle and tube at least daily; **sterile water should fill the bottle** (#26, IB) | Daily HLD/sterilization or replacement; sterile water **for mucosa-traversing procedures**; otherwise manufacturer IFU or unit risk assessment of sterile vs tap water |
| Eyewash stations | "Should **not** be placed on sinks used for washing or soaking soiled endoscopes" (#30, IB and IC) | "Placed **near** sinks used for washing or soaking soiled endoscopes" (Strong, Low) |
| Infection reporting | Institution, public health agency, FDA, manufacturer(s) (#41, IB and IC) | Adds **the patient who underwent the procedure** as the first recipient |
| 2016 recommendations with no 2021 counterpart | Ultrasonic cleaning of accessories (#9, II); semicritical endoscopes get at least HLD after each patient use (#10, IA — 2021 states it in text only, p. 20); avoid a disinfectant the endoscope manufacturer warns against (#13, second sentence, IB); new disinfectants chosen by FDA clearance (#14, II); damaged endoscopes and accessories removed from use (#21, last sentence, II — 2021 text only, p. 26); nonimmersible GI endoscopes should not be used (#15, IB); infection prevention staff review FDA advisories and manufacturer alerts (#19, II); chemical-hazard education (#33, IC); personal protective equipment (#34, IC); aseptic medication administration (#39, IC) | Not restated as recommendations (the 2021 text says prior recommendations on personal protective equipment, vaccinations and universal precautions should be followed, p. 18) |
| Recommendation topics new in 2021 | — | Simethicone (lowest concentration ≤.5%, via working channel); borescopes (no recommendation); loaner-endoscope rules; maintenance/repair rules; leadership team; backflow valve per procedure, vacuum tubing and canisters at least daily; cabinets not in procedure rooms |

- **Wording conflict on eyewash placement:** the 2016 text keeps eyewash stations off soiled-endoscope sinks; the 2021 Table 2 and Appendix 2 text both say "near". The 2021 wording governs; the guideline does not comment on the change.
- **Hang time:** data favor shorter storage for any-organism contamination but show no difference for pathogenic organisms; no maximum is set (Table 3 lists it as a research gap).
- **Endorser lists:** this guideline's Appendix 2 list of "2016 Endorsing Organizations" omits the American Association for the Study of Liver Disease, which the 2016 document itself lists among its endorsers; the erratum's 2021 list includes it.
- **Table 2 vs text:** 5 conditional "Suggest" statements (S1–S5) appear only in the body text. Table 2 reads "routine use of ethylene sterilization" where the text reads "routine use of EtO sterilization" — same meaning.
