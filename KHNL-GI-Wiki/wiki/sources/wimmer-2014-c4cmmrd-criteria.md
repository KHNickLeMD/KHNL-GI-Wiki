## Summary

The paper that defines **who gets tested for constitutional mismatch repair deficiency (CMMRD)** — the syndrome the US literature usually calls **biallelic mismatch repair deficiency (BMMRD)**. Its contribution is a **three-point scoring system** that converts an unweighted list of clinical clues into a testing threshold: **≥3 points in a cancer patient is an indication for CMMRD testing.**

The structure matters as much as the points. The table has **two blocks**. The **malignancy/premalignancy block is mandatory** — at least one of its items must be present, so **no combination of "additional features" alone reaches the threshold**; a patient with no qualifying tumour is not scored. The **additional-features block is optional** and additive. Within each block, **if more than one item is present, the points add**.

The consortium's framing: CMMRD is under-recognized because the index tumours (childhood brain tumours, T-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma, early colorectal and small-bowel carcinoma, adenomatous polyposis without an *APC*/*MUTYH* mutation) are each rare, and the neurofibromatosis-like skin findings send families down the wrong diagnostic path. **PMS2 biallelic mutations predominate**, and because PMS2 heterozygotes have low penetrance, the family history is usually unremarkable.

**The "LS spectrum" is defined in the table's own footnote** — colorectal, endometrial, small bowel, ureter, renal pelvis, biliary tract, stomach and bladder carcinoma — and that definition governs four of the scored items.

## Key Findings / Claims

### The C4CMMRD scoring system (Table 5) — indication for CMMRD testing: **≥3 points**

**Malignancies / premalignancies — one is mandatory; if more than one is present, add the points**

| Feature | Points |
|---|---|
| Carcinoma from the **Lynch syndrome (LS) spectrum\*** at age **<25 years** | **3** |
| **Multiple bowel adenomas at age <25 years with absence of *APC*/*MUTYH* mutation(s)**, OR a **single high-grade dysplasia adenoma at age <25 years** | **3** |
| **World Health Organization (WHO) grade III or IV glioma at age <25 years** | **2** |
| **Non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL) of T-cell lineage** or **supratentorial primitive neuroectodermal tumour (sPNET)** at age **<18 years** | **2** |
| **Any malignancy at age <18 years** | **1** |

**Additional features — optional; if more than one is present, add the points**

| Feature | Points |
|---|---|
| **Clinical sign of neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1)** and/or **≥2 hyperpigmented and/or hypopigmented skin alterations >1 cm in diameter** in the patient | **2** |
| **Diagnosis of LS in a first- or second-degree relative** | **2** |
| **Carcinoma from the LS spectrum\* before age 60** in a first-, second- or third-degree relative | **1** |
| **A sibling with LS-spectrum carcinoma\*, high-grade glioma, sPNET or NHL** | **2** |
| **A sibling with any type of childhood malignancy** | **1** |
| **Multiple pilomatricomas** in the patient | **2** |
| **One pilomatricoma** in the patient | **1** |
| **Agenesis of the corpus callosum** or **non-therapy-induced cavernoma** in the patient | **1** |
| **Consanguineous parents** | **1** |
| **Deficiency / reduced levels of IgG2/4 and/or IgA** | **1** |

\* **LS spectrum as defined by this table:** colorectal, endometrial, small bowel, ureter, renal pelvis, biliary tract, stomach, bladder carcinoma.

### How the score behaves
- **A single qualifying tumour can reach the threshold alone:** an LS-spectrum carcinoma under 25, or multiple bowel adenomas under 25 without an *APC*/*MUTYH* mutation (or one high-grade dysplasia adenoma under 25), each score **3**.
- **A 2-point tumour needs one more point** — e.g. a WHO grade III/IV glioma under 25 **plus** consanguineous parents, or **plus** a single pilomatricoma, reaches 3.
- **"Any malignancy at age <18" scores only 1**, so a childhood cancer reaches the threshold only with **2 points of additional features** — which is exactly the NF1-like skin phenotype (≥2 hyper-/hypopigmented patches >1 cm), a sibling with a qualifying tumour, multiple pilomatricomas, or LS in a close relative.
- Café-au-lait-type skin findings are scored by **number and size** (**≥2 lesions, >1 cm**), not by appearance alone.
- The consortium reports that **nearly all CMMRD patients reach ≥3 points** under this system.

### Supporting observations
- **PMS2 biallelic mutations are the most frequent**, followed by MSH6; MLH1/MSH2 biallelics are comparatively rare — the inverse of the gene distribution in Lynch syndrome.
- Age at first malignancy in CMMRD ranged from **0.4 to 39 years**.
- **Brain malformations** (agenesis of the corpus callosum) and **non-therapy-induced cavernomas** are part of the phenotype, not incidental — hence their point value.
- **Vocal-cord polyps in infancy** were seen in one patient; the consortium states the link to CMMRD **is not proven**.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **No contradiction with [[usmstf-2017-bmmrd]].** USMSTF's Table 2 is an unweighted suspicion list; this is a weighted testing threshold for the same syndrome, and USMSTF cites it. Where both pages speak, the newer US guideline keeps the wiki's management content; this document supplies only the scoring.
- **The score selects for testing, not for diagnosis.** Reaching ≥3 points is an indication to test; the diagnosis still requires **confirmation of biallelic germline mismatch repair mutations** (and PMS2 is the hardest to confirm, because of its ~20 pseudogenes).
- **No surveillance protocol is set by this score** — intervals and modalities on [[bmmrd-syndrome]] come from the US guideline, not from here.
- The criteria are from a **European consortium (2014)** and are the only scored criteria in the corpus; no US society has published a competing point system.
