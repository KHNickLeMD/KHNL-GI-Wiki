> ⚠ **Superseded — historical record.** The regimens, doses, durations and stopping rules below belong to the **first-generation protease-inhibitor era** (boceprevir or telaprevir added to peginterferon alfa + ribavirin). They were replaced by interferon-free [[direct-acting-antivirals|direct-acting antiviral (DAA)]] therapy — for current care see [[aasld-idsa-2023-hcv|American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD)–Infectious Diseases Society of America (IDSA) 2023 Hepatitis C Guidance]]. Nothing on this page should be used to treat a patient today.

## Summary
This 2011 AASLD update added the first two DAAs — the hepatitis C virus (HCV) nonstructural protein 3/4A (NS3/4A) protease inhibitors **boceprevir** and **telaprevir** — to the prior standard of care (SOC), peginterferon alfa (PegIFN) + ribavirin (RBV), for genotype 1 chronic [[hepatitis-c|hepatitis C]]. Triple therapy raised sustained virologic response (SVR) rates substantially over PegIFN/RBV alone but introduced **response-guided therapy (RGT)**, futility (stopping) rules, and new toxicities — anemia with both agents, rash with telaprevir. Neither protease inhibitor (PI) may be given without PegIFN alfa and weight-based RBV; as monotherapy each rapidly selects resistant variants.

Scope is deliberately narrow: the guideline states what treatment for genotype 1 chronic HCV is now regarded as optimal, and **does not address prioritization, patient selection for treatment, or treatment of special populations**. Neither PI should be used for genotype 2 or 3 infection. Recommendations are graded with the AASLD Class 1/2/2a/2b/3 + Level A/B/C scheme adapted from the American College of Cardiology (ACC) and American Heart Association (AHA) — **not GRADE**.

The content is now of historical interest only: both agents were withdrawn and the interferon-based paradigm was replaced by interferon-free DAA regimens. It is preserved to document the transitional first-generation PI era and the origins of RGT and IL28B-based prognostication.

## Key Definitions
*These definitions assign the treatment stratum and drive every duration and stopping decision below.*

| Term | Definition in this guideline |
|---|---|
| Undetectable HCV RNA | <10–15 IU/mL (as defined in the package insert) |
| Rapid virologic response (RVR) — boceprevir | Undetectable HCV RNA at week 8 (= week 4 of triple therapy; the interval reflects the 4-week lead-in) |
| Extended RVR (eRVR) — telaprevir | Undetectable HCV RNA at **both** week 4 and week 12 |
| eRVR — boceprevir, treatment-experienced | HCV RNA negative at weeks 8 **through** 20 |
| Slow virologic response — boceprevir, treatment-naive | HCV RNA detectable at any visit from week 8 up to but not including week 24 |
| Null responder | HCV RNA did **not** decline by at least 2 log IU/mL at treatment week 12 |
| Partial responder | HCV RNA dropped by at least 2 log IU/mL at week 12 but was still detected at treatment week 24 |
| Relapser | HCV RNA became undetectable during treatment, then reappeared after treatment ended |
| Virologic breakthrough | >1 log increase in serum HCV RNA above nadir |

- SVR: regarded as a virologic "cure" — long-term clearance of HCV, improved morbidity and mortality. The guideline does not state the post-treatment week at which SVR is measured.
- SOC (PegIFN + RBV) SVR before the PIs: 40%–50% in genotype 1 (48 weeks); 80% or more in genotypes 2 and 3 (24 weeks).

## Regimens
| | Boceprevir | Telaprevir |
|---|---|---|
| Dose | 800 mg by mouth **three times per day (every 7–9 h), with food** | 750 mg by mouth **three times per day (every 7–9 h), with food — not low-fat** (in particular a fatty meal) |
| Lead-in | **Yes** — 4 weeks of PegIFN alfa + weight-based RBV before the PI is added | **No** |
| PI duration | 24–44 weeks of triple therapy | 12 weeks of triple therapy, then a further 12–36 weeks of PegIFN alfa + RBV |
| Backbone | Never without PegIFN alfa + weight-based RBV | Never without PegIFN alfa + weight-based RBV |

### Response-Guided Therapy — Treatment-Naive
| Scenario | Total duration |
|---|---|
| Boceprevir, **no cirrhosis**, HCV RNA undetectable at weeks 8 **and** 24 | 28 weeks total (4 weeks lead-in + 24 weeks triple therapy) |
| Boceprevir, slow virologic response | Boceprevir stopped; SOC continued an additional 20 weeks (48 weeks total). FDA alternative, based on modeling: 4 weeks SOC lead-in → 32 weeks triple therapy → 12 weeks PegIFN/RBV alone (differs from the phase 3 design) |
| Telaprevir, **no cirrhosis**, eRVR (undetectable weeks 4 **and** 12) | 24 weeks total |
| Telaprevir, no eRVR | 48 weeks total |
| Either PI **with cirrhosis** | 48 weeks. The Food and Drug Administration (FDA) recommends that patients with compensated cirrhosis **not** receive RGT (limited data) |

### Response-Guided Therapy — Treatment-Experienced
| Scenario | Total duration |
|---|---|
| Boceprevir, eRVR (HCV RNA negative weeks 8 through 20) | 36 weeks total (4 weeks lead-in + 32 weeks triple therapy) |
| Boceprevir, no eRVR but HCV RNA undetectable at week 12 | Boceprevir stopped at week 32, then 12 weeks of SOC (48 weeks total) |
| Boceprevir, fixed duration | 4 weeks lead-in + 44 weeks triple therapy (48 weeks total) |
| Telaprevir | 12 weeks triple therapy + 36 weeks PegIFN/RBV (48 weeks total); RGT was not studied for telaprevir re-treatment, but the FDA endorses an RGT approach in relapsers as for boceprevir |
| Boceprevir **with cirrhosis** | SVR was significantly lower with RGT than with the full 48 weeks (35% vs 77%) |

### Stopping (Futility) Rules
*Stop **all three** drugs; continuing futile therapy drives resistance.*

| PI | Population | Stop everything if |
|---|---|---|
| Boceprevir | Treatment-naive | HCV RNA >100 IU/mL at **week 12**, or detectable at **week 24** |
| Boceprevir | Re-treatment | HCV RNA still detectable at >100 IU/mL at **week 12** |
| Telaprevir | Treatment-naive | HCV RNA >1,000 IU/mL at **week 4 or week 12**, and/or detectable at **week 24** |
| Telaprevir | Re-treatment | HCV RNA still detectable at >1,000 IU/mL at **week 4 or 12** |
| Either | Any | Discontinue the PI for virologic breakthrough (>1 log rise above nadir) |

- The narrative text states the week-24 cut-points explicitly as >10–15 IU/mL (i.e. detectable) for both drugs, and gives the telaprevir week-4/12 threshold as ≥1,000 IU/mL; the numbered recommendations use >1,000 IU/mL and "detectable."
- Week 4 and 12 on triple therapy are the key decision points; viral stopping rules varied widely across the phase 2 and 3 trials.

## Key Findings / Claims — Recommendations
All 18 numbered recommendations, near-verbatim (American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association [ACC/AHA]-style classes; Level A/B/C). **Obsolete — historical record only.**

1. The optimal therapy for genotype 1 chronic HCV infection is the use of boceprevir or telaprevir in combination with peginterferon alfa and ribavirin. (Class 1, Level A)
2. Boceprevir and telaprevir should not be used without peginterferon alfa and weight-based ribavirin. (Class 1, Level A)
3. (Treatment-naïve) The recommended dose of boceprevir is 800 mg with food three times per day (every 7–9 hours) together with peginterferon alfa and weight-based ribavirin for 24–44 weeks, preceded by 4 weeks of lead-in peginterferon + ribavirin alone. (Class 1, Level A)
4. Patients without cirrhosis on boceprevir triple therapy (after 4-week lead-in) whose HCV RNA at weeks 8 and 24 is undetectable may be considered for shortened total treatment of 28 weeks (4-week lead-in + 24 weeks triple therapy). (Class 2a, Level B)
5. Stop all three drugs (boceprevir, peginterferon, ribavirin) if HCV RNA is >100 IU/mL at treatment week 12 or detectable at week 24. (Class 2a, Level B)
6. The recommended dose of telaprevir is 750 mg with food (not low-fat) three times per day (every 7–9 hours) with peginterferon alfa and weight-based ribavirin for 12 weeks, then an additional 12–36 weeks of peginterferon + ribavirin. (Class 1, Level A)
7. Patients without cirrhosis on telaprevir triple therapy whose HCV RNA at weeks 4 and 12 is undetectable should be considered for shortened therapy of 24 weeks. (Class 2a, Level A)
8. Patients with cirrhosis on either boceprevir or telaprevir triple therapy should receive 48 weeks of therapy. (Class 2b, Level B)
9. Stop all three drugs (telaprevir, peginterferon, ribavirin) if HCV RNA is >1,000 IU/mL at weeks 4 or 12 and/or detectable at week 24. (Class 2a, Level B)
10. (Treatment-experienced) Re-treatment with boceprevir or telaprevir + peginterferon + weight-based ribavirin can be recommended for prior relapsers or partial responders to standard/peginterferon ± ribavirin. (Class 1, Level A)
11. Re-treatment with telaprevir + peginterferon + ribavirin may be considered for prior null responders. (Class 2b, Level B)
12. Response-guided therapy in treatment-experienced patients can be considered for relapsers, may be considered for partial responders, but cannot be recommended for null responders. (Class 2a–3, Levels B–C as detailed)
13. Withdraw all therapy in patients re-treated with boceprevir triple therapy who still have HCV RNA >100 IU/mL at week 12 (high resistance risk). (Class 1, Level B)
14. Withdraw all therapy in patients re-treated with telaprevir triple therapy who still have HCV RNA >1,000 IU/mL at weeks 4 or 12 (high resistance risk). (Class 1, Level B)
15. Manage anemia on protease-inhibitor therapy by reducing the ribavirin dose. (Class 2a, Level A)
16. Closely monitor HCV RNA on protease-inhibitor therapy and discontinue the protease inhibitor if virologic breakthrough (>1 log rise above nadir) occurs. (Class 1, Level A)
17. Patients who fail to respond, break through, or relapse on one protease inhibitor should not be re-treated with the other protease inhibitor. (Class 2a, Level C)
18. IL28B genotype is a robust pretreatment predictor of SVR (to peginterferon/ribavirin and to PI triple therapy) in genotype 1; testing may be considered when additional information on probability of response or likely treatment duration is desired. (Class 2a, Level B)

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- Wholly superseded. Every regimen here (peginterferon, ribavirin, boceprevir, telaprevir, RGT, stopping rules) conflicts with modern interferon-free DAA practice and must not be applied. IL28B testing is also no longer clinically used given near-universal SVR with DAAs.
