## Summary
AGA Commentary on hepatitis delta virus (HDV) — a defective RNA virus that replicates only in the presence of HBV, occurring as coinfection or superinfection in chronic hepatitis B (CHB). Concurrent HDV markedly increases the risk of cirrhosis, hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), and liver-related mortality versus HBV monoinfection. The update emphasizes that HDV is underdiagnosed: testing rates among people with CHB are low (often <11%) and the diagnostic cascade (reflexing anti-HDV–positive patients to HDV RNA) frequently breaks down. The authors **suggest universal HDV screening of all patients with HBV** (aligning with EASL/APASL/WHO), using **anti-HDV antibody as the initial test and HDV RNA to confirm viremia**; double-reflex laboratory testing dramatically improves completion. All HDV patients should undergo noninvasive fibrosis/cirrhosis assessment (FIB-4, VCTE) and HCC surveillance. The only US-available therapy is **pegylated interferon-α** (SVR ~23%–57%, but ~50% relapse); **bulevirtide** (entry inhibitor) is EMA-approved and available in the US only via an expanded-access program, with several agents (BJT-778, tobevibart+elebsiran, lonafarnib) in trials.

## Key Findings / Claims
- **Virology/risk:** HDV requires HBV (HBsAg) to replicate; coinfection (acute, with HBV) vs superinfection (on existing CHB). HDV accelerates progression to cirrhosis, HCC, and liver-related death; ~50%–70% of anti-HDV–positive patients have detectable HDV RNA (active infection).
- **Who to screen:** Authors **suggest universal anti-HDV screening of all HBsAg/HBV patients** (EASL, APASL, WHO 2024 align; one-time universal testing is cost-effective). Retest those with ongoing risk behaviors, high-prevalence-area residence, or new ALT elevation/decompensation.
- **Testing cascade:** **anti-HDV Ab first → if positive, HDV RNA** to confirm viremia. Automated reflex anti-HDV on HBsAg-positive samples raised testing from 8% → 93%; **double reflex** (anti-HDV on all HBsAg+, HDV RNA on all anti-HDV+) should be implemented. HDV antigen and anti-HDV IgM are **not** advised (limited availability/sensitivity/specificity). US HDV RNA testing is send-out (Quest, ARUP, LabCorp, CDC); assay variability remains a limitation.
- **Staging/monitoring:** Assess for cirrhosis with noninvasive testing — **VCTE** AUROC 0.90 (vs FIB-4 0.88, APRI 0.83); ≥14.0 kPa cutoff (sens 0.78, spec 0.86, NPV 0.93). FIB-4 + VCTE for fibrosis. **HCC surveillance for all HDV patients.**
- **Current treatment — peg-IFN-α-2a/2b** (only US-available): 48-week SVR 23%–57%; relapse ~50% even after prolonged therapy (up to 9 years later); responders have improved survival/fewer liver-related events. **Contraindicated in advanced liver disease** and major extrahepatic disease; significant neuropsychiatric/hematologic toxicity. Per AASLD HBV guidance, peg-IFN-α weekly ×12 months for elevated HDV RNA + elevated ALT; add a **nucleos(t)ide analogue** if HBV DNA elevated (NAs lack direct anti-HDV activity).
- **Emerging treatment — bulevirtide** (sodium taurocholate co-transporting polypeptide / entry inhibitor, daily SC): MYR-301 phase 3 — 45%–48% on-treatment response (≥2-log HDV RNA decline and/or ALT normalization) at 48 wk; MYR-204 combination with peg-IFN raised post-treatment undetectable HDV RNA to 46% (vs 12% monotherapy). EMA-approved (2 mg) since 2020; in the US available only via Gilead **expanded-access program** (NCT06780579) for compensated cirrhosis. Other trial agents: **BJT-778 (brelovitug)** anti-HBsAg mAb, **tobevibart + elebsiran** (SOLSTICE/ECLIPSE), **lonafarnib** (farnesyltransferase inhibitor), peg-IFN-λ (discontinued), nucleic acid polymers.
