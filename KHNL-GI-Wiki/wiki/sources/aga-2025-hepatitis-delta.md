## Summary

American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) Commentary on hepatitis delta virus (HDV) — a defective RNA virus that replicates only in the presence of hepatitis B virus (HBV), occurring as coinfection or acute superinfection in chronic hepatitis B (CHB). Scope is deliberately narrow: US epidemiology, advice on screening and diagnosis, and current plus future therapies, "with a focus on data in the United States."

Concurrent HDV markedly increases the risk of [[cirrhosis]], [[hepatocellular-carcinoma|hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)]], hepatic decompensation, and liver-related mortality versus HBV monoinfection. The update's central problem is underdiagnosis: testing rates among people with CHB are low, and the diagnostic cascade (reflexing anti-HDV–positive patients to HDV RNA) collapses at the second step. The authors **suggest universal HDV screening of all patients with HBV** — aligning with the European Association for the Study of the Liver (EASL), the Asian Pacific Association for the Study of the Liver (APASL), and the World Health Organization (WHO), and going beyond the risk-based American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD) approach — using **anti-HDV antibody as the initial screening test and HDV RNA to confirm viremia**. A **double reflex** laboratory protocol is advised to close the cascade.

Patients with HDV should be evaluated with imaging and noninvasive testing for cirrhosis (Fibrosis-4 index [FIB-4], vibration-controlled transient elastography [VCTE]), and the authors advise HCC surveillance for all of them. The only US-available therapy is **pegylated interferon-α (peg-IFN-α)** — sustained virologic response (SVR) 23%–57% but ~50% relapse, and contraindicated in advanced liver disease. **Bulevirtide**, an entry inhibitor, is European Medicines Agency (EMA)-approved at 2 mg and reachable in the US only through a Gilead expanded-access program; brelovitug (BJT-778), tobevibart plus elebsiran, and lonafarnib are in trials.

## Key Findings / Claims — the Commentary's own advice

*Format: this is a Clinical Practice Update **Commentary**, not a Best Practice Advice set. It states no numbered recommendations and applies **no GRADE, evidence level, or strength rating** to anything. The numbering and grouping below are the wiki's, applied to the authors' own advisory sentences; the force word is the document's.*

**Screening and diagnosis**

1. **Suggest universal HDV screening in all patients with HBV** — the authors add that they expect US guidelines may be updated in the future to reflect this.
2. **Anti-HDV antibody is the initial screening test; anti-HDV–positive individuals should have follow-up HDV RNA testing** to confirm viremia, which is what reflects active HDV infection.
3. **A double reflex test should be implemented to improve the diagnostic cascade of care** — anti-HDV testing of all hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg)-positive individuals, plus HDV RNA testing of all anti-HDV–positive individuals.
4. **Testing with HDV antigen or anti-HDV immunoglobulin M (IgM) is not currently advised** — limited availability and limited sensitivity and specificity for assessing HDV infection.
5. **Consider HDV retesting in those at risk of HDV superinfection** — ongoing risk behaviors, living in an area of high HDV prevalence, or HBV presenting with new liver test elevation or hepatitis B decompensation.

**Staging and surveillance**

6. **Patients with HDV should be evaluated with imaging *and* noninvasive testing for the presence of cirrhosis.**
7. **Assessment of hepatic fibrosis and prompt identification of cirrhosis by noninvasive methods (e.g., FIB-4 and VCTE) is a critical step in HDV management.**
8. **Advise HCC surveillance for all patients with HDV**, given their increased HCC risk — stated as being in line with current guidance. No interval, modality, or cirrhosis qualifier is attached to this advice in the document.

**Treatment**

9. **Finite treatment with bulevirtide plus peg-IFN may be considered in select patients** — framed on the MYR-204 combination result; "select patients" is not defined.
10. **After peg-IFN, close assessment and follow-up is necessary with ongoing laboratory *and* imaging surveillance**, because of the high relapse rate. No follow-up interval is given.

### Guidance the Commentary relays from other societies

*Not the AGA's own statements — reproduced here because they carry the numeric treatment triggers the Commentary itself does not supply.*

| Body | Statement as relayed |
|---|---|
| **AASLD** (current) | HDV testing with anti-HDV antibody in **high-risk individuals with CHB** (risk list below); testing **can be considered** for people with CHB who have **persistently low HBV DNA and elevated alanine aminotransferase (ALT)** |
| **AASLD HBV guidance** | **Peg-IFN-α weekly for 12 months** is the recommended therapy for those with **elevated HDV RNA levels and elevated ALT**; concurrent **nucleos(t)ide analogue (NA)** therapy is also recommended **if HBV DNA levels are elevated**, even though NAs have no efficacy against HDV specifically |
| **EASL** · **APASL** | Screen **all individuals with CHB** for HDV using anti-HDV antibody |
| **EASL HDV clinical practice guidelines** | **NA therapy only in patients with HBV DNA >2000 IU/mL and/or cirrhosis** |
| **WHO** (March 2024 policy update) | Testing with anti-HDV antibody in **all individuals with CHB** |

**AASLD high-risk groups** (the risk-based criteria universal screening would replace): human immunodeficiency virus (HIV)-positive · people who inject drugs · men who have sex with men · individuals at risk of acquiring sexually transmitted diseases · immigrants from areas of high HDV endemicity — Africa (West Africa, horn of Africa), Asia (Central and Northern Asia, Vietnam, Mongolia, Pakistan, Japan, Taiwan), Pacific Islands (Kiribati, Nauru), Middle East, Eastern Europe (Eastern Mediterranean regions and Turkey), South America (Amazonian basin), and Greenland.

- One-time universal HDV testing among adults living with CHB **has been found to be cost-effective**.

### Other decision inputs and data the document supplies

**US epidemiology**

- Polaris Observatory modelling: US base of **1.65 million** people with positive HBsAg, of whom **3.0%** anti-HDV–positive; **66.0%** of anti-HDV–positive have detectable HDV RNA → ~**32,700** with active HDV.
- Systematic review/meta-analysis (fixed effects): anti-HDV positivity ~**75,005**; viremic HDV **37,500–52,500** in the US.
- Among non–US-born individuals with CHB, weighted average anti-HDV prevalence **4.20%** (45% emigrated from Africa, 25% Asia, 14% Europe).
- High-risk populations, anti-HDV prevalence: **6.7%** (95% confidence interval [CI], 5.2%–8.1%) with HIV coinfection · **43.8%** (39.5%–48.0%) in people who inject drugs · **5.1%** (3.7%–6.5%) in men who have sex with men.
- All-Payer Claims database: among 144,975 US adults with CHB, **6719 (4.6%)** identified with HDV by diagnosis codes — likely reflecting anti-HDV positivity rather than viremia.
- **Authors' own estimate: approximately 30,000–60,000 individuals with active viremic HDV infection in the United States.**
- **~50%–70% of individuals with anti-HDV positivity have detectable HDV RNA.**

**Size of the care-cascade gap** (the evidence behind the double-reflex advice)

- 41,658 veterans with positive HBsAg: only **10.7%** completed HDV testing — not significantly different from a study 10 years earlier in the same population, and **no difference in testing rates among those meeting AASLD risk-based criteria**.
- Of 135 individuals who were anti-HDV–positive in that cohort, only **32** were tested for HDV RNA.
- 13,218 individuals with CHB across 3 US safety-net health systems: anti-HDV testing performed in **6.1%** (4.9% among those with AASLD-recommended risk factors); of 2075 anti-HDV–positive individuals, only **1.6%** had follow-up HDV RNA testing.
- 1-year pilot offering universal HDV testing to consecutive CHB patients in hepatology clinics: of 91 patients with anti-HDV antibody ordered, **70 (76.9%)** completed testing; only 2 completed follow-up HDV RNA, with 1 confirmed active HDV infection.
- Multicenter US study of 1112 HBsAg-positive patients: **58.0%** completed anti-HDV testing; 6 of 9 completed follow-up HDV RNA.
- **Automated reflex anti-HDV testing** in all HBsAg-positive patients raised anti-HDV testing from **8% to 93%** and shortened time to HDV testing after the initial HBV result.
- Accuracy and reliability of available HDV RNA assays are challenged by **virus-specific and assay-specific factors** — critical for drug development and for monitoring treatment response.

**Noninvasive staging thresholds**

- Area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) for cirrhosis in chronic HDV: **VCTE 0.90** vs **FIB-4 0.88** vs aspartate aminotransferase to platelet ratio index (APRI) **0.83**.
- **VCTE cutoff ≥14.0 kPa:** sensitivity **0.78**, specificity **0.86**, negative predictive value **0.93**, positive predictive value **0.64** for identifying cirrhosis in HDV.

**Treatment detail beyond Table 1**

- **Peg-IFN-α-2a or -2b** is the only treatment available in the US. SVR at **6 months post-treatment ranges 23%–57% when given for 48 weeks**; some studies found **duration >2 years** associated with higher response rates.
- **Relapse after discontinuation occurs in ~50%**, even after prolonged treatment, and **can occur as late as 9 years** after completing therapy.
- Responders may have improved survival and reduced liver-related events: in a small study of 12 patients given ≥6 months of interferon and followed 8.8 years, **4 of 5 (80%) nonresponders died vs 1 of 7 (14%) responders**.
- **Peg-IFN-α is contraindicated in patients with advanced liver disease and in those with major extrahepatic diseases**, and carries significant neuropsychiatric and hematologic adverse effects that can limit its use.
- **Bulevirtide** blocks viral entry at the sodium taurocholate co-transporting polypeptide receptor; **daily subcutaneous (SC) injection**. MYR-301 (phase 3, 144 weeks, 2 mg or 10 mg): at 48 weeks, **45% (2 mg) and 48% (10 mg)** achieved the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-specified on-treatment response — **≥2-log decline of HDV RNA and/or ALT normalization**; prolonged treatment increased response rates further.
- MYR-204 (phase 2b) treated patients with bulevirtide with and without peg-IFN-α; primary outcome **undetectable HDV RNA 24 weeks after end of treatment** reached in **up to 46%** of the combination arm receiving **bulevirtide 10 mg** vs **12%** in the bulevirtide-only arm.
- **Bulevirtide 2 mg has been EMA-approved since 2020**, with real-world data (including in patients with cirrhosis) showing efficacy similar to clinical trials. It is **not FDA-approved and the optimal treatment duration is not defined**; US access is a **case-by-case pathway for bulevirtide 10 mg through a Gilead Sciences expanded access program** for patients with chronic HDV and **compensated liver disease with cirrhosis** (NCT06780579).
- **Brelovitug (BJT-778)**, a monoclonal antibody targeting HBsAg: phase 2 showed **67% rapid achievement of combined response at 24 weeks**; a phase 2b/3 trial will be initiated.
- **Tobevibart (monoclonal antibody targeting HBsAg) plus elebsiran (small interfering RNA)**: phase 3 **SOLSTICE**; at week 24 on treatment ~**50%** achieved ALT normalization, **100%** achieved ≥2-log plasma HDV RNA decline, and **41%** achieved target not detected (no HDV RNA amplification in plasma) — rising to ~**83%** in a group treated for 48 weeks. The global phase 3 **ECLIPSE** program was expected to launch in 2025.
- **Lonafarnib** (oral inhibitor of farnesyltransferase, a key step in the HDV viral life cycle), **peg-IFN-λ** (liver-targeted peg-IFN), and **nucleic acid polymers** (block HBV subviral particle assembly and inhibit HDV replication) have been evaluated.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Universal vs risk-based screening.** The Commentary goes **beyond** the current AASLD risk-based approach by suggesting **universal** anti-HDV screening of all patients with HBV (consistent with EASL, APASL, and WHO); the authors expect US guidelines may be updated to reflect this. Both positions are carried on [[hepatitis-d]] and [[chronic-hepatitis-b]].
- **Internal inconsistency on lonafarnib's phase.** Table 1 lists lonafarnib as **Phase 3**, while the body text groups lonafarnib with peg-IFN-λ and nucleic acid polymers as agents that "have also been evaluated, but are not currently being investigated in phase 3 studies."
- **Prevalence estimates diverge across the cited studies** — ~32,700 viremic (Polaris-based), 37,500–52,500 viremic (meta-analysis), 20,240–53,130 viremic extrapolated from diagnosis-code data, 4.6% of insured adults with CHB by diagnosis codes. The authors settle on **30,000–60,000** with active viremic HDV in the US.
- **The treatment trigger is not quantified.** The relayed AASLD HBV guidance reads "elevated HDV RNA levels and elevated ALT" with **no IU/mL and no U/L or ×upper limit of normal cut-point**, and the NA add-on trigger is likewise just "elevated" HBV DNA — the only number anywhere is EASL's HBV DNA >2000 IU/mL and/or cirrhosis. The document gives peg-IFN-α's route and frequency (weekly SC, 12 months) but **no µg dose**.
- **HCC surveillance advice carries no interval, modality, or cirrhosis qualifier**, and the post-peg-IFN follow-up advice names "ongoing laboratory and imaging surveillance" with **no interval**. "Select patients" for finite bulevirtide plus peg-IFN is also left undefined.
- **Staging cut-points cover cirrhosis only.** The VCTE ≥14.0 kPa threshold is for identifying cirrhosis in HDV; **no FIB-4 cut-point and no fibrosis-stage thresholds** are given, although FIB-4 is advised alongside VCTE.
