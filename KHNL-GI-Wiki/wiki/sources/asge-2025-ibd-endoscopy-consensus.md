## Summary

An ASGE-convened panel of 14–15 voting experts in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), endoscopy and GI pathology built a practical consensus document covering six domains: diagnosis, disease monitoring and assessment of treatment response, postoperative care, dysplasia surveillance, interventional IBD, and future perspectives. Literature search spanned 1 January 2000 to 31 July 2024.

**Methodology — read this before quoting any statement.** This is a **modified Delphi consensus, not a GRADE guideline.** There is **no systematic review, no certainty-of-evidence rating and no recommendation strength.** The only quantitative attribute each statement carries is the **percentage of panelists voting "agree" or "strongly agree"**; a statement was accepted when that exceeded **80%**, excluding "neutral" and "do not have expertise" votes (those were not counted in the denominator). Three voting rounds were used, with virtual meetings on 2 and 10 October 2023 to discuss statements that had not reached consensus, plus an additional May 2024 survey on the ulcerative colitis (UC) surveillance interval. Statement numbering below is the **document's own**.

**38 statements achieved consensus** (Table 1). **17 statements did not** (Table 2) and are published separately — several of them are positions commonly assumed to be settled practice, so the failure to reach consensus is itself informative.

The document positions endoscopy as the reference standard for disease activity (symptom scores correlate poorly with endoscopic inflammation, especially in Crohn's disease [CD]), formalizes biopsy density and site rules, and stratifies dysplasia surveillance for the colon, the diverted large bowel and the ileal pouch separately.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Diagnosis (statements 1–10)

| # | Statement (condensed; meaning unchanged) | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ileocolonoscopy is indicated in all patients with chronic diarrhea (>4–6 wk), persistent bleeding, new or unexplained anemia, weight loss, growth failure in children, or other alarming presentations. | 100% (SA 85.7%, A 14.3%) |
| 2 | Ileocolonoscopy **with tissue biopsy sampling** is required for the diagnosis of CD and UC. | 100% (SA 85.7%, A 14.3%) |
| 3 | Medications known to induce inflammation in IBD, such as nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), should be avoided before diagnostic ileocolonoscopy. | 78.5% of all votes (SA 7.1%, A 71.4%; 2 neutral, 1 no expertise) |
| 4 | Adequate cleansing **with documentation of bowel preparation scores** should be obtained for diagnostic and therapeutic IBD endoscopy. | 100% (round 2: SA 50.0%, A 50.0%) |
| 5 | At the index ileocolonoscopy, take **at least 4 sets of biopsy samples** — one set each from terminal ileum, right colon, left colon and rectum — **plus biopsy samples from all endoscopically reachable strictures.** Index ileocolonoscopy with biopsy sampling should be done **before initiating medical therapy**, to avoid "treatment effect", assess disease extent, and differentiate CD from UC. | 100% (SA 50.0%, A 50.0%) |
| 6 | Biopsy samples should be taken from **strictures, representative nodular and inflamed mucosa, and the edge of an ulcer.** | 92.8% (SA 71.4%, A 21.4%) |
| 7 | Cross-sectional imaging (CT enterography [CTE] or magnetic resonance enterography [MRE]) is recommended **around the time of the index ileocolonoscopy**, to verify the diagnosis, assess disease extent and evaluate for adverse events (eg, stricture, fistula). | 85.7% (SA 64.3%, A 21.4%) |
| 8 | Intestinal ultrasound (IUS), **where available**, can be considered a tool to assess mucosal and transmural inflammation, stricture, fistula and abscess, as part of diagnosis, disease monitoring and assessment of treatment response. | 85.7% (round 2: SA 28.6%, A 57.1%) |
| 9 | Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD) is recommended for **symptomatic adults and all pediatric patients** suspected of CD. | 78.5% (SA 57.1%, A 21.4%) |
| 10 | Capsule endoscopy **after a passed patency capsule** may be performed in patients suspected of small-bowel CD **without obstructive symptoms**, if ileocolonoscopy and/or small-bowel imaging (CTE, MRE or IUS) is not conclusive. | 92.9% (SA 42.9%, A 50.0%) |

Supporting detail:
- **Biopsy density:** 3 biopsy samples per region of interest is the **minimum** needed to overcome microscopic heterogeneity and grade inflammation accurately in active colitis.
- Granulomas appear in only ~**9%–67%** of CD patients; their absence does not exclude CD.
- Medical treatment of UC produces a **patchy** inflammation distribution in about **one-third** of patients — hence biopsy before therapy.
- **Paneth cell metaplasia** (Paneth cells distal to the splenic flexure) indicates chronic mucosal injury in CD or UC; it is seen in only **0%–1.9%** of non-IBD distal large bowel (infectious colitis, ischemic colitis, mucosal prolapse).
- **Pyloric gland metaplasia** with severe active inflammation or crypt distortion in small-bowel mucosa, in a young patient with no prior intestinal surgery, is strongly indicative of CD.
- **Mimics with a biopsy-site rule:** segmental colitis associated with diverticulosis spares the rectum (so biopsy the rectum); solitary rectal ulcer syndrome / prolapse / rectocele affects the distal rectum, preferentially anterior wall (so biopsy the anterior wall).
- **Capsule retention rates in CD:** 3.32% (95% CI 2.62–4.2) overall, **2.35%** (1.31–4.19) suspected CD, **4.63%** (3.42–6.25) established CD; adult CD 3.49%, pediatric CD 1.64%.

### Disease monitoring and assessment of treatment response (statements 11–15)

| # | Statement (condensed) | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | Flexible sigmoidoscopy, colonoscopy or ileocolonoscopy, **together with clinical and laboratory biomarkers**, is preferred after initiation, escalation or switch of medical therapy to document treatment response. Interval and modality depend on IBD type, mechanism of action of the drug, and response. | 92.8% (SA 57.1%, A 35.7%) |
| 12 | Disease activity instruments — **Simple Endoscopic Score for Crohn's Disease (SES-CD)** for CD, **Mayo score** for UC, **Rutgeerts score** for postoperative CD — should be used for disease assessment. | 100% (round 2: SA 71.4%, A 28.6%) |
| 13 | In patients with pelvic or abdominal pouches and persistent symptoms, **pouchoscopy** should be performed to assess treatment response. | 100% (round 2: SA 50.0%, A 50.0%) |
| 14 | **Endoscopic mucosal healing, even when histologic healing has not occurred, is sufficient to justify continuing the current therapy.** | 92.8% (SA 57.1%, A 35.7%) |
| 15 | Flexible sigmoidoscopy with biopsy sampling should be performed to assess disease activity **and evaluate for cytomegalovirus (CMV) infection** in hospitalized (or acute ambulatory surgical center) UC patients being treated with steroids, biologics or small molecules. | 92.9% (SA 50.0%, A 42.9%) |

Supporting detail:
- Endoscopic remission **without** partial or complete histologic remission **is not a treatment failure**; histologic healing is a potential future target with no consensus on implementation.
- **CMV biopsy technique and yield:** take samples **from the base and the edge of the ulcer**, an adequate number, at different colonic locations. One study recommended **11 biopsy samples by flexible sigmoidoscopy for UC and 16 by colonoscopy for CD** (the document notes this is not common practice). **>2 immunohistochemistry-positive CMV cells per biopsy sample** was significantly associated with colectomy in active UC on multivariate analysis. Immunostain for CMV antigen is added when suspicion is strong and hematoxylin & eosin findings are equivocal; the pathologist should report the density of positive cells per section. A tissue polymerase chain reaction (PCR)-positive, histology-negative result is of unclear significance. False negatives occur on both biopsy and blood CMV-DNA.
- Named histology indices: **Geboes score, Nancy Histological Index, Robarts Histopathology Index** (clinical trials and practice).

### Postoperative care (statements 16–19)

| # | Statement (condensed) | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| 16 | Ileocolonoscopy to monitor disease activity should be performed **6–12 mo after ileocolonic resection and ileocolonic anastomosis**, and after **stoma closure** in those with a temporary ileostomy, in CD. | 100% (SA 78.6%, A 21.4%) |
| 17 | **Pouchoscopy with biopsy sampling** is recommended for the diagnosis and differential diagnosis of pouchitis in symptomatic patients. Evaluation and description should include the anatomic landmarks: **prepouch ileum, inlet, pouch body, anastomosis, cuff.** | 100% (SA 71.4%, A 28.6%) |
| 18 | Disease activity instruments such as the **Pouchitis Disease Activity Index (PDAI)** can be used to assess pouchitis, CD of the pouch and cuffitis. | 85.7% (round 2: SA 28.6%, A 57.1%) |
| 19 | **Ileoscopy through the stoma is recommended before stoma closure** in CD patients with temporary fecal diversion; the ileoscope should be introduced **beyond the level of the fascia.** | 85.8% (SA 42.9%, A 42.9%) |

Supporting detail:
- After the index postoperative ileocolonoscopy the interval **is not certain**, but **1–3 y is reasonable**, depending on disease activity, fecal calprotectin, and whether IUS was used.
- Intensified postoperative monitoring (ileocolonoscopy as early as 6 mo after ileocolonic resection) gave better clinical outcomes than standard practice (18-mo ileocolonoscopy with no 6-mo exam) in a randomized trial.
- The bowel segment **between the stoma and the fascia** may carry ulcers, strictures or fistula from mechanical or ischemic causes that **do not represent recurrent CD** — the reason the scope must pass the fascia. Fecal calprotectin from ileostomy output is an alternative predictor of small-bowel disease.
- Biopsy of prepouch ileum, pouch body and rectal cuff assesses severity and gives clues to superimposed CMV infection, ischemia, CD of the pouch and prolapse. Endoscopic scoring of pouch inflammation varies greatly between investigators, but **documentation of ulceration and ulcerated surface area in the pouch body is consistent.**

### Surveillance (statements 20–29)

| # | Statement (condensed) | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| 20 | **Surveillance colonoscopy every 1–5 y** is recommended in Crohn's colitis or UC **8–10 y after diagnosis** with disease extending beyond the rectum. Interval is set by patient-specific factors (prior severity of colonic inflammation, history of dysplasia, family history of colorectal cancer [CRC], primary sclerosing cholangitis [PSC]) and by the frequency, quality and modality of prior surveillance exams. | 93.3% (round 3: SA 13.3%, A 80%; 1 disagree) |
| 21 | **All UC-associated strictures** in the colon or rectum should be biopsy sampled **at least once per year** to evaluate for neoplasia. | 85.8% (SA 42.9%, A 42.9%) |
| 22 | **All patients with PSC should undergo a diagnostic colonoscopy for the evaluation of UC at the time of PSC diagnosis.** All patients with UC, CD involving the large bowel, **and** PSC should have **yearly** surveillance colonoscopy **regardless of extent and duration of IBD**, with enhanced imaging (narrow-band imaging [NBI] or chromoendoscopy) and mucosal biopsy sampling. | 85.7% (SA 57.1%, A 28.6%) |
| 23 | Biopsy samples of **CD-associated colonic, rectal and anorectal strictures** should be taken at the index colonoscopy and **every 1–3 y** afterward, to rule out malignancy. | 100% (SA 42.9%, A 57.1%) |
| 24 | Surveillance biopsy sampling should be performed **every 1–3 y** for patients with a **diverted large bowel** and UC or CD duration **>8–10 y**. | 92.9% (SA 28.6%, A 64.3%) |
| 25 | **Yearly** surveillance pouchoscopy for IBD patients at **high risk** for dysplasia — those with **precolectomy colitis-associated neoplasia (CAN)**. | 100% (SA 64.3%, A 35.7%) |
| 26 | Surveillance pouchoscopy **every 1–3 y** at **moderate risk** — PSC, chronic pouchitis, CD of the pouch, chronic cuffitis, family history of CRC. | 85.7% (SA 64.3%, A 21.4%) |
| 27 | Surveillance pouchoscopy **every 3 y** at **average risk**. | 85.7% (SA 21.4%, A 64.3%) |
| 28 | **High-definition (HD) white-light (WL) endoscopy or image-enhanced endoscopy** (virtual or dye-based chromoendoscopy) with **targeted *or* random** biopsy sampling should be used for surveillance colonoscopy. | 92.9% (round 2: SA 50.0%, A 42.9%) |
| 29 | HD WL endoscopy or image-enhanced endoscopy with **targeted *and* random** biopsy sampling should be used for surveillance colonoscopy **in high-risk patients, such as those with PSC.** | 92.8% (round 2: SA 57.1%, A 35.7%) |

Supporting detail:
- CD- or UC-associated adenocarcinoma has a **worse prognosis than sporadic CRC** — higher incidence of poorly differentiated and laterally spreading cancer. It is often found **within primary or anastomotic strictures, more commonly primary strictures.**
- Surveillance is **better performed while the disease is in endoscopic remission.** Visible lesions should be described by **site, size, shape (Paris classification) and surrounding inflammation.**
- The **Seattle protocol** (4-quadrant random biopsy samples every 10 cm plus 5 random rectal samples, minimum 33 pieces) **is being phased out in many IBD centers for average-risk patients**; random biopsies can still be done at the initial surveillance exam as a baseline for staged biopsy. Image-enhanced endoscopy with targeted biopsy reduces the total number of samples with improved or comparable sensitivity.
- **Who still benefits from added random biopsies:** PSC, tubular colon and rectum, prior CAN — particularly prior **invisible** dysplasia. Nonconventional dysplasia variants (**hypermucinous, goblet cell–deficient, crypt cell dysplasia**) are often endoscopically invisible, and PSC patients are both more likely to develop dysplasia and more likely to have these invisible nonconventional features.
- **UC strictures are generally considered malignant until proven otherwise** (though benign strictures from submucosal fibrosis are not uncommon); UC-associated stricture is a major risk factor for CAN.
- Imaging-modality evidence: a network meta-analysis of 6 randomized controlled trials (1038 patients) ranked **HD NBI > chromoendoscopy > WL endoscopy and standard-definition WL endoscopy**. A separate meta-analysis of 7 randomized controlled trials (2457 patients) found dye-based chromoendoscopy superior to standard-definition WL endoscopy, but **no difference between dye-based and virtual chromoendoscopy** on any dysplasia-detection endpoint.
- Diverted-bowel risk base: estimated rectal-stump cancer incidence **3.9–4.5 per 1000 diverted patient-years** (meta-analysis, 1211 IBD patients). Surveillance is recommended if completion proctectomy is not feasible.
- Pouch neoplasia: the only **established** risk factor is **precolectomy CAN**; the moderate-risk factors in statement 26 are proposed and **none is confirmed with statistical confidence** (sample-size limits). Prognosis is poor — 6 of 14 patients (42.9%) in one pouch-cancer series died at a median 2.1 y. **Ileal pouch–anal anastomosis (IPAA)-associated neoplasia mostly involves the cuff or anal transition zone**, so the surveillance biopsy should include both.

### Interventional IBD (statements 30–36)

Main indications for interventional IBD: strictures; fistulas and abscesses; intraluminal lesions, bezoars and foreign bodies; IBD surgery–associated adverse events; and CAN. The document states these therapies are done by **select endoscopists at select centers that also have specialized IBD surgeons.**

| # | Statement (condensed) | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| 30 | **Intralesional injection of long-acting steroids is not recommended** before, during or after endoscopic balloon dilation (EBD) for strictures. | 85.7% (SA 50.0%, A 35.7%) |
| 31 | **EBD of deeply ulcerated strictures should be avoided.** | 85.7% (SA 50.0%, A 35.7%) |
| 32 | Long-term success of endoscopic stricture therapy is measured by **reintervention-free survival and surgery-free survival.** | 100% (SA 35.7%, A 64.3%) |
| 33 | **Large (>1 cm) inflammatory polyps may be removed** to reduce symptoms of bleeding, obstruction and anemia, **even though the risk of dysplasia is low.** | 85.7% (SA 21.4%, A 64.3%) |
| 34 | **Endoscopic stents should not be used** for benign disease–associated or anastomotic strictures in pre- or postoperative CD or UC. | 78.6% (SA 50.0%, A 28.6%) |
| 35 | **Surgical intervention should occur for CD strictures**, especially **long (>4–5 cm)** or **complex** (fistula- and/or abscess-associated, refractory to previous intervention, or recurrent — requiring endoscopic intervention **more often than every 3–6 mo**) strictures, after previously successful endoscopic interventions. | 100% (SA 71.4%, A 28.6%) |
| 36 | **Polypectomy, EMR or endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) may be performed on polypoid or raised, liftable dysplastic lesions with a clear, well-defined border.** | 100% (SA 57.1%, A 42.9%) |

Supporting detail:
- Evidence on intralesional steroids at EBD is **conflicting** across two small randomized controlled trials and case series; a prior expert panel also did not recommend it.
- For deeply ulcerated strictures, the association between EBD and efficacy or risk of bleeding/perforation **has not been confirmed** — avoidance is precautionary.
- Outcome reporting should include procedure-associated adverse events: **bleeding, perforation, and ileus or aspiration.**
- **Treating asymptomatic strictures is explicitly controversial** (this was one of the statements that failed consensus, see below). The argument for treating: symptoms correlate poorly with objective findings; untreated strictures can cause prestenotic dilation, acute obstruction or fistula; subtle progression ends in resection. EBD was more cost-effective than resection with anastomosis for CD stricture.
- Postinflammatory polyps are themselves a **risk factor for neoplasia (odds ratio 2.54, 95% CI 1.40–4.60)**, and can contribute to anemia or obstruction.
- A randomized controlled trial of **self-expandable metal stents failed to show better efficacy or safety than EBD** for CD strictures.
- Endoscopic-vs-surgical choice is set by patient (comorbidity), disease (CD vs UC, concurrent inflammation, medications), stricture (primary vs secondary, short vs long with a **4–5 cm cutoff**, simple vs complex), endoscopist and surgical factors.
- **Endoscopic resection of CAN performance** (meta-analysis, 7 studies, 506 patients, 610 lesions): complete endoscopic resection **97.9%** (95% CI 95.3–99.7), perforation **0.8%** (0.1–2.2), bleeding **1.6%** (0.4–3.3), and **6.6%** (3.6–10.2) of patients underwent surgery after endoscopic resection.
- **Technique requirements for CAN resection:** biopsy the **base** of the polypoid or resectable lesion; biopsy the **margin** if removed piecemeal; ensure EMR/ESD specimens are appropriately processed for histology.

### Statements that did NOT achieve consensus (Table 2, all 17)

These are published as *not* agreed. Several are positions a reader may assume are standard, so the non-consensus is the finding.

1. NSAIDs and other inflammation-inducing agents should be **held for 4 wk** before ileocolonoscopy in known IBD. *(The general "avoid before diagnostic ileocolonoscopy" statement 3 did pass; the 4-week hold did not.)*
2. **Sodium phosphate–based bowel preparation should be avoided; polyethylene glycol–based preparation preferred.**
3. A **jumbo biopsy sample** is preferred for primary or anastomotic strictures in patients at risk for dysplasia (long IBD duration, concurrent PSC, or bowel resection for neoplasia).
4. Onsite use of IUS is **encouraged** (including for lymphadenopathy). *(The weaker "can be considered, where available" version — statement 8 — passed.)*
5. Diagnostic or therapeutic **push or device-assisted enteroscopy** is recommended in suspected small-bowel CD when ileocolonoscopy and small-bowel imaging are infeasible or inconclusive.
6. **Ileoscopy through the stoma every 1–3 y** in CD patients with **permanent** fecal diversion. *(Ileoscopy before closure of a *temporary* diversion — statement 19 — passed.)*
7. Periodic surveillance for **glandular or squamous cell neoplasia in perianal CD** (endoscopic biopsy, anal smears, examination under anesthesia), especially in longstanding CD, human papillomavirus or HIV.
8. **Avoid direct biopsy of lesions highly suspicious for dysplasia** when EMR/ESD is imminent (to avoid fibrosis), biopsying and tattooing adjacent mucosa instead.
9. Endoscopic evaluation of a **strictureplasty site** should include inlet, lumen and outlet.
10. A **bypassed bowel segment** (eg, strictured duodenum with gastrojejunostomy) should be endoscopically surveyed **every 3 y** for malignancy.
11. **Yearly** surveillance biopsy to exclude glandular or squamous cell dysplasia in **chronic (>5 y) perianal CD.**
12. **Image-enhanced endoscopy** (virtual or dye-based chromoendoscopy) with targeted or random biopsy is **recommended** for surveillance colonoscopy. *(The version allowing HD white light as an equal alternative — statement 28 — passed. The panel would not make image enhancement mandatory.)*
13. Endoscopic stricture treatment may be performed in **all** IBD-associated strictures, **regardless of symptoms.**
14. **Technical (short-term) success** of endoscopic stricture treatment is measured by passage of a pediatric colonoscope (or upper endoscope) with or without resistance. *(The long-term definition — statement 32 — passed; the short-term one did not.)*
15. Establishment of a **multidisciplinary team** for interventional IBD service (IBD specialist, advanced endoscopist, colorectal surgeon, anesthesiologist, GI radiologist, GI pathologist).
16. **Interventional IBD training** incorporated into the advanced endoscopy or IBD fellowship (postgraduate year 7) curriculum.
17. An **interventional IBD Center of Excellence with ASGE credentialing.**

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Surveillance interval: 1–5 y here vs 1–3 y from ACG.** This document's own synopsis names the conflict: the American College of Gastroenterology guideline suggests **every 1–3 y** for left-sided or extensive UC beyond 8–10 y, while the American Gastroenterological Association, European Crohn's and Colitis Organisation and British Society of Gastroenterology permit extension **up to every 5 y** in a subset with long-term disease in endoscopic and histologic remission and a series of negative surveillance exams. ASGE 2025 adopts the wider **1–5 y** band and makes the interval explicitly patient-specific. This is the newest tier-1 US-society document on the question, so the wider band governs the entity pages, with the narrowing factors stated alongside it.
- **Image-enhanced endoscopy is permitted, not required.** [[asge-2015-scenic-ibd-dysplasia]] recommended chromoendoscopy **over** white light. ASGE 2025 passed the statement that makes **HD white light *or* image-enhanced endoscopy** acceptable (28) and **failed** the statement that would have recommended image enhancement (Table 2 #12) — a genuine softening of SCENIC in the same society's own later consensus. The exception is high-risk patients, where statement 22 does require NBI or chromoendoscopy for the PSC group.
- **No evidence grades at all.** Unlike [[asge-2015-ibd]] and [[asge-2015-scenic-ibd-dysplasia]], which carry GRADE ratings, every statement here is expert consensus with only an agreement percentage. Statements must not be relayed as though they carried a strength or certainty rating.
- **Targeted-vs-random biopsy is unresolved by design.** Statement 28 says targeted *or* random; statement 29 says targeted *and* random for high risk. The Seattle protocol is described as "being phased out in many IBD centers" for average-risk patients rather than withdrawn.
- **Pouch surveillance: PSC drops out of the annual tier.** [[asge-2015-ibd]] and [[aga-2021-ibd-colorectal-dysplasia]] both place PSC *alongside* prior colorectal dysplasia/cancer at **annual** pouchoscopy. ASGE 2025 keeps **only precolectomy colitis-associated neoplasia** annual and reassigns PSC, chronic pouchitis, CD of the pouch and chronic cuffitis to **1–3 y**. Newest same-tier document, so it governs [[pouchitis]]; surfaced on that page. **Colonic** surveillance in PSC-IBD is unaffected and in fact tightened to annual (statement 22).
- **Moderate-risk pouch surveillance rests on unconfirmed risk factors** — the panel says so explicitly (none of PSC, chronic pouchitis, CD of the pouch, chronic cuffitis or family history of CRC is statistically confirmed).
- **Asymptomatic IBD strictures: no position.** The statement that they could all be treated endoscopically failed consensus, and the document lays out the argument for treating without endorsing it.
- **Short-term success of stricture therapy has no agreed definition** (Table 2 #14), while the long-term definition does (statement 32).
