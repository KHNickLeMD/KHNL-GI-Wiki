## Summary

An international Delphi consensus on the entity previously called "PBC–AIH overlap syndrome", endorsed by the European Reference Network on Hepatological Diseases (ERN RARE-LIVER), the Global PBC Study Group and the International Autoimmune Hepatitis Group, in partnership with the European Society of Pathology. It exists because the international guidelines contradict each other on this condition and because, in practice, clinicians treat patients who do not meet the 1998 Paris criteria with combined immunosuppressive and anti-cholestatic therapy anyway.

Method: a **modified RAND/UCLA Appropriateness Method** over **two voting rounds** (a third round was needed for nomenclature alone), with a final panel of **74 hepatologists and 14 liver pathologists** from diverse regions, plus patient-representative input. Each item was rated on a 9-point Likert scale; a median of 1–3.5 is inappropriate, 3.5–6.5 uncertain and 6.5–9.0 appropriate. Agreement was measured by a **Disagreement Index** (0 = maximum agreement, 1 = maximum disagreement), and **consensus required ≥80% rating an item appropriate.** The liver pathologists met separately after round 1 to refine the histology statements, and several of those reached consensus **among pathologists only**.

**What the panel settled.** PBC–AIH is a **variant, not an overlap syndrome** — a subtype in which one disease dominates and presents atypically, rather than a patient simultaneously meeting two sets of criteria. The agreed term is **"PBC–AIH"** (64% of votes, after three rounds). Diagnosis rests on a **combination** of biochemical, serological and histological findings rather than on any single parameter or fixed threshold; **the Paris criteria and the newer Zhang criteria both failed to reach consensus**, as did statements built on clinical judgment alone or histology alone. **Liver biopsy is mandatory** in a PBC patient with features suggestive of PBC–AIH. The diagnosis must be **re-evaluated over time**, because the phenotype evolves and features appear sequentially. Untreated, PBC–AIH carries a **worse prognosis than PBC alone**.

**What it did not settle.** No strict timing for starting immunosuppression. No consensus on whether treatment improves prognosis. No consensus on whether PBC–AIH can be suspected at the time of PBC diagnosis, before treatment. No consensus (among hepatologists and pathologists together) on any specific histological feature defining PBC–AIH in a patient with established PBC. No consensus on the grade of lobular hepatitis required, nor on referral to an expert centre, nor on metabolic dysfunction–associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) as a differential.

## Key Findings / Claims

The consensus does not number its statements; the groupings below follow the document's own section headings and Tables 2 and 3. Percentages are the proportion rating the item **appropriate**.

### Nomenclature and definition

- **PBC–AIH is a variant, not an overlap syndrome.** Overlap = a patient meets criteria for multiple distinct conditions (as in mixed connective tissue disease); **variant = a subtype of a single disease, one condition dominant and presenting atypically**. In clinical practice either PBC or AIH predominates, with some features of the other; true overlap, where both full diagnostic criteria are met, exists but is rare.
- **The agreed term is "PBC–AIH"** — 32 of 50 votes (64%), reached only after a dedicated third round, the only item requiring one.
- **"Continuous spectrum" did not reach consensus** as a framing.

### Diagnosis

- **"The diagnosis of PBC–AIH should be based on a combination of laboratory, serological, and histological findings, rather than relying on a single parameter or strict criteria" — 95.1%.** Specifically: concurrent elevation of transaminases and cholestatic markers, increased immunoglobulin G (IgG), relevant autoantibodies, and compatible histopathology should **collectively** support the diagnosis.
- **"The diagnosis of PBC–AIH should be re-evaluated over time" — 85.4%**, and should be periodically re-evaluated, because features can occur sequentially.
- **Did not reach consensus:** the **Paris criteria**; the **Zhang criteria**; any statement resting on a single diagnostic item (clinical judgment alone, histology alone); **response to therapy as an additional diagnostic criterion** (58.8% of hepatologists, 50% of pathologists); consultation with an expert centre; MASLD as a differential or concomitant diagnosis.
- **Diagnostic criteria developed for AIH should not be used for PBC–AIH** — appropriate to **pathologists (78.6%)** but not hepatologists **(45.6%)**, p = 0.044; no overall consensus.

**Suspecting PBC–AIH in a patient with established PBC:**
- Focus on **incomplete response to ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) after 12 months**, accompanied by a **disproportionate elevation of transaminases relative to cholestasis**.
- **What "disproportionate" means is explicitly still debated.** The document cites the conventional **R ratio = (ALT ÷ ALT upper limit of normal) ÷ (ALP ÷ ALP upper limit of normal)**, where **>5 indicates hepatocellular injury and <2 cholestatic injury**, while acknowledging that some PBC patients have a "hepatitic" biochemical profile that does **not** indicate PBC–AIH.
- **Escalate anti-cholestatic therapy before reaching for immunosuppression.** Second-line PBC agents — peroxisome proliferator–activated receptor (PPAR) and farnesoid X receptor (FXR) agonists — significantly reduce transaminases along with cholestatic markers, as does UDCA itself; **reassess liver biochemistry after 6–12 months (or earlier)**. The panel notes that the availability of these agents should reduce misclassification of PBC as PBC–AIH.
- **No consensus on whether PBC–AIH can be suspected at the time of PBC diagnosis**, before treatment is started.

**Suspecting PBC–AIH in a patient with established AIH** (consensus reached):
- **AMA, gp210 or sp100 positivity alongside a disproportionate elevation of alkaline phosphatase (ALP), regardless of the timing of assessment.**
- **Gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT) lacks specificity** — influenced by medications, alcohol and hepatic steatosis — and requires careful interpretation.
- **Biliary abnormalities also occur in acute AIH**, so longitudinal evaluation matters.
- Where typical features of both diseases are present at presentation, a **presumed diagnosis of PBC–AIH can be made on the overall picture** — biochemical, serological and histological.

### Liver biopsy

- **Required: "a liver biopsy should be performed in patients with an established diagnosis of PBC who exhibit clinical or biochemical features suggestive of PBC–AIH, to confirm the diagnosis and guide management decisions" — 93.5%** (100% of pathologists, 92% of hepatologists).
- **"Reassessment of the original liver biopsy should be performed before a new liver biopsy in patients with an established diagnosis of AIH with disproportionate chronic cholestasis and positivity to PBC-related autoantibodies (AMA or gp210 or sp100)" — 87.8%.** The point is to avoid an unnecessary procedure.

### Histology (Table 3 — pathologists' responses)

**In a patient with established AIH** (all these reached consensus among liver pathologists):
- **Florid bile duct lesions (non-suppurative destructive granulomatous cholangitis) are supportive but not required** — 81.7%.
- **Portal granulomas are supportive but not required** — 81.7%.
- Also supportive, **consensus among pathologists only, not hepatologists:** bile duct loss; periportal copper or copper-associated protein granules (metallothionein); keratin 7–positive intermediate periportal hepatocytes.

**In a patient with established PBC:**
- **No consensus on any specific histological feature** when hepatologists and pathologists are counted together — including **no consensus on the grade of interface hepatitis required for the diagnosis**.
- **Among pathologists alone: interface hepatitis should be at least moderate, ≥2/4 on Ishak's modified Histologic Activity Index** — 85.7%.
- **Grade of lobular hepatitis failed consensus** even among pathologists (71.4% favoured at least moderate: ≥2/4 for focal lytic necrosis, focal inflammation, and/or ≥2/6 for confluent necrosis on the Ishak modified index).

### Treatment

- **"The benefit of treatment should outweigh risk and side effects, taking into account disease severity, stage, demographic factors, comorbidities, and patient preferences" — 93.5%.**
- **No strict timing for starting immunosuppression was endorsed** — neither "at diagnosis" nor "defer to a later phase" reached consensus.
- **Treatment endpoints should align with the most recent AIH guidelines (90.2%) and PBC guidelines (95.1%)**, and with local clinical practice.
- **Choice of immunosuppressive drug should follow existing AIH treatment recommendations and local practice — 85.9%.** This is the **only approved statement with a significant difference between the two professions**: 89.7% of hepatologists (70/78) supported it, while 5 of 14 pathologists (35.7%) were uncertain, p < 0.023.
- **The histological threshold that prompts immunosuppression in established PBC — 80.5%:** **severe interface hepatitis (≥3/4 on the Ishak modified Histologic Activity Index) is an indication; moderate interface hepatitis (≥2/4) is not.**
- **Starting immunosuppression for *any* grade of interface hepatitis in PBC was deemed inappropriate**, though only the pathologists were near-unanimous (92.9% inappropriate); among hepatologists 13.2% called it appropriate and 17.6% were uncertain (p = 0.16).

### Natural history

- **"The natural history (without treatment) of PBC–AIH is worse than PBC alone" — 91.5%.** The panel stresses early recognition and timely therapeutic intervention.
- **No consensus on the role of treatment on the prognosis of PBC–AIH.**

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Supersedes the Paris criteria on [[primary-biliary-cholangitis]].** The wiki's PBC page stated that antinuclear antibody and anti-smooth muscle antibody positivity "does not establish AIH overlap unless accompanied by elevated ALT/IgG and histologic interface hepatitis meeting Paris criteria". This consensus **explicitly failed to endorse the Paris criteria**, holding that they identify a more severe phenotype but do not capture the full picture. Both are tier-1; this document is newer (2026 vs the AASLD PBC guidance the page carries), so the combination-of-findings framing governs and the Paris criteria are retained only as named historical criteria.
- **"Treatment should be targeted at the predominant histological pattern of injury"** ([[aasld-2018-pbc]] Guidance Statement 4) is not contradicted, but this consensus puts a number on it that AASLD did not: **≥3/4 interface hepatitis, not ≥2/4**.
- **Hepatologists and pathologists disagree in print, and the document reports it.** Several histological statements reached consensus only among the 14 pathologists; the one approved statement with a statistically significant split is the choice of immunosuppressive drug. Statements carried as "pathologist-only consensus" must not be reported as consensus of the whole panel.
- **Unresolved by the consensus, and therefore unresolved on the wiki:** when to start immunosuppression; whether treatment alters prognosis; whether PBC–AIH can be suspected at PBC diagnosis; what counts as a "disproportionate" transaminase rise; what histology defines PBC–AIH in a patient with established PBC.
