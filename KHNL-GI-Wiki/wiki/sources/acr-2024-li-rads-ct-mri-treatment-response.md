## Summary

- Explains the **2024 Liver Imaging Reporting and Data System (LI-RADS) computed tomography (CT)/magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) Treatment Response Assessment (TRA) algorithm** for [[hepatocellular-carcinoma|hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)]] after local-regional therapy (LRT), and why the 2017 version was changed (abstract, p 1).
- 2017 TRA: one algorithm, four LI-RADS treatment response (LR-TR) categories — Nonevaluable, Viable, Nonviable, Equivocal — driven mainly by masslike arterial phase hyperenhancement (APHE), with washout and enhancement similar to pretreatment as additional criteria (section *LI-RADS TRA*, p 1).
- 2017 performed well after arterial embolic and loco-ablative therapy, fair after radiation-based therapy, with limited validating data for radiation (abstract, p 1). Radiation-treated HCC often keeps masslike enhancement while dying slowly, so 2017 can miscall it Viable and prompt unnecessary re-treatment (pp 6–7).
- **2024 changes** (Summary box p 2; section *LI-RADS TRA Version 2024*, p 7; Figure 1, p 3):
  - **Two independent cores**: a **nonradiation** TRA algorithm and a new **radiation** TRA core.
  - Criteria simplified to **masslike enhancement (any degree, any phase)**; washout and enhancement similar to pretreatment dropped as Viable criteria.
  - Optional MRI **ancillary features (AFs)** — restricted diffusion or mild-to-moderate T2 hyperintensity — used **only to upgrade** (nonradiation: Equivocal → Viable; radiation: Nonprogressing → Viable).
  - New radiation category **LR-TR Nonprogressing** (persistent masslike enhancement, stable or decreasing in size); LR-TR Equivocal is not used after radiation.
- Not applicable to systemic or immunotherapy response (section *Limitations and Conclusion*, p 11).

## Key Findings / Claims

### Scope and Timing

- **Nonradiation LRT** = thermal ablation (microwave ablation [MWA], radiofrequency ablation) and intraarterial macroembolic therapy (transarterial embolization, transarterial chemoembolization [TACE]) (introduction, p 1). **Radiation-based LRT** = stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT) and transarterial radioembolization (TARE, yttrium 90 [Y-90]) (p 1; section *Update for Radiation-Based Therapies*, p 10).
- **Imaging:** multiphase contrast-enhanced CT or MRI, extracellular or hepatobiliary contrast agents (section *LI-RADS TRA*, p 1).
- **Timing:**

| Setting | When to apply TRA | Locator |
|---|---|---|
| After LRT, general | About every 3 months | *LI-RADS TRA*, p 1 |
| After TARE | 1–3 months after treatment, then 3-month intervals | *Update for Radiation-Based Therapies*, p 10 |
| After SBRT | 3 months after treatment, then 3-month intervals | *Update for Radiation-Based Therapies*, p 10 |
| LR-TR Equivocal (nonradiation) | Short-term follow-up at 3-month intervals acceptable | *LR-TR Equivocal*, p 9 |
| LR-TR Nonprogressing (radiation) | 3-month follow-up imaging to reassess | *LR-TR Nonprogressing*, p 11 |

- **New lesion outside the treated territory** → categorize with the LI-RADS v2018 diagnostic algorithm, not the TRA (p 8).
- MRI **subtraction imaging** and **noncontrast CT** are often necessary to confirm lack of enhancement, because nonradiation LRT can cause T1 precontrast hyperintensity from coagulation necrosis (section *TRA and Challenges After Ablative and Embolic LRT*, pp 1–2).

### Nonradiation TRA Algorithm (2024)

*Figure 1A, p 3; category text pp 8–10.* Step 1: assess for **masslike enhancement (any degree, any phase)** in the treated lesion, along the treated lesion margin, or along the surgical margin.

| Category | 2024 criteria | Locator |
|---|---|---|
| **LR-TR Nonevaluable** | Presence of masslike enhancement cannot be assessed due to image degradation or omission | Figure 1A |
| **LR-TR Nonviable** | No masslike enhancement present (any degree, any phase) in treated lesion, along treated lesion margin, or along surgical margin. Examples: complete lesion disappearance, no intralesional enhancement, smooth perilesional enhancement, parenchymal perfusional changes without masslike enhancement | Figure 1A footnote; *LR-TR Nonviable*, pp 8–9 |
| **LR-TR Equivocal** | Uncertainty about masslike enhancement (presence or morphology) | Figure 1A; *LR-TR Equivocal*, p 9 |
| **LR-TR Viable** | Masslike enhancement (any degree, any phase) in treated lesion, along treated lesion margin, or along surgical margin; the enhancement may be nodular, smooth, or irregular | Figure 1A; *LR-TR Viable*, p 9 |

**Step 2 (optional) — AFs favoring viability, upgrade LR-TR Equivocal → LR-TR Viable only** (Figure 1A; p 10):
- What: **restricted diffusion (any degree)** or **mild-to-moderate T2 hyperintensity**.
- Where: must correspond to the **area of uncertain masslike enhancement**; at any time after nonradiation LRT.
- **Hepatobiliary phase (HBP) hypointensity is not an AF** despite strong evidence it improves sensitivity: supporting data come only from patients with hepatitis B; distinguishing HBP-hypointense nonviable from viable tumor may be hard and raise interreader variability; working-group consensus (p 10).

**What changed from 2017 (nonradiation)** (pp 8–10):

| Category | 2017 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Nonviable | "Treatment-specific expected enhancement pattern" was one criterion (subjective; expected pattern differs by LRT) | No masslike enhancement; explicit nonviable patterns listed (smooth perilesional enhancement, parenchymal perfusional changes without masslike enhancement) |
| Viable | Nodular, masslike, or thick irregular tissue in or along treated lesion with **any of** APHE, washout, or enhancement similar to pretreatment | Masslike enhancement (any degree, any phase) only — **washout and enhancement similar to pretreatment removed** (low sensitivity and specificity after treatment) |
| Equivocal | Enhancement atypical for the treatment-specific expected pattern and not meeting probably/definitely viable criteria | Uncertainty about masslike enhancement (presence or morphology); **kept** by consensus |
| AFs | None used in TRA | Optional upgrade Equivocal → Viable (diffusion restriction, mild-to-moderate T2 hyperintensity) |

- **Why Equivocal was kept** (p 9): may harbor small areas of viable tumor not large enough to diagnose; HCC doubling time approximately 86–150 days, so 3-month follow-up should show minimal growth; these patients may have more morbidity and mortality from underlying liver disease than from HCC, and early unnecessary treatment can cause hepatic decompensation.

### Radiation TRA Core (2024, new)

*Figure 1B, p 3; category text pp 10–11.* For HCC after SBRT or TARE. Step 1: assess for **masslike enhancement (any degree, any phase)** and its **change over time** after LRT, in the treated lesion or along its margin. Basis: two imaging biomarkers — **enhancement** and **size** of the treated tumor (p 10).

| Category | 2024 criteria | Locator |
|---|---|---|
| **LR-TR Nonevaluable** | Presence of masslike enhancement cannot be assessed due to image omission or degradation | Figure 1B |
| **LR-TR Nonviable** | No masslike enhancement (any degree, any phase) in treated lesion, along treated lesion margin, or along surgical margin. Examples as for nonradiation. **No size-based criteria** (tumor no longer enhancing) | Figure 1B; *LR-TR Nonviable*, p 10 |
| **LR-TR Nonprogressing** | Masslike enhancement (any degree, any phase) in treated lesion or along its margin that is **stable or decreased in size** over time after LRT (both criteria must be met). Used as long as residual enhancement persists | Figure 1B; *LR-TR Nonprogressing*, p 11 |
| **LR-TR Viable** | Masslike enhancement (any degree, any phase) in treated lesion or along its margin that is **new or increased in size** over time after LRT | Figure 1B; *LR-TR Viable*, p 10 |

**Step 2 (optional) — AFs favoring viability, upgrade LR-TR Nonprogressing → LR-TR Viable only** (Figure 1B; p 11):
- What: **restricted diffusion (any degree)** or **mild-to-moderate T2 hyperintensity**; Figure 1B adds "which is new or increased in size over time after LRT".
- Where: in the area of **stable or decreasing masslike enhancement**; at any time after radiation-based LRT.

**Rules and pitfalls after radiation** (pp 7, 10–11):
- **LR-TR Equivocal is not used** for radiation-treated HCC; questionable viable disease → LR-TR Nonprogressing → 3-month follow-up imaging (p 11).
- Radiation-induced **perilesional parenchymal enhancement is expected** and does not by itself qualify for Nonprogressing (p 11); do not confuse it with new or increasing tumoral enhancement. External beam radiation: changes surround the target per planning volumes; TARE: wedge-shaped vascular distribution. Compare pre- and posttreatment images (p 10).
- **Long gap between pretreatment imaging and treatment** → first posttreatment study may show apparent growth and be miscalled Viable; if available, use intraprocedural TARE imaging or SBRT simulation imaging as the size baseline, since size is now a criterion (p 11).
- **Ablative-dose Y-90 segmentectomy** often causes immediate cell death → no residual enhancement → Nonviable is straightforward (p 10).
- **Persistent enhancement after radiation (particularly external beam radiation therapy) is expected:** watch-and-wait is warranted despite possible pathologically viable cells (p 7).
- **Nonradiation vs radiation — the re-treatment rule:** after nonradiation LRT, posttreatment enhancement calls for **immediate re-treatment**; radiation-treated HCC with persistent enhancement → imaging surveillance, with **re-treatment only after multidisciplinary conference discussion** (p 7).
- Persistent APHE after radiation usually seen at least 3–6 months after treatment, then slowly starts resolving; timing data extremely limited (p 6). Complete necrosis without enhancement can also occur after TARE, particularly segmental or ablative-dose treatment (p 6).

### Supporting Evidence (as cited by the review)

**2017 TRA features after nonradiation LRT** (pp 2–3; the ref 16 meta-analysis used explant pathology as reference; ref 7 is an individual-patient-data meta-analysis):

| Feature | Specificity (meta-analysis, ref 16) | Sensitivity (refs 8–10, 16) | Diagnostic odds ratio (refs 8–10, 16) | Pooled sensitivity (ref 7) | κ (refs 16, 17) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Masslike APHE | 95% (95% confidence interval [CI]: 88, 98) | 81% (63, 92) | 81 (25, 261) | 67% (51, 81) | 0.67–0.75 |
| Washout | 96% (94, 98) | 55% (34, 75) | 32 (13, 82) | 43% (26, 62) | 0.52–0.64 |
| Enhancement similar to pretreatment | 98% (92, 100) | 21% (6, 53) | 14 (5, 39) | 24% (1.5, 36) | 0.41–0.76 |

- In HCCs with incomplete pathologic necrosis, only 2% showed washout alone and 1% enhancement similar to pretreatment alone (ref 7, p 2).
- Sensitivity higher at MRI than CT (ref 16, p 2): APHE 87% (67, 91) vs 50% (36, 64); washout 66% (41, 84) vs 35% (17, 59); enhancement similar to pretreatment 32% (16, 54) vs 15% (1, 68).
- Evidence low quality by Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation (GRADE); masslike APHE inferred to play the larger role (p 2).

**Overall 2017 categorization, nonradiation LRT** (pp 2–3):
- Interreader agreement: pooled κ 0.71 (95% CI: 0.53, 0.89) MRI, 0.71 (0.65, 0.78) CT (ref 19).
- Viable disease at explant: pooled sensitivity 62% (49, 74), specificity 87% (76, 93) (ref 17).
- LR-TR Viable for incomplete necrosis: sensitivity 30%–79%, specificity 85%–100%; complete necrosis: sensitivity 81%–87%, specificity 81%–85% (refs 8–10, 20, 21).
- Versus other systems: pooled κ 0.70–0.71 LI-RADS TRA, 0.69–0.76 European Association for the Study of the Liver (EASL), 0.67–0.78 modified Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (mRECIST) (ref 22). Viable disease: LR-TR Viable sensitivity 58% (45, 70), specificity 93% (88, 96); mRECIST Viable 56% (42, 69) and 86% (72, 94) (ref 21).
- **LR-TR Equivocal is unreliable:** interreader κ 0.25–0.71 (refs 6, 20, 23). Treating Equivocal as Viable vs as Nonviable: positive predictive value for incomplete necrosis 86%–96% vs 43%–54%; specificity 81%–85% vs 85%–97% (refs 9, 10). Meta-analysis: >50% and up to 83% of LR-TR Equivocal HCCs incompletely necrotic at pathology (ref 17). Moderate quality by GRADE (p 3).
- Conclusion: 2017 Viable and Nonviable categories apply well to loco-ablative and nonradiation macroembolic intraarterial therapies (p 3).

**AFs** (section *Diagnostic Utility of Ancillary Features for TRA*, p 6): adding diffusion restriction, T2 hyperintensity or HBP hypointensity to major features raised sensitivity 66%–92% vs 57%–85% with no impact on specificity (91%–98% vs 94%–97%) (refs 24–29); Equivocal lesions upgraded to Viable when one of the three AFs was present. Moderate quality by GRADE.

**Radiation** (pp 6–7, 10):
- Limited radiology-pathology data (historically few transplanted after radiation). Persistent masslike enhancement with unchanged or decreasing size common, particularly after SBRT; delayed necrosis after TARE. Moderate quality by GRADE (p 6).
- Mechanism: radiation causes either immediate cell death or cellular senescence (viable, metabolically active, cannot replicate) — so tumors can keep APHE (p 7).
- One explant study (ref 12): persistent enhancement in completely necrotic tumors in approximately 45% of SBRT-treated HCCs imaged within 3 months of transplant; longer time to transplant → greater loss of APHE (odds ratio 0.68 [95% CI: 0.45, 0.91]; P = .03) and increasing necrosis (odds ratio 0.2 [0.04, 0.79]; P = .03) (p 7).
- Y-90 has been incorporated into the updated Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) classification system (p 10).

## Relevance to Wiki

- [[li-rads]] — adds the LR-TR categories for treated HCC, absent there now: the 2024 nonradiation algorithm and radiation core tables, optional AF upgrade rules, timing after TARE/SBRT, and the 2017 → 2024 changes. Complements [[acr-2018-li-rads-ct-mri-core]] (diagnostic algorithm; still used for new lesions outside the treated territory).
- [[hepatocellular-carcinoma]] — posttreatment imaging after ablation, TACE, TARE and SBRT: follow-up intervals, Equivocal/Nonprogressing → 3-month imaging, persistent post-radiation enhancement → surveillance and multidisciplinary discussion before re-treatment; TRA not for systemic/immunotherapy (mRECIST applicable there).

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Figure 1B vs text, radiation AF upgrade:** Figure 1B requires the AF to be "new or increased in size over time after LRT"; the *LR-TR Nonprogressing* text (p 11) requires only that it correspond to the area of stable or decreasing masslike enhancement, at any time after radiation LRT.
- **Version label:** Figure 4 legend (p 5) says the upgrade is possible "in version 2023"; everywhere else the update is version 2024.
- **Surgical margin:** radiation Nonviable text (p 10) includes "along surgical margin", but radiation Viable/Nonprogressing criteria and Figure 1B Step 1 name only the treated lesion and its margin.
- **Running header** reads "Tumor Response Assessment"; the title reads "Treatment Response Assessment".
- **Article type:** a review describing the algorithm approved by the LI-RADS Steering Committee; the ACR TRA core documents themselves are on the ACR LI-RADS website (p 8).
- **Stated limitations / needs validation** (p 11): not applicable to systemic or immunotherapy (other algorithms, eg mRECIST, apply); AFs exclude HBP hypointensity — needs study in patients without hepatitis B; updated lexicon and structured reporting template needed; only one study has evaluated 2017 TRA after SBRT; validation studies of 2024 across all LRTs, especially radiation, and longitudinal prospective survival trials are required.
- Evidence quality for 2017 feature performance graded low, for Equivocal performance, AFs and post-radiation imaging moderate (GRADE, pp 2–3, 6).
