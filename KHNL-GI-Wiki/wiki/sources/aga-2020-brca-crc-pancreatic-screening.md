## Summary

An American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) Clinical Practice Update addressing a gap that sends patients to gastrointestinal (GI) clinics without an answer: *BRCA1/2* carriers are identified constantly by multigene panel testing, and they ask whether their colon and pancreas need special screening. Breast and ovarian risks are well characterized; the gastrointestinal risks are not, and the resulting vacuum has produced a wide range of screening practices.

This is a **Commentary-format Clinical Practice Update**. It reviews the published colorectal cancer (CRC) and pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) risk literature and provides expert perspective. **It does not issue numbered Best Practice Advice statements and assigns no Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) ratings of its own** — the only GRADE ratings appearing in the document belong to the external Cancer of the Pancreas Screening (CAPS) Consortium consensus it cites. Its operative guidance sits in the *Pancreatic Ductal Adenocarcinoma Screening Studies* section and the Conclusions.

The bottom line the authors reach is asymmetric between the two organs and between the two genes. For **CRC**, evidence for increased risk in *BRCA1* is limited and the magnitude very modest; for *BRCA2* there is no evidence of increased CRC risk at all. For **PDAC**, there is evidence supporting an approximate **2- to 6-fold increased risk in *BRCA2* carriers** ascertained from high-risk families, while risk in *BRCA1* carriers is less convincing — some studies report no increased risk, others up to 4-fold.

That asymmetry drives the practical split: **no change to CRC screening** on the basis of carrier status alone, but **consideration of PDAC screening** in selected carriers with family history, performed at a high-volume center and preferably in a research setting.

## Key Findings / Claims

### The decision rule for CRC screening

The update's expert perspective, in its own terms:

- *BRCA* carriers with a **first-degree relative with CRC or advanced adenoma** → offer CRC screening at **age 40 years**, as per current family history–based recommendations.
- Any carrier **with symptoms** — the document names **rectal bleeding** and **iron deficiency anemia** — → consider **expedited diagnostic evaluation**.
- *BRCA* carriers **without signs or symptoms of CRC and without family history of CRC or advanced adenoma** → **follow average-risk guidelines** for screening, until further evidence is available to clarify whether alternate strategies may be warranted.

**The reasoning behind the threshold, which is the part that makes the rule usable:** a **2-fold** increased risk over the general population — the level conferred by having a first-degree relative with CRC — is the criterion current guidelines use for earlier or more intensive screening. It is not clear that the **1.5-fold** increased risk among *BRCA1* carriers is sufficient to warrant earlier or more intensive screening. For scale, CRC risks in *BRCA1/2* carriers are significantly lower than in Lynch syndrome, where risk is increased **10- to 17-fold**.

### The decision rule for PDAC screening

- **CAPS (International Cancer of the Pancreas Screening) Consortium:** reached **93% consensus** on offering screening to ***BRCA2* carriers with at least 1 affected first-degree relative, or at least 2 affected relatives of any degree** — a **GRADE 2** recommendation.
- **Consensus was not reached** for screening in ***BRCA1* carriers with at least 1 affected first-degree relative**, for a **GRADE 3** recommendation.
- **National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN):** recommends consideration of PDAC screening in *BRCA1/2* carriers with an affected **first- or second-degree relative**, performed in experienced high-volume centers, ideally under research conditions, after careful discussion with patients about limitations and uncertainties.
- **Recommendations differ on the criterion of the number and degree of affected family members that warrant screening in carriers** — the document states this disagreement explicitly rather than resolving it.
- **Modality:** emerging evidence supports **endoscopic ultrasound and magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography** screening, with higher rates of lower stage at PDAC detection, higher rates of surgical resection, and favorable short-term survival — although larger and longer studies are needed before PDAC screening can be definitively endorsed.
- **Setting:** at this time PDAC screening should be performed in a **high-volume center, preferably in a research setting**, in order to generate more evidence to support and refine screening in high-risk populations.

### CRC risk — the underlying data

**Case-control studies (5 large studies; Ashkenazi Jewish or Polish founder mutations):** did not detect an increased risk of CRC associated with *BRCA1* or *BRCA2* pathogenic variants overall.

| Study | Design | Key result |
|---|---|---|
| Suchy 2010 (largest) | 2398 unselected CRC cases (Poland), 4570 unaffected controls; 3 *BRCA1* Polish founder mutations sequenced | prevalence **0.42% in cases vs 0.48% in controls**; **odds ratio (OR) 0.8**, *P* = .8 — not statistically different |
| Suchy 2010 (age) | same cohort | mean age of CRC significantly younger in *BRCA1* carriers: **57.0 vs 64.0 years**, *P* = .05 |
| Niell 2004 | 1422 unselected CRC + 1566 matched controls (Israel), Ashkenazi founder *BRCA* | CRC at **age 65 or younger: OR 3.14, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.64–15.43** |

**Cohort studies — mixed, and the document says so:**

| Cohort | Result |
|---|---|
| 1098 Ashkenazi women with personal history of breast or ovarian cancer | CRC risk increased **4-fold** after breast/ovarian diagnosis vs noncarriers in *BRCA1* (**hazard ratio [HR] 4.0, 95% CI 1.1–15.2**); **not significantly elevated** for *BRCA2* (HR 3.3, 95% CI 0.61–17.4) |
| 7015 *BRCA1*/*BRCA2* female carriers | **no** increased risk among all carriers vs general population; but **4.7-fold** increased risk among *BRCA1* carriers **aged 30–49** (**standardized incidence ratio [SIR] 4.76, 95% CI 2.21–9.0**) |
| 11,847 men and women from 699 families | **2-fold** increased risk for **colon** cancer (**relative risk [RR] 2.03, 95% CI 1.45–2.85**); **reduced** risk for **rectal** cancer (**RR 0.23, 95% CI 0.09–0.59**) |
| 74 male + 178 female *BRCA1/2* carriers | cumulative CRC risk **not increased** for males or females vs general population |
| Korean study | significant **1.66-fold** increased risk among all *BRCA1/2* carriers, but **risks were not reported by gene** |

**Meta-analysis (Oh et al 2018)** — 14 moderate-quality studies (4 case-control, 10 cohort):

| Group | Odds ratio (95% CI) |
|---|---|
| All *BRCA* carriers | **OR 1.24 (1.02–1.51)** — small but statistically significant |
| *BRCA1* | **OR 1.49 (1.19–1.85)** — increased |
| *BRCA2* | **OR 1.10 (0.77–1.58)** — not increased |

Results were independent of study design, ascertainment, age and sex; the meta-analysis was of adequate quality with no evidence of publication bias, but there was heterogeneity in study designs, which limited assessment of risk in younger age groups.

**Prevalence of *BRCA1/2* pathogenic variants among CRC patients:** early-onset CRC **1.3%**, high-risk CRC **0.2%**, unselected CRC **1.0%**. Higher than expected by chance — but the document states this association is **not proof that *BRCA1/2* pathogenic variants play a causative role in CRC**.

**Limitations the authors attach to the cohort evidence:** focus on individuals with personal and/or family history of breast or ovarian cancer; examination of risk only among women in Ashkenazi cohorts; inclusion of anal cancer as an outcome in one study. All reviewed studies **lacked data on exposure to CRC screening** and generally had wide confidence intervals. Taken together, the authors conclude currently available cohort studies offer **insufficient evidence to support increased CRC risk**, and evidence for increased young-onset CRC comes from a subgroup analysis of a single study.

### PDAC risk — the underlying data

**Case-control (Mayo Clinic prospective PDAC registry, Minnesota/Arizona/Florida; 21 cancer predisposition genes sequenced; race/ethnicity-matched public-database controls):**

| Gene | Prevalence in cases | Odds of PDAC |
|---|---|---|
| *BRCA1* | **0.59%** | increased **2.58-fold** |
| *BRCA2* | **1.95%** | increased **6.20-fold** |

*Limitations: use of public reference controls; cases from referral centers that might not reflect general-population risk.*

**Cohort:**
- **Breast Cancer Linkage Consortium (BCLC), *BRCA2*:** 3728 individuals from 173 breast and ovarian cancer families; 14 PDACs observed among people with known, probable and unknown mutation status → **RR 3.51 (95% CI 1.87–6.58)** compared with expected PDAC cases in the general population.
- **BCLC, *BRCA1*:** 11,847 individuals from 699 families → **2.26-fold** increased risk of PDAC.
- The BCLC ascertained individuals based on family history of breast and/or ovarian cancer, controlled for ascertainment bias in multiple ways (although residual bias was still possible), and made efforts to confirm the PDAC diagnosis (not possible in all cases). **Multiple testing was not accounted for**, given that risk of more than 20 cancer types was assessed in the same cohort.
- Additional cohort studies ascertained people based on personal and/or family history of cancer and/or positive mutation status; **not all controlled for ascertainment bias, which may inflate risk estimates**. The studies finding more than 20-fold and 4.7-fold increased risk in *BRCA2* and *BRCA1* carriers respectively included a moderate sample from a single referral center and did not correct for ascertainment bias, **which likely overestimated risks**. The only study that did not find a statistically significantly increased risk among *BRCA2* carriers was limited by inclusion of women only and small numbers of incident cases.
- Further limitations across these studies: short follow-up time; lack of verification of PDAC diagnosis; lack of information on other risk factors including **tobacco use and obesity**; and lack of correction for multiple testing.

**Prevalence of *BRCA1/2* pathogenic variants among PDAC patients:**

| Population | Combined *BRCA1/2* prevalence |
|---|---|
| Unselected PDAC | **1.76% – 10.7%** |
| Familial PDAC | **4.3% – 24.4%** |

*BRCA2* prevalence was overall higher than *BRCA1* in both unselected and familial cases, although estimate ranges were wide. These estimates are higher than the population frequency of *BRCA1/2* mutations in the general population — although these studies do not prove causality.

### Conclusions, as stated

- **CRC:** "there is limited evidence to support increased risk in *BRCA1* carriers, although the magnitude of risk is very modest and there is no evidence to support increased risk for CRC among *BRCA2* carriers."
- **PDAC:** "there is evidence to support an approximate 2- to 6-fold increased risk in *BRCA2* carriers ascertained from high-risk families, and risk in *BRCA1* carriers is less convincing, with some studies reporting no increased risk and others reporting up to a 4-fold increased risk."
- **Future research** should determine appropriate family history criteria, incorporate risk factors, and investigate the role of **hemoglobin A1C** measurements and novel biomarkers for early PDAC detection.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **This update issues no numbered Best Practice Advice statements and no GRADE ratings of its own.** The GRADE 2 and GRADE 3 labels in the text belong to the CAPS Consortium consensus, not to the AGA. Any numbering applied to this document would not be the document's own.
- **CRC risk by gene is internally inconsistent across designs.** Case-control studies found no increased risk; the Oh meta-analysis found a significant *BRCA1* signal (OR 1.49) and none for *BRCA2* (OR 1.10); individual cohorts range from no increase to 4.7-fold in a narrow age band. The authors' resolution is that the evidence is insufficient, not that the risk is established.
- **Colon versus rectum diverge in the same cohort** — 11,847 individuals from 699 families showed a 2-fold increased risk for colon cancer alongside a *reduced* risk for rectal cancer (RR 0.23). The document reports both without explanation.
- **PDAC screening eligibility criteria are openly unsettled** between CAPS and NCCN on the number and degree of affected relatives required — the update names the disagreement rather than resolving it, so a carrier can be eligible under one and not the other.
- **Screening efficacy for PDAC is not established.** The EUS/MRCP outcome data are described as emerging, with larger and longer studies needed before PDAC screening can be definitively endorsed — which is why the setting recommendation is a high-volume center, preferably in research.
- **Ascertainment bias runs through nearly all the PDAC cohort evidence**, and the authors state it likely overestimated the highest risk figures.
