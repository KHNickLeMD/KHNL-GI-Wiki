## Summary

- **What it is:** AGA CPU on using the [[flip-panometry|functional lumen imaging probe (FLIP)]] in esophageal practice — evaluation of esophageal symptoms, motor dysfunction, gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), and eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE). Updates the 2017 AGA FLIP CPU, emphasizing data published since.
- **Method:** commissioned and approved by the AGA Institute Governing Board and CPU Committee; internal (CPU Committee) and external peer review. Based on literature review + expert opinion. **No formal systematic review → the 11 Best Practice Advice (BPA) statements carry no ratings of evidence quality or recommendation strength.**
- **Position of FLIP:** complementary, not stand-alone. Neither high-resolution manometry (HRM), timed barium esophagram (TBE), nor FLIP is a "gold standard" for esophageal motility disorders in isolation; FLIP is interpreted within the clinical presentation, the endoscopy findings, and complementary tests. Protocol and terminology follow the [[dallas-2025-flip-panometry|Dallas Consensus FLIP Panometry classification]] (the CPU's reference 1).
- **Main uses:** inconclusive workup of obstructive symptoms; ruling out esophagogastric junction (EGJ) outflow obstruction (high negative predictive value [NPV] of normal EGJ opening); diagnosing achalasia when endoscopy is strongly supportive; persisting dysphagia in treated EoE; intraprocedural guidance of myotomy; persisting obstructive symptoms after achalasia treatment, antireflux surgery (ARS), or bariatric surgery. **Not** for diagnosing GERD.
- **Conclusions:** FLIP is safe, adds minimal time to endoscopy, does not appear to be affected by standard endoscopic sedation, and can give immediate management options from real-time interpretation. **Operator education is paramount** — reliably obstructive metrics must be distinguished from reactive spastic contractions or normal lumen occlusion.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Best Practice Advice Statements

*Ungraded (no evidence/strength ratings), numbered 1–11 as in the source.*

1. **BPA 1:** Clinicians should perform a high-quality upper endoscopy evaluating for esophageal pathology immediately prior to considering FLIP.
2. **BPA 2:** Clinicians should consider performing FLIP if alternate investigations are inconclusive in patients with symptoms of esophageal obstruction (ie, dysphagia, esophageal-type regurgitation, and/or meal-related chest pain), and may consider FLIP as part of index endoscopy for symptomatic patients when the procedure and interpretation expertise are readily available.
3. **BPA 3:** Clinicians may use common sedation options for endoscopy (ie, propofol, fentanyl, and/or midazolam), as these medications have not been demonstrated to have a clinically significant impact on FLIP findings.
4. **BPA 4:** Normal esophagogastric junction opening on FLIP has a high negative predictive value for disorders of esophagogastric junction outflow obstruction on high-resolution manometry. *(Body text heading: "NEO [normal EGJ opening] on FLIP has a high negative predictive value for disorders of EGJOO [EGJ outflow obstruction] on HRM.")*
5. **BPA 5:** If upper endoscopy demonstrates findings strongly supportive of achalasia, clinicians may use FLIP findings to diagnose achalasia.
6. **BPA 6:** If upper endoscopy does not demonstrate findings supportive of achalasia, abnormal esophagogastric junction opening on FLIP should prompt further diagnostic testing.
7. **BPA 7:** Clinicians may consider performing FLIP if the mechanism of persisting dysphagia in treated EoE is not apparent on high-quality upper endoscopy or histopathology.
8. **BPA 8:** Clinicians should not use FLIP findings to diagnose gastroesophageal reflux disease or to determine the necessity for antireflux intervention.
9. **BPA 9:** When available, clinicians should consider performing FLIP intraprocedurally during myotomy (per-oral endoscopic myotomy or laparoscopic Heller myotomy) to guide adequacy of LES [lower esophageal sphincter] disruption.
10. **BPA 10:** Clinicians may consider performing FLIP in the evaluation of persisting symptoms of esophageal obstruction (ie, dysphagia, esophageal-type regurgitation, and/or meal-related chest pain) after treatment of achalasia spectrum disorders.
11. **BPA 11:** Clinicians may consider performing FLIP in the evaluation of symptoms of esophageal obstruction after invasive foregut intervention (ie, antireflux or bariatric intervention).

### FLIP Protocol and Interpretation

| Catheter | Electrodes | Measures | Positioning (confirm EGJ "waist" on real-time display) | Fill sequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **8-cm (325N)** | 16 pairs, 0.5 cm apart | Primarily EGJ metrics | EGJ at balloon center; waist at **20 mL** | 30, 40, 50 mL; EGJ diameter, pressure, and EGJ distensibility index (EGJ-DI) at each volume; **record metrics from the 40-mL fill** |
| **16-cm (322N)** | 16 pairs, 1 cm apart | EGJ metrics **+ esophageal body contractile response** | **1–3 impedance sensor pairs distal to the EGJ**; waist at **30–40 mL** | 50, 60, 70 mL, each held **≥30 s**; EGJ-DI + pressure at **60 mL**; maximum EGJ diameter at **70 mL**; contractile pattern from the aggregate of 50/60/70 mL |

- Performed in the sedated patient, **after** high-quality endoscopy, **left lateral** position; catheter calibrated and referenced to atmospheric pressure, placed transorally.
- Resistance during positioning → withdraw and reposition (avoids damaging the distal pressure transducer). Endoscopic visualization may help with abnormal anatomy (esophageal diverticulum, hiatus hernia) or prior foregut surgery (e.g., Roux-en-Y gastric bypass).
- Distensibility index (DI) = ratio of cross-sectional area to bag pressure.
- **EGJ opening:** normal (NEO), reduced EGJ opening (REO), or inconclusive EGJ opening (IEO) from EGJ-DI + maximum EGJ diameter (Table 1).
- **Contractile response:** normal = multiple distinct antegrade contractions and/or repetitive antegrade contractions (RACs); abnormal = absent, diminished, disordered, or spastic (Figure 1). When distinct antegrade contractions or a spastic response are not seen, FLIP pressure separates **diminished (≤40 mm Hg)** from **disordered (>40 mm Hg)** in the text (Table 1 words it as <40 vs >40 mm Hg pressure increase).
- **Contraindication:** actively bleeding varices. Most data concern the EGJ and tubular esophagus.

#### Table 1 — Use of FLIP Findings in Making Obstructive Structural and Motor Diagnoses

| Diagnosis | EGJ metrics | Associated esophageal body contractile response | Clinical impression |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Normal** — no obstructive structural or motor explanation for symptoms | **NEO:** EGJ-DI **≥2.0 mm²/mm Hg** *and* maximum EGJ diameter **≥16 mm** | **Normal:** multiple (>1) distinct antegrade contractions | A major, actionable motor disorder is unlikely |
| **Obstruction** — findings support structural or motor obstruction in the presence of compatible symptoms and endoscopy findings | **REO:** EGJ-DI **<2.0 mm²/mm Hg** *and* maximum EGJ diameter **<12 mm** | **Absent:** no esophageal body contractions · **Diminished:** criteria not fulfilled for spastic, normal, or absent response, **<40 mm Hg** pressure increase | **Nonspastic achalasia is likely** |
| Obstruction (as above) | REO (as above) | **Spastic:** sustained LES contraction (sLESc) or sustained occluding contraction (SOC) · **Disordered:** criteria not fulfilled for spastic, normal, or absent response, **>40 mm Hg** pressure increase | **Spastic achalasia is likely** |
| Obstruction (as above) | REO (as above) | **Normal** | **Mechanical obstruction is possible** |
| **Inconclusive** — additional evaluation is necessary to define the mechanism for symptoms | **IEO:** isolated EGJ-DI **<2.0 mm²/mm Hg** *or* isolated maximum EGJ diameter **<16 mm**, but not REO | Any contractile response pattern | Motor and mechanical obstruction remain possible |

- *Note (source):* **EGJ metrics drive the final diagnosis** of obstruction or no obstruction; esophageal body contractile responses add characterization of secondary peristalsis.
- sLESc = **>5 s** LES diameter reduction with increase in pressure; SOC = **>10 s** nonpropagating lumen-occluding contraction with increase in pressure.

### Supporting Content by BPA

**BPA 1 — endoscopy first**
- Higher yield for erosive reflux changes or EoE if [[proton-pump-inhibitors|proton pump inhibitors (PPIs)]] are withheld for **2–4 weeks**.
- Note strictures, rings, mass lesions, diverticula, EoE mucosal changes, erosive esophagitis, Barrett's esophagus, and spastic changes; biopsy when appropriate.
- Retroflexion after gastric air insufflation: [[hiatal-hernia|hiatus hernia]] (report Hill or American Foregut Society grade), gastric cardia abnormalities, and postoperative anatomy (integrity and location of ARS) — all can affect catheter placement.
- No mucosal/structural explanation → withdraw endoscope, position FLIP catheter.

**BPA 2 — when to perform FLIP**
- EGJOO on HRM is **inconclusive** under Chicago Classification v4.0 → FLIP or TBE advised to confirm or refute its clinical significance.
- Achalasia remains possible despite normal integrated relaxation pressure on HRM → FLIP can identify obstructive EGJ metrics if clinical suspicion is high or HRM and TBE disagree.
- Discordance is common: in 126 symptomatic patients with HRM, FLIP, and TBE, concordance among all 3 for LES obstruction was **57%**.
- Concordant findings on >1 modality raise confidence before **irreversible LES disruption**; conservative treatment if a confident achalasia spectrum diagnosis cannot be made.
- At index endoscopy (expertise + equipment available): cost-effective when FLIP is completely normal or allows early achalasia diagnosis; adds on average **5 minutes**.
- Endoscopy not suggestive of achalasia + reduced or inconclusive EGJ opening → may prompt re-inspection for a subtle stricture or ring that could respond to dilation; FLIP is typically done **before** dilation.
- Spastic contractile responses may be primary or **secondary** to anatomic factors (hiatus hernia) or mucosal inflammation (e.g., EoE).
- Option when HRM fails or is unlikely to succeed: catheter cannot traverse the LES, discomfort/gagging prevents an adequate study, or other tests show a dilated or tortuous esophagus.

**BPA 3 — sedation**
- Chronic opiate use is associated with opiate-induced esophageal dysmotility on HRM; HRM evidence for an effect of acute opiate use is lacking.
- FLIP and HRM diagnoses did not differ significantly between conscious sedation (midazolam + fentanyl, n = 174) and monitored anesthesia care (propofol ± fentanyl, n = 280).
- **Sevoflurane** (inhalation general anesthetic) can give higher EGJ diameter and EGJ-DI than propofol and can impair secondary peristalsis; desflurane and isoflurane effects unknown → **avoid inhalation anesthetics** when assessing motility with FLIP until more data are available.

**BPA 4 — NPV of normal EGJ opening**
- 687 symptomatic patients without mechanical obstruction: NEO in **<1%** of 243 with HRM achalasia or TBE-confirmed conclusive EGJOO.
- 184 patients: **0 of 23** with HRM-defined achalasia had NEO.
- 17 patients with HRM-only EGJOO and NEO: none had a **≥5-cm barium column at 5 minutes** on TBE; 9 of 10 followed longitudinally did well with conservative treatment.
- → Further tests add little once NEO is found. EGJ-DI **without** maximum diameter has a lower NPV for ruling out REO; NPV for achalasia or conclusive EGJOO by EGJ-DI threshold: **90% (>2.0), 96% (>2.8), 97% (>3.0 mm²/mm Hg)**.
- 706 symptomatic patients: none of 108 with a normal contractile response (RACs) had achalasia on HRM.
- Absent contractile response occurs in **23%–43%** of patients with normal primary peristalsis on HRM → with NEO, absent secondary peristalsis does not necessarily indicate a distinct motor disorder.

**BPA 5 — endoscopy strongly supportive of achalasia**
- **92% (185/202)** with REO and absent or impaired/disordered contractile response had achalasia or conclusive (TBE-confirmed) EGJOO on HRM.
- High-quality endoscopy supplies pretest probability and helps separate idiopathic achalasia from **pseudoachalasia**. Endoscopic features: retained food/fluid, dilated lumen, resistance traversing the EGJ, mucosal stasis changes = CARS score.
- CARS **≥4** alone: specificity **99%**, sensitivity **68%–72%** for achalasia. CARS **0–1 + NEO:** NPV **100%** for achalasia spectrum disorders. CARS **≥4 + REO:** PPV **97%** for nonspastic achalasia.
- → Achalasia with **absent or diminished** contractile response can be diagnosed when the clinical presentation is complemented by an abnormal CARS score and REO.
- Diagnostic uncertainty, or FLIP suggesting **spasm** → HRM and/or TBE before irreversible LES disruption.

**BPA 6 — endoscopy not supportive of achalasia**
- REO is seen in **5%–11%** of symptomatic patients without HRM disorders of EGJOO; significance unclear, though TBE retention and symptom response to EGJ-directed therapy are reported in carefully selected patients.
- Borderline or inconclusive EGJ opening: **25%–28%** of patients with normal EGJ outflow pressure on HRM; may indicate obstruction.
- Abnormal EGJ opening without endoscopic support → more testing before diagnosing achalasia: re-inspect in the same session for strictures/rings (dilate); mechanical obstruction excluded → HRM and/or TBE.

**BPA 7 — EoE**
- **Distensibility plateau** (static esophageal diameter despite rising distention volume and pressure) is lower with prior food impaction than with dysphagia alone; low DI and especially low plateau correlate with longer symptom duration and diagnostic delay.
- Abnormal body contractile response is associated with more severe fibrostenosis. Composite FLIP assessment (body compliance, contractility, EGJ-DI, maximum diameter) correlated with higher eosinophil counts and endoscopic reference scores (n = 215).
- An **achalasia-like pattern** can occur in EoE (possibly fibrostenotic pseudoachalasia) → **irreversible LES-disruptive treatment only cautiously and only if supported by complementary tests.**
- Before treatment (n = 46), abnormal composite FLIP scores were associated with histologic PPI nonresponse.
- **FLIP should not be used to diagnose EoE**; it may help explain persistent dysphagia (luminal restriction, food-impaction likelihood, superimposed obstructive motility disorder), guide therapy, and monitor response.

**BPA 8 — GERD**
- No discriminative EGJ-DI threshold separates symptomatic GERD from controls; DI values in GERD fell within normative ranges.
- No correlation between EGJ-DI and acid exposure time (AET) in 2 retrospective cohorts (164 and 69 patients).
- 18 PPI nonresponders with normal AET on all 4 days of 96-hour wireless pH monitoring: DI **<2.8 mm²/mm Hg** was more likely (83.3%) than with abnormal reflux burden (23.5%) — attributed to FLIP detecting abnormal EGJ opening, not to a role in GERD.
- Impaired body contractile response associated with abnormal AET (n = 25).
- Hiatus hernia: **84%** correlation with HRM-identified hernia (n = 100), but FLIP can elicit or enlarge a hernia in up to **25%**; these patients trended toward TBE barium retention (possible obstructive component).
- Need for antireflux intervention is set by symptoms and reflux burden, not EGJ compliance or hernia; FLIP can identify achalasia and obstructive syndromes that refute reflux disease in PPI nonresponders.

**BPA 9 — intraprocedural FLIP during myotomy**
- During [[poem|per-oral endoscopic myotomy (POEM)]] or [[heller-myotomy|laparoscopic Heller myotomy (LHM)]]: can direct myotomy length and confirm adequacy of LES disruption in real time; EGJ-DI reliably improves after myotomy.
- [[pneumatic-dilation|Pneumatic dilation]]: data less robust, but FLIP has been found to assess adequacy of dilation.
- Intraoperative EGJ-DI **<2–3 mm²/mm Hg** may predict persisting symptoms (Eckardt scores; some inconsistent data) and may prompt additional myotomy.
- **High** intraoperative EGJ-DI may be associated with postoperative reflux and/or erosive esophagitis after LHM or POEM.
- Ideal target EGJ-DI after LES myotomy, and FLIP's role in guiding myotomy length (e.g., type III achalasia), still need defining.

**BPA 10 — persisting symptoms after achalasia treatment**
- After LES disruption, EGJ-DI **<2–3 mm²/mm Hg** in symptomatic patients → higher Eckardt scores and suboptimal emptying (ineffective or incomplete disruption) → may support **additional LES-directed therapy**.
- **NEO** → consider alternate explanations, including abnormal esophageal body anatomy and GERD.
- EGJ-DI rises dramatically during/immediately after POEM or LHM; mean values are lower at 12 months but remain above preoperative values.

**BPA 11 — after antireflux or bariatric intervention**
- Intraoperative FLIP during [[antireflux-surgery|ARS]] and endoscopic reflux procedures is feasible; EGJ-DI has been used to tailor fundoplication to prevent postoperative dysphagia.
- EGJ-DI **<2 mm²/mm Hg** after ARS associated with dysphagia (n = 175). Hiatal-closure tightness affects DI more than the fundoplication itself.
- **Proposed** intraoperative ranges: **Toupet EGJ-DI 2.6–3.7 mm²/mm Hg; Nissen EGJ-DI >2.2 mm²/mm Hg** — potential to guide deferring fundoplication, changing ARS type, or loosening/tightening the wrap. Another study found no metric differences between patients with and without suboptimal outcomes up to 2 years.
- Magnetic sphincter augmentation: lower diameter and EGJ-DI than ARS with similar symptomatic outcomes in one study; in another, more postoperative dysphagia than partial or complete fundoplication despite similar EGJ-DI.
- After Roux-en-Y gastric bypass or sleeve gastrectomy ([[bariatric-surgery]]): more secondary dysmotility (most often **absent contractility**) than obese patients with native anatomy.
- Symptomatic patients after ARS: abnormal secondary peristalsis in **84%**, possibly worsening with longer time since ARS and/or EGJ outflow resistance.
- **Provided EGJOO disorders were ruled out before the intervention**, FLIP can guide management, including EGJ dilation when postoperative obstructive symptoms are confirmed by **REO**.

### Future Directions (source)
- Next steps for inconclusive and discordant findings; interpretation of paroxysmal and sustained LES/distal esophageal contractions (reactivity, hypersensitivity); artificial intelligence; response prediction (e.g., EoE); FLIP's role in functional dysphagia.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **Diminished vs disordered cut at exactly 40 mm Hg:** AGA text — diminished **≤40**, disordered **>40** mm Hg; AGA Table 1 — **<40** vs **>40** mm Hg pressure increase; [[dallas-2025-flip-panometry|Dallas 2025]] statements — diminished **<40**, disordered **≥40** mmHg at 60 mL. Both are 2025 tier-1 documents; the wiki uses the Dallas definition (dedicated consensus, cited by AGA as its classification reference).
- **SOC / sLESc definitions:** AGA Table 1 gives only duration + pressure rise (SOC >10 s; sLESc >5 s); Dallas adds lumen occlusion **<6 mm** and pressure rise **>35 mmHg** for SOC. Not conflicting — Dallas is more specific.
- **Catheter positioning:** AGA — 1–3 impedance sensor pairs distal to the EGJ (16-cm); Dallas — 2 sensors (2 cm) below the crural diaphragm.
- **Wording of the 202-patient cohort:** AGA describes REO with "absent or impaired/disordered" contractile response; Dallas reports the same 92% figure for nonspastic obstruction (diminished/absent).
- **Open (source):** ideal EGJ-DI targets after myotomy; FLIP role in myotomy length (type III achalasia); effects of desflurane/isoflurane; significance of REO with normal HRM outflow pressures; FLIP in functional dysphagia.
