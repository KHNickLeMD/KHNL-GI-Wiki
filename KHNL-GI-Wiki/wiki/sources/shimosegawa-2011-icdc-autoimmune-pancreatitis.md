## Summary

The consensus of a working group convened at the 14th Congress of the International Association of Pancreatology (Fukuoka, 11–13 July 2010), merging the previously competing Japanese (JPS 2002/2006), Korean, Asian, HISORt and Italian criteria into one set. It is **the defining document for how autoimmune pancreatitis (AIP) is diagnosed** and the source of the **level 1 / level 2** vocabulary used everywhere since.

Its architecture: **five cardinal features** — **P** parenchymal imaging, **D** ductal imaging, **S** serology, **OOI** other organ involvement, **H** histology — plus an **optional sixth criterion, Rt (response to steroid)**. Each feature is graded **level 1** (higher diagnostic reliability) or **level 2**. The combination of features, read against whether parenchymal imaging is **typical** or **indeterminate**, yields **definitive** or **probable** **type 1 AIP** (lymphoplasmacytic sclerosing pancreatitis, LPSP — an IgG4-related disease), **type 2 AIP** (idiopathic duct-centric pancreatitis, IDCP), or **AIP–not otherwise specified**.

Two design decisions drive the whole document. First, **the enemy is pancreatic cancer**: the stated goal is to diagnose AIP without misdiagnosing cancer as AIP, so atypical imaging in a patient with obstructive jaundice or a mass is managed as cancer unless collateral evidence is strong and the cancer workup is negative. Second, **the diagnostic steroid trial is deliberately constrained** — prednisone **0.6–1 mg/kg for 2 weeks**, run by pancreatologists, **only after a negative cancer workup including endoscopic ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration**, with reassessment of imaging and CA 19-9.

**AIP in the burnt-out stage is explicitly out of scope.**

## Key Findings / Claims

### Level 1 and level 2 criteria for type 1 AIP (Table 2)

| | Criterion | **Level 1** | **Level 2** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P** | Parenchymal imaging | **Typical:** diffuse enlargement with delayed enhancement (sometimes with rim-like enhancement) | **Indeterminate** (incl. atypical): segmental/focal enlargement with delayed enhancement |
| **D** | Ductal imaging (endoscopic retrograde pancreatography, ERP) | **Long (>1/3 the length of the main pancreatic duct) or multiple strictures without marked upstream dilatation** | Segmental/focal narrowing without marked upstream dilatation (**duct size <5 mm**) |
| **S** | Serology | **IgG4 >2× the upper limit of normal (ULN)** | **IgG4 1–2× ULN** |
| **OOI** | Other organ involvement | **a** Histology of extrapancreatic organs — **any 3 of:** (1) marked lymphoplasmacytic infiltration with fibrosis and without granulocytic infiltration, (2) storiform fibrosis, (3) obliterative phlebitis, (4) abundant (**>10 cells/high-power field [HPF]**) IgG4-positive cells. **b** Typical radiological evidence — **at least 1 of:** (1) segmental/multiple proximal (hilar/intrahepatic), or proximal **and** distal, bile duct stricture; (2) retroperitoneal fibrosis | **a** Histology of extrapancreatic organs, **including endoscopic bile-duct biopsies** — **both of:** (1) marked lymphoplasmacytic infiltration without granulocytic infiltration, (2) abundant (>10 cells/HPF) IgG4-positive cells. **b** Physical or radiological evidence — **at least 1 of:** (1) symmetrically enlarged salivary/lachrymal glands, (2) radiological renal involvement of the type described with AIP |
| **H** | Histology of the pancreas | **LPSP (core biopsy or resection), at least 3 of:** (1) periductal lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate without granulocytic infiltration, (2) obliterative phlebitis, (3) storiform fibrosis, (4) abundant (>10 cells/HPF) IgG4-positive cells | **LPSP (core biopsy), any 2 of** the same four features |
| **Rt** | Response to steroid (optional) | **Diagnostic steroid trial — rapid (≤2 weeks) radiologically demonstrable resolution or marked improvement** in pancreatic/extrapancreatic manifestations | — |

- **Atypical imaging footnote (the cancer guard):** low-density mass, pancreatic ductal dilatation or distal atrophy in a patient with obstructive jaundice and/or a pancreatic mass is **highly suggestive of pancreatic cancer**. Manage as cancer **unless** collateral evidence for AIP is strong **and** a thorough cancer workup is negative.
- **Endoscopic biopsy of the duodenal papilla is a useful adjunct**, because the ampulla is often pathologically involved in AIP.

### Diagnosis of definitive and probable type 1 AIP (Table 1)

| Diagnosis | Primary basis | Imaging evidence | Collateral evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Definitive type 1** | Histology | Typical / indeterminate | **Histologically confirmed LPSP (level 1 H)** |
| **Definitive type 1** | Imaging | **Typical** | **Any non-D level 1 or level 2** |
| **Definitive type 1** | Imaging | **Indeterminate** | **Two or more from level 1** (+ level 2 D\*) |
| **Definitive type 1** | Response to steroid | Indeterminate | **Level 1 S/OOI + Rt**, or **level 1 D + level 2 S/OOI/H + Rt** |
| **Probable type 1** | — | Indeterminate | **Level 2 S/OOI/H + Rt** |

\* In this setting **level 2 D counts as level 1**.

### Level 1 and level 2 criteria for type 2 AIP (Table 5)

| | Criterion | **Level 1** | **Level 2** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P** | Parenchymal imaging | **Typical:** diffuse enlargement with delayed enhancement (± rim-like enhancement) | **Indeterminate** (incl. atypical): segmental/focal enlargement with delayed enhancement |
| **D** | Ductal imaging (ERP) | Long (>1/3 of the main pancreatic duct) or multiple strictures without marked upstream dilatation | Segmental/focal narrowing without marked upstream dilatation (duct size <5 mm) |
| **OOI** | Other organ involvement | — | **Clinically diagnosed inflammatory bowel disease** |
| **H** | Histology (core biopsy/resection) | **IDCP — both of:** (1) granulocytic infiltration of the duct wall (granulocytic epithelial lesion, GEL) with or without granulocytic acinar inflammation; (2) **absent or scant (0–10 cells/HPF) IgG4-positive cells** | **Both of:** (1) granulocytic **and** lymphoplasmacytic acinar infiltrate; (2) absent or scant (0–10 cells/HPF) IgG4-positive cells |
| **Rt** | Response to steroid | Rapid (≤2 weeks) radiologically demonstrable resolution or marked improvement | — |

### Diagnosis of type 2 AIP (Table 3) and AIP–NOS (Table 4)

| Diagnosis | Imaging evidence | Collateral evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Definitive type 2** | Typical / indeterminate | **Histologically confirmed IDCP (level 1 H)**, OR **clinical inflammatory bowel disease + level 2 H + Rt** |
| **Probable type 2** | Typical / indeterminate | **Level 2 H / clinical inflammatory bowel disease + Rt** |
| **AIP–not otherwise specified** | Typical / indeterminate | **D1/2 + Rt** (a case with only ductal evidence) |

- **Type 2 requires tissue.** "Definitive type 1" can be reached with surrogate criteria and no histology; **definite IDCP requires histological confirmation.**
- AIP that meets none of these — including histologically unconfirmed but clinically suspected type 2 — is categorized as **"probable AIP."**

### Serology — the panel's four points
1. Elevation of **one or more of IgG, IgG4 and antinuclear antibody** is common in AIP, but each is **falsely elevated in pancreatic cancer and other disorders**; measured as a panel, **false positivity can be as high as 40%**.
2. **Serum IgG4 is the single best marker**, so **only serum IgG4 is admitted as the serological criterion** in the ICDC. Because the **upper limit of normal varies between laboratories, the ICDC uses fold elevation above normal, never an absolute value** — the document prints **no mg/dL cut-point**.
3. **Marked elevation (>2× ULN) is strongly suggestive of AIP** in the setting of obstructive jaundice or a pancreatic mass.
4. **Serological elevation alone is not sufficient** to diagnose AIP unless it is seen with a typical imaging finding.

### Other organ involvement — what does and does not count
- OOI here is a manifestation of **IgG4-related systemic disease**. **Other unrelated autoimmune disorders — rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthropathy, true seropositive Sjögren — are not typically associated with AIP and should not be counted as OOI.**
- OOI may be established by **histology**, by **imaging** (proximal bile duct stricture, retroperitoneal fibrosis) or by **clinical examination** (salivary gland enlargement).

### Distinguishing type 1 from type 2
- **Neither imaging nor steroid response distinguishes the types.** Only serology, OOI and histology do.
- **Typical serological abnormalities and OOI are seen only in type 1.**
- **Inflammatory bowel disease associates with both, more so with type 2.**
- **Absence of serological abnormality or OOI does not imply type 2** — type 1 can also be seronegative and without OOI.

### Probability of AIP vs pancreatic cancer by computed tomography (CT)/magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)
1. Obstructive jaundice with a **diffusely enlarged pancreas (especially with a capsule-like rim), no ductal dilatation/cutoff, no mass** → **highly likely AIP**; less collateral evidence is required.
2. Findings typical of cancer — **low-density mass on contrast-enhanced CT, ductal dilatation/cutoff, distal atrophy** → **treat as pancreatic cancer** unless the cancer workup is negative and collateral evidence for AIP is strong.
3. Findings typical of neither → **investigate for cancer first**; consider AIP only if that workup is negative and collateral evidence is strong.

**Before AIP was recognized as a clinical entity, 2%–3% of patients resected for suspected pancreatic cancer had AIP.**

### Response to steroids — the diagnostic trial
- **Regimen: prednisone 0.6–1 mg/kg, reassess imaging and CA 19-9 after 2 weeks.** Two weeks was chosen on the single study that examined the question (Moon et al.), in which **no patient with pancreatic cancer "responded" to steroids**.
- **What counts as a response:** definitive improvement of imaging abnormalities, including biliary strictures and pancreatic enlargement. **CA 19-9 falls with treatment; a rising CA 19-9 means the AIP diagnosis is wrong.**
- Resolution of imaging abnormalities **may take weeks to months** and may be accompanied by **gland atrophy**.
- **Only after a negative cancer workup including endoscopic ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration**, and **only by pancreatologists**.
- **Use sparingly.** A steroid trial is not a substitute for a thorough search for an etiology; *"if steroids work, it must be AIP"* **is fraught with problems when there is no collateral evidence**.
- Symptomatic improvement and a sense of well-being are **not** the endpoint — they occur nonspecifically on steroids, including in pancreatic cancer patients. The endpoint is radiological.
- **A fall in IgG4 is not an endpoint either.** Steroids lower IgG4 in AIP, but falsely elevated IgG4 in pancreatic cancer and other non-AIP states also falls on steroids, so **an IgG4 "response" cannot be used to diagnose AIP**.
- **Spontaneous radiological improvement in pancreatic cancer–induced pancreatitis can be mistaken for steroid response.**

### Diagnostic algorithms (Figures 1–3)
- **Figure 1 — typical (level 1 P) imaging, type 1.** Recreated as a Mermaid flowchart on [[autoimmune-pancreatitis]].
- **Figure 2 — indeterminate/atypical imaging, type 1.** Requires a negative cancer workup first; then review imaging/physical exam for OOI, measure IgG4, and if still insufficient add endoscopic pancreatogram, ampullary biopsies with IgG4 stain, and review of any previous biopsy/resection specimen. Two or more level 1 criteria (with level 2 D counting as level 1) → **definitive type 1**; otherwise pancreatic core biopsy, and failing that a steroid trial routed by whether level 1 S/OOI, level 1 D + level 2 S/OOI/H, or only level 2 S/OOI/H is present.
- **Figure 3 — type 2.** Entered when there are no cardinal type 1 criteria on serology or OOI; negative cancer workup, then pancreatic core biopsy → level 1 H gives **definite type 2**, level 2 H goes to a steroid trial with the **presence of inflammatory bowel disease** deciding definite vs probable type 2; where biopsy is inconclusive or not performed, endoscopic pancreatogram with level 1/2 D plus steroid response gives **AIP–NOS** when IBD is absent.

### Histology notes
- AIP can be diagnosed on resection **or** biopsy, provided the specimen shows the characteristic features.
- A biopsy showing **some but not all** features of LPSP or IDCP (lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate with storiform fibrosis) is **supportive evidence**, not diagnostic.

### Presentations *not* suggestive of AIP
- **Marked cachexia, inability to eat, and narcotic-requiring pain** are more suggestive of cancer and rare in AIP.
- Patients with a typical acute presentation may concurrently meet criteria for acute pancreatitis (2 of 3: 3-fold elevated pancreatic enzymes, abdominal pain, CT evidence of pancreatic swelling), but **typical idiopathic pancreatitis and painful chronic pancreatitis are not commonly seen in histologically confirmed AIP**.
- Pancreatic atrophy, calcification, ductal dilation and other features of advanced **painless** chronic pancreatitis may appear on follow-up after a typical acute presentation.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **The serum IgG4 cut-point is written differently by the two sources.** This document's Table 2 gives level 1 serology as **IgG4 >2× ULN** and level 2 as **1–2× ULN**, deliberately in fold elevation only, because laboratory upper limits differ. [[aga-2019-autoimmune-pancreatitis]] reports it as **≥2× ULN** and attaches **280 mg/dL**; that absolute number is AGA's, not the ICDC's. Both are recorded on [[autoimmune-pancreatitis]] with their attributions. Otherwise no conflict: AGA 2019 names the same five cardinal features and remains the newer source on management.
- **Western practice and ductal imaging.** The D criterion is built on endoscopic retrograde pancreatography, which the document itself notes Western endoscopists generally avoid in suspected AIP — so a level 1 D finding is often unobtainable in US practice, and the criteria are in effect applied without it.
- **The 2-week steroid-trial duration rests on a single study** (Moon et al.), which the document states explicitly.
- **Burnt-out AIP is out of scope** — the criteria do not apply to it and the document says diagnosis at that stage is not easy.
