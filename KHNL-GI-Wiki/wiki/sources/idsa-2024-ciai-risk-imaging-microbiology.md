## Summary

Overview article of **part 1** of the IDSA update to its 2010 complicated intra-abdominal infection (cIAI) guideline: **21 recommendations** on risk assessment, diagnostic imaging and microbiological evaluation in adults, children and pregnant people. Part 1 = this overview + 7 topic manuscripts (risk assessment; imaging of appendicitis, of cholecystitis/cholangitis — see [[idsa-2024-ciai-imaging-cholecystitis-cholangitis]] — of diverticulitis and of abscess; blood cultures; intra-abdominal fluid cultures). Later parts will cover antimicrobial therapy and source control. Reviewed and endorsed (relevant recommendations) by the European Society of Clinical Microbiology and Infectious Diseases (ESCMID), the American Society for Microbiology (ASM) and the Pediatric Infectious Diseases Society (PIDS).

- **Definitions (p. S81):**
  - *Complicated* intra-abdominal infection — extends beyond the hollow viscus of origin into the peritoneal space or an otherwise sterile region of the abdominal cavity; associated with peritonitis ± abscess. Term does not describe severity or anatomy.
  - *Uncomplicated* — intramural inflammation of the gastrointestinal tract only, no peritoneal extension; can progress to complicated if not adequately treated.
- **Scope (p. S81–S82):** acute appendicitis, acute cholecystitis (acalculous and calculous), acute cholangitis, acute diverticulitis, abdominal abscess, secondary bowel perforation, acute necrotizing pancreatitis; children, pregnant adults and non-pregnant adults; community- or hospital-acquired infection. Several questions concern initial diagnosis, so some recommendations cover uncomplicated as well as complicated infection; each statement names its population.
- **Excluded (p. S82):** cancer; solid organ or bone marrow transplant; tubo-ovarian abscess; spontaneous bacterial peritonitis; liver cirrhosis; continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis–associated infection; inflammatory bowel disease (incl. Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis); non-perforated primary enteritis and/or colitis; perforation from diseases rare in North America (eg, intra-abdominal tuberculosis, histoplasmosis, strongyloidiasis).
- **Methods (p. S82):** systematic reviews; Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development, and Evaluation (GRADE). **Strong** = applies to most people with few exceptions; **conditional** = applies to the majority with many exceptions, shared decision-making important. **No high-quality evidence for any recommendation.** Where evidence was missing the panel gave limited guidance labeled **knowledge gap** rather than none.

Every recommendation is **conditional** or a **knowledge gap**; none is strong. Certainty is very low for most; low for the severity score, for not culturing at appendectomy, for HIDA and for not routinely obtaining blood cultures in children; moderate only for intra-abdominal cultures at source control. This part makes **no recommendation** specific to acute necrotizing pancreatitis or secondary bowel perforation.

## Key Findings / Claims

The document numbers no recommendations; **R1–R21 numbering is the wiki's**, in the order printed. Each is preceded in the document by its population/intervention/comparison/outcome (PICO) question. Strength and certainty as printed.

### Risk assessment (p. S82)

PICO: which severity-of-illness score, calculated within 24 hours of hospital or intensive care unit (ICU) admission, best predicts 30-day or in-hospital mortality in adults and children with cIAI?

| Wiki # | Population | Recommendation | Strength / certainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Adults with cIAI | Risk stratification by severity of illness is important for cIAI management. **If a severity of illness score is used, panel suggests APACHE II** as the preferred score for risk stratification **within 24 hours** of hospitalization or ICU admission | Conditional recommendation, low certainty of evidence |

- Remarks:
  - **World Society of Emergency Surgery Sepsis Severity Score** — specific to cIAI and performs well → **acceptable alternative** to APACHE II in adults.
  - **Children:** no cIAI-specific severity score can be recommended to guide management at present.
- The overview does not print the components or point values of either score.

### Imaging — suspected acute appendicitis (p. S82–S83)

PICO: initial imaging — ultrasound (US), computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI); and which modality for subsequent imaging if initial imaging is inconclusive (US or MRI only in pregnancy).

| Wiki # | Population | Step | Recommendation | Strength / certainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R2 | **Non-pregnant adults** | Initial | Panel suggests **abdominal CT** | Conditional recommendation, very low certainty of evidence |
| R3 | **Children and adolescents** | Initial | Panel suggests **abdominal US** | Conditional recommendation, very low certainty of evidence |
| R4 | Children and adolescents | Initial US **equivocal/non-diagnostic and clinical suspicion persists** | Panel suggests **abdominal MRI or CT** as subsequent imaging, **rather than another US** | Conditional recommendation, very low certainty of evidence |
| R5 | **Pregnant people** | Initial | Panel suggests **abdominal US** | Conditional recommendation, very low certainty of evidence |
| R6 | Pregnant people | Initial US **equivocal/non-diagnostic and clinical suspicion persists** | Panel suggests **MRI** as subsequent imaging | Conditional recommendation, very low certainty of evidence |

- Remarks — non-pregnant adults (R2):
  - **Intravenous (IV) contrast usually appropriate** whenever CT is obtained; CT without IV contrast also has high diagnostic accuracy and may be appropriate.
  - Because of CT's accuracy, immediate additional imaging beyond CT usually not necessary.
  - **CT negative but suspicion persists** → consider observation and supportive care, with or without antibiotics; **suspicion high** → consider surgical intervention.
  - US (when definitively positive or definitively negative) and MRI are also reasonably accurate and **may precede CT**, depending on patient and circumstances.
- Remarks — children (R3, R4):
  - US: generally readily available, but operator-dependent; can be equivocal. MRI: not always readily available; young children may need sedation. CT: generally readily available, but radiation exposure; may need IV contrast or sedation.
  - **CT with IV contrast usually appropriate** in children after equivocal US; CT without IV contrast may be appropriate.
  - Depending on the clinical situation, **observation may be appropriate instead of subsequent imaging**.
  - **Strong clinical suspicion after equivocal imaging** → exploratory laparoscopy or laparotomy may be considered if subsequent imaging would delay appropriate management.
- Remarks — pregnancy (R5, R6): **initial MRI also reasonable if MRI is readily available**; the US-then-MRI strategy would likely give the same results as MRI only.

### Imaging — suspected acute cholecystitis or acute cholangitis (p. S83–S84)

PICO: initial imaging — abdominal US or CT (US or MRI in pregnancy); subsequent imaging if inconclusive — CT, MRI/magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP) or hepatobiliary iminodiacetic acid (HIDA) scan. Full topic article: [[idsa-2024-ciai-imaging-cholecystitis-cholangitis]].

| Wiki # | Population | Step | Recommendation | Strength / certainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R7 | **Non-pregnant adults**, suspected acute cholecystitis **or** acute cholangitis | Initial | Panel suggests **abdominal US** | Conditional recommendation, very low certainty of evidence |
| R8 | Non-pregnant adults, suspected acute cholecystitis or acute cholangitis | Initial US **equivocal/non-diagnostic and clinical suspicion persists** | Panel suggests **abdominal CT** as subsequent imaging | Conditional recommendation, very low certainty of evidence |
| R9 | Non-pregnant adults, suspected acute **cholecystitis** | **Both US and CT equivocal/non-diagnostic and clinical suspicion persists** | Panel suggests **either abdominal MRI/MRCP or HIDA scan** as subsequent imaging | Conditional recommendation, low certainty of evidence for HIDA, knowledge gap for MRI/MRCP |
| R10 | **Pregnant people**, suspected acute cholecystitis or acute cholangitis | Initial | **US or MRI can be considered**; panel **unable to recommend one versus the other** (not a recommendation) | Knowledge gap |

- Remarks:
  - **Acute cholangitis diagnosis** should include clinical signs (jaundice, fever, chills, right upper quadrant [RUQ] abdominal pain), laboratory findings (indicators of inflammation and biliary stasis) and imaging findings (biliary dilatation or evidence of an etiology, eg, stricture, stone, obstructing mass) (R7–R10).
  - **CT with IV contrast preferable and usually appropriate** when CT is the subsequent test (R8).
  - No study assessed US or CT accuracy for acute cholangitis; indirect evidence from acute cholecystitis used (R7, R8).
  - **US and CT both inconclusive but acute cholangitis suspected → MRI/MRCP is a reasonable option** (R9).
  - **Children:** uncommon, evidence not systematically reviewed; **reasonable to mirror the adult imaging pathway** (R7–R9).

### Imaging — suspected acute diverticulitis (p. S84–S85)

PICO: initial imaging — CT, US or MRI, in adults and in pregnant adults. Children not addressed.

| Wiki # | Population | Step | Recommendation | Strength / certainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R11 | **Non-pregnant adults** | Initial | Panel suggests **abdominal CT** as the initial diagnostic modality | Conditional recommendation, very low certainty of evidence |
| R12 | Non-pregnant adults | Initial, **CT unavailable or contraindicated** | Panel suggests **US or MRI** as the initial diagnostic modality | Conditional recommendation, very low certainty of evidence |
| R13 | **Pregnant adults** | Imaging | **US or MRI can be considered**; panel **unable to recommend one versus the other** (not a recommendation) | Knowledge gap |

- Remark (R11): **IV contrast usually appropriate** whenever CT is obtained — helps characterize and detect subtle bowel wall abnormalities and complications of diverticulitis; CT without IV contrast may be appropriate.

### Imaging — suspected acute intra-abdominal abscess (p. S85)

PICO: initial imaging — abdominal US or CT (US or MRI in pregnancy); whether MRI for subsequent imaging if inconclusive.

| Wiki # | Population | Step | Recommendation | Strength / certainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R14 | **Non-pregnant adults and adolescents** | Initial | Panel suggests **abdominal CT** | Conditional recommendation, very low certainty of evidence |
| R15 | **Children** | Initial | Panel suggests **abdominal US** | Conditional recommendation, very low certainty of evidence |
| R16 | Children | Initial US **negative/equivocal/non-diagnostic and clinical suspicion persists** | Panel suggests **either CT or MRI** as subsequent imaging | Conditional recommendation, very low certainty of evidence |
| R17 | **Pregnant people** | Initial | **US or MRI can be considered**; panel **unable to recommend one versus the other** (not a recommendation) | Knowledge gap |

- Remarks — adults (R14): **IV contrast may improve visualization of the abscess wall**; because of CT's accuracy, immediate additional imaging beyond CT usually not necessary.
- Remarks — children (R15, R16): at least 1 study suggests MRI is a reasonable option for **initial** imaging in children; same US/MRI/CT trade-offs as for appendicitis (US operator-dependent; MRI availability and sedation in young children; CT radiation, IV contrast or sedation).

### Blood cultures (p. S85–S86)

PICO: in adults and children with known or suspected intra-abdominal infection (**uncomplicated or complicated**), should blood cultures be obtained to effect a meaningful change in antimicrobial therapy?

| Wiki # | Population | Recommendation | Strength / certainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| R18 | Adults and children with suspected intra-abdominal infection "who have an elevated temperature AND: hypotension and/or tachypnea and/or delirium, OR there is concern for antibiotic-resistant organisms that would inform the treatment regimen" (punctuation as printed) | Panel suggests **obtaining blood cultures** | Conditional recommendation, very low certainty of evidence |
| R19 | **Non-immunocompromised** adults and children with suspected intra-abdominal infection who have a **normal/elevated temperature but no hypotension, tachypnea or delirium**, **and no** concern for antibiotic-resistant organisms that would inform the treatment regimen | Panel suggests **not routinely** obtaining blood cultures | Conditional recommendation, very low certainty of evidence for adults / low certainty of evidence for children |

- **Combination rule:** elevated temperature **plus** at least one of hypotension, tachypnea or delirium → blood cultures (R18). Normal/elevated temperature, none of those features, no resistance concern, not immunocompromised → not routinely (R19). The printed punctuation ("elevated temperature AND: … OR there is concern for antibiotic-resistant organisms") does not make clear whether a resistance concern qualifies **without** fever or only together with it.
- **"Concern for antibiotic-resistant organisms"** = any of (remarks, R18 and R19):
  - high regional resistance to agents commonly used as empiric treatment for intra-abdominal infection
  - history of colonization or infection with organisms not susceptible to common empiric regimens **within the previous 90 days**
  - antibiotic treatment **within the previous 90 days**
  - elderly or immunocompromised patients, or other significant comorbidities
  - healthcare-associated infection
- Remarks (R19): use best judgment on benefits and risks; in select cases blood cultures may help decision-making — eg, **concern for resistant organisms, concern for ascending cholangitis, complex intra-abdominal abscess**.
- Direct evidence on blood cultures in intra-abdominal infection is lacking (R18, R19).

### Intra-abdominal fluid cultures (p. S86)

PICO: in adults and children with known or suspected intra-abdominal infection (uncomplicated or complicated), should cultures of intra-abdominal fluid be obtained to effect a meaningful change in antimicrobial therapy?

| Wiki # | Population | Recommendation | Strength / certainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| R20 | Adults and children with **cIAI having a procedure for source control** | Panel suggests **obtaining intra-abdominal cultures** to guide antimicrobial therapy | Conditional recommendation, moderate certainty of evidence |
| R21 | Adults and children with **uncomplicated appendicitis undergoing appendectomy** | Panel suggests **not routinely** obtaining intra-abdominal cultures | Conditional recommendation, low certainty of evidence |

- Remark (R20): **fluid inoculation** is the preferred collection method.
- Remarks (R21):
  - **Immunocompromised patients** → higher risk of antibiotic-resistant organisms; intra-abdominal cultures **generally warranted**.
  - **Complicated disease suspected/recognized at surgery** → intra-abdominal cultures may be advised.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Intra-abdominal (peritoneal) fluid cultures — IDSA 2024 vs SIS 2024.**
  - IDSA R20: suggests obtaining intra-abdominal cultures in adults and children with cIAI having a source-control procedure (conditional, moderate certainty); R21: not routinely at appendectomy for uncomplicated appendicitis.
  - [[sis-2024-intra-abdominal-infection]] source page: recommends **against routine peritoneal fluid cultures in lower-risk patients with community-acquired IAI** (Grade 1-B; a carried-forward 2017 recommendation) and against changing therapy on culture results in lower-risk patients responding to treatment.
  - Both are 2024 US guidelines. IDSA's statement is the newer GRADE review (moderate certainty); SIS's is a reaffirmed 2017 recommendation. The two agree for uncomplicated appendicitis; they diverge for lower-risk community-acquired cIAI undergoing source control.
- **Severity scoring.** IDSA R1 prefers APACHE II (or the World Society of Emergency Surgery Sepsis Severity Score) if a score is used, without a cut-off. SIS 2024 uses **APACHE-II ≥10** as one criterion for higher risk ([[sis-2024-intra-abdominal-infection]]). Not a conflict; IDSA gives no threshold. [[acg-2024-acute-pancreatitis]] calls APACHE scoring cumbersome in acute pancreatitis — different disease, not in conflict.
- **Diverticulitis imaging.**
  - [[diverticulitis]] page and [[acg-2026-diverticulitis]] source page: CT essential/useful to confirm diagnosis, especially at first presentation and in severe cases; neither page addresses US, MRI or pregnancy.
  - [[aga-2021-colonic-diverticulitis]] source page: CT should be considered in patients without prior imaging-confirmed diagnosis (Best Practice Advice 1); CT with **oral and IV** contrast cited at sensitivity/specificity 95%; MRI very sensitive but less specific than CT and **generally not used acutely**.
  - IDSA agrees on CT first (R11) but says CT **without** IV contrast may be appropriate, does not mention oral contrast, and suggests **US or MRI when CT is unavailable or contraindicated** (R12) — a role for MRI the AGA 2021 text describes as generally not used acutely. Pregnancy left open (R13).
- **Blood cultures — IDSA vs TG18.** [[acute-cholangitis]] and [[acute-cholecystitis]] (from Tokyo Guidelines 2018 [TG18]): blood culture "preferably if high fever is present"; not routine for Grade I community-acquired cholecystitis. IDSA R18 requires elevated temperature **plus** hypotension, tachypnea or delirium (or a resistance concern); fever alone with no such features and no resistance concern → not routinely (R19). IDSA is the US guideline and newer → its triggers should lead; IDSA itself flags concern for ascending cholangitis as a case where cultures may help.
- **Cholecystitis second-line imaging (CT vs MRI/MRCP).** Already recorded on [[idsa-2024-ciai-imaging-cholecystitis-cholangitis]]; same recommendations here.
- **Open:** no high-quality evidence for any recommendation; no direct evidence on blood cultures in intra-abdominal infection; no pediatric severity score; pregnancy imaging for cholecystitis/cholangitis, diverticulitis and abscess unresolved (knowledge gaps).
