## Summary

ACG's first dedicated guideline on the **hereditary adenomatous colorectal polyposis syndromes** — incurable, systemic cancer-predisposition syndromes that differ in inheritance, age at onset, phenotype and cancer risk. It covers who should be assessed for a hereditary syndrome, the modality and timing of germline testing, endoscopic and surgical cancer-risk mitigation, and chemoprevention. The two commonest syndromes — **familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP)**, from a germline pathogenic variant (GPV) in *APC*, and ***MUTYH*-associated polyposis (MAP)**, from biallelic *MUTYH* GPVs — are treated in depth; the rarer adenomatous syndromes (*POLE*/*POLD1*, *NTHL1*, *AXIN2*, *MLH3*, *MSH3*, *MBD4*) are described and routed to expert centres.

The document is built on **12 GRADE recommendations (Table 2)** plus **31 ungraded key concepts (Table 3)**. Three structural shifts stand out. First, the trigger for germline **multigene panel testing (MGPT)** is now a **cumulative lifetime adenoma count**: a strong recommendation at **≥20**, a conditional one at **10–19**, with a minimum 16-gene panel specified. Second, a named category — **colonic polyposis of unknown etiology (CPUE)** — is defined for the patient with ≥10 cumulative adenomas and negative MGPT, and given its own surveillance scheme. Third, **upper gastrointestinal (GI) management is laid out in operative detail**: Spigelman-stage-driven duodenal surveillance intervals, a therapeutic threshold for duodenal adenomas, criteria for endoscopic over surgical ampullectomy, and a defined set of high-risk gastric features that must be resected and reported.

Surgical guidance is phrased as a trade-off rather than a rule: **ileorectal anastomosis (IRA) for rectal-sparing disease (≤20 rectal adenomas)** to preserve quality of life, **ileal pouch–anal anastomosis (IPAA) for >20 rectal adenomas** when the patient prioritises the marginal reduction in secondary proctectomy and rectal cancer over function. Chemoprevention is explicitly **not** a substitute for colectomy when surgery is indicated (the only strong recommendation with moderate certainty), but sulindac 150 mg twice daily or celecoxib 400 mg twice daily may be an adjunct to endoscopic surveillance.

Extraintestinal surveillance is specified for the first time at this granularity: baseline **thyroid ultrasound by age 18** in FAP/AFAP, repeat at 5 years if normal and shorter if nodules are present; **cross-sectional abdominopelvic imaging only for a palpable mass** or preoperatively with a family history of desmoid; and **no routine imaging screening** for adrenal adenomas or desmoids.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Recommendations (the document's own numbering and GRADE wording; 12 total — Table 2 prints 1–11, no. 12 appears in the Upper GI text)

| # | Recommendation | Strength; certainty |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **≥20 cumulative lifetime adenomatous colorectal polyps** → MGPT to evaluate for a GPV associated with an underlying hereditary adenomatous colorectal polyposis or cancer syndrome | **Strong**; low |
| 2 | **10–19 cumulative lifetime adenomatous colorectal polyps** → *suggest* MGPT for the same purpose | Conditional; low |
| 3 | Any of these extracolonic features of FAP — **desmoid tumors, cribriform morular carcinoma of the thyroid, bilateral and/or multifocal congenital hypertrophy of the retinal pigment epithelium (CHRPE), hepatoblastoma** → **germline *APC* testing** | **Strong**; low |
| 4 | FAP with **>20 rectal adenomas** who require surgery, and who prioritise the marginal reduction in risk of secondary proctectomy and rectal cancer over functional outcomes → *suggest* **total proctocolectomy with IPAA over total colectomy with IRA** | Conditional; very low |
| 5 | **Rectal-sparing FAP (≤20 rectal adenomas)** who require surgery → *suggest* **total colectomy with IRA over total proctocolectomy with IPAA**, to improve quality of life after surgery | Conditional; very low |
| 6 | **Recommend against chemoprevention as a replacement for colectomy** in patients with polyposis who have an indication for surgery | **Strong**; **moderate** |
| 7 | *Suggest* chemoprevention with **sulindac 150 mg twice daily or celecoxib 400 mg twice daily**, case-by-case, as an **adjunct** to endoscopic surveillance to reduce polyp burden in colon, rectum or pouch; impact on need for surgery or cancer risk is unclear | Conditional; low |
| 8 | FAP, AFAP and MAP → *suggest* **upper endoscopic surveillance with visualization of the duodenal papilla**, over no surveillance. Ongoing surveillance is indicated, but **intervals are not well-defined** | Conditional; low |
| 9 | FAP, AFAP and MAP → *suggest* duodenal surveillance **at intervals based on Spigelman stage (SS)**: every 3–5 yr SS 0 · every 3 yr SS I · every 2–3 yr SS II · every 6–12 mo SS III · every 3–6 mo SS IV | Conditional; low |
| 10 | **Duodenal adenomas >10 mm or any high-grade dysplasia (HGD)** → *suggest* **therapeutic intervention over surveillance alone**; endoscopic vs surgical resection individualised by duodenal and ampullary disease burden, patient preference, and access to an experienced therapeutic endoscopy team | Conditional; low |
| 11 | Adenomatous polyposis with an **ampullary adenoma and no indication for duodenectomy** → *suggest* **endoscopic over surgical ampullectomy** for (i) ampullary adenoma **≥10 mm**, (ii) ampullary adenoma with **HGD**, (iii) ampullary adenoma with **pancreatico-biliary symptoms** — **provided there is no intraductal extension >10 mm** | Conditional; very low |
| 12 | FAP, AFAP, MAP **with gastric polyposis** → *suggest* **endoscopic resection of all antral polyps, proximal polypoid mounds, polyps ≥10 mm, gastric adenomas, and white mucosal patches** | Conditional; very low |

### Key concepts (ungraded; the document's numbering KC 1–31, condensed)

**Genetic testing (KC 1–6)**

- **KC 1 — minimum MGPT gene set:** *APC, AXIN2, BMPR1A, EPCAM, GREM1, MLH1, MSH2, MSH3, MSH6, MUTYH, NTHL1, POLD1, POLE, PMS2, PTEN, SMAD4, STK11*.
- **KC 2** — single-gene testing may be offered instead of MGPT when a known familial GPV explains the entire familial clinical phenotype.
- **KC 3** — previous negative testing that omitted the minimum genes, or a changed personal/family cancer history, warrants further evaluation; refer to a hereditary-cancer provider or centre.
- **KC 4** — ≥20 adenomatous polyps with **uninformative MGPT** → consider referral for assessment of ***APC* mosaicism** testing, case-by-case. Where access and expertise are limited, manage as CPUE.
- **KC 5** — polymerase proofreading-associated polyposis (PPAP), *NTHL1* tumor syndrome, *AXIN2*-, *MSH3*-, *MLH3*-associated polyposis and *MBD4*-associated neoplasia syndrome → **refer to an expert centre** (rarity, evolving cancer risks, multispecialty need).
- **KC 6** — **≥10 cumulative lifetime colorectal adenomas with negative MGPT meets criteria for CPUE.**

**Colorectum: endoscopy and surgery (KC 7–15)**

- **KC 7** — asymptomatic *APC* GPV carriers, or untested at-risk for **classic FAP** → baseline colonoscopy by **age 10–15 yr**, then **every 1–3 yr** by polyp number, size and histology.
- **KC 8** — *APC* GPV, biallelic *MUTYH* GPVs, or untested at-risk for **AFAP or MAP** → baseline colonoscopy by **age 18–20 yr**, then every **1–3 yr**.
- **KC 9** — document **polyp burden** (estimated number and range of sizes) **separately for colon and rectum** at baseline and follow-up; this drives surgical decision-making.
- **KC 10** — resect colorectal polyps **≥10 mm** without features of submucosal invasion; excise polyps **≥4 mm** where feasible.
- **KC 11** — colectomy is the cornerstone of colorectal cancer (CRC) risk reduction in FAP; the two commonest operations are total colectomy with IRA and total proctocolectomy with IPAA.
- **KC 12** — surgical choice and timing individualised on CRC risk, rectal involvement, symptoms, desmoid risk, surgical morbidity including functional outcomes, and psychosocial impact; early multidisciplinary involvement advised.
- **KC 13** — after IPAA or IRA → **annual lower endoscopy**; shorter intervals for large size, advanced pathology, or high polyp numbers.
- **KC 14** — polyp burden not amenable to endoscopic management (± chemoprevention) → consider **secondary surgery**.
- **KC 15** — evidence for **surveillance ileoscopy is not established**; may be considered **every 3 yr** with annual visual inspection of the stoma.

**Upper GI tract (KC 16–24)**

- **KC 16** — the **Spigelman stage guides duodenal surveillance intervals and prognosticates duodenal cancer risk**; document its components in the endoscopy report.
- **KC 17** — **biopsy of the papilla** gives prognostic information if it harbours adenomatous change. Consider at **baseline EGD even if the papilla appears normal**; warranted on follow-up if size or morphology changes.
- **KC 18** — after **pancreas-sparing duodenectomy (PSD) or pancreatico-duodenectomy**: surveil the **afferent limb** including the biliary-enteric and pancreatico-enteric anastomoses and the proximal efferent jejunum; start at **2 yr postoperatively**, intervals by **neo-Spigelman stage** of jejunal polyps.
- **KC 19** — upper endoscopic surveillance begins at **age 20–25 yr in FAP and AFAP** and **30–35 yr in MAP**, or earlier per the family's upper GI phenotype; intervals set by severity of gastric or duodenal polyposis.
- **KC 20** — the EGD report should describe polyp size and number ranges, areas of stomach involved, and the presence of the **"high-risk" gastric features**: **carpeting, polypoid mounds, solitary polyps ≥10 mm, white mucosal patches**.
- **KC 21** — **fundic gland polyps (FGPs) with low-grade dysplasia (LGD) are present in >40% of individuals with FAP**; treat and surveil based on their size and the presence of HGD.
- **KC 22** — report and accession **antral polyps separately** from proximal gastric polyps; they are most often adenomas, and resection should be considered.
- **KC 23** — offer **endoscopic surveillance of the duodenum after gastrectomy**, frequency by Spigelman stage.
- **KC 24** — select individuals with **SS III–IV duodenal polyposis**: **celecoxib 400 mg twice daily, or erlotinib ± sulindac**, may be considered as an adjunct to endoscopic surveillance and resection of adenomas >10 mm, after discussing risks, benefits and side effects. Does not supplant duodenectomy when indicated; no long-term durability or duodenal surgery-/cancer-free survival data.

**Extraintestinal surveillance (KC 25–28)**

- **KC 25** — **cross-sectional abdominopelvic imaging** in FAP, AFAP and MAP **with a palpable abdominal mass**, to assess for desmoid disease; can be considered preoperatively if there is a family history of desmoid. **No role for screening cross-sectional imaging for adrenal adenomas or desmoids.**
- **KC 26** — thyroid cancer in FAP and AFAP has a **mean onset in the third decade** → consider a **baseline thyroid ultrasound by age 18 yr**.
- **KC 27** — FAP/AFAP with a **normal** baseline thyroid ultrasound → consider repeat in **5 yr**. With prevalent or incident **nodules** → shorter interval, based on progression in nodule size and associated imaging features.
- **KC 28** — counsel parents of a child with an *APC* GPV about hepatoblastoma risk and the **lack of evidence supporting hepatoblastoma screening**; after informed discussion, screening can be considered case-by-case.

**Colonic polyposis of unknown etiology (KC 29–31)**

- **KC 29** — CPUE with **≥20 adenomas** → colonoscopy surveillance **every 1–3 yr**, interval by size, number and histology. **Colectomy is indicated for CRC or endoscopically unmanageable polyposis.**
- **KC 30** — CPUE → **baseline EGD at diagnosis** for gastric polyposis, duodenal polyposis and ampullary adenomas; surveil by Spigelman stage or by the features of gastric polyposis/ampullary adenomas, if present.
- **KC 31** — CPUE with **≥20 adenomas** → offer **baseline thyroid ultrasound**; surveillance ultrasound could be considered per the FAP/AFAP recommendations.

### Epidemiology and natural history worth carrying

- Approximately **10% of all CRC is hereditary**; a GPV associated with a hereditary adenomatous polyposis syndrome is found in **1%–2% of patients with CRC**.
- Estimated population prevalence: **FAP 1:2,800 to 1:18,000**; **MAP 1:20,000 to 1:60,000**.
- **GPV yield rises with adenoma count** (Table 6; Grover 2012, n = 8,676): *APC* in 5% at 10–19 adenomas, 10% at 20–99, 56% at 100–999, 80% at ≥1,000; biallelic *MUTYH* 4%, 7%, 7%, 2% across the same bands.
- FAP: **near-complete penetrance for colonic polyposis**, variable for extracolonic features; **de novo in up to 25%**. Classic FAP (>1,000 adenomas) maps to codons 1250–1464, particularly codon 1309. Without risk-reducing intervention the **lifetime CRC risk is historically >90%**; in 582 *APC* carriers, cancer caused death in 59% — CRC 42%, duodenal cancer 21%.
- AFAP: ≤100 adenomas at age ≥25 yr in one definition; cumulative CRC risk by age 80 of **69%**, mean age at cancer **58 yr**, 15–20 yr later than classic FAP; CRC rare before age 20.
- **Presymptomatic detection works:** in 353 patients, registry enrolment cut CRC incidence from **40% to 3%** and raised average survival from 58 to 74 yr. A European study of 194 symptomatic probands vs 225 presymptomatic relatives showed **RR 0.24 (95% CI 0.16–0.36)** for mortality in the relatives.
- Duodenal cancer: risk tracks Spigelman stage — **0%–1% at SS 0–I, 2%–3% at SS II–III, 16% at SS IV**. Ampullary adenomas in ~50%–90% of FAP patients. Mean age at ampullary cancer diagnosis 48–68 yr.
- Gastric: FGPs in up to **90%** of FAP; gastric adenocarcinoma in **1.3%–5%** of Western FAP/AFAP at median age 50–52 yr, and **becoming a leading cause of FAP-related cancer mortality** in patients who undergo colectomy.
- MAP: **autosomal recessive**, typically <100 adenomas; CRC risk **43%–63%** at mean age 40–60 yr, earliest reported CRC at age 21. **CRC can occur without associated adenomas, and polyp number does not predict CRC risk.** Duodenal adenomas in 14%–34% (vs 50%–90% in FAP), later median age 48–51 yr. Duodenal cancer risk **0.35%–1.8%**. Standardized incidence ratio for duodenal cancer 4.2 (0.9–12.3). Other cancers by age 75: skin 17%, ovary 10%, bladder 6%.
- **Monoallelic *MUTYH*** is not convincingly associated with adenomatous polyposis or a heightened CRC risk warranting increased surveillance; prevalence 1.8% in cases vs 1.2% in controls (OR 1.2; 95% CI 0.99–1.5, *P* = 0.064). Manage by personal/family history of polyps or CRC.
- ***APC* mosaicism** explains up to **63%** of unexplained FAP cases with >20 colorectal adenomas in one systematic review; in a systematic review of mosaicism with polyposis, **65% had an attenuated phenotype (20–100 adenomas)**, 30% a classic phenotype, 5% no adenomas.
- CPUE: in two hereditary registries (83 patients, 10–99 adenomas, age ≥25 yr), **19.3% had CRC on the baseline colonoscopy** (median age 58.3 yr); on surveillance (median 3.9 yr) adenoma count increased in 23.9%, colorectal surgery was performed in 17, and metachronous CRC occurred in 3 of the 16 who had CRC at baseline.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Germline-testing threshold moved, and the logic changed again.** [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]] used a single trigger (>10 cumulative adenomas); [[asge-2020-fap]] joined ">10 at one exam" **and** "≥20 lifetime" with *and*; [[usmstf-2020-followup-colonoscopy]] kept them as alternatives. ACG 2026 settles on **two graded bands by cumulative lifetime count alone** — **≥20 strong, 10–19 conditional** — with no single-exam element and no age cut-off. Newest same-tier source; it governs the wiki.
- **Colonoscopy start age in classic FAP is now 10–15 yr** (KC 7), against the "age 10–12" framing carried from older documents. Surveillance interval is **1–3 yr** in both.
- **Relative indication for colectomy by polyp size.** [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]] listed "multiple adenomas >6 mm"; ACG 2026's Table 8 lists **adenomas ≥10 mm with HGD, all lesions ≥10 mm resected, or a clinically significant increase in burden between examinations** instead. The newer, more detailed table governs.
- **Upper endoscopy start age in MAP (30–35 yr)** is later than FAP/AFAP (20–25 yr) — the first ingested source to separate them.
- **Interval uncertainty is stated, not hidden.** Recommendation 8 calls ongoing upper endoscopic surveillance indicated while saying the **intervals are not well-defined**; recommendation 9 then supplies Spigelman-based intervals at low certainty. Both stand as written.
- **No recommendation on hepatoblastoma screening** — KC 28 is explicitly a counselling statement about the *lack* of evidence, not a screening recommendation.
- Open: no prospective evidence informs colonoscopy or EGD intervals in CPUE; the long-term effectiveness of surveillance colonoscopy as an alternative to surgery in polyposis is unproven; the durability of chemoprevention and its effect on duodenal surgery- or cancer-free survival is unknown.
