## Summary

Full update of the 2013 ACG eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE) guideline, using Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development, and Evaluation (GRADE) methodology across diagnosis, treatment, monitoring and assessment of response, and pediatric-specific considerations. It issues **19 graded recommendations (Table 1)** and **25 ungraded key concepts (Table 2)**.

EoE is diagnosed on three criteria: symptoms of esophageal dysfunction, ≥15 eosinophils per high-power field (eos/hpf) on esophageal biopsy, and evaluation for non-EoE disorders that cause or contribute to esophageal eosinophilia. The headline change from 2013 is the **elimination of the proton pump inhibitor (PPI) trial as a diagnostic requirement** and retirement of the term *PPI-responsive esophageal eosinophilia*; PPIs are now a treatment option.

Other major updates: dupilumab US Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved 2022 (≥12 years) and 2024 (1–11 years); budesonide oral suspension (BOS) FDA-approved 2024; a step-up diet approach (one-food elimination diet [1FED] or two-food elimination diet [2FED] before six-food elimination diet [6FED]); and maintenance therapy to prevent recurrence graded **strong**. The guideline emphasizes assessing and controlling both the inflammatory and the fibrostenotic aspects of disease.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Diagnostic Criteria

Three criteria, all required (Figure 1):

1. Symptoms of esophageal dysfunction
2. ≥15 eos/hpf on esophageal biopsy
3. Evaluation for non-EoE disorders that cause or potentially contribute to esophageal eosinophilia

- Differential to evaluate at step 3: [[gerd|gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)]], pill esophagitis, drug hypersensitivity reactions, non-EoE eosinophilic gastrointestinal diseases (EGIDs), hypereosinophilic syndrome, [[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease]], [[achalasia]], infections, connective tissue or autoimmune diseases.
- **The PPI trial is no longer a diagnostic criterion.** The critical change since the 2013 ACG guideline is elimination of the requirement for a PPI trial before diagnosis, and removal of the term *PPI-responsive esophageal eosinophilia* (PPI-REE). PPI-responsive patients were clinically, histologically, immunologically, and molecularly similar to PPI-nonresponsive patients, and PPIs were likely treating EoE through mechanisms independent of gastric acid suppression. PPIs are now positioned as a **treatment**, not a diagnostic criterion.
- **Biopsy protocol:** ≥6 biopsies from ≥2 esophageal levels (proximal/mid and distal), **targeting endoscopic findings** where possible.
- Assess for fibrostenosis at the diagnostic endoscopy, alongside EREFS.
- **EREFS** (EoE Endoscopic Reference Score — edema, rings, exudates, furrows, strictures): the systematic endoscopic scoring system the guideline recommends at every endoscopy.
- Atopy raises suspicion: 60%–94% of patients with EoE have a concomitant atopic condition (immediate-type food allergy, asthma, atopic dermatitis, allergic rhinitis), and the more atopic a patient is, the more likely they are to have EoE. There are **no data to support screening** of this population.
- Family history of EoE/EGID should increase clinical suspicion in a symptomatic patient.
- **Avoidance behaviors mask severity** — the IMPACT acronym (guideline Table 4) assesses food avoidance and modification; in children, food refusal, increased time to complete meals, and regurgitation can be signs of occult dysphagia.

- Statement 16 is worded "we suggest" but is graded **strong** in Table 1; the strength column is what governs.
- The guideline makes **no recommendation on prescribing a PPI regardless of reflux symptoms** — counselling the patient on the rationale for PPI use is a key concept (Table 2), not a graded recommendation.
- GRADE strength definitions used (Table 3): **strong** = desirable effects clearly outweigh undesirable, most patients should receive it; **conditional** = trade-offs less certain, shared decision-making appropriate.

### Treatment Details

**PPI**

- High-dose twice daily (bid) × 8–12 weeks → endoscopy with biopsies
- ~50% histologic remission; mechanism beyond acid suppression
- Adults: omeprazole 20mg bid (or equivalent); children: 2mg/kg/day
- Maintain long-term if effective (70–85% sustain remission at 1y)

**Topical Steroids**

- Histologic remission 60–70% across trials
- **BOS:** 2mg bid — FDA-approved 2024
- **Budesonide orodispersible tablet (BOT):** 1mg bid — European Medicines Agency (EMA)-approved
- **Fluticasone metered-dose inhaler (MDI):** 1760mcg/day divided (adults); swallowed, NOT inhaled
- Off-label viscous budesonide or fluticasone powder remain options
- Side effects: candidal esophagitis (4–24%); adrenal insufficiency uncommon with short-term use; test adrenal function with long-term use or multiple corticosteroid formulations
- Loss of response can occur over time; every-other-day dosing may not be effective

**Dietary Elimination**

| Diet | Foods Eliminated | Histologic Response |
|---|---|---|
| 1FED | Dairy only | 35–45% |
| 2FED | Dairy + wheat | 40–45% |
| 4FED | Dairy, wheat, egg, soy | 40–50% |
| 6FED | Dairy, wheat, egg, soy, nuts, seafood | 40–70% |
| Elemental | All (amino acid formula) | >90% (if adherent) |

- Step-up approach preferred: start with 1FED or 2FED; escalate if no response
- Food reintroduction: add 1 food group × 6–8 weeks → endoscopy; re-eliminate if flare
- **Do NOT use** skin prick, patch test, or serum IgE/IgG to direct diet
- Dietitian referral strongly recommended
- "Diet holidays" with bridge medical therapy may improve long-term adherence

**Dupilumab (anti-IL-4Rα)**

- FDA-approved: ≥12y (2022), 1–11y (2024)
- Indication: PPI-nonresponder
- Dosing: ≥40kg → 300mg subcutaneously (SQ) weekly; 30–<40kg → 300mg SQ every 2 weeks (q2w); 15–<30kg → 200mg SQ q2w
- Phase 3 randomized controlled trial (RCT): 60% histologic remission (≤6 eos/hpf) vs 5% placebo
- Symptom improvement, endoscopic improvement (EREFS), and improved esophageal distensibility
- No tuberculosis (TB)/human immunodeficiency virus (HIV)/hepatitis screening required; no therapeutic drug monitoring
- Cost and access are key barriers

**Esophageal Dilation**

- Indication: fibrostenotic EoE with dysphagia despite medical therapy
- Goal: ≥16mm luminal diameter
- Safety: 95% clinical improvement; perforation <0.5%; hospitalization <1%
- Endoscopy insensitive for stricture detection (sensitivity ~15%); esophagram more sensitive
- **Always pair with anti-inflammatory therapy** — dilation alone does not treat underlying EoE
- Technique: start conservative, look for "dilation effect" (mucosal disruption), advance incrementally

### Maintenance

- Virtually universal relapse (histologic and symptomatic) within weeks to months of stopping therapy
- Fibrostenotic progression occurs with treatment interruptions
- EoE does NOT resolve spontaneously ("atopic march" endpoint)
- Dose reduction acceptable after achieving remission (especially with BOT: 0.5mg bid effective)
- 2y gaps in care → increased risk of fibrostenosis

### Monitoring

- Check response at **8–12 weeks** after starting PPI or STC or FED; 12–24 weeks for dupilumab
- Assess: symptoms + EREFS + esophageal biopsies
- Histologic goal: <15 eos/hpf
- Endoscopic goal: EREFS ≤2
- "Deep remission" = all 3 domains (symptoms, endoscopy, histology) — ideal but achieved in <10%

### Experimental/Future Agents

- Cendakimab (anti-IL-13): phase 3 ongoing
- Benralizumab, lirentelimab: complete eosinophil depletion but no symptom benefit in trials
- Fluticasone dissolvable tablet: phase 3 ongoing
- Mometasone-impregnated membrane: early trials

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Supersedes [[acg-2013-eoe]] on the diagnostic pathway.** The 2013 guideline required a PPI trial and recognized PPI-responsive esophageal eosinophilia (PPI-REE) as a separate entity; the 2025 guideline eliminates both. Where the two disagree, the 2025 document governs.
- Optimal step-up diet sequence and endoscopy timing not settled
- No validated noninvasive biomarkers for monitoring
- Whether eosinophil count is the right primary outcome metric (eosinophil-depleting agents lack symptom benefit)
- Long-term safety of dupilumab in pediatric EoE not fully established
