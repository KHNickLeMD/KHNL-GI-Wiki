## Summary

- Third ACG–ASGE quality indicator set for [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]]: **20 indicators** — 4 preprocedure, 12 intraprocedure, 4 postprocedure (Table 1).
- **5 priority indicators:** #1 appropriate indication, #7 subepithelial mass echogenicity + layer of origin, #11 diagnostic yield of EUS-guided sampling of malignant pancreatic mass, #12 technical success of EUS-guided pancreatic fluid collection drainage (EUS-PFCD), #18 documentation of adverse events (AEs). Quality-improvement work should start with these.
- Indicators common to all endoscopy (consent basics, sedation, etc.) are in a separate ACG–ASGE document; this one covers only EUS-specific modifications. Unit-level structural measures are also in a separate document.
- Each indicator is graded on the Guyatt framework (1A → 3; Table 2), labeled **process** or **outcome**, and given a performance target. Where expert consensus judged failure a "never event," target is set at **>98%**.
- Targets are a framework for quality improvement — not standard of care, credentialing, or training requirements; using them as such is called a misuse.
- **Therapeutic procedures now make up >50% of accepted EUS indications** (Table 3) — a major change from the 2006 and 2015 versions. The appropriate-indication target is therefore **>90%** (vs >95% for other procedures) to leave room for novel interventional uses, provided benefit/safety justify them and the patient is fully informed.
- Several interventional targets (EUS-guided gallbladder drainage [EUS-GBD], biliary drainage [EUS-BD], gastroenterostomy [EUS-GE]) were deliberately set **below published success rates**, because published data come from expert centers and early literature.
- Literature search: PubMed, January 2014 – March 2024.

## Grading Framework (Table 2)

| Strength | Clarity of benefit | Methodologic strength of evidence | Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1A | Clear | Randomized trials without important limitations | Strong; applies to most clinical settings |
| 1B | Clear | Randomized trials with important limitations | Strong; likely applies to most practice settings |
| 1C+ | Clear | Overwhelming evidence from observational studies | Strong; can apply to most practice settings in most situations |
| 1C | Clear | Observational studies | Intermediate strength; may change with stronger evidence |
| 2A | Unclear | Randomized trials without important limitations | Intermediate; best action may differ by circumstance or patient/societal values |
| 2B | Unclear | Randomized trials with important limitations | Weak; alternatives may be better in some circumstances |
| 2C | Unclear | Observational studies | Very weak; alternatives likely better in some circumstances |
| 3 | Unclear | Expert opinion only | Weak; likely to change as data become available |

### Preprocedure — Supporting Points

- **Indication (#1):** established and evolving indications are listed in Table 3 (reproduced as the indications lists on [[endoscopic-ultrasound]]). EUS remains mainstay for primary staging of upper GI cancers (esophageal, gastric) when no distant metastasis; [[colorectal-cancer|rectal cancer]] best staged with endorectal ultrasound **plus** magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). EUS for pancreatic cancer screening in high-risk individuals is a new diagnostic indication.
- **Consent (#2):** beyond the common-to-all elements, discuss EUS-specific AEs (pancreatitis, infection, bile leak, perforation — depending on the procedure). Where relevant, discuss and document the possibility of pivoting to an EUS-guided salvage approach after failed ERCP and its risks.
- **Antibiotics (#3):** give only where evidence supports benefit; indication-by-indication table (Table 4) is on [[endoscopic-ultrasound#Preprocedure]].
  - [[pancreatic-cysts|Pancreatic cyst]] FNA: randomized controlled trial (RCT) of 226 patients — infection 0.4% vs 0.9% with antibiotics vs placebo (P > 0.05); meta-analyses show no significant benefit → routine prophylaxis not justified. Still **consider** when the patient is immunosuppressed or has many comorbidities, when a cyst is incompletely aspirated, or when ascites is present.
  - Mediastinal cysts: cyst infection / mediastinitis risk should preclude routine sampling (antibiotics not shown to prevent it).
  - Rectal ultrasound-guided FNA/FNB and lower GI tract lesions: infection risk (incl. perirectal abscess) ~15% despite prophylactic antibiotics → same caution.
  - Interventional EUS: limited data; a 2022 European Society of Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ESGE) technical review recommended intravenous (IV) broad-spectrum antibiotic prophylaxis for all therapeutic EUS (weak recommendation, low-quality evidence).
- **Credentialing (#4):** competency should be measured objectively and documented before credentialing — not inferred from procedure numbers alone (ASGE credentialing guidance). One learning-curve study: average advanced endoscopy trainee reached competence at ~225 cases. No formal credentialing standards exist yet, even for ERCP.

### Intraprocedure — Supporting Points

- **Documentation (#5)** — record per indication:
  - Mediastinal lesions: anatomic level (aortopulmonary [A-P] window, aortic arch, subcarinal) and relation to adjacent structures (azygous, aorta).
  - Esophageal cancer: location, tumor proximal/distal extent from the incisors, obstruction, T stage, locoregional and celiac adenopathy, left liver lobe involvement when feasible. Dilating an obstructing cancer just to satisfy this indicator adds risk and little value.
  - Pancreaticobiliary disease: whole pancreas with duct and its size; key vascular landmarks relative to the lesion; worrisome features of cysts; chronic pancreatitis changes per Rosemont criteria; aberrant biliary anatomy.
  - Rectal cancer: tumor location, surrounding structures (iliac vessels, genitourinary organs, sphincters), lymphadenopathy.
  - Photodocumentation should mirror the text.
- **Luminal cancer staging (#6):** EUS T-staging accuracy in esophageal cancer 71%–92% vs computed tomography (CT) 42%–60%; N-staging weaker. Rectal ultrasound: T 80%–90%, N 70%–80%, but MRI has largely supplanted it for primary rectal staging. For suspected superficial lesions, pathology of the resection specimen beats EUS for depth (T1a vs T1b vs T2).
- **Subepithelial lesions (#7):** up to 30% of suspected subepithelial lesions (SELs) are extrinsic. Hypoechoic from muscularis propria → likely leiomyoma or GI stromal tumor; hyperechoic from submucosa → likely lipoma. Submucosal origin may permit endoscopic resection; muscularis propria origin → full-thickness resection or surgery. EUS imaging alone ~43%–80% accurate for benign vs malignant; adding fine-needle sampling raises accuracy to 75%–100%. Use EUS-FNB or EUS-FNA with rapid on-site evaluation (ROSE).
- **[[liver-biopsy|Liver biopsy]] (#8):** adequacy defined in the literature as total length >15 mm with >6 complete portal triads (stricter society definition: >2 cm and >11 triads). Pooled diagnostic yield ~94%–95%, adequacy 84%, AEs 3%. Target set at >85% to allow for expert-center bias. 19-gauge FNB needles (fork-tip or Franseen) give longer specimens and more portal triads than FNA but more AEs (6% vs 1%); Franseen yield 99% vs 88% for other FNB designs. Heparin flush with wet suction improves yield.
- **[[pancreatic-cancer|Pancreatic mass]] detection (#9):** EUS sensitivity 93%–100%, specificity 53%–100%; especially useful for tumors <2.0 cm. In CT-indeterminate cases: pooled sensitivity 85%, specificity 58% (mean lesion 2.1 cm).
- **Pancreatic mass evaluation (#10):** measure lesion; assess lymph nodes by AJCC station (celiac = station 9; peripancreatic = 17 and 18); vascular invasion (length, luminal compromise, thrombus); liver metastases; ascites. Vascular invasion: sensitivity 66%–85%, specificity 89%–94%. Lymph node involvement: sensitivity 69%, specificity 81%. EUS found liver metastases in 16% of patients in one cohort, 22% of them missed on CT.
- **Pancreatic mass sampling (#11):** pooled sensitivity 85% / specificity 98% (33 studies); 86.8% / 95.8% (41 studies). Target ≥87% is higher than in prior versions.
  - FNB vs FNA: pooled diagnostic accuracy 87% vs 80%; FNB not superior in RCTs where ROSE was available. FNA with ROSE is acceptable; **favor FNB when ROSE is unavailable or core tissue is needed**.
  - Needle size: 22- vs 25-gauge — most meta-analyses find no difference; 2 favored 25-gauge.
- **EUS-PFCD (#12):** lumen-apposing metal stent (LAMS) era technical success 92%–100%; prospective LAMS cohort 91% technical success, 93% collection resolution. Fewer AEs (especially pancreaticocutaneous fistula) vs surgical/percutaneous drainage.
- **[[eus-guided-gallbladder-drainage|EUS-GBD]] (#13):** indications — acute cholecystitis or symptomatic cholelithiasis in high-surgical-risk patients; after failed or impossible percutaneous/other drainage; converting percutaneous cholecystostomy to internal drainage in non-surgical candidates; distal malignant biliary obstruction when ERCP or direct bile-duct drainage is not feasible. Caution: emphysematous cholecystitis, contained gallbladder perforation, large-volume ascites. Discuss multidisciplinarily if future cholecystectomy is possible. Technical success: EUS-GBD 95.3%, percutaneous 98.7%, ERCP-GBD 83%; EUS-GBD clinical success highest (96.7%).
- **EUS-BD (#14):** indications — failed ERCP with accessible ampulla, inaccessible ampulla with [[gastric-outlet-obstruction|gastric outlet obstruction (GOO)]], or difficult-to-reach ampulla after surgically altered anatomy. Contraindications — tumor at the puncture site, duct not visualized, massive ascites, coagulopathy, standard endoscopy/sedation contraindications. Guideline-recommended over percutaneous drainage for rescue of malignant extrahepatic obstruction after failed ERCP when expertise is available. Technical/clinical success 90%–96%; AEs 8%–27%.
- **EUS-GE (#15):** most accepted for malignant, unifocal GOO; also benign obstruction (chronic pancreatitis, peptic ulcer disease, superior mesenteric artery syndrome) and afferent loop syndrome. Technical success 92%–100%. Techniques: direct, nasobiliary-catheter-assisted, or assist device.
- **EDGE (#16):** for access to the excluded stomach/duodenum after Roux-en-Y gastric bypass. Technical success 96%–99%. Persistent gastrogastric or gastrojejunal fistula risk; LAMS dwell ≥40 days → odds ratio 4.5 for persistent fistula (+9.5% per extra 7 days).

### Postprocedure — Supporting Points

- **Report (#17):** report elements per the common-indicator document, expert opinion, and the document's Supplementary Appendix 1.
- **AE documentation (#18):** one study found 0% of reports listed AEs; 15%–45% of AEs go unrecognized or unreported → actively contact patients, ideally **2 weeks** after the procedure.
- **Diagnostic EUS AEs (#19):**
  - Perforation: esophageal 0.02% overall in a 13,000-procedure series, gastric/duodenal 0.06%; more with linear echoendoscope. Risk factors: trainee involvement, older age, difficult esophageal intubation history, esophageal malignancy, cervical osteophytes.
  - Bleeding after FNA: pooled 0.13% (>10,900 patients); not linked to needle size or pass number.
  - Infection after pancreatic cyst FNA: pooled 0.4%. Transrectal FNA of cystic lesions: substantial infection rate despite antibiotics.
  - Pancreatitis after FNA/FNB of pancreatic lesions: 0.5%–1.5% (0.92% pooled for cysts).
  - Needle-tract seeding: pooled 0.3%. Avoid EUS-FNA/FNB of a primary perihilar biliary stricture/mass in potential transplant candidates (prior transperitoneal sampling is an exclusion in most cholangiocarcinoma transplant protocols); nodal sampling is acceptable.
  - Celiac plexus block / neurolysis: transient diarrhea 2% / 10%; hypotension 2% / 5%; rare paraplegia and fatal AEs reported.
- **Interventional EUS AEs (#20):**
  - EUS-PFCD with LAMS: overall AEs 13%–15% (bleeding, stent misdeployment/dysfunction lead). Bleeding ~7% cumulative; associated with LAMS dwell >4 weeks.
  - EUS-GBD: overall morbidity <10% to 20%; mortality 3.9%, mainly from ongoing sepsis. Bleeding ~8%.
  - EUS-BD: overall AEs 3%–39%; bile leak most common (3.5%–4.9%); biloma 0.6%; bleeding ~4%; perforation <1%; mortality 0.5%.
  - EUS-GE/EDGE: stent misdeployment with perforation most common (~10%). EUS-GE misdeployment types: I (63%) distal flange in peritoneum, no enterotomy; II (30.4%) with enterotomy; III (2.2%) in small bowel with proximal flange in peritoneum; IV (4.4%) distal flange in colon (gastrocolic anastomosis). Type I (often II): stent removal + closure; keep fasted ≥24 h after an AE.
  - Minimize capnoperitoneum with minimal dilating force and carbon dioxide insufflation.
  - Long-term: EUS-GBD 1-year cumulative AEs lower than percutaneous (25.6% vs 77.5%); recurrent cholecystitis 2.6%. EUS-GE long-term AEs ~10% (migration, food obstruction, tissue ingrowth/overgrowth).

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Antibiotics for pancreatic cyst FNA:** [[asge-2015-antibiotic-prophylaxis|ASGE 2015]] suggests prophylaxis for EUS-FNA of pancreatic and mediastinal cystic lesions; this 2025 document (newer, same tier) lists pancreatic cystic lesions as "not routinely indicated," citing the [[colan-hernandez-2020-eus-fna-panc-cyst-antibiotics|Colán-Hernández 2020]] RCT. The 2025 position governs.
- **Mediastinal cysts:** ASGE 2015 covers them with prophylaxis; the 2025 document says not to sample them routinely, because antibiotics have not been shown to prevent cyst infection/mediastinitis.
- **Interventional EUS antibiotics:** the 2025 table says "strongly consider" for all interventional EUS; the ESGE 2022 review it cites makes a weak recommendation to give them to all. Evidence for either is limited (largely extrapolated from interventional radiology and surgery).
- **EUS-GBD figures:** [[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage|AGA 2023]] reports EUS-GBD AEs of 4.8%–22% and meta-analytic morbidity 20.4%; this document sets the AE ceiling at <20% — consistent ranges, with the ceiling at the upper end.
- **Needle gauge for solid pancreatic masses:** meta-analyses conflict (most find 22- = 25-gauge; 2 favor 25-gauge). No gauge is mandated.
