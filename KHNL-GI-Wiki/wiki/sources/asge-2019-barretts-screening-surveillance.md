# ASGE 2019 — Screening and Surveillance of Barrett's Esophagus

## Bibliographic Info

- **Authors:** Qumseya B, Sultan S (co-first authors), Bain P, Jamil L, Jacobson B, Anandasabapathy S, Agrawal D, Buxbaum JL, Fishman DS, Gurudu SR, Jue TL, Kripalani S, Lee JK, Khashab MA, Naveed M, Thosani NC, Yang J, DeWitt J, Wani S (Chair); ASGE Standards of Practice Committee
- **Year:** 2019
- **Journal/Publisher:** Gastrointestinal Endoscopy 2019;90(3):335-359
- **Type:** guideline (GRADE methodology, official ASGE recommendations)

## Summary

This ASGE guideline addresses **screening and surveillance** of [[barretts-esophagus|Barrett's esophagus]] (BE), the companion to the 2018 ASGE EET guideline. It uses GRADE methodology across 6 PICO questions covering surveillance of nondysplastic BE (NDBE), screening of at-risk populations, and advanced imaging/sampling adjuncts (chromoendoscopy, confocal laser endomicroscopy, EUS, WATS-3D, volumetric laser endomicroscopy).

Key positions: surveillance endoscopy is suggested for NDBE (conditional, very low quality — no RCT exists; benefit may be inflated by lead-/length-time bias). There is insufficient evidence on whether screening reduces mortality, but if screening is performed it should target an **at-risk population** — high risk = family history of EAC or BE; moderate risk = GERD plus ≥1 additional risk factor (age >50, obesity/central adiposity, smoking, male sex). For surveillance imaging, **chromoendoscopy (dye-based or virtual) plus Seattle-protocol biopsies is strongly recommended over white-light + Seattle alone** (moderate quality; ~9% absolute / ~30% relative increase in dysplasia detection). Routine **CLE is suggested against** (conditional, low). **EUS is strongly recommended against** for routinely differentiating T1a vs T1b mucosal/submucosal disease (moderate; high false-positive rate, EUS T1a-vs-T1b sensitivity only 41%). **WATS-3D as an adjunct to Seattle-protocol biopsies is suggested** (conditional, low). There is **no recommendation** for or against **VLE**.

The guideline endorses (without re-grading) NDBE surveillance every **3-5 years** based on the low NDBE progression rate (0.1%-0.3%/year), and the **Seattle protocol**: 4-quadrant biopsies every 2 cm (no prior dysplasia) or every 1 cm (prior dysplasia), plus targeted biopsies of any mucosal abnormality.

## Key Findings / Claims (Recommendations — near-verbatim, with strength and quality)

**Summary of recommendations (Table 4):**

1. **Statement 1 (Conditional, Very low quality):** In patients with nondysplastic BE, we suggest performing surveillance endoscopy compared with no surveillance.
2. **Statement 2 (No graded strength / NA quality):** There is insufficient evidence on the effectiveness of screening for BE. However, if screening endoscopy for BE is performed, we suggest a screening strategy that identifies an at-risk population. An at-risk population is defined as individuals with a family history of EAC or BE (high risk) or patients with GERD plus at least 1 other risk factor (moderate risk). [Additional risk factors: age >50, obesity/central adiposity, history of smoking, male sex.]
3. **Statement 3 (Strong, Moderate quality):** In patients with BE undergoing surveillance, we recommend using chromoendoscopy, including virtual chromoendoscopy and Seattle protocol biopsy sampling, compared with white-light endoscopy with Seattle protocol biopsy sampling.
4. **Statement 4 (Conditional, Low quality):** In patients with BE undergoing surveillance, we suggest against routine use of confocal laser endomicroscopy compared with white-light endoscopy with Seattle protocol biopsy sampling.
5. **Statement 5 (Strong, Moderate quality):** In BE patients with high-grade dysplasia/IMC or nodules, we recommend against routine use of EUS to differentiate mucosal vs submucosal disease.
6. **Statement 6a (Conditional, Low quality):** In patients with known or suspected BE, we suggest using WATS-3D in addition to Seattle protocol biopsy sampling compared with white-light endoscopy with Seattle protocol biopsy sampling.
7. **Statement 6b (No recommendation / NA quality):** In patients with BE undergoing surveillance, there is insufficient evidence to recommend for or against routine use of VLE.

**Supporting evidence by question:**

- **Q1 — NDBE surveillance vs none (Codipilly SR/MA):** Unadjusted EAC-related mortality lower with surveillance (RR .60; .50-.71); EAC patients with prior BE diagnosis had lower EAC mortality (RR .73; .57-.94). **Lead-time adjustment eliminated the mortality benefit** (El-Serag HR 1.72 [.78-2.07]; Tramontano HR .89 [.78-1.01]; pooled adjusted HR .85 [.75-.95]). All-cause mortality unadjusted HR .75 (.59-.94); RR .48 (.37-.63) in EAC patients with prior BE. Surveillance associated with earlier-stage diagnosis (RR 2.11; 1.08-4.11). Adverse events of diagnostic EGD very low (1/200 to 1/10,000). NDBE EAC progression 0.1%-0.3%/year → supports **3-5 year** NDBE surveillance intervals. Diagnostic EGD AEs: cardiopulmonary 1/170-1/10,000; perforation 1/2500-1/11,000.
- **Q2 — Screening / at-risk population:** No studies compared screening vs no screening. Panel used a ≥10% BE prevalence threshold to recommend screening. Risk factors (Table 6): GERD OR 2.9 (4.5 if GERD ≥2 weeks); family history of BE/EAC OR 12; male-to-female ratio 2.13; obesity OR 1.15 (NS adjusting for GERD) but central adiposity OR 1.44 (1.2-1.74); ever-smoking OR 1.42 (1.15-1.76), 1.96 adjusted. BE risk increases ~1.2% per additional risk factor. <10% of EAC patients have a prior BE diagnosis; ~40% of EAC patients report no prior GERD; ~50% of short-segment BE patients have no GERD symptoms. PPI (OR .55) and statins (OR .48) linked to lower progression. AspECT-type trial: high-dose PPI + aspirin improved composite of all-cause mortality/HGD/EAC (time ratio 1.59; 1.14-2.23). AGA Clinical Practice Update recommended against alternative (non-endoscopic) screening tests at the time. Endoscopic screening of GERD patients cost-effective at <$100,000/QALY.
- **Q3 — Chromoendoscopy (CE/VC) + Seattle vs WLE + Seattle:** Absolute increase in dysplasia detection **9% (4.1%-14%; P<.001)**; relative increase **30.3% (16.2%-44.3%)**. No significant difference VC vs dye-based CE. Performance (ASGE Technology Committee): conventional dye CE sensitivity 91.9%, NPV 95.5%, specificity 89.9%; acetic acid sensitivity 96.6%, NPV 98.3%, specificity 84.6% (met PIVI thresholds); NBI/VC sensitivity 94.2%, NPV 97.5%.
- **Q4 — CLE:** Absolute increase in dysplasia detection 10.2% (1.4%-19.1%; CI near zero); relative increase **not significant** (36%; -5.4% to 77.5%). Overall CLE sensitivity 90.4%, NPV 96.2%, specificity 89.9% — did not meet PIVI thresholds (endoscope-based CLE met thresholds but is no longer available). High false-positive rate in low-prevalence settings (16.3% at 5% prevalence; 12% at 30%). RCT prevalence (19%-35%) far exceeds community surveillance prevalence → limited real-world yield.
- **Q5 — EUS for T1a vs T1b:** Pooled sensitivity **41% (35%-48%)**, specificity 89% (86%-91%), accuracy 75% (59%-86%) for differentiating mucosal vs submucosal disease. High false-positive rate (6% at high prevalence to 10.5% at low prevalence); ~one-third of EUS-positive "advanced disease" calls are false positives. Reinforces ASGE 2018 position: resect all visible lesions with EMR/ESD first as the diagnostic/staging step rather than relying on EUS. (EUS remains relevant for confirmed/advanced EAC and nodal assessment.)
- **Q6a — WATS-3D added to Seattle protocol:** Relative increase in dysplasia detection **48% (34%-60%)** overall; 47% (32%-61%) in patients with prior dysplasia; absolute increase 10.6% (1.5%-19.8%) (history of dysplasia) and ~2% in known/suspected BE without dysplasia history. Much of the additional detection is LGD (absolute LGD increase ~1.8%); the clinical meaning of "LGD on cytology" vs histologic LGD is uncertain. Exceeded the expert-derived 27% threshold. No safety/cost-effectiveness data identified.
- **Q6b — VLE:** Evidence insufficient/evolving (VLE with laser marking promising but immature) — no recommendation.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Concordant with [[acg-2022-barretts]] and [[aga-2025-barretts-surveillance]] on the Seattle protocol, chromoendoscopy, and risk-factor-based screening. ASGE 2019 is more cautious than some societies on accepting non-endoscopic screening devices (Cytosponge/capsule) — ACG 2022 explicitly endorses them as an acceptable alternative.
- ASGE 2019 does NOT require intestinal metaplasia for a BE diagnosis in its framing, whereas ACG/AGA require IM with goblet cells — note the international disagreement.
- Surveillance mortality benefit largely disappears after lead-time adjustment; no RCT (BOSS trial result awaited at publication).
