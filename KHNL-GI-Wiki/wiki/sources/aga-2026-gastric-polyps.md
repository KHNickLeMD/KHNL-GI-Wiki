## Summary

AGA Expert Review on diagnosis and management of gastric mucosal polyps — raised epithelial lesions arising from mucosal hyperplasia, adenoma, fundic gland proliferation, or enterochromaffin-like (ECL) cell proliferation. Explicitly written to **complement**, not replace, four companion AGA documents: [[aga-2020-gastric-intestinal-metaplasia|AGA 2020 guidelines on gastric intestinal metaplasia]], [[aga-2021-atrophic-gastritis|AGA 2021 CPU on atrophic gastritis]], [[aga-2024-upper-endoscopy-quality|AGA 2024 CPU on high-quality upper endoscopy]], and [[aga-2025-gastric-cancer-screening|AGA 2025 CPU on screening and surveillance of individuals at increased risk for gastric cancer]] — those four, not this CPU, carry the background-mucosa staging and surveillance protocols this one defers to.

Histologic subtypes: **fundic gland polyps (FGPs)**, **gastric hyperplastic polyps (GHPs)**, hamartomatous polyps, **gastric adenomas (GAs)**, pyloric gland adenomas, oxyntic gland adenomas, and **gastric neuroendocrine tumors (G-NETs)**. In the West most are FGPs or GHPs (70%–94%); subtypes have shifted toward sporadic FGPs as *Helicobacter pylori* (H pylori) prevalence has fallen. Key principle: evaluate both the polyp **and** the surrounding/background mucosa ([[helicobacter-pylori-infection|H pylori]] gastritis, [[atrophic-gastritis|autoimmune/atrophic gastritis]], [[gastric-intestinal-metaplasia|gastric intestinal metaplasia (GIM)]]), because field changes drive risk and surveillance. Test and treat H pylori for adenomatous or hyperplastic polyps; proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) need not be stopped for FGPs. Resect the largest polyps; surveillance is keyed to polyp histology, size, dysplasia grade, and background mucosa.

Two decision tools carry the operative detail and are reproduced on [[gastric-polyps]]: **Figure 2** (management of newly visualized polyps, including the size-banded resection-technique box) and **Table 2** (surveillance intervals by polyp histology). Both apply to countries with low H pylori prevalence and common PPI use, in patients **without** a known genetic cancer syndrome.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Best Practice Advice — all 14, verbatim, in the document's own BPA order:**

1. Gastric polyps are frequently identified during upper endoscopy exams and include different histologic subtypes, such as fundic gland polyps (FGPs), gastric hyperplastic polyps (GHPs), hamartomatous polyps, gastric adenomas (GAs), pyloric gland adenomas, oxyntic gland adenomas, and gastric neuroendocrine tumors (G-NETs).
2. Clinicians should be aware that different types of gastric polyps may coexist in the same person.
3. Clinicians should be aware that different types of gastric polyps are associated with varying spectra of histopathologic abnormalities in the surrounding gastric mucosa, which may aid in their identification and diagnosis.
4. Systematic endoscopic examination of the polyps and the surrounding gastric mucosa is essential in assessing the underlying gastric mucosa pathology (eg, Helicobacter pylori gastritis, autoimmune gastritis, gastric intestinal metaplasia [GIM]) and determining subsequent management: biopsies of the polyps, biopsies of the surrounding mucosa, and resection of the polyps.
5. All patients with adenomatous or hyperplastic gastric polyps should be tested and treated if positive for H pylori infection.
6. Patients who are using proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) for valid reasons do not need to discontinue these medications in the presence of documented fundic gland hyperplasia-related gastric polyps.
7. Clinicians should be aware that different histological types of gastric polyps have unique/characteristic topographical features, endoscopic features, and size.
8. Endoscopic evaluation of patients with gastric polyps should include complete inspection with high-definition white-light and enhanced imaging, such as virtual chromoendoscopy. Endoscopists should recognize and photo-document the endoscopic features of gastric polyps as well as the surrounding gastric mucosal abnormalities.
9. Clinicians should be aware that endoscopic resection of the polyps includes traditional techniques (snare and biopsy forceps, mucosal resection) or endoscopic submucosal dissection.
10. In the presence of numerous gastric polyps of varied sizes, the largest polyps should be resected when possible, and the smaller polyps sampled or resected.
11. Suspected abnormalities in the surrounding mucosa, such as GIM or atrophic gastritis, should undergo targeted biopsies according to the existing protocols.
12. Surveillance plans in patients with gastric polyps should be formulated based on the histopathological type of the polyps and the surrounding gastric mucosa.
13. When a dysplastic lesion in the polyp is confirmed and resected completely, a follow-up surveillance endoscopy should be completed in 1 year for patients with low-grade dysplasia polyps and 6 months for patients with high-grade dysplasia polyps. If the polyp is biopsied or resection is incomplete, follow-up endoscopy is advised within 3 months for high-grade dysplasia and 6 months for low-grade dysplasia.
14. Endoscopic surveillance is advised in patients with gastric polyps when the histopathology of adjacent mucosa confirms GIM and/or atrophic gastritis.

**Natural history / epidemiology:**
- Prevalence of gastric polyps ~6.35% of biopsied endoscopies; ~70%–94% are FGPs or GHPs in the West; multiple types coexist in 2%–3%.
- **FGP:** body, often multiple, <1 cm, glassy; PPI-associated/sporadic, or [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis|familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP)]]/gastric adenocarcinoma and proximal polyposis syndrome of the stomach (GAPPS). Dysplastic transformation is exceedingly low in sporadic FGPs — **~1%** (Table 1: <1%); **25%–46%** in FAP-associated (Table 1: up to 48%); high in GAPPS. Adenocarcinoma progression in up to 15% of FAP cases, but low- to high-grade progression is slow — ~4% over a mean 6 y of follow-up in FAP. Higher risk with **mucosal carpeting** (FGPs from fundus to antrum without intervening normal mucosa) or polyps **>1 cm**.
- **Multiple FGPs at age 20–40 or in childhood, off PPIs → consider FAP.** >80% of syndromic patients develop FGPs, typically within the first 3 decades; syndromic FGPs are smaller, morphologically subtler, and lack parietal cell hyperplasia.
- **GHP:** dysplasia ~4%; malignant transformation 0.8%–10%, with polyps **>2 cm** more likely to harbor dysplasia or adenocarcinoma; surrounding mucosa may harbor dysplasia at similar or higher rates than the polyp itself.
- **GA:** intestinal-type (56%) and foveolar-type (41%) most common; precursors of adenocarcinoma; progression 3%–4% low-grade and 5%–30% high-grade, rising with size, particularly **>20 mm**, and with villous histology. Foveolar "raspberry-like" GAs in the H pylori-naïve stomach carry very low to nonexistent risk.
- **Pyloric gland adenomas:** high-grade dysplasia up to 42%, adenocarcinoma 12%–47% (Table 1 gives progression 12%–30%); risk with larger size, tubulo-villous architecture, autoimmune gastritis. Higher-grade lesions in [[lynch-syndrome|Lynch syndrome]] and in male patients.
- **G-NET:** >70% are type 1 (autoimmune gastritis, ECL-cell proliferation); type 2 with Zollinger-Ellison/multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1; **type 3 (~15% of G-NETs)** larger, solitary, up to 5 cm, no hypergastrinemia, worst prognosis. Size bands: type 1 ≤2 cm, type 2 ≤1 cm, type 3 >2 cm.
- **Acid suppression:** PPI use raises FGP risk **1.5- to 2.5-fold**, up to **5-fold with ≥12 months** of use (a second meta-analysis: 1.43 [1.24–1.64] fixed effects, 2.45 [1.24–4.83] random effects); some of this is confounded by age, sex, and endoscopy indication. [[potassium-competitive-acid-blockers|Potassium-competitive acid blockers (P-CABs)]] behave similarly — VISION trial: GHP prevalence **3.7% → 14.7% over 3 y** after starting a P-CAB; >3-fold higher GHP risk with P-CAB use >1 y.
- **H pylori eradication** reduces GHP prevalence by **59% (95% confidence interval [CI] 43%–75%)** after treatment and **79% (95% CI 72%–86%)** after confirmed eradication.
- **Missed lesions:** neoplastic lesions missed at upper endoscopy and diagnosed as cancer within 3 y — **4.7%–11.3%**.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **H pylori serology vs [[acg-2024-hp-treatment|ACG 2024 H pylori treatment guideline]].** This CPU accepts serology as one option when biopsy-based sampling is unavailable; ACG 2024 recommends **against** serologic testing in low-prevalence populations absent a high pre-test probability, and treats only a non-serological positive as an indication to treat. AGA 2026 is the newer source and the wiki follows it, but the permission is narrow — it applies only where sampling is unavailable, so urea breath test or stool antigen is preferred whenever either is obtainable. Reflected on [[gastric-polyps]].
- **Internal inconsistencies between the text and Table 1.** FAP-associated FGP dysplasia is 25%–46% in the text and up to 48% in Table 1; sporadic FGP dysplasia is ~1% in the text and <1% in Table 1; pyloric gland adenoma malignant progression is 12%–47% adenocarcinoma in the text and 12%–30% in Table 1. Both numbers are given above in each case; the document does not reconcile them.
- **Ethnicity and family history do not change surveillance frequency.** Although both associate with gastric cancer risk, this CPU states the frequency of surveillance should not be altered on those grounds, citing little evidence that the association is mediated by anything other than gastric mucosal status. That is a statement about *surveillance of a patient with polyps*, not about *screening eligibility* — [[aga-2025-gastric-cancer-screening|AGA 2025]] and [[acg-2025-gastric-premalignant|ACG 2025]] do use ethnicity and family history to select who gets screened. Keep the two decisions separate; population-level stratification lives on [[gastric-cancer-screening]].
- **No biomarker.** No noninvasive biomarker screens or monitors gastric polyps or dysplasia, and none separates low- from high-risk patients; gastrin adds nothing diagnostically in most polyp patients, and pepsinogen I:II is unavailable in most US laboratories.
- **Undetermined intervals.** The duration of surveillance for GIM and/or atrophic gastritis on adjacent mucosa has not been determined. BPA 12 and BPA 14 advise that surveillance be done without naming an interval; the intervals come from Table 2 and from the companion AGA documents.
- **Scope limit, stated by the document:** the Figure 2 algorithm and the Table 2 intervals are written for countries with low H pylori prevalence and common PPI use (e.g. the United States), in patients without a known genetic cancer syndrome. Where multiple polyp types coexist, the advice for the highest-grade polyp takes precedence.
- **Resection-threshold evidence is thin and partly borrowed.** Cold-forceps use for polyps ≤3 mm is extrapolated from colonic data; the document states little direct gastric data exists.
