## Summary

Retrospective analysis of the **TriNetX Global Network** asking whether **ultra-early ERCP (<24 h)** improves survival in the sickest cholangitis patients — **Tokyo Guidelines Grade III** cholangitis with **septic shock**. The novelty is the clock: timing is indexed to **vasopressor initiation** (an objective marker of shock onset) rather than to hospital arrival or symptom onset, so "time to drainage" is measured from the moment the patient became hemodynamically unstable.

Three propensity-matched comparisons were built, all anchored on the same ERCP <24 h cohort (n=887): vs **24–48 h** (n=420), vs **48–72 h** (n=217), and vs a broad **24 h–7 days** comparator. Matching balanced age, sex, comorbidity burden, TG III organ-dysfunction domains, early antibiotics, mechanical ventilation, acute dialysis, AKI, and acute respiratory failure. Outcome: **30-day mortality** (Kaplan–Meier, log-rank, Cox).

**ERCP <24 h conferred no survival advantage in any comparison.** Against 24–48 h, survival was equivalent (HR 1.07, p=0.65). Against 48–72 h, the <24 h group had **higher** early mortality (HR 1.47, p=0.046) — which the authors read as confounding by indication (the sickest, most unstable patients get drained first) surviving the match. The authors conclude that **"stabilize, then drain"** is preferable to rigid ultra-early intervention in septic-shock cholangitis.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Cohorts and comparisons

| Comparison (all vs **ERCP <24 h**, n=887) | n | 30-day mortality HR (95% CI) | p | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **24–48 h** | 420 | **1.07 (0.81–1.42)** | 0.65 | Survival equivalent |
| **48–72 h** | 217 | **1.47 (1.00–2.16)** | 0.046 | **Higher** early mortality with <24 h |
| **24 h – 7 days** (broad) | — | **1.25 (0.98–1.60)** | 0.07 | No benefit to <24 h |

- **Timing anchor:** hours from **vasopressor initiation** (shock onset), not from admission or symptom onset — the methodological point of the study.
- **Population:** adults, **TG III** cholangitis **with septic shock** (vasopressor requirement) — the group *excluded* from the [[jagtap-2026-urgent-vs-early-ercp-cholangitis]] RCT.
- **Kaplan–Meier:** curves for <24 h and 24–48 h were **nearly identical**; the authors report divergence "only when ERCP was delayed beyond 48 hours."
- **Authors' interpretation of HR 1.47:** not harm from early drainage per se, but "the concentration of the sickest, most unstable patients in the earliest interval despite matching."
- **Conclusion as written:** ERCP within 24 h of shock onset does **not** improve survival vs drainage after brief stabilization; **stabilize, then drain**.
- **Limitations stated by the authors:** residual confounding; **no physiologic markers such as lactate**; procedural heterogeneity across centers.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Internal inconsistency in the abstract.** Results state the <24 h cohort had *higher* mortality than the 48–72 h cohort (HR 1.47, p=0.046) — i.e. later drainage did better — yet the Conclusions state "mortality increases significantly when ERCP is delayed beyond 48 hours." Those two sentences point in opposite directions and the abstract does not reconcile them. **Do not carry the "delay beyond 48 h increases mortality" claim onto a wiki page** until a full manuscript clarifies the direction.
- **"1:1 propensity-score–matched" cohorts are not 1:1** (887 vs 420; 887 vs 217). Covariates balance well, but the matching procedure as described does not produce these ns.
- **Vs [[asge-2021-cholangitis]] (guideline, higher tier):** guideline calls for **urgent/emergent drainage in severe (TG III) cholangitis**. This abstract finds no survival benefit to <24 h from shock onset. Guideline wins the page; the contradiction is surfaced here and inline.
- **Vs [[jagtap-2026-urgent-vs-early-ercp-cholangitis]] (RCT):** not contradicted — complementary. The RCT found no benefit (and net harm) from <24 h in **mild-to-moderate** cholangitis; this abstract extends the same direction into **severe/shock**, at much weaker evidence.
- **Appraisal note (reviewer, not from the abstract):** a retrospective timing comparison is exposed to **confounding by indication** (which the authors name) and to **immortal-time bias** — patients in the 48–72 h arm had to survive 48–72 h to enter it. Both bias against early ERCP and could generate the HR 1.47 by themselves.
- **Unanswered:** no lactate, vasopressor dose, or shock severity gradient; no drainage-technique or technical-success data; no breakdown by biliary etiology (benign vs malignant obstruction — the sole mortality predictor in the Jagtap RCT).
