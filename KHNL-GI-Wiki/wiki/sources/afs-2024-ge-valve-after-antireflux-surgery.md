## Summary

An American Foregut Society white paper establishing a **standardised nomenclature for describing the endoscopic appearance of the esophagogastric junction (EGJ) after anti-reflux surgery**. It fills a specific gap: [[asge-2024-gerd|ASGE 2025]] makes photo-documentation of GEJ landmarks and integrity a *strong* recommendation and directs the use of Hill or AFS grading — but those grading systems describe the **native, unoperated** valve. There was no accepted way to describe the valve **after** it has been surgically reconstructed, and therefore no way to compare a post-operative endoscopy against a later one in a patient with recurrent symptoms.

An 11-member collaborative of gastroenterologists and foregut surgeons analysed the anatomic manipulation and corresponding endoscopic appearance of five operations: **360° anterior/posterior (Nissen) fundoplication, posterior partial (Toupet) fundoplication, anterior partial (Dor, Watson) fundoplication, magnetic sphincter augmentation (MSA), and combined hiatal hernia repair with transoral fundoplication (cTIF)**. The predecessor work — Jobe et al. 2004, an 11-domain evaluation — is judged "somewhat cumbersome," derived from cherry-picked ideal cases with unblinded evaluators, and "never widely adopted."

The resulting schema reduces the description to **four attributes** measured in retroflexion, all referenced to the **angle of His** rather than to anatomic anterior/posterior. Its practical purpose is the workup of the patient with **recurrent GERD or new late dysphagia after anti-reflux surgery**, where the question is whether the valve has failed and how.

## Key Findings / Claims

### The four attributes of the post-surgical valve (the operative schema)

Assessed **in retroflexion**, after adequate gastric insufflation. This is the nomenclature the paper exists to establish:

1. **Depth of the anterior and posterior grooves** — graded **shallow / moderate / deep**. These are the discriminating features between operations (see the table below).
2. **Apposition of the valve collar to the endoscope** — gauged **relative to the diameter of the endoscope**, along the whole length of the valve. It may be tightest at the lip *or anywhere along the length*; **it is the narrowest part of the collar that is functionally most important**, and this may be at the proximal end. **If there is resistance to passage of the endoscope, note it.**
3. **Length of the valve** — measured **from the apex of the angle of His to the diaphragm**. (Intragastric fundoplication length is gauged endoscopically as the distance from the **lip to the base** of the valve.)
4. **Position of the squamocolumnar junction (SCJ) relative to the lip of the valve** — reflects whether intra-abdominal esophageal length was effectively restored. Assess from the retroflexed view (SCJ relative to the valve lip seen from below) or antegrade (relative to the caudal end of the esophagus/fundoplication).

Plus, for every operation: **hiatal disruption** — after any procedure that included hiatal hernia repair, **the hiatal aperture should no longer be visible in the retroflexed view**.

### Radial orientation — the reference point changes

- Endoscopic radial nomenclature: **anterior = 12 o'clock, posterior = 6 o'clock, greater curve = 3 o'clock, lesser curve = 9 o'clock**, correlating with the anatomic transverse plane.
- The apex of the **angle of His sits at approximately 3–4 o'clock** in the transverse plane, so **all fundoplications begin there**, with the fundus enveloping the esophagus ~90° anteriorly and ~90° posteriorly.
- **The Collaborative adopted the apex of the angle of His — not anatomic anterior/posterior — as the radial point of endoscopic reference for fundoplication.** This is a deliberate break from the laparoscopic convention and is why an operation described as "posterior" laparoscopically does not map onto "posterior" endoscopically.

### Technique — how to avoid under-grading

- **Adequate gastric insufflation is the key to optimal EGJ visualisation.** Insufflate for approximately **30–45 seconds**, or until there is **flattening of the fundic rugal folds**.
- **Rotate in the retroflexed position**, and perform maneuvers to induce potential axial hiatal herniation — **gently pull up the tip of the retroflexed endoscope along the depths of the grooves**. This gives the most accurate assessment of the hiatal opening and the extent of herniation.
- **Without these maneuvers the valve is often "under-graded"**, contributing to interobserver variability.
- Position the endoscope **toward the greater curvature with about 2 cm of the endoscope visible**. Ideally image the entire valve; if not feasible, take **separate images of the anterior and posterior grooves**.

### Procedure-specific endoscopic appearance

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nissen** (360° anterior/posterior) | Complete circumferential encirclement; sutured at ~10 o'clock with 3 sutures; **2 cm long** fundoplication | **Deep** | **Deep** | Generally snug around the scope both anteriorly and posteriorly; collar diameter may vary along its length. **You should not be able to see through the fundoplication into the esophagus** — that indicates an overly loose valve (a gap at the lip alone is acceptable). Lesser-curve portion of the wrap is not visible endoscopically |
| **Toupet** (posterior partial) | **270°** posterior wrap, secured at **10 and 2 o'clock**; greater posterior than anterior wrap relative to the angle of His | **Shallow** | **Deep** | "Partial" because of the exposed esophagus between 10 and 2 o'clock |
| **Dor** (anterior partial) | Anterior plication to ~**9–10 o'clock**; ~**120°–180°** wrap | **Deep** | **Shallow** | No posterior component, so the **lip may be loose around the scope posteriorly**, with visible esophageal mucosa where there is no fundoplication |
| **Watson** (anterior partial) | Anterior plication to ~**7 o'clock**; ~**230°** wrap | **Deep** | **Shallow** | As for Dor |
| **Magnetic sphincter augmentation (MSA)** | **No fundoplication created**; device placed around the distal esophagus after crural repair | **Shallow** | **Shallow** | Closely resembles the **native flap valve**. The implant is hard to see but may appear as a **"bulked-up" lip** of the valve. **Assessment is essentially the same as in the non-operated patient** — revert to the AFS hiatus grade and denote **FV+ or FV−** |
| **cTIF** (lap hiatal hernia repair + transoral fundoplication) | Initiated at the angle of His (3–4 o'clock); Esophyx rotated ~**135° posteriorly and 135° anteriorly**; ~**270°** immediately post-op, **omega-shaped**; reverse-C in transverse view | **Moderate to deep** | **Deep** | Valve lip closely approximates the scope shaft (**partly due to edema**). Valve length clearly visualised; **fasteners** visible, and transmural fasteners also visible antegrade along the **distal 2–3 cm** of esophageal mucosa |

- Note that anterior fundoplications (Dor/Watson) and the posterior fundoplication (Toupet) produce **mirror-image** groove patterns — this is the single most useful discriminator at endoscopy.
- 12 o'clock is the anterior midline of the esophagus for the Dor/Watson descriptions.

### Principles of anti-reflux surgery (what the endoscopist is checking was achieved)

Three key components, per the paper:

1. **Restoration of intra-abdominal esophageal length** by reduction and repair of the hiatal hernia.
2. **Reapproximation of the crural diaphragm** — decreases hernia recurrence and potentiates the crural diaphragm sphincter mechanism.
3. **Modification of gastroesophageal anatomy to restore valve function** — **except in MSA**, where this function is left to the implant.

Net effect: reduce the occurrence of reflux **and** restrict the diameter of EGJ opening when reflux does occur.

### Relationship to the AFS hiatus grade

- **Successful anti-reflux surgery would necessarily be equivalent to an AFS hiatus grade 1** — no hiatal hernia and a snug fit to the endoscope.
- With an intact valve (AFS hiatus grade 1) the gastric fundus envelops the intra-abdominal esophagus semi-circumferentially, and the **native SCJ sits at the lip of the valve**, corresponding to the apex of the angle of His.
- With **AFS hiatus grades II, III, or IV** the GEJ has migrated cephalad, toward or above the hiatus.
- **If there is no surgically constructed valve, the assessment reverts to the AFS hiatus grade classification** (the paper's Figure 9 algorithm makes this the first branch point).
- Sample notation seen in the paper's figures: **"AFS hiatus grade III (L0 cm, D2.5 cm, FV−)"** — i.e. length, diameter and flap-valve present/absent parameters.

### The dilated-distal-esophagus confounder

- As the distal esophagus progressively dilates it takes on gastric characteristics endoscopically — apparent **"rugal folds" and glandular mucosa** — while laparoscopically it lies **cephalad to the angle of His**, suggesting it is still esophageal tube.
- Histologically this has been called **"cardiac mucosa"**: glandular tissue **without intestinal metaplasia or parietal cells**, variably with submucosal esophageal glands and squamous islands. The paper flags this as **"a very controversial topic."**
- Consequence for the post-surgical valve: rather than extending to the lip, **the SCJ is seen proximally in the distal tubular esophagus, closely mimicking a distally placed fundoplication** — i.e. a correctly placed wrap can be misread as malpositioned.
- **The Collaborative's remedy:** document the anatomy **intraoperatively, after esophageal dissection and before creating the fundoplication**, when the end of the esophageal tube can be directly compared with the SCJ location. That record "becomes invaluable in clarifying future examinations assessing whether a fundoplication is improperly positioned on the proximal stomach or is properly placed on the distal esophagus."

### Clinical use case

- Figure 2 traces one patient: baseline **AFS hiatus grade IV** → cTIF with immediate post-op appearance → **2 years later, recurrent reflux with a wide-open hiatus and complete loss of the gastroesophageal valve** → restoration with laparoscopic hiatal hernia repair and partial fundoplication.
- The paper urges surgeons to **routinely perform intraoperative endoscopy to document the baseline post-operative valve appearance as a comparator** for future evaluations.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **This paper does NOT define the AFS hiatus grades I–IV.** It repeatedly *builds on* them, citing a separate **"AFS hiatus grade white paper"** (reference 13), and uses grades in its figure legends — but the grade criteria themselves are not printed here. **The standing corpus block on AFS grading (and on Hill grades I–IV) is therefore NOT closed by this ingest.** The AFS hiatus-grade white paper would need to be ingested separately. Do not infer the grade definitions from the sample notation in the figure legends.
- **No outcome data.** The Collaborative explicitly states it has established nomenclature, not validated it: it "recognized the need to further understand the degree to which the endoscopic characteristics of the EGJ after anti-reflux surgery correlate with the effective control of pathologic reflux." No accuracy, reproducibility, or predictive-value figures are reported. The proposal is that the endoscopic-orientation scheme "be evaluated."
- **Descriptions are of the "classic" and typical *immediate* post-operative appearance.** The paper says so directly. Appearance may differ with time (the cTIF lip approximation is attributed in part to **edema**), and variations warranting additional description are acknowledged.
- **Hill and Collis esophageal-lengthening procedures were explicitly not examined** — the schema does not cover them.
- **Groove depth is a three-level subjective scale** (shallow / moderate / deep) with no stated anchors, measurement, or interobserver data. The paper's own critique of the Jobe 2004 schema — unblinded evaluators, selected ideal cases — has not been answered for this schema either.
- **Definition of pathologic reflux used here:** abnormal esophageal acid exposure on pH-metry **or** Los Angeles grade B, C, or D esophagitis on endoscopy. (The LA grade criteria themselves remain corpus-blocked — see [[reflux-testing]].)
- **Figures are not captured.** Figures 1–9 (including **Figure 9, the assessment algorithm**, and the endoscopic photographs of each valve type) are the visual core of this paper and are not in the wiki — figure-capture tooling is permission-blocked in this environment. The per-procedure table above is a text substitute; the endoscopic example images are what would let a reader actually recognise each valve.
