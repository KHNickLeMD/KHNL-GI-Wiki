## Summary

The FDA prescribing information for **fecal microbiota spores, live-brpk (VOS)** — the oral, capsule-delivered microbiota product for recurrent *Clostridioides difficile* infection (CDI), and the counterpart to the rectal product in [[rebyota-2022-live-jslm-label]]. It was fetched to close the last half of the gap [[fmt]] names in its own text: [[aga-2024-fmt]] gives **no product dosing at all** and directs the reader to the manufacturer's package insert.

The **indication is narrow and the limitation is explicit** — prevention of *recurrence* in individuals **18 years and older following antibacterial treatment for recurrent CDI** — and the label states in its own Limitation of Use that VOS **is not indicated for treatment of CDI**. The regimen the wiki already carried second-hand from [[aga-2026-cdiff-ibd]] is confirmed verbatim: **4 capsules once daily for 3 consecutive days**, each dose **on an empty stomach before the first meal of the day**.

What the label adds is the **preparation sequence**, which no ingested guideline prints in full and which is the part that actually goes wrong at the bedside: finish the antibacterial course **2 to 4 days before** starting VOS; drink **296 mL (10 oz) of magnesium citrate on the day before and at least 8 hours prior to the first dose**; and **eat and drink nothing but small amounts of water for at least 8 hours** before that first dose. The trials substituted **250 mL polyethylene glycol (PEG) electrolyte solution (GoLYTELY, not approved for this use)** in participants with impaired kidney function — the label reports this as a study practice, not as an approved alternative.

Two differences from the rectal product are practical rather than academic. VOS has **no contraindications at all** (the label's own word is "None"), where the rectal product has one; and VOS is stored at **2 °C to 25 °C** — ordinary refrigerator or room temperature, **just not the freezer** — against the rectal product's −60 °C to −90 °C ultracold chain. Its one drug interaction is the obvious one: the capsules contain live bacterial spores, so **antibacterials must not be given concurrently**.

The efficacy section is the label's rendering of the trial the wiki already holds as [[feuerstadt-2022-ser109-cdiff]], and it carries the follow-up the paper's headline does not: recurrence **12.4% vs 39.8% at 8 weeks** (relative risk 0.32), **18.0% vs 46.2% at 12 weeks** (0.40) and **21.3% vs 47.3% at 24 weeks** (0.46) — the separation narrows with time but does not close.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Indication and limitation of use**

- Indicated to **prevent the recurrence of CDI in individuals 18 years of age and older following antibacterial treatment for recurrent CDI (rCDI)**.
- **Limitation of Use, stated on the label: not indicated for treatment of CDI.**
- **For oral administration only.**

**Before the first dose (label §2.1)**

- **Complete antibacterial treatment for rCDI 2 to 4 days before initiating VOS.**
- **Drink 296 mL (10 oz) of magnesium citrate** on the day before and **at least 8 hours prior** to the first dose.
  - In the clinical studies, participants with **impaired kidney function** received **250 mL polyethylene glycol electrolyte solution (GoLYTELY)** instead — the label flags this as **not approved for this use**.
- **Do not eat or drink, except small amounts of water, for at least 8 hours before the first dose.**

**Dosing regimen (label §2.2, §3)**

- **4 capsules taken orally once daily for 3 consecutive days.** A single dose = **4 capsules**.
- Take each dose **on an empty stomach, prior to the first meal of the day**.
- **Swallow each capsule whole — do not crush, chew, or break** (Patient Information).

**Contraindications (label §4)**

- **None.**

**Warnings and precautions**

- **Transmissible infectious agents** — manufactured from human fecal matter, so it may carry a risk of transmitting infectious agents. Any infection a provider suspects was transmitted by the product should be reported to the manufacturer.
- **Potential presence of food allergens** — may contain them (human fecal origin); the potential to cause reactions from food allergens is **unknown**.

**Drug interactions**

- VOS contains bacterial spores → **antibacterials should not be administered concurrently**. Repeated in Patient Counseling.

**Adverse reactions** (Study 1, within 8 weeks, reported in ≥5% and more often than placebo)

| Reaction | VOS (N=90) | Placebo (N=92) | Median duration (days) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Abdominal distension *(solicited)* | 31.1% | 29.3% | 3.0 |
| Fatigue *(solicited)* | 22.2% | 21.7% | 4.5 |
| Constipation *(solicited)* | 14.4% | 10.9% | 2.0 |
| Chills *(solicited)* | 11.1% | 7.6% | 1.5 |
| Diarrhea *(unsolicited)* | 10.0% | 4.3% | 5.0 |

- **Most reactions occurred within 10 days of starting treatment**, declining thereafter; the majority were **mild or moderate**.
- **Study 2** (open-label) most common: flatulence 4.2%, diarrhea 3.4%, nausea 3.0% — all unsolicited, mostly mild to moderate.
- **Among 349 VOS recipients there were no serious adverse events considered related to VOS.**
- Safety population: Study 1 (NCT03183128, phase 3 randomized double-blind, n=90) and Study 2 (NCT03183141, phase 3 open-label, n=259), United States and Canada. **99.1% (346/349) received all scheduled doses.** Median age 66 y (range 21–100); 92.3% white, 5.2% Black.
- **Trial exclusions: neutropenia (absolute neutrophil count <500 cells/mm³), toxic megacolon, and small bowel ileus.**

**Use in specific populations**

- **Pregnancy:** **no data** on use in pregnant individuals; **no animal developmental toxicity studies** conducted. Background US population risk of major birth defects 2–4% and miscarriage 15–20%.
- **Lactation:** not known whether excreted in human milk; no data on the breastfed infant or on milk production.
- **Pediatric:** safety and effectiveness **not established under 18 years**.
- **Geriatric:** 52.4% of recipients were ≥65 y (n=183) and 28.1% ≥75 y (n=98); data are **not sufficient to determine whether they respond differently**. No separate dose.

**Composition and mechanism**

- A **bacterial spore suspension in capsules**, manufactured from **human fecal matter from qualified donors** routinely tested for a panel of transmissible pathogens. **Donors have no dietary restrictions** with respect to potential food allergens.
- Manufacture: fecal matter is **treated with ethanol to kill organisms that are not spores**, then **filtered** to remove solids and residual ethanol.
- **Each capsule contains 1×10⁶ to 3×10⁷ *Firmicutes* spore colony-forming units (CFU)** in **92 ± 4% (w/w) glycerol in saline**. (The placebo capsules in the trials were the glycerol-in-saline vehicle alone.)
- **Mechanism of action: not established** (the label's own words).
- **Nonclinical toxicology:** not evaluated for carcinogenicity, genotoxicity, mutagenic potential, or impairment of male or female fertility in animals.

**Efficacy and trial population (Study 1)**

- **Entry required ≥3 total CDI episodes within 12 months.** The entry episode was defined as **diarrhea (≥3 unformed stools per day for at least 2 consecutive days) plus a positive *C. difficile* stool toxin assay**.
- Participants had to have **symptom resolution — <3 unformed stools in 24 hours for ≥2 consecutive days** — following **10 to 21 days of standard-of-care vancomycin or fidaxomicin**. Stratified by antibacterial received and by age (<65 vs ≥65 y).
- Prep as above (296 mL magnesium citrate, or 250 mL PEG if renally impaired); **fast ≥8 h before the first dose and continue fasting 1 h after it**; **Days 2 and 3 taken in the morning before breakfast**.
- Intent-to-treat population **182 randomized — 89 VOS, 93 placebo**. Mean age **65.5 y** (18–100), 93.4% white, 59.9% female, **73.1% had received vancomycin**.
- **Primary endpoint:** CDI recurrence through 8 weeks after completing treatment — **≥3 unformed stools per day for 2 consecutive days** with continued diarrhea until antibacterial treatment was started, **plus** a positive toxin assay, **plus** investigator assessment that the clinical condition warranted antibacterial treatment.

| Interval after treatment | VOS | Placebo | Relative risk (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **8 weeks** (primary) | **12.4%** (11/89) | **39.8%** (37/93) | **0.32 (0.18, 0.58)** |
| 12 weeks | 18.0% (16/89) | 46.2% (43/93) | 0.40 (0.24, 0.65) |
| 24 weeks | 21.3% (19/89) | 47.3% (44/93) | 0.46 (0.30, 0.73) |

- Pre-specified success criterion: **upper bound of the two-sided 95% CI of the relative risk below 0.83** — met.
- Participants lost to follow-up, withdrawn, or who died without a recorded recurrence (1 VOS, 2 placebo) were **counted as recurrences**.

**Supply and storage**

- Bottles of **12 white capsules imprinted "SER109"**, child-resistant cap; NDC 71881-400-12.
- **Store at 2 °C to 25 °C (36–77 °F) in the original packaging — refrigerator or room temperature. Do not store in the freezer.**

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **"No contraindications" is not the same as no restrictions, and the wiki must not read it that way.** The label lists **none at all**, while [[aga-2024-fmt]] recommends **against any fecal microbiota–based therapy in severe immunocompromise** and finds insufficient evidence for the FDA-approved products in *any* immunocompromised patient. These are different instruments and do not conflict; the guideline governs patient selection and the wiki keeps its restriction. The label's own trial exclusions point the same way — **neutropenia (absolute neutrophil count <500 cells/mm³), toxic megacolon and small bowel ileus were excluded**, so the efficacy and safety numbers describe none of those patients.
- **Per-capsule versus per-dose CFU.** The label gives **1×10⁶ to 3×10⁷ CFU per capsule**, and a dose is 4 capsules. [[fmt]] carries "dose ≈3 × 10⁷ spore CFU" from [[feuerstadt-2022-ser109-cdiff]]. The two are stated on different denominators and the label does not print a per-dose total, so neither figure is restated as the other; the page now cites the label's per-capsule range and attributes the paper's figure to the paper.
- **The magnesium citrate prep has a renal caveat the guidelines do not carry.** The label prescribes 296 mL magnesium citrate but notes the trials substituted **250 mL PEG electrolyte solution in impaired kidney function**, explicitly **not approved for this use**. No ingested guideline addresses what to do when magnesium citrate is unsafe, so the page reports the study practice as a study practice.
- **Durability is reported, not claimed.** Recurrence separation narrows across follow-up (relative risk 0.32 → 0.40 → 0.46 at 8, 12 and 24 weeks) and the label offers no interpretation. This is a different pattern from [[rebyota-2022-live-jslm-label]], whose 8-week advantage lost significance by 6 months; the two products were never compared head to head and neither result licenses a claim about the other.
- **Mechanism of action is unestablished by the label's own statement**, and no nonclinical toxicology was conducted. Any mechanistic account on a wiki page must come from elsewhere and be attributed there.
