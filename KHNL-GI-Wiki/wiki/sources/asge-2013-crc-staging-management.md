## Summary
ASGE position statement on the endoscopic role in staging and managing [[colorectal-cancer|CRC]]. Covers EUS for locoregional rectal staging, endoscopic vs surgical management of malignant polyps, and endoscopic palliation/bridge-to-surgery for malignant colonic obstruction.

EUS rectal staging: high sensitivity (80–96%) and specificity (75–98%) for T0–T3 disease, with T-staging accuracy generally higher than other cross-sectional imaging; nodal staging modest (67% sensitivity, 78% specificity) and not significantly different from CT/MRI. Primary clinical impact of rectal staging is to separate T1N0/T2N0 from T3 or N1–2 disease (chemoradiation added for the latter).

Malignant obstruction: endoscopic alternatives to surgical decompression include self-expandable metal stent (SEMS), tumor debulking, and decompression tube. Colonic SEMS can serve as "bridge to surgery"; single-stage elective surgery succeeds in 60–85% after SEMS decompression. Major SEMS adverse events: obstruction, migration, perforation. Early surgical consultation advised even after successful decompression; avoid endoscopy with peritoneal signs or suspected perforation.

## Key Findings / Claims
- Remove suspected neoplastic lesions at colonoscopy when feasible and not contraindicated.
- EUS-FNA allows sampling of suspicious perirectal nodes.
- Endoscopic tattooing localizes lesions for subsequent surgery.

## Recommendations (verbatim, with GRADE)
1. We recommend removal of suspected neoplastic lesions at the time of colonoscopy when not contraindicated and as technical expertise allows. *(High)*
2. We recommend EUS in the preoperative locoregional staging of rectal cancer to guide therapy. *(Moderate)*
3. We recommend weighing the risk of recurrence against the individual's operative risk in all cases in which surgery is being considered as a treatment for CRC. *(Moderate)*
4. We recommend surgical management of all malignant polyps with unfavorable histological features if the patient is an appropriate surgical candidate. *(Moderate)*
5. We recommend that pedunculated polyps found to contain cancer confined to the submucosa of the polyp or stalk and with favorable histological features be managed endoscopically. *(Moderate)*
6. We recommend surgery for sessile or flat colonic neoplasia that demonstrates submucosal invasion if the patient is an appropriate surgical candidate. *(Moderate)*
7. We suggest surgical management for sessile or flat colonic neoplasia that is determined to be malignant after piecemeal endoscopic resection if the patient is an appropriate surgical candidate. *(Low)*
8. We recommend EMR only be attempted if complete resection of neoplastic lesions is anticipated. *(Moderate)*
