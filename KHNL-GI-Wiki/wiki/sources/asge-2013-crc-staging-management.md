## Summary
American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) Standards of Practice statement on the endoscopic role in staging and managing [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer (CRC)]]. Covers presurgical localization, endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) for locoregional rectal staging, endoscopic vs surgical management of malignant polyps, and endoscopic palliation / bridge-to-surgery for malignant colonic obstruction. CRC screening and surveillance are explicitly out of scope.

**EUS rectal staging:** high sensitivity (80%–96%) and specificity (75%–98%) for T0–T3 disease, with T-staging accuracy generally higher than other cross-sectional imaging; nodal staging modest (67% sensitivity, 78% specificity) and not statistically different from computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). The primary clinical impact of rectal staging is to separate T1N0/T2N0 from T3 or TxN1–2 disease, for which chemoradiation is added to surgical resection.

**Malignant obstruction:** endoscopic alternatives to surgical decompression are self-expandable metal stent (SEMS), tumor debulking, and decompression tube. [[colonic-stenting|Colonic SEMS]] can serve as bridge to surgery; single-stage elective surgery succeeds in 60%–85% after SEMS decompression.

## Recommendations
*Quality of evidence in parentheses; the verb carries the strength. Numbering is the wiki's.*

1. **Recommend** removal of suspected neoplastic lesions at the time of [[colonoscopy]] when not contraindicated and as technical expertise allows. *(High)*
2. **Recommend** EUS in the **preoperative locoregional** staging of **rectal** cancer to guide therapy. *(Moderate)*
3. **Recommend** weighing the risk of recurrence against the individual's operative risk in **all** cases in which surgery is being considered as a treatment for CRC. *(Moderate)*
4. **Recommend** surgical management of **all** malignant polyps with **unfavorable** histological features, if the patient is an appropriate surgical candidate. *(Moderate)*
5. **Recommend** that **pedunculated** polyps found to contain cancer **confined to the submucosa of the polyp or stalk** and with **favorable** histological features be managed endoscopically. *(Moderate)*
6. **Recommend** surgery for **sessile or flat** colonic neoplasia demonstrating **submucosal invasion**, if the patient is an appropriate surgical candidate. *(Moderate)*
7. **Suggest** surgical management for **sessile or flat** colonic neoplasia determined to be malignant **after piecemeal endoscopic resection**, if the patient is an appropriate surgical candidate. *(Low)*
8. **Recommend** [[endoscopic-mucosal-resection|endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR)]] only be attempted if **complete** resection of neoplastic lesions is anticipated. *(Moderate)*

## Classifications and Thresholds

### Unfavorable histological features in a malignant polyp
*These criteria decide recommendation 4 vs recommendation 5 — endoscopic management vs surgery.*

- **<1 mm cancer-free margin**
- **Poor histological differentiation**
- **Vascular or lymphatic invasion**

Surgery is also recommended when the cancer invades the **submucosa of the bowel wall below the polyp's stalk**, or extends **through the submucosa into deeper wall layers** — endoscopic removal is then unlikely to be curative. Tattoo the resection site to aid surgical identification.

**Favorable side:** a pedunculated polyp with cancer confined to the submucosa of the polyp/stalk and **no** unfavorable feature carries a **0.3%** risk of recurrence or lymph node metastasis after complete endoscopic removal — surgery is not necessary.

**Sessile/flat side:** malignant lesions with submucosal invasion carry a **6%–12%** risk of lymph node metastasis → manage surgically. Sessile lesions removed piecemeal and found malignant should be considered for surgery because **margin adequacy cannot be determined**.

### Size and technique thresholds
- **EMR** indicated for sessile or flat neoplastic lesions **confined to the mucosa or submucosa** of the colon. Lesions **≤2 cm** can often be removed **en bloc**; larger lesions may require **piecemeal** resection.
- **[[colorectal-esd|Endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD)]]** was developed for en bloc resection of lesions **>2 cm**. Its role in the colon is not well established — more technically challenging than gastric ESD (less space, difficult positioning, thinner wall, colonic folds).
- EMR can be **definitive** treatment for **intramucosal (T1mN0)** CRC, where lymph node risk is negligible.
- **Non-lifting sign:** inability to raise the polyp base after submucosal injection can indicate deep submucosal cancer and **precludes endoscopic resection**. Non-lifting from fibrosis (prior biopsy or prior resection attempt) also makes EMR technically difficult.
- **EMR should not be used for ulcerated lesions or lesions that do not lift.**
- **Non-traversable malignant rectal stricture** may predict advanced stage (T3, T4, or Tx with N1–2) → stage locally with radiographic cross-sectional imaging.

### Surveillance after piecemeal resection
- Follow-up colonoscopy at **2–6 months** after piecemeal EMR of large sessile lesions, with **both endoscopic and pathological** assessment of the scar.
- Residual/recurrence after piecemeal EMR: short-term (**2–6 months**) **0%–55%**; late (**>12 months**) **<5%**.
- Normal endoscopic appearance of the site **plus** negative scar biopsies at first surveillance predicted long-term eradication in **97.9%**.
- Close surveillance after piecemeal [[polypectomy]] is mandatory; tattoo polyps that cannot be identified by anatomic landmarks.

## Key Findings / Claims
- **Presurgical localization:** endoscopic tattooing and metallic clip placement help localize flat, small, or subtle lesions. India ink tattoos remain visible at surgery for up to **5 months**. No guidelines exist on optimal tattoo or clip placement — communicate with the surgeon. Some experts suggest standardizing injection **3 cm downstream** of the lesion, distinct from the polypectomy site to avoid fibrotic reaction.
- **[[endoscopic-ultrasound|EUS]]-guided fine-needle aspiration (EUS-FNA)** of perirectal nodes is helpful when nodal status would change management; benign vs malignant perirectal nodes are hard to separate by EUS alone because inflammatory nodes occur in rectal cancer. EUS accuracy may be subject to publication bias.
- **EUS restaging after neoadjuvant chemoradiation is modest to poor:** **38%–75%** for T staging, **57%–84%** for N staging. In a 90-subject prospective comparison after neoadjuvant therapy, T-staging accuracy was 37% (CT), 34% (MRI), 27% (EUS); N staging 62%, 68%, 65%.
- MRI also shows the anatomic relationship between rectal tumor, pelvic floor, and sacrum, which may guide surgery; in one series MRI visualized no T1 tumors and EUS understaged all T4 tumors.
- **Malignant obstruction safety:** do **not** perform endoscopy with peritoneal signs or suspicion of perforation. Early surgical consultation is recommended even after successful endoscopic decompression, because patients may deteriorate rapidly. **Avoid dilation after colonic SEMS placement** — perforation risk.
- Major SEMS adverse events: **obstruction, migration, perforation**.
- **Residual tissue after piecemeal EMR:** all visible adenomatous tissue should be resected or ablated if snare excision is not feasible; options include taking a small margin of surrounding mucosa at the polyp edges, or ablation (mostly [[argon-plasma-coagulation|argon plasma coagulation (APC)]], one report of snare-tip diathermy). Evidence is conflicting — one small randomized study found prophylactic APC lowered recurrence (1/10 vs 7/11), while a larger EMR study found APC use was an **independent predictor** of recurrence.
- Major adverse events of EMR and ESD are the same as standard polypectomy (bleeding, perforation) but at a **higher rate**.
- Maximizing complete eradication on the **initial** resection attempt is the most important principle; this may require referral to a center with advanced polypectomy expertise.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- Older (2013) source; oncologic/systemic management is superseded by [[nccn-2026-colon-cancer]]. Endoscopic-staging and malignant-polyp guidance remains complementary and was added without overwriting newer content.
- Prophylactic APC at piecemeal EMR margins: the document reports directly conflicting data (protective in a small randomized study, an independent predictor of recurrence in a larger series) and does not resolve it.
- The published guideline carried an erratum: the original print version stated the EUS recommendation incorrectly; the corrected wording — EUS in the **preoperative locoregional** staging of rectal cancer to guide therapy — is what this page uses.
