## Summary

- Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) guideline; updates the American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2016 guideline on solid pancreatic neoplasia.
- Seven clinical questions: [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]]-guided tissue acquisition (EUS-TA) — needle type, needle caliber, fine-needle biopsy (FNB) needle design, rapid on-site evaluation (ROSE); [[ercp|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]] stent choice for distal malignant biliary obstruction; celiac plexus neurolysis (CPN) for pain.
- Strength wording: "we recommend" = strong; "we suggest" = conditional.

## Key Recommendations

Numbering, strength and quality as printed (Recommendations 1–7; Table 1).

| Rec | Population | Recommendation | Strength / quality of evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Solid pancreatic mass undergoing EUS-TA | **Recommends FNB over fine-needle aspiration (FNA) needles** | Strong / moderate |
| 2 | Solid pancreatic mass undergoing EUS-TA | **Suggests 22-gauge over 25-gauge** needles | Conditional / moderate |
| 3 | Solid pancreatic mass undergoing EUS-guided FNB (EUS-FNB) | **Recommends novel or contemporary FNB needles (fork-tip or Franseen)** over alternative designs | Strong / moderate |
| 4 | Solid pancreatic mass undergoing EUS-TA | **Suggests against routine use of ROSE** | Conditional / low |
| 5 | Distal malignant biliary obstruction undergoing ERCP | **Suggests self-expandable metal stents (SEMS) over plastic stents** | Conditional / low |
| 6a | Distal malignant biliary obstruction undergoing ERCP with SEMS | **Suggests covered SEMS (cSEMS) over uncovered SEMS (uSEMS)** | Conditional / low |
| 6b | Distal biliary obstruction from a pancreatic mass and **unconfirmed malignancy** | **Recommends against uSEMS** | Strong / low |
| 7 | Unresectable pancreatic cancer with abdominal pain | **Suggests CPN as an adjunct to medical analgesic therapy** | Conditional / low |

### Rationale (condensed)

Evidence counts are randomized controlled trials (RCTs) in the guideline's meta-analyses.

- **Rec 1 (25 RCTs):** FNB → higher diagnostic accuracy, higher sample adequacy, fewer passes, more often diagnosis in a single pass; trend toward higher diagnostic yield, **not statistically significant**; no difference in adverse events or post-procedure pancreatitis. FNB samples preferred for ancillary molecular testing (e.g., next-generation sequencing). FNB costlier, but 2 FNB passes without ROSE more cost-effective than FNA passes guided by on-site cytopathology (1 analysis).
- **Rec 2 (12 RCTs):** 22-gauge → greater odds of a high-quality specimen; no difference in diagnostic accuracy, adequacy, passes or adverse events. 25-gauge should be selected when a 22-gauge is expected to have limited manipulability (e.g., excessive endoscope torque) or cannot be advanced into the lesion.
- **Rec 3:** fork-tip/Franseen vs side-fenestrated, Menghini, Tru-cut → greater diagnostic accuracy and sample adequacy, similar adverse events; no difference fork-tip vs Franseen.
- **Rec 4 (6 RCTs):** ROSE did not change diagnostic accuracy, yield, adequacy or repeat sampling; with FNB needles, ROSE gave lower sample adequacy and longer procedures; with FNA needles, ROSE reduced the number of passes. A ROSE preliminary diagnosis may change on final histopathology.
- **Rec 5 (15 RCTs):** SEMS → longer patency, less stent failure, cholangitis and reintervention; no difference in survival, 30-day mortality, post-ERCP adverse events, resectability. Plastic stents appear more cost-effective with survival <3 months or liver metastasis.
- **Rec 6 (10 RCTs):** cSEMS → 70.5-day longer patency; no other differences (incl. acute cholecystitis). uSEMS become embedded and are extremely difficult or impossible to remove → not for unconfirmed malignancy; with resectable/borderline resectable tumors, place the top of the stent well below the hilum.
- **Rec 7 (8 RCTs):** CPN → lower pain severity at 4 weeks and 6 months, less constipation; no difference in survival, opioid use, quality of life. Adverse events from a separate review: transient diarrhea 9%, pain exacerbation 8%, hypotension 6%, spinal cord infarction with paralysis 0.2%. Analgesic therapy alone may be preferred first line; EUS and percutaneous routes performed similarly.

### General Concepts (Table 1, ungraded)

**EUS sampling**
- Linear echoendoscope to identify the lesion and advance the needle; color Doppler to identify and avoid interposing vessels.
- 22-gauge cannot be advanced into the lesion → switch to 25-gauge; 25-gauge may be considered when a 22-gauge is expected to have limited manipulability (e.g., excessive endoscope torquing).
- Fork-tip or Franseen needle; suction with slow stylet withdrawal and/or a 5- to 20-mL syringe; fanning technique; keep the needle tip visible during sampling.
- Express tissue with an air flush; reinsert the stylet if that fails.
- **FNB needle:** 2–4 passes, sample directly into a 10% formalin jar; consider macroscopic on-site evaluation to guide the number of passes.
- **FNA needle:** 4–7 passes, sample in CytoLyt solution, or consider ROSE.
- **Consider ROSE when:** prior nondiagnostic EUS · lesion not clear on EUS or obscured by artifact (e.g., stent, pancreatitis) · a preliminary diagnosis may guide immediate decisions (e.g., biliary stent selection, CPN, management of gastric outlet obstruction). Text adds use of an FNA needle (not recommended except when FNB needles are unavailable).

**ERCP stent placement**
- Native papilla → consider biliary sphincterotomy before stent insertion.
- Pancreas mass with simultaneous EUS-TA and high suspicion of malignancy → consider cSEMS (text: fully covered SEMS).
- Pancreas mass without simultaneous EUS-TA → consider ERCP with tissue acquisition and plastic stents (text: also plastic stent if low suspicion for malignancy).
- Pancreatic head cancer with liver metastasis or expected survival <3 mo → consider plastic stents.
- Planned surgical resection within 3 mo → consider plastic stents.
- Use 10-mm fully covered or partially covered SEMS.
- Shortest stent length that bridges the stricture by ≥10 mm and sits ≥2 cm below the hepatic hilum.
- When possible, proximal stent end below the cystic duct take-off if the gallbladder is intact.

**Pain management (CPN)**
- Consider CPN when abdominal pain is refractory to medical therapy or opioid adverse effects are not well tolerated.
- By EUS or percutaneously; EUS route may be preferred when an upper endoscopy or EUS is being done for other purposes, or percutaneous CPN is unavailable.
- EUS: at least a 22-gauge FNA needle (or larger), not the needle used for EUS-TA.
- Central or bilateral injection of 10–20 mL of 99% alcohol.
- 1 L intravenous normal saline periprocedurally.
- Monitor ~2 h postprocedure with vital signs and orthostatic parameters.

## Clinical Relevance

EUS-FNB with a fork-tip or Franseen needle is the recommended sampling approach for solid pancreatic masses, without routine ROSE; SEMS (covered) are suggested for distal malignant biliary obstruction, and uSEMS are avoided until malignancy is confirmed. Core to the pancreatic-mass diagnostic pathway and adjacent to [[pancreatic-cysts]] evaluation.
