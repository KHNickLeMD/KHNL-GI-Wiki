## Summary

AGA CPU Expert Review on using **noninvasive tests (NITs)** to risk-stratify and longitudinally monitor NAFLD, where **fibrosis stage is the strongest predictor of liver-related outcomes** and **advanced fibrosis (AF) = stage F3 (bridging) or F4 (cirrhosis)**. Eight Best Practice Advice statements; no formal evidence grade.

Central algorithm: **FIB-4 as the first-line point-of-care test.** FIB-4 **<1.3** has strong negative predictive value and excludes AF (in patients >65 y, use a **<2.0** cutoff instead, because FIB-4 performs poorly at age extremes — poor in <35 y and low specificity in older adults). FIB-4 **≥1.3** (indeterminate/high) → add a **second NIT** (imaging elastography preferred: VCTE or MRE; ELF as a serum alternative when elastography unavailable). Combining ≥2 NITs is preferred over any single NIT for staging/risk stratification once FIB-4 >1.3. Liver biopsy is reserved for **indeterminate/discordant** NITs, conflict with other data, or suspected alternative etiology.

Downstream: patients with NIT results suggesting **F3/F4** enter complication surveillance — **HCC screening and variceal screening per Baveno criteria** — and are followed with **serial LSM (VCTE or MRE)**, which correlates with clinically significant portal hypertension (CSPH) and decompensation. Serial NITs also track treatment/lifestyle response.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Best Practice Advice (8 statements):**

1. NITs can be used for **risk stratification** in the diagnostic evaluation of NAFLD.
2. A **FIB-4 score <1.3** has strong **NPV** for advanced hepatic fibrosis and may exclude AF.
3. A combination of **≥2 NITs** (serum and/or imaging biomarkers) is preferred over a single NIT for staging and risk stratification when **FIB-4 >1.3**.
4. Use NITs per **manufacturer specifications** (e.g., not in patients with ascites or pacemakers) to minimize discordant results/adverse events.
5. Interpret NITs **in clinical context** (exam, biochemical, radiographic, endoscopic data) to optimize PPV for identifying AF.
6. **Liver biopsy** should be considered when NITs are **indeterminate or discordant**, conflict with other findings, or when alternative etiologies are suspected.
7. **Serial longitudinal monitoring** with NITs may inform management (response to lifestyle/therapy).
8. Patients with NITs suggestive of **AF (F3) or cirrhosis (F4)** should enter surveillance for liver complications (**HCC screening + variceal screening per Baveno**) and be monitored with **serial LSM by VCTE or MRE** (correlates with CSPH and decompensation).

**Table 1 — NIT cutoffs to rule in advanced fibrosis (F3–4), AUROC (95% CI):**

| Noninvasive test | Rule-in cutoff for AF | AUROC (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|
| FIB-4 score | >2.67 | 0.83 (0.79–0.86) |
| NAFLD-FS | >0.676 | 0.75 (0.71–0.79) |
| APRI | >0.84 | 0.76 (0.74–0.79) |
| ELF | >9.8 | 0.81 (0.77–0.85) |
| VCTE (kPa) | >12.0 | 0.93 (0.89–0.96) |
| SWE (kPa) | >8.0 | 0.89 (0.80–0.98) |
| MRE (kPa) | >3.6 | 0.93 (0.90–0.96) |

**Key operative thresholds:**
- FIB-4 **<1.3** rule-out AF (NPV ~90% in specialty populations; rises to ~96% at 10% prevalence, falls to ~73% at 50% prevalence — **NPV/PPV are prevalence-dependent**).
- FIB-4 **>2.67** → associated with AF; **>3.25** → associated with cirrhosis (high specificity/low sensitivity → high PPV, poor NPV).
- **Age adjustment:** in patients **>65 y** use FIB-4 **<2.0** to exclude AF.
- Intermediate FIB-4 **1.3–2.67** → add VCTE; LSM **8–12 kPa** suggests clinically significant fibrosis; **>12 kPa** high-sensitivity threshold for cirrhosis.
- Sequential rule-out (**FIB-4 <1.3 + VCTE <8.0 kPa**) and rule-in (**FIB-4 ≥3.48 + VCTE ≥20 kPa**) reduces biopsies (33% → 19%).
- CSPH: **LSM by VCTE ≥25 kPa** reliably rules in CSPH across cACLD; ANTICIPATE model (platelets and LSM) for stratification.
- Serial testing reduces indeterminate AF stratification by ~20% vs single NIT; MEFIB (MRE + FIB-4) outperformed FAST for at-risk NASH (AUROC 0.90 vs 0.73).

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- This is a **2023** CPU under the older **NAFLD/NASH** nomenclature; **AASLD 2024 NILDA** guidances ([[noninvasive-liver-disease-assessment]]) are newer and higher-detail for the same testing questions — where they differ on specific cutoffs, the newer AASLD guidance governs the page; this CPU corroborates the FIB-4-first sequential framework. Also predates **MASLD** nomenclature ([[aasld-2023-masld-nomenclature]]).
- No strong evidence base yet for optimal **monitoring frequency** (expert suggestion: FIB-4 annually in high-risk e.g. T2DM, every 2 y in low-risk).
