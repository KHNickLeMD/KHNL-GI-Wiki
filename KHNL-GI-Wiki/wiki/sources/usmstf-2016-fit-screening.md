## Summary

- Reviews fecal immunochemical test (FIT) performance for colorectal cancer (CRC) and advanced neoplasia, compares FIT with other screening tests, and gives guidance statements plus quality metrics for FIT programs.
- FIT measures **human hemoglobin** with antibodies to globin. Guaiac fecal occult blood test (gFOBT) detects peroxidase activity, so food peroxidases and some drugs confound it; FIT avoids that.
- Most FITs are **qualitative** (positive above a preset threshold); a few are **quantitative** (hemoglobin measured, positive above a chosen cut-off). Results are reported as µg hemoglobin per g feces.
- Focused on tests approved for US use.

### Grading and format

- Modified Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE), qualitative review without formal meta-analysis.
- Strong = "we recommend"; weak = "we suggest". Quality: high / moderate / low / very low.
- **Statements are not numbered.** Table 7 summarizes 10 of them plus 4 program targets; three further graded statements appear only in the text (repeat testing, colonoscopy not repeat FIT after a positive test, FIT over gFOBT).

| Follow-up test | In **screen-eligible** people with a positive FIT, **colonoscopy** is the recommended follow-up test. | Strong; moderate | Table 7 |
| Upper GI evaluation | Positive FIT + negative colonoscopy should **not** prompt upper gastrointestinal (GI) evaluation **absent iron-deficiency anemia or upper GI signs/symptoms** (Table 7 wording omits iron-deficiency anemia). | Weak; very low | Table 7 |
| Recent colonoscopy | Positive FIT after a **recent colonoscopy** (before repeat endoscopy is due) → **generally offer repeat colonoscopy**. Weigh clinical context (other signs, symptoms, labs), patient factors (advanced-neoplasia risk, preference), and prior exam quality (poor prep, endoscopist adenoma detection rate). | Weak; low | Table 7 |
| Diet and drugs | **We recommend** explicitly telling patients they **need not adjust diet or medications** (including anticoagulants and antiplatelets) for FIT. | Strong; moderate | Table 7 |

| Quality assurance | Programs should set up quality assurance to monitor key metrics (targets below). | Weak; very low | Table 7 |

### Program quality targets (committee suggestion)

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| FIT completion among those offered | ≥60% |
| Returned FITs the laboratory cannot process | <5% |
| Colonoscopy completion after positive FIT | ≥80% |
| Adenoma detection rate (ADR) on colonoscopy for positive FIT (hemoglobin threshold ≤20 µg/g) | >45% men; >35% women |

## Key Findings / Claims

### One-time FIT performance

- **CRC (meta-analysis, 19 studies, asymptomatic average-risk adults; Table 1):** sensitivity 79% (95% confidence interval [CI] 69–86%), specificity 94% (92–95%). Colonoscopy-reference studies only: 77% / 94%.
- OC FIT-CHEK in 9,989 people having colonoscopy: CRC sensitivity 74%, specificity 96%.
- Brands differ: OC-Sensor beat RIDASCREEN for CRC (73.3% vs 60.0%, specificity ~95%), at cut-offs of 6.1 vs 24.5 µg/g. Taiwan program at 20 µg/g: OC-Sensor more sensitive than HM-Jack (80% vs 68%). Spain: OC-Sensor vs FOB Gold — participation 61.8% vs 59.1%, no difference in cancer detection.
- **Advanced adenoma (Table 2):** sensitivity 6–56% across studies; spread reflects brand and cut-off. German 5-brand study (n = 1,319): sensitivity 25–56%, specificity 68–96%.
  - Lower cut-off raises sensitivity, lowers specificity: OC-Sensor 20 → 10 µg/g, 29% → 35% and 97% → 93%; RIDASCREEN 14 → 2 µg/g, 23.9% → 40.0% and 97.4% → 89.6%.
- One-time PPV: CRC 2.9–7.8%; advanced neoplasia 33.9–54%. Screening colonoscopy yield, for contrast: cancer 0.5–1%, advanced neoplasia 5–10%.

### Multiple rounds (Table 3)

- Participation stays stable (Netherlands >60% after 3 rounds; Kaiser Permanente: 48% of 670,841 responded initially, 75–86% of responders continued over 3 more rounds; Italy: 78% of 1,862 ever attended, 38% all 4 rounds).
- Positivity, colonoscopy demand, detection rate and CRC PPV fall in later rounds (prevalent lesions removed); advanced-neoplasia PPV stays ~30–40%. No cancers in round 4 of one biennial program.

### Comparisons with other tests

- **vs gFOBT (Table 4):** FIT more sensitive for CRC and advanced neoplasia with similar or slightly lower specificity.
  - Meta-analysis: cancer detection RR 1.96 (1.2–3.2); advanced neoplasia RR 2.28 (1.68–3.10); adherence RR 1.16 (1.03–1.30).
  - At matched positivity: CRC sensitivity 73.3% FIT vs 33.3% gFOBT, specificity >95% both. French program at the same false-positive rate: advanced-neoplasia true-positive rate 9.7% vs 4.2%.
  - Participation: ≥4 RCTs, ~10% absolute increase; 2 meta-analyses, ~20% greater with FIT (fewer samples, no diet or drug restriction).
- **vs multitarget stool DNA (one-time, OC FIT-CHEK 20 µg/g, ~10,000 people):** stool DNA more sensitive for cancer (92.3% vs 73.8%) and advanced lesions (42.4% vs 23.8%), less specific (86.6% vs 94.9%). Sessile serrated polyps ≥1 cm: FIT 5% vs stool DNA 42% — FIT is ineffective for these.
- **vs flexible sigmoidoscopy (Table 5):** 3 RCTs — participation higher with FIT in 1 (61% vs 32%), similar in 2; advanced adenoma detection higher with sigmoidoscopy in all 3; cancer detection not significantly different. Simulated sigmoidoscopy (ColonPrev data): advanced neoplasia 6.3% vs 2.7%; proximal advanced neoplasia no different (odds ratio [OR] 1.17; 0.78–1.76).
- **vs colonoscopy (Table 6):** ColonPrev first round (26,703 colonoscopy vs 26,599 biennial FIT, 15 µg/g) — participation 34.2% FIT vs 24.6%; CRC detection no different; advanced adenoma 1.9% vs 0.9% favoring colonoscopy. Uninsured US population: participation 40.7% FIT vs 24.6%; cancer 0.4% each; advanced neoplasia 1.3% vs 0.8%. Long-term comparative effectiveness awaits 3 ongoing mortality RCTs.
- **Meta-analysis (mostly non-US):** endoscopic strategies had lower participation than FIT (RR 0.67; 0.56–0.80) but higher advanced-neoplasia detection (RR 3.21; 2.38–4.32).
- **Sequential testing** (offer one test, then another to decliners) raised participation from 19% to 25% in 2 studies and cancer detection by ~20%.

### Programmatic factors

- **Number of samples:** pooled CRC sensitivity 0.79 (1 sample), 0.77 (2), 0.80 (3); specificity 0.94 / 0.93 / 0.93. Dutch 2-sample FIT raised advanced neoplasia detection (4.1% vs 3.1%) without changing participation; a Korean 2-sample FIT raised CRC sensitivity (92.3% vs 76.9%) with small specificity loss. Cost-effectiveness (MISCAN-Colon [Microsimulation Screening Analysis-Colon]) favored shorter intervals of 1-sample FIT over 2-sample.
- **Interval:** annual programmatic gFOBT cuts CRC mortality by up to 33%; optimal FIT interval unknown (2 RCTs of annual vs biennial ongoing). Modeling: annual high-sensitivity FIT gave life-years similar to colonoscopy every 10 years. Dutch program: advanced neoplasia detection unaffected by intervals of 1–3 years.
- **Qualitative vs quantitative:** meta-analysis of 4 + 4 brands — CRC sensitivity 77% quantitative vs 85% qualitative, specificity 94% both. Qualitative tests: subjective reading, brand variability, quality-control failures in 2 US tests. US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) requires quantitative tests to be reported as positive/negative. Quantitative systems in the US: OC-Auto Micro 80 and OC-Sensor Diana (Polymedco), i-Chroma (Boditech). Fecal hemoglobin >177 µg/g → advanced neoplasia OR 3.80 (3.07–4.71) (Barcelona, n = 3,109).
- **Cut-off (meta-analysis):**

| Cut-off | CRC sensitivity | CRC specificity | Positivity (1-sample) |
|---|---|---|---|
| <20 µg/g | 0.86 (0.75–0.92) | 0.91 (0.89–0.93) | 5.3–14.2% |
| 20–50 µg/g | — | — | 1.4–7.5% |
| >50 µg/g | 0.67 (0.59–0.74) | 0.96 (0.94–0.98) | — |

  - <20 µg/g gave the best sensitivity–specificity balance. Modeling (OC-Sensor, 10–150 µg/g): 10 µg/g most efficient and cost-effective at 95.8% specificity. Korea (n = 14,289, OC-Sensor Diana): advanced neoplasia 29.9% at 20 vs 30.8% at 10 µg/g — no significant difference.
- **Other follow-up tests after a positive stool test:** computed tomography (CT) colonography (5 studies) — sensitivity ≥6 mm 89% (84–92%), specificity 75.4% (59–87%). Colon capsule (n = 62) — sensitivity 95%, specificity 65%; complete exam in 73%.
- **Upper GI:** in a study pairing FIT with barium meal, gastric cancer detection was 0.15% FIT-positive vs 0.13% FIT-negative.
- **After recent colonoscopy:** interval FIT in surveillance cohorts found significant neoplasia in 1.8% (first FIT); another detected 12 of 14 CRCs (86%) and 60 of 96 advanced adenomas (63%).
- **Medications:** aspirin, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, and anticoagulants did not reduce FIT PPV; sensitivity improved on antiplatelet or anticoagulant therapy with little specificity loss.
- **DRE sample (n = 1,688):** positivity 5.4% vs 3.5% with passed stool → significantly lower PPV for adenoma and cancer.
- **Temperature:** Italy — each 1 °C rise lowered positivity by 0.7%, 13% fewer advanced neoplasia in summer. Netherlands — OR 0.97 per °C; winter vs summer positivity 9.7% vs 8.0%. Korea (n = 8,316) — at ≥25 °C sample hemoglobin was slightly lower, but positivity and adenoma detection did not differ. France — summer positivity 2.3% vs 3.0%.
- **Return time:** processing delay ≥5 days lowered positivity (6% vs 8.7%) in one study; up to 10 days had no effect in Dutch and French programs.
- **Program benchmarks:** Ontario — 29.8% participation, 74.6% colonoscopy within 6 months of positive FOBT; England 52% participation and 83% colonoscopy; Finland 70% participation. Colonoscopy completion after positive FIT generally 80–90%. Adenoma PPV generally >45%; Kaiser (20 µg/g) 47.4–51.5% over 4 rounds; men 55% vs women 42%.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Cut-off wording within the document:** text says <20 µg/g; Table 7 says 20 µg/g or lower. [[aga-2022-noninvasive-crc-screening|AGA 2022]] uses ≤20 µg/g.
- **Upper GI qualifier within the document:** text exempts iron-deficiency anemia; Table 7 does not mention it. Use the text (narrower).
- **ADR benchmarks differ by setting:** >45% men / >35% women after a positive FIT here vs screening-colonoscopy benchmarks in [[aga-2021-colonoscopy-quality|AGA 2021]] (≥30%) and [[usmstf-2020-followup-colonoscopy|USMSTF 2020]] (≥30% men / ≥20% women). Different denominators, not a direct conflict; see [[colonoscopy-quality-indicators]].
- **Screening age and test menu** are not addressed here; [[usmstf-2021-crc-screening-age|USMSTF 2021]] and [[acg-2021-crc-screening|ACG 2021]] keep annual FIT as a first-tier test.
- **Serrated lesions:** FIT detected only 5% of sessile serrated polyps ≥1 cm.
- **Long-term FIT vs colonoscopy** effect on CRC mortality awaited 3 RCTs at publication; optimal FIT interval (annual vs biennial) also unsettled.
