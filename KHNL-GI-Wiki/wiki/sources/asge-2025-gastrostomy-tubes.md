## Summary

This American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) guideline addresses endoscopic enteral access, comparing **percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy (PEG)** with interventional-radiology gastrostomy (IR-G), the timing of feeding, periprocedural antithrombotic management, and gastrostomy technique in malignant dysphagia. It conditionally favors PEG in normal foregut anatomy and strongly recommends early feeding. Placement is performed via [[upper-endoscopy]] and supports [[nutrition-in-hospitalized-patients|enteral nutrition]] in patients who cannot meet needs orally.

## Key Recommendations

All four from Table 1 (PDF p. 3), with the guideline's own strength and quality-of-evidence labels and its best practice advice (BPA) for each.

| Rec | Statement | Strength | Quality of evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Normal foregut anatomy** needing enteral access → ASGE **suggests PEG over interventional radiology–guided gastrostomy (IR-G)** as the initial approach | Conditional | Low |
| 2a | On **antiplatelet drugs, including dual-antiplatelet therapy**, needing PEG → ASGE **suggests against routine withholding** of antiplatelet agents | Conditional | Very low |
| 2b | On **anticoagulants** needing PEG → periprocedural anticoagulant management should be based on a **multidisciplinary discussion including the patient**, weighing bleeding vs cardiovascular events | Conditional | Very low |
| 3 | After PEG → ASGE **recommends** PEG tubes may be used for **feeding early (within 4 h)** over routine delays in initiation | Strong | Moderate (randomized controlled trials); Low (all studies) |
| 4 | **Malignant dysphagia** needing gastrostomy → ASGE **suggests either transoral pull PEG, transcutaneous direct PEG, or IR-G** | Conditional | Very low |

Rec 4 wording is from the boxed statement in the text (p. 30); the Table 1 version omits "or IR-G".

**Best practice advice (Table 1):**
- **Rec 1:** transillumination, 1-to-1 indentation and a safe-track needle technique with air aspiration **should be performed** during PEG to reduce colon perforation. Prophylactic antibiotics (e.g., cefazolin) **should be given** at the time of initial gastrostomy to reduce infection.
- **Rec 2:** PEG may be performed while on antiplatelet medications. Patients on antithrombotics who **restrict blood transfusions** (antibodies or religious beliefs) or are at **high bleeding risk** → continuing antiplatelet and anticoagulant drugs decided by a multidisciplinary discussion with the team and patient. Tightening the PEG bumper for **24 hours and then loosening it may be considered** to tamponade potential bleeding — data limited; avoid excessive tightening (buried bumper).
- **Rec 3:** absent clinical contraindications, **medications may be given through the PEG immediately** after placement. **Routine gastric residual volume measurement is not indicated.**
- **Rec 4:** counsel about **implantation-metastasis** risk during informed consent; **periodically examine the gastrostomy site** for implantation metastasis.

## Clinical Relevance

Codifies PEG-first enteral access (conditional), feeding within 4 hours (strong), and no routine interruption of antiplatelet agents, including dual-antiplatelet therapy (conditional). Decisions about anticoagulants intersect with broader periprocedural bleeding-risk management in [[anticoagulation-gi-bleeding]], and downstream care supports [[nutrition-in-hospitalized-patients]].
