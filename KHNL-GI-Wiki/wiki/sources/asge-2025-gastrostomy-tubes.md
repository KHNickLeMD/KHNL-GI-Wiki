## Summary

This ASGE guideline addresses endoscopic enteral access, comparing **percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy (PEG)** with interventional-radiology gastrostomy (IR-G), the timing of feeding, and periprocedural antithrombotic management. It supports PEG as the default modality and earlier feeding than historically practiced. Placement is performed via [[upper-endoscopy]] and supports [[nutrition-in-hospitalized-patients|enteral nutrition]] in patients who cannot meet needs orally.

---

## Key Recommendations

- **Rec 1:** In patients with **normal foregut anatomy** needing enteral access, **PEG is preferred over IR-G** as the initial gastrostomy technique.
- **Early feeding:** Tube feeding can be **safely started within 4 hours** of gastrostomy.
- **Rec 2a:** On **antiplatelet therapy**, suggests **against routine withholding** of antiplatelet agents before PEG.
- **Rec 2b:** On **anticoagulants**, periprocedural management is **individualized** (managed differently from antiplatelets, balancing thrombotic vs bleeding risk).
- **Rec 3:** PEG tubes may be placed via **transoral "pull" or transcutaneous** techniques; tightening the external bumper for ~24 h is reasonable.

---

## Clinical Relevance

Codifies PEG-first enteral access, feeding within 4 hours, and no routine interruption of antiplatelet agents — reducing delays and unnecessary antithrombotic holds. Decisions about anticoagulants intersect with broader periprocedural bleeding-risk management in [[anticoagulation-gi-bleeding]], and downstream care supports [[nutrition-in-hospitalized-patients]].
