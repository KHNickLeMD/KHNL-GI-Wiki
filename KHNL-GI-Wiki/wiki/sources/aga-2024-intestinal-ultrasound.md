## Summary

AGA CPU Commentary reviewing **intestinal ultrasound (IUS)** for monitoring inflammatory bowel disease. IUS is a noninvasive, radiation-free, point-of-care exam of the bowel wall, mesentery, and adjacent structures — performed transabdominally without bowel prep, fasting, or contrast. Used for over a decade in Europe, US adoption is rising rapidly. Because clinical remission poorly correlates with endoscopic healing and repeat endoscopy/biomarker sampling is burdensome, IUS offers an alternative objective activity indicator supporting a **treat-to-target** approach.

**Technique:** high-resolution system with a low-frequency convex transducer (global survey) then a high-frequency linear transducer (activity assessment); linear alone often suffices in children/small body habitus. Primary activity measures = **bowel wall thickness (BWT)** and **bowel wall hyperemia (color Doppler)**. Inflammatory mesenteric fat, loss of wall stratification, and lymphadenopathy indicate more severe disease. IUS detects strictures, fistulae, sinus tracts, phlegmons, abscesses. Accurate for small-bowel and colonic inflammation but less accurate for mid/proximal small bowel location, disease length (esp. >10 cm), and proximal small-bowel and **rectal** inflammation.

**Comparative accuracy:** IUS by trained operators is comparable to MRE and CTE for ileal CD activity and for detecting complications (fistula, stricture, abscess), with practical advantages (no contrast/radiation, point-of-care, real-time treatment change). Interobserver reliability moderate-excellent, and higher than serial MRE in one substudy (82% vs 69% agreement). Ileocolonoscopy/VCE directly visualize mucosa but are more time-/cost-intensive and require sedation.

**Special value** in pregnant patients (avoids radiation/sedation; sensitivity 84%, specificity 98% vs clinical activity/FC; TI + colon visualized in up to 92% in 3rd trimester), patients with serious comorbidities (renal failure, sedation risk), and obese patients (image quality may drop but findings not significantly different by weight). Limitations: poor for proximal/duodenal disease (bowel gas), rectal disease (needs distended bladder), precise disease-length measurement, and **not advised for dysplasia surveillance** (use endoscopy). No US certification for GI yet; IBUS offers a 3-module training/certification pathway.

## Key Findings / Claims (decision-relevant thresholds)
- **Postoperative CD recurrence:** neo-terminal ileum/adjacent colon **BWT ≥3 mm + FC ≥50 µg/g** → detects recurrence with **PPV 74%** (95% CI 63–84), NPV 71% (95% CI 42–92).
- **CD treatment response:** IUS improvement identifiable within **3 months** of biologic initiation. Higher baseline BWT predicts failure (42% less likely to reach transmural healing at 1 year). In a treat-to-target ustekinumab RCT: **sonographic response** = ≥25% BWT reduction (46.3% at wk 48); **transmural healing** = normalization of all IUS parameters (24.1% at wk 48); lack of sonographic response at wk 4 → poor wk-48 endoscopic response (NPV 73%). Single-center: ≥18% BWT reduction at 4–8 wk post-anti-TNF → 10× likelier wk-12–32 endoscopic response.
- **UC treatment response:** BWT normalization as early as **2 weeks** after treatment change. Tofacitinib: **absolute BWT <2.8 mm** predicts wk-8 endoscopic remission (sens 73%, spec 100%, AUC 0.87); **≥32% BWT reduction** predicts wk-8 endoscopic response (sens 71%, spec 90%, AUC 0.87). **Acute severe UC:** **≥20% BWT reduction within 48 h** of IV steroids → more likely endoscopic response (OR 22.6) and avoidance of rescue biologic (OR 6.3).
- **Screening/rule-out:** IUS can rule out IBD akin to FC; positive findings (↑BWT or hyperemia) expedite ileocolonoscopy.
- **CPT:** no IUS-specific code yet; e.g. 76705 (limited abdominal US) used.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- Multiple IUS scoring systems exist but **none is regularly used in clinical practice** — reporting not yet standardized.
- Most data are single-center; role of IUS in UC **in remission** is understudied.
- Operator-dependent; US-based GI certification not yet available.
