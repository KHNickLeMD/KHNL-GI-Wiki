## Summary

American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) guideline against reflexive, protocol-driven laboratory testing before endoscopy. Most outcomes data are extrapolated from the surgical/anesthesia literature, where routine preoperative testing rarely alters management: unsuspected chemistry abnormalities appear in only 0.2-1.0% of healthy patients and do not change anesthetic or procedural care. When tests are ordered on the basis of history/exam, up to 30% are abnormal and often change management — arguing for **selective, risk-based testing** rather than universal panels.

The guideline sets the framework for pre-endoscopy labs: an abnormal partial thromboplastin time (PTT) does not reliably predict periprocedural bleeding; coagulation studies, chest radiography, electrocardiography (ECG), blood type/screen, hemoglobin/hematocrit, urinalysis, and chemistries should be individualized to patient and procedural risk. It is referenced by other ASGE procedural documents (eg, enteral feeding) that state "routine laboratory testing before endoscopy is not indicated."

## Key Findings / Claims

- Routine preoperative/pre-endoscopy chemistry testing rarely yields management-altering abnormalities in patients without a suggestive history (unsuspected abnormalities 0.2-1.0%).
- Selective testing (ordered from clinical suspicion) yields abnormalities in up to ~30% and more often changes management.
- Abnormal PTT does not reliably predict periprocedural bleeding.
- Mild renal impairment does not affect outcomes with moderate/deep sedation; renal-function testing is not required for endoscopy on that basis alone. (Creatinine >1.9 mg/dL predicts poor outcome after *major surgery*; no endoscopy data.)
- In insulin-requiring diabetes, preprocedural blood glucose may be warranted.
- **Routine ECG is not recommended — with one exception:** droperidol sedation is being considered. Droperidol prolongs the QT interval and is contraindicated with a prolonged QT interval.
- PT and INR **do not predict bleeding risk in liver disease** — they measure procoagulant activity only, not the anticoagulants that fall in parallel.
- Hemoglobin <8 mg/dL has been associated with cardiac morbidity and operative death; baseline hemoglobin predicts transfusion need in surgery with significant blood loss.
- The document explicitly **does not define acceptable INR or platelet thresholds** for endoscopy — it selects who gets tested, not how to act on the result.

## Recommendations

**The document numbers its own recommendations 1–8** and grades each with a Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) evidence-quality symbol only (no separate strength rating): ⊕⊕⊕⊕ high · ⊕⊕⊕◯ moderate · ⊕⊕◯◯ low · ⊕◯◯◯ very low (its Table 1). Strength is carried by the verb — *"We recommend"* is the stronger form, *"We suggest"* the weaker — as the document states in its opening paragraph. Wording below is condensed; the qualifiers are the document's.

1. **Recommend** endoscopists pursue preprocedure testing **selectively**, based on medical history, physical examination, and patient and procedural risk factors. (⊕⊕⊕⊕)
2. **Recommend against** routine testing with coagulation studies, chest radiography, ECG, blood typing or screening, hemoglobin or hematocrit, urinalysis, and chemistry tests before endoscopy in healthy patients; individualize to patient and procedural risk factors. (⊕⊕⊕⊕)
3. **Suggest** pregnancy testing be considered before endoscopy and fluoroscopy use in female patients of childbearing age with an uncertain pregnancy history or a history suggestive of current pregnancy, absent a previous total hysterectomy, bilateral tubal ligation, or absent menses for 1 year (menopause). (⊕⊕◯◯)
4. **Suggest** coagulation studies before endoscopy in patients with active bleeding, a known or clinically suspected bleeding disorder, medication risk (eg, anticoagulant use, prolonged antibiotics), prolonged biliary obstruction, history of abnormal bleeding, malnutrition, or other conditions associated with acquired coagulopathies. (⊕⊕◯◯)
5. **Suggest** obtaining a chest radiograph before endoscopy in patients with new respiratory symptoms or decompensated cardiac failure. (⊕⊕◯◯)
6. **Recommend** blood typing and screening before endoscopy when a blood transfusion is considered likely, in patients with active bleeding or anemia. (⊕⊕⊕⊕)
7. **Suggest** testing the hemoglobin/hematocrit before endoscopy in patients with preexisting anemia or ongoing bleeding, or when there is a high risk of significant blood loss during the procedure. (⊕⊕⊕◯)
8. **Suggest** selective chemistry testing before endoscopy in patients with significant endocrine, renal, or hepatic dysfunction before using medications that may further impair function. (⊕⊕◯◯)
