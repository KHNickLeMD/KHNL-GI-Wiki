## Summary

ASGE guideline against reflexive, protocol-driven laboratory testing before endoscopy. Most outcomes data are extrapolated from the surgical/anesthesia literature, where routine preoperative testing rarely alters management: unsuspected chemistry abnormalities appear in only 0.2-1.0% of healthy patients and do not change anesthetic or procedural care. When tests are ordered on the basis of history/exam, up to 30% are abnormal and often change management — arguing for **selective, risk-based testing** rather than universal panels.

The guideline sets the wiki's framework for pre-endoscopy labs: an abnormal PTT does not reliably predict periprocedural bleeding; coagulation studies, chest radiography, ECG, blood type/screen, hemoglobin/hematocrit, urinalysis, and chemistries should be individualized to patient and procedural risk. It is referenced by other ASGE procedural documents (eg, enteral feeding) that state "routine laboratory testing before endoscopy is not indicated."

## Key Findings / Claims

- Routine preoperative/pre-endoscopy chemistry testing rarely yields management-altering abnormalities in patients without a suggestive history (unsuspected abnormalities 0.2-1.0%).
- Selective testing (ordered from clinical suspicion) yields abnormalities in up to ~30% and more often changes management.
- Abnormal PTT does not reliably predict periprocedural bleeding.
- Mild renal impairment does not affect outcomes with moderate/deep sedation; renal-function testing is not required for endoscopy on that basis alone.
- In insulin-requiring diabetes, preprocedural blood glucose may be warranted.

## Recommendations (verbatim)

1. Recommend endoscopists pursue preprocedure testing **selectively** based on medical history, physical examination, and patient and procedural risk factors. (High/strong)
2. Recommend **against** routine testing with coagulation studies, chest radiography, ECG, blood typing or screening, hemoglobin/hematocrit, urinalysis, and chemistry tests before endoscopy in healthy patients; individualize based on patient and procedural risk factors. (High/strong)
3. Suggest pregnancy testing be considered before endoscopy and fluoroscopy in female patients of childbearing age with uncertain pregnancy history or history suggestive of current pregnancy (absent prior hysterectomy, bilateral tubal ligation, or ≥1 year amenorrhea/menopause). (Moderate/conditional)
4. Suggest coagulation studies before endoscopy in patients with active bleeding, known/suspected bleeding disorder, medication risk (anticoagulant use, prolonged antibiotics), prolonged biliary obstruction, history of abnormal bleeding, malnutrition, or other conditions associated with acquired coagulopathies. (Moderate/conditional)
5. Suggest a chest radiograph before endoscopy in patients with new respiratory symptoms or decompensated cardiac failure. (Moderate/conditional)
6. Recommend blood typing and screening before endoscopy when transfusion is considered likely (active bleeding or anemia). (High/strong)
7. Suggest testing hemoglobin/hematocrit before endoscopy in patients with preexisting anemia, ongoing bleeding, or high risk of significant blood loss during the procedure. (High/conditional)
8. Suggest selective chemistry testing before endoscopy in patients with significant endocrine, renal, or hepatic dysfunction before using medications that may further impair function. (Moderate/conditional)
