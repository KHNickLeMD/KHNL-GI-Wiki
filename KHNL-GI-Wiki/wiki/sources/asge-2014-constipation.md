## Summary
American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) position statement on the diagnostic and therapeutic role of endoscopy in [[chronic-idiopathic-constipation|chronic constipation]]. Endoscopy is **not** indicated in the initial evaluation of chronic constipation when no alarm features or suspicion of organic disease exist. The yield of colonoscopy for isolated constipation is low — comparable to average-risk screening — and a meta-analysis of 28 studies found no increase in [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer]] among patients with chronic constipation.

Colonoscopy is indicated for alarm features (rectal bleeding, heme-positive stool, [[iron-deficiency-anemia|iron deficiency anemia]], weight loss, obstructive symptoms), in selected patients to exclude obstruction from cancer, stricture, or extrinsic compression, before surgery for constipation, and in patients aged >50 y without prior colorectal cancer (CRC) screening. Therapeutically, colonoscopy can dilate fibrotic colonic strictures from [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]], surgery, or ischemia, and create percutaneous endoscopic cecostomy/colostomy. Colonoscopy has **no role in stool disimpaction**. Chronic constipation is an independent risk factor for inadequate bowel preparation — consider a more aggressive cleansing regimen.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Definition used**
- Chronic constipation = **Rome III** criteria for functional constipation (document's Table 2): criteria met for the previous 3 months with symptom onset ≥6 months before diagnosis; **≥2 of 6 symptom items** (each at ≥25% of defecations, except <3 defecations/week); **plus** loose stools rarely present without laxatives; **plus** insufficient criteria for [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|irritable bowel syndrome]]. Item list reproduced on [[chronic-idiopathic-constipation]].
- Symptoms include excessive straining, discomfort at defecation, or hard/pellet-like stools — **even when defecation frequency is normal**.

**Diagnostic role**
- Suspected Hirschsprung's disease requires [[anorectal-manometry|anorectal manometry]] + **deep biopsy** (to examine for absence of myenteric neurons).
- In younger patients, flexible sigmoidoscopy may be sufficient to exclude distal disease — the document sets no age cut-point for "younger".
- Associated incidental findings: solitary rectal ulcer syndrome (indicates [[rectal-prolapse|rectal prolapse]]), [[anal-fissure|anal fissure]], melanosis coli (indicates chronic laxative use).

**Yield of lower endoscopy for constipation**
- Yield in isolated constipation is low and **comparable to asymptomatic average-risk CRC screening**.
- Series of 563 sigmoidoscopies/colonoscopies done for constipation: CRC 8 (1.4%), adenomas 82 (14.6%), advanced lesions 24 (4.3%) — *advanced lesion* = cancer, or adenoma with malignancy, high-grade dysplasia, villous features, or size >10 mm.
- Review of 41,775 index colonoscopies: constipation alone carried a **lower** risk of significant findings than average-risk screening; a second study likewise found neoplasia prevalence lower than in routine screening.

**Therapeutic role**
- Fibrotic strictures from IBD, surgery, or ischemia can be dilated at colonoscopy.
- Percutaneous endoscopic cecostomy/colostomy: favorable results **in children** with severe refractory constipation (e.g., neurogenic bowel); **in adults**, percutaneous endoscopic cecostomy may be effective in [[acute-colonic-pseudo-obstruction|acute colonic pseudo-obstruction]] and neurogenic bowel when conservative treatment fails.
- No role in stool disimpaction — the only reported exception is colonoscopic removal of bezoar-induced fecal impaction.
- Chronic constipation is an **independent risk factor for inadequate bowel preparation**; the document does not specify which regimen to use, only that a more aggressive one be considered.

**Epidemiology**
- Prevalence 2%–27%; ~2.5 million United States physician visits/year; more common in women, rises with age. Reported risk factors: low socioeconomic status, physical inactivity, history of sexual abuse, depression.

## Recommendations (verbatim)

**How this document grades.** Its "Summary" numbers the four statements 1–4 (numbering below is the document's own). The only grade attached is a **GRADE quality-of-evidence** rating — High (⊕⊕⊕⊕), Moderate (⊕⊕⊕○), Low (⊕⊕○○), Very low (⊕○○○). **No separate strength-of-recommendation label is assigned**: strength is carried by the verb — "We recommend" is the stronger form, "We suggest" the weaker. All four statements here are "We recommend".

1. We recommend that GI [gastrointestinal] endoscopy should not be performed in the initial evaluation of patients presenting with symptoms of chronic constipation in the absence of alarm features or suspicion of organic GI disease. *(⊕⊕⊕○ Moderate quality)*
2. We recommend that patients with constipation undergo colonoscopy to exclude organic disease if they have rectal bleeding, heme-positive stool, iron deficiency anemia, or weight loss prior to surgical therapy for chronic constipation. *(⊕⊕⊕⊕ High quality)*
3. We recommend that patients aged >50 years presenting with constipation who have not previously had colon cancer screening should have a colonoscopy. *(⊕⊕⊕⊕ High quality)*
4. We recommend colonoscopy to allow dilation of benign colon strictures and creation of percutaneous cecostomy when clinically appropriate and feasible. *(⊕⊕⊕○ Moderate quality)*

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **Constipation and colorectal cancer risk is unresolved within the document.** Two United States population-based retrospective studies, one Australian retrospective study, and one Japanese cohort of frequent laxative users reported increased cancer risk; a prospective cohort of female nurses did not, and a meta-analysis of 28 studies found no increase. The document calls the findings inconsistent and still recommends colonoscopy at >50 y without prior screening — on screening grounds, not because constipation raises risk.
- **Recommendation 2 is narrower than the body text.** The body lists **obstructive symptoms** among the alarm features mandating colonoscopy and treats "before surgery for constipation" as a *separate* indication; Recommendation 2 omits obstructive symptoms and reads as though the alarm features apply only to the presurgical setting. Use the body-text list.
- **Recommendation 1 vs Recommendation 3.** No endoscopy in the initial evaluation without alarm features, yet colonoscopy for everyone >50 y never screened — the overlap is not addressed; the second is a screening indication.
- **Cecostomy evidence is largely pediatric.** Favorable results come from children with severe refractory constipation; adult support is a case series in acute colonic pseudo-obstruction and neurogenic bowel. Recommendation 4 gives no adult selection criteria.
- **"No role in stool disimpaction" has one reported exception** — colonoscopic removal of bezoar-induced fecal impaction.
- Uses Rome III; [[rome-v-2026-dgbi|Rome V]] renames functional constipation to chronic constipation and changes the criteria. Primary CRC screening now starts at 45 y ([[colorectal-cancer-screening]]), superseding the ">50 y" framing of Recommendation 3.
- Bowel-preparation intensification is advised but no regimen, volume, or timing is specified.
