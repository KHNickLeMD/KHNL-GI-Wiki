## Summary

The US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) recommendation statement that **moved the US starting age for average-risk colorectal cancer (CRC) screening from 50 to 45**, replacing the 2016 statement. It is the document behind the age-45 grade that payers and quality programs cite.

Three lettered recommendations: **Grade A** for ages 50–75, **Grade B** for ages 45–49, and **Grade C** — selectively offer, individualized — for ages 76–85 in adults previously screened. The 2016 statement's position that screening be **discontinued after age 85** is continued. The 45–49 extension rests on moderate certainty of moderate net benefit, driven by Cancer Intervention and Surveillance Modeling Network (CISNET) modeling rather than by direct trial evidence in that age band: no screening trial reported results separately for adults younger than 50.

The Task Force takes the deliberate position of **not ranking the recommended strategies** — seven strategies with their intervals are listed as equivalent options, and the choice is framed as an adherence and implementation problem rather than a test-performance one. It also explicitly **excludes serum tests, urine tests and capsule endoscopy** from the recommended set for lack of evidence, and states that a positive result on any non-colonoscopy test requires follow-up colonoscopy for the benefit to be realized.

The statement devotes a dedicated section to **CRC in Black adults** — higher incidence and mortality — and concludes that the available evidence does not support a different strategy or a different starting age by race, while noting that the US Multi-Society Task Force does recommend starting at 45 for Black adults and 50 for others. It attributes the disparity primarily to access and quality-of-care inequities rather than biological difference.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Recommendations (3, graded with the USPSTF's own letter grades)

| Population | Statement | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| **Adults aged 50 to 75 years** | The USPSTF **recommends screening** for colorectal cancer in all adults aged 50 to 75 years. | **A** |
| **Adults aged 45 to 49 years** | The USPSTF **recommends screening** for colorectal cancer in adults aged 45 to 49 years. | **B** |
| **Adults aged 76 to 85 years** | The USPSTF recommends that clinicians **selectively offer** screening for colorectal cancer in adults aged 76 to 85 years. Evidence indicates that the net benefit of screening all persons in this age group is small. In determining whether this service is appropriate in individual cases, patients and clinicians should consider the patient's **overall health, prior screening history, and preferences**. | **C** |

- **Stop rule:** the Task Force "continues to conclude that … screening should be discontinued after age 85." In adults 86 or older, evidence on benefits and harms is lacking and **competing causes of mortality likely preclude any survival benefit**.
- The Grade C statement applies to adults **who have been previously screened**; adults who have **never** been screened are more likely to benefit.

### Population to whom this applies
- **Asymptomatic adults 45 years or older at average risk** — defined as **no prior diagnosis of colorectal cancer, adenomatous polyps, or inflammatory bowel disease**, and **no personal diagnosis or family history of a known genetic disorder predisposing to high lifetime CRC risk** (such as Lynch syndrome or familial adenomatous polyposis).
- **All adults 45 or older should be offered screening even if other risk factors are absent.** Family history of CRC without a known inherited syndrome, male sex, obesity, diabetes, long-term smoking and unhealthy alcohol use raise risk but do not change the offer.

### Recommended screening strategies and intervals
*The USPSTF does not rank these; all are recommended options.*

| Strategy | Interval |
|---|---|
| High-sensitivity guaiac fecal occult blood test (HSgFOBT) | **Every year** |
| Fecal immunochemical test (FIT) | **Every year** |
| Stool DNA–FIT (sDNA-FIT, multitarget stool DNA test) | **Every 1 to 3 years** |
| Computed tomography (CT) colonography | **Every 5 years** |
| Flexible sigmoidoscopy | **Every 5 years** |
| Flexible sigmoidoscopy **every 10 years plus annual FIT** | as stated |
| Colonoscopy | **Every 10 years** |

- **Not recommended for screening, for limited evidence:** **serum tests, urine tests, and capsule endoscopy**.
- **A positive result on any stool-based or non-colonoscopy visualization test requires follow-up colonoscopy** — "to achieve the benefits of screening, abnormal results on stool-based tests, CT colonography, and flexible sigmoidoscopy should be followed up with colonoscopy."
- Only one stool DNA test is FDA-approved and it includes a FIT component (sDNA-FIT).
- **Test-collection differences that drive adherence:** HSgFOBT requires **dietary and medication restrictions** and samples from **3 separate bowel movements**; FIT and sDNA-FIT require **neither restriction**, FIT from a **single** stool sample, sDNA-FIT from an **entire bowel movement**.
- Flexible sigmoidoscopy visualizes only rectum, sigmoid and descending colon; availability in the US has declined and it may be unavailable in some communities.

| | 45–49 y | 50–75 y | ≥76 y |
|---|---|---|---|
| Detection | **Adequate** evidence tests accurately detect early-stage CRC and adenomatous polyps | **Convincing** | **Convincing** |

| Harms | **Adequate** evidence harms are **small** | **Adequate** evidence harms are **small to moderate** | **Small to moderate**; serious adverse events from colonoscopy and extracolonic findings on CT colonography **increase with age** |
| Net benefit | **Moderate certainty of moderate net benefit** | **High certainty of substantial net benefit** | **Moderate certainty of small net benefit** (76–85 y, previously screened) |

### What starting at 45 instead of 50 buys (CISNET modeling, per 1000 adults screened)
- **+2 to 3 CRC cases averted**, **+1 CRC death averted**, **+22 to 27 life-years gained** — i.e. an estimated **8 to 10 additional days of life per person screened**.
- Absolute totals across strategies starting at 45 and continuing to 75: **286–337 life-years gained**, **42–61 CRC cases averted**, **24–26 CRC deaths averted** per 1000 screened (104–123 days of life per person).
- **Cost in procedures:** colonoscopy every 10 years starting at 45 adds **784 lifetime colonoscopies per 1000 screened** (4248 vs 3464) and **2 additional complications per 1000** (16 vs 14) — the largest added complication burden of any strategy.
- sDNA-FIT **annually** adds ~850 follow-up/surveillance colonoscopies per 1000 screened versus annual FIT; **every 2 years**, ~300 more. Annual sDNA-FIT and every-3-year HSgFOBT did **not** give an efficient balance of life-years gained versus colonoscopy burden.
- Incidence rationale: CRC incidence in adults **40–49** rose nearly **15%** from 2000–2002 to 2014–2016; **10.5%** of new CRC cases occur in people younger than 50. Age-specific incidence 20.0 per 100,000 at 40–49 vs 47.8 at 50–59 and 105.2 at ≥60.

### Harms (direct estimates)
- **Follow-up colonoscopy after a positive stool test:** **17.5 serious bleeding events** (95% CI 7.6–27.5; 11 studies, n = 78,793) and **5.4 perforations** (95% CI 3.4–7.4; 12 studies, n = 341,922) **per 10,000 colonoscopies**.
- **Screening colonoscopy** (67 observational studies, n = 27,746,669) — lower: **14.6 major bleeding events** per 10,000 (95% CI 9.4–19.9) and **3.1 perforations** per 10,000 (95% CI 2.3–4.0), presumably from fewer biopsies and adenoma removals.
- No studies reported harms from the stool tests themselves; harms are from false-positive/false-negative results and from the workup of positives.
- Cardiopulmonary events may rarely occur with sedation; precise frequency unknown.

### Test accuracy (single application, colonoscopy as reference)
| Test | Sensitivity for CRC | Specificity |
|---|---|---|
| HSgFOBT | 0.50–0.75 (2 studies, n = 3503) | 0.96–0.98 |
| FIT (OC-Sensor family, 20 µg hemoglobin/g threshold) | **0.74** (95% CI 0.64–0.83; 9 studies, n = 34,352) | **0.94** (0.93–0.96) |
| sDNA-FIT | **0.93** (95% CI 0.87–1.0; 4 studies, n = 12,424) | **0.84** (0.84–0.86) |
| Colonoscopy (adenomas ≥10 mm) | 0.89–0.95 | 0.89 (single study) |
| CT colonography (adenomas ≥10 mm) | 0.86–1.0 (6 of 7 studies) | pooled 0.94 (0.89–1.0) |

- Sensitivity for **advanced adenomas** is much lower than for cancer: HSgFOBT 0.06–0.17; FIT 0.23 (0.20–0.25); sDNA-FIT 0.43 (0.40–0.46).
- No studies reported flexible sigmoidoscopy accuracy against a colonoscopy reference standard.
- Accuracy studies generally found **no significant difference by age strata**; two suggested lower specificity for CRC detection at age ≥70 and one suggested decreasing sDNA-FIT specificity with increasing age.

### Direct evidence of benefit
- **Flexible sigmoidoscopy:** 4 RCTs (n = 458,002) — CRC mortality rate ratio **0.74** (95% CI 0.68–0.80) over 11 to 17 years. **None of the trials included people younger than 50.**
- **gFOBT:** after 2–9 rounds of biennial screening, CRC mortality relative risk **0.78 (0.65–0.93) to 0.91 (0.84–0.98)** at 11–30 years. Trials used Hemoccult II, an older gFOBT no longer commonly used; trials of **high-sensitivity** gFOBT are lacking.
- **Colonoscopy:** no RCT. Cohort evidence — adjusted hazard ratio **0.32** (0.24–0.45) for CRC mortality at 22 years after ≥1 colonoscopy; Medicare cohort standardized risk **0.42%** (0.24–0.63) at 8 years in adults 70–74 (not 75–79).
- **FIT:** Taiwanese program cohort (n = 5,417,699), adults 50–69 — adjusted relative risk **0.90** (0.84–0.95) at 6 years after 1–3 rounds of biennial screening.

### CRC in Black adults
- **Incidence 2013–2017 per 100,000:** Black 43.6 · American Indian/Alaska Native 39.0 · White 37.8 · Hispanic/Latino 33.7 · Asian/Pacific Islander 31.8.
- **Mortality 2014–2018 per 100,000:** Black 18.0 · American Indian/Alaska Native 15.1 · non-Hispanic White 13.6 · Hispanic/Latino 10.9 · Asian/Pacific Islander 9.4.
- **The USPSTF does not make a separate recommendation by race** — few studies reported screening accuracy or harms by race/ethnicity, and modeling assuming equal accuracy and natural history estimated a similar balance of benefits and harms. Its statement recommends **starting at 45 for everyone, including Black adults**.
- It names the driver as **inequities in access to and quality of screening and treatment, not genetic difference**, and strongly encourages clinicians to ensure Black patients receive recommended screening, follow-up and treatment.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **Starting age for Black adults — a stated disagreement with the US Multi-Society Task Force.** The USPSTF names it in the document: the USMSTF recommends starting at **45 in Black adults and 50 in others**, while the USPSTF recommends **45 for everyone**. Since the USPSTF statement also moves everyone to 45, the two now converge for Black adults and differ only for other races.
- **Grade B at 45–49 rests on modeling, not trials.** No screening trial reported results separately for adults younger than 50; the benefit estimate comes from CISNET models assuming 100% adherence and an elevated population risk reflecting birth-cohort effects.
- **HSgFOBT evidence is indirect** — the mortality trials used Hemoccult II, an older gFOBT no longer commonly used, and there is "considerable uncertainty" in the model predictions for HSgFOBT strategies.
- **Open:** whether sDNA-FIT's higher cancer sensitivity justifies its lower specificity and the extra colonoscopy burden — the statement calls the evidence on longitudinal follow-up after a negative sDNA-FIT insufficient and does not resolve the interval (1 to 3 years).
- **Scope limit, not a contradiction:** this statement covers **average-risk screening only**. Post-polypectomy surveillance, inflammatory bowel disease surveillance and hereditary-syndrome screening are explicitly outside its population and are governed by other sources.
