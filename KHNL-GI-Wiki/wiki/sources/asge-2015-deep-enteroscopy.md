## Summary
ASGE position statement on deep enteroscopy (DE) for small-bowel disorders. DE encompasses double-balloon enteroscopy (DBE), single-balloon enteroscopy (SBE), and spiral enteroscopy (SE) — the techniques of choice for tissue acquisition and therapy in the midgut (ampulla of Vater to ileocecal valve). DBE (introduced 2001, Yamamoto) is the most studied and most effective for total enteroscopy. Video capsule endoscopy (VCE) remains first-line for small-bowel evaluation in obscure GI bleeding (OGIB); DE is reserved for positive VCE findings, surgically altered anatomy, high suspicion of angiectasias, tissue diagnosis of tumors, or Crohn's disease abnormalities within reach.

Depth of intubation with DBE: 240–360 cm past ligament of Treitz (antegrade) and 102–140 cm past ileocecal valve (retrograde). Antegrade route for proximal two-thirds, retrograde for distal one third (based on capsule transit times). Total enteroscopy = intubation of entire small bowel by one or both routes; DBE total enteroscopy rate 0–86% (highest in Asian populations). Pooled DE adverse-event rate for all indications: minor 9.1%, major 0.72%. Interventions during DE: biopsy, mucosal injection, polypectomy, stricture dilation, hemostasis (APC, electrocoagulation, hemoclips), and foreign-body/retained-capsule retrieval.

## Key Findings / Claims
- DBE, SBE, and SE all have diagnostic and therapeutic capability; DBE most established.
- SBE potential advantages over DBE: shorter setup, single-balloon cycle, simpler control panel, non-latex balloon.
- SE uses rotate-to-advance technology; swift exam, stability, mucosal inspection on insertion and withdrawal.
- NaviAid (through-the-scope balloon on a standard adult colonoscope) is a newer device with limited data — no recommendation yet.
- Short DBE model used for difficult ileocolonoscopy, ERCP in altered anatomy, proximal small-bowel endoscopy.

## Recommendations (verbatim, with GRADE)
1. We recommend DE as an effective and safe technique for small-bowel examination. *(High)*
2. We recommend DBE as the most effective deep enteroscopy technique for achieving total enteroscopy. *(Moderate)*
3. We suggest either DBE or SBE for retrograde enteroscopy. *(Low)*
4. We recommend VCE as the first-line diagnostic tool for small-bowel evaluation in patients with OGIB. DE may be considered when positive findings are identified on VCE. *(Very low)*
5. We suggest that in select circumstances (eg, surgically altered anatomy or high suspicion for small-bowel angiectasias) DE may be considered as the initial small-bowel diagnostic procedure in patients with OGIB. *(Low)*
6. We suggest that DE be used for tissue diagnosis and therapeutic interventions in patients with small-bowel tumors detected by other diagnostic tests (eg, VCE) or in those with high suspicion for tumors despite initial negative testing. *(Low)*
7. We suggest that DE be considered in Crohn's disease patients with abnormalities seen on other imaging studies, if these abnormalities are within reach of the enteroscope. DE allows endoscopic and histologic evaluation and the potential for therapeutic interventions such as hemostasis, stricture dilation, or foreign body retrieval. *(Low)*
