## Summary

Expert consensus that standardizes the steps of **endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty (ESG)**, the procedure that uses an endoscopic suturing device (OverStitch) to place tissue folds along the greater curvature and reduce gastric volume by **~70%**. The authors cite **~18% total weight loss (TWL) at 1 year**, durable for **≥5 years**, plus improvement in hypertension, diabetes and gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD). In **2022 the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) granted de novo authorization** of the OverStitch device for treating obesity, and the number of endoscopists performing ESG has risen since. Technique still varies between operators: the Multicenter Endoscopic Sleeve Gastroplasty Randomized Interventional Trial (MERIT) used a suture pattern that gave only **13.6% TWL**, which most experts consider inadequate. No mandatory curriculum or best-practice standard existed.

**Method (Foundation for Interventional and Therapeutic Endoscopy [FITE]-1 study).** A 7-member working group from the FITE endobariatrics committee drafted statements in Round 0 (June 2023) from a literature review of 32 studies and their own experience. The expert panel had **6 bariatric endoscopists** (5 gastroenterologists, 1 surgeon; half academic, half private practice). Each had performed **≥300 ESG procedures** and published on ESG. Over **3 survey rounds (September 2023–February 2024)**, panelists voted on whether each step was essential, for both average bariatric endoscopists and trainees. Items with **>70% agreement** were included. Items at 50%–70% or <50% were discussed between rounds, and **only items with unanimous final agreement were kept**. Response rates were 100%, 67% and 100% across the 3 rounds.

**Result.** **27 statements**: 6 cognitive (100% consensus throughout) and 21 technical, in three groups: diagnostic endoscopy and presuturing (97% consensus by round 3), fundamental suturing steps (100%) and suture pattern (97%). The points with the highest consensus were **starting at the incisura, using at least 6 sutures, and avoiding the fundus**. Disputed steps that ended as *optional*: argon plasma coagulation (APC) marking, the overtube, and an interrupted reinforcement suture.

**Evidence level.** Expert opinion from a 6-person panel. The statements are not GRADE-rated. The authors plan to build and validate a skill-assessment tool from them. Several authors disclose consulting and research ties to Boston Scientific, which makes the OverStitch.

## Key Findings / Claims

*All 27 statements as the paper numbers them (Table 1), condensed where long. Votes are n/N (%) for round 1 → round 2 → round 3. Final wording is the round-3 version. The statements are ungraded.*

### Cognitive skills
1. Describe primary endoscopic bariatric and metabolic therapies and the available treatment options for patients with obesity. — 6/6 → 4/4 → 6/6 (100%)
2. Understand ESG indications, contraindications and patient-selection criteria. — 100% all rounds
3. Make sure patients are properly consented for possible risks and adverse events. — 100% all rounds
4. Understand pre- and postprocedural management, including antiemetics, pain control, antibiotics and the diet plan. — 100% all rounds
5. Be competent in diagnosing and managing adverse events. — 100% all rounds
6. Communicate well with team members. — 100% all rounds

### Technical skills — diagnostic endoscopy and presuturing steps
1. Perform a diagnostic endoscopy and identify the key landmarks: antrum, incisura, anterior wall, greater curvature, posterior wall, fundus and gastroesophageal junction. — 100% all rounds
2. Evaluate for any contraindications to ESG. — 100% all rounds
3. Measure the gastric length. — 100% all rounds
4. APC of the anterior and posterior walls of the gastric body to define the gastric boundaries — **optional**. — 2/6 (33) → 3/4 (75) → 5/6 (83)
5. Pass the endoscope with the mounted suturing device into the stomach, **with or without an overtube**. — 3/6 (50) → 4/4 (100) → 6/6 (100)

### Technical skills — fundamental suturing steps
6. Advance the helix to capture the gastric wall and apply **3–4 clockwise turns**, with initial forward pressure and **early retraction as needed to avoid capturing tissue outside the stomach**. — 2/6 (33) → 4/4 → 6/6 (100)
7. Pull the helix with the gastric wall, maneuver the endoscope and place a **full-thickness stitch**. — 5/6 (83) → 100 → 100
8. Create suture slack and work from a distance from the gastric wall. — 100% all rounds
9. Load the needle driver without crossing the suture. — 100% all rounds
10. Drop the tissue anchor at the end of the suture pattern. — 100% all rounds
11. Cinch the suture with enough tension to compress the stomach while **avoiding overtensioning**; the suture should **not be visible after cinching**. — 5/6 (83) → 100 → 100
12. Be able to troubleshoot during the procedure. — 100% all rounds
13. Identify and manage intraprocedural adverse events such as bleeding. — 100% all rounds
14. Remove the device **with the handle closed**. — 100% all rounds
15. Examine the final sleeve: the endoscope must be able to **traverse the whole sleeve into the gastric antrum** and identify the post-ESG landmarks (fundus, body, antrum), with **no active bleeding or esophageal tear**. — 5/6 (83) → 100 → 100

### Technical skills — suture pattern
16. Suture 1: **start at the incisura** and place the first stitch on the **anterior wall**. — 5/6 (83) → 3/4 (75) → 6/6 (100)
17. Suture 1: **6–12 full-thickness bites** per suture. — 5/6 (83) → 3/4 (75) → 6/6 (100)
18. Suture 1: a **continuous** pattern, either single-row running or U-pattern. — 100 → 100 → 5/6 (83)
19. Suture 2: an **interrupted reinforcement suture** immediately next to the running or U-suture — **optional**. — 3/6 (50) → 4/4 → 6/6 (100)
20. A total of **6–10 sutures** is recommended. — 100% all rounds
21. Identify the fundus and **avoid suturing it**. — 5/6 (83) → 4/4 → 6/6 (100)

### Discussion points (not statements)
- **Contraindications named as examples** in the cognitive-skills discussion: **active smoking** and **clinically significant portal hypertension**. The paper gives no full list.
- Adverse events to discuss at consent range from common ones (pain, nausea, vomiting) to serious ones (leak, bleeding). Periprocedural care includes strategies to minimize nausea, proton pump inhibitors and postprocedural diet instructions.
- **Why avoid the fundus:** it is thin and vascular, so suturing it carries risk with no benefit.
- **Why the overtube and APC are optional:** experienced endoscopists can pass the suturing device safely without an overtube, and knowing the gastric anatomy reduces the need for APC marking.
- The number of bites depends on the pattern: 6–12, depending on whether suture 1 is running or U-pattern.
- **Left out:** grasping tissue with forceps instead of the helix (too little safety and efficacy data), and torquing the scope to minimize alpha loops in the U-pattern (too advanced to make mandatory).
- No suture pattern has strong evidence of superiority. New patterns should be developed within clinical studies.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **ESG suture extent.** [[aga-2017-endoscopic-bariatric-therapies|AGA 2017]] describes sutures running from the prepyloric antrum to the gastroesophageal junction. The 2025 consensus starts suture 1 at the **incisura** and says to **avoid the fundus**. This newer consensus is what the wiki follows.
- **No outcome data.** The paper sets practice standards and reports no new outcomes. The TWL figures it quotes (~18% at 1 year; 13.6% in MERIT) come from the studies it cites, not from this panel.
- The [[asge-esge-2024-primary-ebmt|ASGE–ESGE 2024 primary endoscopic bariatric and metabolic therapies guideline]] (its reference 1) is the graded guideline on ESG efficacy and indications.
