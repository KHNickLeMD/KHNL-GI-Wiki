## Summary

- **Problem (Scope of Problem and Purpose):** [[glp-1-receptor-agonists|glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs)]] slow gastric emptying (part of the "ileal brake") → residual gastric contents on the day of a procedure despite traditional fasting; pulmonary aspiration reported under procedural sedation and/or general anesthesia. Their common side effects (nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, constipation) can also confuse pre- and postoperative diagnosis.
- **Why this document:** prior society guidance was inconsistent, leaving providers uncertain. Purpose = one unified multisociety guidance for periprocedural care of patients on a GLP-1RA **regardless of indication** (currently type 2 diabetes, overweight and obesity, heart failure).
- **Scope:** surgery **and** [[upper-endoscopy|gastrointestinal endoscopy]] (perioperative/periprocedural period).
- **Evidence basis:** limited data; based on pharmacology and clinical experience. **No evidence grading and no strength-of-recommendation scheme**; two numbered recommendations with lettered sub-statements.
- **Central themes:** shared decision-making across procedural, anesthesia and prescribing teams; **continue** the drug when no elevated risk; risk-mitigate (24-hour liquid diet, point-of-care gastric ultrasound, rapid sequence induction) rather than cancel; if held, use the ASA hold intervals; withholding only because of overweight/obesity = bias.
- **Future:** may need modification for dual and triple agonists and as evidence develops; applies now to current medications containing a GLP-1RA (Conclusion).

## Key Recommendations

Numbering and lettering are the document's own (Recommendations section, page 2084). No evidence grades are given.

### Recommendation 1 — shared decision-making on use and holding

| Label | Statement (condensed; force as printed) |
|---|---|
| **Rec 1** | Perioperative GLP-1RA use **should be based on shared decision-making** of the patient with procedural, anesthesia and prescribing care teams, balancing the **metabolic need** for the GLP-1RA with **individual patient risk**. Achievable by developing multidisciplinary protocols/procedures appropriate for individual practices. |
| **Rec 1a** | Care teams **should consider** the five variables in the table below as **elevating the risk of delayed gastric emptying and aspiration** with periprocedural GLP-1RA use. Assess these risk factors **with enough advance time before surgery** to allow adjustments in preoperative care if indicated — diet modification and evaluating the feasibility of medication bridging if discontinuation is indicated. |
| **Rec 1b** | GLP-1RA therapy **may be continued** preoperatively in patients **without elevated risk** (per Rec 1a). When elevated risk exists, withholding **should be balanced** against the surgical and medical risk of a hazardous metabolic state such as hyperglycemia. Bridging off a GLP-1RA may be resource-intensive, cost- or insurance-prohibitive, and risk other adverse effects such as hypoglycemia. Withholding perioperatively **only** because the patient has overweight or obesity, without a Rec 1a indication, could constitute **overweight and obesity bias, which should be avoided**. |
| **Rec 1c** | If holding is indicated (unacceptable safety profile after shared decision-making), **the duration to hold is unknown**. At this time it is **suggested** to follow the original ASA guidance: hold **the day of surgery** for **daily** formulations and **a week prior to surgery** for **weekly** formulations. **All patients should still be assessed on the day of procedure** for symptoms suggestive of delayed gastric emptying. |

**Rec 1a — variables that elevate risk of delayed gastric emptying and aspiration**

| # | Variable | What the document says |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Escalation phase** | Escalation phase, versus maintenance phase, associated with higher risk of delayed gastric emptying |
| 2 | **Higher dose** | The higher the dose, the more likely gastrointestinal (GI) side effects |
| 3 | **Weekly dosing** | GI side effects more common with weekly than daily formulations |
| 4 | **GI symptoms present** | Symptoms suggestive of delayed gastric emptying and intestinal transit: nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, [[dyspepsia]], constipation |
| 5 | **Other medical conditions that delay gastric emptying** | Evaluate for conditions that may worsen GI symptoms and delay emptying — such as, but not limited to, bowel dysmotility, [[gastroparesis]], Parkinson's disease |

- The document does **not** say how many variables, or which combination, make a patient "elevated risk" — the table is a list to consider, not a score.

### Recommendation 2 — minimize aspiration risk instead of cancelling

| Label | Statement (condensed; force as printed) |
|---|---|
| **Rec 2** | Safe perioperative GLP-1RA use **should include efforts to minimize the aspiration risk** of delayed gastric emptying — by **preoperative diet modification and/or altering the anesthesia plan** to consider rapid sequence induction of general anesthesia for tracheal intubation. |
| **Rec 2a** | **Preoperative liquid diet for at least 24 hours** (as done before [[colonoscopy]] and [[bariatric-surgery\|bariatric surgery]]) **can be utilized** when there is concern for delayed gastric emptying based on clinical symptom review (Rec 1a). |
| **Rec 2b** | When clinical concern for retained gastric contents exists **on the day of the procedure**, **point-of-care gastric ultrasound could be used** to assess aspiration risk. May be limited by institutional resources, interuser variability and credentialing requirements. |
| **Rec 2c** | When concern for retained gastric contents **exists or is confirmed** on the day of the procedure, providers **should** engage patients in shared decision-making and **consider** the benefits and risks of **rapid sequence induction of general anesthesia for tracheal intubation** to minimize aspiration risk **versus procedure cancellation**. |

- **Closing statement (after Rec 2c, unlabelled):** safe continuation of surgery and GI endoscopy, and prevention of procedure cancellation, **can be prioritized** by following these recommendations — as for other patient populations with gastroparesis.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

**Publication order:** ASA consensus guidance 2023 (as cited in this document and in [[aga-2024-glp1-endoscopy]]) → [[aga-2024-glp1-endoscopy|AGA 2024 rapid CPU]] → this multisociety guidance (© 2024, accepted August 2024; print issue November 2025) → [[asge-2025-glp1-sglt2-periendoscopic|ASGE 2025 position statement]]. All are tier-1 US documents without formal evidence grading.

- **Hold vs continue (default).** This guidance: **continue** when no Rec 1a risk features (Rec 1b); hold only after shared decision-making for an unacceptable safety profile. Concordant with [[aga-2024-glp1-endoscopy|AGA 2024]] (individualized, no routine cessation). **Conflicts with [[asge-2025-glp1-sglt2-periendoscopic|ASGE 2025]]** (as stated on [[endoscopy-sedation]]), which suggests holding before elective endoscopy (daily ≥24 h, weekly ≥7 days; Statement 9) and is the newer document the sedation page currently follows for the elective question.
- **ASA's own position changed in effect.** The 2023 ASA consensus (as quoted in [[aga-2024-glp1-endoscopy|AGA 2024]]) suggested considering holding irrespective of indication, dose or type of procedure; here ASA participated and its Administrative Council affirmed a document that continues the drug in low-risk patients and keeps the ASA intervals only for patients in whom holding is chosen (Rec 1c).
- **Hold interval when holding.** This guidance: daily → **day of surgery**; weekly → **a week prior** (Rec 1c, ASA intervals). [[asge-2025-glp1-sglt2-periendoscopic|ASGE 2025]]: daily **≥24 h**, weekly **≥7 days**. Daily-dosed wording differs (day of vs ≥24 h). This guidance states the needed duration is **unknown**.
- **Liquid diet.** This guidance: liquid diet **≥24 hours** only "when there is concern for delayed gastric emptying" on symptom review (Rec 2a). ASGE 2025: liquid diet **24 h before for all** patients on a GLP-1RA. AGA 2024: a liquid diet **the day before** sedated procedures "may be a more acceptable strategy" in lieu of stopping (no symptom condition).
- **Who is high risk.** This guidance names five variables (escalation phase, higher dose, weekly dosing, GI symptoms, other conditions delaying emptying). AGA 2024 asks teams to note dose and frequency, comorbidities, medications and, most importantly, GI symptoms, without calling dose or weekly dosing risk features; ASGE 2025 stratifies by symptoms and urgency and names neither escalation phase, dose nor weekly formulation. None of the documents defines how many features make a patient high risk.
- **Day-of-procedure management.** Concordant across all three on gastric ultrasound being resource-limited/evidence-limited and on rapid sequence induction for suspected retained contents; this guidance uniquely frames the choice explicitly as rapid sequence induction **versus cancellation** within shared decision-making, and adds the obesity-bias caution.
- **Bridging.** This guidance warns bridging may be resource-intensive, costly, and risk hypoglycemia (Rec 1b); ASGE 2025 Statement 10 (per [[endoscopy-sedation]]) advises considering bridging when stopping for a week. Different emphasis, not a direct conflict.
- Scope differs: this guidance covers surgery and endoscopy and cites the ASA intervals as "day of surgery"/"a week prior to surgery"; the endoscopy documents frame them per procedure.
