## Summary
The five major liver societies (each nominating two chairs, five members and one to two young investigators) wrote one harmonised definition of [[acute-on-chronic-liver-failure|acute-on-chronic liver failure (ACLF)]] to replace the competing regional definitions (EASL-CLIF, NACSELD, APASL) for practice, trials and regulators. Working groups covered definition/grading, aetiology (alcohol, drug- and herb-induced liver injury, viral hepatitis), survival in critical care, [[liver-transplantation|liver transplantation (LT)]] and trial endpoints. Statements were graded by the Oxford Centre for Evidence-Based Medicine (evidence **A** strong / **B** moderate / **C** weak; recommendation **1** strong / **2** weak) and adopted only with **>70% agreement**. After an open meeting before EASL 2026 (Barcelona, 27 May 2026) with US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) input, 12 statements were revised and passed a second Delphi round.

The core change: **liver failure (international normalised ratio [INR] ≥1.5 and bilirubin ≥5 mg/dL) is the obligatory requisite** in a patient with **advanced chronic liver disease (ACLD, ≥F3 fibrosis)**, and grade is set by the number of **extrahepatic organ dysfunctions or failures** — a new "dysfunction" tier allows earlier recognition. Type A (no prior decompensation) and type B (prior decompensated cirrhosis) are both included. Organ failure definitions are re-based on KDIGO acute kidney injury (AKI) staging, Berlin acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) oxygenation thresholds and vasopressor need (excluding vasopressors given only for hepatorenal syndrome).

Management statements cover corticosteroid limits in alcohol-related hepatitis with ACLF, antiviral timing, ICU organ support (sedation, ventilation, fluids, vasopressors, renal replacement, infection screen, viscoelastic testing), time-limited ICU trials with day 3–7 reassessment, and LT candidacy (expected 1-year survival >75–80%). The last section defines regulatory-aligned trial endpoints.

*Statement numbers below follow Appendix 1. The main text numbers statements 58–60 in a different order (resource use / MDRO / 75–80% survival); the content is identical.*

## Key Findings / Claims

### Definition (Statements 1–8)
1. Definition and diagnosis should be evidence-based but simple and pragmatic for bedside use worldwide (C1).
2. Include patients with **ACLD (≥F3 fibrosis)**, to avoid overlap with [[acute-liver-failure|acute liver failure (ALF)]] (C1).
3. **Liver failure = INR ≥1.5 and bilirubin ≥5 mg/dL** — chosen for wider inclusion and closer short-term mortality prediction (B1).
4. Liver failure is the **primary requisite** for ACLF and the core of the syndrome (B1).
5. Liver failure is the index presentation either in compensated ACLD/cirrhosis with no previous or current decompensation (**type A**) or in prior decompensated cirrhosis (**type B**) (A1).
6. Without prior decompensation, **type A** may be diagnosed when liver failure comes with **new-onset decompensation ([[ascites]] and/or [[hepatic-encephalopathy|hepatic encephalopathy (HE)]])**, present at presentation or developing **within 2–4 weeks** (A1).
7. Further research needed to compare outcomes with vs without current/prior decompensation (B1).
8. Decompensation and organ failures can be triggered by hepatic or extrahepatic precipitants (A1).
- Precipitants (text): bacterial infection most frequent; ongoing alcohol, hepatitis B virus (HBV) reactivation/superinfection, drug-induced liver injury, GI bleeding, major surgery/invasive procedures; no trigger found in a substantial proportion.

**Table 1 — establishing ACLD in a patient without known cirrhosis:**

| Category | Type | Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| Invasive | Biopsy | F3 or F4 fibrosis (METAVIR) |
| Non-invasive | Instrumental | Vibration-controlled transient elastography (VCTE) / magnetic resonance elastography (MRE) / shear wave elastography (SWE) at validated ≥F3 thresholds* |
| Non-invasive | Imaging | Portal hypertension on ultrasound, CT or MRI (varices, collaterals/shunts, splenomegaly) |
| Non-invasive | Clinical | Hepatosplenomegaly, spider angiomata, clinically detectable ascites |

*\*Interpret with caution in suspected ACLF — acute liver injury can raise stiffness into the ACLD range; never use non-invasive tests alone to establish ACLD or to separate ACLF from ALF.* A previously established diagnosis of cirrhosis/ACLD also counts; endoscopic portal hypertension supports the clinical route. Integrate all modalities rather than rely on one.

### Diagnostic criteria and organ failures (Statements 9–16)
9. Diagnosis = **liver failure plus ≥1 extrahepatic organ dysfunction/failure** (depending on grade) (B1).
10. **Grades** (B1):
    - **Grade 1:** liver failure + **one extrahepatic organ dysfunction** (circulatory/respiratory dysfunction only counts in a monitored high-dependency/ICU setting)
    - **Grade 2:** liver failure + **any one extrahepatic organ failure**
    - **Grade 3:** liver failure + **≥2 extrahepatic organ failures**
11. **HE grade 3–4** = an additional organ failure (A1).
12. **AKI stage 2–3 (KDIGO)** = renal failure (A1).
13. **Respiratory failure** = mechanical ventilation for respiratory reasons, or PaO₂/FiO₂ ≤225, or SpO₂/FiO₂ ≤244 if PaO₂/FiO₂ missing (B1).
14. **Circulatory failure** = need for vasopressors for circulatory failure — **not vasopressors given only to treat hepatorenal syndrome–AKI (HRS-AKI)** (B1).
15. Mean arterial pressure needs continuous assessment; monitor respiratory and circulatory criteria in high-dependency/ICU care (C1).
16. Other organ failures (immune dysregulation, gut failure) need further definition (A2).
- Grading framework predicted short-term mortality independently in several prospective international cohorts (Table 3 of the paper).

**Table 2 — organ dysfunction and failure:**

| Organ | No dysfunction | Dysfunction | Failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Liver | — | — | Bilirubin ≥5 mg/dL **and** INR ≥1.5 |
| Kidney | No AKI | AKI stage 1 (KDIGO) | AKI stage 2–3 (KDIGO) or new renal replacement therapy (RRT) |
| Encephalopathy | West Haven 0–1 | West Haven 2 | West Haven 3–4, or mechanical ventilation for HE |
| Circulatory | MAP ≥65 mmHg | MAP <65 mmHg* | Vasopressor use (not for HRS-AKI alone) |
| Respiratory | PaO₂/FiO₂ >300; SpO₂/FiO₂ >333 if PaO₂/FiO₂ missing; or no supplemental O₂ | 225 < PaO₂/FiO₂ ≤300; 244 < SpO₂/FiO₂ ≤333* | Mechanical ventilation (for respiratory failure), PaO₂/FiO₂ ≤225, or SpO₂/FiO₂ ≤244 |

*\*Monitored only in high-dependency or intensive care settings.* Table 2 has no separate coagulation row; INR is part of the liver-failure criterion.

### Alcohol (Statements 17–22)
- Alcohol-related liver disease = leading cause of ACLF worldwide; [[alcohol-associated-hepatitis|severe alcohol-related hepatitis (SAH)]] coexists in up to half of cases.
- SAH without ACLF or with 1–2 organ failures: individualise corticosteroids; early LT = only intervention with substantial survival benefit for medical non-responders and advanced ACLF.
- Steroids raise invasive fungal infection risk → stop promptly in non-responders (typically after 4–7 d), no taper.
17. Biopsy not required when diagnosis is clear and alcohol-related hepatitis is not suspected. Suspected alcohol-related hepatitis with steroids being considered → **biopsy recommended**; if transjugular biopsy unavailable or precluded locally, **NIAAA criteria** may diagnose probable alcohol-related hepatitis. **ACLF with ≥3 organ failures** (steroids not indicated) → biopsy not recommended (B1).
18. **ACLF grade 3 → do not prescribe corticosteroids**; Lille-predicted response very low (B1).
19. **ACLF with two organ failures** → steroid response probability **~40%**; decide case by case (B1).
20. On steroids → **Lille score at days 4 and 7**, to limit exposure and infection risk in non-responders (A1).
21. **No minimum abstinence period** before waitlisting, given very high short-term mortality (B1).
22. Return-to-alcohol risk assessment: social/familial support, prior decompensation, prior abstinence failure; select per national/local multidisciplinary early-LT guidelines for alcohol-related hepatitis non-responders (B1).

### Drug- and herb-induced liver injury (Statements 23–30)
- [[drug-induced-liver-injury|Drug-induced (DILI) and herb-induced liver injury (HILI)]] cause up to 10% of ACLF in some series. Asia-Pacific: antituberculosis drugs, herbal/complementary medicines; West: antibiotics, antineoplastics/immunomodulators, antiepileptics, cardiovascular drugs, immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs).
- Causality tools (CIOMS/RUCAM/RECAM) support but do not replace multidisciplinary judgement.
23. Structured diagnostic approach — exposure history, injury pattern, exclusion of competing aetiologies, causality tools, expert judgement (C1).
24. Distinguish drug hepatotoxicity from spontaneous progression by temporal relationship, injury pattern, trajectory after withdrawal, exclusion of competing precipitants (C1).
25. Biopsy not routine; consider if immune-mediated DILI/HILI, ICI hepatotoxicity, rejection or infiltrative disease suspected; **transjugular route** with coagulopathy, thrombocytopenia or ascites (C2).
26. Trials enrolling ACLD/cirrhosis/ACLF: pre-specified multidisciplinary adjudication of drug hepatotoxicity (C1).
27. Risk-stratify by injury pattern, exposure duration, timing of withdrawal, host factors (C2).
    - Hepatocellular → rapid deterioration; cholestatic/mixed → protracted jaundice. Herbal/dietary supplements, complementary medicines and antituberculous drugs → more severe, higher mortality. **Total bilirubin and serum lactate** = independent prognostic factors.
28. **Immediate discontinuation of the offending agent is mandatory**; withdrawal alone may not reverse established ACLF (C1). Essential life-saving drug → case-by-case with the specialist team.
29. Use aetiology-specific therapies/antidotes where available; consider N-acetylcysteine, corticosteroids (selected immune-mediated cases) and drug-clearance strategies by suspected mechanism (A2). Table 4 recreated on [[drug-induced-liver-injury]].
30. **Early transplant-centre referral**, especially progressive disease or high-risk features (A1); grade 2–3 not improving after withdrawal → urgent LT evaluation.

### Viral hepatitis (Statements 31–38)
- Hepatotropic: HBV, hepatitis C virus (HCV), hepatitis D virus (HDV) via primary infection or reactivation; acute hepatitis A (HAV) or E (HEV) on chronic liver disease. Non-hepatotropic: cytomegalovirus (CMV), Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) mostly by reactivation; herpes simplex virus (HSV), SARS-CoV-2, dengue (DENV), yellow fever (YFV) as superimposed primary infections.
31. **Immediate** serological and molecular testing for HAV, HBV, HCV, HDV, HEV (B1).
32. Non-hepatotropic viruses (CMV, EBV, HSV, SARS-CoV-2, DENV, YFV; enterovirus/adenovirus as indicated) tested **sequentially** by clinical context and regional prevalence (B1).
33. **Antiviral therapy as soon as possible, preferably within 24 h of aetiological confirmation**, in all virus-induced ACLF (B1). Per virus (text):
    - HBV: entecavir, tenofovir disoproxil fumarate or tenofovir alafenamide first-line; HBV/HDV → consider HDV therapy incl. bulevirtide where approved (not approved for decompensated cirrhosis).
    - HCV: pangenotypic direct-acting antivirals (DAAs); **avoid protease-inhibitor regimens** in decompensated cirrhosis/ACLF.
    - HEV: ribavirin may be considered (esp. immunocompromised), duration by virological response.
    - HAV/DENV/YFV: supportive only.
    - CMV: ganciclovir first-line; HSV: aciclovir; EBV: limited evidence. Cytopenias → screen for haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (ferritin, triglycerides).
    - SARS-CoV-2: per current guidance; nirmatrelvir/ritonavir generally avoided in decompensated cirrhosis.
34. Early virological monitoring may be considered after starting antivirals (B2).
35. Relevance of early viral-load decline varies by virus; **don't modify treatment on early kinetics alone** (C2). HBV DNA before LT guides prophylaxis; HCV — no on-treatment RNA, assess SVR12; HEV — serial RNA guides ribavirin duration.
36. Tailor prevention/treatment of complications to viral aetiology and reactivation pattern (C2).
37. Start antivirals early before/during the peri-transplant period — less replication, more stability, less recurrence (B1).
38. **Do not delay LT solely to achieve virological suppression** (B1). HBV: nucleos(t)ide analogue long term; post-LT prophylaxis, hepatitis B immunoglobulin for selected patients.

### Survival in critical care (Statements 39–53)
**Prognostic scores**
39. Scores reflecting hepatic **and** extrahepatic organ failure recommended over conventional cirrhosis scores (Child-Turcotte-Pugh, MELD, MELD-Na) in critically ill ACLF (B1).
40. **Serial** calculation discriminates prognosis better than baseline alone (B1).
- CLIF-C ACLF: most extensively validated (organ failures + age + inflammation; 28-/90-day mortality). NACSELD: advanced extrahepatic failure only — may underestimate mortality, may suit futility. AARC: liver-failure-based, uses lactate. AARC and COSSH perform well in Asia; **COSSH is HBV-ACLF-specific**. SOFA 2.0 and A-TANGO need external validation.

**Neurological**
41. **West Haven 3–4 HE or Glasgow coma scale (GCS) <12** = severe impairment and aspiration risk → ICU where possible; **GCS <9 → consider intubation** (C1).
42. **Short-acting sedation/analgesia (propofol, dexmedetomidine)** in intubated cirrhosis/ACLF (B1); remifentanil also preferred; avoid benzodiazepines; titrate to minimum, interrupt regularly.
- Use "acute encephalopathy", not "brain failure"; not synonymous with HE. Alternative/additional causes in up to one-quarter (sepsis, stroke, intracranial haemorrhage, metabolic, nutritional, drugs/alcohol).
- Workup: routine labs, **blood ammonia**, toxicology as indicated, brain imaging; lumbar puncture/EEG selected cases. **Normal ammonia makes HE unlikely → look for other causes.**
- Treat precipitants; [[lactulose]] first-line (rectal if impaired gut motility); polyethylene glycol alternative in selected critically ill. Routine [[rifaximin]] added to broad-spectrum antibiotics not supported for overt HE in critically ill ACLF. IV L-ornithine L-aspartate + lactulose + rifaximin improved HE grade, recovery time and 28-day mortality in grade 3–4 HE vs lactulose + rifaximin (not widely available).

**Respiratory**
43. Mechanical ventilation for acute lung injury: lung-protective — **tidal volume 4–6 mL/kg predicted body weight, plateau <30 cm H₂O**, optimal positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP), plus spontaneous breathing (C1).
44. Acute respiratory failure: **high-flow nasal cannula (HFNC)** may be considered with close monitoring, only in alert patients who protect their airway (C1).
- ARDS: 6 mL/kg low tidal volume is standard; lower PEEP without severe ARDS, higher PEEP in moderate–severe ARDS. HFNC must not delay intubation — in ARDS a brief trial, reassess every 1–2 h; ROX index to detect failure. Treat [[hepatic-hydrothorax]], tense ascites ([[paracentesis]]/thoracentesis); [[hepatopulmonary-syndrome-portopulmonary-hypertension|hepatopulmonary syndrome]] → oxygen pending LT.

**Circulatory**
45. Early baseline assessment of volume, perfusion and cardiac function **including bedside echocardiography** in all critically ill cirrhosis/ACLF (C1). Consider cirrhotic cardiomyopathy (pulmonary oedema risk with fluids).
46. Shock: **balanced crystalloids**, with human [[albumin]] for **established indications** (large-volume paracentesis, [[spontaneous-bacterial-peritonitis|spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP)]], HRS-AKI) (B1). Albumin not supported for routine initial resuscitation (pulmonary oedema/fluid overload, esp. 20%).
47. Hypotensive despite adequate volume → **norepinephrine first-line, target MAP ≥65 mmHg** (B1). Higher targets not beneficial in randomised data.
48. Escalating norepinephrine → **vasopressin second-line + empiric trial of IV hydrocortisone** (B1). Extracorporeal cytokine removal not recommended.

**Renal**
49. **RRT for HRS-AKI failing pharmacotherapy in patients listed for, or being considered for, LT** (B1).
- HRS-AKI and acute tubular necrosis = a spectrum; no routine pre-emptive RRT; continuous RRT in haemodynamic instability; no routine intraoperative RRT or RRT for hyperammonaemia alone without AKI.

**Infections**
50. Hospitalised cirrhosis complications, especially ACLF → **full septic workup: paracentesis, blood, urine and sputum cultures**; early appropriate antibiotics may improve survival (B1).
- Reassess infection with any deterioration; empiric choice by severity, source, healthcare exposure, local resistance; multidrug-resistant organism (MDRO) colonisation screening (rectal/nasal swabs) where available; procalcitonin limited; prolonged organ failure/ICU → consider empirical antifungals; on steroids → consider CMV/HSV reactivation.

**Coagulation**
51. **Viscoelastic tests** (thromboelastography, rotational thromboelastometry) may reflect haemostasis better than INR, activated partial thromboplastin time, fibrinogen and platelets; need validation before routine use (B1). VET-guided venous thromboembolism prophylaxis not recommended.

**ICU futility**
52. Non-LT candidates with severe multiorgan failure: ICU case by case, by resources, ideally a **time-limited trial** with dynamic prognostication and palliative care (C2).
53. **Reassess at day 3–7** after ICU admission to decide on continuing aggressive support vs palliative care (C2).
- Do not deny ICU on initial presentation alone. **AARC >10, persistent multiorgan failure, or CLIF-C ACLF >64 despite maximal therapy** = extremely poor prognosis → structured transition to [[palliative-care-in-cirrhosis|palliative care]].

### Liver transplantation (Statements 54–63)
54. Severe ACLF → **expedited multidisciplinary assessment and rapid LT** (B1). Higher grades: waitlist mortality 50–70%+ at 28 d; selected patients transplanted early → 1-year survival >80%. Ideal goal: transplant severe ACLF **within one week**.
55. In severe ACLF, death risk may exceed MELD prediction (C2). UNOS: 90-day waitlist mortality 21.6% overall, 39% in ACLF grade 3b; CLIF-C, SOFA, CATCH-LIFE-MELD and AARC outperform MELD.
56. Objective ACLF prioritisation policies needed as alternative/addition to MELD allocation (C2).
57. Post-LT 1-year mortality risk ↑ with **respiratory failure, moderate–high-dose vasopressors, no improvement in organ failures, diabetes, frailty** (C1). Also: elevated arterial lactate; stabilisation before LT improves survival. LT unlikely to benefit with persistent deterioration despite maximal support — especially ≥3 organ failures, severe respiratory failure, high-dose vasopressors, uncontrolled infection.
58. **Offer LT only with expected 1-year survival >75–80%** (B1). Acceptable outcomes: 1-year >75–80% and 5-year >50%; selected EASL-CLIF grade 3 achieve ~75–84%. TAM and SALT-M scores help identify poor expected outcome — most informative at organ offer, not ICU admission; decide dynamically, not on fixed thresholds.
59. Higher resource use before/after LT is still justified if post-LT survival stays acceptable (B1); not an absolute contraindication.
60. **MDRO or fungal infection before LT** → more post-LT complications (B1).
61. In experienced centres **living donor LT (LDLT)** may match deceased donor LT and widen access to expedited LT (C2). Recognise "temporary unsuitability" — evaluate/list in parallel with optimisation.
62. **Older adults (>60 y)**: higher waitlist and post-LT mortality, but carefully selected candidates achieve acceptable 1-year survival (B1); judge physiology, frailty, comorbidity, trajectory — not age alone.
63. Awaiting LT: **extracorporeal therapies incl. therapeutic plasma exchange (TPE)** may bridge, stabilising organs and improving short-term survival vs standard care (B2). Evidence: 2026 RCT (n=194) — better 28-day, similar 90-day survival; APACHE trial (n=374, grade 1b–3) — better 28- and 90-day survival; 2025 meta-analysis. Timing, intensity, replacement fluid and selection undefined.

### Clinical trial endpoints (Statements 64–81)
*Research methodology, informed by FDA discussion — not bedside management.*
64. Phase I/IIa: endpoints = safety/tolerability, target engagement, organ function; survival and LT as secondary/exploratory (A1).
65. LT listing **not** a primary endpoint (access, delays, donor availability) (A1).
66. Survival with or without LT only as endpoints from phase IIb/III onward, with competing-risk analysis (B1).
67. Phase IIb/III survival with or without LT at day 30 to month 6, competing-risk analysis, by design and intervention duration (B2).
68. Organ-failure score change confirmed by ≥2 observations (within one day up to 3–7 days apart) (B1).
69. Blinded investigator or adjudication committee may adjudicate organ-failure changes (C2).
70. Assess status at several timepoints with a multicategory ordinal scale (higher = worse) (B1).
71. Validated biomarkers (metabolites, inflammatory markers) could support stratification, enrichment and response monitoring (B1).
72. Metabolites and inflammatory biomarkers predicted ACLF development, infection and mortality in multicentre studies (B1). None are surrogate endpoints yet.
73. Patient-reported outcome measures need instruments fit for the context of use (C1).
74. In-hospital functional recovery (off respiratory support, oral intake, mobility, cognition, anxiety, sleep) = important outcome domain; patient-, observer- or clinician-reported (C1).
75. Patient-reported experience measures (patients and caregivers/surrogates) = important secondary outcomes, validated scales (C2).
76. Consider stratifying by socioeconomic status (C2).
77. Desirability of outcome ranking (DOOR) could be considered in phase IIa/b, III, IV (C2).
78. Outcome choice should fit the clinical question, objective, stage and severity — no single universal endpoint (C2).
79. Prespecify handling of death, LT, rescue therapy, discontinuation and organ-failure worsening/resolution/recurrence (C2).
80. Analytic strategy follows the outcome definition (competing-risk, win-ratio, repeated events, multistate) (C2).
81. No single analytic framework is universally preferable (C2).
- Mortality, with LT as a competing event, remains the preferred primary regulatory endpoint.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **Liver-failure threshold** — bilirubin **≥5 mg/dL + INR ≥1.5** (this consensus) vs CLIF-SOFA bilirubin ≥12 mg/dL ([[moreau-2013-canonic-aclf|CANONIC]]) and the unquantified "elevated bilirubin and INR" of [[aasld-2024-aclf|AASLD 2024]] GS 1. Liver failure is now obligatory; EASL-CLIF allowed ACLF without liver failure. This consensus wins (newer tier 1).
- **Kidney / lung / circulation cut-offs** — KDIGO stage 2–3 vs CLIF creatinine ≥2.0 mg/dL; PaO₂/FiO₂ ≤225 vs ≤200; vasopressors given only for HRS-AKI no longer count (CLIF counted terlipressin).
- **Ammonia** — AASLD 2024 GS 11: routine ammonia not recommended; this consensus (text): include blood ammonia in the workup; normal ammonia makes HE unlikely.
- **GCS cut-offs** — AASLD 2024 GS 4: GCS <8 = severe; this consensus: GCS <12 = severe/ICU, GCS <9 → consider intubation.
- **Tidal volume without ARDS** — AASLD 2024 GS 20: 6–10 mL/kg; this consensus Statement 43: 4–6 mL/kg for acute lung injury.
- **Futility** — AASLD 2024: CLIF-C ACLF >70; this consensus: CLIF-C ACLF >64 or AARC >10 despite maximal therapy at day 3–7.
- **Brain imaging** — ACG 2026 HE Rec 6: routine imaging not required without focal deficits; this consensus (ungraded text) lists brain imaging in the encephalopathy workup. Shown as unresolved on [[hepatic-encephalopathy]].
- **Alcohol-related hepatitis biopsy** — AASLD 2020 / ACG 2024 biopsy only *possible* alcohol-related hepatitis and treat probable cases on clinical criteria; this consensus recommends biopsy whenever it is suspected and steroids are considered (Statement 17).
- **Lille timing** — ACG 2024: day 4 *or* 7; this consensus: days 4 *and* 7 (Statement 20, A1).
- **Living donor LT** — AASLD/AST 2025 statement 53 urges caution with LDLT at MELD >30; this consensus (Statement 61, C2) sets no MELD limit in ACLF. Tension rather than direct conflict.
- **Steroids in DILI** — Table 4 lists corticosteroids for immune-mediated and ICI injury; ACG 2021 Rec 9 finds no studies supporting or refuting them outside autoimmune-hepatitis-like features.
- Grade 1 footnote in the paper's Table 3 says "cardiac and respiratory" where Statement 10 and Table 2 say circulatory and respiratory — read as circulatory.
- Main-text vs Appendix numbering of Statements 58–60 differs (content identical).
