# AGA Clinical Practice Guidelines on Intragastric Balloons in the Management of Obesity (2021)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Authors:** Muniraj T, Day LW, Teigen LM, Ho EY, Sultan S, Davitkov P, Shah R, Murad MH
- **Year:** 2021 (April)
- **Journal/Publisher:** Gastroenterology 2021;160:1799–1808. https://doi.org/10.1053/j.gastro.2021.03.003
- **Type:** GRADE-based clinical practice guideline (AGA Institute), accompanied by a separate Technical Review (Shah et al, Gastroenterology 2021;160:1811–1830)

---

## Summary

This AGA guideline addresses the use of [[intragastric-balloon|intragastric balloons]] (IGBs) for weight loss in adults with [[obesity]]. Obesity affects roughly 40% of US adults, yet only ~1.1% of eligible patients receive primary bariatric surgery and <5% of patients seeking weight loss are aware of endoscopic options. IGBs (on the US market since 1982, withdrawn in the 1980s–90s after devastating adverse events, then re-introduced with newer models) are endoscopically placed, restrictive devices that augment lifestyle modification over a 6–12 month implantation period.

Using GRADE methodology, the panel issued **7 recommendations** (2 strong, 5 conditional). The central finding is that IGB therapy combined with lifestyle modification produces greater short-term weight loss than lifestyle modification alone across multiple measures (%TBWL, percent excess weight loss, achievement of 5% and 10% TBWL), with the greatest effect at 6–8 months and incrementally smaller benefit thereafter. IGBs also improve metabolic parameters (HbA1c, fasting glucose, LDL trend, transaminases) and increase remission of diabetes, hypertension, and dyslipidemia versus standard of care.

Serious adverse events are uncommon but higher than SOC (5.6% vs 1.1%; RR 3.07): perforation 0.3%, esophageal mucosal injury 0.8%, gastric ulcer/bleeding 0.76%, gastric outlet/bowel obstruction 0.12%; no deaths in the 7 pooled RCTs, though post-marketing surveillance reports rare hyperinflation, acute pancreatitis, and death. Early removal for intolerance occurred in 9.4%. Fluid-filled balloons achieve ~3% more weight loss than gas-filled but with higher adverse-event rates and lower tolerability; the panel makes no recommendation on a specific device, deferring to shared decision-making.

The guideline also covers concomitant lifestyle intensity, PPI prophylaxis, peri-procedural antiemetics, nutritional screening and multivitamin supplementation, and post-removal maintenance strategies.

## Recommendations (verbatim, with strength and certainty)

**Recommendation 1.** In individuals with obesity seeking a weight-loss intervention who have failed a trial of conventional weight-loss strategies, AGA suggests the use of IGB therapy with lifestyle modification over lifestyle modification alone. *(Conditional recommendation, moderate certainty)*
- *Implementation remark:* Trials in the United States were limited to a body mass index (BMI) range between 30 and 40 kg/m². Individuals with BMI values outside this range were sometimes included in international trials.
- *Implementation remark:* Fluid-filled balloons may be associated with more weight loss, lower tolerability, and less favorable safety profile than gas-filled balloons. Shared decision-making is suggested for determining device choice.

**Recommendation 2.** In individuals with obesity undergoing IGB therapy, AGA recommends moderate- to high-intensity concomitant lifestyle modification interventions to maintain and augment weight loss. *(Strong recommendation, moderate certainty)*

**Recommendation 3.** In individuals undergoing IGB therapy, AGA recommends prophylaxis with PPIs. *(Strong recommendation, moderate certainty)*

**Recommendation 4.** In individuals undergoing IGB therapy, AGA suggests using the intraoperative anesthetic regimens associated with the lowest incidence of nausea along with perioperative antiemetics. AGA suggests a scheduled antiemetic regimen for 2 weeks after IGB placement. *(Conditional recommendation, low certainty)*
- *Implementation remark:* Evidence is insufficient to recommend a specific antiemetic regimen. The choice of regimen is based on institutional policy, clinical context, and availability.

**Recommendation 5.** In individuals undergoing IGB therapy, AGA suggests against perioperative laboratory screening for nutritional deficiencies. *(Conditional recommendation, low certainty)*

**Recommendation 6.** AGA suggests daily supplementation with 1–2 adult dose multivitamins after IGB placement. *(Conditional recommendation, very low certainty)*

**Recommendation 7.** After IGB removal, AGA suggests subsequent weight-loss or maintenance interventions that include dietary interventions, pharmacotherapy, repeat IGB, or bariatric surgery. The choice of weight-loss or maintenance method after IGB is determined based on patient's context and comorbidities following a shared decision-making approach. *(Conditional recommendation, low certainty)*

## Key Findings / Claims

- **Weight loss vs SOC (pooled RCTs):** ~15.46 lbs at 6 mo (7 RCTs; 95% CI 10.42–20.51), ~13.12 lbs at 9 mo (3 RCTs; 10.53–15.70), ~9.76 lbs at 12 mo (2 RCTs; 6.38–13.14) — benefit declines with time.
- **%TBWL:** greatest at 6–8 mo (MD 6.89%; 95% CI 4.09–9.70). **Percent excess weight loss:** greatest at 9 mo (MD 20.43%; 16.09–24.77).
- **Responder rates (6–8 mo):** ≥5% TBWL in 85.1% (IGB) vs 34.6% (SOC), RR 2.44; ≥10% TBWL in 61.9% vs 13.7%, RR 4.31.
- **Metabolic:** lowers HbA1c and fasting glucose (esp. glucose >100, HbA1c >6.5%, BMI >40); ALT −9 U/L, AST −3 U/L; waist circumference −4.1 cm; greater remission of diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidemia; LDL downward trend; no triglyceride benefit.
- **Safety:** serious AE 5.6% (IGB) vs 1.1% (SOC), RR 3.07 (95% CI 1.16–8.11); perforation 0.3%, esophageal mucosal injury 0.8%, gastric ulcer/bleeding 0.76%, gastric outlet/bowel obstruction 0.12%. No deaths in 7 pooled RCTs; post-marketing: rare hyperinflation, acute pancreatitis, death. Early removal 9.4% (intolerance: fullness, epigastric pain, reflux, nausea, emesis).
- **Device type:** meta-analysis of 22 RCTs — fluid-filled IGBs ~3% more weight loss than gas-filled but numerically higher AE rates; gas-filled better tolerated/safer. No device-specific recommendation made.
- **Lifestyle (Rec 2):** high-intensity very-low-calorie ketogenic diet > low-calorie diet after IGB (MD 7.1 kg; 12% greater excess weight loss); ongoing diet sustains loss after removal.
- **PPI (Rec 3):** no direct RCT; indirect evidence — PPIs reduce rebleeding risk and IGB+PPI arms had fewer serious upper GI bleeding events. Use lowest dose/frequency/duration given PPI risks (C. difficile, pneumonia, fracture, kidney disease, micronutrient deficiency).
- **Post-removal (Rec 7):** sibutramine and liraglutide 3 mg trials showed maintained/added loss; sequential IGB gave greater BMI reduction (MD 5.49 kg/m²) but more complications; bariatric surgery after IGB and IGB-before-surgery both supported by limited data.
- **BMI eligibility note:** US trials limited to BMI 30–40 kg/m².

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- No long-term (>1 yr) efficacy data vs SOC; weight-loss benefit declines 6–12 mo.
- No direct RCT for PPI prophylaxis, antiemetic regimen, nutritional screening, or multivitamin dosing — several recommendations rest on indirect/very-low-certainty evidence.
- No device-specific recommendation despite fluid- vs gas-filled trade-offs.
