# AGA Clinical Practice Guidelines on Intragastric Balloons in the Management of Obesity (2021)

## Summary

This AGA guideline addresses the use of [[intragastric-balloon|intragastric balloons]] (IGBs) for weight loss in adults with [[obesity]]. Obesity affects roughly 40% of US adults, yet only ~1.1% of eligible patients receive primary bariatric surgery and <5% of patients seeking weight loss are aware of endoscopic options. IGBs (on the US market since 1982, withdrawn in the 1980s–90s after devastating adverse events, then re-introduced with newer models) are endoscopically placed, restrictive devices used to augment lifestyle modification; pooled outcomes are reported at 6, 9, and 12 months after placement, with safety pooled over a 6- to 8-month period of balloon dwell.

**Scope.** Endoscopically placed balloons only — non-endoscopic (swallowable) balloons were not assessed, as those devices are not available in the United States. Target audience: health care providers and patients. Where a recommendation names no comparator, the comparison is against a potentially equivalent strategy or doing nothing.

Using GRADE methodology, the panel issued **7 recommendations** (2 strong, 5 conditional). The central finding is that IGB therapy combined with lifestyle modification produces greater short-term weight loss than lifestyle modification alone across multiple measures (percent total body weight loss [%TBWL], percent excess weight loss, achievement of 5% and 10% TBWL), with the greatest effect at 6–8 months and incrementally smaller benefit thereafter. IGBs also improve metabolic parameters (hemoglobin A1c, fasting glucose, low-density lipoprotein [LDL] trend, transaminases) and increase remission of diabetes, hypertension, and dyslipidemia versus standard of care (SOC).

Serious adverse events are uncommon but higher than SOC (5.6% vs 1.1%; relative risk [RR] 3.07): perforation 0.3%, esophageal mucosal injury 0.8%, gastric ulcer/bleeding 0.76%, gastric outlet/bowel obstruction 0.12%; no deaths in the 7 pooled randomized controlled trials (RCTs), though post-marketing surveillance reports rare hyperinflation, acute pancreatitis, and death. Early removal for intolerance occurred in 9.4%. Fluid-filled balloons achieve ~3% more weight loss than gas-filled but with higher adverse-event rates and lower tolerability; the panel makes no recommendation on a specific device, deferring to shared decision-making.

The guideline also covers concomitant lifestyle intensity, proton pump inhibitor (PPI) prophylaxis, peri-procedural antiemetics, nutritional screening and multivitamin supplementation, and post-removal maintenance strategies.

## Recommendations (verbatim, with strength and certainty)

Numbering, strength, and certainty below are the guideline's own (its Table 3). Strong recommendations lead with "we recommend"; conditional (also called weak) recommendations lead with "we suggest". Certainty of evidence is rated on a 4-point scale — high, moderate, low, very low.

| # | Topic | Strength | Quality of evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | IGB + lifestyle modification over lifestyle modification alone, after failed conventional weight-loss strategies | Conditional | Moderate |
| 2 | Moderate- to high-intensity concomitant lifestyle modification during IGB therapy | Strong | Moderate |
| 3 | PPI prophylaxis during IGB therapy | Strong | Moderate |
| 4 | Lowest-nausea anesthetic regimen + perioperative antiemetics; scheduled antiemetics for 2 weeks after placement | Conditional | Low |
| 5 | *Against* perioperative laboratory screening for nutritional deficiencies | Conditional | Low |
| 6 | Daily 1–2 adult dose multivitamins after placement | Conditional | Very low |
| 7 | Weight-loss/maintenance intervention after IGB removal (diet, pharmacotherapy, repeat IGB, or bariatric surgery) | Conditional | Low |

**Recommendation 1.** In individuals with obesity seeking a weight-loss intervention who have failed a trial of conventional weight-loss strategies, AGA suggests the use of IGB therapy with lifestyle modification over lifestyle modification alone. *(Conditional recommendation, moderate certainty)*
- *Implementation remark:* Trials in the United States were limited to a body mass index (BMI) range between 30 and 40 kg/m². Individuals with BMI values outside this range were sometimes included in international trials.
- *Implementation remark:* Fluid-filled balloons may be associated with more weight loss, lower tolerability, and less favorable safety profile than gas-filled balloons. Shared decision-making is suggested for determining device choice.

**Recommendation 2.** In individuals with obesity undergoing IGB therapy, AGA recommends moderate- to high-intensity concomitant lifestyle modification interventions to maintain and augment weight loss. *(Strong recommendation, moderate certainty)*

**Recommendation 3.** In individuals undergoing IGB therapy, AGA recommends prophylaxis with PPIs. *(Strong recommendation, moderate certainty)*

**Recommendation 4.** In individuals undergoing IGB therapy, AGA suggests using the intraoperative anesthetic regimens associated with the lowest incidence of nausea along with perioperative antiemetics. AGA suggests a scheduled antiemetic regimen for 2 weeks after IGB placement. *(Conditional recommendation, low certainty)*
- *Implementation remark:* Evidence is insufficient to recommend a specific antiemetic regimen. The choice of regimen is based on institutional policy, clinical context, and availability.

**Recommendation 5.** In individuals undergoing IGB therapy, AGA suggests against perioperative laboratory screening for nutritional deficiencies. *(Conditional recommendation, low certainty)*

**Recommendation 6.** AGA suggests daily supplementation with 1–2 adult dose multivitamins after IGB placement. *(Conditional recommendation, very low certainty)*

**Recommendation 7.** After IGB removal, AGA suggests subsequent weight-loss or maintenance interventions that include dietary interventions, pharmacotherapy, repeat IGB, or bariatric surgery. The choice of weight-loss or maintenance method after IGB is determined based on patient's context and comorbidities following a shared decision-making approach. *(Conditional recommendation, low certainty)*

## Key Findings / Claims

- **Weight loss vs SOC (pooled RCTs):** ~15.46 lbs at 6 mo (7 RCTs; 95% confidence interval [CI] 10.42–20.51), ~13.12 lbs at 9 mo (3 RCTs; 10.53–15.70), ~9.76 lbs at 12 mo (2 RCTs; 6.38–13.14) — benefit declines with time.
- **%TBWL:** greatest at 6–8 mo (mean difference [MD] 6.89%; 95% CI 4.09–9.70). **Percent excess weight loss:** greatest at 9 mo (MD 20.43%; 16.09–24.77).
- **Responder rates (6–8 mo):** 5% TBWL in 85.1% (IGB) vs 34.6% (SOC), RR 2.44 (95% CI 2.05–2.91; 3 RCTs); 10% TBWL in 61.9% vs 13.7%, RR 4.31 (95% CI 3.21–5.80; 4 RCTs). Similar but smaller trends at 9 and 12 mo.
- **Metabolic:** lowers hemoglobin A1c and fasting blood glucose more than noninvasive therapy (5 RCTs + 18 observational studies); effect most apparent in patients with fasting blood glucose >100 mg/dL, hemoglobin A1c >6.5%, or BMI >40 kg/m². Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) −9 U/L, aspartate aminotransferase (AST) −3 U/L; waist circumference −4.1 cm; greater remission of diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidemia; LDL downward trend; no triglyceride benefit.
- **Safety:** serious adverse events (AE) 5.6% (IGB) vs 1.1% (SOC), RR 3.07 (95% CI 1.16–8.11); perforation 0.3%, esophageal mucosal injury 0.8%, gastric ulcer/bleeding 0.76%, gastric outlet/bowel obstruction 0.12%. No deaths across the 7 pooled RCTs during the 6- to 8-month period with the balloon in place; post-marketing: rare hyperinflation, acute pancreatitis, death. Early removal 9.4% — device intolerance (sense of fullness) and symptomatic intolerance (epigastric pain, reflux, nausea, emesis).
- **Device type:** meta-analysis of 22 RCTs — fluid-filled IGBs nearly 3% more weight loss than gas-filled. All 3 current fluid-filled models beat controls for 6-month %TBWL; only 1 of 2 gas-filled models did. Fluid-filled had numerically higher AE rates; gas-filled better tolerated/safer. No device-specific recommendation made.
- **Lifestyle (Rec 2):** 1 RCT, 80 patients, balloon in place 4 mo plus 2 mo after removal (6-mo treatment period) — high-intensity very-low-calorie ketogenic diet beat moderate-intensity low-calorie diet (MD 7.1 kg, 95% CI 6.30–7.90; percent excess weight loss MD 12%, 95% CI 10.66–13.34). Continuing a moderate- to high-intensity diet for 6 mo after IGB therapy: ongoing weight loss 17 kg and BMI reduction 6 kg/m², with progressive loss even after removal.
- **PPI (Rec 3):** no RCT has directly assessed PPI use in IGB patients. Indirect evidence — PPIs reduce rebleeding risk in high-risk upper gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding stigmata; in 4 RCTs, IGB patients given PPI had fewer device-/non-procedure-related serious adverse events, especially upper GI bleeding. Rationale: gastric mucosa can erode and bleed during and after placement. Use the lowest dose, frequency, and duration given PPI risks (enteric infections incl. *Clostridium difficile*, community-acquired pneumonia, bone fracture, kidney disease, micronutrient deficiency).
- **Antiemetics (Rec 4):** 2 RCTs. Midazolam + ondansetron vs ondansetron alone trended toward benefit for nausea/vomiting (RR 0.57; 95% CI 0.32–1.02), with lower early balloon removal (0/29 vs 3/28; RR 0.14; 95% CI 0.01–2.56). A second trial (alizapride, tropisetron, tropisetron + droperidol) was not applied because those agents have limited US availability. Evidence graded low to very low; no specific regimen endorsed.
- **Nutritional screening (Rec 5):** no direct and no indirect evidence for perioperative laboratory screening. Observational bariatric data: preoperative thiamine deficiency 0%–29% (5 studies), folate deficiency 0%–24% (4 studies), no hypomagnesemia in 3 pre-sleeve-gastrectomy cohorts. The panel still advises individual clinical judgment on whether to screen.
- **Multivitamins (Rec 6):** no direct evidence for post-IGB dosing. In bariatric cohorts, prophylactic 1–3 multivitamin tablets/day left postoperative thiamine deficiency at 0%–9%; 1 multivitamin/day maintained normal folate for 3–12 mo; de novo folate deficiency 6%–9.2% without prophylaxis; hypomagnesemia persisted up to 5 y despite 1–2 multivitamins with minerals; asymptomatic hypokalemia from vomiting 6.8%–8.5% at 1 week post-placement.
- **Post-removal (Rec 7):** sibutramine 10 mg/day vs moderate-/high-intensity diet for 6 mo after removal — both groups lost 17 kg and >6 kg/m² BMI at 1 y, pharmacotherapy only slightly better (>10% total weight loss RR 1.50, 95% CI 0.81–2.78). Liraglutide 3 mg/day added to IGB for 6 mo beat IGB alone (10% total weight loss MD 14.72%, 95% CI 8.81–21.26; weight MD 3.8 kg loss, 95% CI 2.43–5.17). Sequential IGB (2 RCTs; second balloon, 13-mo endpoint) gave greater BMI reduction (MD 5.49 kg/m², 95% CI 4.82–6.16) but more frequent risks/complications. Bariatric surgery after IGB (1 observational cohort): 16.6 kg/m² greater BMI reduction and 42.5% greater excess weight loss at 12 mo vs refusing maintenance. IGB before laparoscopic gastric banding shortened hospitalization by 1 day and lowered intraoperative risk and moderate-to-severe postoperative complications. Overall quality of evidence: low.
- **BMI eligibility note:** US trials were limited to BMI 30–40 kg/m²; some international trials included patients outside that range.

## What the Guideline Does Not Specify

- No contraindications to IGB placement, no patient-selection criteria beyond the BMI 30–40 kg/m² range studied in US trials, and no age criteria.
- No balloon dwell time or removal timing is prescribed; outcomes are simply reported at 6, 9, and 12 months after placement.
- No fill volume, filling medium, or named device is specified — device choice is left to shared decision-making.
- No PPI agent, dose, frequency, or duration is given; the instruction is only to use the lowest dose, frequency, and duration.
- No antiemetic agent, dose, or schedule is given beyond "scheduled, for 2 weeks after placement".
- No multivitamin formulation or micronutrient targets; only 1–2 adult-dose tablets daily.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- No long-term (>1 y) efficacy data vs SOC; weight-loss benefit declines incrementally from 6 to 12 mo, and efficacy beyond 1 year is an explicit evidence gap.
- No direct RCT for PPI prophylaxis, antiemetic regimen, nutritional screening, or multivitamin dosing — several recommendations rest on indirect, low, or very-low-certainty evidence.
- No device-specific recommendation despite fluid- vs gas-filled trade-offs; moderate certainty limited by serious imprecision and very small numbers of included studies and patients.
- Where IGB belongs in the weight-loss algorithm — alone, sequentially, with concomitant therapy, or as a bridge to longer-term intervention — is unsettled.
- Predictors of device intolerance (to identify non-responders and patients at higher adverse-event risk) are not established; the role of exercise in sustaining loss after removal is untested.
- Trial populations were predominantly White, so findings may not generalize across racial and ethnic groups.
- The document gives two different update timelines: an anticipated update 3 years from publication (2024), and a statement that the need for an update will be determined no later than 2022.
- Prior US documents cited for comparison: an American Society for Metabolic and Bariatric Surgery / Society of American Gastrointestinal and Endoscopic Surgeons position statement (2016), covering 2 balloons and reporting rare adverse events with a voluntary removal rate of 4.2%–7.0%; and an American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy systematic review and meta-analysis against Preservation and Incorporation of Valuable Endoscopic Innovations thresholds (2015), covering 1 balloon only and reporting a 7% early removal rate. A Brazilian consensus statement based on >40,000 placements gives guidance on indications, contraindications, and pre-/post-procedure evaluation that this guideline does not reproduce.
