## How This Document Is Structured

**It contains no numbered recommendations.** Guidance is delivered as **43 unnumbered bullets grouped under 6 named "Statements"** (Table 1, *Statements of Best Practice in This Document*, reproduced in full below). Several widely quoted points — the non-lifting sign, underwater EMR, cold snare EMR, the NICE 3 / Kudo Vn algorithm, "endoscopy is first-line for benign lesions", ESD indications, eFTR — come from the narrative body and carry **no GRADE rating**; they are listed under *Narrative Content (Not Graded)*. The `S1.1`-style labels below are reference handles for this page only, not the document's own numbering.

## Summary

Comprehensive multi-society consensus on optimal techniques for endoscopic removal of colorectal lesions. Covers lesion assessment (Paris classification, LST subtypes, optical diagnosis with NICE/Kudo), resection methods by lesion size and morphology, [[endoscopic-mucosal-resection|EMR]] technique (inject-and-cut, underwater EMR, cold snare EMR), ESD and hybrid ESD, endoscopic full-thickness resection (eFTR), lesion marking and tattoo, post-EMR surveillance, equipment, and quality standards. Central thesis: endoscopy is first-line for all benign colorectal lesions, replacing surgical referral.

**GRADE vocabulary used by this document:** strength is **Strong** or **Conditional** (not "weak"); quality is **very low / low / moderate / high**. Per the document's own Table 3, *"recommend"* signals a strong recommendation and *"suggest"* signals a conditional one.

### Statement 2: Lesion removal

*Preamble (ungraded): "The primary aim of polypectomy is complete removal of the colorectal lesion, and the subsequent prevention of colorectal cancer. Endoscopists should employ the safest, most complete, and efficient resection techniques based on available evidence."*

**2a: Diminutive (≤5 mm) and small (6–9 mm) lesions**

| # | Statement (verbatim) | Strength | Quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| S2a.1 | We recommend cold snare polypectomy to remove diminutive (≤5 mm) and small (6–9 mm) lesions due to high complete resection rates and safety profile. | Strong | **High** in Table 1; **Moderate** in body §2a — see note below |
| S2a.2 | We recommend against the use of cold forceps polypectomy to remove diminutive (≤5 mm) lesions due to high rates of incomplete resection. For diminutive lesions ≤2 mm, if cold snare polypectomy is technically difficult, jumbo or large-capacity forceps polypectomy may be considered. | Strong | Moderate |
| S2a.3 | We recommend against the use of hot biopsy forceps for polypectomy of diminutive (≤5 mm) and small (6–9 mm) lesions due to high incomplete resection rates, inadequate histopathologic specimens, and complication rates. | Strong | Moderate |

> ⚠ **Internal discrepancy in the published document (present in both printings).** Table 1 grades S2a.1 *high-quality*; the body text of section 2a grades the identical sentence *moderate-quality*. The document does not reconcile them.

> ⚠ **Scope note on S2a.2.** The recommendation is against cold forceps for **all diminutive (≤5 mm) lesions**. The ≤2 mm clause is a narrow carve-out permitting **jumbo or large-capacity** forceps when CSP is technically difficult — it is *not* a general ">2 mm" threshold for standard cold forceps. The prior page wording ("not recommended for polyps >2 mm") inverted this.

## Narrative Content (Not Graded)

Real content from the document's body text and figures. **None of it carries a strength or evidence grade** — cite it as narrative, never as a graded recommendation.

### Deep submucosal invasion — management

> "The NICE type 3 and Kudo Vn patterns are specific for deep (>1000 μm) invasion… Therefore, the presence of these features should be followed by cold biopsy of the portion of the lesion demonstrating the features, tattoo of the area, and referral to surgery. Non-pedunculated lesions with superficial (<1000 μm) submucosal invasion are candidates for endoscopic resection."

Also narrative: Kudo pit pattern V, white spots (chicken-skin appearance), redness, expansion, firmness, and fold convergence are associated with submucosal invasive carcinoma. *The only graded statement in this area is S1.6 (proficiency in recognizing deep SMI) — Strong, moderate.*

### Non-lifting sign

Body section "Non-Lifting Lesions" + Figure 9. The lesion infolds while surrounding normal submucosa expands. May be due to **submucosal invasion** or to **submucosal fibrosis** (prior polypectomy cautery, colitis). Non-lifting areas are difficult to capture with a snare; hot/cold avulsion is described as a salvage technique.

### Underwater EMR

Full water immersion obviates submucosal injection: with the lumen distended by water, mucosa and submucosa involute as folds while the muscularis propria remains circular. Borders marked with APC or snare-tip coagulation, then hot snare resection (**Drycut, effect 5, 60 W, ERBE VIO 300D**).

- Large LSTs, median 30 mm (range 20–40): **55% removed in 1 piece** with a 33-mm snare; of those 29 en-bloc resections, **79% had histologically free margins**
- Prospective dual-centre UK study, 97 lesions (median 25 mm, range 10–160): submucosal lift still needed in **30%**, correlating with polyp size ≥30 mm; **adenoma recurrence 13.6%** at median 6 months
- For colon lesions ≥10 mm: **2%–5% delayed bleeding**; **1 case report** of perforation (proximal colon lesion removed in retroflexion)

### Cold snare EMR

- 94 lesions removed piecemeal (median 20 mm, range 12–60), 76.7% followed at 2–10 months → **9.7% local recurrence**
- **163 serrated lesions ≥10 mm** (median 15 mm, range 10–40), succinylated gelatin + dilute methylene blue injection then piecemeal snare without diathermy; surveillance of 82% (n=134) at 6 months → **a single recurrence (0.6%)**
- 41 sessile serrated polyps (median 15 mm, range 10–35) by CSP **without** submucosal injection; <50% followed at 6 months → no recurrence
- Need for epinephrine in the injectate remains uncertain; snare-tip soft coagulation of edges and clip closure have not been used in cold EMR
- **Long-term and comparative data are necessary**

### Colorectal ESD

Indications are "relatively few"; within the gate of a lesion ≥20 mm destined for endoscopic rather than surgical resection in which en-bloc inject-and-cut EMR would be difficult:

- Lesions ≥20 mm with suspected superficial (not deep) SMI not amenable to en bloc EMR
- Mucosal lesions with submucosal fibrosis
- Local residual/recurrent early carcinoma after prior endoscopic resection
- Colitis-associated dysplasia (non-polypoid)

### Endoscopic full-thickness resection (eFTR)

> "Suggested indications for endoscopic full-thickness resection include lesions <30 mm, particularly non-lifting or those involving diverticulum."

Glossary (Table 2) defines eFTR as *"technique involving the use of a full-thickness resection device for lesions <30 mm."*

- FTRD was effective for difficult-to-resect colorectal lesions (non-lifting, challenging locations) **especially for lesions ≤20 mm**; in the 181-patient / 9-centre prospective German study, R0 fell to **58.1% for lesions >20 mm vs 81.2% for lesions ≤20 mm** (P = .0038)
- Cap **outer diameter 21 mm**; cap size and length limit the tissue that can be grasped

### Other narrative data points

- Cold forceps polypectomy incomplete resection **ranges from 9% to 61%**
- Colectomy for benign colon lesions rose from **6% (2000) to 18% (2014)**, mean lesion size 27 (SD 17) mm; 30-day mortality **0.7%** and major adverse events **14%** for surgery, vs **0.08%** 30-day mortality for endoscopic resection of large lesions
- **Figure 5** (management algorithm) assigns 10–19 mm non-pedunculated lesions to cold/hot snare ± injection, with **EMR for non-polypoid or suspected serrated lesions**; pedunculated lesions split at head <20 mm and stalk <5 mm (hot snare, transect at mid-to-lower stalk) vs head ≥20 mm or stalk ≥5 mm (prophylactic ligation)

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Internal to the document:** S2a.1 (cold snare for diminutive/small lesions) is graded *high-quality* in Table 1 and *moderate-quality* in the body text of §2a. Unresolved by the source.
- **Internal to the document:** S6.2 uses conditional wording ("We suggest") but is labelled a Strong recommendation.
- None with [[usmstf-2020-endoscopic-removal]] — same document, verified bullet-by-bullet across the *Gastroenterology* and *GIE* printings.
- Optimal role of underwater EMR vs inject-and-cut: not established
- Cold snare EMR for large lesions: long-term recurrence data lacking
- ESD indications in colorectum: evidence still evolving; not covered by any graded statement
- eFTR: not covered by any graded statement
- Antithrombotic management for ≥20 mm EMR: explicitly individualized (S2c.12), no strong data at time of publication
- Hot avulsion current: superseded by [[aga-2026-electrosurgery]] (same tier, newer) — see [[endoscopic-mucosal-resection]]
