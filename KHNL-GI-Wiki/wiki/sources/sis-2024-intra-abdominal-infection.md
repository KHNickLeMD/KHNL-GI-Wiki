## Summary

Fifth iteration of the Surgical Infection Society (SIS) guideline on intra-abdominal infection (IAI) in adults, updating the 1992, 2002, 2010 (with IDSA) and 2017 versions. Systematic review of PubMed/Medline, Cochrane, Embase and Web of Science, January 2016 → September 2022, refreshed February 2024; 5,070 records → 75 included studies, restricted to randomized controlled trials (RCTs), systematic reviews and meta-analyses. Recommendations are GRADE-rated: strength **1 (strong, "We recommend…")** or **2 (weak, "We suggest…")**, evidence quality **A (high)**, **B (moderate)** or **C (low)**.

Scope is **adults only**, and "IAI" means **complicated** IAI — infection extending beyond a hollow viscus into the peritoneum, mesentery, retroperitoneum, another organ or the abdominal wall. Uncomplicated IAI confined to a hollow viscus is covered by disease-specific guidance (e.g. acute cholecystitis). Explicitly **excluded**: primary (spontaneous) bacterial peritonitis, peritoneal dialysis catheter– and device-related peritonitis, peritonitis after solid organ transplant, and gynecologic/genitourinary-predominant infection. Methods of diagnosis — laboratory testing and radiologic imaging preferences — are also out of scope.

The organizing principle throughout is **risk stratification**, which decides empiric spectrum, antifungal and anti-enterococcal coverage, and source-control timing. The second theme is **shorter therapy**: four days after adequate source control is the default, including after percutaneous drainage and in sepsis, with eight days as the ceiling even in critically ill patients. The third is **reserving the newer broad-spectrum agents** (imipenem-cilastatin-relebactam, ceftolozane-tazobactam, ceftazidime-avibactam, eravacycline, biapenem, doripenem) for higher-risk patients rather than using them first-line, on antimicrobial-resistance grounds.

Several 2017 recommendations were not re-reviewed; the working group voted to carry forward the prior strong, high-to-moderate-quality ones unchanged in Table 3. Those are reproduced below under *Carried-forward 2017 recommendations* and are still live guidance.

## Risk stratification — the input every recommendation depends on

**Higher risk** = any of:
- Meets Surviving Sepsis Campaign criteria for **sepsis or septic shock** (carried-forward 2017 recommendation, Grade 1-B)
- **Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation (APACHE)-II score ≥10 points** (same recommendation)
- Delayed or inadequate initial source control
- Post-operative IAI
- Diffuse peritonitis
- Multiple medical comorbidities — advanced age; malignant disease; cardiovascular, hepatic or renal dysfunction; hypoalbuminemia

Also treated as "higher risk" for spectrum purposes: those likely to harbor resistant or atypical organisms — prior healthcare exposure or prior antimicrobial therapy, i.e. healthcare-associated IAI (HA-IAI), versus community-acquired IAI (CA-IAI).

> *The 2017 criteria are the operative definition; this update does not restate a numeric threshold other than APACHE-II ≥10.*

## Key Recommendations

*Grades are the document's own (strength 1 = recommend / 2 = suggest; quality A/B/C). The guideline numbers none of its recommendations; the grouping headings below are the document's.*

### Antimicrobial agent therapy — agent selection

**Aminoglycosides**
- Recommend **against** aminoglycoside-based combination regimens for empiric therapy of higher risk patients *(Grade 1-A)*.

**Cefoperazone-sulbactam**
- Recommend cefoperazone-sulbactam for empiric therapy of **lower risk** patients *(Grade 1-B)*.

**Imipenem-cilastatin-relebactam**
- Recommend imipenem-cilastatin-relebactam for empiric therapy *(Grade 1-A)*.
- Suggest **reserving** it for higher risk patients, because of its broader spectrum *(Grade 2-C)*.

**Ceftolozane-tazobactam**
- Recommend ceftolozane-tazobactam **plus metronidazole** for empiric therapy *(Grade 1-A)*.
- Suggest reserving ceftolozane-tazobactam for higher risk patients, including those with resistant *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* infections *(Grade 2-C)*.
- Suggest ceftolozane-tazobactam for empiric therapy of patients at risk for infection with **extended-spectrum β-lactamase (ESBL)-producing Enterobacterales** *(Grade 2-B)*.

**Ceftazidime-avibactam**
- Recommend ceftazidime-avibactam **plus metronidazole** for empiric therapy *(Grade 1-A)*.
- Suggest reserving ceftazidime-avibactam for higher risk patients, because of its broader spectrum *(Grade 2-C)*.
- Recommend ceftazidime-avibactam plus metronidazole for empiric therapy of patients **at risk for ESBL-producing Enterobacterales** *(Grade 1-B)*.

**Eravacycline**
- Recommend eravacycline for empiric therapy *(Grade 1-A)*.
- Suggest reserving eravacycline for higher risk patients, because of its broader spectrum *(Grade 2-C)*.

**Tigecycline**
- Recommend **against** tigecycline for empiric therapy of low- and high-risk patients *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Suggest reserving tigecycline for patients with resistant infections, or as part of a combination regimen **when no therapeutic alternative exists** *(Grade 2-B)*.

**Biapenem**
- Suggest biapenem for empiric therapy *(Grade 2-B)*.
- Suggest reserving biapenem for higher risk patients, because of its broader spectrum *(Grade 2-C)*.

**Doripenem**
- Recommend doripenem for empiric therapy *(Grade 1-A)*.
- Suggest reserving doripenem for higher risk patients, because of its broader spectrum *(Grade 2-C)*.

**Fluoroquinolones**
- Recommend **ciprofloxacin plus metronidazole** for empiric therapy of lower risk patients *(Grade 1-A)*.
- Suggest **moxifloxacin** for empiric therapy of lower risk patients *(Grade 2-A)*.

**Vancomycin**
- Recommend **against** vancomycin for empiric therapy of low- and high-risk patients *(Grade 1-B)*.

**Metronidazole**
- Recommend metronidazole as the **preferred anti-anaerobic agent** in combination regimens for empiric therapy of low- and high-risk patients *(Grade 1-A)*.

**Polymyxin B**
- Suggest polymyxin B for empiric therapy of higher risk patients with multi-drug-resistant (MDR) infection, including *Acinetobacter baumannii* complex, *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, *Escherichia coli* and *Enterobacter* spp. *(Grade 2-B)*.

### Anti-fungal therapy
- Suggest **against** empiric anti-fungal therapy in **lower risk** patients *(Grade 2-B)*.
- Recommend **anidulafungin** for empiric therapy of higher risk patients with infections from *Candida* spp. *(Grade 1-B)*.

### Anti-enterococcal therapy
- Recommend **against** empiric therapy targeting *Enterococcus* spp. in **lower risk** patients with CA-IAI *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Suggest empiric therapy targeting *Enterococcus* spp. in **higher risk** patients *(Grade 2-B)*.

### Specific disease states

**Hepatopancreatobiliary malignant disease**
- Recommend **targeted** perioperative antibiotic prophylaxis **based on a positive preoperative bile culture** in patients undergoing surgical procedures for hepatopancreatobiliary malignant disease *(Grade 1-A)*.
- Suggest antibiotic prophylaxis for **at least 24 hours** in patients undergoing **pancreaticoduodenectomy with positive bile cultures following preoperative biliary drainage**, to prevent organ-space surgical site infection (SSI) *(Grade 2-C)*.

**Acute uncomplicated appendicitis — non-operative management**
- Recommend **oral moxifloxacin** for empiric therapy of lower risk patients undergoing non-operative management of acute uncomplicated appendicitis *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Suggest a **carbapenem** for empiric therapy of patients undergoing non-operative management of acute appendicitis *(Grade 2-B)*.

**Acute complicated appendicitis**
- Recommend **substituting oral for intravenous (IV) antibiotics** to complete a short **24-hour** course in lower risk patients with acute complicated appendicitis after adequate source control by laparoscopic appendectomy *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Suggest limiting post-operative antibiotics to **24–48 hours** for low- and high-risk patients with adequate source control for complicated appendicitis undergoing appendectomy *(Grade 2-B)*.

**Chronic appendicitis**
- Suggest **against** antibiotic prophylaxis in lower risk patients undergoing elective laparoscopic appendectomy for chronic appendicitis *(Grade 2-B)*.

**Abdominal trauma**
- Suggest **ertapenem** for antibiotic prophylaxis in low- and high-risk patients undergoing laparotomy for traumatic injury *(Grade 2-B)*.

### Duration of antibiotic therapy
- Recommend limiting antimicrobial therapy to **four days** for low- and high-risk patients with source control achieved via a **percutaneous drainage** procedure *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Recommend limiting antimicrobial therapy to **four days** after achieving source control in **high-risk patients with sepsis** *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Recommend limiting antimicrobial therapy to **four days** after achieving source control in higher risk patients at increased risk of complications from **diabetes mellitus, obesity, or higher illness severity** *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Suggest limiting antimicrobial therapy to **eight days** in **critically ill** patients after achieving adequate source control of IAI *(Grade 2-B)*.

### De-escalation
- Suggest de-escalation of antibiotic therapy in intensive care unit (ICU) patients with HA-IAI *(Grade 2-B)*.
- Suggest **maintaining anaerobic coverage** when de-escalating *(Grade 2-C)*.

### Timing of source control
- Recommend undertaking source control within **12 hours** in **lower risk** patients *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Recommend undertaking source control within **six hours** in **higher risk** patients with associated **septic shock** *(Grade 1-B)*.

### Antimicrobial stewardship
- Recommend initiating antimicrobial therapy **within one hour** in higher risk patients with sepsis *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Recommend implementation of **empiric antimicrobial therapy protocols** to improve antimicrobial stewardship *(Grade 1-B)*.

### Carried-forward 2017 recommendations (Table 3)

*Not re-reviewed in 2024; the working group voted to retain the prior strong (Class 1), high-to-moderate-quality (Level A or B) recommendations unchanged. Table 3 note: where the table says Enterobacteriaceae (formerly an order, now a family), read **Enterobacterales**.*

**Patient assessment**
- Recommend considering phenotypic and physiologic factors in assessing risk for treatment failure and death: signs of sepsis or septic shock, extremes of age, comorbidities, extent of abdominal infection, adequacy of initial source control, and presence/persistence of resistant or opportunistic pathogens *(Grade 1-A)*.
- Recommend classifying as **high risk** patients meeting Surviving Sepsis Campaign criteria for sepsis or septic shock, or with **APACHE-II ≥10 points** *(Grade 1-B)*.

**Source control**
- Recommend a source control procedure to remove infected fluid and tissue or prevent ongoing contamination, except where evidence shows non-interventional approaches give acceptable outcomes *(Grade 1-A)*.
- Recommend **no more than four days (96 h)** of antimicrobial therapy after adequate source control *(Grade 1-A)*.
- Recommend giving the antimicrobial agent **within one hour before** a source control procedure if two or more half-lives of the prior dose have passed at the time of incision *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Recommend the **least invasive** approach that achieves definitive source control or controls the infection enough to resolve the inflammatory response and organ dysfunction *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Recommend abbreviated laparotomy and temporary abdominal closure in critically ill patients when closure would cause intra-abdominal hypertension, physiologic reserves are severely compromised, the initial procedure cannot achieve adequate source control, or re-laparotomy is planned for mesenteric ischemia *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Recommend **on-demand rather than routine/scheduled re-laparotomy** in high-risk patients with severe peritonitis when adequate source control was obtained at the index procedure *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Recommend **against routine peritoneal fluid cultures** in lower risk patients with CA-IAI to guide antimicrobial therapy *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Recommend using measures of ongoing or progressive systemic inflammation or organ dysfunction to predict failure of source control *(Grade 1-B)*.

**Empiric antibacterial therapy — all patients**
- Recommend regimens active against gram-negative Enterobacterales, gram-positive cocci, and obligate anaerobes normally isolated in IAI *(Grade 1-A)*.
- Recommend **cefepime plus metronidazole** *(Grade 1-A)*.
- Recommend **imipenem-cilastatin or meropenem** *(Grade 1-A)*.
- Recommend **piperacillin-tazobactam** *(Grade 1-A)*.

**Lower risk patients**
- Recommend **narrower-spectrum** agents active against gram-negative Enterobacterales, aerobic streptococci and obligate anaerobes in lower risk patients with CA-IAI *(Grade 1-A)*.
- Recommend **against** broader-spectrum or additional agents for anti-pseudomonal or anti-enterococcal coverage in lower risk CA-IAI *(Grade 1-A)*.
- Recommend **cefotaxime or ceftriaxone plus metronidazole, or ertapenem** for lower risk CA-IAI *(Grade 1-A)*.
- Recommend **ciprofloxacin plus metronidazole, or moxifloxacin monotherapy**, for CA-IAI in lower risk patients with serious β-lactam allergy *(Grade 1-A)*.
- Recommend treating **perforated appendicitis** with the same agents/regimens as lower risk CA-IAI, unless the patient meets higher risk criteria *(Grade 1-A)*.

**Higher risk patients**
- Recommend **against** adding an adjunctive aminoglycoside or fluoroquinolone to a β-lactam for empiric therapy of CA-IAI in higher risk patients *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Recommend an **echinocandin** (anidulafungin, caspofungin or micafungin) for empiric or pathogen-directed therapy of severely ill patients with suspected or proven *Candida* spp. infection *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Recommend **against empiric anti-fungal therapy** for CA-IAI in higher risk patients *(Grade 1-B)*.

**Antimicrobial stewardship**
- Recommend limiting antimicrobial therapy to **24 hours** in patients with traumatic bowel perforations operated on within 12 hours *(Grade 1-A)*, patients with acute or gangrenous appendicitis **without perforation** *(Grade 1-A)*, and patients with acute or gangrenous **cholecystitis without perforation** *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Recommend **oral agents with good bioavailability** as substitutes for IV agents once adequate gastrointestinal function returns *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Recommend oral agents to complete a short course, **not to prolong** antimicrobial duration beyond current recommendations *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Recommend **oral ciprofloxacin plus metronidazole** to complete a short course *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Recommend **against** most cephalosporin-, aztreonam- or fluoroquinolone-based empiric regimens for CA-IAI in geographic areas with a high prevalence of ESBL-producing Enterobacterales *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Recommend **ertapenem** for CA-IAI in lower risk patients, or a **broad-spectrum carbapenem** (doripenem, imipenem-cilastatin or meropenem) for CA-IAI in higher risk patients who reside in **MDR-endemic areas** *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Recommend **standard dosages** for lower risk patients with CA-IAI who are not severely obese and have no substantial renal or hepatic impairment *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Recommend **against changing therapy based on culture results** in lower risk patients with CA-IAI who have a satisfactory clinical response to source control and empiric therapy *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Recommend **routinely de-escalating** to the narrowest-spectrum agent(s) active against isolated microorganisms in higher risk patients with CA-IAI and HA-IAI *(Grade 1-B)*.
- Recommend **against antimicrobial agents to prevent infection in patients with severe or necrotizing pancreatitis** *(Grade 1-B)*.

## Key Findings / Claims

- **Four days is the default duration after adequate source control**, and this now explicitly extends to source control achieved by *percutaneous drainage* — previously an open question — on post hoc analysis of the STOP-IT trial (129 patients drained percutaneously; no difference in recurrent IAI, *Clostridioides difficile* infection, hospital days).
- **Diabetes, obesity and higher illness severity do not justify a longer course** (post hoc STOP-IT analysis, 152 obese / 64 diabetic / 83 higher-APACHE-II patients).
- **Source control timing moved earlier than the 2017 guidance**: within 12 h generally, within 6 h with septic shock. In the supporting systematic review, mortality risk from operative delay in septic shock was **zero at delays under six hours**.
- **Vancomycin should not be part of empiric coverage** — adding it to piperacillin-tazobactam gave no difference in SSI, recurrent IAI or death, and the vancomycin group had significantly higher rates of healthcare-associated infection.
- **Tigecycline is now a recommend-against**: network meta-analysis showed significantly higher all-cause mortality versus cefepime plus metronidazole, and significantly more serious adverse events versus imipenem-cilastatin.
- **Bile cultures drive hepatopancreatobiliary prophylaxis.** *Enterococcus* was the organism most often isolated where prophylaxis lacking anti-enterococcal activity failed; targeted prophylaxis based on preoperative bile culture significantly reduced both incisional and organ-space SSI. After preoperative biliary drainage, prolonged (>24 h) prophylaxis significantly reduced organ-space SSI — but optimal duration remains unknown.
- **Oral moxifloxacin was non-inferior to IV ertapenem → oral levofloxacin/metronidazole** for non-operative uncomplicated appendicitis (APPAC II, 599 patients).
- **Prophylactic antibiotics remain contraindicated in severe/necrotizing pancreatitis** (carried forward from 2017, Grade 1-B).

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Anti-fungal therapy in higher risk patients reads two ways within the same document.** The 2024 text recommends **anidulafungin for empiric therapy of higher risk patients with infections from *Candida* spp.** *(Grade 1-B)*, while carried-forward Table 3 recommends **against empiric anti-fungal therapy for CA-IAI in higher risk patients** *(Grade 1-B)* and recommends an echinocandin only for **suspected or proven** *Candida* infection in severely ill patients. Read together: echinocandin when *Candida* is suspected or proven, not as blanket empiric cover. The wiki states it that way.
- **Optimal prophylaxis duration after preoperative biliary drainage is explicitly unresolved** — "at least 24 hours" is a floor, not a defined course.
- The guideline **excludes spontaneous bacterial peritonitis**, so it does not bear on [[spontaneous-bacterial-peritonitis]]; the dosing gap there remains open.
- **Doripenem and biapenem** carry positive recommendations but are not marketed in the United States; the document does not flag availability.
- Evidence for more aggressive empiric therapy in critically ill surgical patients is described by the authors as sparse, so several strong recommendations rest on carried-forward rather than newly reviewed evidence.
