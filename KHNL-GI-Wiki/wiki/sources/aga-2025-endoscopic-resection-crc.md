## Summary
AGA Expert Commentary on the endoscopic management of T1 colorectal cancer (CRC) — cancer with invasion confined to the submucosa, without invasion of the muscularis propria. It reviews the definition of early CRC, the role of endoscopic resection, histopathologic factors driving risk of lymph node metastasis (LNM) and recurrence, and post-resection management (surveillance vs adjuvant surgery). This is an Expert Commentary, so it contains no numbered Best Practice Advice; the points below are its key clinical claims, drawn largely from USMSTF, ESGE, and JGES/JSCCR guidance.

Optical lesion assessment is the first critical step. Endoscopists should recognize features suggesting submucosal invasion (SMI) — depressed/excavated lesions, irregular pit/vascular pattern, NICE type 3, and lateral-spreading granular-mixed or nongranular lesions. Optical diagnosis alone is highly operator-dependent (real-world sensitivity often only 40%–50%); when diagnosis is unclear or CRC is highly suspected, refer to a high-volume center with resection and pathology expertise. Suspected T1 CRC should ideally be removed **en bloc**, because piecemeal resection prevents accurate histologic assessment and is itself an independent risk factor for luminal recurrence.

Endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD), with its higher en-bloc rate than EMR regardless of size, is often the preferred strategy for suspected superficially invasive T1 CRC; endoscopic full-thickness resection (EFTR) is a reasonable alternative for select T1 CRCs <2 cm where deep SMI is suspected. Resection is considered curative when all five criteria are met. Importantly, emerging data downgrade deep SMI as a solitary risk factor: in a meta-analysis (Zwager et al), SMI alone carried an absolute LNM risk of only ~2.6% and was **not** an independent predictor of LNM. LVI is the strongest predictor of LNM; tumor budding (Bd 2/3) and adverse histology (G3/G4, signet ring, mucinous) also confer high risk.

Recurrence after curative endoscopic resection of low-risk T1 CRC is low (~0.7%) vs 7.0% for high-risk (noncurative) T1 CRC; most recurrences occur within 36 months and 96% within 72 months. For high-risk T1 CRC surgery remains standard, but >80% of "high-risk" cases have no LNM at surgery, so the decision should be individualized against operative morbidity. Rectal T1 CRC carries higher local-recurrence and LNM risk than colonic, but rectal surgery is more morbid — favoring organ-preserving strategies. Full-thickness scar resection (EFTR, TAMIS, combined endo-lap) is an emerging alternative to completion surgery after R1/Rx margins without other risk factors.

## Key Findings / Claims
- **Definitions:** T1 CRC = neoplastic invasion through muscularis mucosae into submucosa, without muscularis propria invasion ("malignant polyp"). Without SMI, the word "cancer" should be avoided; terms *intramucosal adenocarcinoma* / *in situ adenocarcinoma* should be avoided (no LNM risk).
- **En-bloc imperative:** Suspected T1 CRC should be approached with en-bloc resection (USMSTF/ESGE/JGES). Piecemeal resection impairs histology and is an independent risk factor for luminal recurrence; NCCN states piecemeal-removed T1 inevitably requires adjuvant surgery.
- **Resection modality:** ESD preferred for suspected superficial T1 CRC (higher en-bloc rate irrespective of size); limited by complexity, learning curve, and inability to confidently resect deep SMI. EFTR reasonable for select T1 CRC <2 cm with suspected deep SMI.
- **Curative resection criteria (USMSTF/ESGE/JGES) — ALL five required:** (1) complete resection with histopathologic margins free of tumor; (2) superficial SMI (<1000 μm); (3) absence of lymphovascular invasion (LVI); (4) well-to-moderately differentiated histology (G1/G2); (5) low or absent tumor budding.
- **Depth of SMI:** Measured in μm per JSCCR — for nonpedunculated lesions from bottom of muscularis mucosae (or surface if not identifiable) to invasive front; pedunculated lesions use Haggitt classification (levels 0–4). Deep SMI = ≥1000 μm.
- **Deep SMI is a weaker solitary risk factor than historically believed:** >95% of patients with deep SMI as the *only* high-risk criterion have no LNM. Meta-analysis (Zwager et al, 8 studies, 1146 pts): SMI as solitary risk factor → absolute LNM risk 2.6% (pooled incidence 2.83; 95% CI 1.66–4.78); deep SMI was NOT an independent predictor of LNM; 37.9% of deep-SMI cancers had no other high-risk feature.
- **Operative risk:** Population cohort of 5170 surgical T1 CRC pts — 30-day mortality 1.7%, severe adverse events requiring re-intervention 8.3%.
- **LVI:** Strongest independent predictor of LNM (RR 5.2; 95% CI 4.0–6.8). Small-vessel involvement more strongly associated with LNM than large. USMSTF/JSCCR/ESGE all consider LVI a high-risk criterion prompting surgery. Perineural invasion also a risk factor.
- **Tumor budding:** Single tumor cell or cluster of ≤4 cells at invasive margin. Bd1 = 0–4 buds (low); Bd2 = 5–9 (intermediate); Bd3 = ≥10 (high). Bd2/Bd3 → increased LNM (OR 2.83 per Zwager). High-risk criterion (USMSTF/JSCCR/ESGE).
- **Adverse histology:** Poorly differentiated (G3), undifferentiated (G4), signet ring cell, mucinous → worse prognosis; adverse histology independent LNM risk factor (OR 2.14, meta-analysis of 67 studies/21,238 pts). WHO grades by lowest level of differentiation; JSCCR by predominant.
- **Resection margins:** R0 = both horizontal and vertical margins microscopically free. Lateral/horizontal positivity → local recurrence risk; vertical/deep positivity → deeper invasion + LNM risk. Margin <1 mm traditionally a risk, but a recent multicenter study found local recurrence not significantly higher at 0.1–1 mm (0.8%; 95% CI 0.1%–4.4%) vs >1 mm absent tumor budding. ESGE prefers vertical margin ≥1 mm.
- **Surveillance after curative low-risk T1 CRC:** Recurrence ~0.7% (low-risk) vs 7.0% (high-risk/noncurative); 60% of recurrences within 36 mo, 96% within 72 mo. Surveillance colonoscopy alone (no nodal/distant imaging) is reasonable after curative resection of low-risk T1 CRC. JSCCR and ESGE recommend surveillance colonoscopy within 1 year; NCCN recommends 1-year colonoscopy after curative colon resection but more frequent for rectal. Rectal T1 may warrant sooner/more frequent surveillance.
- **High-risk T1 CRC:** Surgery is standard of care, but >80% of "high-risk" cases have no LNM at surgery → individualize. Rectal T1 CRC: higher local recurrence and LNM than colon, but higher surgical morbidity → favor organ-preserving options.
- **R1/Rx margins without other risk factors:** Risk of intramural residual cancer 5%–11% and LNM 8%–9%. ESGE/JSCCR recommend endoscopic surveillance at 3–6 months for isolated lateral margin positivity. Full-thickness scar resection (EFTR / TAMIS / combined endoscopic-laparoscopic) is an emerging alternative to completion surgery; retrospective long-term data showed recurrence 2.2% (adjuvant surgery) vs 9.0% (EFTR) with similar 5-year metastasis-free and overall survival.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **Deep SMI as a surgical trigger:** Historically (and in the existing [[polypectomy-emr]] "Favorable vs Unfavorable" table) deep SMI (≥1 mm) is an unfavorable criterion prompting surgical evaluation. This 2025 CPU nuances that — deep SMI *in isolation* carries only ~2.6% LNM risk and is not an independent predictor — supporting individualized, organ-preserving decisions rather than automatic surgery. Surfaced inline on the entity page; existing guideline-based criteria remain the page's primary recommendation.
- **Margin <1 mm:** Traditional ≥1 mm clear-margin threshold (USMSTF 2020) is questioned by newer multicenter data showing no significantly higher recurrence at 0.1–1 mm absent tumor budding; still unsettled.
- **Surveillance intervals after curative endoscopic resection of T1 CRC are not well defined** (no clear evidence); most data originate from Europe/Asia — generalizability to North American populations is uncertain.
