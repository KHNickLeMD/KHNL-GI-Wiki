## Summary

The FDA prescribing information for **golimumab**, fetched to close a dose cell [[anti-tnf-agents]] had been carrying with a warning on it. That page's table gave golimumab as **200 mg → 100 mg** induction then **100 mg every 4 weeks** maintenance, flagged beneath: [[acg-2025-uc]] sources the 100 mg maintenance *dose* but never the *interval*. **The label confirms the interval** and sharpens the schedule the wiki had blurred: maintenance is **100 mg at week 6 and every 4 weeks thereafter** — week 6, not week 4, is where the q4wk clock starts, with week 2 taking the second induction dose.

Golimumab's place in the class is narrow and the label draws it: **ulcerative colitis is its only gastrointestinal indication**, and it is approved there for **adults and pediatric patients weighing at least 15 kg** — a pediatric indication certolizumab pegol does not have in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). The weight band matters because the dose halves below it: patients 15 kg to <40 kg get **100 mg → 50 mg → 50 mg q4wk**.

Two drug-specific items separate golimumab from [[cimzia-2025-certolizumab-pegol-label|certolizumab pegol]] rather than repeating the class. First, **the label lists no contraindications at all** — the section reads "None", where certolizumab's contraindicates prior hypersensitivity. Second, and clinically more consequential, **golimumab crosses the placenta in the third trimester**, the opposite of certolizumab's negligible transfer, and the label turns that into a rule for the baby: **live vaccines are not recommended in an infant exposed in utero for 6 months after the mother's last golimumab dose during pregnancy**.

The ulcerative colitis safety data are reassuring in the controlled window and specific enough to quote. Infection rates in the induction trial were **~12% on golimumab and on placebo**; through week 60 of maintenance the two adverse reactions most often ending treatment were **tuberculosis (0.3% vs 0.6% placebo)** and **anemia (0.3% vs 0%)**.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Indications (§1) — one GI indication**

- **Ulcerative colitis:** **adults and pediatric patients weighing ≥15 kg** with **moderately to severely active** disease.
- Non-GI: rheumatoid arthritis (RA, **in combination with methotrexate**), psoriatic arthritis (PsA), ankylosing spondylitis (AS).
- **No Crohn's disease indication** — consistent with [[acg-2025-crohns]] not listing golimumab among the agents recommended for Crohn's.

**Dosage — ulcerative colitis (§2.3, Table 1), subcutaneous**

| Weight | Week 0 | Week 2 | **Week 6 and every 4 weeks thereafter** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Adults and pediatric patients ≥40 kg** | **200 mg** | **100 mg** | **100 mg** |
| Pediatric patients ≥15 kg to <40 kg | **100 mg** | **50 mg** | **50 mg** |

- Non-GI dosing for contrast (§2.2): RA, PsA, AS = **50 mg subcutaneously once a month** — a different dose and interval from the UC regimen.
- **Before initiating (§2.1):** evaluate for **active tuberculosis** and test for **latent** infection; **test for hepatitis B virus (HBV)**; if possible complete all age-appropriate vaccinations first.
- **Administration (§2.4):** prefilled syringe self-injection permitted from **age 12 and older**; the **SmartJect autoinjector is adults only** — pediatric self-administration with it has not been evaluated. Allow the device to sit at **room temperature ≥30 min** outside the carton before injecting.

**Contraindications (§4)**

- **None.** The label states this outright.

**Boxed warning — serious infections and malignancy**

- Increased risk of **serious infection leading to hospitalization or death**, including tuberculosis, bacterial sepsis and invasive fungal infection. **Test for latent tuberculosis before initiating and treat if positive.**
- **Lymphoma and other malignancies, some fatal**, reported in children and adolescents on tumor necrosis factor (TNF) blockers.

**Warnings and precautions (§5) — golimumab-specific points**

- **Serious infections (5.1):** opportunistic bacterial, mycobacterial, invasive fungal, viral and parasitic infections reported with TNF blockers — aspergillosis, blastomycosis, candidiasis, coccidioidomycosis, histoplasmosis, legionellosis, listeriosis, pneumocystosis, tuberculosis. Patients **frequently present with disseminated rather than localized disease**.
- **Hematologic cytopenias (5.10):** pancytopenia, leukopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, aplastic anemia and thrombocytopenia reported **with golimumab specifically**. Caution in patients who have or have had significant cytopenias.
- **Switching between biologics (5.9):** take care switching from one biological product to another — **overlapping biological activity may further increase infection risk**.
- **Vaccinations / therapeutic infectious agents (5.11):** patients may receive vaccines **except live vaccines**. Bring **pediatric** patients up to date with all immunizations before starting if possible. **Other therapeutic infectious agents — e.g. BCG bladder instillation for cancer — could also cause clinical infection.**
- Also carried, as class warnings already held by [[anti-tnf-agents]]: malignancy incl. hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma (5.2), congestive heart failure (5.3), demyelinating disorders (5.4), HBV reactivation (5.5), autoimmunity (5.6), and **not for use with abatacept (5.7) or anakinra (5.8)**.

**Pregnancy and the exposed infant (§8.1)**

- **Postmarketing case reports have not identified a drug-associated risk** of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse maternal/fetal outcomes; a northern European observational birth cohort found **similar unadjusted major-birth-defect rates** versus no treatment or non-biologic systemic therapy, with limitations the label notes.
- ⚠ **Golimumab is transported across the placenta during the third trimester** and **may affect the immune response of the in-utero–exposed infant**. Another TNF-blocking monoclonal antibody was **detected in infant serum for up to 6 months**; these infants **may be at increased risk of infection**.
- ⚠ **Live vaccines are not recommended in the exposed infant for 6 months following the mother's last golimumab injection during pregnancy.**
- **Disease activity is itself a risk:** increased activity in RA or ulcerative colitis is associated with **preterm delivery (<37 wk), low birth weight (<2500 g) and small-for-gestational-age** birth.
- Animal reproduction: no adverse fetal effects at exposures ~**360×** the maximum recommended human dose during organogenesis; no adverse developmental effects on infants at maternal concentrations ~**460×** in a pre-/post-natal study.

**Adverse reactions (§6.1) — ulcerative colitis data**

- Safety base: **1,233 golimumab-treated adults with UC** across three pooled controlled Phase 2/3 trials (UC-1, UC-2, UC-3), plus **69 pediatric patients ≥15 kg** in an open-label trial.
- **Discontinuation for adverse reactions through week 60** of the UC trials, golimumab-100 mg maintenance vs placebo maintenance: **tuberculosis 0.3% vs 0.6%**, **anemia 0.3% vs 0%**.
- **Infections:** in UC-1, rates were **similar between golimumab 200/100 mg and placebo, ~12%**; per-patient-year incidence through week 60 similar between 100 mg maintenance and placebo maintenance.
- **Demyelination:** none in golimumab-treated patients through week 6 of UC-1 or in the 100 mg maintenance group of UC-2. The one CNS demyelination case was in a **placebo** maintenance patient who had received 400/200 mg induction.
- **Liver enzymes:** incidence of **alanine aminotransferase (ALT) ≥5 × upper limit of normal (ULN)** similar across UC-1/2/3. In the RA/PsA/AS trials, ALT ≥5 × ULN occurred in **0.7% golimumab vs 0.2%** control and ≥3 × ULN in **2% vs 2%**; the label notes the relationship is unclear because many patients took methotrexate and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs.
- RA/PsA/AS discontinuation for adverse reactions through week 16 was **2% golimumab vs 3%** placebo — most common: sepsis, ALT increased, aspartate aminotransferase increased, each 0.2%.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **No contradiction with the guidelines; the label fills their silence.** [[acg-2025-uc]] Rec 29 recommends golimumab for moderate-to-severe UC and sources the 100 mg maintenance dose, but prints no interval — which is why the wiki's figure carried a caveat. The label's interval matches what the wiki had.
- **The schedule was imprecise, not wrong.** The wiki's table implied maintenance begins after the week-2 dose. The label starts it at **week 6**, so there is a **4-week gap between the second induction dose and the first maintenance dose**.
- **Placental transfer is the real divergence inside the class.** Golimumab crosses the placenta in the third trimester and triggers a **6-month live-vaccine hold** in the exposed infant; [[cimzia-2025-certolizumab-pegol-label|certolizumab pegol]] showed infant concentrations **at least 75% lower than maternal** and no such hold. Both labels are tier-equal, so this is a genuine drug-level difference, not a conflict — and it is the kind of difference that drives agent choice in pregnancy.
- **Open:** the label gives **no renal or hepatic dose adjustment and no therapeutic drug monitoring guidance** for golimumab. Neither is answerable from any ingested source for this agent.
- **Open:** the label sets **no stopping rule for non-response in ulcerative colitis** — unlike [[humira-2025-adalimumab-label|adalimumab]], whose label says to discontinue if no clinical remission by week 8.
