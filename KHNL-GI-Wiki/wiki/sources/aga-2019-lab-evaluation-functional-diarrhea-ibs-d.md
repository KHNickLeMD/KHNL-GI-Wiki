## Summary

AGA Institute clinical practice guideline answering one narrow, high-frequency question: **which laboratory tests to send, and which to skip, when excluding other diagnoses in a patient you suspect has functional diarrhea or IBS-D.** It is a GRADE guideline with a companion technical review (Carrasco-Labra et al., *Gastroenterology* 2019;157:859–880). Seven numbered recommendations; the panel met face-to-face on 8 September 2017, and the document states it "should expire in 5 years."

**The population definition is the guideline's most important content, because it is what makes the recommendations safe.** It applies only to the **immunocompetent** adult with **"watery" diarrhea of at least 4 weeks' duration**. It explicitly **excludes** patients with bloody diarrhea; diarrhea with signs of fat malabsorption; alarm features (weight loss, anemia, hypoalbuminemia); a family history of IBD, colon cancer or celiac disease; and a travel history to regions with recognised diarrhea-related pathogens. Applying the against-testing recommendations outside that population is a misuse of the guideline — notably Recommendation 4, whose entire force depends on the absence of travel or immigration from a high-risk area.

Two recommendations are **Strong** — test for *Giardia*, and test for celiac disease with IgA-tTG plus a second test to cover IgA deficiency. Both stand on the good test performance of the assays rather than on outcome data. Everything else is Conditional on low-quality evidence, and the striking pattern is that **the guideline is as much about not testing as testing**: it suggests against ESR and CRP for IBD screening, and against routine stool ova and parasites other than *Giardia*.

Recommendation 7 is a formal **"No recommendation; knowledge gap"** on serologic tests for diagnosing IBS — specifically the anti-CdtB (cytolethal distending toxin B) and anti-vinculin antibody strategy proposed for post-infectious IBS-D. The reasoning is quantitative: specificity is around 90%, but sensitivity of only **20%–40%** is not sufficient for routine use, and the case–control design in secondary/tertiary care settings likely inflates even those estimates.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Recommendations — all 7, verbatim, with the document's own strength and quality (Table 3)

| # | Recommendation (verbatim) | Strength | Quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "In patients presenting with chronic diarrhea, the AGA suggests the use of either fecal calprotectin or fecal lactoferrin to screen for IBD." | Conditional | Low |
| 2 | "In patients presenting with chronic diarrhea, the AGA suggests against the use of erythrocyte sedimentation rate or C-reactive protein to screen for IBD." | Conditional | Low |
| 3 | "In patients presenting with chronic diarrhea, the AGA recommends testing for *Giardia*." | **Strong** | **High** |
| 4 | "In patients presenting with chronic diarrhea with no travel history to or recent immigration from high-risk areas, the AGA suggests against testing for ova and parasites (other than *Giardia*)." | Conditional | Low |
| 5 | "In patients presenting with chronic diarrhea, the AGA recommends testing for celiac disease with IgA tissue transglutaminase and a second test to detect celiac disease in the setting of IgA deficiency." | **Strong** | Moderate |
| 6 | "In patients presenting with chronic diarrhea, the AGA suggests testing for bile acid diarrhea." | Conditional | Low |
| 7 | "In patients presenting with chronic diarrhea, the AGA makes no recommendation for the use of currently available serologic tests for diagnosis of IBS." | **None** | **Knowledge gap** |

⚠ **Recommendation 7 is not a recommendation against testing.** The document's own Table 2 defines "No recommendation" as meaning "the confidence in the effect estimate is so low that any effect estimate is speculative at this time." It must never be cited as an AGA position for or against anti-CdtB/anti-vinculin serology.

### The printed Comments — these carry the operative thresholds

Three recommendations are printed with a **Comment** block in the boxed statement. The thresholds live there, not in the recommendation text:

- **Rec 1 Comment:** "A threshold value of **50 µg/g** for fecal calprotectin is recommended to optimize sensitivity for IBD. Threshold values in the range of **4.0–7.25 µg/g** for fecal lactoferrin are recommended to optimize sensitivity."
- **Rec 3 Comment:** "Use of a *Giardia* **antigen test** or **polymerase chain reaction** for *Giardia* test is recommended."
- **Rec 5 Comment:** "Testing options for IgA-deficient subjects include **IgG tissue transglutaminase** and **IgG or IgA deaminated gliadin peptides**."
- **Rec 6 Comment:** "In settings with limited availability of commercial assays, an **empiric trial of a bile acid binder** could be considered."

### Test performance, as printed

| Test | Threshold used | Pooled sensitivity for IBD | Pooled specificity for IBD |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fecal calprotectin | **50 µg/g** | 0.81 (95% CI 0.75–0.86) | 0.87 (95% CI 0.78–0.92) |
| Fecal lactoferrin | **4.0–7.25 µg/g** | 0.79 (95% CI 0.73–0.84) | 0.93 (95% CI 0.63–0.99) |
| CRP | **5–6 mg/L** | 0.73 (95% CI 0.64–0.80) | 0.78 (95% CI 0.58–0.91) |
| ESR | **10–15 mm/h** | *lower estimates of diagnostic accuracy for IBD than CRP* | — |

- **Raising the calprotectin cutoff is the wrong move.** A higher threshold (**100–164 µg/g**) is "associated with a markedly decreased sensitivity without a marked increase in specificity." The 50 µg/g figure is chosen deliberately to optimise sensitivity.
- Calprotectin evidence downgraded to low for risk of bias and statistical imprecision; lactoferrin for risk of bias, significant heterogeneity and imprecision (note the very wide specificity CI, 0.63–0.99).
- **Why only Conditional despite reasonable numbers:** the low quality "is compounded by the small likelihood that a positive test would initiate further confirmatory evaluation, leading to an earlier diagnosis of IBD compared to the **10% likelihood** that persons without IBD might be needlessly exposed to further confirmatory testing."
- **CRP has a named exception.** "There are few settings where ESR should be considered as an appropriate screening test for IBD," but "if testing for fecal lactoferrin or calprotectin are either not available or not covered by insurance, the use of CRP might be considered to be a reasonable option to screen for IBD."
- ***Giardia*:** modern antigen and PCR (for the *Giardia* small subunit ribosomal RNA) tests show **sensitivity and specificity >95%** in many studies. Rated High quality — the only High-quality rating in the document. The rationale is explicitly risk-benefit: "because treatments are straightforward, there is little risk in utilizing these tests."
- **Celiac:** serum IgA-tTG at thresholds in the **7–15 AU/mL** range gives sensitivity typically **>90%**, with specificity typically slightly higher. **A positive test warrants confirmation by duodenal biopsy**, and "in adults, small bowel biopsy should be used to confirm a serologic diagnosis of celiac disease before committing a patient to a strict gluten-free diet."
- **IBS serology (anti-CdtB / anti-vinculin):** specificity ~**90%** in the 2 available studies, but sensitivity only **20%–40%** — not sufficient for routine use. Case–control design in secondary/tertiary care likely inflates the estimates.

### The two IgA-deficiency strategies (Rec 5)

The guideline gives two distinct ways to cover IgA deficiency after a negative IgA-tTG, and they are not interchangeable:

1. **Quantitative IgA level** — if normal, it confirms the accuracy of the negative result.
2. **IgG-tTG or IgG deaminated gliadin peptides** — for IgA-deficient patients, *or* combined with IgA-tTG as an initial strategy when IgA levels are not available.

### Bile acid diarrhea testing (Rec 6) — availability drives the choice

- **⁷⁵SeHCAT** (selenium homotaurocholic acid test) — nuclear medicine test, **moderate** diagnostic efficiency; used in Europe, **not available in North America**.
- **Total fecal bile acids in a 48-hour stool collection** — documents increased fecal bile acids. Available in the US.
- **Serum FGF19** (fibroblast growth factor 19) — measures defective feedback of bile acid synthesis. Available in the US.
- **Serum 7α-hydroxy-4-cholesten-3-one (C4)** — a measure of bile acid synthesis; described as **"a test that is not yet available."**
- Because these tests "are not widely available or Food and Drug Administration–approved, it is reasonable to use an **empiric trial of bile acid binders**," with clinical response suggesting excess bile acids as the cause.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Routine stool ova and parasites.** [[chronic-diarrhea]] listed "ova & parasites" as a routine step-4 stool study. AGA 2019 Rec 4 suggests **against** it in the absence of travel to or recent immigration from a high-risk area, reserving only *Giardia*-specific testing. The wiki page has been corrected; the travel qualifier is preserved, because without it the recommendation is unsafe.
- **Calprotectin threshold.** The wiki previously carried calprotectin with no threshold at all ("normal calprotectin has high NPV for IBD"). No contradiction — a decision gap, now closed.
- **Definition boundary of chronic diarrhea.** AGA 2019 uses **at least 4 weeks**, matching [[asge-2010-diarrhea]] (>4 weeks) and differing from [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (>30 days). Two of three ingested sources now sit at 4 weeks; the discrepancy remains clinically inert.
- **Guideline expiry.** The document states it "should expire in 5 years" — i.e. **2024**. It has not been superseded within the ingested corpus, so it governs the laboratory questions it answers, but the self-declared expiry should be noted wherever it is the sole support for a claim.
- **Scope, not disagreement.** This guideline does **not** cover: the mechanistic (secretory/osmotic/inflammatory/fatty) classification, the stool osmotic gap or its cut-points, the endocrine panel (chromogranin A, gastrin, VIP, 5-HIAA), fecal elastase, fecal fat, or *C. difficile* testing. Those parts of [[chronic-diarrhea]] remain outside this source and keep their existing provenance.
- **Figure 1 is not in the document held in `raw/`.** The Summary refers to "a clinical decision support tool… to guide the evaluation of patients with chronic watery diarrhea (>4 weeks) (Figure 1)", but no such figure appears anywhere in the four-page article. Its content is therefore not reproduced; the algorithm on [[chronic-diarrhea]] is built from the recommendation text, not from the tool. Logged to [[needed-sources]].
