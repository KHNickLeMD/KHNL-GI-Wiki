## Summary

The American Foregut Society (AFS) Clinical Practice Committee's TIF Working Group produced this white paper to consolidate published outcome data for **TIF 2.0** and **concomitant TIF (cTIF)** and to lay out best-practice patient assessment, selection, and perioperative management. Its central anatomic premise is that the reflux barrier comprises the diaphragmatic crura, the **gastroesophageal flap valve (GEFV)**, and the intrinsic LES with its sling fibers — and that TIF works by re-establishing and augmenting the GEFV rather than by wrapping the esophagus.

The document is most useful for the operative details that the society GERD guidelines do not carry. It states explicitly that **no specific guidelines exist for preoperative workup prior to TIF**, and therefore recommends following the same principles used for surgical fundoplication with modifications — then enumerates a five-item workup. It supplies a **relative-contraindication list** for the EsophyX device, a step-by-step technique description with the specific landmarks and fastener counts that separate a safe from an unsafe deployment, and a **detailed postoperative protocol** (antiemetic regimen, antibiotic timing, a six-week staged diet, a lifting restriction, and a short-term PPI course).

The selection rule is the same threshold the GERD guidelines use: **TIF 2.0 is appropriate for patients without a hiatal hernia greater than 2 cm**; larger hernias require either conventional LARS or **cTIF** — laparoscopic hiatal hernia repair followed by TIF in the same anesthetic, FDA-permitted since a 2017 device-labeling modification. The paper adds a Hill-grade layer: **Hill Grade I and some Hill Grade II qualify for TIF 2.0 alone; Hill Grade III and IV should be addressed through LARS or a concomitant approach.** It also introduces a newer **AFS endoscopic classification of the EGJ** based on axial hiatal hernia length (L), hiatal aperture diameter (D), and presence/absence of the flap valve (F) — flagged as **requiring validation in independent studies** before being used for procedure selection.

On efficacy, the paper's honest summary is a trade: with no head-to-head comparison available, **traditional fundoplication appears to have the greatest durability for increasing LES pressure and reducing % time pH < 4 long-term, while TIF appears superior in symptom improvement and patient satisfaction.**

## Key Findings / Claims

### Mechanism and how TIF differs from a surgical fundoplication

| | TIF 2.0 | Traditional fundoplication |
|---|---|---|
| Valve created | **True flap valve** — fundus manipulated around the distal esophagus, distal esophagus partially invaginated into surrounding fundus, accentuating the cardiac notch and sharpening the angle of His | **Wrapped valve construct** — fundus wrapped around the distal esophagus |
| Short gastric vessels | **Not divided** | Taken down |
| Stomach state during construction | **Inflated** during fixation (30+ fixation points) | Collapsed / not inflated |
| High-pressure zone at distal esophagus | Yes | Yes |

- **Mechanism of action:** TIF reduces **EGJ distensibility**, thereby decreasing transient LES relaxation — "the main mechanism for upright refluxers."
- **Wrap extent:** TIF 2.0 achieves **270°–320°** (TIF 1.0 was 250°–300°).
- **Device evolution:** ELF → TIF 1.0 (plications of the cardia onto the distal esophagus, just proximal to the EGJ) → **TIF 2.0** (rotational wrap of cardia and fundus around the circumference of the distal esophagus). EsophyX (2006) → EsophyX Z (2015) → **EsophyX Z+ (2017)**, the current device. Since **2017** the FDA has permitted same-session laparoscopic hiatal hernia repair followed by TIF (cTIF).

### Indications and patient selection

- Originally FDA-approved for chronic GERD **responsive** to PPIs; indications have since expanded to:
  - Chronic refractory GERD, or **partial responders** to acid suppression
  - Patients who do not want long-term acid suppression due to concern about PPI side effects (**PPI-adverse**)
  - Patients with underlying disorders that preclude PPI use
  - Patients intolerant of acid-suppressing medications
  - Patients who are **not candidates for MSA based on manometry**
  - Patients who do not wish to undergo traditional fundoplication given concerns about post-procedure bloating and dysphagia
- **Emerging/limited applications** (case reports): post-[[poem|POEM]] reflux disease; GERD with severe obesity undergoing bariatric surgery.
- **Barrett's esophagus:** *"very limited data on TIF to facilitate remission of intestinal metaplasia and dysplasia in patients undergoing endoscopic eradication."* An ongoing trial exists; **efficacy of TIF in the setting of Barrett's is yet to be defined.**

**Anatomic selection rule:**

| Anatomy | Procedure |
|---|---|
| Hiatal hernia **≤2 cm** (axial length and transverse diameter), **Hill Grade I** and **some Hill Grade II** | **TIF 2.0 alone** |
| Hiatal hernia **>2 cm** length, or **>2 cm** transverse hiatal diameter, or **Hill Grade III / IV** | **Conventional LARS**, or **cTIF** (laparoscopic hiatal hernia repair + TIF) |

- **Caution on sizing:** hiatal hernia size observed at laparoscopic repair is **often larger than what is perceived on endoscopy** — extra caution is advised even when a hernia measures <2 cm endoscopically.
- **AFS endoscopic EGJ classification** — adds assessment of axial hiatal hernia **length (L)**, hiatal aperture **diameter (D)**, and **presence or absence of the flap valve (F)**; "may be incorporated into the selection of antireflux procedure **after validation in independent studies**."

**Table 1 — Conditions of specific / relative contraindication for TIF with the EsophyX device (verbatim list):**

- BMI ≥ 35
- Hiatal hernia > 2 cm
- Paraesophageal hernia
- Bleeding disorders
- Severe esophagitis (**LA grade C and D**)
- Esophageal diverticulae
- Esophageal varices
- Esophageal infections or fungal disease
- Esophageal stenosis
- Esophageal strictures
- Esophageal obstructions
- Limited neck mobility
- Osteophytes of the spine
- Any kind of normal or abnormal esophageal anatomy which would not permit insertion of the device

> **Internal nuance to preserve:** BMI ≥ 35 appears in the relative-contraindication table, but the Conclusions state that patients with **BMI > 35 may still be candidates for TIF, though they "may be better served overall with bariatric surgery."** This is a preference statement, not an absolute exclusion.

### Preoperative evaluation

> The paper states there are **no specific guidelines available for preoperative workup prior to TIF**; it recommends following the same principles and diagnostic evaluation used for surgical fundoplication, with slight modifications. In addition to a detailed history of symptoms and response to PPIs and/or H2 receptor blockers:

1. **High-quality endoscopic exam with esophageal biopsies within 1 year** before the planned procedure is **essential**. Documents: EGJ integrity by **Hill classification**, hiatal hernia size measured in **axial length** and the **greatest diameter of the diaphragmatic hiatus**, and complications of GERD.
   - **LA grade C & D esophagitis** and **long-segment Barrett's esophagus obviate the need for objective pH testing.**
2. **Objective reflux testing** with esophageal pH monitoring **off PPI** (± impedance) is **highly recommended in non-erosive reflux disease**.
   - **LA grade A and B esophagitis** and **endoscopic short-segment Barrett's** have **high inter-observer variability** as objective evidence of pathologic reflux — **formal objective reflux testing should be performed in these cases.**
   - **Wireless pH off PPI for 96 hours** increases diagnostic yield vs **48 hours** and vs **24-hour catheter-based** testing.
3. **Barium esophagram** — presence, size, and type of hiatal hernia; alerts to the possibility of a **short esophagus**. Important in choosing TIF vs cTIF: with a hiatal hernia it is technically challenging for TIF alone to pull the esophagus down far enough to create adequate intra-abdominal length, and the hernia creates **axial tension that can cause sliding and re-herniation of the newly created flap valve.**
4. **HRM only if dysphagia** — in patients with dysphagia symptoms, **HREM ± timed barium esophagram is strongly advised** to rule out a major motility disorder such as achalasia. **GERD patients without dysphagia do not routinely require manometry prior to TIF.**
   - **This differs from the pre-LNF workup**, where a manometric diagnosis of **ineffective esophageal motility (IEM)** carries more risk of causing dysphagia and may alter the type of fundoplication.
   - **TIF in IEM may carry less dysphagia risk** — it is a partial fundoplication performed over a **60 Fr** device.
   - **FLIP is an acceptable alternative to routine HREM** as a screening tool to detect achalasia and EGJOO, and can be performed at the same time as diagnostic endoscopy; "in general more appealing to patients."
5. **Gastric emptying study** case-by-case based on symptoms. With dyspeptic symptoms (nausea, early satiety, fullness, bloating) in whom gastroparesis is suspected, a **formal 4-hour gastric emptying study** should be performed.

### Technical aspects of TIF 2.0

- Single-use **EsophyX Z+** device; **general anesthesia**; left lateral, supine, or modified left lateral position. Requires **2 proceduralists** (or 1 proceduralist plus a helper) — one handles the device, one the endoscope.
- **Diagnostic EGD first** — determine presence/absence of esophagitis, exclude strictures or webs that would prevent safe passage, measure anatomical landmarks, and **document the Hill Grade**.
- **Empiric dilation** to aid device passage: **57–60 Fr (18–20 mm) Savary-Gilliard dilator** over a wire, or an **18–20 mm TTS hydrostatic balloon** at the upper esophageal sphincter.
- **Lubrication:** mineral oil or Endo Lube on the scope, device channel, stylet port, and helical retractor — **avoid standard lubricant** on these (standard lubricant is used only on the device tip before oral insertion).
- **Insufflation: CO₂ only, set to 15 mmHg. Air insufflation is discouraged.**
- Scope kept in **retroflexion** throughout, with direct visualization of the device at all times.
- **Start posteriorly at the 11 o'clock position.** Helical retractor advanced to engage tissue just below the EGJ, rotating clockwise **~4 turns**.
- **Critical safety step:** advance the device into the stomach (**usually 0.5–1.0 cm**) to the level of the **pre-procedure EGJ measurement** before deploying. *"Deployment of fasteners above this landmark may be deployed through the diaphragm which can lead to potential mediastinitis, abscess, or leak."*
- **Plication sequence:** ≥3 plications posterior at the EGJ → 3 anterior → then rotate toward the greater curvature at **4, 6, and 8 o'clock**. The 4/6/8 o'clock positions are where the diaphragm lies close to the esophagus and fasteners are **more liable to traverse the mediastinum and diaphragm** if the device is not adequately advanced below the EGJ.
- **Fastener counts:** the first **20 fasteners (10 plications)** are the **minimum**. Typically **1–2 reinforcements** at each corner, plus **3–4 "mid-valve" fasteners** at 8, 6, and 4 o'clock (placed without retraction).
- **Never place additional fasteners over prior fasteners** — risk of esophageal injury with potential mediastinal infection and/or abscess (and fistula/perforation).
- **Final valve: approximately 270°–320° in circumference and 3–4 cm in length.**

### Technical aspects of cTIF

- Indicated when hiatal hernia is **>2 cm in length or >2 cm transverse hiatal diameter**. The hernia must be repaired with reduction of intrathoracic stomach back into the abdomen **prior to** the TIF.
- **Hiatal hernia size limits have not been defined from randomized controlled trials.** Published cTIF series span hernias of **3 cm+** (Ihde 2011), **2–5 cm** (Janu 2019), and **all sizes up to 8 cm** (Choi 2021, dividing short gastrics and ensuring **3 cm of intra-abdominal esophageal segment**). *"It appears that cTIF can be safely performed for a wide range of hiatal hernia sizes."* The **primary objective is attaining adequate intra-abdominal esophageal length**, not a specific hernia size cutoff.
- **Dissection as high in the mediastinum as possible** is crucial, to obtain **2–3 cm of intra-abdominal esophageal length**.
- **Hard stop:** *"If adequate intraabdominal esophageal length is unable to be obtained, then TIF should not be performed as the firing of the T fasteners may injure mediastinal structures."*
- **Fixation of the stomach to the crura is not recommended** — it impedes tissue mobility for the TIF portion that follows.
- Crural repair per surgeon technique; **interrupted sutures more commonly used than running**. **Mesh (biologic or biosynthetic) is left to surgeon discretion** and should lie flat against the diaphragm without folded edges; bioabsorbable mesh does not appear to increase adverse events, but the use of mesh in cTIF **remains to be studied**.
- Preferred hernia-repair approach is minimally invasive (laparoscopic or robotic) — less postoperative pain, faster recovery, reduced morbidity, shorter length of stay.
- Can be done by a single surgeon performing both, or as a **gastroenterologist–foregut surgeon collaboration**.

### Postoperative care

| Element | Protocol |
|---|---|
| **Disposition — TIF alone** | Observe briefly, **discharge same day on clear liquid diet** |
| **Disposition — cTIF** | **NPO**, observe overnight for hydration and control of nausea/vomiting/cramps; same-day discharge acceptable at team discretion if pain and nausea are controlled |
| **Antiemetics — preop** | **Scopolamine patch** |
| **Antiemetics — intraop** | **Ondansetron + dexamethasone** |
| **Antiemetics — postop** | Scheduled, may continue for the **first few days**. If no contraindication, **double or even triple**: ondansetron **8 mg**, droperidol **0.625 mg**, aprepitant **80 mg** |
| **Antibiotics** | Broad-spectrum **preoperatively, within 30 minutes prior**; cover **enteric gram-negatives, anaerobes, and enterococci**. Some continue several days post-procedure |
| **Diet progression** | Full liquid **2 weeks** → pureed **1–2 weeks** → soft **1–2 weeks** → **regular diet at week 6** |
| **PPI** | **Continue 4–6 weeks** post-TIF to allow gastric mucosal healing and avoid ulceration/bleeding at fastener sites |
| **Lifting restriction** | **≤15 lbs for the first 8 weeks** (heavy lifting can cause hiatal recurrence) |
| **Bowel regimen** | e.g. polyethylene glycol, to avoid constipation and straining |
| **Symptomatic** | Simethicone for bloating; **hyoscyamine** for esophageal spasm; anxiolytics at bedtime; acetaminophen and/or ketorolac intra-op for pain |

- **Retching and vomiting can be detrimental** — may disrupt the hiatus and the newly created valve. This is the rationale for the aggressive antiemetic protocol.
- Expected after the procedure (full-thickness plication): **sore throat, left shoulder pain, substernal chest pain.**

### Adverse events

- Meta-analysis **serious adverse event rate 2.4%** — perforations, post-TIF bleeding, and pneumothorax.
- **Mediastinal abscesses reported in less than 2% of cases.** T-fasteners deploy transmurally through esophageal and gastric walls, a potential pathway for bacterial translocation — hence pre-procedural antibiotics.
- **Bleeding:** may occur at helical-device and fastener sites. **First maneuver is pressure** — clamp the tissue in the tissue mold, tighten, hold several minutes; this resolves the majority. If not, clips, epinephrine injection, or fibrin glue. **An immediate post-TIF endoscopy should be performed after withdrawal of the device** to evaluate for bleeding or injury.
- **Mucosal tears/perforation:** typically at initial device introduction, from excessive force past the cervical esophagus. Mitigations — pre-dilation with a **57–60 Fr Savary** over a guidewire; gentle back-and-forth rocking during advancement; left lateral decubitus positioning; manual guidance of the tip; jaw thrust; **temporary deflation of the endotracheal tube cuff**. *"The key is to avoid the use of excessive forces during insertion."* Perforation may also occur during fastener deployment if firing in the area of previous fasteners.
- **Abscess complication warrants immediate surgical consultation** if a surgeon is not already involved; admit for IV hydration and IV antibiotics after CT diagnosis.

### Outcomes

Data presented are restricted to studies meeting: TIF 2.0 technique, RCT design, symptom assessment with GERD questionnaires, PPI use, and objective endoscopy and/or pH data.

| RCT | n / centers | Key results |
|---|---|---|
| **RESPECT** (Hunter et al.) | 129, 8 US centers; TIF + placebo (n=87) vs sham + PPI (n=42) | At 6 mo TIF eliminated regurgitation in **67%**; **28%** of TIF group resumed PPI; **77%** healed esophagitis; % AET improved after TIF, **no improvement after sham on any measure**; 7 adverse events in TIF vs 1 in sham |
| **Håkansson et al.** | 44, 5 European centers; TIF (n=22) vs sham (n=22) | At 6 mo **59% of TIF remained in remission off PPI** vs no improvement in sham; **80% of TIF Hill grade I–II** (no change in sham); **69% of TIF normalized AET vs 20% sham**; 20 adverse events in TIF vs 7 sham; dysphagia 4 TIF vs 2 sham (P = NS) |
| **Trad et al.** (crossover) | 63, 7 US centers; TIF vs high-dose PPI | 6 mo post-TIF **65% elimination of symptoms**; **71% of crossover patients off PPI** at 6 mo, **82% off PPI at 12 mo**; **100% esophagitis healed at 12 mo**; AET normalization/decrease **52% on HD-PPI → 33% after TIF off PPI** (P = .388); 12 mo **45% normalized AET**; **no adverse events** |
| **TEMPO at 5 years** (Trad et al.) | 44, 7 US centers | **86% elimination of troublesome regurgitation**; **80% elimination of troublesome atypical symptoms**; no significant difference between 1, 3, and 5 years; **patient satisfaction 70%**; PPI status at 5 y — **46% off PPI, 34% on daily PPI, 20% PRN**; **no severe adverse events** |

**cTIF published data** (all non-randomized) — e.g. Janu et al., prospective, n=99, hiatal hernia 2–5 cm: improvement in all three symptom questionnaires (HRQL, GERSS, RSI) at 6 and 12 months; daily PPI **60% at baseline → 11% at 6 months**; **74% off PPI at 12 months**; gas bloat decreased at 6 and 12 months; **70% satisfied**; **no adverse events**.

- **Head-to-head trade-off (no direct comparison exists):** *"traditional fundoplication has the greatest durability to increase LES pressure and decreases % time pH < 4 long-term. On the other hand, TIF seems to be superior in symptom improvement and patient satisfaction."* Prospective direct comparison is needed.
- All cited studies had post-procedural follow-up of **at least 6 months to 5 years**.

### Reimbursement

- TIF has a **Category 1 CPT code effective January 1, 2016**, jointly sponsored by **ACG, AGA, ASGE, and SAGES**.
- National Medicare payment (2021, unadjusted): **$8,907.66** outpatient facility, **$439.30** physician; **7.75 work RVU, 12.60 total RVU.**

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Source priority — this white paper does not override the newer society guidelines.** It is a tier-1 consensus document, but [[asge-2024-gerd]] (and its 2025 publication date) is **newer**, so where the two touch the same claim, the ASGE document's content is what the wiki asserts. In practice they **agree** on the operative selection rule (hernia ≤2 cm + Hill I/II → TIF 2.0; >2 cm or Hill III/IV → cTIF or LARS), so AFS 2023 contributes **net-new procedural, perioperative, and workup detail** rather than competing recommendations.
- **Internal inconsistency on BMI** — Table 1 lists **BMI ≥ 35** as a relative contraindication while the Conclusions state such patients "may still be candidates for TIF" but "may be better served overall with bariatric surgery." The wiki should present this as a **relative** exclusion with the bariatric-referral preference, not an absolute bar.
- **cTIF hernia-size ceiling is undefined.** The paper states plainly that size limits "have not been defined from randomized controlled trials" and reports safe series up to **8 cm**, while framing **adequate intra-abdominal esophageal length**, not hernia size, as the real determinant. Any wiki page asserting a maximum hernia size for cTIF would be going beyond this source.
- **The AFS L/D/F EGJ classification is explicitly not yet validated** — the paper says it "may be incorporated into the selection of antireflux procedure **after validation in independent studies**." Do not present it as a selection criterion in use.
- **No evidence grades.** This is a white paper: it contains **no GRADE ratings, no numbered recommendations, and no strength-of-evidence labels.** Wiki pages must not attribute grades to it. Contrast with [[asge-2024-gerd]], which grades TIF 2.0 Conditional/Low and cTIF Conditional/Very Low.
- **Barrett's application unresolved** — limited data, an ongoing trial, efficacy "yet to be defined."
- **Adverse-event rate differs from the ASGE meta-analysis** as expected from different pooling: AFS cites a **2.4% serious AE rate** and **<2% mediastinal abscess**; [[asge-2024-gerd]] reports serious AEs **8% vs 1.9%** (not statistically different from PPI/sham) with perforation 0.9%, bleeding 0.6%, pneumothorax 0.5%. Both are already on the wiki page; the ASGE figures are the newer source and remain what the page asserts.
