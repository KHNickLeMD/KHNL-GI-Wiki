## Summary

The FDA prescribing information for **certolizumab pegol**, fetched to settle a dose [[anti-tnf-agents]] had been carrying with an explicit warning on it. That page's dosing table gave certolizumab induction as **400 mg at weeks 0, 2 and 4**, with a flag directly beneath: *neither [[acg-2025-crohns]] nor [[aga-2021-crohns-pharm]] states an induction schedule for certolizumab — both discuss the agent only qualitatively. Confirm against FDA labeling.* **The label confirms it exactly**, and supplies the maintenance arm the guidelines also omit: **400 mg every four weeks** — but **only in patients who obtain a clinical response** to induction, a conditional the wiki's table did not carry.

The dose is the smaller half of what the label adds. Certolizumab is the only anti-tumor necrosis factor (anti-TNF) agent in the Crohn's armamentarium given as **two separate 200 mg subcutaneous injections at separate sites** for every 400 mg dose, and the only one supplied in **both** a lyophilized vial requiring reconstitution (healthcare professional only) and a self-injectable prefilled syringe. The label is also where the **pregnancy** data live, and they are the reason this drug is chosen in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) at all: **placental transfer is negligible to low** — infant plasma concentrations at birth were **at least 75% lower than maternal** — and **no dosage adjustment is required during pregnancy**, with concentrations through pregnancy inside the non-pregnant range.

Two items are safety traps rather than background. **Certolizumab causes erroneously elevated activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT)** on several named assays, in patients with entirely normal coagulation and with no effect on coagulation *in vivo*; prothrombin time and thrombin time are unaffected. A clinician who works up a prolonged aPTT in a treated patient is chasing an artifact. And the boxed warning's latent tuberculosis rule carries a number the guidelines do not print: treat for latent tuberculosis before starting, and **consider an induration of ≥5 mm a positive tuberculin skin test — even in a patient previously vaccinated with Bacille Calmette-Guérin (BCG)**.

The label's Crohn's-specific adverse-reaction data are modest and worth knowing in proportion: serious adverse reactions **10% vs 9%** placebo, discontinuation for adverse reactions **8% vs 7%**, and the three reactions most often ending treatment are **abdominal pain, diarrhea and intestinal obstruction** — each at 0.4%, each easily mistaken for the disease.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Indications (label §1) — one GI indication**

- **Crohn's disease:** reducing signs and symptoms, and maintaining clinical response, in **adults** with **moderately to severely active** disease who have had an **inadequate response to conventional therapy**.
- Non-GI: rheumatoid arthritis, polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis (≥2 y), psoriatic arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, non-radiographic axial spondyloarthritis, plaque psoriasis. **No ulcerative colitis indication** — consistent with [[acg-2025-uc]] not recommending it.

**Dosage — Crohn's disease (§2.1)**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Induction** | **400 mg** subcutaneously — **two 200 mg injections** — **initially, and at weeks 2 and 4** |
| **Maintenance** | **400 mg every 4 weeks** — **only in patients who obtain a clinical response** to induction |
| **Route rule (§2)** | Rotate sites; never into tender, bruised, red or hard skin. A 400 mg dose = **two injections at separate sites** in the thigh or abdomen |
| **Supplied (§3)** | **200 mg lyophilized powder** in a single-dose vial (reconstitution required) **or 200 mg/mL solution** in a single-dose prefilled syringe |

- **Lyophilized powder (§2.8):** healthcare professional only. Room temperature 30 min before reconstituting; **1 mL Sterile Water for Injection** with the supplied **20-gauge** needle directed at the vial wall; **swirl gently ~1 min, never shake**, repeat every 5 min — full reconstitution may take **up to 30 min**; final concentration **200 mg/mL**. Reconstituted solution: **≤24 h at 2–8 °C**, do not freeze; at room temperature **≤2 h** before administration. Swap the 20-gauge for a **23-gauge** needle to inject.
- **Prefilled syringe (§2.9):** patient self-injection permitted after training. Inject **≥2 inches from the navel** and **≥1 inch from the previous site**; avoid scars and stretch marks. ⚠ **The needle-shield cap contains a natural rubber latex derivative** — handle with caution in latex-sensitive patients.
- No preservative in either form — **discard any unused portion**.

**Before starting, and alongside (§2.10, 2.11)**

- **Evaluate every patient for active *and* latent tuberculosis before initiation** — tuberculin skin test and chest x-ray in all patients; specifically consider undetected latent infection in patients who have immigrated from or travelled to high-prevalence countries, or had close contact with an active case.
- **May be used as monotherapy or with non-biological disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs).**
- **Combination with biological DMARDs or another TNF blocker is not recommended.**

**Contraindication (§4)**

- **History of hypersensitivity to certolizumab pegol or any excipient** — reactions have included **angioedema, anaphylaxis, serum sickness and urticaria**.

**Boxed warning — serious infections and malignancy**

- Increased risk of **serious infection leading to hospitalization or death**: tuberculosis, bacterial sepsis, invasive fungal infection (histoplasmosis), other opportunistic pathogens. **Most patients who developed these were on a concomitant immunosuppressant such as methotrexate or a corticosteroid.**
- **Discontinue if a serious infection or sepsis develops.**
- **Test for latent tuberculosis; if positive, treat before starting.** Monitor all patients for active tuberculosis during treatment **even if the initial latent test was negative** — tests may also be **falsely negative while on therapy**.
- **Lymphoma and other malignancies, some fatal**, reported in children and adolescents on TNF blockers.

**Warnings and precautions (§5) — the decision-relevant detail**

| Warning | Label detail and action |
|---|---|
| **Serious infections** (5.1) | Do not initiate during an active infection, including a clinically important localized one. Higher risk: **age >65 y**, comorbidities, concomitant immunosuppressants. Weigh risks in chronic/recurrent infection, prior tuberculosis exposure, prior opportunistic infection, residence or travel in areas endemic for tuberculosis or mycoses, predisposing conditions |
| **Latent tuberculosis threshold** (5.1) | **Consider an induration ≥5 mm a positive tuberculin skin test, even after BCG vaccination.** Consider anti-tuberculosis therapy before starting in a patient with past latent or active tuberculosis whose adequate treatment cannot be confirmed, and in a test-negative patient with risk factors. Consult a tuberculosis expert |
| **Invasive fungal infection** (5.1) | In endemic regions, suspect it in any serious systemic illness and **consider empiric antifungal therapy while the workup proceeds** — **histoplasmosis antigen and antibody testing may be negative in active infection** |
| **Malignancy** (5.2) | **Hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma (HSTCL)** — rare, aggressive, usually fatal; the majority of reported TNF-blocker cases were **adolescent and young adult males with Crohn's disease or ulcerative colitis**, and **almost all had received azathioprine and/or 6-mercaptopurine** concomitantly or earlier. Weigh the combination carefully. Melanoma and Merkel cell carcinoma reported → **periodic skin examination** |
| **Heart failure** (5.3) | New-onset and worsening congestive heart failure reported. Not formally studied in heart failure; another TNF blocker showed worsening failure and increased mortality. Use caution and monitor |
| **Hypersensitivity** (5.4) | Angioedema, anaphylaxis, dyspnea, hypotension, rash, serum sickness, urticaria — **some after the first dose**. **Discontinue** and treat. No data on use after a severe reaction to a different TNF blocker |
| **Hepatitis B reactivation** (5.5) | **Test for hepatitis B virus (HBV) before starting.** Positive → consult a hepatitis B expert. Carriers: monitor for clinical and laboratory signs of active infection **throughout therapy and for several months after**. Reactivation → **stop certolizumab and start antiviral therapy**; safety of resuming is unknown |
| **Neurologic** (5.6) | New-onset or exacerbated central demyelinating disease incl. multiple sclerosis, and peripheral demyelinating disease incl. Guillain-Barré. Caution with pre-existing or recent-onset demyelinating disorders |
| **Hematologic** (5.7) | Rare pancytopenia incl. aplastic anemia with TNF blockers; cytopenias infrequently with certolizumab. Advise patients to seek care for **persistent fever, bruising, bleeding, pallor**; **consider discontinuation** for confirmed significant abnormalities |
| **Other biologics** (5.8) | **Not recommended** with anakinra, abatacept, rituximab or natalizumab — increased serious infection, no added benefit |
| **Autoimmunity** (5.9) | Autoantibody formation; rarely a **lupus-like syndrome** → **discontinue** if suggestive symptoms develop |
| **Immunizations** (5.10) | **Avoid live vaccines** during or immediately before therapy. **Update immunizations before starting.** Concomitant methotrexate lowered humoral vaccine response |

**Drug interactions (§7)**

- **§7.1** — see 5.8: anakinra, abatacept, rituximab, natalizumab all not recommended.
- **§7.2** — avoid **live (including attenuated) vaccines**.
- ⚠ **§7.3 Laboratory tests — erroneously elevated aPTT.** Certolizumab interferes with certain coagulation assays, producing **falsely prolonged activated partial thromboplastin time in patients with no coagulation abnormality**. Documented with the **PTT–Lupus Anticoagulant (LA)** and **STA-PTT Automate** tests (Diagnostica Stago) and **HemosIL APTT-SP liquid** and **HemosIL lyophilized silica** (Instrumentation Laboratories); other aPTT assays may also be affected. **Thrombin time and prothrombin time are not affected, and there is no evidence of any effect on coagulation *in vivo*.**

**Pregnancy and lactation (§8.1–8.2)**

- **Placental transfer is negligible in most infants and low in the rest.** In third-trimester studies, with the last dose a mean 19 days before delivery (range 5–42 d), cord blood ranged from not measurable to **1.66 mcg/mL** and infant blood to **1.58 mcg/mL**, against maternal **1.87–59.57 mcg/mL** — **infant concentrations at least 75% lower than maternal**.
- **No dosage adjustment is required in pregnancy** — plasma concentrations throughout pregnancy and to ≥12 weeks postpartum stayed within the non-pregnant range for the same diseases.
- **Disease activity is itself the risk:** active rheumatoid arthritis or Crohn's disease in pregnancy correlates with **fetal loss, preterm delivery (<37 wk), low birth weight (<2500 g) and small-for-gestational-age** birth.
- **The infant's vaccines are the open question** — the safety of **live or live-attenuated vaccines in an in-utero–exposed infant is unknown**, and one infant's level fell only from 1.02 to 0.84 mcg/mL over 4 weeks, suggesting **slower elimination in infants than adults**.
- **Lactation:** minimal concentrations in breast milk in 17 lactating women (200 mg q2wk or 400 mg q4wk), no serious reactions in their infants, and **undetectable plasma levels in 9 breastfed infants at 4 weeks postpartum**.
- A **pregnancy exposure registry** exists (MotherToBaby / OTIS AutoImmune Diseases Study).

**Adverse reactions in Crohn's disease specifically (§6.1)**

- Controlled trials: **620** patients on 400 mg vs **614** placebo; 1,564 exposed overall.
- **Serious adverse reactions 10% vs 9%** placebo. **Discontinuation for adverse reactions 8% vs 7%.**
- Reactions most often **leading to discontinuation**, each 0.4%: **abdominal pain** (0.2% placebo), **diarrhea** (0% placebo), **intestinal obstruction** (0% placebo).
- Most common (≥5% and above placebo): upper respiratory infection **20% vs 13%**, urinary tract infection **7% vs 6%**, arthralgia **6% vs 4%**.
- Pooled across all adult indications, most common (≥8%): upper respiratory infection 18%, rash 9%, urinary tract infection 8%.
- Malignancy rate (excluding non-melanoma skin cancer) **0.5 (95% CI 0.4–0.7) per 100 patient-years** in 4,650 treated vs **0.6 (0.1–1.7)** in 1,319 placebo. **One lymphoma among 2,657** certolizumab-treated Crohn's/investigational patients vs one Hodgkin's among 1,319 placebo. The label states the control group is too small and the controlled periods too short to draw firm conclusions.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **No contradiction with the guidelines; the label fills their silence.** [[acg-2025-crohns]] and [[aga-2021-crohns-pharm]] both discuss certolizumab qualitatively and neither prints a schedule, which is exactly why the wiki's figure carried a caveat. The label's regimen matches what the wiki had.
- **The maintenance conditional is new and is a real restriction.** The label's maintenance dose applies **only to patients who obtained a clinical response** to the three induction doses. The wiki's dosing table listed 400 mg q4wk unconditionally.
- **The label has no ulcerative colitis indication**, which agrees with [[acg-2025-uc]] not recommending certolizumab for UC and with [[aga-2021-crohns-pharm]] Rec 10B's comment that it may not be effective for induction of fistula remission. The label is silent on perianal fistulizing disease altogether — it does not support or contradict that recommendation.
- **Open:** the label gives **no renal or hepatic dose adjustment and no therapeutic drug monitoring guidance**. Neither is answerable from any ingested source for this agent.
- **Open:** whether live or live-attenuated vaccines are safe in an infant exposed in utero — the label states outright that this is unknown.
