## Summary

ASGE Standards of Practice guideline on **subepithelial lesions (SELs)** — tumors originating from the muscularis mucosa, submucosa, or muscularis propria. SELs are most common in the stomach (~1 in 300 endoscopies), usually small (<2 cm) and incidental; <15% are malignant at presentation. Management centers on diagnosis and determination of malignant potential. **EUS is the most accurate imaging test**, delineating the 5 wall layers (1st/2nd = mucosa incl. muscularis mucosa; 3rd = submucosa; 4th = muscularis propria; 5th = serosa/adventitia), distinguishing intramural from extramural lesions (up to 30% of suspected SELs are extramural), and superior to CT/MRI for small (<2 cm) lesions. High-frequency (20–30 MHz) miniprobes improve subcentimeter and proximal-colon assessment.

The document characterizes each lesion type by endoscopic and EUS appearance (Table 2): **GIST** (4th layer, hypoechoic), **leiomyoma** (esophageal, 2nd/3rd/4th), **lipoma** (3rd layer, hyperechoic, pillow sign 98% specific), **GI neuroendocrine neoplasms** (carcinoid; 2nd/3rd), **granular cell tumor** (2nd/3rd, S-100+), **pancreatic rest** (antral, umbilicated), **duplication cyst** (anechoic), and others (varices, glomus tumor, lymphangioma, inflammatory fibroid polyp, Brunner's gland hyperplasia, metastasis, lymphoma). GISTs originate from interstitial cells of Cajal, stain CD117/DOG1+, and have malignant potential stratified by size, mitotic rate, and location (gastric vs small-bowel tables). EUS-FNA cannot reliably assess mitotic rate, so final malignant potential is determined by surgical pathology.

Tissue acquisition is challenging because SELs lie deep to the epithelium. Techniques: standard biopsy (low yield), bite-on-bite (30–40%), jumbo biopsy, unroofing, single-incision needle-knife (SINK, 93% yield), and EUS-FNA/FNB (FNB ≥ FNA for immunohistochemistry). Endoscopic resection/ligation and tunneling techniques include ESMR (ESMR-C, ESMR-L), ESD, submucosal tunneling endoscopic resection (STER), and endoscopic full-thickness resection (EFTR). Surgery is indicated for symptomatic or malignant lesions and those with metastatic risk; asymptomatic benign lesions (lipoma, vascular, cysts, pancreatic rests, leiomyomas) need no intervention or follow-up. Recommendations graded by GRADE evidence quality.

## All Recommendations (Verbatim, with GRADE Evidence Quality)

Evidence-quality symbols (GRADE): 4444 = High; 444B = Moderate; 44BB = Low; 4BBB = Very low. Recommendation strength: "we recommend" = stronger; "we suggest" = weaker.

1. We suggest that EUS be used to further characterize indeterminate SELs. **(Moderate — 444B)**
2. We suggest surveillance EUS for gastric GI stromal tumors <2 cm in size. **(Low — 44BB)**
3. We recommend surgery for gastric and colorectal GI stromal tumors >2 cm in size and those with high-risk features. **(Moderate — 444B)**
4. We recommend that rectal GI neuroendocrine neoplasms <1 cm in size may be managed by local endoscopic or transanal excision. **(Moderate — 444B)**
5. We suggest EUS for staging of rectal GI neuroendocrine neoplasms >1 cm. Endoscopic or transanal excision may be considered for rectal tumors 1 to 2 cm in diameter that do not invade the muscularis propria. **(Low — 44BB)**
6. We recommend surgical resection for GI neuroendocrine neoplasms identified in the jejunum and ileum. **(Moderate — 444B)**
7. We recommend that asymptomatic leiomyomas do not require endoscopic surveillance or therapy unless symptomatic. **(Moderate — 444B)**
8. We recommend that GI lipomas do not require follow-up or therapy unless symptomatic. **(Moderate — 444B)**
9. We suggest that lesions arising from the muscularis propria be sampled with FNA or fine-needle biopsy for histologic evaluation. **(Low — 44BB)**
10. We suggest that a firm, round subepithelial lesion with central umbilication along the greater curve of the antrum of the stomach be considered diagnostic for a pancreatic rest. Further investigation with EUS and follow-up is not required. **(Low — 44BB)**
11. We suggest that lesions with malignant potential requiring treatment can be removed either endoscopically or surgically based on the type of lesion, size, location, patient preference, and available expertise. **(Low — 44BB)**

## Key Findings / Claims

- **EUS layers:** 1st/2nd = mucosa incl. muscularis mucosa; 3rd = submucosa; 4th = muscularis propria; 5th = serosa/adventitia. EUS distinguishes intramural vs extramural (up to 30% extramural). Low-frequency EUS (<10 MHz) cannot reliably see the muscularis mucosa; 20–30 MHz miniprobes improve subcentimeter and proximal-colon imaging.
- **GIST:** most common GI mesenchymal neoplasm (~4.3/million/yr US); arise from interstitial cells of Cajal; stomach 60–70%, small intestine 20–30%, colon 5%, esophagus <5%. Spindle (70%)/epithelioid (20%)/mixed (10%); CD117+ in 95%, DOG1+ (useful when CD117−). EUS-FNA cannot reliably assess mitotic rate → malignant potential determined by surgical pathology. Small-intestine GISTs more aggressive (40–50% malignant vs 20–25% gastric). EUS malignant features (inconsistent across studies): size >3 cm, irregular margins; less consistent — echogenic foci, cystic spaces, heterogeneity, ulceration.
- **Gastric GIST malignant-potential table (size × mitotic rate; metastasis/mortality):** see [[gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor]] and [[subepithelial-lesion]].
- **Leiomyoma:** benign smooth-muscle tumor, mostly esophageal (90% in lower/middle third), from muscularis mucosa or propria; CD117−/CD34−, desmin+/SMA+ (distinguishes from GIST). Resect only if symptomatic.
- **Lipoma:** colon and gastric antrum; yellow, soft, pillow sign 98% specific (low sensitivity); homogeneous hyperechoic from submucosa. No tissue/surveillance needed unless symptomatic; negligible malignant potential.
- **GI neuroendocrine neoplasms (carcinoids):** originate from mucosa, penetrate submucosa; standard mucosal biopsy usually diagnostic. **Gastric types:** Type I (multifocal, chronic atrophic gastritis/hypergastrinemia/pernicious anemia, low metastatic potential); Type II (Zollinger-Ellison/MEN-1, intermediate; nodal mets 10–30% if >2 cm); Type III (sporadic, often metastatic, 5-yr survival <50%, manage surgically). EMR for type I/II lesions <2 cm; lesions <1 cm may not need removal.
- **Rectal NET:** size-based — <1 cm/submucosal/no LVI: local endoscopic or transanal excision (5-yr survival 98.9–100%); 1–2 cm: EUS then endoscopic/transanal excision if no MP invasion; >2 cm: surgery with lymph node dissection (up to 80% nodal disease). Nodal-metastasis incidence for 11–19 mm tumors 17–81%. Proposed surveillance: EUS at 3 months then every 6 months for 3 years (NCCN: no follow-up for fully resected <1 cm).
- **Duodenal NET:** managed like rectal carcinoids; <1 cm well-differentiated → endoscopic polypectomy/EMR; refer for surgery if angioinvasion, MP invasion, or grade 2/3. Nodal risk 2% (<1 cm), 4.7% (1–2 cm), 20% (>2 cm).
- **Jejunal/ileal NET:** surgical resection recommended (mid-gut metastatic disease incurable); follow-up ≥7 years.
- **Pancreatic rest:** heterotopic pancreas, greater-curve antrum, central umbilication; firm SEL with umbilication considered diagnostic — EUS not required. EUS (when done): heterogeneous/hypoechoic, any layer.
- **Granular cell tumor:** nerve-sheath (Schwann cell) tumor, mostly esophageal; "submucosal pill," grayish-yellow; S-100+; mucosal/submucosal, hypoechoic homogeneous. Malignant transformation rare (>4 cm). Subcentimeter lesions safe to surveil; endoscopic resection (snare/EMR/ESD) may obviate surveillance.
- **Duplication cyst:** congenital, GI-epithelium-lined; anechoic, 3–5 layer wall, submucosal or extrinsic; avoid FNA when clearly anechoic (infection risk; use antibiotic prophylaxis if FNA needed). No surveillance data; resect only if symptomatic.
- **Other SELs:** metastasis (stomach body/fundus, central depression, biopsy diagnostic >90%); varices (anechoic, Doppler+, can occur in duodenum/rectum); glomus tumor (antrum, MP, hypervascular, actin+/vimentin+, CD117−); inflammatory fibroid polyp; Brunner's gland hyperplasia; lymphangioma; lymphoma.
- **Tissue acquisition yields:** standard pinch biopsy rarely sufficient; bite-on-bite 30–40%; jumbo forceps higher yield for submucosal lesions but bleeding 35.7% (34.9% needed hemostasis); SINK 93%; ESMR 87% (vs bite-on-bite 17%). EUS-FNA accuracy for GIST 46–93%; FNB (22G core) yield 75% vs FNA 20% — FNB salvages nondiagnostic FNA. FNA accuracy plateaus after the 4th pass; stylet, suction, fanning, and ROSE have unproven incremental benefit. Partial-resection/jumbo techniques risk perilesional fibrosis complicating later resection.
- **Endoscopic resection:** ESMR (≤20 mm, mucosa/submucosa; ESMR-C cap, ESMR-L ligation — bleeding up to 9%; caution in duodenum and for MP lesions). ESD for MP lesions (gastric MP ESD complete resection 92%, perforation 14% — all managed endoscopically); limited for >5 cm (perforation up to 19%). STER (mucosal incision ≥5 cm proximal; preserves mucosa; en bloc 78–100%, adverse events 9.4–33%). EFTR for MP-infiltrating lesions up to 4 cm; requires secure defect closure.
- **Management algorithm (Fig 3):** EUS triages extramural vs intramural; hyperechoic + pillow sign → lipoma (observe); anechoic Doppler+ → varix; anechoic Doppler− → cyst; 2nd/3rd-layer hypoechoic → tissue (EMR/EUS-FNA/FNB/bite-on-bite/unroofing/jumbo/SINK), treat by histology; 4th-layer ≥2 cm or symptomatic/suspicious → ESD/STER/EFTR vs surgery; <2 cm asymptomatic → surveillance 3–12 months.
