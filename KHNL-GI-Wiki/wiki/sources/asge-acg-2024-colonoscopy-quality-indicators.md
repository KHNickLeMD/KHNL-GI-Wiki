## Summary

The **canonical colonoscopy quality-indicator set** for US practice, from the joint American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) / American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) Task Force on Quality in Endoscopy. It is the third edition, superseding the task force's **2006** and **2015** documents, and reflects evidence published since 2015 (PubMed search January 2014 – December 2022). It defines **15 numbered indicators**, each with a **strength of recommendation**, a **measure type** (process or outcome) and a **numeric performance target** — and names **5 of them as priority indicators** whose measurement is "considered essential".

Three of the headline benchmarks moved, and they moved **upward**, which is the single most consequential thing in the document for anyone auditing against an older set: **adenoma detection rate (ADR) ≥35%** (was 25% in 2015-era guidance), **average withdrawal time ≥8 minutes** (was 6), and a **new serrated indicator, sessile serrated lesion detection rate (SSLDR) ≥6%**. **Cecal intubation rose to ≥95%.** A unit passing an audit against the 2015 or [[aga-2021-colonoscopy-quality|AGA 2021]] numbers can fail this one.

The **ADR denominator also changed, and the change is larger than the threshold change.** ADR was confined to *screening* examinations in 2006; this update **expands it to screening, surveillance *and* diagnostic colonoscopy in patients aged ≥45 years**, with four exclusions (positive non-colonoscopy screening tests, genetic cancer syndromes, inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), and colonoscopy for therapy of known neoplasms). It also settles two questions explicitly: **no threshold adjustment is needed for including 45–49-year-olds**, and **second and subsequent screening colonoscopies count without adjustment**. Any ADR computed on the old screening-only denominator is not comparable to the ≥35% target.

The document adds two **resection** indicators (documentation of lesion size/shape/location/method; **cold snare for 4–9 mm lesions, ≥90%**, its only 1A-graded indicator) and, for the first time, three **IBD-specific** indicators requiring a formal endoscopic activity score to be recorded. It also prints **Table 13**, a synthesized surveillance-interval grid for ulcerative colitis (UC) / indeterminate colitis after a dysplasia-negative examination, reconciling four prior guidelines.

Two framing statements matter for how the set is used. The task force states that, given the impact of inadequate performance on cancer development, **"failure to measure performance is unacceptable"**. And it states the opposite guardrail just as plainly: the indicators and targets **do not represent the standard of care, credentialing requirements or training standards, and applying them that way is a misuse of the document**.

## Key Findings / Claims

### The 15 indicators

All numbering, grades, measure types and targets are the document's own (Table 3). Grading framework is the task force's own 8-level scheme (Table 5), **not GRADE** — see below. Targets are minimums ("≥") unless stated.

| # | Indicator | Strength | Type | Target |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Colonoscopy performed for an **appropriate indication**, and the indication documented | **1C+** | Process | **≥95%** |
| **2** | ⭐ **Bowel preparation adequacy rate** | **1C** | Process | **≥90%** |
| **3** | ⭐ **Cecal intubation rate** | **1C** | Process | **≥95%** |
| **4** | ⭐ **Adenoma detection rate (ADR)** | **1C+** | Outcome | **≥35%** |
| **5** | ADR in patients with a **positive fecal screening test** (fecal blood or multitarget stool DNA, mt-sDNA) | **1C** | Outcome | **≥50%** |
| **6** | **Adenomas per colonoscopy (APC)** | **2C** | Outcome | **≥0.6** |
| **7** | ⭐ **Sessile serrated lesion detection rate (SSLDR)** | **2C** | Outcome | **≥6%** |
| **8** | **Average withdrawal time** in normal colonoscopies | **2A** | Process | **≥8 min** |
| **9** | Polyp resections documenting **size, shape, location and method of resection** | **3** | Process | **≥98%** |
| **10** | **4–9 mm lesions resected with a cold snare** | **1A** | Process | **≥90%** |
| **11** | ⭐ **Use of appropriate screening and surveillance intervals** | **1C** | Process | **≥90%** |
| **12** | **Serious adverse events** (perforation, postpolypectomy bleeding, mortality) tracked, documented and **reviewed by a quality-improvement committee** | **1C** | Process | **≥95%** |
| **13** | **UC:** formal assessment of **disease extent and activity** recorded (Mayo Endoscopic Score [MES], Modified MES, Ulcerative Colitis Endoscopic Index of Severity [UCEIS], or Ulcerative Colitis Colonoscopic Index of Severity) | **3** | Process | **≥90%** |
| **14** | **Crohn's disease (CD):** formal **endoscopic activity score** reported (Crohn's Disease Endoscopic Index of Severity [CDEIS], Simple Endoscopic Score for Crohn's Disease [SES-CD], or Rutgeerts score) | **3** | Process | **≥90%** |
| **15** | Appropriate **follow-up surveillance interval** recommended after dysplasia screening in UC / indeterminate colitis **with no dysplasia detected** | **3** | Process | **≥90%** |

⭐ = **priority indicator** (Table 2): ADR, SSLDR, use of recommended screening/surveillance intervals, bowel preparation adequacy rate, cecal intubation rate. **Measurement of the priority indicators is considered essential.** **SSLDR and bowel preparation adequacy rate are new to the priority list** in this edition.

- **Cecal intubation carries an explicit measurement carve-out:** it should be measured for all colonoscopists, but **may be measured intermittently or not at all** where consistent high-level performance has been demonstrated — justified by limited evidence of variable performance, evidence that high performance is sustained, and the ceiling effect at 100%.

### Definitions that decide whether your number is comparable

- **Indicator 2 — adequate bowel preparation**, preferably defined as a **Boston Bowel Preparation Scale (BBPS) segment score ≥2 in each of the 3 colon segments**, *or* a description of the preparation as **excellent, good or adequate**. The recommended screening/surveillance interval must be consistent with US Multi-Society Task Force (MSTF) recommendations.
- **Indicator 3 — cecal intubation:** patients **with intact colons** who have full intubation of the cecum, **with photodocumentation of cecal landmarks**.
- **Indicator 4 — ADR denominator:** patients **aged ≥45 y** having colonoscopy for **screening, surveillance *or* diagnostic** indications **other than** positive non-colonoscopy screening tests (e.g. fecal tests, CT colonography). Numerator: **≥1 conventional adenoma detected and verified by pathology**. **Excluded from the calculation:** positive non-colonoscopy screening tests, genetic cancer syndromes (e.g. polyposis), IBD, and colonoscopy for therapy of known neoplasms.
  - **No adjustment to the minimum acceptable threshold** for including **45–49-year-olds** — adenoma prevalence at 45–49 is only slightly below 50–54, mean ADRs have been rising, and that age group is still a small share of volume.
  - **Second and subsequent screening colonoscopies are included without adjustment** — adenoma incidence at a 10-year repeat screening examination is only slightly below that at an initial one, despite 10 years of ageing.
- **Indicators 6, 7, 8 use the same ≥45 y denominator and the same four exclusions** as indicator 4.
- **Indicator 5:** the **same ≥50% threshold applies to mt-sDNA-positive and fecal immunochemical test (FIT)-positive patients.**
- **Indicator 8 — withdrawal time:** average, in **normal** colonoscopies **without biopsy sampling or polypectomy**, measured appendiceal orifice → completion of rectal retroflexion.

### Why the thresholds moved

- **ADR ≥35%**: ADR is "the most clinically relevant and best validated quality indicator in colonoscopy", proposed in 2002 and validated as a predictor of post-colonoscopy colorectal cancer (PCCRC).
- **APC was added because ADR has a structural blind spot** — ADR does not reward detection of additional adenomas once the first is found, and many patients have several. ADR and APC correlate highly, **but not always**: in one study **47.6% of endoscopists in the lowest APC quartile had ADRs ≥25%**. Higher APC is also associated with lower PCCRC risk.
- **Withdrawal time ≥8 min**: the 2002 recommendation was an average of 6–10 min; 2006 set 6 min off a single landmark private-practice study. Evidence since 2015 shows **optimal detection typically needs an average of at least 8–9 minutes** (Table 11), including retrospective data that **PCCRC prevention is optimized at ≥8–9 min**. High-quality inspection — cleaning retained pools, systematically exposing mucosa behind every fold, flexure and valve, adequate distention, intraprocedural cleansing — simply takes that long.
- **SSLDR ≥6%** was adopted after extended debate, accepting the measurement burden because of the cancer-prevention evidence. Three caveats the task force names: **substantial interobserver variation among pathologists** distinguishing sessile serrated lesions from hyperplastic polyps (not a problem for conventional adenomas); an ideal serrated indicator should **not reward resecting diminutive distal hyperplastic polyps**; and any indicator relying on endoscopist-reported location **could be gamed**. Alternative candidates considered (Table 10): **clinically significant serrated detection rate, benchmark 9%**, and **proximal serrated polyp detection rate, benchmark 17%** — each associated with the lowest PCCRC risk at that level.

> **The 8-minute rule is a benchmark for an average, not a stopwatch for each case.** The document states the ≥8–9 min recommendation "should not be translated to mean that best practice or the standard of medical care requires that every withdrawal time should last 8 minutes."

- **But the converse is actionable:** an **ADR and/or SSLDR below threshold accompanied by an average withdrawal time <8 minutes "could be construed as evidence of an inadequate withdrawal technique"** → evaluate and correct the inspection technique.

### Resection indicators

- **Indicator 9 (≥98%, grade 3):** report **size, shape, location by segment** (or distance in cm from the anus for left-sided polyps) **and method of removal** for all polyps.
  - **Shape:** the **Paris classification** is recommended; "sessile", "flat" and "pedunculated" are also acceptable.
  - **Grouping rules:** polyps in the same segment may be recorded as a range (e.g. "4 flat and sessile polyps 2–4 mm removed by cold snare). **Polyps ≥10 mm must be described individually**; several ≥10 mm from the same segment removed by the same method with similar predicted histology may be grouped. **Any lesion of any size suggesting advanced histology or endoscopic features of cancer (submucosal or deeper invasion) by morphology or pit pattern must be submitted individually**, never bottled with polyps from other segments.
  - **Sizing technique:** open biopsy forceps over and against the polyp; for smaller polyps the **snare sheath tip (2.4 mm diameter)** against the polyp base.
- **Indicator 10 (≥90%, grade 1A — the document's only 1A):** **4–9 mm lesions resected with a cold snare.**

### Intervals, adverse events, IBD

- **Indicator 11 (≥90%):** adherence to recommended post-polypectomy and post-cancer-resection surveillance intervals, **and** to the **10-year interval between screening colonoscopies** in average-risk patients with negative results and adequate cleansing. **Shorter screening intervals are appropriate only for inadequate bowel preparation.** Clinical decision support tools improve guideline compliance.
- **Indicator 12 (≥95%):** serious adverse events — the document lists **perforation, postpolypectomy bleeding, sedation-related cardiovascular events, unplanned hospitalization and mortality** — tracked, documented and reviewed by a quality-improvement committee. Serious adverse events are **under 1%** across large integrated health systems.
- **Indicators 13–14 — why a score, not a description:** symptoms often do not correlate with endoscopic findings, so objective endoscopic activity markers are needed; extent and severity drive **disease-severity classification, prognosis, choice of medical therapy, and colorectal neoplasia risk**. Extent may be reported by involved segments or by the **Montreal classification** (**E1** rectum only, **E2** left-sided, **E3** proximal to the splenic flexure).
  - **Endoscopic healing targets cited from STRIDE-II:** in UC, **MES 0 and UCEIS ≤1**; in CD, **SES-CD <3 points or an SES-CD ulceration subscore of 0**.
  - **MES** is easily implemented with good agreement; **UCCIS** final scoring is complex and may not be practical. **SES-CD is increasingly favoured** over CDEIS for simplicity. **Rutgeerts** grades neoterminal ileal lesions after resection on a 5-point scale (**i0** no lesions · **i1** ≤5 aphthous lesions · **i2** >5 aphthous lesions with normal mucosa between, or lesions confined to the ileocolonic anastomosis · **i3** diffuse aphthous ileitis with diffusely inflamed mucosa · **i4** diffuse inflammation with large ulcers, nodules and/or narrowing).
- **Indicator 15 (≥90%) — Table 13, timing of the next colonoscopy in UC / indeterminate colitis with no dysplasia on the current examination.** A synthesis of four guidelines/updates; assumes a complete examination with adequate preparation.

| 1 year | 2–3 years | 3–5 years |
|---|---|---|
| Primary sclerosing cholangitis (**including after liver transplant**) | Prior **resected visible** dysplasia <5 y ago | **Mucosal healing** on the current examination **and 2 examinations without dysplasia** |
| Family history of colorectal cancer in a **first-degree relative aged <50 y** | Family history of colorectal cancer (but no first-degree relative aged <50 y) | Overall disease extent affecting **one-third or less** of the colon |
| Prior **invisible** dysplasia <5 y ago | **Mildly** active inflammation | |
| Active inflammation **more than mild** | Pseudopolyps (**but not dense**) | |
| **Dense** pseudopolyps | | |
| **Colonic stricture** | | |

- **When to start IBD dysplasia surveillance:** after **8 years of disease**; in IBD with concomitant **primary sclerosing cholangitis, at the time of the PSC diagnosis**. The task force notes evidence for clinical or mortality benefit is **limited and of low quality**.
- **Technique for dysplasia surveillance:** high-definition colonoscopy **complemented with one modality of chromoendoscopy** and targeted biopsy sampling of suspicious areas; whether dye-spray or electronic chromoendoscopy adds benefit over high definition alone is **uncertain**.
- ⚠ **Table 13 reconciles a real disagreement:** the **ACG guideline caps the interval at 3 years**, while the three other documents allow **up to 5 years**. Table 13's 3–5 y column follows the latter.

### Interventions to raise ADR (Table 14)

Physician report cards · **public reporting of ADR** · water assistance · second look (cecal retroflexion or a second forward look in the proximal colon) · dynamic change in patient position · high-definition endoscopes · distal attachment devices · enhanced imaging (narrow-band imaging, i-SCAN, linked-color imaging, blue-laser imaging, chromoendoscopy, methylene blue-MMX) · computer-aided detection · **split-dose bowel preparation** · a nurse assigned to observe the monitor · focused educational interventions · leadership training in colonoscopy technique.

- **Split-dose or same-day preparation raised ADR in randomized trials.**
- **Computer-aided detection:** most randomized studies show significant ADR improvement and reduced miss rates, but **several clinical-practice assessments have been negative** and generalizability to routine practice "remains to be demonstrated convincingly".
- **Last resort:** the document states that **removal of colonoscopy privileges may be appropriate** if satisfactory performance cannot be achieved.

### Methodology and grading — read the labels literally

- **Grading is the task force's own framework (Table 5), not GRADE.** Eight levels, keyed to clarity of benefit plus methodologic strength: **1A** (clear benefit, randomized trials without important limitations — strong, applies to most settings) · **1B** (randomized trials with important limitations) · **1C+** (clear benefit, **overwhelming evidence from observational studies** — strong) · **1C** (clear benefit, observational studies — **intermediate strength**, may change) · **2A** (benefit unclear, randomized trials without important limitations — intermediate strength) · **2B** (weak) · **2C** (**very weak**) · **3** (**expert opinion only** — weak, likely to change).
- Indicators are grouped by **preprocedure / intraprocedure / postprocedure** and classified **outcome** or **process**. Process measures are used as surrogates where outcomes need large datasets or long follow-up.
- **Performance targets were set from published benchmarking data.** Where data were absent and expert consensus held that failing the indicator would be a **"never event"** (e.g. failure to monitor vital signs during sedation), the target is expressed as **>98%**.
- A companion document covers **unit-level structural measures**, and another covers **indicators common to all GI endoscopic procedures**, reprinted here as Table 4 (Elmunzer BJ et al. *Gastrointest Endosc* 2024;100:382–94).

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- ⚠ **ADR: ≥35% here vs ≥30% in [[aga-2021-colonoscopy-quality|AGA 2021]] vs "<25% → remedial training" in [[acg-2021-crc-screening|ACG 2021]] vs ≥30% men / ≥20% women in [[usmstf-2020-followup-colonoscopy|USMSTF 2020]].** The 2024 document governs. It also **does not use sex-specific ADR thresholds** at all, where AGA 2021 allowed them for markedly sex-skewed practices and USMSTF 2020 split by sex outright.
- ⚠ **The denominators differ even where the numbers look similar.** This document's ADR includes **surveillance and diagnostic** examinations in patients **≥45 y**; AGA 2021's includes **screening only, ages 50–75**. Two endoscopists quoting "my ADR is 33%" off those two definitions are not describing the same thing. This is the more dangerous of the two discrepancies because it is silent.
- ⚠ **Withdrawal time: ≥8 min here vs ≥6 min (aspirational ≥9) in AGA 2021 and ≥6 min in ACG 2021.** The 2024 document governs; it treats 6 minutes as a 2006 figure superseded by post-2015 evidence.
- ⚠ **SSLDR ≥6% here vs SDR ≥7% (aspirational ≥10%) in AGA 2021** — and the 2024 target is the **lower** of the two, which is unusual among these changes. The two documents also differ on priority: AGA 2021 says that if only one of ADR/SDR can be collected, **prioritize ADR**; the 2024 document makes **both** priority indicators.
- ⚠ **Cecal intubation ≥95% here, against the same two numbers the wiki already flagged as denominator-dependent** (AGA 2021: ≥90% of screening + surveillance, ≥95% excluding inadequately prepped exams; ACG 2021: ≥95% in screening subjects). This document's denominator is **patients with intact colons**, which is a third definition — but it is the current one, and it requires photodocumentation.
- ⚠ **IBD surveillance interval ceiling, 3 vs 5 years** — explicitly surfaced *by* this document: ACG caps at 3 y, three other guidelines allow 5 y, and Table 13 follows the 5-y option. Relevant to [[ulcerative-colitis]] and [[colonoscopy-surveillance]].
- **No aspirational targets.** AGA 2021 pairs each goal with an aspirational figure; this document gives a single minimum per indicator. Nothing is lost clinically, but the page can no longer print an "aspirational" column sourced to the current set.
- **The document declines to be used as a credentialing or standard-of-care instrument.** Quoting a target in that context is a stated misuse — worth remembering wherever the wiki prints these numbers.
