## Summary

Consensus update setting post-[[colonoscopy]] and post-[[polypectomy]] surveillance intervals for **average-risk** adults, stratified by the number, size, and histology of lesions at a **high-quality baseline examination**. Built from a PubMed/Embase/CINAHL search of English-language articles published since January 1, 2012 (1,904 unique articles screened; literature search run March 30, 2017, supplemented through September 2018).

The document is organised around three interval grids: Table 4 (normal colonoscopy and conventional adenomas), Table 5 (serrated polyps), and Table 7 (second surveillance, stratified by findings at **both** baseline and first surveillance). **The full grids live on [[colonoscopy-surveillance]]**; this page carries the recommendation statements as published, with their strength and evidence grade.

The headline shifts from 2012 are a longer interval after 1–2 small tubular adenomas (7–10 y rather than 5–10 y), a far more granular serrated-polyp grid, an explicit 1-year interval after >10 adenomas (replacing "<3 y"), and an option of 3–5 y rather than a strict 3 y after 3–4 small adenomas. Evidence underpinning the serrated-polyp grid is almost entirely **very low quality**, and every serrated recommendation except two is **Weak**.

## Grading Scheme

**USMSTF 2020 numbers none of its recommendations.** Each appears as a bolded statement in the text, followed in parentheses by a **strength of recommendation** (Strong or Weak) and a **quality of evidence** rating; Tables 4 and 5 repeat both in dedicated columns. Evidence ratings use the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation (GRADE) definitions reproduced in Table 2:

| Rating of evidence | Definition |
|---|---|
| A: High quality | Further research is very unlikely to change our confidence in the estimate of effect |
| B: Moderate quality | Further research is likely to have an important impact on our confidence in the estimate of effect and may change the estimate |
| C: Low quality | Further research is very likely to have an important impact on our confidence in the estimate of effect and is likely to change the estimate |
| D: Very low quality | Any estimate of effect is very uncertain |

- **Strong recommendation:** most informed patients would choose the recommended management, and clinicians can structure their interactions with patients accordingly.
- **Weak recommendation:** patients' choices will vary according to their values and preferences, and clinicians must ensure that patients' care is in keeping with their values and preferences.
- Statements summarising the evidence base (as opposed to directing management) carry a quality rating alone, or the label **Insufficient evidence**.

## Scope and Exclusions

Recommendations are for **average-risk** adults. They **do not apply** to individuals with:

- A hereditary colorectal cancer (CRC) syndrome (e.g. [[lynch-syndrome]], [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis|familial adenomatous polyposis]]) or a personal history of a hereditary cancer syndrome
- Personal history of [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease]]
- [[serrated-polyposis-syndrome|Serrated polyposis syndrome]]
- A malignant polyp
- Personal history of CRC
- Family history of CRC or colorectal neoplasia

In those groups the recommendations are applied judiciously, **favoring the shortest indicated interval based on either history or polyp findings**. Polypectomy technique, management of the malignant polyp, and follow-up of serrated polyposis syndrome are explicitly deferred to separate Task Force documents.

A 10-year follow-up after normal colonoscopy is recommended **regardless of the indication for the colonoscopy**, except in those increased-risk groups.

## Terms and Definitions (Table 3)

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Average risk for CRC | Absence of inflammatory bowel disease, family history of CRC, hereditary syndrome associated with increased risk, serrated polyposis syndrome, personal history of CRC |
| Normal colonoscopy | A colonoscopy where no adenoma, sessile serrated polyp (SSP), traditional serrated adenoma (TSA), hyperplastic polyp (HP) ≥10 mm, or CRC is found |
| Low-risk adenoma | 1–2 nonadvanced adenomas <10 mm in size |
| Advanced adenoma | 1 or more of: adenoma ≥10 mm in size; adenoma with tubulovillous/villous histology; adenoma with high-grade dysplasia |
| Advanced neoplasia | 1 or more of: adenoma ≥10 mm; tubulovillous/villous histology; high-grade dysplasia; CRC |
| High-risk adenoma | 1 or more of: advanced neoplasia; 3 or more adenomas |
| Adequate adenoma detection rate (ADR) | ADR ≥30% in men and ≥20% in women |
| Adequate bowel preparation | Bowel preparation adequate for visualization of polyps >5 mm in size |
| Complete examination | Complete colonoscopy to cecum, with photo documentation of cecal landmarks, such as the appendiceal orifice, terminal ileum, or ileocecal valve |
| High-quality examination | Examination complete to cecum with adequate bowel preparation performed by colonoscopist with adequate adenoma detection rate and attention to complete polyp excision |

- Table 3 footnote: the Task Force proposes that, going forward, research articles **specify the individual criteria being captured** (e.g. use "1–2 adenomas <10 mm" instead of "low-risk adenoma"), because evidence supporting level of risk for various criteria is constantly evolving.
- The proportion of adenoma with villous features is **deliberately not quantified** — it is rarely reported in clinical practice, and criteria used to define villous histology are often not reported and, when reported, often variable.
- Text benchmarks in the Methods section are stated as ADR **>30% in men; >20% in women**, with >85% of examinations adequately prepared and >95% complete to cecum; Table 3 states the ADR definition with **≥**.

## Summary of Evidence Statements

Stated verbatim, with the rating as published.

| Statement | Rating |
|---|---|
| Normal colonoscopy is associated with sustained reduced risk for incident and fatal CRC. | High quality of evidence |
| Incremental effectiveness of repeat colonoscopy after baseline normal colonoscopy for further reducing CRC incidence and mortality is uncertain. | **Insufficient evidence** |
| Risk for incident and fatal CRC after baseline adenoma removal is uncertain. | Low quality of evidence |
| Surveillance colonoscopy after baseline removal of adenoma with high-risk features (eg, size ≥10 mm) may reduce risk for incident CRC, but impact on fatal CRC is uncertain. | Low quality of evidence |
| Incremental impact of surveillance colonoscopy after baseline removal of adenoma with low-risk features (such as 1–2 adenomas <10 mm) on risk for incident and fatal CRC is uncertain. | Low quality of evidence |
| Risk for incident and fatal CRC among individuals with baseline SSP is uncertain. | Very low quality of evidence |

## Recommendations

Verbatim, in document order. Strength and quality as published.

| Recommendation | Strength | Quality of evidence |
|---|---|---|
| For patients with normal, high-quality colonoscopy, repeat CRC screening in 10 years. | Strong | High |
| For patients with 1–2 tubular adenomas <10 mm in size completely removed at a high-quality examination, repeat colonoscopy in 7–10 years. | Strong | Moderate |
| For patients with 3–4 tubular adenomas <10 mm in size completely removed at a high-quality examination, repeat colonoscopy in 3–5 years. | Weak | Very low |
| For patients with 5–10 tubular adenomas <10 mm in size completely removed at a high-quality examination, repeat colonoscopy in 3 years. | Strong | Moderate |
| For patients with 1 or more adenomas ≥10 mm in size completely removed at a high-quality examination, repeat colonoscopy in 3 years. | Strong | High |
| For patients with adenoma containing villous histology completely removed at a high-quality examination, repeat colonoscopy in 3 years. | Strong | Moderate |
| For patients with adenoma containing high-grade dysplasia completely removed at high-quality examination, repeat colonoscopy in 3 years. | Strong | Moderate |
| For patients with >10 adenomas completely removed at high-quality examination, repeat colonoscopy in 1 year. | Weak | Very low |
| For patients with ≤20 HPs <10 mm in size in the rectum or sigmoid colon removed at a high-quality examination, repeat CRC screening in 10 years. | Strong | Moderate |
| For patients with ≤20 HPs <10 mm in size proximal to the sigmoid colon removed at a high-quality examination, repeat colonoscopy in 10 years. | Weak | Very low |
| For patients with 1–2 SSPs <10 mm in size completely removed at a high-quality examination, repeat colonoscopy in 5–10 years. | Weak | Very low |
| For patients with SSP ≥10 mm at a high-quality examination, repeat colonoscopy in 3 years. | Weak | Very low |
| For patients with HP ≥10 mm, repeat colonoscopy in 3–5 years. A 3-year follow-up interval is favored if concern about pathologist consistency in distinguishing SSPs from HPs, quality of bowel preparation, or complete polyp excision, whereas a 5-year interval is favored if low concerns for consistency in distinguishing between SSP and HP by the pathologist, adequate bowel preparation, and confident complete polyp excision. | Weak | Very low |
| For patients with TSA completely removed at a high-quality examination, repeat colonoscopy in 3 years. | Weak | Very low |
| For patients with 3–4 SSPs <10 mm at high-quality examination, repeat colonoscopy in 3–5 years. | Weak | Very low |
| For patients with any combination of 5–10 SSPs <10 mm at high-quality examination, repeat colonoscopy in 3 years. | Weak | Very low |
| For patients with SSP containing dysplasia at a high-quality examination, repeat colonoscopy in 3 years. | Weak | Very low |
| For patients with history of baseline adenoma removal and 1 subsequent colonoscopy, recommendations for subsequent surveillance should take into account findings at baseline and first surveillance (Table 7). | Weak | Low |
| There is insufficient evidence to recommend use of currently published prediction models for polyp surveillance recommendations. | Weak | Very low |
| Evidence is insufficient to recommend differential management for patients with proximal adenoma. | Weak | Very low |
| For patients with piecemeal resection of adenoma or SSP >20 mm, repeat colonoscopy in 6 months. | Strong | Moderate |

**Footnotes attached to the interval tables (Tables 4 and 5), which carry the operative qualifiers:**

- The 10-y follow-up after a normal examination, and after ≤20 rectosigmoid HPs <10 mm, **may be with colonoscopy or another average-risk screening modality**; every other interval is a colonoscopy.
- Patients given a recommendation **before 2020** for shorter than 7–10 y follow-up after 1–2 tubular adenomas <10 mm **may follow the original recommendation**. If feasible, physicians may re-evaluate and reasonably provide an updated 7–10 y recommendation, taking into account baseline examination quality, polyp history, and patient preferences.
- The 3-y interval after tubulovillous/villous histology, after high-grade dysplasia, and after SSP with dysplasia **assumes high confidence of complete resection**.
- Patients with >10 adenomas at one examination, **or >10 cumulative lifetime adenomas**, may need to be considered for genetic testing based on absolute/cumulative adenoma number, patient age, and other factors such as family history of CRC.
- Cumulative **>20 HPs distributed throughout the colon with ≥5 proximal to the rectum**, or **5 serrated polyps proximal to the rectum >5 mm with ≥2 being ≥10 mm**, meet criteria for [[serrated-polyposis-syndrome|serrated polyposis syndrome]] and may require specialized management — out of these tables.
- The piecemeal-resection rows cross-reference the Task Force's separate recommendations on endoscopic removal of colorectal lesions ([[usmstf-2020-endoscopic-removal]]).

The recommendation statement for piecemeal resection is written **>20 mm**, while the corresponding rows of Tables 4 and 5 are written **≥20 mm**; the document does not reconcile the two.

## Piecemeal Resection — the Full Sequence

Piecemeal removal of a large lesion is the one finding that overrides the size/number/histology grid, and the document specifies three exams, not one:

- **First surveillance at approximately 6 months**
- **Second surveillance 1 year from the first surveillance**
- **Third surveillance 3 years from the second surveillance**

Supporting data: a meta-analysis of 33 studies found recurrent neoplasia in **20% after piecemeal vs 3% after en bloc** [[endoscopic-mucosal-resection|endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR)]]. In the subgroup of 10–20 mm polyps removed by EMR, piecemeal resection carried an **18% risk** of recurrent neoplasia, similar to the 19% observed for 20–30 mm and >30 mm lesions. Incomplete resection was more common with piecemeal (20%) than en bloc (8.4%) resection, but piecemeal was not an independent predictor of incomplete resection after adjusting for size and histology. Above 20 mm, strategies for verifying complete excision should be considered.

## Key Updates Since 2012 (Table 9)

- New evidence based on risk of CRC outcomes, rather than based only on risk of advanced adenoma during surveillance, is provided to strengthen polyp surveillance recommendations
- **7- to 10-y rather than 5- to 10-y** follow-up is recommended after removal of 1–2 tubular adenomas <10 mm in size (Table 4)
- More detailed recommendations for follow-up after removal of serrated polyps have been provided (Table 5)
- Importance of high-quality baseline examination has been emphasized
- **1 y rather than <3-y** follow-up is recommended after removal of >10 adenomas
- **Option to recommend 3–5 y instead of 3-y** follow-up after removal of 3–4 adenomas <10 mm in size

## Key Findings / Claims

**Risk after a normal colonoscopy**

- Cohort of 1,251,318 average-risk adults: among 99,166 with a normal screening colonoscopy, **46% relative reduction in incident CRC** (hazard ratio [HR] 0.54; 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.31–0.94) and **88% relative reduction in fatal CRC** (HR 0.12; 95% CI 0.02–0.82) through the traditionally recommended 10-year follow-up; reduced risk was noted **out to 12 years**.
- Cohort of 304,774 individuals with normal colonoscopy vs 980,154 with no lower endoscopy: HR 0.44 (95% CI 0.38–0.52) for incident CRC, persisting from HR 0.35 at ≤3 years to 0.65 at ≥15 years; HR 0.32 (95% CI 0.24–0.45) for fatal CRC.
- Modelling of 4.3 million individuals suggested a normal colonoscopy carries a **<0.5% 10-year risk** of subsequent CRC.

**Risk after adenoma removal**

- French cohort (n = 5,779): baseline **advanced adenoma** → 2.2-fold increased CRC risk vs the general population (standardized incidence ratio [SIR] 2.23; 95% CI 1.67–2.92); baseline **nonadvanced adenoma** → *reduced* risk (SIR 0.68; 95% CI 0.44–0.99). 10-year cumulative CRC probability after advanced adenoma was **2.05% (95% CI 1.14–3.64) with** vs **6.22% (95% CI 4.26–9.02) without** subsequent surveillance colonoscopy; risk fell to general-population level with ≥1 follow-up colonoscopy (SIR 1.10; 95% CI 0.62–1.82) and was >4× higher without (SIR 4.26; 95% CI 2.89–6.04).
- Meta-analysis of 10,139 individuals across 8 studies: 5-year cumulative incidence of metachronous advanced adenoma was **3.3%** (no adenoma), **4.9%** (1–2 adenomas <10 mm), **17.1%** (advanced adenoma) — the 1–2 small adenoma group is no more than 2% above the no-adenoma group.
- Intermediate-risk cohort (n = 11,944; 1–2 adenomas ≥10 mm or 3–4 adenomas <10 mm): 43%–48% relative reduction in incident CRC with 1–2 surveillance examinations (adjusted HR 0.57 for 1 examination, 0.52 for 2).
- Cohort of 214 patients with >10 adenomas: **26.6% had metachronous advanced adenoma** at median 4.3 years.

**Risk after serrated lesions**

- New Hampshire Colonoscopy Registry (Table 6) — high-risk adenoma (HRA) at second surveillance by baseline finding: no adenoma 4.8%, low-risk adenoma 9.7%, HRA 18.2%, low-risk adenoma + SSP 18.4%, **HRA + SSP 46.4%**, SSP/SSP-with-dysplasia 2.9%, serrated polyp ≥10 mm 3.1%. Serrated polyp ≥10 mm at second surveillance: 0.7%, 0.5%, 1.0%, 8.2%, 3.6%, 9.6%, 12.3% respectively.
- Danish case-control study (2,045 CRC cases, 8,105 controls): SSP → odds ratio (OR) 3.07 (95% CI 2.30–4.10) for CRC; SSP with dysplasia → OR 4.76 (95% CI 2.59–8.73).
- Large serrated polyp with a synchronous advanced adenoma → OR 3.3 (95% CI 1.3–8.6) for incident CRC.
- TSA vs conventional adenoma: metachronous high-risk adenoma 47.3% vs 32.0% (adjusted OR 2.37; 95% CI 1.55–3.63).
- Pohl et al. reported **47% of SSPs 10–20 mm had evidence of incomplete resection** — a central reason for the conservative serrated-polyp grid.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Piecemeal size threshold stated two ways within the document** — >20 mm in the recommendation text, ≥20 mm in Tables 4 and 5.
- **ADR benchmark stated two ways within the document** — >30% men / >20% women in the Methods text, ≥30% / ≥20% in Table 3. Neither matches the single **≥30% for both sexes** of [[aga-2021-colonoscopy-quality]] or the **overall >25%** of [[usmstf-2015-crc-surveillance]]; the reconciliation table is on [[colonoscopy-quality-indicators]].
- **1–2 small adenomas** — [[aga-2021-colonoscopy-quality]] (newer, same tier) says to *consider* a 10-year interval, against this document's 7–10 y.
- Evidence for the serrated-polyp grid is **almost entirely very low quality**; every serrated recommendation is Weak except ≤20 rectosigmoid HPs <10 mm and piecemeal resection of SSP ≥20 mm.
- Whether HPs ≥10 mm represent lesions associated with increased risk is unresolved; pathologist distinction between SSP and HP is inconsistent in usual care, and a conservative approach may be to assume HPs ≥10 mm are SSPs.
- Whether 1–2 SSPs <10 mm warrant 5-y or 10-y follow-up is unresolved; the 5–10 y range is more aggressive than the 7–10 y given for 1–2 isolated conventional adenomas because the evidence base for the longer interval is strong for adenomas and weak for SSPs.
- Second-surveillance intervals rest on retrospective data; more evidence is needed for patients with low-risk adenoma at both baseline and follow-up, and for serial surveillance after SSPs and large HPs.
- Insufficient evidence to recommend modification of surveillance intervals based on lifestyle, chemoprevention, race, diet, or metabolic factors.
- Does not cover inflammatory bowel disease, hereditary CRC syndromes, serrated polyposis syndrome, malignant polyps, personal or family history of CRC, or polypectomy technique.
