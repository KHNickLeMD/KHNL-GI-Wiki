## Summary

- Focused update: **when to start and stop** colorectal cancer (CRC) screening in average-risk people, and which tests are recommended.
- **Average risk** = no personal or family history of CRC or colorectal neoplasia, and no clinical features of CRC (e.g., gastrointestinal bleeding, iron deficiency anemia, abnormal imaging). Studies in inflammatory bowel disease, family history, prior CRC/polyps, and hereditary syndromes were excluded.
- **Main change:** screening offered from **age 45** (previously 50; 45 for Black individuals only in 2017). No study shows screening under 50 lowers CRC incidence or mortality; the case rests on disease burden, neoplasia yield at 45–49 close to 50–59, and modeling of benefit vs harm and cost.
- **Stopping guidance unchanged** from 2017: consider stopping at 75 (or with life expectancy <10 years) if up to date with negative screening; individualize at 76–85; screening not recommended after 85.
- Test menu and grades restated **unchanged** from 2017.

### Grading and format

- Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE), as adapted by the USMSTF.
- Strong = "we recommend"; weak = "we suggest".
- Recommendations are unnumbered; the boxed list labels them **Updated** or **Unchanged**.

- **High-quality colonoscopy** (footnote): complete to the cecum (photo-documentation of appendiceal orifice, ileocecal valve, or terminal ileum), adequate bowel preparation, colonoscopist with **≥25% overall adenoma detection rate**.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Burden of CRC under 50 (early-age onset CRC [EAO-CRC])

- 2020: ~11% of colon and 15% of rectal cancers diagnosed under 50.
- Incidence under 50 rose 1.1%/year (95% confidence interval [CI] 0.3–2.0%) 2006–2015: colon 0.7%/year, rectal 1.7%/year. Steepest rises in ages 20–39; ages 40–49 saw +13% colon and +16% rectal adenocarcinoma 2000–2016.
- Incidence at 45–49 (2001–2010: 30.8 men / 25.9 women per 100,000) now resembles 50-year-olds in 1992 (25.6 per 100,000), when screening at 50 began.
- Mortality at 45–49: colon 6.4 → 6.6 and rectal 1.3 → 1.7 per 100,000 (1999–2019), while falling in older groups.
- Strong **birth-cohort effect** (inflection for those born after 1960) → early-life exposures likely contribute.
- Race/ethnicity: increase limited to White individuals in mortality; Black individuals still carry higher CRC death risk (colon hazard ratio [HR] 1.36; rectal HR 1.52) and lower 5-year survival (54.9% vs 68.1%).
- Somatic profiles of tumors at 40–49 resemble those at ≥50 → same screening target.

### EAO-CRC vs later-age onset (LAO) features (Table 1, condensed)

| Feature | EAO-CRC (<50) | LAO-CRC (≥50) |
|---|---|---|
| Presenting with symptoms | 86.4–95.6% | 33.9–79.0% |
| Symptom duration | 243 days | 154 days |
| Time to diagnosis | 152–217 days | 29.5–87 days |
| Rectal location | 25.4–49.1% | 20.0–35.2% |
| Late stage | 61.2–89.0% | 30.3–62.5% |
| Mucinous / signet ring | 10.0–15.0% / 1.0–13.0% | 4.7–16.0% / 0.9–4.0% |

- Most common EAO-CRC symptoms: rectal bleeding 50.8%, abdominal pain 32.5%, bowel-habit change 18.0%.
- Later stage at diagnosis is not explained by longer delay alone.
- Stage-adjusted cancer-specific survival is better in younger patients (local 95.1% vs 91.9%; regional 76% vs 70.3%; distant 21.3% vs 14.1%).
- Left-sided tumors predominate in sporadic EAO-CRC (72.6–74.9%) vs those with germline pathogenic variants (38.0–58.3%).

### Yield of screening at 45–49

- Meta-analysis (17 studies, 51,811 average-risk people <50): at 45–49, any neoplasia 17.8% (14.5–21.6%), **advanced neoplasia 3.6% (1.9–6.7%)** vs 4.2% (3.1–5.7%) at 50–59 (P = .69). ~28 colonoscopies at 45–49 to find 1 advanced lesion.
- New Hampshire registry (screening-equivalent group): advanced neoplasia 3.7% at 45–49 vs 3.6% at 50–54; clinically significant serrated polyps 5.9% vs 6.1%.
- International studies with 45–49 data (Table 2): advanced adenoma at 45–49 1.2–12.5%; nonadvanced 8.2–20.2%.
- Two-step: Black individuals 45–50 offered FIT (n = 10,232) — 33.1% completed, 4.0% positive, 85.3% had colonoscopy; of those, 33.6% advanced adenoma, 2.6% CRC. FIT–fecal DNA at 45–49 (n = 816): sensitivity 32.7% for advanced neoplasm.

### Modeling at 45 vs 50 (Table 3, per 1,000 screened 45–75 vs 50–75)

| Strategy | Extra life-years | CRC prevented | CRC deaths averted | Extra tests | Extra adverse events |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Colonoscopy every 10 y | 16–34 | 1–4 | 1–2 | 756–800 colonoscopies | 2 |
| Annual FIT | 17–33 | 1–4 | 1 | FIT 3387–3520; colonoscopy 175–205 | 1 |
| Triennial stool DNA–FIT | 16–31 | 1–4 | 1 | 1166–1201; colonoscopy 177–196 | <1 |
| Flexible sigmoidoscopy every 5 y | 13–30 | 1–3 | 1 | 743–801; colonoscopy 170–192 | <1 |
| CT colonography every 5 y | 14–31 | 1–3 | 1 | 798–806; colonoscopy 153–165 | 1 |

- Cancer Intervention and Surveillance Modeling Network (3 models; assumes 100% adherence): **47 of 57** efficient strategies began at 45.
- Cost per quality-adjusted life-year (QALY) for starting at 45: colonoscopy every 10 y **$33,900**; annual FIT **$7,700**. Hybrids: one flexible sigmoidoscopy at 45 then colonoscopy 50–75 $55,900; annual FIT 45–49 then colonoscopy 50–75 $2,500. Another analysis: all single-screen strategies at 40 cost-effective at $50,000/QALY; FIT–fecal DNA costliest.
- No controlled studies of screening outcomes under 50; colonoscopy is safer in younger than older people.

### Stopping screening

- 2021 simulation (FIT only): benefit beyond 74 for some. Unscreened women without comorbidity benefited from annual FIT to **90**, unscreened men to **88**; with **severe comorbidity** (≥1 of AIDS, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, cirrhosis, chronic hepatitis, chronic renal failure, dementia, congestive heart failure, or combined moderate conditions) no benefit **beyond 66**.
- Those never screened gain most after 75. Risk of advanced polyps and CRC rises with age, but so do comorbidity and adverse events (bleeding, perforation, cardiopulmonary events); emergency visits and admissions after colonoscopy are higher over 75.
- Over 85: mortality risk and adverse events outweigh polypectomy's life-expectancy benefit.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Grade for ages 45–49 differs by society:** USMSTF weak / low-quality vs [[acg-2021-crc-screening|ACG 2021]] conditional / very low-quality; same direction.
- **Stopping at 75:** USMSTF suggests *considering stopping* at 75 (or life expectancy <10 years) when up to date and negative; ACG 2021 suggests *individualizing* beyond 75. Compatible in practice; different default framing.
- **Over 85:** USMSTF states screening is not recommended after 85, but as summary text, not a graded statement.
- **Adenoma detection rate in "high-quality colonoscopy":** ≥25% overall here vs ≥30% in [[aga-2021-colonoscopy-quality|AGA 2021]] and ≥30% men / ≥20% women in [[usmstf-2020-followup-colonoscopy|USMSTF 2020]]; see [[colonoscopy-quality-indicators]].
- **Unanswered (Table 5):** precision vs age-based starting; tiered vs menu approach; hybrid noninvasive-then-colonoscopy strategies; equity of access; whether surveillance intervals should differ in younger people.
