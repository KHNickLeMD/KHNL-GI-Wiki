## Summary

Three-year update of the 2021 SAGES guideline on [[poem|peroral endoscopic myotomy (POEM)]] for [[achalasia]], built on a literature search from 2/18/2019 to 3/8/2023 combined with the 2021 data. Two key questions (KQ): POEM vs Heller myotomy (HM) and POEM vs pneumatic dilation (PD). The panel describes **3 recommendations for adults**; the KQ1 text also carries a fourth, expert-opinion statement on routine endoscopy. "Suggests" = conditional; "recommends" = strong — every statement here is a suggestion.

The surgical comparison is reframed: **POEM with appropriate proton pump inhibitor (PPI) use** vs **laparoscopic Heller myotomy with fundoplication (HMF)**, rather than POEM vs myotomy alone. No new pediatric comparative data were found — the 2021 guideline stands for children. The POEM-vs-PD question is retired from future updates; POEM vs HMF will be re-evaluated in three years.

|---|---|---|---|
| KQ1 | Adults with **type I and II** achalasia: either **POEM with appropriate use of PPI** or **laparoscopic HM with fundoplication**, by surgeon–patient shared decision-making | Conditional (suggests) | Low |
| KQ1 | **POEM over laparoscopic HM for type III** adult achalasia | Expert opinion (suggests) | — |
| KQ1 | Consider **routine upper endoscopy in both groups** (POEM and HMF) to detect the asymptomatic patient with significant reflux who could be at risk of adenocarcinoma; no data on timing or protocol. ## Key Findings / Claims

*GERD = gastroesophageal reflux disease · OR = odds ratio · RR = risk ratio · CI = confidence interval · RCT = randomized controlled trial · MD = mean difference*

### KQ1 — POEM vs HMF (26 studies: 3 RCTs, 23 observational; outcomes >6 months)

- **Similar:** quality of life (1 RCT, 202 patients, moderate certainty); DeMeester score >12 months; dysphagia >6 months (OR 0.97); Eckardt score; regurgitation; reintervention for failure (OR 1.65, moderate); mortality (very low); unexpected ICU admission
- **POEM better:** unrecognized leak (OR 0.66, very low certainty)
- **POEM worse:** grade C/D esophagitis >12 months (OR 2.18, CI 0.17–27.50; 64 more per 1,000; very low) and PPI use >12 months (OR 2.62, CI 1.40–4.89; 193 more per 1,000; high)
- Overall certainty low (risk of bias, imprecision); benefits and harms both small
- Esophagitis after POEM fell over time — pooled grade A–D and C/D rates higher at <12 than >12 months; RCTs started PPI when esophagitis was found
- **Appropriate PPI use** = treating patients with objectively confirmed abnormal distal esophageal acid exposure. Empiric or symptom-based starts are acceptable, but objective testing should follow at some point
- Nearly 60% of patients with abnormal acid exposure or esophagitis on endoscopy have no symptoms; achalasia symptoms can mimic GERD
- Clinically relevant outcome = **medically uncontrollable reflux**, not esophagitis alone; rare anecdotal cases needed partial fundoplication after POEM
- Long-term effects of severe post-POEM esophagitis are not reported
- Post-fundoplication side effects (gas-bloat, diarrhea) are not clearly reported in comparative studies
- HM without fundoplication → reflux ~30% or higher on objective testing; HM is nearly always paired with fundoplication
- Quality of life at 24 months equivalent with either option
- Implementation: adequate infrastructure and training; preoperative high-resolution manometry and upper GI series are critical — otherwise patients may get the wrong intervention (Nissen fundoplication for presumed GERD, multiple dilations and Botox); complex procedures at high-volume centers

### KQ2 — POEM vs PD (9 studies: 2 RCTs, 7 observational; outcomes >6 months)

- **Similar:** perforation (7 observational studies, 5,244 patients, OR 0.99, CI 0.58–1.69); unexpected ICU admission
- **POEM better:** dysphagia (1 RCT, 125 patients, RR 0.45, CI 0.24–0.83; 218 fewer per 1,000; moderate) and reintervention for failure (1 RCT, 125 patients, RR 0.27, CI 0.14–0.54; 348 fewer per 1,000; high)
- **POEM worse:** esophagitis C/D >12 months (2 RCTs, 141 patients, OR 2.89, CI 0.34–24.82; very low)
- Desirable effects moderate, undesirable small; the evidence section rates overall certainty very low, while the recommendation is labeled moderate certainty
- **PD may be preferred:** repeated treatment failure, elderly, frail, inability to tolerate prolonged procedures and general anesthesia; possible bridge while awaiting POEM or HMF; may be the only option where no POEM/HMF infrastructure exists
- Previous PD did not make POEM or HMF more difficult in the panel's experience
- True achalasia dilation = achalasia balloon ≥30 mm; through-the-scope balloon dilation is not an achalasia dilation

### Monitoring Suggested by the Panel

- More than 1 year after POEM and after LHM with fundoplication: reflux esophagitis on endoscopy, pH testing in the symptomatic patient, PPI use and uncontrolled reflux on PPI, quality of life, regurgitation and dysphagia

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **How often post-myotomy reflux is silent — this update vs [[aga-2024-poem-advances|AGA 2024]].** SAGES 2024 reports that **nearly 60%** of patients with measured abnormal esophageal acid exposure or endoscopic esophagitis have no symptoms (citing its reference 22); the AGA 2024 clinical practice update puts the asymptomatic share at **~one-quarter**. Both are tier-1 and both 2024 (this update printed in *Surgical Endoscopy* 2025). [[poem]] states the AGA figure and notes this one. Either way the operative conclusion is the same and is what the pages assert: **symptoms alone are insufficient to exclude post-myotomy reflux**, which is why both documents support objective testing or endoscopy rather than symptom-based follow-up.
- **Surveillance after POEM.** The panel notes that "the ACGE" advises no indication for long-term surveillance after POEM because squamous cell carcinoma reports are low, and counters that this ignores adenocarcinoma risk, for which there are no meaningful data — hence its expert-opinion suggestion to consider routine endoscopy.
- **Long-term consequences of asymptomatic esophagitis are unreported.** The panel states the long-term effects of severe post-POEM esophagitis are not currently in the literature, and flags the absence of data on the timing and protocol for routine surveillance endoscopy as an explicit research gap.
