## Summary

AGA Clinical Practice Update (CPU) Expert Review on endoscopic bariatric therapies (EBTs) — devices and techniques positioned between lifestyle/pharmacologic management and bariatric surgery. The authors searched English-language databases for human trials published 1 January 2000 – 30 September 2016 (Ovid MEDLINE, EMBASE, Cochrane CENTRAL, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, Scopus), supplemented by manual review of references, meeting abstracts, and expert-identified publications. The stated rationale: bariatric/metabolic surgery induces 20%–40% total body weight loss (TBWL) and reduces obesity-related morbidity and mortality, but is offset by increased morbidity and rare mortality; EBTs are less invasive, lower cost, and applicable to a larger segment of the population with moderate obesity. Obesity affects 35% of US adults (body mass index [BMI] ≥30 kg/m²), and obesity with its comorbidities accounts for ~21% of US health expenditure. Four EBTs were US Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved at the time of review.

EBTs are separated into **gastric** interventions (space-occupying balloons and devices, endoscopic suturing/plication, aspiration therapy) and **small-bowel** interventions (duodenojejunal bypass sleeve, gastroduodenojejunal bypass sleeve, duodenal mucosal resurfacing, self-assembling magnets). Gastric EBTs work by restriction and by altering gastric mechanical/chemical receptor signaling, gastric emptying, and orexigenic hormone levels; small-bowel EBTs exploit the proximal small intestine's role in glucose homeostasis and incretin signaling.

The update's central practical message is programmatic rather than device-specific: weight loss after an EBT is **variable and can be temporary**, particularly with removable and anatomy-preserving devices, so weight recidivism should be anticipated and managed. Obesity is a chronic disease, and no single intervention, device, or operation will cure it. Gastroenterologists adopting EBTs should develop expertise in obesity as a disease alongside a multidisciplinary team (medical obesity specialists, behavioral therapy professionals, registered dietitians, bariatric surgeons).

**The document is ungraded.** Seven pieces of Best Practice Advice are given without evidence grades, strength ratings, or a GRADE assessment; the authors state they are "based on our review of the available literature, current practice patterns, and our general knowledge and experience," and that where data are lacking the advice is "logical, conservative, and in our patient's best interest." The acknowledgment states the review is "a consensus summary of expert opinion in the field without a formal systematic review of evidence." It must not be cited as graded evidence.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Best Practice Advice (verbatim; 7 statements, ungraded)

1. **Best Practice Advice 1:** "EBTs should be considered in patients with obesity who have been unsuccessful in losing or maintaining weight loss with lifestyle interventions."
2. **Best Practice Advice 2:** "EBTs can be used in patients with severe obesity as a bridge to traditional bariatric surgery. They also can be used as a bridge to allow unrelated interventions that are unable to be performed because of weight limits (ie, orthopedic surgery, organ transplantation)."
3. **Best Practice Advice 3:** "Clinicians should use EBTs as part of a structured weight loss program that includes dietary intervention, exercise therapy, and behavior modification, in both the active weight loss phase and the long-term maintenance phase."
4. **Best Practice Advice 4:** "Clinicians should screen all potential EBT candidates with a comprehensive evaluation for medical conditions, comorbidities, and psychosocial or behavioral patterns that contribute to their condition before enrolling patients in a weight loss program that includes EBTs."
5. **Best Practice Advice 5:** "Clinicians incorporating EBTs into their clinical practice should follow up patients prospectively to capture the impact of the EBT program on weight and weight-related comorbidities, and all related adverse outcomes. Poor responders should be identified and offered a detailed evaluation and alternative therapy."
6. **Best Practice Advice 6:** "Clinicians embarking on incorporating EBTs into their clinical practice should have a comprehensive knowledge of the indications, contraindications, risks, benefits, and outcomes of individual EBTs, as well as a practical knowledge of the risks and benefits of alternative therapies for obesity."
7. **Best Practice Advice 7:** "Institutions should establish specific guidelines that are applied consistently across disciplines for granting privileges in EBTs that reflect the necessary knowledge and technical skill a clinician must achieve before being granted privileges to perform these procedures."

### Device data (Table 1 and text)

- **Orbera intragastric balloon (IGB):** silicone, saline-filled **450–700 mL**, 6-month therapy. Meta-analysis of 55 studies / 6645 implantations — %TBWL **13.2% at 6 mo** (95% confidence interval [CI] 12.4–13.95). Adverse events pooled from 68 studies: abdominal pain 33.7%, nausea 29.0%, early removal ~7.5%, migration 1.4%, gastric perforation 0.1% (4 of 8 perforations occurred in patients with previous gastric surgery). Pivotal US randomized controlled trial (RCT) (125 vs 130 lifestyle): %TBWL at 6 mo among completers **10.7% ± 6.8% (n = 116) vs 4.7% ± 5% (n = 99)**.
- **ReShape Duo IGB:** dual balloons with independent channels, filled with **900 mL** saline/methylene blue total (**450 mL per balloon** by power pump), endoscopic removal at 6 months. Pivotal US blinded sham-controlled RCT: %TBWL at 6 mo among completers **7.6% ± 5.5% (n = 167) vs 3.6% ± 6.3% (n = 126)**. Early retrieval 15%; spontaneous deflation 6% without migration; gastric ulcers/erosions 39.6% initially, falling to **10.3% after device design modification**, with ulcer size 1.6 → 0.8 cm.
- **Obalon IGB:** gas-filled, swallowed in a gelatin capsule, inflated through a thin catheter from a gas canister; up to 3 balloons over the same or sequential sessions; endoscopic removal at **12–26 weeks**. Pivotal blinded sham RCT (185 vs 181): %TBWL **6.9% ± 5 vs 3.6% ± 5**.
- **Spatz adjustable IGB:** saline-filled with an extractable inflation tube permitting volume adjustment in situ; approved outside the United States for up to **12-month** implantation (US pivotal trial underway at the time of review).
- **Elipse IGB:** swallowed capsule, balloon filled with **550 mL** fluid, detachable catheter removed; a valve opens at ~**4 months** and the emptied balloon is excreted spontaneously — no endoscopy required for placement or removal.
- **TransPyloric Shuttle:** large spherical silicone bulb tethered to a smaller cylindrical bulb; the small bulb advances into the duodenum with peristalsis, creating an intermittent pyloric seal that delays gastric emptying. Prospective single-arm (n = 10): %TBWL 14.5% ± 5.8 at 6 mo; gastric erosion 75%, gastric ulcer 50%.
- **Full Sense Device:** modified fully covered gastroesophageal stent — cylindrical esophageal component plus a gastric disk on struts applying pressure to the cardia to induce satiety.
- **Endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty (ESG):** endoluminal full-thickness sutures from the prepyloric antrum to the gastroesophageal junction reduce capacity along the greater curvature. Multicenter study of 242 patients at 3 centers: %TBWL **16.8% ± 6.4 at 6 mo (n = 137)**, **18.2% ± 10 at 12 mo (n = 53)**, **19.8% ± 11.6 at 18 mo (n = 30)**. Five (2%) adverse events, all recovering fully without surgery.
- **Primary obesity surgery endolumenal (POSE):** transmural tissue-anchor plications to restrict fundic accommodation plus 3 distal gastric body plications to delay emptying. Pivotal US blinded sham RCT (221 vs 111): %TBWL at 12 mo **4.94% ± 7 vs 1.38% ± 5.6**; serious adverse event rate 4.7%.
- **Aspiration therapy (A-Tube / AspireAssist):** percutaneous gastrostomy tube; aspiration by siphon ~20 minutes after a meal removes about **a third** of the meal, taking 10–15 minutes. Pivotal US open-label RCT (111 vs 60): %TBWL among completers at 12 mo **14.2% ± 9.8% (n = 82) vs 4.9% ± 7% (n = 31)**. Stoma granulation tissue 40.5%, stoma infection 14.4%, peritonitis 0.9%, gastric ulcer 0.9%. Rates of spontaneous fistula closure after A-Tube removal are not yet clear.
- **EndoBarrier duodenojejunal bypass sleeve (DJBS):** impermeable polymer liner anchored in the duodenal bulb by a barbed nitinol crown, extending **65 cm** into the small bowel; endoscopic removal at 12 months. Meta-analysis: **35.3% excess weight loss (%EWL) at 12 mo** (95% CI 24.6–46.1). Across 4 RCTs the mean %EWL difference vs control was **9.4%** (95% CI 8.26–10.65). Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) change: −0.7 (95% CI −1.76 to 0.2; *P* = .16) at 12 weeks, −1.7 (95% CI −2.5 to −0.86; *P* < .001) at 24 weeks, −1.5 (95% CI −2.2 to −0.78; *P* < .001) after 52 weeks, an additional −1% (95% CI −1.67 to −0.4; *P* = .001) vs controls. A US multicenter double-blinded sham-controlled trial was **terminated early after 325 of 500 enrollments because of a 3.5% incidence of hepatic abscess**; at that point %TBWL was 7.7% ± 9.6% vs 2.1% ± 5.4% (*P* < .0001) and HbA1c −1.1% ± 1.5% vs −0.3% ± 1.6%, with early device retrieval for adverse events in 10.9%.
- **Other small-bowel EBTs (earlier development):** gastroduodenojejunal bypass sleeve; duodenal mucosal resurfacing (thermal ablation of superficial duodenal mucosa after submucosal lift, hypothesized to reset diseased duodenal enteroendocrine cells); self-assembling magnets creating a dual-path jejunoileal bypass.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **No contradiction with [[aga-2021-intragastric-balloons]].** The 2021 balloon guideline is both newer and formally graded; it governs balloon recommendations. The 2017 CPU adds non-conflicting device-level detail (fill volumes, dwell times, per-device trials) that the 2021 document does not carry.
- **Eligibility thresholds are not specified.** The advice conditions EBT use on failure of lifestyle intervention and on severe obesity as a bridge, but this document gives no BMI cut-points, contraindication list, or age criteria for EBTs.
- **Durability is unresolved.** The authors state weight loss after EBT is variable and can be temporary, and call for research matching a patient to the EBT most likely to succeed, and for study of combined and sequential EBT plus obesity pharmacotherapy.
- **Two devices carry unresolved safety signals:** EndoBarrier DJBS (3.5% hepatic abscess, trial terminated early) and aspiration therapy (unknown rate of spontaneous fistula closure after tube removal).
