## Summary

The FDA prescribing information for **linaclotide**, the guanylate cyclase-C (GC-C) agonist that is the only drug the American Gastroenterological Association recommends *strongly* for irritable bowel syndrome with constipation (IBS-C). It was fetched to close the gap the wiki's own download list named: the guidelines behind [[linaclotide]] give the adult doses but say nothing about **contraindications, age limits, or renal and hepatic adjustment** — and the guideline on chronic idiopathic constipation (CIC) states outright that it does not apply to children.

The label answers all three. **There is no renal or hepatic dose adjustment** and none is expected: linaclotide is metabolised inside the gut lumen and plasma concentrations are below the limit of quantitation at every approved dose, so standard pharmacokinetic parameters cannot even be calculated. There are exactly **two contraindications** — age **under 2 years**, and known or suspected **mechanical gastrointestinal (GI) obstruction**. And the age limit is a floor, not a blanket exclusion: linaclotide is **indicated** in children, at **≥7 years for IBS-C** and **≥2 years for functional constipation (FC)**, each at its own lower dose.

The boxed warning is the under-2 contraindication, and the reason is mechanistic rather than statistical: in neonatal mice a single clinically relevant adult-equivalent dose caused **death from dehydration within 24 hours**, through age-dependent elevated GC-C agonism. Tolerability rises steeply with age in juvenile mice. A clinical GC-C ontogeny study in 99 children found **no age-dependent trend in intestinal GC-C expression from 2 to 18 years**, but **insufficient data below 2 years** to assess the risk — which is why the line sits where it does.

The one adverse reaction that drives management is **diarrhea**, and the label quantifies it by dose rather than leaving it a class effect: severe diarrhea in **2%** of adults on 145 µg or 290 µg but **<1%** on 72 µg, with the majority of all cases starting **within the first 2 weeks**. Post-marketing reports add the consequence that matters — severe diarrhea with dizziness, syncope, hypotension and **hypokalemia or hyponatremia**, requiring hospitalisation or intravenous fluids. The instruction is to **suspend dosing and rehydrate**, not to dose-reduce blindly.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Indications (section 1)

- **IBS-C** — adults and pediatric patients **≥7 years**.
- **CIC** — **adults only**.
- **FC** — pediatric patients **≥2 years**.

### Dosing (section 2.1) — four different doses by indication and age

| Population | Indication | Dose |
|---|---|---|
| Adults | IBS-C | **290 µg orally once daily** |
| Pediatric ≥7 y | IBS-C | **145 µg orally once daily** |
| Adults | CIC | **145 µg orally once daily**; **72 µg once daily** may be used based on individual presentation or tolerability |
| Pediatric ≥2 y | FC | **72 µg orally once daily** |

- Capsule strengths: **72 µg, 145 µg, 290 µg**.

### Administration (section 2.2)

- **Empty stomach, ≥30 minutes before a meal, at about the same time each day.**
- **Missed dose → skip it** and take the next at the regular time; **never two doses at once**.
- **Swallow whole** — do not crush or chew the capsule or its contents.
- Cannot swallow a capsule, or has a nasogastric or gastrostomy tube → the capsule may be opened:
  - **Applesauce:** sprinkle the beads on **one teaspoonful** of room-temperature applesauce, consume immediately, do not chew the beads, do not store.
  - **Water:** sprinkle into **~30 mL** room-temperature bottled water, swirl **≥20 seconds**, swallow immediately; add another 30 mL to residual beads, swirl 20 seconds, swallow.
  - **NG/G-tube:** same 30 mL mixture, draw into a catheter-tipped syringe, dispense at **10 mL per 10 seconds**, repeat with another 30 mL, then flush the tube with **≥10 mL** water.
  - **The beads do not dissolve** — the drug is coated on their surface and dissolves off, so it is **not necessary to consume or flush all the beads** to deliver the full dose.
- Sprinkling on other soft foods or into other liquids **has not been tested**.

### Boxed warning and contraindications (sections 4, 5.1, 8.4)

- **Boxed warning: risk of serious dehydration in pediatric patients <2 years of age.** Contraindicated in that group; in neonatal mice (human age equivalent ~0–28 days) a single clinically relevant adult oral dose caused **deaths due to dehydration**.
- Second contraindication: **known or suspected mechanical GI obstruction**.
- Juvenile animal data: **10 µg/kg/day killed neonatal mice on post-natal day 7**; 2-week-old mice tolerated 50 µg/kg/day but died after a single 100 µg/kg dose; 3-week-old mice tolerated 100 µg/kg/day but died after a single 600 µg/kg dose — tolerability increases with age.
- GC-C ontogeny study, **99 children aged 6 months to <18 years**, duodenal and colonic GC-C messenger RNA: **no age-dependent trend from 2 to <18 years**; **insufficient data <2 years**.
- Safety and effectiveness **not established**: IBS-C **<7 years**, FC **<2 years**.

### Diarrhea (section 5.2) — the action rule and the numbers

- **Severe diarrhea → suspend dosing and rehydrate the patient.**
- Adults: severe diarrhea **2%** on 145 µg or 290 µg; **<1%** on 72 µg.
- **Majority of cases begin within the first 2 weeks** of treatment (stated for every trial population).
- Pediatric: IBS-C 7–17 y — diarrhea **7%** on 145 µg, **8%** on 290 µg, with the single severe case occurring **above** the recommended 145 µg pediatric dose. FC 6–17 y on 72 µg — diarrhea **4%** (placebo 2%), one severe case.
- Post-marketing: severe diarrhea with **dizziness, syncope, hypotension and electrolyte abnormalities (hypokalemia, hyponatremia)** requiring hospitalisation or intravenous fluid.

### Adverse reactions (section 6.1)

**Adult IBS-C, Trials 1–2 (290 µg, n=807 vs placebo n=798), ≥2% and above placebo:**

| Adverse reaction | Linaclotide 290 µg | Placebo |
|---|---|---|
| Diarrhea | 20% | 3% |
| Abdominal pain | 7% | 5% |
| Headache | 4% | 3% |
| Flatulence | 4% | 2% |
| Viral gastroenteritis | 3% | 1% |
| Abdominal distension | 2% | 1% |

**Adult CIC, Trials 3–4 (145 µg, n=430 vs placebo n=423), ≥2% and above placebo:**

| Adverse reaction | Linaclotide 145 µg | Placebo |
|---|---|---|
| Diarrhea | 16% | 5% |
| Abdominal pain | 7% | 6% |
| Flatulence | 6% | 5% |
| Upper respiratory tract infection | 5% | 4% |
| Abdominal distension | 3% | 2% |
| Sinusitis | 3% | 2% |

- Trial 5 (CIC, 72 µg vs 145 µg vs placebo, n=411/411/401): diarrhea **19% / 22% / 7%**.
- **Discontinuation for adverse reactions** — IBS-C **9%** vs placebo 3% (diarrhea 5%, abdominal pain 1%); CIC **3%** on 72 µg and **5%–8%** on 145 µg.
- **Dose reduction or suspension in open-label long-term use at 290 µg daily for up to 18 months:** **29%** of 2,147 IBS-C patients and **27%** of 1,129 CIC patients, mostly for diarrhea or other GI reactions.
- Less common (<2%, above placebo): defecation urgency, fecal incontinence, vomiting, gastroesophageal reflux disease (IBS-C); defecation urgency, fecal incontinence, dyspepsia, viral gastroenteritis (CIC).
- **Post-marketing (6.2):** anaphylaxis, angioedema, rash including hives/urticaria; hematochezia, nausea, rectal hemorrhage.

### Specific populations (section 8)

- **Pregnancy:** negligibly absorbed, so maternal use is **not expected to result in fetal exposure**; human data insufficient to inform a risk of major birth defects or miscarriage. No embryo-fetal effects in rats or rabbits; severe maternal toxicity with fetal morphologic effects in mice only at ≥40,000 µg/kg/day (maximum recommended human dose ~5 µg/kg/day at 60 kg).
- **Lactation:** linaclotide and its active metabolite were **not detected in breast milk** (<0.25 ng/mL and <1 ng/mL) in any sample over 24 hours after 3 days of therapeutic dosing.
- **Geriatric:** too few patients ≥65 years (7% of IBS-C, 11% of CIC trial populations) to determine a different response; general advice is cautious dose selection in the elderly.
- **Renal and hepatic impairment (12.3):** **not expected to affect clearance** of linaclotide or its active metabolite — metabolism is intraluminal and plasma levels are unmeasurable. **No adjustment is specified.**

### Pharmacology (sections 12.1, 12.3) and overdose (10)

- Structurally related to human guanylin and uroguanylin; linaclotide and its active metabolite bind GC-C and act **locally on the luminal surface** of the intestinal epithelium. ↑ intracellular and extracellular cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP); intracellular cGMP drives chloride and bicarbonate secretion into the lumen **mainly via the cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator** → ↑ intestinal fluid, accelerated transit.
- **In an animal model of visceral pain** (not human data), linaclotide reduced abdominal muscle contraction and decreased pain-sensing nerve activity by raising **extracellular** cGMP.
- **Minimally absorbed with negligible systemic availability**; plasma below the limit of quantitation at 72, 145 and 290 µg, so **area under the curve, maximum concentration and half-life cannot be calculated**. No food effect detectable for the same reason.
- Metabolised in the GI tract by loss of the terminal tyrosine to the active metabolite; both degraded proteolytically in the lumen. Stool recovery of active peptide ~**5% fasted, 3% fed**, all as the active metabolite.
- **Overdose:** single doses of **2,897 µg** in 22 healthy subjects produced a safety profile consistent with the overall treated population.

### Label history

- **Initial US approval 2012.** Recent major changes dated **5/2026** to Indications and Usage, Recommended Dosage, and the Diarrhea warning — i.e. the pediatric FC indication and the dose-stratified severe-diarrhea figures are recent additions.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **No contradiction with any ingested guideline.** The adult doses on [[linaclotide]] from [[aga-acg-2023-constipation]], [[acg-2020-ibs]] and [[aga-2022-ibs-c]] match the label exactly (CIC 72 µg or 145 µg; IBS-C 290 µg).
- **The wiki's previous framing was wrong, and the label is the higher authority on its own drug's approved use.** [[linaclotide]] carried the note that "the pediatric contraindication is on the FDA label", which read as excluding all children. The contraindication is **age <2 years only**; children ≥7 years with IBS-C and ≥2 years with FC are within the approved indication. Corrected on the page.
- **Pediatric indications sit outside what the ingested guidelines address at all** — [[aga-acg-2023-constipation]] states explicitly that it does not apply to pediatric populations, so the label is the only source for them and the page says so.
- **Open:** the label gives no maximum treatment duration, and no ingested source gives one either; trials ran 12–26 weeks with open-label extensions to 18 months.
