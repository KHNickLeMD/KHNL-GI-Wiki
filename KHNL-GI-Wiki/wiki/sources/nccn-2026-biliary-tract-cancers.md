## Summary

The NCCN Biliary Tract Cancers guideline covers four related malignancies on parallel pathways: **gallbladder cancer (GALL)**, **intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (iCCA, INTRA)**, **perihilar and distal extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (eCCA, EXTRA)**, and the shared principles of pathology, biomarker testing, systemic therapy, and radiation. Workup centers on multiphasic cross-sectional imaging, tissue diagnosis (with **core biopsy preferred over FNA**), assessment of resectability, and multidisciplinary review. Management is stage- and site-specific: surgical resection (with adequate margins and regional lymphadenectomy) for resectable disease; adjuvant therapy after resection; and systemic ± locoregional therapy for unresectable or metastatic disease.

The defining feature of biliary tract cancer management in this guideline is **molecular biomarker testing**, which is recommended for all patients with unresectable or metastatic disease who are systemic-therapy candidates. A comprehensive multi-gene panel (MGPT) — with **RNA-based testing preferred to maximize detection of gene fusions** — interrogates actionable targets including FGFR2 fusions, IDH1 mutations, BRAF V600E, HER2 (ERBB2) amplification/overexpression, NTRK1/2/3 fusions, RET fusions, NRG1 fusions (new in this version), KRAS G12C, and the tumor-agnostic immunotherapy markers MSI-H/dMMR and TMB-H. Version 1.2026 adds NRG1 fusion to the testing tables, strengthens the preference for RNA-/transcriptome-based fusion detection, notes ctDNA as a complementary option (with the caveat that it may miss fusions), and refines liver-transplant exception criteria for unresectable iCCA.

## Key Findings / Claims

- **Four pathways, shared principles.** Gallbladder cancer, iCCA, perihilar eCCA, and distal eCCA each have their own workup/treatment algorithm but share the pathology, biomarker, systemic-therapy, and RT principles pages.
- **Tissue diagnosis — core biopsy preferred over FNA** (new/expanded footnotes this version), reflecting tissue requirements for comprehensive molecular profiling.
- **Comprehensive molecular profiling is standard for advanced disease.** Recommended for all unresectable/metastatic biliary tract cancer candidates for systemic therapy. Actionable targets: **FGFR2 fusions/rearrangements** (iCCA), **IDH1 mutation** (iCCA), **BRAF V600E**, **HER2 (ERBB2)**, **NTRK1/2/3 fusions**, **RET fusions**, **NRG1 fusions** (added this version), **KRAS G12C**, and tumor-agnostic **MSI-H/dMMR** and **TMB-H**. **RNA-/transcriptome-based MGPT is the preferred assay for fusion detection** (higher sensitivity than DNA-based testing); ctDNA may complement tissue but can miss fusions.
- **First-line systemic therapy for advanced disease** is a gemcitabine + cisplatin backbone combined with a PD-L1/PD-1 checkpoint inhibitor (durvalumab per TOPAZ-1; pembrolizumab per KEYNOTE-966); carboplatin may substitute when cisplatin-ineligible. Biomarker-matched targeted agents (e.g., FGFR2-fusion and IDH1-mutation inhibitors, HER2-directed therapy, BRAF/MEK, NTRK/RET inhibitors, and immunotherapy for MSI-H/dMMR/TMB-H) are used in subsequent lines where a target is present.
- **Adjuvant therapy after resection.** Capecitabine is a category 1 adjuvant option (BILCAP); gemcitabine-based regimens and fluoropyrimidine-based chemoradiation are alternatives, with greatest benefit signaled in node-positive disease. Adjuvant therapy is given for up to ~6 months.
- **Neoadjuvant therapy** is addressed specifically for gallbladder cancer (individualized, 2–6 months with reassessment), reflecting its often advanced presentation; clinical-trial participation is encouraged given limited data.
- **Liver transplantation for iCCA.** Refined transplant-exception criteria for biopsy-proven unresectable iCCA (or mixed HCC-iCCA) in the setting of cirrhosis after locoregional/systemic therapy with a defined observation period — i.e., transplant is an option for selected patients, distinct from the established role in perihilar CCA.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Algorithm-based guideline (category 2A consensus by default), not a GRADE systematic review — strength labels differ from the ASGE biliary-stricture guidelines already cited on the cholangiocarcinoma page. The two are complementary: ASGE governs how to obtain the diagnosis; NCCN governs oncologic staging and treatment.
- Optimal assay strategy (tissue RNA-based MGPT vs ctDNA) for fusion detection remains an evolving area; ctDNA sensitivity for fusions depends on panel/partner gene.
