## Summary

Four related malignancies run on parallel algorithms — **gallbladder cancer** (GALL), **intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma** (iCCA, INTRA), and **perihilar and distal extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma** (eCCA, EXTRA) — sharing the imaging, biomarker, systemic-therapy, and radiation principles pages (BIL-A–D). Workup is multiphasic cross-sectional imaging, tissue diagnosis (**core biopsy preferred over fine-needle aspiration [FNA]**, new footnote this version), resectability assessment, and multidisciplinary review. Carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) and CA 19-9 are baseline tests and **should not be used to confirm the diagnosis**.

Resection with negative margins plus regional lymphadenectomy is the curative option; adjuvant therapy follows, stratified by margin and nodal status. Unresectable or metastatic disease gets biomarker testing and systemic therapy, with locoregional therapy (ablation, arterially directed therapy, radiation) for liver-confined iCCA. Molecular biomarker testing is recommended for all unresectable/metastatic biliary tract cancer (BTC) patients who are systemic-therapy candidates, with **RNA-based multigene panel testing (MGPT) preferred for fusion detection**; circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) is an alternative when tissue is scant but may miss fusions and rearrangements.

Version 1.2026 changes: *NRG1* gene fusion added to the biomarker tables and **zenocutuzumab-zbco** added for *NRG1*-fusion cholangiocarcinoma (category 2B first line, category 2A subsequent line); "RNA-based MGPT is the preferred approach for gene fusion detection" added; core biopsy preferred over FNA across gallbladder pathways; iCCA transplant-exception criteria restated with the 6-month observation requirement and hepatology referral; eCCA surveillance schedule specified; GALL-3 bottom pathway changed to "consider neoadjuvant systemic therapy or clinical trial"; frozen section of the proximal bile duct margin added for distal cholangiocarcinoma.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Imaging and biliary drainage (BIL-A)

- Gallbladder cancer: multiphase CT or contrast-enhanced magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) with magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP) plus chest computed tomography (CT). **MRI preferred** for evaluating gallbladder masses and demonstrating bile duct involvement. Attention to porta hepatis, left gastric, and aorto-caval nodal basins.
- Cholangiocarcinoma: **obtain biliary protocol imaging before stenting** — reactive change from a stent can obscure the true extent of tumor. MRCP is preferred; endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) and percutaneous transhepatic cholangiography are used more for therapeutic intervention. Delayed-phase imaging is preferred when iCCA is suspected.
- **Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) or ERCP** may be helpful when bile duct dilation is present with no mass on CT or MRI, and can establish tissue diagnosis and relieve obstruction.
- Positron emission tomography (PET)/CT: limited sensitivity, high specificity; case-by-case for equivocal findings; routine preoperative use not established.
- **Biliary drainage:** multidisciplinary discussion of drainage is recommended, especially with potentially resectable disease. Route (percutaneous vs endoscopic) depends on local expertise. **All segments that are opacified should be drained**, aiming to drain **>50% of the viable liver volume including the future liver remnant (FLR)**; atrophic segments should generally not be drained.

### Gallbladder cancer (GALL-1 … GALL-6, GALL-A/B)

- **Incidental suspicious mass found during surgery:** if hepatobiliary expertise is unavailable, visually inspect and document all findings, do **not** biopsy a suspicious mass (risk of peritoneal dissemination), and refer. With expertise and convincing evidence of cancer, proceed to definitive resection.
- **Definitive resection** = radical cholecystectomy including **segments IVB and V** with lymphadenectomy clearing the porta hepatis, plus extended hepatic or biliary resection as needed for a negative margin, ± bile duct excision for malignant involvement. Bile duct resection solely to facilitate lymphadenectomy increases morbidity without proven survival benefit. **Port-site resection is not effective** — a port-site implant marks disseminated disease.
- **Incidental cancer on pathologic review (GALL-3) — the decision turns on T stage and margin:**

| Pathologic finding | Action |
|---|---|
| **T1a** (tumor invades lamina propria) with **negative** margins | Observe |
| Cystic duct node positive | Restaging imaging ± staging laparoscopy, then resection pathway |
| **T1b** (invades muscle layer) **or greater**, or T1a with **positive** margins | Restaging imaging ± staging laparoscopy → resectable: hepatic resection + lymphadenectomy ± bile duct excision; unresectable: biopsy + biomarker testing |

- Diagnostic laparoscopy is low-yield overall; higher yield with T3 or greater, poorly differentiated tumors, or a margin-positive cholecystectomy.
- **Jaundice** usually portends a poor prognosis and is a relative contraindication to resection, but curative-intent resection can be attempted in selected patients at centers with expertise. Consider biliary drainage before resection and systemic therapy, and a baseline CA 19-9 **after** biliary decompression.
- Germline testing: for mismatch repair-deficient (dMMR)/microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) tumors or a family history suggestive of *BRCA1/2* pathogenic variants, consider germline testing and/or genetic counselor referral.
- **Post-surgical treatment (GALL-6)** — R0 with negative nodes or carcinoma in situ at margin: systemic therapy (preferred), clinical trial (preferred), observation, or **combination of chemotherapy and chemoradiation (category 2B)**. R1 or node-positive: systemic therapy (preferred), clinical trial (preferred), combination of chemotherapy and chemoradiation, or chemoradiation. R2 (gross residual) → treat as unresectable.
- **Surveillance after resection:** imaging **every 3–6 mo for 2 y, then every 6–12 mo for up to 5 y**, or as clinically indicated; consider CEA and CA 19-9 as clinically indicated. The document states there are **no data supporting a specific surveillance schedule or set of monitoring tests** — the interval above is based on the schedule used in the phase III BILCAP trial.

### Intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (INTRA-1/2, INTRA-A/D)

- Workup of an isolated intrahepatic mass whose imaging is malignant but not consistent with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC): multidisciplinary evaluation, multiphasic abdomen/pelvis CT/MRI with intravenous contrast (preferred) or contrast-enhanced ultrasound, chest CT, consider CEA and CA 19-9, liver function tests, surgical consultation, **consider esophagogastroduodenoscopy and colonoscopy**, viral hepatitis serologies, consider biopsy, consider alpha-fetoprotein, consider hepatology referral.
- Surgery (INTRA-A): preoperative biopsy is not always needed — a suspicious mass in the proper setting is treated as malignant. **Lymph node metastases beyond the porta hepatis and distant metastatic disease contraindicate resection.** Multifocal liver disease generally represents metastatic disease and contraindicates resection; gross porta hepatis nodal metastases portend poor prognosis. Resection goal is negative margins; wedge and segmental resections are acceptable if margins can be achieved. Regional lymphadenectomy of the porta hepatis is carried out.
- **Ablation** is an option for **small single tumors <3 cm**, as a reasonable alternative to resection particularly in high-risk patients; the tumor plus a margin of normal tissue up to 1 cm must be treatable. Options: radiofrequency, microwave, irreversible electroporation.
- **Arterially directed therapy** (transarterial embolization, transarterial chemoembolization, drug-eluting-bead chemoembolization, yttrium-90 [Y-90]): may be used alone or with systemic chemotherapy to prolong survival or downstage to resection. Personalized dosimetry/radiation segmentectomy to achieve **>205 Gy** to the tumor may improve outcome. **Y-90 is relatively contraindicated when bilirubin >3 mg/dL**; grade 3–4 hepatic toxicity occurs in <10% of well-selected patients but may be considerably higher in cirrhosis. Select patients with limited extrahepatic disease (hilar node ≤3 cm or ≤5 lung nodules each ≤1 cm) may still be candidates in combination with systemic therapy.
- **Liver transplant exception points for iCCA — all of the following (footnote s):** biopsy-proven iCCA or mixed HCC-iCCA; presence of cirrhosis; unresectable; has received locoregional or systemic therapy; and **6 months from time of diagnosis or last treatment with no new lesions or extrahepatic disease**. Consider hepatology referral. Re-evaluation for liver transplantation is considered at response assessment.
- Pathology reporting (INTRA-C) includes **histologic differentiation as small duct type or large duct type** (new wording this version), vascular invasion, perineural invasion, margin status, and peritoneal perforation/extrahepatic invasion.
- Post-resection (INTRA-2): R0 → systemic therapy (preferred), clinical trial (preferred), or observation. R1 or node-positive → systemic therapy (preferred), clinical trial (preferred), combination chemotherapy and chemoradiation, or chemoradiation. R2 → treat as unresectable. Surveillance: multiphasic abdomen/pelvis CT/MRI with contrast and chest CT ± contrast **every 3–6 mo for 2 y, then every 6–12 mo for up to 5 y**, or as clinically indicated.

### Extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (EXTRA-1/2, EXTRA-A)

- Workup for pain, jaundice, abnormal liver function tests, or obstruction/imaging abnormality: multidisciplinary evaluation; multiphasic abdomen/pelvis CT/MRI with contrast **assessing vascular invasion**; chest CT ± contrast; biliary protocol imaging; cholangiography (MRCP preferred); consider CEA and CA 19-9; **consider EUS after surgical consultation**; and **consider serum IgG4 to rule out IgG4-related sclerosing cholangitis**.
- **Before biopsy, determine whether the patient is a resection or transplant candidate.** In potential transplant candidates, consider referral to a transplant center before biopsy — **transperitoneal and surgical biopsy may be contraindicated in transplant candidates.**
- **Perihilar transplant criteria (footnote h):** unresectable perihilar/hilar cholangiocarcinoma measuring **≤3 cm in radial diameter**, with **absence of intrahepatic and extrahepatic metastases** and **without nodal disease**, as well as those with [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis]], may be considered for liver transplantation **at a center with a UNOS-approved protocol for transplantation of cholangiocarcinoma**.
- Surgery (EXTRA-A): the basic principle is complete resection with negative margins and regional lymphadenectomy — generally **pancreaticoduodenectomy for distal tumors** and **major hepatic resection for hilar tumors**; **occasionally** a mid bile duct tumor can be resected with bile duct resection and regional lymphadenectomy (reworded from "rarely" this version). Hilar tumors require resection on the involved side to encompass the biliary confluence and **generally require caudate resection**; the contralateral hemi-liver must retain intact arterial and portal inflow and biliary drainage. Preoperative biliary drainage of the FLR and contralateral portal vein embolization should be considered when the FLR is small. Reconstruction is generally Roux-en-Y hepaticojejunostomy. **Frozen section of proximal and distal bile duct margins** is recommended if further resection is possible; for distal cholangiocarcinoma, consider assessing frozen section of the proximal bile duct margin (new).
- Post-resection and surveillance as for iCCA, including the **every 3–6 mo for 2 y, then every 6–12 mo for up to 5 y** schedule.

### Biomarker testing (BIL-B)

**Recommended for patients with unresectable or metastatic BTC who are candidates for systemic therapy.** Table 1 is subsite-specific — the "–" cells are not oversights:

| Target | Gallbladder | iCCA | eCCA |
|---|---|---|---|
| *NTRK1/2/3* gene fusion | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ |
| MSI-H/dMMR | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ |
| Tumor mutational burden-high (TMB-H) | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ |
| *BRAF* V600E mutation | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ |
| *FGFR2* gene fusion or rearrangement | — (consider) | ✔ | ✔ |
| *IDH1* mutation | — (consider) | ✔ | ✔ |
| HER2 (*ERBB2*) overexpression and/or amplification | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ |
| *RET* gene fusion | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ |
| *KRAS* G12C mutation | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ |
| *NRG1* gene fusion | — | ✔ | ✔ |

- *FGFR2* and *IDH1* testing is **recommended** in unresectable/metastatic iCCA and eCCA and **should be considered** in unresectable/metastatic gallbladder cancer. If the primary subsite within the biliary tree is unclear, test for *FGFR2* and *IDH1* anyway.
- **RNA-based MGPT is the preferred assay for fusion detection** (*NTRK*, *FGFR2*, *NRG1*, *RET*) — higher sensitivity than DNA-based testing; *NRG1*'s large introns are commonly missed by DNA-based MGPT and whole-exome sequencing. ctDNA may be considered for mutations if tissue is scant, but **may not reliably identify fusions or rearrangements**. Consider repeat biopsy or ctDNA if the initial sample has insufficient tumor content.
- MMR immunohistochemistry (MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2) and MSI are highly correlated; either guides therapy. If all four proteins are retained, MSI is unlikely. HER2 testing in hepatobiliary cancer **has no standardized cutoff or algorithm** — various cutoffs borrowed from breast and gastroesophageal junction neoplasms have been used, and activating missense mutations are missed by standard immunohistochemistry and fluorescence in situ hybridization.
- **Approximate incidence of therapeutic biomarkers in advanced BTC:** *FGFR2* fusion 9%–15% of iCCAs (rare in other subsites); *IDH1* mutation 10%–20% of iCCAs (rare elsewhere); HER2 5%–20% of cholangiocarcinomas and 15%–30% of gallbladder cancer; *BRAF* V600E 1%–5%; MSI-H/dMMR 1%–3%; TMB-H <5%; *KRAS* G12C 1%; *NTRK*, *RET*, and *NRG1* fusions each <1%.
- **Germline:** up to 10%–15% of BTCs may be associated with an inherited cancer predisposition syndrome; germline pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants in roughly 9%–11% of unselected BTC patients, most often *BRCA2*, then *BRCA1*, and to a lesser extent *MLH1*, *MSH2*, *PALB2*, *RAD51D*, *BAP1*, *ATM*. Evidence is insufficient for universal germline testing; **consider genetic counseling referral for** young age at diagnosis, strong personal or family history of cancer, no known liver-disease risk factors, or tumor-testing mutations suspected to be germline. For patients with a known germline variant (e.g. [[lynch-syndrome]] or hereditary breast and ovarian cancer syndrome), there is **currently insufficient evidence to support screening for biliary tract malignancy**.

### Systemic therapy (BIL-C)

Order within each cell does not indicate preference. Carboplatin may substitute if the patient is cisplatin-ineligible.

|---|---|---|
| **Neoadjuvant — gallbladder cancer only** | — | See the unresectable/metastatic first-line regimens. Decision must be individualized with the surgical oncologist and the multidisciplinary team; **a period of 2 to 6 months with reassessment every 2 to 3 months is reasonable**; limited trial data define no standard regimen or definitive benefit |
| **Adjuvant** (**up to 6 months**) | **Capecitabine (category 1)** | Capecitabine/gemcitabine; cisplatin/gemcitabine; single-agent fluorouracil/leucovorin or gemcitabine |
| **First line, unresectable/metastatic** | Cisplatin/gemcitabine **+ durvalumab** (category 1); cisplatin/gemcitabine **+ pembrolizumab** (category 1) | Cisplatin/gemcitabine (category 1); albumin-bound paclitaxel/gemcitabine; capecitabine/gemcitabine; capecitabine/oxaliplatin; FOLFOX (leucovorin/fluorouracil/oxaliplatin); gemcitabine/oxaliplatin; single-agent capecitabine, fluorouracil/leucovorin, or gemcitabine |
| **Subsequent line** | **FOLFOX** | FOLFIRI (leucovorin/fluorouracil/irinotecan); fluorouracil/leucovorin/liposomal irinotecan (category 2B); regorafenib (category 2B) |

- **BILCAP caveat, stated in the document:** the phase III BILCAP study showed improved overall survival for adjuvant capecitabine **in the per-protocol analysis**, and overall survival **did not reach statistical significance in the intent-to-treat analysis** — despite which capecitabine carries the category 1 label.
- Agents used with concurrent radiation: capecitabine, fluorouracil. Adjuvant chemotherapy or chemoradiation has been associated with survival benefit especially in **lymph node-positive** disease.
- Cisplatin/gemcitabine is also a recommended option for patients who recur **>6 months after curative-intent surgery and >6 months after completing adjuvant therapy**.

### Radiation therapy (BIL-D)

- Image-guided radiation therapy (RT) is strongly recommended with RT, intensity-modulated RT, and stereotactic body RT (SBRT). Dosing depends on normal-organ constraints and underlying liver function.
- Unresectable tumors: **SBRT 40–60 Gy in 3–5 fractions (biologically effective dose [BED10] >100 Gy)** preferred if constraints can be met; hypofractionation 58–67.5 Gy in 15 fractions (median equivalent dose at 2 Gy 80.5 Gy); if SBRT/hypofractionation is not possible, conventional fractionation 60 Gy/30 fractions to 77 Gy/35 fractions, or chemoradiation up to 60 Gy/30 fractions.
- Postoperative RT is an option for resected eCCA and gallbladder cancer: **45 Gy at 1.8 Gy/fraction** to the draining regional nodes (porta hepatis, celiac, superior mesenteric, gastrohepatic, para-aortic) and **50–60 Gy at 1.8–2 Gy/fraction** to the tumor bed depending on margin positivity.
- Brachytherapy may be considered at experienced centers (new this version).

### Staging reproduced (ST pages)

American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM 8th edition tables for gallbladder carcinoma, intrahepatic bile duct tumors, and the separately staged perihilar and distal bile duct cancers. The gallbladder T definitions carry the GALL-3 decision: **T1a invades lamina propria, T1b invades the muscle layer**; T2a/T2b split perimuscular connective tissue on the peritoneal vs hepatic side; T3 perforates serosa and/or directly invades liver or one adjacent organ; T4 invades main portal vein or hepatic artery or two or more extrahepatic organs. N1 = 1–3 regional nodes, N2 = ≥4.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Grading vocabulary differs from the American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) biliary-stricture guidelines cited alongside it on the cholangiocarcinoma page.** NCCN categories are consensus labels, not GRADE ratings. The two are complementary — ASGE governs how the tissue diagnosis is obtained, NCCN governs oncologic staging and treatment.
- **Category 1 for adjuvant capecitabine rests on a per-protocol result**; the intent-to-treat overall survival in BILCAP was not statistically significant. The document states both facts without reconciling them.
- **No evidence base for the surveillance schedule it prints** — the document says there are no data supporting a specific schedule or set of monitoring tests, and derives the 3–6 mo / 6–12 mo intervals from the BILCAP trial.
- Optimal fusion-detection strategy (tissue RNA-based MGPT vs ctDNA) is unsettled; ctDNA sensitivity depends on panel and partner gene.
- **HER2 positivity has no standardized cutoff in hepatobiliary cancer**, yet HER2-directed regimens are specified by immunohistochemistry score — the thresholds behind IHC3+/ISH+/NGS+ come from other tumor types.
- Neoadjuvant therapy is defined only for gallbladder cancer, and only as a duration window (2–6 months) with no standard regimen or demonstrated benefit.
- Germline association is documented (up to 10%–15% of BTCs) but the document explicitly declines to recommend biliary surveillance in known carriers.
