## Summary

The first internationally endorsed, unified definition and diagnostic framework for **porto-sinusoidal vascular disorder (PSVD) or non-cirrhotic portal fibrosis (NCPF)**. Noncirrhotic portal hypertension had been described under region-specific names — idiopathic portal hypertension (IPH), NCPF, obliterative portal venopathy, nodular regenerative hyperplasia (NRH), hepatoportal sclerosis, incomplete septal fibrosis — and the AASLD, EASL and APASL guideline definitions differed from one another. A Nomenclature Group of 16 steering-committee members and 13 scientific-advisory-board members, appointed by eleven liver, pathology and pediatric hepatology societies across the Americas, Europe and Asia, worked through four dedicated working groups (biopsy adequacy and histology; exclusion criteria; associated conditions; signs of portal hypertension), then externally validated the output with a 42-member Delphi panel. Consensus was pre-defined as ≥75% agreement. The steering committee ratified the final document in person in Paris on 3 December 2025.

The headline terminological decision is that **"PSVD" and "NCPF" may be used interchangeably provided the identical diagnostic criteria defined here are applied**, and that the entity should be written **"PSVD or NCPF"**. A formal vote (74% agreement) retained *disorder* rather than *disease*, on the rationale that it better encompasses multiple subgroups that may later prove to be distinct entities. The group explicitly declined to introduce a new term, acknowledging that changing established nomenclature is costly in regions where the disease is highly prevalent.

Three fundamental concepts anchor the definition: a **high-quality liver biopsy is mandatory**, **cirrhosis must be excluded**, and **the entity can be diagnosed in the absence of any signs of portal hypertension**. Around those, the consensus produced standardized **major and minor histological criteria** derived from a data-driven pathology effort (218 liver biopsies scored by nine European liver pathologists through the EURO-VALDI-NET COST Action), a **harmonized split of portal-hypertension signs into specific and nonspecific**, a revised **exclusion list**, a **list of associated conditions**, and — the practical centrepiece — an **integrated four-domain diagnostic score** yielding *no*, *possible*, or *definite* PSVD or NCPF.

The score was built by constructing 36 hypothetical clinical scenarios spanning all combinations of the four domains, having the Nomenclature Group vote on each for compatibility with the diagnosis, and fitting a weighted point system that reproduced 100% of the voted classifications. The independent Delphi panel then endorsed it with 87% agreement. The authors are explicit that the score derives from **expert opinion rather than prospective validation**, that the derivation biopsy cohort was exclusively European and included only patients with specific signs of portal hypertension, that **pediatric biopsies were not in the analyzed dataset**, and that age-specific clinical, laboratory and elastographic thresholds for children are not yet defined.

## Key Findings / Claims

### The eight numbered consensus statements

Agreement percentages are the document's own. The statements are numbered by the document.

| # | Statement | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | A liver biopsy is **mandatory** for the diagnosis of PSVD or NCPF. | 95% |
| **2** | Exclusion of cirrhosis **using a good-quality liver biopsy** is necessary. | 100% |
| **3** | PSVD or NCPF **can be diagnosed in the absence of signs of portal hypertension**. | 92% |
| **4** | A biopsy of **15 mm length with at least 1 fragment ≥10 mm** is sufficient to reliably exclude cirrhosis in case of suspicion of PSVD or NCPF. | 100% |
| **5** | The conditions listed in Table 3 should be excluded to establish the diagnosis. | 100% |
| **6** | **Major** histological criteria include **nodular regenerative hyperplasia (NRH), muscularized portal venules, or portal venule stenosis involving ≥50% of portal tracts**. | 100% |
| **7** | **Minor** histological criteria include **regenerative changes in the absence of NRH, abnormal distribution of vascular structures, or portal venule stenosis involving 25%–49% of portal tracts**. | 100% |
| **8** | **Concurrent presence of all 3 minor criteria is considered a major histological criterion.** | 100% |

### The diagnostic score (Figure 2)

Applied only **after** cirrhosis is excluded on an adequate biopsy **and** no exclusion criterion is present.

| Domain | Level | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Signs of portal hypertension | No signs | 0 |
| | Nonspecific | **+2** |
| | Specific | **+3** |
| Histological lesions | Normal histology | 0 |
| | Minor | **+2** |
| | Major | **+5** |
| Condition known to be associated with PSVD/NCPF | Absent | 0 |
| | Present | **+1** |
| Concomitant cause of cirrhosis | Absent | 0 |
| | Present | **−1** |

**Bands:** **≤2 → no PSVD or NCPF** · **3–4 → possible PSVD or NCPF** · **≥5 → definite PSVD or NCPF**.

**Scoring rules stated in the figure legend and text — these are the part most easily got wrong:**
- **Within each domain, score the highest applicable criterion only; points are not cumulative within a domain.** If both minor and major histological criteria are present, only the major is counted.
- More than one major histological lesion **does not** raise the score beyond 5; multiple signs of portal hypertension likewise add nothing.
- The combination of major and minor criteria does not generate additional points.
- A **possible** result (3–4) should prompt further investigation — new liver biopsy, liver stiffness measurement — **referral to a centre expert in vascular liver disease**, and follow-up for new signs appearing later.
- **Metabolic syndrome without histological evidence of MASLD is not counted as a cause of cirrhosis** and therefore does **not** trigger the −1.

Voting analysis showed **major histological criteria were the strongest factor** supporting the diagnosis, followed by specific signs of portal hypertension; **coexisting cause of cirrhosis reduced diagnostic confidence**.

### Signs of portal hypertension (Table 5)

| Specific | Nonspecific |
|---|---|
| Gastrointestinal bleeding due to varices | Clinical ascites |
| Medium/large esophageal varices | Small varices |
| Gastric varices | Low platelet count (<150 G/L) |
| Portosystemic shunt | Splenomegaly |
| **Spleen stiffness >40 kPa** in the absence of myeloproliferative neoplasia | |

- **Footnote:** hepatic venous pressure gradient (HVPG) **or** direct measurement of the portocaval pressure gradient **≥10 mm Hg** are unequivocal signs of portal hypertension and **should be considered specific signs**.
- HVPG **underestimates** portal pressure in PSVD/NCPF because the predominant component is presinusoidal; direct portocaval gradient by percutaneous puncture without general anesthesia is the most reliable measurement but is not widely available. EUS-guided porto-caval gradient assessment is "probably useful" but **validated cutoffs remain to be established**.

### Exclusion criteria (Table 3) — all 14

Abernethy syndrome · Budd-Chiari syndrome or hepatic venous outflow obstruction · cardiac failure or Fontan-associated liver disease · cholestatic liver diseases · congenital hepatic fibrosis · Gaucher disease · hepatic amyloidosis · hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia · hypervitaminosis A · intrahepatic arteriovenous shunts · liver infiltration by tumor cells · peliosis hepatis · **schistosomiasis\*** · **sinusoidal obstruction syndrome (SOS) in the last 6 months\*\***

- **\* Schistosomiasis:** in endemic areas and in the absence of hepatosplenic schistosomiasis, a **positive serology or the incidental finding of a few parasite eggs in the liver can coexist** with PSVD/NCPF and is **not** an exclusion criterion.
- **\*\* SOS:** diagnosed **≥6 months before** does not preclude the diagnosis — SOS survivors may develop portal hypertension long-term, and repeat biopsies >6 months after SOS have shown a PSVD-like histological pattern. SOS after hematopoietic stem cell transplantation should **not** be treated as synonymous with PSVD/NCPF.

**Three conditions were removed as exclusion criteria in this revision:** **portal cavernoma**, **SOS** (now time-limited to 6 months) and **schistosomiasis** (now qualified). On the cavernoma: new longitudinal imaging data from a European cohort showed patients with PSVD/NCPF may **develop** portal cavernoma — multiple dilated peribiliary and/or gallbladder wall veins >2 mm — **irrespective of main portal vein patency**, and in all described cases the cavernoma was intrahepatic; the panel agreed an **intrahepatic cavernoma does not exclude** the diagnosis.

### Histological criteria (Figure 1)

| Major | Minor |
|---|---|
| Nodular regenerative hyperplasia (NRH) | Regenerative changes without clear NRH |
| Muscularized portal venules | Abnormal distribution of vascular structures |
| Portal venule stenosis in **≥50%** of portal tracts | Portal venule stenosis in **25%–49%** of portal tracts |
| **All three minor criteria present** | |

- Derivation: major criteria defined a priori as lesions with **specificity >0.95 and sensitivity >0.10**; minor criteria as **specificity 0.80–0.94 and sensitivity >0.10** in biopsies lacking major criteria. Scored across 218 biopsies (41 normal donor livers, 89 chronic liver disease controls, 88 PSVD/NCPF cases with specific signs of portal hypertension); biliary disease excluded as it is itself an exclusion criterion. A lesion counted as present when >50% of pathologists reported it.
- The group **abandoned the term "specific lesions"** in favour of the major/minor framework, because the lesions are **not pathognomonic** and occur in other liver diseases.
- Sinusoidal dilatation and sinusoidal fibrosis may be seen but were **redundant** with the defined criteria and did not independently improve discrimination.
- An illustrated atlas of the standardized lesion definitions was made publicly available on the VALDIG website.

### Associated conditions (Table 4)

Retained at a deliberately **lowered consensus threshold of ≥50%**, reflecting regional rather than universal relevance. Grouped as:
- **Drug exposure:** azathioprine, 6-thioguanine, methotrexate, oxaliplatin, didanosine, stavudine, arsenic, vinyl chloride, trastuzumab-emtansine.
- **Genetic diseases:** Adams-Oliver syndrome (*NOTCH1*, *ARHGAP31*), telomere biology disorders (*TERT*, *TERC*, *DKC1*), retinal vasculopathy with cerebral leukoencephalopathy (*TREX1*), chronic granulomatous disease, X-linked agammaglobulinemia (*BTK*), activated PI3K delta syndrome, hyper-IgM syndrome, cystic fibrosis (*CFTR*), cystinosis, Williams-Beuren syndrome (*ELN*), Turner syndrome, Pierpont syndrome, *NOTCH1*/*CTC1* developmental disorders, familial obliterative portal venopathy (*FOPV*), and *CNN3*, *DGUOK*, *GIMAP5*, *FCHSD1*, *TRMT5*, *HRG*, *TACI* mutations, DADA2.
- **Hematologic/prothrombotic:** antiphospholipid syndrome, protein C/S and antithrombin deficiency, factor V Leiden, prothrombin gene mutation, ADAMTS13 deficiency, Hodgkin lymphoma, multiple myeloma, marginal B-cell lymphoma, MGUS, chronic lymphoid leukemia, paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria, aplastic anemia, ITP, myeloproliferative neoplasms.
- **Immune/inflammatory:** common variable immunodeficiency, Castleman disease, SLE, systemic sclerosis, rheumatoid arthritis/Felty syndrome, Still disease, vasculitis, Sjögren syndrome, mixed connective tissue disease, **inflammatory bowel disease**, **celiac disease**, POEMS, autoimmune nephropathy, Behçet disease, sarcoidosis without biliary/centrilobular involvement, dermatomyositis.
- **Transplantation:** solid organ transplantation. **Infection:** recurrent/chronic low-grade abdominal infections, HIV.

Prevalence of an identifiable associated condition differs by region: **~70% in Latin American cohorts, 60% in European series, 40% in China**; up to one-third have multiple, and roughly one-third have none.

### Biopsy adequacy

Previous definitions asked for **≥20 mm with ≥10 portal tracts and minimal fragmentation**, largely on expert opinion. New evidence showed **15 mm with at least one 10 mm fragment** reliably excludes cirrhosis with no added diagnostic benefit from longer samples; the group adopted it as the minimum standard. **No robust data yet define the biopsy characteristics needed to optimally identify the histological lesions themselves** — the 15 mm standard is validated for excluding cirrhosis, not for finding PSVD lesions.

### Concomitant cirrhosis etiologies

A recognized cause of chronic liver disease **lowers the pretest probability** but does not exclude the diagnosis — PSVD/NCPF has been identified retrospectively on explanted livers from patients transplanted with a preoperative clinicoradiological diagnosis of cirrhosis. The judgement is whether **the vascular component is the main driver** of the portal hypertension and the clinical phenotype.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **No contradiction with [[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension]].** Baveno VIII statements 8.38–8.44 relay this consensus accurately — the four domains, the point values, the major/minor histological split and the biopsy-adequacy standard all match. This document is the primary source and supplies the detail Baveno VIII compressed out.
- **Supersedes the older society definitions it was convened to harmonize** — the AASLD 2021 vascular liver disease guidance, the EASL vascular liver disease CPG and the APASL NCPF/IPH recommendations each used a different definition; this statement is endorsed by all three bodies and replaces those definitions.
- **Baveno VII's specific/compatible histology split is superseded** — see [[porto-sinusoidal-vascular-disorder]] for the full before/after.
- **The score is expert-derived, not prospectively validated.** The authors state this directly and call for validation studies. The derivation biopsy cohort was exclusively European and contained only patients with *specific* signs of portal hypertension, so performance in patients without portal hypertension — a group the definition explicitly admits — is untested.
- **Pediatric application is explicitly uncertain.** Pediatric liver biopsies were not in the analyzed dataset and age-specific clinical, laboratory and elastographic thresholds are not defined; the authors caution against direct application in children.
- **EUS-guided portosystemic gradient has no validated cutoff** in this setting, although the group considers the technique probably useful.
- **Whether a single shared pathogenic mechanism justifies the umbrella** remains, in the authors' words, not conclusively demonstrated.
