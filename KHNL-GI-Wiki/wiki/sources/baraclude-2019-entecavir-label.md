## Summary

The FDA prescribing information for **entecavir (ETV)**, one of the three preferred nucleoside analogs for chronic hepatitis B. It was fetched for a single, narrow reason: [[aasld-idsa-2025-chb-treatment|AASLD/IDSA 2025]] states that entecavir **requires dose adjustment for renal dysfunction or dialysis** but **prints neither the adult milligram dose nor the creatinine clearance cut-points at which to adjust it** — so [[hbv-nucleoside-analogs]] could name the rule without letting anyone follow it. The label supplies both.

Two adult doses exist and the distinction is clinically load-bearing: **0.5 mg once daily** is the standard nucleoside-naïve dose, and **1 mg once daily** applies in two separate situations — **lamivudine or telbivudine resistance/prior viremia on lamivudine**, and **decompensated liver disease**. Renal adjustment then runs off a **four-band creatinine clearance table** that is specified twice over, once for each of those two starting doses, either as a reduced daily dose or as a lengthened interval (the label states the **once-daily regimens are preferred**). Doses below 0.5 mg require the oral solution, since the smallest tablet is 0.5 mg.

Administration is unusually strict for a GI drug and is easy to get wrong: entecavir must be taken on an **empty stomach — at least 2 hours after a meal and 2 hours before the next one**. **No adjustment is needed for hepatic impairment**, which is worth stating explicitly on a hepatology page where the opposite assumption is natural.

The label carries a **boxed warning with three parts**: severe acute exacerbation of hepatitis B after stopping therapy; the risk of selecting human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor resistance if entecavir is used in untreated HIV / hepatitis B virus (HBV) co-infection; and lactic acidosis with severe hepatomegaly and steatosis. The HIV component converts directly into a pre-treatment action — **offer HIV antibody testing to every patient before starting**.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Indication.** Chronic HBV infection in **adults and pediatric patients ≥2 years of age** with evidence of **active viral replication** *and* either **persistent aminotransferase elevation (ALT or AST)** or **histologically active disease**.

**Contraindications:** **none listed.**

**Adult and adolescent (≥16 y) dosing:**

| Situation | Dose |
|---|---|
| **Nucleoside-naïve**, compensated liver disease | **0.5 mg once daily** |
| History of **viremia on lamivudine**, or known **lamivudine/telbivudine resistance substitutions** (rtM204I/V ± rtL180M, rtL80I/V, rtV173L) | **1 mg once daily** |
| **Decompensated liver disease** | **1 mg once daily** |

- **Administration: on an empty stomach — ≥2 h after a meal and ≥2 h before the next meal.**
- **Hepatic impairment: no dosage adjustment necessary.**
- **Duration:** the label states the optimal treatment duration, and the relationship between treatment and long-term cirrhosis/hepatocellular carcinoma outcomes, are **unknown**.

**Renal impairment — adjust at creatinine clearance (CrCl) <50 mL/min**, including hemodialysis and continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis (CAPD). Apparent oral clearance of entecavir falls as CrCl falls. **Once-daily regimens are preferred** over the interval-extension alternatives.

| CrCl (mL/min) | Usual dose (0.5 mg track) | Lamivudine-refractory **or** decompensated liver disease (1 mg track) |
|---|---|---|
| **≥50** | 0.5 mg once daily | 1 mg once daily |
| **30 to <50** | **0.25 mg once daily** *or* 0.5 mg every 48 h | **0.5 mg once daily** *or* 1 mg every 48 h |
| **10 to <30** | **0.15 mg once daily** *or* 0.5 mg every 72 h | **0.3 mg once daily** *or* 1 mg every 72 h |
| **<10**, or **hemodialysis / CAPD** | **0.05 mg once daily** *or* 0.5 mg every 7 days | **0.1 mg once daily** *or* 1 mg every 7 days |

- **For any dose below 0.5 mg, the oral solution is required** (0.05 mg/mL; 10 mL = 0.5 mg, 20 mL = 1 mg).
- **On a hemodialysis day, give entecavir after the session.**
- **Pediatric renal impairment:** data are insufficient for a specific recommendation; the label advises considering a dose reduction or interval increase **similar to the adult adjustments**.

**Pediatric dosing (≥2 y and ≥10 kg)** — by body weight, using the oral solution for weight up to 30 kg:

| Body weight (kg) | Treatment-naïve (mL once daily) | Lamivudine-experienced (mL once daily) |
|---|---|---|
| 10–11 | 3 | 6 |
| >11–14 | 4 | 8 |
| >14–17 | 5 | 10 |
| >17–20 | 6 | 12 |
| >20–23 | 7 | 14 |
| >23–26 | 8 | 16 |
| >26–30 | 9 | 18 |
| **>30** | **10 mL (0.5 mg), or one 0.5 mg tablet** | **20 mL (1 mg), or one 1 mg tablet** |

**Boxed warning — three components:**

1. **Severe acute exacerbations of hepatitis B** have occurred after discontinuing anti-hepatitis B therapy, entecavir included. **Monitor hepatic function closely — clinical and laboratory follow-up for at least several months after stopping.** Restarting anti-HBV therapy may be warranted.
2. **HIV/HBV co-infection:** limited experience suggests **potential for selecting resistance to HIV nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors** if entecavir is used for chronic HBV in patients whose **HIV infection is not being treated**. **Entecavir is not recommended in HIV/HBV co-infection unless the patient is also receiving highly active antiretroviral therapy (HAART).** → **Offer HIV antibody testing to all patients before initiating.** Entecavir is not a treatment for HIV.
3. **Lactic acidosis and severe hepatomegaly with steatosis, including fatal cases**, reported with nucleoside analog inhibitors alone or with antiretrovirals. **A majority of cases have been in women**; **obesity and prolonged nucleoside exposure** may be risk factors. **Patients with decompensated liver disease may be at higher risk.** **Suspend treatment** in any patient developing clinical or laboratory findings suggestive of lactic acidosis or pronounced hepatotoxicity. Cases have also occurred in patients with no known risk factors.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **No contradiction with [[aasld-idsa-2025-chb-treatment|AASLD/IDSA 2025]]** — the label fills a gap the guideline leaves open rather than disputing it. Source priority is unaffected: the guideline governs *whom* to treat and *which* agent to pick; the label supplies the milligram amount and the renal arithmetic.
- **A label is not a guideline** and sits outside the wiki's three-tier scheme. It is authoritative for dose, administration and labelled safety, and should not be used to settle questions of treatment indication, agent selection or monitoring strategy where a society guideline speaks.
- **The label predates the 2025 guideline** (revised 11/2019). Nothing in it conflicts with current practice on the points captured, but the HIV co-infection language reflects the older "HAART" vocabulary.
- **Entecavir in pregnancy is not addressed above** because the wiki's pregnancy stance comes from guidelines; the page already records that ETV is avoided in pregnancy for insufficient safety data.
