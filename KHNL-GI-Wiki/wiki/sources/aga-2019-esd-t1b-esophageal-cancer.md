## Summary

Three-author AGA Clinical Practice Update (CPU) on whether submucosal (T1b) esophageal cancer can be treated endoscopically rather than by esophagectomy. Its premise is the cost of surgery: even at a high-volume centre (Mayo Clinic) operative mortality for T1a esophageal cancer was **4%**, **34%** of patients had a postoperative complication (anastomotic leak or stricture, cardiopulmonary complication, feeding-jejunostomy leak), and most survivors live with reflux through an absent lower esophageal sphincter. Early-stage (T1a/T1b) disease is about **20%** of all esophageal adenocarcinoma diagnosed in the United States, so the question is not rare.

The document's substance is **depth-of-invasion anatomy and nodal risk**, not a treatment algorithm. It supplies the micrometer definitions that split T1b into SM1/SM2/SM3 — and makes them **histology-specific**: the SM1 ceiling is **200 µm for squamous cell carcinoma and 500 µm for adenocarcinoma**. It then tabulates predictors of lymph node metastasis (LNM) and LNM rates by depth and cell type, the central asymmetry being that **submucosal squamous cancer metastasizes far more than submucosal adenocarcinoma at the same depth** (SM1: 27% vs 6%).

On staging it is deflationary. Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) **overstages**: in a meta-analysis of 11 studies in Barrett's esophagus the pooled false-positive rate was **35.85%** (95% CI 25.3%–47.8%) — about a third of patients called T1b by EUS turn out to have low-grade dysplasia, high-grade dysplasia or T1a disease. Narrow-band imaging (NBI) with magnification beat EUS for separating mucosal from SM1 squamous disease, but magnifying endoscopy is not widely available in the US. Positron emission tomography (PET)/computed tomography (CT) is judged unnecessary in early-stage disease and a source of confounding benign findings that delay therapy.

The authors' position is permissive but narrow: endoscopic resection is for submucosal cancers with **low metastatic potential**, and the ideal candidate is a **well-differentiated T1b lesion under 2 cm, limited to SM1, without lymphovascular invasion (LVI) on the resected specimen**. Outside that profile esophagectomy remains the mainstay, with adjuvant chemoradiation (CRT) after endoscopic resection as an option for patients who decline or cannot have surgery. The supporting series for ESD ± CRT and ESD + radiofrequency ablation (RFA) are small and retrospective, and the paper says so.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Definition and substaging of T1b

- **T1a** = tumor limited to the mucosa. **T1b** = limited to the submucosa, no invasion of the muscularis propria.
- **SM1** = superficial submucosa, depth up to **200 µm (squamous cell carcinoma, ESCC)** or **500 µm (esophageal adenocarcinoma, EAC)**.
- **SM2 / SM3** = submucosal invasion **>200 µm (ESCC)** or **>500 µm (EAC)**.
- **Duplicated or trifurcated muscularis mucosae** occurs in Barrett's esophagus and causes overstaging by EUS and on endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR) specimens. In 99 esophagectomy patients (41 with duplicated muscularis mucosae), LNM risk for tumor invading the space *between* the duplicated layers was the same as for mucosa-confined (T1a) disease → **invasion into the duplicated muscularis mucosae space should be classified T1a, not T1b**. (Earlier work had suggested that space was as lymphatic-rich as submucosa; the more recent analysis finds its lamina propria mucosa-like.)

### Endoscopic prediction of deep invasion

- Features associated with invasion beyond SM1 in ESCC (retrospective, 203 patients): **surface nodularity, granularity, deep depression, thick notch** (a very deep groove within a mass).
- **Paris morphology:** in ESCC, type **0-I (protruding) 79%** and type **0-III (excavated) 84%** carry the highest risk of deep submucosal invasion — the **opposite of the colon**, where 0-I protruding lesions are generally superficial.
- **Japan Esophageal Society NBI classification** (intrapapillary capillary loops, IPCLs): type A = normal; **type B** = irregular, dilated, loop-forming; marked dilation/caliber increase/tortuosity within type B raises suspicion of **SM2** invasion. Type B vessels estimated invasion depth with **90.5% accuracy** in early ESCC. Requires magnifying endoscopy, limiting US use.
- **Chromoendoscopy:** Lugol's iodine stains normal squamous mucosa dark brown, leaving precancerous and early cancerous ESCC unstained ("void lesions"); voiding lesions can be **false positive** over neosquamous tissue and in some well-differentiated squamous cancers, so observation and biopsy are still required. Methylene blue or crystal violet is used for margin definition in Barrett's esophagus (affinity for intestinal epithelium; highlights dysplastic pit patterns).

### Imaging

- **EUS overstages Barrett's neoplasia, T1b especially** — pooled false-positive rate **35.85%** (95% CI 25.3%–47.8%) across 11 studies; **36%** of EUS-diagnosed T1b lesions were low-grade dysplasia, high-grade dysplasia or T1a on histology.
- In 174 ESCC patients, **NBI was more accurate than EUS** at separating tumors confined to superficial mucosa from those involving muscularis mucosae or SM1.
- **PET/CT is not needed for early-stage cancer** despite routinely being recommended in esophageal-cancer workup: benign findings on initial staging act as confounders and their evaluation delays therapy.
- EUS remains the best available submucosal imaging; volumetric laser endomicroscopy with infrared light may discriminate submucosal disease better (preliminary).

### Lymph node metastasis risk

**Table 1 — predictors of higher LNM risk in esophageal cancer** (reproduced):

| Factor | Higher-risk feature |
|---|---|
| Tumor size | Tumors larger than **2 cm** |
| Morphology | Paris **0-I** protruded lesions; Paris **0-III** excavated lesions |
| Narrow-band imaging | **Type B3 IPCL** pattern in ESCC; **avascular area** |
| Histology | **Poorly differentiated** tumors; **lymphovascular invasion (LVI)** on the resected specimen |
| Depth of invasion | **ESCC** with submucosal invasion **>200 µm**; **EAC** with submucosal invasion **>500 µm** |
| Biological markers | **E-cadherin**; **cyclin D1** |

**Table 2 — LNM rate in submucosal esophageal cancer by depth and cell type** (reproduced; from a review of surgically resected patients):

| Depth of invasion | ESCC | EAC |
|---|---|---|
| **SM1** | **27%** | **6%** |
| **SM2** | **36%** | **23%** |
| **SM3** | **55%** | **58%** |

- Other depth-specific figures given in the text: mucosal ESCC limited to epithelium (**M1**) or lamina propria (**M2**) has **negligible** LNM risk; deep muscularis mucosae (**M3**) ≈ **10%**; ESCC **SM1 8%–26.5%**, ESCC **SM2 up to 60%**.
- **EAC, well-differentiated and <2 cm: LNM 4.2%** (782 US esophagectomy patients); poorly differentiated and/or >2 cm had higher rates.
- In 90 T1 EAC esophagectomies (M. D. Anderson), LNM in **8%** of superficial-submucosal tumors; stratified by size, **75% of T1b tumors larger than 1.2 cm** had LNM.
- Meta-analysis of 7645 T1b patients: LNM depends on depth within submucosa **and** histology; overall **LNM higher in SM1 and SM2 ESCC than in EAC**.
- **Ideal scenario for endoscopic treatment alone:** well-differentiated, **<2 cm**, T1b limited to **SM1**, **no LVI** on the resected specimen.
- Immunohistochemistry markers associated with higher LNM (emerging): **E-cadherin, cyclin D1**.
- **Practical caveat:** micrometer-based depth assessment is **harder to obtain in US centres** and should be discussed with pathologists before starting endoscopic resections.

### AJCC 8th-edition staging of early disease

- **Grade (G) is a new modifier** in the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging of early esophageal cancer: **GX** differentiation cannot be assessed, **G1** well, **G2** moderately, **G3** poorly differentiated.
- Differentiation is assessed by percentage of well-formed glands: **>95%** in differentiated tumors, **<50%** in poorly differentiated.
- **T1a G2–G3 → stage IB**; **T1a G1 → stage IA**; **all T1b lesions → stage IB**. **Not location-specific, even in squamous cancers.**

### Endoscopic resection vs esophagectomy — the data cited

- **EMR vs ESD:** EMR cannot accurately assess lateral margins in piecemeal specimens; **ESD gives en-bloc specimens regardless of tumor size** with lower residual and local recurrence. ESD is also a **diagnostic** tool — it upstages or downstages the tumor on examination of the entire resected specimen.
- **For T1b, esophagectomy remains the mainstay** because LNM prevalence is not negligible.
- **Clinical T1bN0M0 ESCC, 173 patients** (102 radical esophagectomy vs definitive CRT): 3-/5-year survival **87.0%/77.7% surgery** vs **77.8%/68.6% CRT**; recurrence **11.8%** (12/102) surgery vs **28.2%** (20/71) CRT.
- **Siewert type II adenocarcinoma, 79 patients** (40 ESD vs 39 surgery; 81% T1a, 19% T1b): 5-year overall survival **93.9% ESD vs 97.3% surgery (P = .376)**; treatment-related adverse events **10.0% vs 17.9% (P = .308)**, no mortality in either arm. Synchronous or metachronous lesions in 4 ESD patients. **ESD data in esophageal adenocarcinoma is sparse.**

### Endoscopic resection combined with other modalities

- **Endoscopic resection then CRT** (for patients who decline or are unfit for surgery), 66 ESCC patients (65% T1b): 3-/5-year survival **87%/75%**, similar to esophagectomy. Metastatic recurrence in **6 of 36 with LNM** and **0 of 30 without LNM**.
- **ESD + CRT vs CRT alone**, 47 early ESCC patients: 3-year overall survival **90% vs 63.2%**; recurrence **6.3% vs 29.0%**.
- **ESD + RFA** for clearance of Barrett's dysplasia, 27 patients (2 with T1b): dysplasia cleared in **26 (96%)**, intestinal metaplasia in **23 (85%)**. A prospective single-centre trial eradicated intestinal metaplasia in **8 of 10** EAC patients after ESD + RFA. Generally **1–2 RFA sessions** sufficed.

### Recommendations

The document issues **no numbered or graded statements** — it is an Expert Review whose guidance sits in one closing prose section, with no GRADE assessment, no Best Practice Advice numbering and no strength labels. The itemization below is the wiki's; the content is the document's.

1. **ESD or EMR can be successfully applied to submucosal invasive esophageal cancers that have a low risk of metastatic potential** — based on the authors' systematic review of the literature.
2. **Metastatic risk is higher in submucosal squamous cell carcinoma than in submucosal adenocarcinoma** — the cell type changes the calculus at the same depth.
3. **Stage these tumors with EUS**, while recognizing that **EUS accuracy for depth of invasion is limited**.
4. **Additional treatment is determined by tumor grade, lymphovascular invasion status, and depth of invasion** — the three factors with direct influence on metastatic potential.
5. **In patients with submucosal invasion plus risk factors, adjuvant chemotherapy and radiation may mitigate metastatic potential.**
6. **Research priority:** novel biological and immunohistochemistry markers to predict tumor behaviour and LNM in T1b esophageal cancer.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Against [[aga-2021-post-esd-surveillance]] on curability of submucosal ESCC.** AGA 2021 holds that in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma **no depth is curative once the muscularis mucosae is reached** — m3 or any submucosal invasion is noncurative. This 2019 CPU is more permissive: ESD/EMR "can be successfully applied to submucosal invasive cancers that have a low risk of metastatic potential," and it prints an SM1 ESCC LNM range as low as 8%. **The newer 2021 document governs the wiki pages**; this one is kept for the substaging definitions, the nodal-risk tables and the staging caveats, which AGA 2021 does not supply. Note that even on this paper's own Table 2 the SM1 ESCC rate is **27%**, which is not a low-risk number — the permissive sentence and the table pull in different directions within the same document.
- **Internal inconsistency in the SM1 ESCC nodal risk.** The text calls ESCC SM1 risk "relatively low (8%–26.5%)" while Table 2 gives **27%** for the same stratum, citing a different review. The wiki carries the table value and the range side by side rather than choosing.
- **Lesion-size threshold vs [[asge-2023-esd]].** This CPU frames **2 cm** as the size above which LNM risk rises (and <2 cm as part of the ideal endoscopic candidate); ASGE 2023 uses **15 mm (squamous) / 20 mm (adenocarcinoma)** thresholds, but for a different question — ESD *versus* EMR technique, not endoscopic resection versus surgery. The two are not in conflict; the distinction is easy to lose.
- **EUS is both recommended and discounted.** Recommendation 3 says stage with EUS; the staging section documents a 36% false-positive rate at the T1b level and notes NBI outperformed it. [[asge-2019-barretts-screening-surveillance]] goes further and **recommends against** routine EUS to separate mucosal from submucosal Barrett's neoplasia (strong, moderate quality) — the position [[esophageal-adenocarcinoma]] asserts.
- **Figures 1–4 are not captured.** Figure 1 (early ESCC as a Lugol voiding lesion), Figure 2 (Paris type 0-IIa+c adenocarcinoma), Figure 3 (radial EUS showing submucosal invasion), Figure 4 (post-ESD defect with central fibrosis and exposed muscularis propria fibers) are endoscopic/EUS appearance images that belong on the clinical pages.
- **Evidence base.** Every comparative series cited is retrospective and small (40–173 patients) apart from the pooled analyses; there is no randomized comparison of endoscopic resection against esophagectomy for T1b disease. The paper's own framing is that the question is unsettled.
