## Summary

Expert-panel consensus commissioned by the International Society for Hepatic Encephalopathy and Nitrogen Metabolism (ISHEN) at its 13th Symposium, presented at the 14th ISHEN Meeting and circulated for review. It addresses the nutritional management of patients who have cirrhosis **and** hepatic encephalopathy (HE), on the principle that any dietary change aimed at cognition must sit inside the dietary requirements dictated by cirrhosis itself — dietary constituents are not to be changed in isolation.

Its most durable contribution to the wiki is the **two Royal Free Hospital instruments reproduced as figures**: the RFH Global Assessment (RFH-GA, Figure 1) and the **RFH Nutritional Prioritizing Tool (RFH-NPT, Figure 2)**. Figure 2 is the only place in the ingested corpus that prints the RFH-NPT's **item-level scoring** — which answers score which points, and the point bands that map to management. [[borhofen-2016-rfh-npt-cirrhosis]] validates the tool and prints the bands but not the items; [[aasld-2021-malnutrition-cirrhosis]] names the tool and its three input domains only.

On substance, the panel's position is that **energy and protein requirements in HE do not differ materially from cirrhosis per se** (35–40 kcal/kg and 1.2–1.5 g/kg ideal body weight daily), that **protein restriction should be avoided**, and that meal pattern matters as much as totals — small meals through the waking hours, and a late-evening snack of at least 50 g complex carbohydrate. Vegetable and dairy protein are preferred to meat and fish protein; branched-chain amino acid (BCAA) supplements are reserved for the occasional genuinely protein-intolerant patient. Fiber 25–45 g/day is encouraged, probiotics are explicitly *not* settled, a 2-week multivitamin course is justifiable in decompensated cirrhosis, and hyponatremia must always be corrected slowly.

Nutritional assessment in cirrhosis is treated as a methodological problem in its own right: percentage ideal body weight and plasma albumin are unusable (fluid retention, altered protein metabolism); anthropometry, bioelectric impedance analysis and dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry rest on fat-free-mass density and hydration assumptions that cirrhosis violates; and **hand-grip dynamometry and CT-defined central sarcopenia have no predictive validity in women**, because muscle function correlates with muscle mass in men but not women and muscle mass is better preserved in women with cirrhosis.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Grading scheme (Table 1, modified from Shekelle)

- **Strength of recommendation:** **Strong = 1** (weighing evidence quality, presumed patient-important outcomes, costs) · **Weak = 2** (variability in preferences/values or more uncertainty; made with less certainty, higher cost or resource consumption).
- **Quality of evidence:** **High = A** (further research unlikely to change confidence in the estimate) · **Moderate = B** (further research may change confidence) · **Low = C** (further research very likely to affect confidence).

| # | Statement | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | Encourage ingestion of diets containing **25–45 g of fiber daily** (prebiotics). | 2B |

> **Probiotics are deliberately not recommended.** The panel reviewed them and concluded the benefits and harms remain uncertain; comparative efficacy, optimal doses and duration are listed as research questions, not advice. Lactulose is classified by some as a prebiotic and its efficacy in HE is treated as proven.

### Table 5 — nutritional management of patients with HE, by nutritional status and body weight

*Estimated BMI uses **estimated dry weight** where there is fluid retention; energy and protein use **ideal body weight**.*

| | Adequately nourished | | | Moderately malnourished / at risk | | | Severely malnourished | | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Estimated BMI (kg/m²)** | 20–30 | 30–40 | >40 | 18–30 | 30–40 | >40 | 18–30 | 30–40 | >40 |
| **Daily energy (kcal/kg)** | 35–40 | 25–35 | 20–25 | 35–40 | 25–35\* | 20–25\* | 35–40 | 25–35\* | 20–25\* |
| **Daily protein (g/kg)** | 1.2–1.5 | 1.0–1.5 | 1.0–1.5 | 1.2–1.5 | 1.2–1.5 | 1.2–1.5 | 1.2–1.5 | 1.2–1.5 | 1.2–1.5 |

\* Achieved by reducing the carbohydrate and fat content of the diet and increasing dietary fiber.

Applied across **all** columns of Table 5:
- **Meal pattern:** small frequent meals throughout the waking hours.
- **Late-evening snack:** encourage ingestion of **50 g of complex carbohydrate**.
- **Dietary nitrogen source:** adequately nourished — promote vegetable and dairy protein **to the level of tolerance**; moderately/severely malnourished — promote **high protein intake per patient preference to encourage intake**.
- **Daily fiber:** encourage diets containing **25–45 g**, especially in overweight patients. *Useful to aid weight loss, but care is needed not to induce diarrhea in patients receiving lactulose.*
- **Decompensated cirrhosis:** supplement as indicated.
- **HE incompletely/poorly controlled:** consider probiotics and/or BCAA supplements.

### Figure 2 — RFH-NPT item-level scoring (the instrument itself)

**Step 1 — Does this patient have acute alcoholic hepatitis, or are they being tube fed?** No = **score 0**, continue to Step 2. **Yes = score 6** → go straight to the management guidelines (high risk).

**Step 2 — Does the patient have fluid overload (peripheral oedema/ascites)?** No = **score 0** → score the left-hand column. Yes = **score 1** → score the right-hand column.

| No fluid overload — score these three | Points |
|---|---|
| BMI >20 (>30 obese) | 0 |
| BMI 18.5–20 | 1 |
| BMI <18.5 | 2 |
| Unplanned weight loss in past 3–6 months <5% | 0 |
| Unplanned weight loss 5–10% | 1 |
| Unplanned weight loss >10% | 2 |
| Patient acutely ill **and** there has been, or is likely to be, **no nutritional intake for >5 days** | 2 |

| Fluid overload present — score these three | Points |
|---|---|
| Does the fluid overload interfere with the ability to eat? No | 0 |
| …Occasionally | 1 |
| …Yes | 2 |
| Has dietary intake reduced by ½ or more over the last 5 days? No | 0 |
| …Yes | 2 |
| Has the patient lost weight in the past 3–6 months? No | 0 |
| …Difficult to assess as patient taking diuretics | 1 |
| …Yes | 2 |

**Step 3 — add the scores together to calculate the overall risk of malnutrition.**

| Total | Risk | Management |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Low | Routine clinical care · repeat screening weekly |
| **1** | Moderate | Routine clinical care · monitor food charts · encourage eating and offer snacks · repeat screening weekly |
| **2–7** | High | **Discuss referral with dietitian** · monitor food charts · encourage eating and offer snacks · repeat screening weekly |

The schema takes **less than 3 minutes**, can be used by non-specialist staff, has excellent intra- and interobserver reproducibility and significant external validity against RFH-GA, and was validated in a UK multicenter trial — though the panel notes further, more extensive validation is needed.

### Figure 1 — RFH-GA (Royal Free Hospital Global Assessment)

- Semi-structured algorithm combining **BMI calculated from estimated dry body weight**, **mid-arm muscle circumference (MAMC)** and **dietary intake**, sorting patients into **adequately nourished · moderately malnourished (or suspected of being) · severely malnourished**.
- A **subjective override** — based on factors such as profound recent weight loss, or recent significant improvement in appetite and dietary intake — **may modify the classification by one category only**.
- Excellent intra- and interobserver reproducibility; validated against a multicomponent model of body composition. **Needs skilled staff and takes up to 1 hour**, which is why the RFH-NPT was developed.
- *The figure's branch-by-branch assignment of BMI / MAMC / intake combinations to the three categories is a raster image whose arrow paths cannot be read unambiguously; it is not reproduced here.*

### Why the usual assessment tools fail in cirrhosis

- **Unusable:** percentage ideal body weight, plasma albumin — confounded by fluid retention and altered protein metabolism.
- **Unreliable used singly:** anthropometry, bioelectric impedance analysis (BIA), dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) — all assume a two-component (fat / fat-free mass) model whose density and hydration assumptions cirrhosis violates. Reported malnutrition prevalence consequently ranges **5.4%–68.2%**, **5.0%–74%** and **19%–99%** across studies using multiple traditional techniques.
- **Subjective Global Assessment (SGA)** consistently **underestimates** malnutrition prevalence versus objective measures and does not accurately predict outcome.
- **Sex matters:** hand-grip dynamometry and CT-defined central sarcopenia are **not reliable in women** — muscle function correlates with mass in men but not women, and muscle mass is better preserved in women with cirrhosis. Sarcopenia prevalence is significantly higher in men, and rises with liver-dysfunction severity in men only.

### Energy, protein and meal pattern — supporting points

- Resting energy expenditure is increased relative to lean body mass; cirrhosis produces a **starvation-like response to fasting** (early excessive lipolysis, switch from glycogenolysis to gluconeogenesis), and gluconeogenesis is energy-expensive.
- **Ideal body weight may be the safest basis** for calculation — actual weight, or errors estimating dry weight, push values to the extremes. The panel debated this explicitly.
- **Avoid fasting longer than 3–6 hours during the daytime.**
- A systematic review of the **late-evening snack** found it reverses the aberrant substrate-utilization pattern, beats daytime calorie supplementation alone for substrate utilization and nitrogen retention, may improve quality of life and survival, and may reduce HE frequency and severity. Optimal caloric content cannot be determined from published data, but evidence suggests **at least 50 g complex carbohydrate**.
- **Protein restriction should be avoided**, except perhaps very briefly during stabilization of gastrointestinal bleeding; patients with HE tolerate and benefit from normoproteinemic diets. Surveys in the UK, Australia and Italy show protein restriction is still widespread and the panel says this needs urgently addressing.
- **Obesity:** weight loss is encouraged in well-compensated overweight/obese cirrhosis — gentle, monitored, proportionate reductions in both energy and protein plus increased physical activity. In **decompensated** cirrhosis, reduce carbohydrate and fat while **maintaining high protein**, which may need to exceed the recommended range if protein depletion progresses; **dietary protein retention remains efficient up to 2 g/kg/day without precipitating or exacerbating HE**.
- **Vegetable protein:** patients should take as high a percentage as they can; **30–40 g/day of vegetable protein** is usually achievable provided they are not also salt-restricted (which makes the diet unpalatable). Rationale: more fiber (prebiotic — decreased transit time, reduced intraluminal pH, increased fecal ammonia excretion), low in methionine and cysteine (mercaptan/indole precursors), high in ornithine and arginine (urea-cycle ammonia disposal).
- **BCAAs:** little convincing evidence that supplementation itself significantly benefits HE. Long-term oral BCAA may confer nutritional benefit and improve event-free survival in cirrhosis, most likely attributable to **leucine alone**; no long-term nutritional intervention study confirms leucine as a pharmaconutrient.

### Micronutrients — supporting points

- **Thiamine:** vigorous **parenteral** thiamine supplementation is **mandatory whenever Wernicke's encephalopathy is suspected**. Both alcohol-related and non-alcohol-related cirrhosis may be vitamin deficient.
- **Distinguishing Wernicke's encephalopathy from HE:** ataxia, confusion and memory loss occur in both; **nystagmus, ptosis and paralysis of lateral conjugate gaze** point to Wernicke's.
- **Hyponatremia** may worsen HE and is more likely when sodium intake is low while water intake is maintained or increased; correct slowly, especially in HE, because the risk of **central pontine myelinolysis** is high, particularly around liver transplantation.
- **Zinc:** tissue concentrations are reduced in cirrhosis and the mechanistic case is good (ornithine transcarbamylase and glutamine synthetase are zinc-dependent), but supplementation trials conflict and **the largest double-blind trial showed no significant benefit** on neuropsychometric performance.
- **Manganese:** total body levels are elevated in cirrhosis and accumulate in globus pallidus, caudate and putamen (T1-MRI hyperintensity), but **no clear relationship** links accumulation to HE development. Hence the recommendation is to avoid long-term manganese-containing formulations, not to treat.
- Hypercalcemia and hypomagnesemia can themselves produce confusion → coma and confound the diagnosis of HE.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Energy target vs newer guidance.** ISHEN 2013 gives **35–40 kcal/kg ideal body weight**; [[acg-2026-preventive-care-cirrhosis]] gives **30–35 kcal/kg/day** and [[aasld-2021-malnutrition-cirrhosis]] **≥35 kcal/kg/day**. The newer guidelines are higher-priority by publication date and are what the wiki pages assert; ISHEN's figure is recorded here and its obesity-banded version (Table 5) agrees with ACG 2026's banding (25–35 at BMI 30–40, 20–25 at BMI >40).
- **Protein in obesity.** ISHEN Table 5 drops protein to **1.0–1.5 g/kg** for *adequately nourished* obese patients while holding **1.2–1.5 g/kg** everywhere else — a distinction no newer wiki source makes.
- **Probiotics.** ISHEN finds the evidence insufficient to recommend them. Newer HE guidance on the wiki should be read as superseding this if it recommends them; flag any page asserting a probiotic recommendation sourced only to older material.
- **Zinc.** ISHEN reports the largest double-blind trial as negative for neuropsychometric benefit, while [[acg-2024-ald]] recommends zinc supplementation in alcohol-associated hepatitis on deficiency-prevalence and mortality-association grounds. These are different questions (correcting deficiency vs treating cognition) and both stand.
- **RFH-GA branch map** is not reproduced — the figure is a raster image whose arrow paths are not unambiguously readable. The three input variables, three output categories and the one-category subjective override are captured.
