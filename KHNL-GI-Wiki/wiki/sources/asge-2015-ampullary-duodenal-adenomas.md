## Summary

ASGE Standards of Practice guideline on endoscopic evaluation and management of **ampullary** (major duodenal papilla) and **nonampullary duodenal adenomas**. Both can be sporadic or arise in genetic syndromes (FAP, Peutz-Jeghers) and both carry malignant potential. Endoscopic resection has become a viable alternative to surgery (pancreaticoduodenectomy/Whipple or transduodenal ampullectomy) in selected cases, with lower adverse-event rates than local surgical excision.

Pre-treatment evaluation: a **side-viewing duodenoscope** is generally needed to visualize the papilla; biopsy of suspicious lesions is required before resection to confirm adenoma and exclude cancer (sample away from the pancreatic duct orifice to avoid pancreatitis; ≥6 specimens or sampling within 10 days of sphincterotomy reduces false-negatives, which range 16–60% for carcinoma). **EUS / intraductal US** is reserved for large ampullary lesions or those with features concerning for malignancy and is superior to CT/MRI/transabdominal US for tumor (T) staging. **ERCP** with biliary and pancreatic duct evaluation is performed at the time of papillectomy to assess intraductal extension; <1 cm of ductal extension does not necessarily preclude endoscopic therapy.

Endoscopic resection of the papilla is termed **endoscopic papillectomy** (true "ampullectomy" is a surgical term requiring ductal reimplantation). En bloc snare resection with electrocautery is preferred when the lesion can be fully ensnared (shorter time, complete pathology, less residual fibrosis); piecemeal resection is used for lesions >2 cm. Submucosal injection, chromoendoscopy, sphincterotomy, and ablative therapies (APC most common, 50–60 W) are adjuncts. **Prophylactic pancreatic duct stenting plus rectal indomethacin** reduce post-procedure pancreatitis. Papillectomy success is 46–92% with recurrence up to 33%; all patients require post-resection surveillance.

For **nonampullary duodenal polyps**, the approach mirrors right-colon polypectomy given the thin duodenal wall. EMR is the mainstay; **ESD is not recommended beyond the duodenal bulb** (perforation rates 23–35% in high-volume series). Lesions with >33% circumferential involvement should be considered for surgery. Patients with sporadic ampullary or duodenal adenomas have higher colorectal neoplasia risk and should be offered screening colonoscopy. Search covered 1980–December 2013; recommendations graded by GRADE evidence quality.

## All Recommendations (Verbatim, with GRADE Evidence Quality)

Evidence-quality symbols (GRADE): 4444 = High; 444B = Moderate; 44BB = Low; 4BBB = Very low. Recommendation strength: "we recommend" = stronger; "we suggest" = weaker.

1. We recommend that biopsy specimens be obtained and evaluated from ampullary lesions suspicious for neoplasia before attempted endoscopic resection. **(Moderate — 444B)**
2. We recommend EUS for large ampullary lesions or duodenal polyps with features concerning for malignancy before endoscopic or surgical resection. **(Moderate — 444B)**
3. We suggest ERCP with both biliary and pancreatic duct evaluation at the time of endoscopic papillectomy to assess for evidence of extension into either ductal system. **(Low — 44BB)**
4. We recommend prophylactic pancreatic duct stent placement and rectal indomethacin during papillectomy to reduce the risk of postprocedural pancreatitis. **(Moderate — 444B)**
5. We recommend that patients undergoing endoscopic removal of ampullary and duodenal neoplasms be included in an endoscopic surveillance program to ensure complete tissue removal and assess for disease recurrence. **(Moderate — 444B)**
6. We suggest that patients with sporadic ampullary or duodenal adenomas be offered screening colonoscopy. **(Low — 44BB)**

## Key Findings / Claims

- **Surgical morbidity/mortality:** Whipple/transduodenal ampullectomy associated with anastomotic dehiscence/fistula in up to 9%/14% and mortality 1–9%. Local surgical excision adverse-event rate (~29%) appears higher than endoscopy (~14%).
- **Ampullary biopsy:** diagnostic (adenoma + carcinoma) rates 45–80%; false-negative for carcinoma 16–60%. Minimize false-negatives with ≥6 specimens or biopsy within 10 days of sphincterotomy; orient forceps parallel to folds to reduce fibrosis.
- **Features suggesting malignancy / surgical referral:** firmness, ulceration, friability, **non-lifting of the periampullary component with submucosal injection** (failure to lift = strongest predictor of malignancy in one study). Many authors avoid endoscopic resection for lesions ≥4 cm.
- **Imaging:** EUS superior to CT/MRI/transabdominal US for T staging; MRI superior to EUS for nodal staging; intraductal US may visualize the tumor better than EUS but can overstage. Lesions <1 cm without malignant features may not require US before resection.
- **ERCP/intraductal extension:** <1 cm of CBD or PD extension does not preclude endoscopic therapy; balloon dilation/catheters can expose intraductal tissue for resection.
- **Papillectomy technique:** en bloc snare + electrocautery preferred when lesion fully ensnarable; piecemeal for >2 cm or residual tissue. No proven superiority of snare type, current type, or snare orientation. Larger-channel therapeutic duodenoscope eases instrument passage. Retrieve specimens immediately; glucagon may reduce peristalsis; CO₂ insufflation advantageous if perforation.
- **Post-ERCP pancreatitis prophylaxis:** prophylactic PD stenting reduces PEP (one small RCT, n=19, of stenting after papillectomy positive); rectal indomethacin + temporary PD stenting lower PEP risk/severity in high-risk populations. Prophylactic biliary stenting not routinely recommended unless inadequate biliary drainage or microperforation concern.
- **Ablation:** APC (50–60 W) most common for residual/recurrent superficial tissue; PD stent usually placed before ablating near the PD orifice; ablated tissue is not available for pathology — biopsy suspicious areas before ablation.
- **Outcomes:** papillectomy clinical success 46–92%; recurrence up to 33%. Predictors of long-term success: age >48 y, lesion ≤24 mm, male sex, absence of polyposis syndrome (e.g., FAP). Intraductal growth → lower cure, more surgery.
- **Surveillance:** all post-papillectomy patients undergo surveillance endoscopy with scar biopsy, typically 1–6 months after the index procedure, then every 3–12 months for ≥2 years; endpoints not established.
- **Nonampullary duodenal polyps:** EMR is mainstay (approach like right colon given thin wall); submucosal injection cushion for flat lesions; lack of lift suggests malignancy/prior manipulation. **ESD beyond the bulb not recommended** (perforation 23–35%; same Japanese group reported no perforations with EMR). Cold-snare polypectomy described for lesions >1 cm to avoid cautery injury. >33% circumferential involvement → consider surgery. Giant (>3 cm) hemicircumferential LSTs have higher adverse-event (bleeding) rates (26.3% vs 3.2% for <3 cm). EUS role unclear but may affect management of polyps >2 cm.
- **CRC risk:** sporadic ampullary/duodenal adenomas are associated with increased colorectal neoplasia → offer screening colonoscopy.
