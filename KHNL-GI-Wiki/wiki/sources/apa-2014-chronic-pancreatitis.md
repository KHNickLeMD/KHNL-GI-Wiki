# APA Practice Guidelines in Chronic Pancreatitis: Evidence-Based Report on Diagnostic Guidelines (2014)

## Bibliographic Info

- **Article:** Conwell DL, Lee LS, Yadav D, et al. American Pancreatic Association Practice Guidelines in Chronic Pancreatitis: Evidence-Based Report on Diagnostic Guidelines. *Pancreas.* 2014;43(8):1143–1162.
- **First author:** Darwin L. Conwell, MD
- **Year:** 2014
- **Journal:** Pancreas
- **DOI:** [10.1097/MPA.0000000000000237](https://doi.org/10.1097/MPA.0000000000000237)
- **Type:** GRADE-based consensus practice guideline (2011 APA Chronic Pancreatitis Conference)
- **Scope:** Part 1 of 3 — **diagnosis only**. Parts 2 (treatment) and 3 (complications) are separate documents.

---

## Summary

First United States practice guideline for [[chronic-pancreatitis]], addressing the central problem that CP diagnosis remains unreliable in early disease. Reviews every diagnostic modality across 9 topics (epidemiology, pathologic definitions, US/CT, MRI, [[endoscopic-ultrasound|EUS]], [[ercp|ERCP]], indirect and direct pancreatic function testing, imaging–function–histology correlation) and grades diagnostic criteria as **definitive, probable, or insufficient evidence**.

Proposes the **STEP-wise algorithm** (**S**urvey → **T**omography → **E**ndoscopy → **P**ancreas function testing), escalating from non-invasive to invasive. The algorithm is explicitly built to **maximize specificity** in patients with chronic abdominal pain and equivocal imaging, accepting missed diagnoses to avoid over-diagnosis.

Also proposes a characterization nomenclature for established CP combining **TIGAR-O** etiology + **Cambridge** gland morphology + physiologic state (exocrine/endocrine function) for research uniformity.

---

## Key Statements

**EUS (Topic 5)** — all three are strong recommendations:

- The ideal threshold number of EUS criteria to diagnose CP is **not firmly established**, but **≥5 features strongly suggests** and **≤2 strongly refutes** the diagnosis (low-quality evidence). *This threshold belongs to the conventional/standard EUS criteria, not to the Rosemont classification.*
- EUS features of CP are **not necessarily pathologic** — they occur with normal aging, as normal variants, or from non-pathologic asymptomatic fibrosis without exocrine/endocrine dysfunction (low).
- **Poor interobserver agreement** for EUS CP features limits diagnostic accuracy and overall utility (moderate).

The guideline's stated posture on EUS: *"If we are to error in the use of EUS, we should do so in a manner that favors more stringent diagnostic criteria to avoid over-diagnosis and delivery of unnecessary and potentially risky therapies."*

**ERCP (Topic 6)** — endoscopic retrograde pancreatogram is rarely used diagnostically; Cambridge–histology correlation is highest in advanced CP; multiple confounders limit interpretation of Cambridge ductal changes.

**Rosemont** — summarized and credited as an important step forward for using weighted criteria, but flagged as **based solely on expert opinion**. See the caveat below.

## ⚠ Known error in this source (Rosemont weighting)

The guideline's Rosemont paragraph (p. 14–15 of the PMC author manuscript) states that parenchymal **major A** criteria included *"(1) hyperechoic foci with shadowing and (2) well-circumscribed lobularity."*

**This is incorrect** and is internally inconsistent with the same paragraph, which then assigns *lobularity with honeycombing* to major B and *lobularity with noncontiguous lobules* to minor — leaving no lobularity variant available to be major A.

In the primary Rosemont consensus ([Catalano et al., *Gastrointest Endosc* 2009;69:1251–61](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19243769/)), "well-circumscribed, ≥5 mm structures with hyperechoic rims relative to their central areas" is the **definition of lobularity itself**, not a separate criterion. Lobularity is then split into honeycombing (**major B**) and non-honeycombing (**minor**). There are exactly **two major A criteria**: hyperechoic foci **with** shadowing (parenchymal) and MPD calculi (ductal).

The guideline's **diagnostic combinations are correct** — "consistent with CP" = 1 major A + ≥3 minor, **or** 1 major A + major B, **or** 2 major A. Use APA for the combinations, Catalano for the feature weighting.

## Clinical Relevance

The reference for *how confidently* CP can be diagnosed at each rung of the workup, and the source of the specificity-first STEP-wise approach used on [[chronic-pancreatitis]]. Its EUS caveats — non-pathologic features, poor interobserver agreement, unestablished thresholds — are the reason EUS findings (including a Rosemont score) should not stand alone as a diagnosis. Superseded on management by [[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]] and [[asge-2024-chronic-pancreatitis-endoscopy]], but still the fullest US treatment of diagnostic criteria.
