## Summary

ASGE Standards-of-Practice guideline on periprocedural management of anticoagulants and antiplatelet agents (APAs). It frames the decision around four factors: procedure urgency, procedure bleeding risk, the drug's effect on bleeding, and the thromboembolic risk of interrupting therapy. It provides a duration-of-action / reversal reference table for every antithrombotic class, a procedure bleeding-risk dichotomy (high vs low), and a CV-risk × bleeding-risk management matrix (Table 13) for the elective setting, plus separate guidance for urgent/emergent bleeding.

This is an **older tier-1 source**; the newer **[[acg-cag-2022-anticoag-gi-bleeding]]** (2022 ACG/CAG) governs the wiki's periendoscopic and acute-bleeding recommendations where they overlap. ASGE 2016's net-new contribution to the wiki is its **drug-specific duration-of-action / hold-interval / reversal reference table** and specific pre-procedure hold intervals, which were added to **[[anticoagulation-gi-bleeding]]** without overwriting the ACG/CAG framework.

## Key Findings / Claims

- **Procedure bleeding risk (Table 3).** High-risk: polypectomy, biliary/pancreatic sphincterotomy, variceal treatment, PEG/PEJ placement, EUS-FNA, endoscopic hemostasis, tumor ablation, cystgastrostomy, ampullary resection, EMR, ESD, pneumatic/bougie dilation, therapeutic balloon enteroscopy, POEM, Zenker's therapy. Low-risk: diagnostic EGD/colonoscopy/flex sig (incl. mucosal biopsy), ERCP with stent without sphincterotomy, push/diagnostic balloon enteroscopy, capsule endoscopy, enteral stent deployment (controversial), EUS without FNA, APC, Barrett's ablation. *PEG on ASA or clopidogrel monotherapy is low risk (not DAPT); EUS-FNA of solid masses on ASA/NSAIDs is low risk.*
- **Duration of action / reversal (Table 2, erratum-corrected).** Aspirin 7-10 d (hold, ± platelets if urgent); NSAIDs varies; dipyridamole 2-3 d; cilostazol 2 d; thienopyridines: clopidogrel/prasugrel 5-7 d, ticagrelor 3-5 d, ticlopidine 10-14 d; GPIIb/IIIa: tirofiban 1-2 s, eptifibatide 4 h, abciximab 24 h; vorapaxar 5-13 d; warfarin 5 d (reverse: vitamin K, PCC); UFH IV 2-6 h / SQ 12-24 h (protamine, partial); LMWH 24 h (protamine ± rVIIa); fondaparinux 36-48 h; factor Xa inhibitors and dabigatran per renal function (reverse: charcoal if last dose <2-3 h, nonactivated/activated PCC; dabigatran also HD).
- **Elective setting (Table 13).** Continue warfarin and NOAC for low-bleeding-risk procedures regardless of CV risk. For high-bleeding-risk procedures: discontinue AC; restart warfarin same day; delay reinitiating NOAC until hemostasis; if high CV risk, add bridge therapy. APAs: continue ASA/NSAIDs; hold thienopyridines ≥5 days before high-risk procedures (ticagrelor 3-5 d, others 5-7 d) or switch to ASA monotherapy.
- **Bridging** decisions in moderate-risk patients should be individualized (Table 5 mechanical valve / VTE risk).
- **Urgent/emergent bleeding.** Hold anticoagulants to achieve hemostasis; give 4-factor PCC + vitamin K (ACCP) or FFP (AHA/ACC accepts either) for life-threatening warfarin-associated bleeding; endoscopic therapy need not be delayed if INR <2.5; give UFH (short half-life) after successful hemostasis of high-risk stigmata; consult the prescriber before stopping APAs in patients with recent (<1 y) drug-eluting stent, <30 d bare-metal stent, or <90 d ACS.
- **Elective procedures should be deferred** in patients with recently placed intracoronary stents / ACS until the minimum DAPT duration (ACCF/ACG) has elapsed.

## Recommendations (verbatim)

**A. Elective procedures — patients on anticoagulants**
1. Recommend elective endoscopic procedures be deferred until short-term anticoagulation (eg, warfarin for VTE) is completed. (High/strong)
2. Suggest discontinuing anticoagulation (warfarin, NOACs) for the drug-specific interval if high-risk endoscopic procedures are planned in a patient at low thromboembolic risk. (Low/conditional)
3. Suggest continuing warfarin and NOAC periendoscopically in patients undergoing low-risk procedures. (Low/conditional)
4. Suggest bridge therapy for patients undergoing high-risk procedures who are at high thromboembolic risk. (Low/conditional)
5. Suggest warfarin be restarted the same day as the procedure in all patients without ongoing bleeding. (Low/conditional)
6. Suggest reinitiation of NOACs after high-risk procedures be delayed until adequate hemostasis is ensured; if NOACs cannot be restarted within 12-24 h after a high-risk procedure in a high-thrombotic-risk patient, consider UFH bridge thromboprophylaxis. (Low/conditional)

**A. Elective procedures — patients on APA therapy**
1. Suggest low-dose ASA and NSAIDs may be continued safely periendoscopically. (High/conditional)
2. Recommend thienopyridines be continued for all low-risk procedures. (High/strong)
3. Recommend discontinuing thienopyridines ≥5-7 days before a high-risk procedure (or switching to ASA monotherapy), continued until the thienopyridine can be safely resumed. (High/strong)
4. Recommend elective procedures be deferred in patients with recently placed intracoronary stents and/or ACS until the minimum antithrombotic therapy duration is complete. (High/strong)
5. Suggest thienopyridines be withheld ≥5-7 days (ticagrelor 3-5 d) before high-risk procedures and that ASA be continued in patients requiring dual APA. (High/conditional)

**B. Urgent/emergent procedures — anticoagulants**
1. Recommend patients with acute GI bleeding on anticoagulation have agents held to facilitate hemostasis. (High/strong)
2. Recommend either 4-factor PCC + vitamin K or FFP for life-threatening GI bleeding on warfarin (ACCP advocates PCC only; AHA/ACC supports either). (High/strong)
3. Suggest endoscopic therapy not be delayed in patients with serious GI bleeding and INR <2.5. (Low/conditional)
4. Suggest patients who require anticoagulation receive UFH (short half-life) after successful endoscopic hemostasis for high-risk stigmata. (Low/conditional)

**B. Urgent/emergent procedures — APA therapy**
1. Recommend consultation with the prescribing specialist before stopping APAs in significant GI bleeding for patients with (1) recent (<1 y) drug-eluting intracoronary stent, (2) <30 days after bare-metal stent, or (3) <90 days of ACS. (High/strong)
2. Recommend APAs be held after discussion with cardiology in patients with life-threatening or serious GI bleeding. (High/strong)

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Bridging.** ASGE 2016 suggests bridge therapy for high-thrombotic-risk patients undergoing high-bleeding-risk procedures. The newer **[[acg-cag-2022-anticoag-gi-bleeding]]** (informed by BRIDGE/PERIOP-2) suggests **against** routine bridging except for mechanical heart valves. **Newer ACG/CAG governs the wiki claim**; ASGE 2016's bridging stance is noted here as superseded.
- **Reversal-agent emphasis.** ASGE 2016 endorses 4F-PCC/FFP more readily for warfarin-related bleeding; ACG/CAG 2022 reserves reversal agents for life-threatening hemorrhage and suggests against routine FFP. Wiki follows ACG/CAG 2022.
