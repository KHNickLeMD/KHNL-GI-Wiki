## Summary

American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) Standards-of-Practice guideline on periprocedural management of anticoagulants and antiplatelet agents (APAs). It frames the decision around four factors: procedure urgency, procedure bleeding risk, the drug's effect on bleeding, and the thromboembolic risk of interrupting therapy. It provides a duration-of-action / reversal reference table for every antithrombotic class, a procedure bleeding-risk dichotomy (high vs low), and a cardiovascular (CV)-risk × bleeding-risk management matrix (Table 13) for the elective setting, plus separate guidance for urgent/emergent bleeding.

This is an **older tier-1 source**; the newer **[[acg-cag-2022-anticoag-gi-bleeding]]** (2022 American College of Gastroenterology [ACG]/Canadian Association of Gastroenterology [CAG]) governs the periendoscopic and acute-bleeding recommendations where they overlap. ASGE 2016's net-new contribution is its **drug-specific duration-of-action / hold-interval / reversal reference table** and specific pre-procedure hold intervals, which were added to **[[anticoagulation-gi-bleeding]]** without overwriting the ACG/CAG framework.

## Key Findings / Claims

- **Procedure bleeding risk (Table 3).** High-risk: polypectomy, biliary/pancreatic sphincterotomy, variceal treatment, percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy/jejunostomy (PEG/PEJ) placement, endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)–guided fine-needle aspiration (FNA; EUS-FNA), endoscopic hemostasis, tumor ablation, cystgastrostomy, ampullary resection, endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR), endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD), pneumatic/bougie dilation, therapeutic balloon enteroscopy, peroral endoscopic myotomy (POEM), Zenker's therapy. Low-risk: diagnostic esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD)/colonoscopy/flexible sigmoidoscopy (incl. mucosal biopsy), endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) with stent without sphincterotomy, push/diagnostic balloon enteroscopy, capsule endoscopy, enteral stent deployment (controversial), EUS without FNA, argon plasma coagulation (APC), Barrett's ablation. *PEG on aspirin (ASA) or clopidogrel monotherapy is low risk (not dual antiplatelet therapy [DAPT]); EUS-FNA of solid masses on ASA/nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) is low risk.*
- **Duration of action / reversal (Table 2, erratum-corrected).** Aspirin 7-10 d (hold, ± platelets if urgent); NSAIDs varies; dipyridamole 2-3 d; cilostazol 2 d; thienopyridines: clopidogrel/prasugrel 5-7 d, ticagrelor 3-5 d, ticlopidine 10-14 d; glycoprotein IIb/IIIa (GPIIb/IIIa) inhibitors: tirofiban 1-2 s, eptifibatide 4 h, abciximab 24 h; vorapaxar 5-13 d; warfarin 5 d (reverse: vitamin K, prothrombin complex concentrate [PCC]); unfractionated heparin (UFH) intravenous (IV) 2-6 h / subcutaneous (SQ) 12-24 h (protamine, partial); low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH) 24 h (protamine ± recombinant factor VIIa [rVIIa]); fondaparinux 36-48 h; factor Xa inhibitors and dabigatran per renal function (reverse: charcoal if last dose <2-3 h, nonactivated/activated PCC; dabigatran also hemodialysis).
- **Elective setting (Table 13).** Continue warfarin and novel oral anticoagulants (NOAC) for low-bleeding-risk procedures regardless of CV risk. For high-bleeding-risk procedures: discontinue anticoagulation; restart warfarin same day; delay reinitiating NOAC until hemostasis; if high CV risk, add bridge therapy. APAs: continue ASA/NSAIDs; hold thienopyridines ≥5 days before high-risk procedures (ticagrelor 3-5 d, others 5-7 d) or switch to ASA monotherapy.
- **Bridging** decisions in moderate-risk patients should be individualized (Table 5 mechanical valve / venous thromboembolism (VTE) risk), with the patient's wishes considered.
- **Table 13 footnote — the one carve-out from "continue ASA/NSAIDs" in high-bleeding-risk procedures:** there is evidence to **hold** APA in patients undergoing ESD and EMR who are at **low** thromboembolic risk.
- **Urgent/emergent bleeding.** Hold anticoagulants to achieve hemostasis; give 4-factor PCC + vitamin K (American College of Chest Physicians [ACCP]) or fresh frozen plasma (FFP) (American Heart Association [AHA]/American College of Cardiology [ACC] accepts either) for life-threatening warfarin-associated bleeding; endoscopic therapy need not be delayed if international normalized ratio (INR) <2.5; give UFH (short half-life) after successful hemostasis of high-risk stigmata; consult the prescriber before stopping APAs in patients with recent (<1 y) drug-eluting stent, <30 d bare-metal stent, or <90 d acute coronary syndrome (ACS).
- **Elective procedures should be deferred** in patients with recently placed intracoronary stents / ACS until the minimum DAPT duration (American College of Cardiology Foundation [ACCF]/ACG) has elapsed.

## Recommendations

**Lettering (A/B), the two patient groups under each, and the numbering within each group are the document's own** ("Recommendations [summarized in Table 13]"). Each statement carries a GRADE evidence-quality symbol only: ⊕⊕⊕⊕ high · ⊕⊕⊕◯ moderate · ⊕⊕◯◯ low · ⊕◯◯◯ very low. Strength is carried by the verb — *recommend* is the stronger form, *suggest* the weaker. Wording below is condensed; qualifiers are the document's.

**A. Elective endoscopic procedures — patients receiving anticoagulant therapy**
1. **Recommend** elective endoscopic procedures be deferred until short-term anticoagulation (eg, warfarin for VTE) is completed. (⊕⊕⊕◯)
2. **Suggest** discontinuing anticoagulation (warfarin, NOACs) for the drug-specific interval if high-risk endoscopic procedures are planned in a patient at low thromboembolic risk. (⊕⊕◯◯)
3. **Suggest** continuing warfarin and NOAC periendoscopically in patients undergoing low-risk procedures. (⊕⊕◯◯)
4. **Suggest** bridge therapy for patients undergoing high-risk procedures who are at high thromboembolic risk. (⊕⊕◯◯)
5. **Suggest** warfarin be restarted the same day as the procedure in all patients without ongoing bleeding. (⊕⊕◯◯)
6. **Suggest** reinitiation of NOACs after high-risk procedures be delayed until adequate hemostasis is ensured, given rapid onset and lack of reversal agents; if therapeutic doses cannot be restarted within 12-24 h after a high-risk procedure in a high-thromboembolic-risk patient, consider UFH bridge thromboprophylaxis. (⊕⊕◯◯)

**A. Elective endoscopic procedures — patients receiving APA therapy**
1. **Suggest** low-dose ASA and NSAIDs may be continued safely periendoscopically. (⊕⊕⊕◯)
2. **Recommend** thienopyridines be continued for all low-risk procedures. (⊕⊕⊕◯)
3. **Recommend** discontinuing thienopyridines ≥5-7 days before a high-risk procedure (or switching to ASA monotherapy), continued until the thienopyridine can be safely resumed. (⊕⊕⊕◯)
4. **Recommend** elective procedures be deferred in patients with recently placed intracoronary stents and/or ACS until the minimum antithrombotic therapy duration is complete. (⊕⊕⊕◯)
5. **Suggest** thienopyridines be withheld ≥5-7 days (ticagrelor 3-5 d) before high-risk procedures and that ASA be continued in patients requiring dual APA. (⊕⊕⊕◯)

**B. Urgent and emergent endoscopic procedures — patients receiving anticoagulant therapy**
1. **Recommend** patients with acute GI [gastrointestinal] bleeding on anticoagulation have anticoagulant agents held to facilitate hemostasis. (⊕⊕⊕◯)
2. **Recommend** either (1) 4-factor PCC + vitamin K or (2) FFP for life-threatening GI bleeding on warfarin — ACCP advocates option 1 only; AHA/ACC supports either. (⊕⊕⊕◯)
3. **Suggest** endoscopic therapy not be delayed in patients with serious GI bleeding and INR <2.5. (⊕⊕◯◯)
4. **Suggest** patients who require anticoagulation receive UFH (short half-life) after successful endoscopic hemostasis for high-risk stigmata. (⊕⊕◯◯)

**B. Urgent and emergent endoscopic procedures — patients receiving APA therapy**
1. **Recommend** consultation with the prescribing specialist (or a colleague) before stopping APAs in significant GI bleeding for patients with (1) recent (<1 y) drug-eluting intracoronary stent, (2) <30 days after a bare-metal stent, or (3) <90 days of ACS — the cardiac risk of stopping the APA likely exceeds the benefit of reducing postendoscopic bleeding. (⊕⊕⊕◯)
2. **Recommend** APAs be held after discussion with the patient's cardiologist in life-threatening or serious GI bleeding. (⊕⊕⊕◯)

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Bridging.** ASGE 2016 suggests bridge therapy for high-thrombotic-risk patients undergoing high-bleeding-risk procedures. The newer **[[acg-cag-2022-anticoag-gi-bleeding]]** (informed by BRIDGE/PERIOP-2) suggests **against** routine bridging except for mechanical heart valves. [[anticoagulation-gi-bleeding]] follows the newer ACG/CAG; ASGE 2016's bridging stance is superseded.
- **Reversal-agent emphasis.** ASGE 2016 endorses 4-factor PCC/FFP more readily for warfarin-related bleeding; ACG/CAG 2022 reserves reversal agents for life-threatening hemorrhage and suggests against routine FFP. Wiki follows ACG/CAG 2022.
