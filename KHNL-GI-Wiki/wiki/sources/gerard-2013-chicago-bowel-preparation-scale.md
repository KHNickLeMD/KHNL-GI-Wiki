## Summary
- **Why a new scale:** existing bowel preparation scales (BPSs) cannot separate preparations that all clean a high percentage of colons; Ottawa BPS penalises clear liquid heavily, Boston BPS ignores residual liquid and effort to wash, and neither converts easily to the poor/fair/good/excellent or adequate/inadequate ratings used in colonoscopy reports (Introduction, pp. 1–2).
- **Design:** prospective study of **150** colons; each rated live by one of three gastroenterologists **and** a physician's assistant (PA) on four scales — Ottawa BPS, Boston BPS, Chicago BPS, and a dichotomous adequate/inadequate BPS (adequate = **at least 95%** of mucosa seen after cleaning) (Abstract; Methods, p. 3).
- **Chicago BPS:** three segments scored **0, 5, 10, 11 or 12** → total **0–36**; whole-colon fluid scored **0–3** and reported separately (Table 1, p. 2).
- **Result:** Chicago BPS inter-rater kappas were "generally higher" (highest for right and left segments; Boston higher for transverse) and its total-score Pearson the highest, but **no difference reached statistical significance**; Chicago BPS total showed the best correspondence with the adequate/inadequate grade (Abstract; Results, p. 5).
- **Authors' conclusion:** valid and reliable; better-defined criteria and scoring make it "an attractive alternative" to Ottawa and Boston BPSs (Abstract, p. 1).

## Key Findings / Claims

### The Chicago BPS (Table 1, p. 2; Methods, pp. 3–4)

**Segments** — anatomic borders by endoscopic landmarks (Methods, innovation 3, pp. 3–4):
- **Right colon:** proximal cecum → mid-hepatic flexure
- **Transverse colon:** mid-hepatic flexure → mid-splenic flexure
- **Left colon:** mid-splenic flexure → distal rectum

**Segment score** — each of the 3 segments (Table 1, p. 2):

| Score | Descriptor (Table 1) |
|---|---|
| **0** | Unprepared colon segment with stool that cannot be cleared (**>15%** of the mucosa not seen) |
| **5** | Portion of mucosa in segment seen after cleaning, but **up to 15%** of the mucosa not seen because of retained material |
| **10** | Minor residual material after cleaning, but mucosa of segment generally well seen |
| **11** | Entire mucosa of segment well seen **after cleaning** |
| **12** | Entire mucosa of segment well seen **without washing** (suctioning of liquid allowed) |

- **Total = right + transverse + left segment scores → 0 (very poor) to 36 (outstanding)** (Table 1, p. 2; Methods, p. 3).
- Methods text (p. 3) words the top two grades as 11 = well seen "after washing", 12 = well seen "before washing" (suctioning of liquid allowed).
- In the authors' earlier retrospective study, Chicago scores were calculated from Boston data within a small range — e.g. a Boston segment score of **3** corresponded to a Chicago segment score of **11 or 12** (Methods, p. 3).
- Segments with **100%** of mucosa seen score **10, 11 or 12**; segments not 100% seen after cleaning score **0 or 5** — the gap is deliberately larger where the cleanliness difference is larger (Methods, innovation 2, p. 3).
- **Segment not seen because of technical difficulty advancing the scope → not rated; segment not seen because the more distal colon was too dirty to pass → worst rating** on each scale (Methods — Measurements, p. 4).

**Fluid score** — total volume of fluid in the whole colon, **excluding any fluid injected** into the colon; **reported separately from the total score**, not added to it (Table 1, p. 2; Methods, p. 3):

| Score | Descriptor (Table 1) |
|---|---|
| **3** | Large amount of fluid (**>300 cc**) |
| **2** | Moderate amount of fluid (**151–300 cc**) |
| **1** | Minimal amount of fluid (**51–150 cc**) |
| **0** | Little fluid (**≤50 cc**) |

**Modified Chicago BPS** — for a measure of mucosal visualization only: collapse segment scores **12 and 11 into 11**; best segment grade then has the same definition as the Boston BPS best grade (Methods, innovation 5, p. 4; Discussion, p. 9). Highest modified total = **33** (Discussion, p. 9).

**Three outputs** (Discussion, p. 10): modified Chicago BPS = mucosal visualization · Chicago BPS total = effort required to wash the colon · fluid score = residual fluid.

The suggested instructions for using the scale are in the article's online Supplementary Page 1, not in the article itself (Methods, p. 3; Discussion, p. 6).

### Adequacy cut-off and descriptive bands (Methods, p. 4; Table 2, p. 5)
- **Designed cut-off:** a total of **≥25** can only be obtained if **at least 95%** of the colon mucosa is visualized (= adequate on the adequate/inadequate BPS); a total of **≤24** is always obtained if **<95%** is visualized (Methods, p. 4).
- **Exception built into the design:** a total of **15–22** can still be an adequate preparation (Methods, p. 4), e.g.:
  - three segments scored 5, each with **<5%** of mucosa not visualized; or
  - two segments scored 5, each with **<7.5%** not visualized, plus one segment scored 10–12.

**Table 2 — segment scores → total → description** (p. 5):

| Colon segment scores | Permutations | Total scores | Description of cleanliness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Any segment 0 | 61 | 0–24 | Poor (inadequate) |
| Two or three segments 5 and no segment 0 | 10 | 15–22 | Usually poor (inadequate), rarely fair (adequate) |
| One segment 5, other two 10–12 | 27 | 25–29 | Fair (adequate) to good |
| All segments 10–12 and total score 30–33 | 17 | 30–33 | Good to excellent |
| All segments 10–12 and total score 34–36 | 10 | 34–36 | Excellent to outstanding |

*Table 2 footnote: five possible grades for each of three segments → 125 permutations (5³), totals 0–36.*

- **Why 95%** (Discussion, p. 6): authors' earlier study used it and it matched colons adequately cleared of solid stool; other preparation studies used cut-offs up to 95% but never higher; many current preparations reach it in a high percentage of colons; less effective preparations do not. Authors call any adequate/inadequate cut-off "somewhat arbitrary".

### Study population (Methods, p. 3; Table 3, p. 6)
- 176 consecutive patients having elective colonoscopy in the office endoscopy suite screened → 151 eligible → **150** enrolled (1 declined; 22 excluded because the PA was not working, 3 for prior colon resection); 9 July – 15 October 2012.
- Inclusion: age **≥18 years**, elective colonoscopy. Exclusion: pregnancy, prior colon resection, PA not working that day.
- Raters: 3 gastroenterologists (19–36 years' experience) each scored about a third of patients; **one PA (29 years' endoscopy experience) scored all**; raters could not discuss cleanliness before completing separate questionnaires, and the suction bottle was covered so neither saw the volume suctioned (Methods, pp. 3–4).
- Any preparation allowed; clear liquid diet the day before; nothing by mouth after midnight; Fleet enema if stool not clear that morning (Methods, p. 3).
- Table 3: 60% male; mean age **58.5 ± 11.3** years; preparation Gatorade + polyethylene glycol (PEG)-3350 **75.3%**, NuLYTELY or TriLyte **22.7%**, other clear liquid + PEG-3350 **2.0%**; screening indication **36.0%**.
- Cecum reached **149 of 150 (99.3%)**; none incomplete because of preparation (Results, p. 5). No significant complications (Results, p. 4).
- Only **3 of 150** colons graded inadequate — study rarely saw poor preparations (Discussion, pp. 8 and 10). Preparations adequately cleansed **98.5%** of colons (Discussion, p. 6).

### Inter-rater reliability — gastroenterologist vs PA (Table 4b, p. 7)

| Scale | Right colon kappa | Middle segment kappa | Distal segment kappa | Fluid kappa | Total score Pearson r (95% confidence interval [CI]) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ottawa BPS | 0.655 | 0.531 (transverse/descending) | 0.493 (sigmoid/rectum) | 0.337 | 0.793 (0.726, 0.846) |
| Boston BPS | 0.644 | 0.661 (transverse) | 0.545 (left) | — | 0.795 (0.721, 0.843) |
| Chicago BPS | **0.702** | 0.624 (transverse) | 0.630 (left) | 0.335 | **0.843 (0.786, 0.881)** |

*Kappa 95% CIs: Chicago right 0.603–0.801, transverse 0.510–0.737, left 0.514–0.745, fluid 0.226–0.445 (Table 4b). n = 149 for segment scores.*

- **Chicago vs other scales — none significant** (bootstrap, Table 4b): kappa right vs Ottawa P = 0.43, vs Boston P = 0.063; total-score Pearson vs Ottawa P = 0.40, vs Boston P = 0.15.
- Interpretation scale used (Data analysis, p. 4): kappa 0.41–0.60 moderate, 0.61–0.80 substantial, 0.81–1.0 near perfect.
- Observed Chicago BPS kappa and Pearson values were **lower than projected** in the power calculation (target Pearson >0.85, kappa >0.75) (Methods, p. 3; Discussion, p. 8).
- Fluid-score kappas for Ottawa and Chicago "very similar" (Results, p. 5).
- Kappa not reported for the modified Chicago BPS (Results, p. 5).

### Gastroenterologist vs PA mean scores (Table 4a, p. 7)

| Chicago BPS (mean ± standard deviation [SD]) | Gastroenterologists | PA | P |
|---|---|---|---|
| Right colon | 10.59 ± 2.01 | 10.74 ± 1.87 | 0.13 |
| Transverse colon | 11.37 ± 1.30 | 11.43 ± 1.28 | 0.33 |
| Left colon | 11.40 ± 1.22 | 11.45 ± 1.29 | 0.43 |
| Fluid, total colon | 0.99 ± 0.79 | 0.83 ± 0.74 | **0.008** |
| Total | 33.36 ± 3.84 | 33.63 ± 3.88 | 0.13 |

- Comparators (Table 4a): Ottawa total 5.52 ± 2.52 vs 5.24 ± 2.43 (P = 0.034); Ottawa fluid P **<0.001**; Boston total 8.12 ± 1.37 vs 8.29 ± 1.36 (P = 0.016); Boston right colon P = 0.004 — significant but small differences (Results, p. 5).
- Inadequate preparations: 3 by each rater type (P = 1.0) (Table 4a); perfect correspondence between the two on the adequate/inadequate grade (Discussion, p. 6).

### Correspondence with the adequate/inadequate grade (Results, p. 5; Table 5, p. 9)
- **146 colons rated adequate with all 3 segments graded → 145 of 146 scored 25–36** by both gastroenterologists and PA; the one exception per rater scored **20** (5/5/10 and 5/10/5) (Results, p. 5).
- **All three inadequate colons scored 0–24** (totals 20 and 15, 20 and 20, 10 and 15 by gastroenterologist and PA) (Results, p. 5; Discussion, p. 6).
- **All totals of 25–36 occurred in colons graded adequate** (Discussion, p. 6).
- Overlap of adequate and inadequate grades across total scores, this study (Results, p. 5):
  - Ottawa BPS **8–10**, 3/15th of scale (6/65 colons with totals ≥8 graded inadequate)
  - Boston BPS at **4**, 1/10th of scale (6/8 colons with totals ≤4)
  - Chicago BPS at **20**, 1/37th of scale (6/8 colons with totals ≤24)
- In the authors' earlier 600-patient study (591 with all segments graded; retrospective for the Chicago BPS): overlap Ottawa **3–10** (27/249 with totals ≥3 inadequate), Boston **4–7** (27/166 with totals ≤7), Chicago 1/28 colons with totals ≤24; **1 of 591** colons was graded adequate yet scored 15–22 (Results, p. 5).
- Two of 298 total scores in this study fell in 15–22 yet were graded adequate — the scale's acknowledged design limitation (Discussion, p. 6).
- Ottawa total can be as low as **3** (low is good) with an inadequate preparation or as high as **11** with an adequate one; Boston total as high as **7** with inadequate or as low as **3** with adequate (Discussion, p. 8).

### Ceiling effect (Discussion, p. 9; Table 5, p. 9)
- **65.1%** of colons scored the Boston maximum of **9** vs **33.6%** at the Chicago maximum of **36**; **31.5%** scored Boston 9 but Chicago 33–35 → Chicago separates excellent from outstanding.
- Modified Chicago BPS: **65.1%** at its maximum of 33. Mean ± SD (n = 300 as printed): right **10.25 ± 1.70**, transverse **10.69 ± 1.01**, left **10.76 ± 1.01**, total **31.69 ± 3.05** (Results, p. 5).

### Fluid score vs measured fluid (Table 4c, p. 7; Table 3, p. 6)
- Net fluid suctioned = total suctioned (600 cc covered Gomco bottle) − irrigation fluid injected (Methods, p. 4). Mean net fluid **87 ± 84 mL**; irrigation **111 ± 122 mL** (Table 3).
- Pearson r, Chicago fluid score vs net fluid — all colonoscopies: gastroenterologists **0.507**, PA **0.700** (P = **0.024**); no fluid injected (n = 50): **0.684** vs **0.793** (P = 0.085).
- Chicago vs Ottawa fluid-score correlation with net fluid: better for the PA (P = **0.004**), not for gastroenterologists (P = 0.94) (Table 4c).
- Correlation much stronger when no irrigation fluid was injected; net-fluid calculation overestimates residual fluid when irrigation is added and underestimates it when irrigation is not all suctioned (Results, p. 5; Discussion, p. 9).

### Limitations (Discussion, pp. 8 and 10)
- Only four observers (one PA scoring all; three gastroenterologists about a third each); very experienced raters — applicability to less experienced raters uncertain.
- Poorly cleansed colons rarely seen.
- Intra-rater agreement not evaluated.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **Adequacy definition differs from the wiki's guideline rule.** [[bowel-preparation]] (from [[usmstf-2025-bowel-prep]]) calls a preparation adequate only when **every segment** scores Boston 2 or 3, validated against detecting adenomas >5 mm. This paper defines adequate as **≥95% of mucosa visualized** and maps **one segment scored 5** (up to 15% of that segment unseen after cleaning) with the other two 10–12 to **total 25–29, "Fair (adequate) to good"** (Table 2, p. 5). The paper gives no Chicago-to-Boston conversion for a segment score of 5 (its only mapping is Boston 3 = Chicago 11 or 12, Methods, p. 3), so whether such a colon meets the guideline segment rule cannot be read off the Chicago total; the guideline rule takes priority.
- **"Fair" as an adequacy statement.** Table 2 equates "fair" with adequate; [[bowel-preparation]] (guideline) says "fair" alone is not an adequacy statement and must be paired with an explicit statement of whether standard intervals apply.
- **Retained fluid.** [[bowel-preparation]] calls the Boston BPS the only scale that does not downgrade for retained fluid. In the Chicago BPS the fluid score is reported separately and not added to the total, and grade 12 allows suctioning of liquid (Table 1, p. 2) — so the Chicago total does not add points for fluid either; it does score washing effort (12 vs 11).
- **Internal wording difference:** grade 11/12 descriptors read "after cleaning" / "without washing" in Table 1 (p. 2) but "after washing" / "before washing" in the Methods text (p. 3).
- **Printed inconsistencies:** Table 4b Ottawa sigmoid/rectum Pearson **0.729 (0.730, 0.797)** — CI lower bound above the point estimate as printed (p. 7); modified Chicago BPS means are given for n = 300 (Results, p. 5) though the study has 298 rated total scores (Table 5, p. 9).
- **Open:** validity in poorly prepared colons (only 3 inadequate); intra-rater agreement; performance with less experienced raters (Discussion, p. 10).
