## Bibliographic Info

- **Authors:** Lindor KD, Kowdley KV, Harrison ME
- **Year:** 2015
- **Journal/Publisher:** Am J Gastroenterol 2015;110:646–659
- **Type:** Guideline (ACG)

## Summary

First ACG clinical guideline for primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC), a chronic cholestatic biliary disease characterized by multifocal intra- and/or extrahepatic bile-duct strictures, strong IBD association (60–70% of adult PSC), and elevated risks of cholangiocarcinoma (CCA), gallbladder cancer, colorectal cancer (in PSC-IBD), hepatobiliary failure, fat-soluble vitamin deficiency, and metabolic bone disease. MRCP is the preferred non-invasive diagnostic study; ERCP is reserved for tissue sampling or therapeutic dilation of dominant strictures. There is no proven medical therapy — UDCA at high dose (>28 mg/kg/d) is harmful (Strong/High against). Liver transplantation is the definitive treatment for decompensated disease.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Diagnosis**

- MRCP preferred over ERCP for diagnosis (Strong/Moderate)
- Liver biopsy not required for typical large-duct PSC (Conditional/Low); recommended for suspected small-duct PSC or AIH overlap
- Test all patients once for elevated IgG4 (Conditional/Moderate) — IgG4-associated cholangitis can mimic PSC and responds to steroids
- AMA testing to exclude PBC (Conditional/Moderate)
- Differential: secondary sclerosing cholangitis, CCA, IgG4-cholangitis, AIH, HIV cholangiopathy, ischemic cholangiopathy, choledocholithiasis

**Medical treatment**

- UDCA >28 mg/kg/d should NOT be used (Strong/High — worse outcomes, more transplants/varices in Lindor 2009)
- No medical therapy proven effective; oral vancomycin is a promising area (especially pediatric) but no RCT data
- Pruritus: skin emollients/antihistamines first; cholestyramine 4–16 g/d; rifampin 150–300 mg BID (monitor LFTs); naltrexone, sertraline, phenobarbital as alternatives

**Endoscopic management of dominant strictures (CBD <1.5 mm or hepatic duct <1.0 mm)**

- ERCP with balloon dilation for symptomatic dominant strictures (Strong/Low)
- Cytology + biopsy + FISH at ERCP to exclude CCA (Strong/Low)
- Antibiotic prophylaxis for ERCP in PSC (Conditional/Low)
- Routine stenting after dilation NOT required; short-term stenting for severe strictures (Conditional/Low)

**Cholangiocarcinoma & Hepatobiliary Malignancy**

- Lifetime CCA risk ~10–15%; most diagnosed within first year of PSC dx
- Annual surveillance with US or MR + serum CA 19-9 every 6–12 months (Conditional/VLow)
- Cholecystectomy for gallbladder polyps >8 mm (Conditional/VLow) — high malignancy risk

**Liver transplant**

- Preferred over medical/surgical drainage in decompensated PSC (Strong/Moderate)
- MELD >14 → refer
- MELD exception points for: recurrent cholangitis (>2 bacteremia or >1 sepsis), CCA <3 cm without metastasis (institutional protocol), intractable pruritus
- Recurrent PSC after LT in ~20% at 5 years

**PSC and IBD**

- Annual colonoscopy with chromoendoscopy/biopsies beginning at PSC diagnosis (Conditional/Moderate) — 4–5× ↑ CRC risk vs IBD without PSC
- PSC-IBD phenotype: often right-sided, rectal-sparing, quiescent, ↑ pouchitis after IPAA, ↑ backwash ileitis
- 5-ASA may reduce CRC risk; low-dose UDCA (~20 mg/kg/d) data mixed

**Special situations**

- Test for AIH overlap if age <25 or AST/ALT >5× ULN (Conditional/Moderate)
- MRCP in pediatric AIH with ALP >2× ULN (Conditional/Moderate)
- Bone disease: DEXA at diagnosis then every 2–4 years (Conditional/Moderate); alendronate improves BMD
- Fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E should be assessed in advanced/jaundiced patients
- Screen for varices if platelets <150 (Conditional/VLow); pre-cirrhotic portal HTN possible from nodular regenerative hyperplasia

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Optimal interval/modality for CCA surveillance unsettled (CA 19-9 has low specificity)
- Role of oral vancomycin awaiting RCT data
- Low-dose UDCA (13–15 mg/kg/d) — possible CRC chemoprevention vs no hepatic benefit; non-recommended position
- IgG4 cutoffs and overlap with IgG4-SC poorly standardized
