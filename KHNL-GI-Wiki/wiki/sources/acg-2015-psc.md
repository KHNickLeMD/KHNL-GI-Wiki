## Summary

First American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) clinical guideline for primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC), a chronic cholestatic biliary disease characterized by multifocal intra- and/or extrahepatic bile-duct strictures, strong [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]] association (60–70% of adult PSC), and elevated risks of [[cholangiocarcinoma]] (CCA), gallbladder cancer, [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer (CRC)]] (in PSC-IBD), hepatobiliary failure, fat-soluble vitamin deficiency, and metabolic bone disease. Magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP) is the preferred non-invasive diagnostic study; [[ercp|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]] is reserved for tissue sampling or therapeutic dilation of dominant strictures. There is no proven medical therapy — ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) at high dose (>28 mg/kg/d) is harmful (Strong/High against). [[liver-transplantation|Liver transplantation]] is the definitive treatment for decompensated disease.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Diagnosis**

- MRCP preferred over ERCP for diagnosis (Strong/Moderate)
- Liver biopsy not required for typical large-duct PSC (Conditional/Low); recommended for suspected small-duct PSC or [[autoimmune-hepatitis|autoimmune hepatitis (AIH)]] overlap
- Test all patients once for elevated IgG4 (Conditional/Moderate) — IgG4-associated cholangitis can mimic PSC and responds to steroids
- Antimitochondrial antibody (AMA) testing to exclude [[primary-biliary-cholangitis|primary biliary cholangitis (PBC)]] (Conditional/Moderate)
- Differential: secondary sclerosing cholangitis, CCA, IgG4-cholangitis, AIH, human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) cholangiopathy, ischemic cholangiopathy, choledocholithiasis

**Medical treatment**

- UDCA >28 mg/kg/d should NOT be used (Strong/High — worse outcomes, more transplants/varices in Lindor 2009)
- No medical therapy proven effective; oral vancomycin is a promising area (especially pediatric) but no randomized controlled trial (RCT) data
- Pruritus: skin emollients/antihistamines first; cholestyramine 4–16 g/d; rifampin 150–300 mg twice daily (BID) (monitor liver function tests [LFTs]); naltrexone, sertraline, phenobarbital as alternatives

**Endoscopic management of dominant strictures (common bile duct [CBD] <1.5 mm or hepatic duct <1.0 mm)**

- ERCP with balloon dilation for symptomatic dominant strictures (Strong/Low)
- Cytology + biopsy + fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) at ERCP to exclude CCA (Strong/Low)
- Antibiotic prophylaxis for ERCP in PSC (Conditional/Low)
- Routine stenting after dilation NOT required; short-term stenting for severe strictures (Conditional/Low)

**Cholangiocarcinoma & Hepatobiliary Malignancy**

- Lifetime CCA risk ~10–15%; most diagnosed within first year of PSC dx
- Annual surveillance with ultrasound (US) or magnetic resonance (MR) + serum carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA 19-9) every 6–12 months (Conditional/Very low)
- Cholecystectomy for gallbladder polyps >8 mm (Conditional/Very low) — high malignancy risk

**Liver transplant**

- Preferred over medical/surgical drainage in decompensated PSC (Strong/Moderate)
- Model for End-Stage Liver Disease (MELD) >14 → refer
- MELD exception points for: recurrent cholangitis (>2 bacteremia or >1 sepsis), CCA <3 cm without metastasis (institutional protocol), intractable pruritus
- Recurrent PSC after liver transplantation (LT) in ~20% at 5 years

**PSC and IBD**

- Annual colonoscopy with chromoendoscopy/biopsies beginning at PSC diagnosis (Conditional/Moderate) — 4–5× ↑ CRC risk vs IBD without PSC
- PSC-IBD phenotype: often right-sided, rectal-sparing, quiescent, ↑ pouchitis after ileal pouch–anal anastomosis (IPAA), ↑ backwash ileitis
- 5-aminosalicylate (5-ASA) may reduce CRC risk; low-dose UDCA (~20 mg/kg/d) data mixed

**Special situations**

- Test for AIH overlap if age <25 or aspartate aminotransferase (AST)/alanine aminotransferase (ALT) >5× upper limit of normal (ULN) (Conditional/Moderate)
- MRCP in pediatric AIH with alkaline phosphatase (ALP) >2× ULN (Conditional/Moderate)
- Bone disease: dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA) at diagnosis then every 2–4 years (Conditional/Moderate); alendronate improves bone mineral density (BMD)
- Fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E should be assessed in advanced/jaundiced patients
- Screen for varices if platelets <150 (Conditional/Very low); pre-cirrhotic [[portal-hypertension|portal hypertension]] possible from nodular regenerative hyperplasia

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Optimal interval/modality for CCA surveillance unsettled (CA 19-9 has low specificity)
- Role of oral vancomycin awaiting RCT data
- Low-dose UDCA (13–15 mg/kg/d) — possible CRC chemoprevention vs no hepatic benefit; non-recommended position
- IgG4 cutoffs and overlap with IgG4-sclerosing cholangitis (IgG4-SC) poorly standardized
