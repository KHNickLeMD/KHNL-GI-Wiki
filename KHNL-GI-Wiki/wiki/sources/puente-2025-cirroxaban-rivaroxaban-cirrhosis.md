## Summary

The first double-blind randomised trial of a direct oral anticoagulant given **to treat the liver disease itself** rather than to treat a thrombus. The rationale is the hypothesis that intrahepatic microthrombi and thrombin/factor Xa-driven stellate-cell activation contribute to parenchymal extinction and fibrosis progression in [[cirrhosis|cirrhosis]]; blocking factor Xa should therefore slow progression of [[portal-hypertension|portal hypertension]] (PHT) and delay decompensation.

Fourteen Spanish hospitals randomised patients with cirrhosis, PHT and **moderate liver dysfunction (Child-Pugh score 7–10)** 1:1 to **rivaroxaban 10 mg once daily** or matched placebo for up to 24 months (enrolment May 2016 – Oct 2020). The planned sample size was 160 (80 per arm); recruitment fell off and the trial was stopped early, leaving **90 patients in the modified ITT analysis (41 rivaroxaban, 49 placebo)** — so the trial is underpowered by design-versus-delivery, and every result below should be read as such. Median Child-Pugh score was 7 in both arms; most patients had alcohol-related liver disease and a history of [[ascites|ascites]].

The primary composite endpoint (first PHT-related complication, liver transplant or death) occurred in **26.8% on rivaroxaban vs 46.9% on placebo — a ~50% relative reduction that missed statistical significance (HR 0.511, 95% CI 0.248–1.049, p = 0.058)**. The effect became significant after adjustment for Child-Pugh score, previous decompensation and concomitant non-selective beta-blocker use (HR 0.418, 95% CI 0.204–0.858), and in the *post hoc* Child-Pugh **B7** subgroup (HR 0.258, 95% CI 0.074–0.900). Liver stiffness fell on rivaroxaban and rose on placebo, reaching significance at 12 months.

Safety was the mirror image: **non-PHT-related bleeding was 2.56× more frequent on rivaroxaban (36.6% vs 14.3%)**, but **major bleeding did not differ** (6 vs 4 patients) and the only fatal bleed occurred in the placebo arm. Bleeding tracked drug exposure — anti-Xa-estimated rivaroxaban levels were markedly higher in patients who bled, while levels did **not** separate responders from non-responders, suggesting the efficacy ceiling is reached below the bleeding threshold.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Design and population**

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | Double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicentre RCT; 14 hospitals, Spain; 1:1 block randomisation, centralised blinding |
| Inclusion | Age 18–75; cirrhosis of any aetiology with clinically significant PHT; **Child-Pugh score 7–10 (B7 to C10)** |
| Key exclusions | Ascites requiring large-volume paracentesis; [[hepatic-encephalopathy\|HE]] grade ≥II; portal hypertensive bleeding within 6 months; **any thrombosis of the porto-spleno-mesenteric axis**; any-size [[hepatocellular-carcinoma\|HCC]]; prior thromboembolic disease; ongoing anticoagulant/antiplatelet therapy; platelets <40 ×10⁹/L; creatinine clearance <15 mL/min; life expectancy <12 months; prior [[tips\|TIPS]]; HCV antiviral therapy within 6 months; active alcohol use (>60 g/d men, >40 g/d women); strong CYP3A4 inhibitors |
| Intervention | **Rivaroxaban 10 mg once daily** vs matched placebo, up to 24 months (a deliberately "prophylactic", sub-therapeutic dose) |
| Flow | 106 screened → 99 randomised → **90 mITT (41 rivaroxaban, 49 placebo)**; per-protocol 78 (37 vs 41); follow-up 10.1 months |
| Target not met | Planned 160 patients (80/arm, powered on a 40–15% event-rate reduction); recruitment declined and enrolment was stopped — **the trial is underpowered** |
| Baseline | Median Child-Pugh 7 (7–8), MELD 12; ~70% alcohol-related; oesophageal varices 63–65%; prior ascites 71.5–73%; platelets ~77 ×10⁹/L; on NSBB 58% (rivaroxaban) vs 73% (placebo) |

**Primary endpoint** — time to first PHT-related complication (new ascites, worsening to grade ≥II ascites, ≥2 paracenteses in 6 months or TIPS; first or recurrent portal hypertensive GI bleeding; overt HE; [[portal-vein-thrombosis|PVT]]; HCC), [[liver-transplantation|liver transplant]], or death, whichever came first:

| Analysis | Rivaroxaban | Placebo | HR (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **mITT (n = 90)** | **11/41 (26.8%)** | **23/49 (46.9%)** | **0.511 (0.248–1.049), p = 0.058** |
| Event-free survival at 1 y / 2 y | 78.7% / 67.1% | 54.4% / 43.0% | log-rank p = 0.058 |
| Adjusted Cox (Child-Pugh score, previous decompensation, concomitant NSBB) | — | — | **0.418 (0.204–0.858)** |
| *Post hoc* **Child-Pugh B7** subgroup (23 vs 32) | — | — | **0.258 (0.074–0.900)** |
| Per-protocol (n = 78) | 9/37 (24.3%) | 19/41 (46.3%) | 0.463 (0.209–1.024) |

- The subgroup analyses were **not prespecified** and are exploratory; the Child-Pugh B7 signal is the trial's main hypothesis-generating result.

**Secondary endpoints** (mITT, death/liver transplant treated as competing risks):

| Endpoint | Rivaroxaban (n = 41) | Placebo (n = 49) | HR (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| New or worsening ascites | 7 (17.1%) | 14 (28.5%) | 0.588 (0.237–1.458) |
| Portal hypertensive bleeding | 2 (4.8%) | 4 (8.2%) | 0.601 (0.117–3.087) |
| Overt hepatic encephalopathy | 7 (17.1%) | 8 (16.3%) | 0.789 (0.228–2.731) |
| Death / liver transplant | 6 / 1 (17.1%) | 12 / 2 (28.5%) | 0.551 (0.222–1.366) |
| **Portal vein thrombosis** | **1 (2.4%)** | **1 (2.0%)** | 1.107 (0.069–17.822) |
| Hepatocellular carcinoma | 0 | 4 (8.1%) | not estimable |

- Cumulative incidence of new/worsening ascites at 1 y / 2 y: **15.4% / 22.2%** (rivaroxaban) vs **24.9% / 34.8%** (placebo).
- Cumulative probability of death/transplant at 1 y / 2 y: **10.3% / 20.0%** vs **18.0% / 34.2%**.
- Overall: 18 deaths (6 [14.6%] rivaroxaban vs 12 [24.4%] placebo) and 3 transplants; only one death was non-liver-related.
- **No signal for PVT prevention** — one event in each arm over the whole trial, far below the incidence reported in earlier anticoagulation trials in cirrhosis.
- **Liver stiffness** decreased at 6 and 12 months on rivaroxaban and increased on placebo, **significant at 12 months** — the first double-blind evidence that anticoagulation may reduce [[liver-stiffness-measurement|liver stiffness]] in cirrhosis. Portal pressure and fibrosis were not measured directly.

**Safety**

| Outcome | Rivaroxaban | Placebo | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| ≥1 adverse event | 32/41 | 39/49 | — |
| **Non-PHT-related bleeding (any)** | **36.6%** | **14.3%** | **RR 2.56 (95% CI 1.16–5.67)** |
| Non-PHT-related **major** bleeding | 6 patients | 4 patients | no significant difference |
| Fatal bleeding | 0 | 1 (spontaneous haemoperitoneum) | — |
| Hepatotoxicity | 2 | 4 | RR 0.60 (0.12–3.10) |
| Infection | 10 | 11 | RR 1.09 (0.51–2.30) |
| AKI | 2 | 2 | RR 1.20 (0.18–8.12) |
| Neoplasm | 2 | 4 | RR 0.60 (0.12–3.10) |

- 31 non-PHT bleeding events occurred in 22 patients (one patient had three, seven had two). Bleeding was the **only** adverse-event category that differed between arms.
- **Bleeding concentrated in the sicker patients.** At Child-Pugh 7: 9 events in 6 rivaroxaban patients vs 7 events in 6 placebo patients (no separation). At **Child-Pugh ≥8: 14 events in 9 rivaroxaban patients vs 1 event in 1 placebo patient**.
- Major bleeding was defined per EASL/ISTH criteria: fatal, in a critical area or organ, causing a haemoglobin drop ≥2 g/dL, or requiring ≥2 units of red cells.
- **No hepatotoxicity signal**; liver parameters trended toward improvement on rivaroxaban.

**Drug levels (anti-Xa) — the dose-finding message**

- Day-15 samples: 22 rivaroxaban, 29 placebo. Rivaroxaban levels were higher and endogenous thrombin potential lower than placebo; levels correlated weakly with Child-Pugh score (r = 0.342).
- **Efficacy did not track level:** median 133 ng/mL (range 22–243) in those reaching the primary endpoint vs 100 ng/mL (23–177) in those who did not — no significant difference (AUC 0.66).
- **Bleeding did track level:** median **119 ng/mL (62–307)** in patients with a non-PHT bleeding event vs **41 ng/mL (21–85)** without; **AUC 0.823, optimal cut-off 118 ng/mL**.
- Authors' reading: levels **above ~100 ng/mL added no benefit but added bleeding risk**, so a fixed 10 mg dose may still overshoot in cirrhosis and plasma-level monitoring may be needed if the benefit is confirmed.

## Relevance to Wiki

- [[portal-hypertension]] → *Therapeutics → Prevention of Decompensation*: supplies the trial-level numbers behind the Baveno VIII position that evidence is insufficient to recommend anticoagulation to prevent decompensation. The guideline stance is unchanged by this trial — the primary endpoint was not met.
- [[cirrhosis-hemostasis]] → bleeding risk of anticoagulation in cirrhosis: the first double-blind quantification of excess **minor** bleeding (RR 2.56) without excess **major** bleeding, plus the Child-Pugh ≥8 gradient and the anti-Xa 118 ng/mL bleeding threshold.
- [[portal-vein-thrombosis]] → cirrhotic PVT: rivaroxaban 10 mg/day did not reduce incident PVT (1 vs 1 over 24 months); the trial is prophylaxis in patients without thrombosis, not treatment, and does not alter the treatment recommendations.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Versus [[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension|Baveno VIII (2026)]] 3.22 / 4.11 — no contradiction in direction, and the guideline governs.** Baveno VIII found the evidence insufficient to recommend anticoagulation (or statins) to prevent first or further decompensation, and says both should be used only for their own approved indications. CIRROXABAN's primary endpoint was **not** met (p = 0.058), so it does not overturn that position; its adjusted and Child-Pugh B7 analyses are exploratory. The wiki continues to assert the Baveno VIII stance.
- **Versus [[aga-2025-pvt-cirrhosis|AGA 2025]] agent-selection advice — a real tension over rivaroxaban in Child-Pugh B.** AGA advises **against** rivaroxaban in Child-Pugh B and C when treating PVT. CIRROXABAN gave rivaroxaban 10 mg/day to a Child-Pugh 7–10 population and saw no excess major bleeding or hepatotoxicity — but at a **prophylactic, not therapeutic, dose**, in patients selected against thrombosis and against decompensation severe enough to need paracentesis, and with minor bleeding concentrated exactly in the Child-Pugh ≥8 patients AGA warns about. The guideline stance stands; this trial narrows rather than contradicts it.
- **Versus the enoxaparin experience (Villa 2012):** that open-label trial reported a 27.7% 2-year PVT incidence in placebo patients, against **2.0%** here — the authors attribute the gap to population differences and note that a PVT-prevention effect could not be tested at this event rate.
- **Open: the right dose.** Efficacy was flat across measured drug levels while bleeding rose steeply above ~100 ng/mL, so 10 mg daily may already be above the therapeutic ceiling in cirrhosis. Whether a lower or level-guided dose preserves benefit is untested.
- **Open: mechanism.** Liver stiffness improved but portal pressure and histological fibrosis were never measured, so the trial cannot say whether benefit came from reduced fibrosis, reduced portal pressure, or neither.
- **Underpowered.** 90 of a planned 160 patients; all confidence intervals cross or nearly cross 1, and alcohol abstinence was assessed only by questioning (no AUDIT, no biochemical markers).
