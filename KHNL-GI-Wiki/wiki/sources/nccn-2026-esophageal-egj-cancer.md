## Summary

Separate algorithms by histology — **adenocarcinoma** (distal esophagus/esophagogastric junction [EGJ], arising through the [[barretts-esophagus|Barrett's esophagus (BE)]] metaplasia–dysplasia sequence) and **squamous cell carcinoma (SCC)** (more often mid/upper esophagus). Workup is [[upper-endoscopy|esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD)]] with biopsy, [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]], computed tomography (CT), and positron emission tomography (PET)/CT. Endoscopic resection (ER) is recommended for superficial lesions **as a staging step**, not only as therapy — EUS is unreliable for distinguishing superficial from deep submucosal invasion.

Universal biomarker testing is specified: microsatellite instability (MSI)/mismatch repair (MMR) and programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) for all newly diagnosed esophageal/EGJ cancers, and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2, gene *ERBB2*) where advanced/metastatic adenocarcinoma is documented or suspected. Treatment is stage-directed: ER ± ablation for high-grade dysplasia (HGD)/Tis and low-risk early invasive disease; perioperative or preoperative therapy then esophagectomy for resectable locally advanced disease; definitive chemoradiation for selected patients (preferred for esophageal SCC); biomarker-directed systemic therapy for metastatic disease.

Version 3.2026 is a small update — it revises the MSI-high (MSI-H)/MMR-deficient (dMMR) non-operative-management footnote (esophagectomy remains standard; if non-operative management is pursued, immunotherapy for **at least 1 year**, with a specified surveillance schedule) and adds a matching post-treatment surveillance bullet to ESOPH-A. The perioperative **fluorouracil, leucovorin, oxaliplatin, docetaxel (FLOT) + durvalumab** sub-bullets and their categories, and the upgrade of fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan-nxki to category 1, entered in Versions 1.2026/2.2026 and carry forward.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Endoscopic Staging and Therapy (ESOPH-A)

- **EUS cannot be relied on to separate superficial from deep submucosal invasion** → ER is recommended to obtain accurate staging. Morphology suspicious for deep submucosal invasion should be specifically considered for [[endoscopic-submucosal-dissection|endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD)]].
- **HGD/Tis (carcinoma in situ)** and BE with flat HGD: treat by ER — it gives a more accurate histologic assessment. Flat lesions **>2 cm** can be treated by ER but with greater complication risk; such lesions may be treated effectively by ablation alone (data for ablation alone in squamous HGD are very limited).
- **Curative features of an endoscopically resected tumor** (all four): size **≤2 cm**; superficially invasive (T1a, or T1b with **≤500 μm** submucosal invasion); well or moderately differentiated; complete excision with **negative lateral and deep margins**.
- **pT1a** (lamina propria or muscularis mucosae) with no nodal metastases, no lymphovascular invasion (LVI), and no poor differentiation → adequately treated by ER.
- **pT1b** must be split into low- vs high-risk:

  | | Criteria |
  |---|---|
  | **Low-risk pT1b** | size ≤2 cm · well or moderately differentiated · no LVI · depth of invasion **≤500 μm for adenocarcinoma, <200 μm for SCC** |
  | **High-risk pT1b** | any low-risk criterion not met |

  - Low-risk → can be considered adequately treated by ER, but a detailed patient–surgeon discussion of esophagectomy risk vs the chance of concurrent nodal disease is advised, especially for larger tumors, deeper invasion, LVI, or positive margins.
  - High-risk → offer surgical consultation for curative salvage esophagectomy, or medical/radiation oncology consultation for adjuvant systemic therapy or chemoradiation.
- **Residual BE after ER must be eradicated** — ablate or endoscopically resect all visible residual BE. Complete eradication may also be achieved up front by widefield endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR) or ESD when needed to resect superficial tumor or mucosal nodularity ≤2 cm.
- Ablation after ER for SCC rests on low-level evidence; may be needed for multifocal HGD/carcinoma in situ elsewhere, and may be unnecessary for completely excised lesions.
- Non-operative management (e.g., immunotherapy): EGD with biopsy (EUS as clinically indicated) **every 12 weeks for 2 years, then every 6 months to year 5** (new this version).

### Biomarker Testing (ESOPH-B)

- **HER2 (*ERBB2*)** — test when trastuzumab is being considered for inoperable locally advanced, recurrent, or metastatic adenocarcinoma. Immunohistochemistry (IHC) first; in situ hybridization (ISH)/fluorescence ISH (FISH) only for IHC 2+ (equivocal). IHC 3+ (positive) and 0/1+ (negative) need no ISH.

  | IHC score | Surgical specimen | Biopsy specimen | HER2 status |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 0 | no reactivity, or membranous reactivity in <10% of cancer cells | no membranous reactivity in any cancer cell | Negative |
  | 1+ | faint/barely perceptible membranous reactivity in ≥10% of cancer cells, reactive in only part of the membrane | cluster of ≥5 cancer cells with faint/barely perceptible membranous reactivity, any percentage | Negative |
  | 2+ | weak-to-moderate complete, basolateral, or lateral membranous reactivity in ≥10% of cancer cells | cluster of ≥5 cancer cells with weak-to-moderate complete/basolateral/lateral membranous reactivity, any percentage | Equivocal → do ISH |
  | 3+ | strong complete, basolateral, or lateral membranous reactivity in ≥10% of cancer cells | cluster of ≥5 cancer cells with strong complete/basolateral/lateral membranous reactivity, any percentage | Positive |

  - **ISH/FISH positive** = HER2:CEP17 ratio **≥2**, or average HER2 copy number **≥6.0 signals/cell**.
  - Repeat biomarker testing may be considered at clinical or radiologic progression in advanced/metastatic disease.
- **MSI/MMR** — **universal testing** of all newly diagnosed esophageal and EGJ cancers (MSI by polymerase chain reaction [PCR] or next-generation sequencing [NGS], or MMR by IHC), on formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue, in laboratories approved under the Clinical Laboratory Improvement Amendments (CLIA). MSI-H/dMMR → refer to a genetic counselor in the appropriate clinical context.

  | Result | Definition |
  |---|---|
  | dMMR | loss of nuclear expression of ≥1 MMR protein |
  | MMR-proficient | no loss of nuclear expression (low probability of MSI-H) |
  | MSI-low | **1%–29%** of markers unstable, or **1 of 5** National Cancer Institute/mononucleotide markers unstable |
  | MSI-H | **≥30%** of markers unstable, or **≥2 of 5** markers unstable |

- **PD-L1** — universal IHC testing for all newly diagnosed patients who are candidates for programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1)/PD-L1 inhibitors, by companion diagnostic in a CLIA-approved laboratory. **≥100 tumor cells** must be present for the slide to be adequate.
  - **Combined positive score (CPS)** = (PD-L1–staining tumor cells + lymphocytes + macrophages) ÷ total viable tumor cells × 100. **A specimen is PD-L1–expressing if CPS ≥1.**
  - **Tumor area positivity (TAP) score** = area occupied by PD-L1–positive tumor and immune cells ÷ tumor area × 100%. CPS and TAP have high concordance and **may be used interchangeably** for identifying candidates for tislelizumab-jsgr or durvalumab.
- IHC/ISH/targeted gene PCR is the **preferred initial** approach; NGS may be considered later in the course when tissue is sufficient. Circulating tumor DNA ("liquid biopsy") by validated NGS multigene panel may be considered when tissue is limited or biopsy is not feasible in advanced/metastatic disease.

### Perioperative and Preoperative Therapy (ESOPH-F)

- **Perioperative systemic therapy is preferred** for resectable esophageal/EGJ adenocarcinoma in patients medically fit with access to frequent toxicity evaluation. Preoperative chemoradiation may be considered for those borderline resectable on medical or surgical grounds. Not a FLOT candidate → perioperative FOLFOX/CAPOX (folinic acid, fluorouracil, oxaliplatin / capecitabine, oxaliplatin) or preoperative chemoradiation. **Preoperative chemoradiation is preferred for esophageal SCC.**
- **Perioperative systemic therapy — Preferred:** FLOT (category 1); **FLOT + durvalumab**, whose category depends on the selection criterion:

  | FLOT + durvalumab, by indication | Category |
  |---|---|
  | **PD-L1 CPS ≥1 or TAP ≥1%** | **1 for EGJ adenocarcinoma; 2A for esophageal adenocarcinoma** |
  | Clinically node-negative tumor | 2A |
  | **PD-L1 CPS <1 or TAP <1%** | 2B |
  | **Diffuse-type EGJ adenocarcinoma** | 2B |

  - *Other recommended:* fluorouracil + cisplatin (category 1); fluoropyrimidine + oxaliplatin.
- **Preoperative chemoradiation.** *Preferred:* paclitaxel + carboplatin (category 1); fluorouracil + oxaliplatin (category 1). *Other recommended:* fluorouracil + cisplatin (category 1); irinotecan + cisplatin (category 2B); paclitaxel + fluoropyrimidine (category 2B). Infusional fluorouracil may be replaced by capecitabine.
- **Neoadjuvant or perioperative immunotherapy — *Useful in Certain Circumstances*, MSI-H/dMMR tumors only:** dostarlimab-gxly (neoadjuvant only); nivolumab + ipilimumab followed by nivolumab; pembrolizumab; tremelimumab + durvalumab (neoadjuvant only).
- Two-drug cytotoxic regimens are preferred in advanced disease for lower toxicity; three cytotoxic drugs are reserved for medically fit patients with excellent performance status and easy access to frequent toxicity evaluation.

### Postoperative Therapy and Definitive Chemoradiation (ESOPH-F)

- **Postoperative — Preferred:** **nivolumab, only after preoperative chemoradiation with R0 resection and residual disease (category 1).** *Other recommended:* capecitabine + oxaliplatin; fluorouracil + oxaliplatin; fluoropyrimidine before and after fluoropyrimidine-based chemoradiation.
- **Definitive chemoradiation — Preferred:** paclitaxel + carboplatin; fluorouracil + oxaliplatin (category 1). *Other recommended:* fluorouracil + cisplatin (category 1); cisplatin with docetaxel or paclitaxel; irinotecan + cisplatin (category 2B); paclitaxel + fluoropyrimidine (category 2B).

### Metastatic Adenocarcinoma — Systemic Therapy (ESOPH-F)

Regimens for advanced esophageal adenocarcinoma, EGJ adenocarcinoma, and gastric adenocarcinoma may be used interchangeably except where indicated ([[nccn-2026-gastric-cancer]]); advanced esophageal SCC has its own list. **Oxaliplatin is preferred over cisplatin** (lower toxicity).

- **Two cross-cutting rules, stated as general principles:**
  - Add **trastuzumab** to first-line chemotherapy for advanced HER2-positive (**IHC 3+, or IHC 2+ with ISH/FISH+**) adenocarcinoma.
  - Add a **checkpoint inhibitor** to first-line chemotherapy for advanced disease with **PD-L1 CPS ≥1**.
- **First line — *Preferred*:**

  | Group | Regimens (category where given) |
  |---|---|
  | HER2-positive | fluoropyrimidine + oxaliplatin + trastuzumab · fluoropyrimidine + oxaliplatin + trastuzumab + **pembrolizumab for CPS ≥1 (category 1)** · fluoropyrimidine + cisplatin + trastuzumab (category 1) · fluoropyrimidine + cisplatin + trastuzumab + **pembrolizumab for CPS ≥1 (category 1)** |
  | HER2-negative | fluoropyrimidine + oxaliplatin + **nivolumab**, **pembrolizumab**, or **tislelizumab-jsgr**, each **for CPS ≥1 (category 1 for CPS ≥5)** · fluoropyrimidine + oxaliplatin + **zolbetuximab-clzb for claudin 18.2 (CLDN18.2)-positive** (category 1 for EGJ adenocarcinoma; category 2A for esophageal adenocarcinoma) · fluoropyrimidine + oxaliplatin · the cisplatin equivalents of the pembrolizumab and tislelizumab regimens · fluoropyrimidine + cisplatin |
  | MSI-H/dMMR (independent of PD-L1) | pembrolizumab · dostarlimab-gxly · nivolumab + ipilimumab · fluoropyrimidine + oxaliplatin + nivolumab · fluoropyrimidine + oxaliplatin + pembrolizumab |

  - *Other recommended:* fluorouracil + irinotecan; paclitaxel ± carboplatin or cisplatin; docetaxel ± cisplatin; fluoropyrimidine alone; docetaxel + (cisplatin or oxaliplatin) + fluorouracil.
- **Second line or subsequent:** **fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan-nxki for HER2-positive disease is category 1** (raised from 2A this guideline year), alongside the other later-line options.
- **Subcutaneous substitutions:** pembrolizumab + berahyaluronidase alfa-pmph may substitute for intravenous (IV) pembrolizumab; nivolumab + hyaluronidase-nvhy may substitute for IV nivolumab **for monotherapy only** — it is **not approved for concurrent use with IV ipilimumab**. Both have different dosing and administration instructions from the IV forms.
- An FDA-approved biosimilar is an appropriate substitute for any recommended systemic biologic therapy.

### MSI-H/dMMR and Non-Operative Management

- Perioperative immunotherapy for an MSI-H/dMMR tumor should be considered in multidisciplinary consultation.
- Radiologic and endoscopic complete responses to neoadjuvant checkpoint inhibitor therapy do occur, but **current imaging and endoscopic assessment do not reliably predict pathologic complete response**.
- **Esophagectomy with appropriate lymphadenectomy remains the standard** outside prospective organ-preservation trials.
- If non-operative management is chosen: **immunotherapy for at least 1 year** (counting perioperative and adjuvant settings), with surveillance = chest/abdomen/pelvis CT (± PET/CT if clinically indicated) **and** EGD with biopsy (EUS as clinically indicated) **every 12 weeks for 2 years, then every 6 months to year 5**.

### Surgery (ESOPH-C)

- **Strong consideration should be given to minimally invasive esophagectomy**, given improved postoperative outcomes compared with open surgery (added this guideline year).

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Internal inconsistency on MATTERHORN.** The footnote on the systemic-therapy page states that diffuse-type gastric or EGJ adenocarcinoma showed **no overall survival advantage** when durvalumab was added to FLOT; the Updates page describing that same footnote revision words it as "no event-free overall survival advantage." The two statements are not equivalent, and the document does not reconcile them. Either way, diffuse-type EGJ adenocarcinoma carries category 2B for FLOT + durvalumab.
- **Category 2B at both ends of the PD-L1 range.** FLOT + durvalumab is listed for PD-L1 CPS <1 or TAP <1% at category 2B *and* for clinically node-negative tumors at category 2A, without a stated rule for a node-negative, CPS <1 tumor that satisfies both bullets.
- **CPS and TAP are called interchangeable** but the interchangeability statement is tied to tislelizumab-jsgr and durvalumab specifically; the first-line CPS ≥1 and CPS ≥5 category-1 thresholds are written in CPS only.
- **Grading vocabulary differs from the society guidelines cited beside it** on the esophageal pages. NCCN categories 1/2A/2B/3 and the preference tiers are consensus labels, not GRADE strength-of-recommendation ratings, and are not interchangeable with American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) or American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) wording. The two are complementary — ACG governs the Barrett's/early-neoplasia surveillance and eradication pathway, NCCN the oncologic staging and treatment.
- **Low-risk pT1b is a shared-decision recommendation, not a rule** — the document pairs "can be considered adequately treated" with an instruction to discuss esophagectomy, and gives no quantitative nodal-metastasis risk to anchor that discussion.
- The depth cutoff differs by histology (≤500 μm adenocarcinoma vs <200 μm SCC) and the document uses "≤" for one and "<" for the other without comment.
