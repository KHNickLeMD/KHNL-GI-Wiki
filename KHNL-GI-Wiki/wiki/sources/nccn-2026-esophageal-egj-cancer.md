## Summary

The NCCN guideline covers esophageal and esophagogastric junction (EGJ) cancers, with separate pathways by histology — **adenocarcinoma** (distal esophagus/EGJ, arising via the [[barretts-esophagus|Barrett's]] metaplasia–dysplasia sequence) and **squamous cell carcinoma** (more often mid/upper esophagus). Workup centers on [[upper-endoscopy|endoscopy]] with biopsy, endoscopic ultrasound and PET/CT for staging, and biomarker testing on advanced/metastatic disease (HER2 [ERBB2], PD-L1 CPS, and MSI-H/dMMR). Management is stage-directed: endoscopic resection ([[endoscopic-eradication-therapy|EMR/ESD]]) for early mucosal (Tis/T1a) disease; neoadjuvant or perioperative chemotherapy and/or chemoradiation followed by esophagectomy for locally advanced resectable disease; definitive chemoradiation for selected (especially SCC) patients; and biomarker-directed systemic therapy for metastatic disease.

Version 3.2026 refines perioperative therapy: in the **MATTERHORN** context, adding **durvalumab to perioperative FLOT** is positioned for selected adenocarcinoma (e.g., clinically node-negative tumors; PD-L1 CPS <1 listed as category 2B), with the explicit caveat that **diffuse-type** gastric/EGJ adenocarcinoma showed no event-free/overall survival advantage from added durvalumab. The guideline integrates HER2-directed therapy (trastuzumab first-line for HER2-overexpressing adenocarcinoma; trastuzumab deruxtecan in later lines) and checkpoint inhibitors (nivolumab/pembrolizumab) into the systemic pathways, and recognizes MSI-H/dMMR tumors as candidates for immunotherapy.

## Key Findings / Claims

- **Two histologic pathways.** Adenocarcinoma (EGJ/distal, Barrett's-associated) and squamous cell carcinoma (mid/upper) have distinct treatment algorithms; SCC is more radiosensitive and more often managed with definitive chemoradiation.
- **Early disease is endoscopically curable.** Tis (HGD) and T1a mucosal cancers are managed with endoscopic resection ± ablation; T1b and deeper generally require esophagectomy or chemoradiation depending on risk and fitness.
- **Locally advanced resectable disease** is treated with neoadjuvant/perioperative therapy then surgery — perioperative **FLOT** (adenocarcinoma) or neoadjuvant **chemoradiation (CROSS-type)** followed by esophagectomy; adjuvant **nivolumab** is an option after neoadjuvant chemoradiation + surgery with residual disease (CheckMate-577).
- **Perioperative durvalumab + FLOT (v3.2026).** Positioned for selected adenocarcinoma (clinically node-negative; PD-L1 CPS <1 as category 2B; diffuse-type EGJ as category 2B), reflecting MATTERHORN — with the caveat that diffuse-type disease did not show a survival advantage from added durvalumab.
- **Biomarker-directed systemic therapy for metastatic disease.** HER2 (ERBB2) testing directs first-line **trastuzumab** added to chemotherapy for HER2-overexpressing adenocarcinoma; **trastuzumab deruxtecan** is a category 1 later-line option for HER2-positive disease. PD-L1 CPS guides addition of **nivolumab or pembrolizumab** to first-line chemotherapy; **MSI-H/dMMR** tumors are immunotherapy candidates.
- **Subcutaneous pembrolizumab** (pembrolizumab–berahyaluronidase alfa) may substitute for IV pembrolizumab.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Algorithm-based guideline (category 2A by default), not a GRADE systematic review; complements the ACG Barrett's guideline already cited on the EAC page (which governs the precursor/early-neoplasia endoscopic pathway).
- Benefit of adding durvalumab to perioperative FLOT appears histology- and biomarker-dependent (no advantage in diffuse-type); optimal patient selection is still being defined.
