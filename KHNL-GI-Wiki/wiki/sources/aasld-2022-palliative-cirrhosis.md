# AASLD Practice Guidance: Palliative Care in Decompensated Cirrhosis (2022)

## Summary

This guidance integrates **palliative care** — multidisciplinary care focused on symptom relief, quality of life, and support for patients and caregivers — into the management of decompensated cirrhosis, emphasizing that it can be delivered **concurrently with disease-directed and transplant care**, not only at end of life.

---

## Key Points

- **Introduce palliative care early and concurrently**, at any disease stage, including in transplant candidates — not reserved for end-of-life.
- **Symptom-based management** of the cirrhosis symptom burden: pain (avoid hepatotoxic/renally-cleared agents and NSAIDs), pruritus, [[hepatic-encephalopathy]], muscle cramps, [[ascites]]-related discomfort, dyspnea, depression/anxiety, and poor sleep.
- **Advance care planning**, goals-of-care discussions, and caregiver support are core components.
- Use both **primary palliative care** (by the hepatology team) and **specialty palliative care** referral for complex needs; coordinate with transplant evaluation rather than viewing them as mutually exclusive.

---

## Clinical Relevance

Normalizes early, concurrent palliative care and structured symptom management in decompensated [[portal-hypertension|cirrhosis]], improving quality of life alongside disease-directed therapy. Complements [[nutrition-in-liver-disease]] and end-stage management of [[hepatic-encephalopathy]] and [[ascites]].
