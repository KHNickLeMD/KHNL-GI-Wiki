## Summary

- **What it is:** standardized protocol, vocabulary, and the **FLIP Panometry Motility Classification, version 2.0** for [[flip-panometry|functional lumen imaging probe (FLIP) panometry]] — updates the 2021 scheme.
- **Panel:** Impedance Planimetry Working Group, 19 members (United States and Canada), each with >100 FLIP studies; a methodologist and 2 non-voting literature reviewers.
- **Process:** initial meeting Dallas, September 2023; ~1 year modified Delphi; 57 draft statements → 3 anonymous voting rounds with meetings → **40 final statements**.
- **Voting:** 1–9 appropriateness scale (1–3 inappropriate, 4–6 uncertain, 7–9 appropriate); "agreement" = % voting ≥7; **consensus = agreement >80%**. Statements are **unnumbered**; reported with median and % agreement. Two statements fell short (75%) and are reported as such.
- **Literature review:** only 2 statements formally reviewed (22 studies) — "major motor disorder unlikely with normal FLIP" and "diminished/absent contractile response with reduced esophagogastric junction (EGJ) opening supports a disorder of EGJ outflow". The rest rest on panel experience.
- **Scope:** patients **without prior foregut surgery, mechanical obstruction, or abnormal anatomy** (e.g., strictures, paraesophageal hernia) — these cause secondary motility findings, as with [[high-resolution-manometry|high-resolution manometry (HRM)]] under [[chicago-classification-v4|Chicago Classification v4.0 (CCv4.0)]].

## Key Findings / Claims

*Statements are unnumbered in the source; listed in the source's order. Median on the 1–9 scale; agreement = % voting ≥7.*

### Overview Statements

| Statement | Median | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Interpret FLIP panometry in the context of the clinical presentation, the accompanying esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD) findings, and other relevant complementary testing | 9.0 | 100% |
| Endoscopy is integral to performing FLIP | 9.0 | 94% |
| FLIP panometry is integral to help diagnose a motility disorder when endoscopy rules out a mechanical obstruction — **did not reach the 80% threshold** | 7.0 | 75% |

### Protocol Statements (Table 1)

| Statement | Median | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Use the **16-cm** FLIP catheter | 9.0 | 94% |
| Set filter settings to "off" for intraprocedural assessment | 8.5 | 100% |
| Calibrate the catheter to atmospheric pressure before transoral placement | 9.0 | 100% |
| Position with **2 sensors (2 cm) below the crural diaphragm**; keep this position for the entire study | 9.0 | 100% |
| Fill sequentially to **50, 60, and 70 mL**, holding each volume **≥30 s** | 9.0 | 94% |
| Target maximum fill volume **70 mL** for a complete motility study | 9.0 | 81% |
| With FLIP correctly positioned, a high-pressure alarm does not increase the risk of the overall endoscopic procedure | 8.0 | 88% |

- Protocol takes ~4–5 min; works on FLIP 1.0, 2.0, or 300 (FLIP 1.0 needs post-procedure processing for topography).
- Holding 60–90 s at a volume is acceptable if needed, especially with sustained or spastic contractions.
- Positioning: EGJ "waist" seen at 40 mL with ~2 cm of the catheter in the stomach; direct endoscopic view if position is uncertain.
- Safety: high-pressure alarms occur at 60–100 mmHg depending on system settings; balloon dilation runs 3–8 atm (2280–6080 mmHg); 722 subjects without a FLIP-related adverse event. Pressure may still limit filling in, e.g., a severely narrow-caliber or strictured esophagus.

### EGJ Metric Acquisition Statements (Table 1)

| Statement | Median | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Key EGJ opening metrics: **EGJ distensibility index (EGJ-DI) at 60 mL** and **maximum EGJ diameter during the 70-mL fill** | 8.5 | 100% |
| Measure EGJ metrics **>5 s after active filling** to avoid filling effects | 8.5 | 88% |
| Avoid EGJ metrics affected by "dry catheter artifact" | 9.0 | 100% |
| Measure EGJ-DI and EGJ diameter at the time of greatest EGJ opening diameter | 9.0 | 100% |
| Classify EGJ opening with the **median of 3 EGJ-DI measurements** taken during the 60-mL fill | 8.0 | 81% |
| **EGJ-DI is not reliable when the associated FLIP pressure is <15 mmHg** | 9.0 | 88% |
| Document EGJ–crural diaphragm separation, which suggests a hiatal hernia | 9.0 | 94% |
| Do not use measures made while the FLIP is emptying for clinical decisions | 8.5 | 94% |

- Dry catheter artifact: lumen occlusion (diameter roughly <6–7 mm) disrupts the impedance current → falsely high diameters at other sensors (can appear distal or proximal to the occlusion); omit those sensors.
- Record the pressure at each EGJ-DI measurement (reliability; pressure also helps predict spasm).
- Hiatal hernia can be induced or enlarged by FLIP filling; lower esophageal sphincter (LES)–crural separation is easiest to see with the filter "off".

### Contraction Assessment Statements (Table 1)

| Pattern / statement | Definition | Median | Agreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Distinct antegrade contraction** | Antegrade contraction extending **≥6 cm** axially with pressure increase **≥10 mmHg** | 9.0 | 100% |
| **Repetitive antegrade contraction (RAC)** — "rule of 6s" | **≥6 consecutive** antegrade contractions of **≥6 cm** at **6 ± 3/min**, regular cadence | 9.0 | 100% |
| **Sustained LES contraction (sLESC)** | Transient LES diameter reduction lasting **>5 s**, with a rise in FLIP pressure, independent of antegrade contractions, respiration, or crural contraction | 8.0 | 100% |
| **Sustained occluding contraction (SOC)** | Non-propagating (retrograde or horizontal), lumen-occluding (diameter **<6 mm**) esophageal-body contraction persisting **>10 s** with FLIP pressure increase **>35 mmHg** | 8.0 | 88% |
| Other contractile features | May be seen; uncertain clinical significance | 9.0 | 94% |
| **Repetitive retrograde contraction (RRC)** | **≥6 consecutive** retrograde contractions of **≥6 cm** at **>9/min** | 9.0 | 88% |
| Clinical relevance of RRCs | Uncertain | 8.0 | 94% |
| **Vigorous antegrade contraction** | Antegrade contraction of consistent body width lasting **≥10 s** with pressure rise **>35 mmHg** — **did not reach the 80% threshold** | 8.0 | 75% |
| Clinical relevance of vigorous antegrade contractions | Uncertain | 8.0 | 94% |
| Pressure at 60 mL | Taken with the EGJ-DI, may help separate diminished vs disordered contractile response | 8.0 | 81% |

### Contractile Response (CR) Category Statements (Table 1)

| Category | Definition | Median | Agreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Absent CR (ACR)** | No contractions in the body | 9.0 | 100% |
| **Spastic CR (SCR)** | SOCs or sustained LES contractions | 8.0 | 100% |
| **Normal CR (NCR)** | Multiple (**more than 1**) distinct antegrade contractions | 8.0 | 100% |
| **Diminished CR** | Contractions present, not meeting ACR, NCR, or SCR, with **low FLIP pressure (<40 mmHg)** | 7.0 | 94% |
| **Disordered CR** | Contractions present, not meeting ACR, NCR, or SCR, with **high FLIP pressure (≥40 mmHg)** | 7.5 | 88% |

- CR is judged over the **aggregate of the 50-, 60-, and 70-mL fills**, not one volume.
- Hierarchy (Figure 5): contractions present? no → absent; SOCs or sLESCs? yes → spastic; >1 distinct antegrade contraction? yes → normal; 60-mL pressure >40 mmHg? no → diminished, yes → disordered. *(Statement wording: diminished <40, disordered ≥40; Figure 5 asks ">40".)*
- NCR does not require the RAC pattern.
- 40 mmHg is a dichotomous cut; extremes shift probability — **<30 mmHg** favors hypomotility, **>55 mmHg** favors spasm.
- Spastic or disordered CR may be **reactive** to subtle mechanical obstruction → endoscopic re-inspection, consider dilation. Note the vertical extent of SOCs and whether distinct antegrade contractions coexist.
- **RRC removed as a spastic-CR criterion** (nonspecific; also in mechanical obstruction; at low volumes 30–40 mL may be respiratory). RRCs at ≥50 mL or with high 60-mL pressure more likely abnormal.
- Not classification criteria (unclear significance): vigorous antegrade, isolated focal, focal sustained, triangle-shaped, bridge-shaped contractions; may prompt re-inspection for rings or strictures.

### EGJ Opening Statements

| Category | Definition | Median | Agreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reduced EGJ opening (REO)** | Maximum EGJ diameter **<12 mm** **and** EGJ-DI at 60 mL **<2.0 mm²/mmHg** | 9.0 | 100% |
| **Normal EGJ opening (NEO)** | Maximum EGJ diameter **≥16 mm** **and** EGJ-DI at 60 mL **≥2.0 mm²/mmHg** | 9.0 | 100% |
| **Inconclusive EGJ opening** (formerly "borderline") | All cases between REO and NEO | 8.0 | 100% |

- Thresholds unchanged from the 2021 scheme; only "borderline" was renamed "inconclusive" to mirror CCv4.0.
- Figure 2 inconclusive zone: EGJ-DI <2.0 **or** diameter <16 mm, but not REO.

### Classification Statements

| Statement | Median | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| The FLIP classification combines contractile response and EGJ opening to give a motility impression | 9.0 | 88% |
| **A major motor disorder is unlikely with a "normal" FLIP classification** (normal CR with NEO) | 9.0 | 94% |
| **Diminished or absent CR with REO ("nonspastic obstruction") supports a disorder of EGJ outflow** | 8.5 | 94% |
| **A spastic or inconclusive FLIP classification should prompt further workup, such as HRM and/or timed barium esophagram (TBE) with tablet** | 9.0 | 81% |

### FLIP Panometry Motility Classification v2.0 (Figure 6)

| Contractile response | NEO | Inconclusive | REO |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spastic | Possible spasm | Possible obstruction* | Spastic obstruction |
| Disordered | Possible spasm | Possible obstruction* | Spastic obstruction |
| Normal | **Normal** | Possible obstruction* | Obstruction with normal contractility |
| Diminished | Hypocontractility | Possible obstruction* | Nonspastic obstruction |
| Absent | Hypocontractility | Possible obstruction* | Nonspastic obstruction |

*\*Inconclusive EGJ opening = "possible obstruction"; describe further by the CR category.*

- Dysmotility is a nonlinear spectrum: disordered CR carries lower probability of a spastic disorder than spastic CR; diminished CR lower probability of clinically relevant hypocontractility than absent CR.

### Clinical Interpretation (discussion)

- **Normal FLIP:** high negative predictive value (NPV) for a major, actionable motility disorder (including when HRM is inconclusive, e.g., EGJ outflow obstruction [EGJOO]) → management can likely be directed toward possible gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) or a functional syndrome.
- **Nonspastic obstruction:** a conclusive EGJ outflow disorder such as [[achalasia]] is suspected; may be enough for a definitive diagnosis with supportive clinical data, including when HRM alone is inconclusive. Discordant endoscopy, HRM, or esophagram → additional testing.
- **Inconclusive EGJ opening ("possible obstruction"):** usually needs TBE with tablet or HRM to define.
- **Possible spasm, spastic obstruction, or possible obstruction with spastic/disordered CR:** may support HRM to define type and extent of spastic activity.
- **Obstruction with normal contractility** (REO + normal CR): suggests **mechanical obstruction** → endoscopic re-inspection for subtle stricture or ring; consider dilation.
- RRCs or vigorous antegrade contractions may prompt further workup in the right context.
- Persistent uncertainty → more testing or monitoring during conservative management.

### Supporting Evidence (literature review)

| Finding | Data |
|---|---|
| Normal FLIP (NCR + NEO), 722-patient cohort | n = 86: **92%** normal motility, 6% absent contractility/ineffective esophageal motility, 2% distal esophageal spasm or hypercontractile esophagus, **no achalasia or EGJOO** on HRM |
| Healthy-subject EGJ-DI (meta-analysis, 15 studies, 154 subjects) | 5th–95th percentile **3.1–8.1 mm²/mmHg** at 60 mL; no healthy subject <2.0 |
| NEO (EGJ-DI ≥2.0 and diameter ≥16 mm), 687 patients | **99%** of 203 with NEO had normal EGJ outflow on HRM/CCv4.0 |
| Achalasia, 184-patient study | 0 of 23 achalasia patients had NEO |
| NEO + inconclusive EGJOO on HRM, conservative non-achalasia therapy | **90%** good symptomatic outcome |
| Older EGJ-DI-only definitions (threshold 2.8–3 mm²/mmHg) | Reduced distensibility in only 0–10% of achalasia, conclusive EGJOO, other major disorders, or abnormal TBE |
| Normal CR, 706 patients + 35 controls | None of 108 with NCR had achalasia or absent contractility; none of 218 with 100% failed peristalsis had NCR |
| Nonspastic obstruction, 722-patient cohort | Of 202, **92%** had HRM findings of achalasia or EGJOO; **77%** abnormal TBE emptying |
| Manometric EGJOO, 139 patients | 72% with conclusive FLIP for EGJ outflow disorder had diminished/absent CR; more abnormal TBE and higher success with sphincter-directed therapy |
| REO (EGJ-DI <2 and diameter <12 mm), large cohort series | Positive predictive value (PPV) **>75%** for manometric EGJ outflow disorder; NPV **>90%** when criteria not met |
| REO and TBE | DI <2: PPV 75%, NPV 100% (DI ≥2); DI <2 sensitivity 80%, diameter <12 mm specificity 90% for abnormal TBE |
| Botulinum toxin, prospective | Odds ratio **22.5** (95% confidence interval 2.5–206.7) for symptomatic response to LES botulinum toxin with diminished/absent CR |

- Supplementary Table 2 adds: Miller 2024 (n = 413) — FLIP vs TBE agreement 49%; DI <2 sensitivity 45.1%, specificity 61.9% for abnormal TBE.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Catheter positioning:** Dallas — 2 sensors (2 cm) below the crural diaphragm; [[aga-2025-flip-esophageal|AGA 2025 clinical practice update]] — 1–3 impedance sensor pairs distal to the EGJ. Compatible in practice; Dallas is the more specific protocol.
- **Diminished vs disordered cut:** statements use <40 vs ≥40 mmHg; Figure 5 asks whether pressure is ">40 mmHg" — a value of exactly 40 falls differently [[aga-2025-flip-esophageal|AGA 2025]] also differs: diminished ≤40 vs disordered >40 mmHg in its text (Table 1: <40 vs >40 mmHg pressure increase).
- **Distinct antegrade contraction:** Table 1 prints the pressure increase as "≥10 mm"; Figure 3 reads "10 mmHg or greater".
- **Scope:** classification not validated after foregut surgery or with mechanical obstruction; no FLIP norms after antireflux surgery ([[padova-2025-hrm-antireflux]] agrees).
- **Inconclusive categories** (inconclusive EGJ opening, RRCs, vigorous antegrade contractions) are named by the authors as targets for future work.
- **Evidence base:** only 2 statements underwent formal literature review; data largely retrospective, single-center (Chicago) cohorts.
