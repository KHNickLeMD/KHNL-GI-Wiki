## Summary

The American College of Gastroenterology's (ACG's) first dedicated diverticulitis guideline, scoped to the **outpatient gastroenterology consultation** after an episode — diagnosis, clinical course, recurrence/complication risk, the role of antibiotics, prevention, and indications for surgical referral. Fourteen numbered GRADE recommendations (the document's own numbering, Table 3), a definitions table (Table 1), six ungraded key concepts on diagnosis and clinical course (Table 4, expert opinion/epidemiology not amenable to GRADE), an antibiotic-use table (Table 6), and an 8th-grade patient education handout (Table 5).

Two practice reversals anchor the document. First, **antibiotics are no longer routine for low-risk acute uncomplicated diverticulitis** — four randomized controlled trials (RCTs) (n=1,809) show little/no effect on mortality, progression to complicated disease, surgery, readmission, or recurrence; antibiotics are reserved for high-risk features, immunocompromise, frailty, or unsafe outpatient management. Second, **the "elective colectomy after 2 episodes" rule is abandoned** — recurrence does not raise the risk of future complicated disease, so the number of episodes should not drive surgery; referral is for recurrent uncomplicated disease that significantly impairs quality of life (QoL), individualized by shared decision-making.

Computed tomography (CT) remains essential to confirm the diagnosis (clinical evaluation alone is inaccurate), especially at first presentation and in severe cases. **Colonoscopy after recovery** is a strong recommendation after *complicated* diverticulitis (6-fold higher odds of colon cancer vs uncomplicated) and conditional after *uncomplicated* disease in patients with alarm symptoms or not current with colorectal cancer (CRC) screening — and not within 6–8 weeks of the acute episode. The guideline explicitly debunks the nut/seed/corn/popcorn avoidance myth and recommends against mesalamine (strong), rifaximin, and probiotics for preventing recurrence.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Recommendation statements (all 14 — ACG's own numbering and grades, Table 3)

*Recommendations 4–12 and 14 are all scoped to **patients with a history of diverticulitis**, to reduce the risk of recurrence.*

1. **Recommend colonoscopy after recovery from an episode of *complicated* diverticulitis** to rule out a missed cancer or premalignant lesion — *strong recommendation, moderate certainty*.
2. **Suggest colonoscopy after recovery from an episode of *uncomplicated* diverticulitis** to rule out a missed cancer or premalignant lesion **in patients with alarm symptoms or in those not current with colon cancer screening** — *conditional, low certainty*.
   - Alarm features named by the guideline: unintentional weight loss, change in bowel habits, iron-deficiency anemia, bloody stools, persistent abdominal pain.
   - **Not within 6–8 weeks** of the acute episode (inflammation, edema, luminal narrowing → incomplete/unsafe exam).
3. **Suggest against routine antibiotics for low-risk patients with acute uncomplicated diverticulitis;** antibiotics are appropriate for patients with high-risk features, immunocompromise, frailty, or inability to ensure safe outpatient management — *conditional, moderate certainty*. (Criteria for both strata: *Antibiotic selection criteria* below.)
4. **Suggest a healthy diet** — high in fruits, vegetables, whole grains and legumes, with limited red meat, processed grains, trans fats and sweets — *conditional, very low certainty*.
5. **Suggest against avoidance of nut, corn, seed or popcorn consumption** — *conditional, very low certainty*.
6. **Unless there is a clinical indication, suggest avoiding regular nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) use** after recovery from an episode — *conditional, low certainty*.
7. **Suggest smoking cessation** in patients who smoke — *conditional, very low certainty*.
8. **Suggest moderation** in patients who consume heavy alcohol — *conditional, very low certainty*.
9. **Suggest weight loss** in patients who are overweight or obese — *conditional, low certainty*.
10. **Suggest regular physical activity** — *conditional, very low certainty*.
11. **In patients with recurrent, uncomplicated diverticulitis that significantly impairs QoL, suggest surgical referral** to discuss the risks and benefits of elective colon resection — *conditional, low certainty*. Does **not** apply to strictures, fistulas, chronic diverticulitis, or diverticulitis previously complicated by abscess or perforation.
12. **Recommend against probiotics** (in patients with a history of acute diverticulitis) — *conditional, very low certainty*.
13. **Recommend against mesalamine** (in patients with a history of acute diverticulitis) — *strong, moderate certainty*.
14. **Recommend against rifaximin** (in patients with a history of acute diverticulitis) — *conditional, very low certainty*.

Note the mismatched verb/strength pairs in 12 and 14: ACG words them "we recommend against" but grades both **conditional**; 13 is the only "recommend against" carrying a **strong** grade.

### Definitions (Table 1)

| Condition | ACG 2026 definition |
|---|---|
| Diverticulosis | Outpouchings of the colon formed when mucosa and submucosa herniate through the muscularis propria, typically at weak points where the vasa recta penetrate the wall. |
| Acute uncomplicated diverticulitis | Localized inflammation of colonic diverticula **without** associated abscess, perforation, stricture, or fistula. CT typically shows colonic wall thickening and/or increased pericolic fat stranding. |
| Smoldering or chronic diverticulitis | Inflammation persisting for **weeks to months**; often partial/complete symptomatic improvement on antibiotics then relapse shortly after they are stopped. An abscess is sometimes associated. |
| Complicated acute diverticulitis | Diverticular inflammation accompanied by **phlegmon, abscess, or perforation**. Graded by the CT-based modified Hinchey classification (stages 0–IV; full table on [[diverticulitis]]): stages 0–I are typically managed nonoperatively, higher stages often need drainage or surgery depending on severity and patient factors. Strictures and fistulas may develop as delayed complications. |
| Segmental colitis associated with diverticulosis (SCAD) | Chronic inflammatory condition of diverticula-bearing colonic segments, typically **sparing the rectum**; inflammation usually centered on and around the diverticula; histology can resemble ulcerative colitis or Crohn's disease. Whether it is a distinct entity or a manifestation of inflammatory bowel disease remains unclear. |
| Symptomatic uncomplicated diverticular disease (SUDD) | Controversial diagnosis — bloating, chronic/recurrent abdominal pain, or altered bowel habits in people with diverticulosis, **without** systemic inflammation or overt diverticulitis. |

### Key concepts — ungraded (Table 4)

Expert opinion/epidemiology, not run through GRADE; ACG does not assign these a strength or certainty.

- **Diagnosis:** clinical evaluation alone is inaccurate in most cases → CT is useful, especially at first presentation, to confirm diverticulitis, exclude alternative diagnoses, grade severity to guide treatment, and identify the affected location when surgery is being considered.
- **Risk of diverticulitis:** among people with diverticulosis, low — ~1%–4% over 7 years of follow-up.
- **Risk of complicated diverticulitis:** highest with the **initial** presentation, and decreases significantly with subsequent recurrences.
- **Risk of recurrence (nonoperative management):** 10-yr risk of a second episode ≥22%; after a second episode, 10-yr risk of a third ≥55%; after ≥3 episodes the likelihood of further recurrence remains high.
- **Risk after elective surgery:** surgery reduces but does not eliminate recurrence — 5-yr recurrence ~60% without surgery vs ~15% after elective colectomy.
- **Chronic gastrointestinal symptoms:** common after recovery (abdominal pain, altered bowel habits) — **more than one-third** report persistent symptoms 1–2 years after an episode.

### Antibiotic selection criteria (Table 6)

**Appropriate for initial NON-antibiotic management — *all* of the following must be present:**
- Immunocompetent · outpatient · no evidence of complicated disease · hemodynamically stable · no signs of systemic inflammatory response (fever, tachycardia, leukocytosis) · not medically frail · able to tolerate oral intake · no recent antibiotic use · reliable follow-up and adequate social support.

**Antibiotics are reasonable when any of these are present:**
- Immunocompromise · frailty · significant comorbid conditions · clinical deterioration or persistent/worsening symptoms · inability to tolerate oral intake · vomiting · elevated inflammatory markers (e.g., markedly elevated C-reactive protein [CRP]) · imaging features associated with higher risk (e.g., fluid collection or longer segment of inflammation) · inadequate outpatient support or inability to ensure close follow-up.

### Supporting numbers

- **Colon cancer risk after diverticulitis:** overall 1.6% (95% confidence interval [CI] 1.1–2.1); advanced neoplasia 5.5–6.7%. Complicated vs uncomplicated: **odds ratio (OR) 5.65 (2.95–10.8)** for cancer, OR 3.85 (2.11–7.03) for advanced neoplasia. Uncomplicated disease carries cancer risk similar to a screening population (OR 1.57, 0.70–3.51) and is actually **less** likely to involve advanced neoplasia (OR 0.68, 0.52–0.90). In a national US colonoscopy registry, outpatients with an indication of complicated diverticulitis had 4× the colon-cancer risk of a screening population (OR 3.57, 1.59–8.01). Cancers generally sit in the same colonic region as the presumed diverticulitis — the signature of misdiagnosis.
- **Colonoscopy timing:** not within **6–8 weeks** of the acute episode (incomplete colonoscopy risk 3.7%, perforation 0.2%).
- **Antibiotics in uncomplicated disease (meta-analysis of 4 RCTs, n=1,809 — mild, left-sided acute uncomplicated disease):** mortality risk ratio (RR) 1.03 (0.22–4.82); progression to complicated RR 0.72 (0.35–1.50); need for surgery RR 0.71 (0.35–1.44); readmission RR 0.86 (0.47–1.58); recurrent diverticulitis RR 0.95 (0.67–1.34).
- **Post-diagnosis complication risk:** ~5% within 3 months, most in the first 10 days. Clinical/radiographic factors flagged in the text: significant comorbidities, symptom duration >5 days, vomiting, **CRP >140 mg/L**, fluid collection on imaging, longer segment of colonic inflammation.
- **Recurrence** does **not** increase the risk of developing complicated disease (the basis for dropping episode count as a surgical trigger); numbers under *Key concepts* above.
- **Surgery:** DIRECT and LASER RCTs showed QoL improvement, but QoL did not improve or was worse in 30% (DIRECT), and in LASER 73% reported baseline abdominal pain while 63% still had pain 6 months postoperatively. Major surgical complications 10–34% in both trials; nearly one-third of patients with diverticulitis report significant decision regret after colectomy. Both trials were small and enrolled younger, healthier patients than the general diverticulitis population. CT confirmation of diverticulitis is essential before considering elective resection.
- **Mesalamine (American College of Physicians [ACP] 2022 meta-analysis, 6 RCTs, n=1,898):** no recurrence reduction (OR 1.15, 0.92–1.44); more discontinuation for adverse events (AEs) (OR 1.59, 1.12–2.27).
- **Smoking:** complicated diverticulitis RR 2.54 (1.49–4.33) current vs never. **Obesity:** recurrence hazard ratio (HR) 1.66 (1.09–2.51) for body mass index (BMI) ≥30 vs <22.5. **NSAIDs:** incident diverticulitis HR 1.72 (nonaspirin), 1.25 (aspirin).

### Antibiotic regimens (when indicated, outpatient acute uncomplicated) — usual duration 4–7 days

- **Monotherapy:** amoxicillin-clavulanate 875/125 mg by mouth (PO) every 12 hours (q12h) **or** moxifloxacin 400 mg PO daily.
- **Combination:** metronidazole 500 mg PO every 8 hours (q8h) **plus** one of: trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX) 1 double-strength (DS) tab (160/800 mg) q12h / ciprofloxacin 500 mg q12h / levofloxacin 500 mg q24h.
- Amoxicillin-clavulanate appears as effective as metronidazole + fluoroquinolone for outpatient diverticulitis and may reduce fluoroquinolone-associated harms (including *[[clostridioides-difficile|Clostridioides difficile]]* infection) without compromising diverticulitis-specific outcomes.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Reverses the historical "colectomy after 2 uncomplicated episodes" teaching** — number of episodes no longer an indication; young age alone is not an indication.
- **Reverses "all acute diverticulitis needs antibiotics"** — now selective.
- Does not apply the no-surgery/observation stance to complicated disease (strictures, fistulas, chronic/smoldering diverticulitis, or prior abscess/perforation).
- Evidence for most *prevention* recommendations is drawn from studies of **incident** (not recurrent) diverticulitis — effects assumed similar; certainty is mostly very low.
- Optimal **timing** of post-recovery colonoscopy beyond the 6–8-week floor is not specified.
- ACG flags its own evidence gaps: no validated prediction tools for who will recur, complicate, or benefit from antibiotics or elective surgery; no robust head-to-head data on which dietary pattern best prevents recurrence; no evidence on what or when to eat during an outpatient episode; few trials comparing elective surgery with conservative management in a real-world population.
- Smoldering/chronic diverticulitis is acknowledged as poorly understood (6% of new diagnoses at 6-month follow-up) and is managed by practice, not by a graded recommendation.
