## Summary

ACG's first dedicated diverticulitis guideline, scoped to the **outpatient gastroenterology consultation** after an episode — diagnosis, clinical course, recurrence/complication risk, the role of antibiotics, prevention, and indications for surgical referral. Fourteen GRADE recommendations plus a table of key concepts (definitions/epidemiology not amenable to GRADE) and an 8th-grade patient education handout.

Two practice reversals anchor the document. First, **antibiotics are no longer routine for low-risk acute uncomplicated diverticulitis** — four RCTs (n=1,809) show little/no effect on mortality, progression to complicated disease, surgery, readmission, or recurrence; antibiotics are reserved for high-risk features, immunocompromise, frailty, or unsafe outpatient management. Second, **the "elective colectomy after 2 episodes" rule is abandoned** — recurrence does not raise the risk of future complicated disease, so the number of episodes should not drive surgery; referral is for recurrent uncomplicated disease that significantly impairs quality of life, individualized by shared decision-making.

CT remains essential to confirm the diagnosis (clinical evaluation alone is inaccurate), especially at first presentation and in severe cases. **Colonoscopy after recovery** is a strong recommendation after *complicated* diverticulitis (6-fold higher odds of colon cancer vs uncomplicated) and conditional after *uncomplicated* disease only if the patient has alarm symptoms or is not current with CRC screening — and never within 6–8 weeks of the acute episode. The guideline explicitly debunks the nut/seed/corn/popcorn avoidance myth and recommends against mesalamine (strong), rifaximin, and probiotics for preventing recurrence.

## Key Findings / Claims

### Recommendation statements (all 14, verbatim intent, with GRADE)

1. **Colonoscopy after recovery from *complicated* diverticulitis** to rule out missed cancer or premalignant lesion — *strong, moderate certainty*.
2. **Colonoscopy after recovery from *uncomplicated* diverticulitis** to rule out cancer/premalignant lesion **only in patients with alarm symptoms or not current with CRC screening** — *conditional, low certainty*.
3. **Suggest against routine antibiotics for low-risk acute uncomplicated diverticulitis;** antibiotics appropriate for high-risk features, immunocompromise, frailty, or inability to ensure safe outpatient management — *conditional, moderate certainty*.
4. **Suggest a healthy diet** (high fruits/vegetables/whole grains/legumes; limited red meat, processed grains, trans fats, sweets) to reduce recurrence — *conditional, very low certainty*.
5. **Suggest against avoiding nuts, corn, seeds, or popcorn** to reduce recurrence — *conditional, very low certainty*.
6. **Suggest avoiding regular NSAID use** after recovery unless clinically indicated — *conditional, low certainty*.
7. **Suggest smoking cessation** in patients who smoke — *conditional, very low certainty*.
8. **Suggest alcohol moderation** in heavy drinkers — *conditional, very low certainty*.
9. **Suggest weight loss** in patients who are overweight or obese — *conditional, low certainty*.
10. **Suggest regular physical activity** — *conditional, very low certainty*.
11. **Suggest surgical referral** for recurrent uncomplicated diverticulitis that significantly impairs QoL, to discuss risks/benefits of elective colon resection — *conditional, low certainty*.
12. **Recommend against probiotics** to reduce recurrence — *conditional, very low certainty*.
13. **Recommend against mesalamine** to reduce recurrence — *strong, moderate certainty*.
14. **Recommend against rifaximin** to reduce recurrence — *conditional, very low certainty*.

### Supporting numbers

- **Colon cancer risk after diverticulitis:** overall 1.6% (95% CI 1.1–2.1); advanced neoplasia 5.5–6.7%. Complicated vs uncomplicated: **OR 5.65 (2.95–10.8)** for cancer, OR 3.85 (2.11–7.03) for advanced neoplasia. Uncomplicated disease carries cancer risk similar to a screening population (OR 1.57, 0.70–3.51).
- **Colonoscopy timing:** not within **6–8 weeks** of the acute episode (incomplete colonoscopy risk 3.7%, perforation 0.2%).
- **Antibiotics in uncomplicated disease (meta-analysis of 4 RCTs, n=1,809):** mortality RR 1.03 (0.22–4.82); progression to complicated RR 0.72 (0.35–1.50); surgery RR 0.71 (0.35–1.44); readmission RR 0.86 (0.47–1.58); recurrence RR 0.95 (0.67–1.34).
- **Post-diagnosis complication risk:** ~5% within 3 months, most in the first 10 days. High-risk features: significant comorbidities, symptoms >5 days, vomiting, **CRP >140 mg/L**, fluid collection on imaging, longer segment of inflammation.
- **Recurrence (nonoperative):** 10-yr risk of a 2nd episode ≥22%; after a 2nd episode, 10-yr risk of a 3rd ≥55%; ≥3 episodes → persistently high risk. Recurrence does **not** increase risk of complicated disease.
- **Surgery:** 5-yr recurrence ~15% after elective colectomy vs ~60% without. DIRECT/LASER RCTs showed QoL improvement but QoL did not improve or was worse in 30% (DIRECT); major surgical complications 10–34%.
- **Diverticulosis → diverticulitis:** low, ~1–4% over 7 years.
- **Mesalamine (ACP 2022 meta-analysis, 6 RCTs, n=1,898):** no recurrence reduction (OR 1.15, 0.92–1.44); more discontinuation for AEs (OR 1.59, 1.12–2.27).
- **Smoking:** complicated diverticulitis RR 2.54 (1.49–4.33) current vs never. **Obesity:** recurrence HR 1.66 (1.09–2.51) for BMI ≥30 vs <22.5. **NSAIDs:** incident diverticulitis HR 1.72 (nonaspirin), 1.25 (aspirin).

### Antibiotic regimens (when indicated, outpatient acute uncomplicated) — usual duration 4–7 days

- **Monotherapy:** amoxicillin-clavulanate 875/125 mg PO q12h **or** moxifloxacin 400 mg PO daily.
- **Combination:** metronidazole 500 mg PO q8h **plus** one of: TMP-SMX 1 DS tab (160/800 mg) q12h / ciprofloxacin 500 mg q12h / levofloxacin 500 mg q24h.
- Amoxicillin-clavulanate appears as effective as metronidazole + fluoroquinolone and may reduce fluoroquinolone-associated harms (incl. *C. difficile*).

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Reverses the historical "colectomy after 2 uncomplicated episodes" teaching** — number of episodes no longer an indication; young age alone is not an indication.
- **Reverses "all acute diverticulitis needs antibiotics"** — now selective.
- Does not apply the no-surgery/observation stance to complicated disease (strictures, fistulas, chronic/smoldering diverticulitis, or prior abscess/perforation).
- Evidence for most *prevention* recommendations is drawn from studies of **incident** (not recurrent) diverticulitis — effects assumed similar; certainty is mostly very low.
