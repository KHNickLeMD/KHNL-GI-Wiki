## Summary

AGA Institute guideline addressing a single, narrow question: what to do after a patient with [[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease]] has been brought into surgically induced remission by bowel resection. Six GRADE-rated recommendations cover whether to start drug prophylaxis immediately or wait for endoscopy to declare recurrence, which drug class to use, which classes to avoid, when to scope, and what to do when the scope finds asymptomatic recurrence.

**Scope is deliberately tight, and the boundaries matter.** The recommendations apply to patients with an **ileocolonic anastomosis** who are **asymptomatic** and have **no macroscopic evidence of CD** after resection. They are explicitly **not** applicable to patients with small-bowel anastomoses not accessible by colonoscopy, those with residual disease after resection, or those who already have clinical symptoms of active CD.

The problem is framed by natural history: nearly half of CD patients require bowel resection within the first 10 years of disease, surgery is not curative, and one-quarter of those patients will require at least another bowel resection within 5 years of the index surgery. Endoscopic recurrence precedes clinical and surgical recurrence and occurs in as many as **90% of neoterminal ilea within 12 months** of resection. Because there were too few surgical-recurrence events in the trials to serve as an endpoint, the panel evaluated **prevention of endoscopic recurrence** — defined throughout as a **Rutgeerts score ≥i2 on ileocolonoscopy** — as a strong surrogate for surgical recurrence.

The panel found **no validated clinical score that predicts postoperative recurrence**, so instead of a formal risk instrument it published two *illustrative* risk groups (Table 4) with their characteristics and their expected recurrence rates at >18 months in the absence of any intervention. The panel favored early prophylaxis for most patients on the reasoning that the majority of real-world post-resection patients carry at least one risk factor, while acknowledging that lower-risk patients may reasonably choose endoscopy-guided treatment instead.

## Key Findings / Claims

### The six recommendations, verbatim (Table 3), with strength and quality

| # | Statement | Strength | Quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "In patients with surgically induced remission of CD, the AGA suggests early pharmacological prophylaxis over endoscopy-guided pharmacological treatment." *Comments: Patients, particularly those at lower risk of recurrence, who place a higher value on avoiding the small risks of adverse events from pharmacological prophylaxis and a lower value on the potential risk of early disease recurrence may reasonably select endoscopy-guided pharmacological treatment over prophylaxis.* | Conditional | **Very low** |
| 2 | "In patients with surgically induced remission of CD, the AGA suggests using anti-TNF therapy and/or thiopurines over other agents." *Comments: Patients at lower risk for disease recurrence or who place a higher value on avoiding the small risk of adverse events of thiopurines or anti-TNF treatment and a lower value on a modestly increased risk of disease recurrence may reasonably choose nitroimidazole antibiotics (for 3–12 months).* | Conditional | Moderate |
| 3 | "In patients with surgically induced remission of CD, the AGA suggests **against** using mesalamine (or other 5-aminosalicylates), budesonide, or probiotics." | Conditional | Low; very low |
| 4 | "In patients with surgically induced remission of CD **receiving** pharmacological prophylaxis, the AGA suggests postoperative endoscopic monitoring at 6 to 12 months after surgical resection over no monitoring." | Conditional | Moderate |
| 5 | "In patients with surgically induced remission of CD **not receiving** pharmacological prophylaxis, the AGA **recommends** postoperative endoscopic monitoring at 6 to 12 months after surgical resection over no monitoring." | **Strong** | Moderate |
| 6 | "In patients with surgically induced remission of CD with asymptomatic endoscopic recurrence, the AGA suggests initiating or optimizing anti-TNF and/or thiopurine therapy over continued monitoring alone." *Comments: Patients who place a higher value on avoiding the small risk of adverse events of thiopurines or anti-TNF treatment and a lower value on the increased risk of clinical recurrence following asymptomatic endoscopic recurrence may reasonably choose continued endoscopic monitoring.* | Conditional | Moderate |

> Recommendations 4 and 5 differ only in whether the patient is on prophylaxis, and that difference changes the strength from **conditional to strong**. The panel issued the strong recommendation for unprotected patients because the likelihood of benefit from detecting endoscopic recurrence is high when the risk of that recurrence runs as high as 90% within 1 year of surgery.

### Table 4 — Illustrative risk groups for recurrence in the absence of any intervention

| Risk group | Typical patient characteristics | Clinical recurrence (>18 mo after surgery) | Endoscopic recurrence (>18 mo after surgery) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lower risk** | Older patient (older than 50 y) · Nonsmoker · First surgery for a short segment of fibrostenotic disease (<10 to 20 cm) · Disease duration >10 y | **20%** | **30%** |
| **Higher risk** | Younger patient (younger than 30 y) · Smoker · ≥2 prior surgeries for penetrating disease, with or without perianal disease | **50%** | **80%** |

- These are **illustrative**, not a validated instrument — the guideline states plainly that **there is no validated clinical score that predicts postoperative recurrence**, and calls the development and validation of a postoperative recurrence scale a prerequisite for more effective implementation of these recommendations.
- The **Rutgeerts score**, although it correlates with natural history, "has not been validated for use in clinical trials of postoperative prophylaxis."

### Supporting efficacy data

- **Early prophylaxis vs endoscopy-guided treatment** — a single clinical trial of **63** postoperative patients using azathioprine failed to show a difference: clinical recurrence **RR 0.83 (95% CI 0.46–1.50)**, endoscopic recurrence **RR 0.91 (95% CI 0.59–1.42)**. "Early" prophylaxis means **started within 8 weeks of surgery**; endoscopy-guided means starting therapy only if endoscopic recurrence is found on colonoscopy at **6 to 12 months**. Because of clinical equipoise, the panel says the choice must be individualized.
- **Anti-TNF vs placebo:** relative reductions in clinical and endoscopic recurrence at 18 months of **49% and 76%**, respectively (moderate quality).
- **Thiopurines:** relative decreases in clinical and endoscopic recurrence of **65% and 60%**, respectively.
- **Antibiotics:** reduced the risk of endoscopic and clinical recurrence by **approximately 50%** (moderate quality) — but are "probably inferior to anti-TNF agents by a large extent" and "may be modestly inferior to thiopurines" (low quality).
- **Nitroimidazoles are the only antibiotic class adequately studied**, so if antibiotics are chosen postoperatively, a nitroimidazole (eg, metronidazole) should be used. **Duration is capped at 3 to 12 months** because cumulative dosing causes peripheral neuropathy — and **disease usually recurs within a couple of years after antibiotics are stopped**.
- **5-ASA:** low-quality evidence suggested reduced clinical recurrence (**RR 0.59; 95% CI 0.43–0.82**), but the evidence for preventing *endoscopic* recurrence was "even less compelling due to imprecision, inconsistency, and strongly suspected publication bias," and indirect evidence from luminal CD supports a lack of benefit for inducing or maintaining remission. Budesonide and probiotics carried very low quality evidence. The main risk of using these three is **disease recurrence by foregoing more effective therapies**.
- **POCER trial** (De Cruz et al., *Lancet* 2015;385:1406) — endoscopic monitoring with algorithmic treatment step-up vs continuing the early postoperative strategy: clinical recurrence **RR 0.82 (95% CI 0.56–1.18)**, endoscopic recurrence **RR 0.73 (95% CI 0.56–0.95)**. 83% of subjects were categorized high risk and received azathioprine or adalimumab after surgery; **all** subjects received metronidazole for 3 months. The guideline notes this design means POCER likely **underestimates** the benefit of endoscopic monitoring in patients on no pharmacological therapy at all.

### Treatment selection at asymptomatic endoscopic recurrence (Rec 6)

- Preference for **anti-TNF agents and thiopurines, as monotherapy or combination therapy**, is based on indirect evidence from the AGA guideline on thiopurines/methotrexate/anti-TNF in luminal CD; **no direct evidence was available** on combining anti-TNF with thiopurines in the postoperative setting, though indirect evidence supports it in patients at highest risk.
- **Thiopurine monotherapy**, with its slower onset of action and potentially lower efficacy, "may be more appropriate for those with less severe endoscopic recurrence (ie, Rutgeerts' score of i2)."
- Patients who have **endoscopic recurrence while already on a thiopurine** should have **step-up therapy with the addition of an anti-TNF agent**, either as monotherapy or combination therapy.

### Explicit evidence gaps stated by the guideline

- **No validated score** predicts postoperative endoscopic or clinical recurrence.
- **Insufficient evidence** to inform **how often** endoscopic monitoring should be performed *following the initial postoperative colonoscopy*.
- **Sparse direct evidence** on the comparative effectiveness of different pharmacological agents once asymptomatic endoscopic recurrence has occurred.
- The role of **newer classes of biologics** for prevention of postoperative recurrence "has yet to be determined."

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Vedolizumab — covered by [[acg-2025-crohns]], absent here.** [[acg-2025-crohns]] gives vedolizumab a conditional/low recommendation for high-risk postoperative prophylaxis. AGA 2017 predates that evidence entirely and says only that the role of newer biologic classes "has yet to be determined." Not a conflict — a gap of vintage. **ACG 2025 is the newer tier-1 source and governs the page.**
- **Metronidazole dose vs duration.** [[acg-2025-crohns]] supplies the dose (**1–2 g/day**); AGA 2017 supplies the **duration cap (3–12 months)** and the reason (cumulative peripheral neuropathy). The two are complementary, and the page needs both to be actionable — neither source gives the full regimen alone.
- **Strength of the monitoring recommendation.** The wiki previously carried postoperative endoscopic monitoring at 6–12 months as a single *conditional* recommendation. AGA 2017 splits it: **conditional** if the patient is on prophylaxis, **strong** if they are not. Both guidelines agree on the 6–12 month interval.
- **Open, per the guideline itself:** the optimal *frequency* of endoscopic monitoring after the first postoperative colonoscopy is unresolved, and no ingested source answers it.
