## Summary
- WEO guideline on the **first step of endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)** — deep biliary cannulation — plus sphincterotomy, papillary balloon dilation, and access in altered anatomy. Intended to apply **worldwide regardless of resources**; positioned as an update to the European Society of Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ESGE) 2016 cannulation/sphincterotomy guideline and the 2017 international difficult-biliary-access consensus (p. 1030).
- **24 numbered statements** under 14 CQs; each carries a strength, a GRADE certainty and its voting result (Table 2). **6 strong** (Statements 1, 2, 3, 5, 11, 12) and **18 conditional**.
- **Core positions:**
  - PEP prophylaxis: rectal nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) for all patients without contraindications; aggressive lactated Ringer's hydration when NSAIDs are unavailable (inpatients); prophylactic pancreatic duct (PD) stent in high-risk patients **if PD access has already been achieved**; combinations suggested.
  - Cannulation: **guidewire-assisted** for primary cannulation; after repeated unintentional PD cannulation → double guidewire (DGW) or transpancreatic sphincterotomy (TPS) then PD stent; difficult cannulation without PD entry → precut or intentional PD cannulation for pancreatic guidewire-assisted techniques; failure → repeat ERCP at a high-volume center **≥48 h** later, or percutaneous / endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)-guided rendezvous if drainage is urgent.
  - Sphincterotomy: **mixed (blended or alternating) current**; size individualized but not beyond the upper margin of the intraluminal biliary bulge; small-balloon dilation only when sphincterotomy is contraindicated by bleeding tendency, dilated **>1 to ≤5 min**; large-balloon dilation + small sphincterotomy for large/difficult stones in a duct **≥12 mm**; **no routine sphincterotomy before stenting malignant obstruction**.
  - Special circumstances: periampullary diverticulum → usual techniques; Billroth II → forward-viewing scope with cap or duodenoscope, guidewire-assisted with rotatable/Billroth II sphincterotomes; other altered anatomy → device-assisted enteroscopy; Roux-en-Y gastric bypass (RYGB) → laparoscopy-assisted ERCP over enteroscopy-assisted ERCP, or EUS-directed transgastric ERCP; duodenal obstruction → EUS- or percutaneous-guided access.
- Two algorithms: PEP prevention by procedural phase (Figure 2) and difficult biliary cannulation (Figure 3), transcribed below.

### Definitions (as printed)

| Term | Definition | Locator |
|---|---|---|
| **Difficult biliary cannulation** (predefined by the panel) | Any of: **>10 min** attempting to cannulate after the papilla is visualized · **>5 cannulation attempts** · **≥2 unintended PD cannulations or PD opacification with contrast** | Methods, p. 1030 |
| **Post-ERCP pancreatitis (PEP)** | New or worsened abdominal pain compatible with pancreatitis + serum amylase or lipase **>3 times** normal at **more than 24 h** after ERCP + requiring new or prolonged hospitalization (cited from ESGE) | p. 1031 |
| **Unintentional PD cannulation/access** with the guidewire | Easy, preferential entry into the PD, **not** requiring repeated cannulation attempts or papilla manipulation | CQ 3 discussion, p. 1036 |

| **Sphincterotomy (ES) size** | **Small** = does not exceed the circumferential hood around the orifice · **Large** = extends to the intramural portion of the bile duct · **Medium** = between these landmarks (Figure S4) | Statement 13 discussion, p. 1038 |
| **Endoscopic papillary small balloon dilation (EPSBD)** | Balloon dilation of the papilla, typically **≤10 mm** diameter, used instead of ES | Statement 14–15 discussion, p. 1039 |
| **Endoscopic papillary large balloon dilation (EPLBD)** | Sphincteroplasty / papillary dilation with a **≥12 mm** balloon | Statement 16 discussion, p. 1039 |
| **Mixed current** | **Blended** (cut and coagulation delivered together) or **alternating** (cut and coagulation interspersed in short bursts) | Statement 12 discussion, p. 1037 |
| **Duodenal stricture types** | **Type 1** proximal to the papilla · **Type 2** involves the ampullary region · **Type 3** distal to the papilla | Statement 24 discussion, p. 1042 |

### PEP prophylaxis (CQ 1)

- Background (p. 1031): meta-analysis of 145 RCTs (19,038 patients) — PEP **10.2%** (95% confidence interval [CI] 9.3–11.3) overall, **14.1%** (11.5–17.2) in high-risk patients; severe PEP **0.5%**, mortality **0.2%**. Patients are stratified high- or low-risk by patient- and procedure-related factors per ESGE (the factors themselves are not listed in this document).

| # | Statement (pp. 1032, 1034–1035) | Strength / evidence | Vote |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | When used alone, WEO **recommends** aggressive periprocedural and postprocedural intravenous (IV) **lactated Ringer's** to prevent PEP **in an inpatient setting when rectal NSAIDs are not available** | Strong / Moderate | SA 83.3%, A 16.7% |
| 2 | When used alone, WEO **recommends rectal NSAIDs from 30 to 90 min before to immediately after ERCP for all patients** if there are no contraindications | Strong / Moderate | SA 83.3%, A 16.7% |
| 3 | When used alone, WEO **recommends a prophylactic pancreatic stent in high-risk patients if PD access has already been achieved** | Strong / Moderate | SA 83.3%, A 16.7% |
| 4 | Some evidence that combination therapy may further reduce PEP; WEO **suggests rectal NSAIDs together with aggressive IV hydration and/or PD stenting** | Conditional / Low | SA 72.7%, A 27.3% |

*SA = strongly agree · A = agree.*

- **Hydration (Statement 1, p. 1034):** regimen as defined above (Buxbaum protocol). Lactated Ringer's used in most studies; data suggest it is superior to normal saline. 9 meta-analyses of RCTs: less PEP with aggressive hydration, no excess adverse events (AEs) vs standard or no hydration; noninferior to rectal NSAIDs in one RCT. Limits: prolonged (up to 24 h) hydration may not suit outpatient ERCP; may be contraindicated with cardiac and/or renal insufficiency.
- **Rectal NSAIDs (Statement 2, p. 1034):**
  - 31 meta-analyses; all but 2 showed reduced PEP in unselected and high-risk populations without more AEs.
  - **Excluded populations** (so the recommendation does not cover them): history of **peptic ulcer disease**, **renal insufficiency**, **allergy to aspirin and NSAIDs**.
  - **Indomethacin or diclofenac** — both effective; no evidence to prefer one. **Rectal route** (higher efficacy than other routes). **Standard adult dose 100 mg.** Low-dose evidence conflicting — a low-dose alternative "can be considered" when the standard dose is contraindicated.
  - Timing in positive studies: before ERCP (**minimum 30 min, maximum 90 min**) or immediately after.
- **PD stent (Statement 3, p. 1034):**
  - 12 meta-analyses (2004–2023): PD stent vs placebo in high-risk and mixed groups — odds ratio 0.22–0.50, absolute risk difference 12.0–13.3%, number needed to treat 7–10; nearly eliminated severe PEP.
  - An American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) meta-analysis also showed benefit with intentional stenting at the end of cannulation, but intentional PD cannulation rates may be lower in community hospitals.
  - **Failed PD stent attempt is itself a PEP risk factor** — PEP **34.7%** with failed PD stenting and no indomethacin (Choksi et al).
  - **Stent spec:** short **(3 to 5 cm) 5F** PD stent with **no internal flange** (spontaneous migration); **external flange/pigtail** to avoid intrapancreatic migration.
  - **Dwell:** not more than **14 days** (stent-induced PD fibrosis, higher PEP risk); **abdominal X-ray within 14 days** to confirm spontaneous passage.
  - Benefit questionable with chronic PD obstruction and atrophic parenchyma, even if PD access is achieved.
- **Combination (Statement 4, p. 1035):** evidence mainly indirect (network meta-analyses). Elmunzer noninferiority RCT: **indomethacin alone was inferior to indomethacin + prophylactic stent** (P = 0.011); benefit most prominent in the highest-risk patients; **19.3%** of the stent arm did not receive a stent for technical reasons. Panel: absent contraindications, rectal NSAIDs with aggressive hydration and/or a PD stent if PD access already achieved.

**Figure 2 — PEP prevention by phase (p. 1035), transcribed:**

| Phase | Measure | Notes printed in the figure |
|---|---|---|
| Preprocedural | Rectal NSAIDs for unselected patients | Avoid with history of peptic ulcer disease, renal insufficiency, or allergy to NSAIDs · Administer **30–90 minutes before / during ERCP** |

### Biliary cannulation techniques (CQ 2–5)

| # | Statement (pp. 1032, 1035–1037) | Strength / evidence | Vote |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | WEO **recommends the guidewire-assisted technique for primary biliary cannulation** — lower PEP risk and higher success rate | Strong / Moderate | SA 81.8%, A 18.2% |
| 6 | **More than one unintentional PD cannulation with deep guidewire access into the PD** → WEO **suggests** attempting biliary cannulation with **TPS or the DGW technique**, followed by **pancreatic stent placement after cannulation** | Conditional / Low | SA 45.5%, A 54.5% |
| 7 | **Failed** biliary cannulation with TPS or DGW **and a guidewire in the PD** → WEO **suggests a PD stent followed by precut sphincterotomy over the stent** | Conditional / Very low | SA 27.3%, A 72.7% |
| 8 | **Difficult biliary cannulation without unintentional PD cannulation** → WEO **suggests advanced biliary cannulation techniques** (such as **precut**, or an **intentional PD cannulation attempt for pancreatic guidewire-assisted techniques**), **with or without a PD stent** after biliary cannulation | Conditional / Low | SA 27.3%, A 72.7% |
| 9 | **Bile duct cannulation failure** → WEO **suggests referral to high-volume centers for repeat ERCP with an interval of at least 48 h** between attempts, provided the patient's condition is suitable for the delay | Conditional / Very low | SA 63.6%, A 36.4% |
| 10 | Patient's condition **requires urgent biliary drainage** and expertise is available → WEO **suggests** biliary cannulation by the **percutaneous rendezvous** technique | Conditional / Very low | SA 75%, A 25% |
| 11 | **Alternatively**, if the expertise is available, WEO **recommends** the **EUS-guided rendezvous** technique to access the biliary system | Strong / Moderate | SA 91.7%, A 8.3% |

- **Statement 5 (p. 1035):** 3 meta-analyses (15 RCTs, 4 as abstracts) — guidewire-assisted cannulation had significantly higher success and lower PEP than contrast-assisted in all three. A "mixed" technique is also described. Careful observation of papillary morphology before attempting cannulation may predict the ease of cannulation (detail in the supplement).
- **Statements 6–7 (p. 1036):**
  - Persisting with standard attempts after unintentional PD entry → repeated PD cannulation → higher PEP risk. DGW and TPS both allow a PD stent after biliary cannulation **without further papilla manipulation**; in a DGW RCT, PD stent placement brought PEP close to 0%.
  - DGW vs persistent single-guidewire attempts (1 RCT, 274 patients): no difference in cannulation rate; DGW more effective in **malignant biliary strictures**; PEP similar (but only 18% of the DGW group received a PD stent).
  - TPS vs DGW: **no clear evidence to prefer either** — similar cannulation success in 2 RCTs; PEP comparable in the larger, higher with DGW in the smaller.
  - One RCT: cannulation with a guidewire after PD stent placement was less successful than DGW, but after **precut over the stent** success was equal.
- **Statement 8 (p. 1036):** options = persistence with standard techniques, or advanced techniques (**early/late precut sphincterotomy or fistulotomy**; PD cannulation attempt followed by pancreatic guidewire-assisted techniques). **Early precut** has some PEP advantage over persistent standard attempts, **especially fistulotomy**. Reserve the PD-cannulation attempt for when precut/fistulotomy is judged challenging — PD cannulation attempts carry higher PEP risk than fistulotomy. Choose by papillary anatomy, operator experience and ERCP indication.
- **Statements 9–11 (p. 1037):**
  - No study compares repeat ERCP with rendezvous; rendezvous needs more expertise and may be more invasive → **repeat ERCP is more reasonable when drainage is not urgent**.
  - **Repeat ERCP:** 10 studies, pooled success **79%** (95% CI 75–82%); most second attempts succeeded with standard techniques; the biliary orifice is usually easier to find if a precut or TPS was done at the index ERCP; AE rate comparable to the index ERCP. Reported intervals **2 to 6 days**, some evidence that longer intervals give higher cannulation rates — but waiting longer than 2 days should be individualized.
  - **Percutaneous rendezvous** (6 retrospective studies after failed cannulation): technical success **95%** (93–97%), AEs **23%** (7–38%).
  - **EUS-guided rendezvous** (18 studies): technical success **87%** (82–92%), AEs **13%** (9–16%).
  - Discussion text: "The panel suggests equally" percutaneous and EUS-guided rendezvous, by center availability and expertise.

**Figure 3 — Algorithm for difficult biliary cannulation (p. 1038), transcribed branch by branch:**

1. **Guidewire-assisted biliary cannulation** → **difficult biliary cannulation** → decision: **≥2 unintentional PD cannulations?**
2. **Yes** → **pancreatic guidewire-assisted techniques** →
   - biliary cannulation achieved → **PD stent**;
   - biliary cannulation failed → **PD stent** → **precut**.
3. **No** → either (the figure prints "or"):
   - **precut**; or
   - **attempt PD cannulation** → PD cannulation achieved? **Yes** → pancreatic guidewire-assisted techniques (step 2); **No** → precut.
4. **Precut** → if **biliary cannulation failed** → **urgent drainage required?**
   - **No** → **repeat ERCP after 2–3 days**;
   - **Yes** → **percutaneous or EUS-guided rendezvous**.

### Biliary sphincterotomy and papillary balloon dilation (CQ 6–9)

| # | Statement (pp. 1033, 1037–1040) | Strength / evidence | Vote |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | WEO **recommends a mixed electrocautery current for endoscopic sphincterotomy (ES)** | Strong / High | SA 83.3%, A 16.7% |
| 13 | WEO **suggests** ES size be **individualized** to the patient's condition and the endoscopist's experience, but **not exceed the upper margin of the intraluminal biliary bulge** | Conditional / Very low | SA 75%, A 25% |
| 14 | WEO **suggests EPSBD as an alternative to ES in patients with hemorrhagic tendencies when ES is contraindicated** | Conditional / Low | SA 66.7%, A 33.3% |
| 15 | WEO **suggests an extended dilation duration (>1 to ≤5 min) when EPSBD is employed**, to reduce pancreatitis risk | Conditional / Low | SA 25%, A 75% |
| 16 | WEO **suggests EPLBD in conjunction with a small ES to retrieve large-size or difficult common bile duct stones in a dilated duct of ≥12 mm** | Conditional / Moderate | SA 58.3%, A 41.7% |
| 17 | WEO **suggests against routine ES before biliary stent placement for malignant obstruction** | Conditional / High | SA 50%, A 50% |

- **Current (Statement 12, pp. 1037–1038):** 4 meta-analyses (up to 11 RCTs) — **pure cut** vs alternating or blended: comparable PEP, **more bleeding with pure cut** (mild bleeding only, not moderate–severe); no difference in perforation. A later RCT: PEP higher with alternating current (5.8% vs 2.2%, P = 0.034); intraprocedural bleeding more with pure cut (P = 0.018), delayed bleeding more with alternating (P = 0.047). **Blended vs alternating:** no PEP difference in 3 meta-analyses — no basis to prefer one. Pure cut first then blended: no advantage shown.
- **Size (Statement 13, pp. 1038–1039):**
  - ES can be extended along the bile duct axis up to the junction of the intraduodenal bile duct with the duodenal wall; this landmark can often be judged endoscopically.
  - Cutting **beyond the superior margin of the ampulla** (the border of the distal common bile duct on the medial duodenal wall) can increase **perforation** risk.
  - Size follows the intended intervention: **minimal** (just enough for a stent) for biliary obstruction from ampullary cancer; **as large as the stone** for a large stone (to allow extraction ± lithotripsy).
  - Extending a prior ES to the level of the **first duodenal fold** can be done safely if needed.
  - Other determinants: distal common bile duct size, ampullary anatomy, scope position, endoscopist experience.
  - **If a large ES cannot be done safely → small ES followed by balloon dilation of the ampulla** (large ES carries more AEs, including perforation and bleeding).
- **EPSBD (Statements 14–15, p. 1039):**
  - 4 meta-analyses vs ES: EPSBD had **less complete stone clearance** (overall and at the index procedure), **higher pancreatitis risk**, more need for mechanical or electrohydraulic lithotripsy; **bleeding close to zero** and significantly lower than ES. A newer meta-analysis of **≤10 mm** balloons favored ES for clearance and PEP.
  - **Dilation ≤1 min** → higher PEP in 3 meta-analyses; **>1 min** → pancreatitis risk comparable with ES.
  - Panel: EPSBD **cannot be recommended as an alternative to ES for small to medium-sized stones**; useful only in select patients with **coagulation disorders where ES is contraindicated**.
- **EPLBD (Statement 16, pp. 1039–1040):**
  - ES before EPLBD ablates the sphincter and directs the tear along the ES, reducing pressure on the PD. Either a small or a large ES can precede it, but a large ES may increase bleeding and perforation. **EPLBD without ES** can be considered where ES is difficult (e.g., surgically altered anatomy).
  - 6 meta-analyses: **ES + EPLBD beat ES alone and EPLBD alone** for overall stone removal and clearance at the index ERCP and needed less mechanical lithotripsy; only single-operator cholangioscopy did better (network meta-analysis). PEP and perforation lower after ES + EPLBD; bleeding more frequent vs EPLBD alone.
  - **Balloon sizing:** match the distal bile duct diameter and the stone's short-axis diameter; **maximum balloon diameter not to exceed the distal bile duct diameter**.
- **ES before stenting malignant obstruction (Statement 17, p. 1040):**
  - 4 meta-analyses: ES adds **bleeding**; no difference in stent insertion success, migration, occlusion, or perforation. One meta-analysis found more cholangitis after ES (driven by a study of proximal stenoses with stents above the papilla).
  - PEP differed across the meta-analyses: one (3 RCTs, 2014) found more PEP **without** ES (again driven by Zhou et al); two found no difference, including when only self-expandable metal stents (SEMS) were analyzed; one showed a trend favoring ES in malignant obstruction, regardless of plastic stent or SEMS. Two RCTs found no PEP difference with or without ES after **large-bore (8.5F or 10F) plastic stents**.
  - Panel: ES not necessary in all malignant stent placements; PEP with or without ES for a **fully covered SEMS (FCSEMS) across the papilla without chronic PD obstruction** needs further study.

### ERCP in special circumstances (CQ 10–14)

| # | Statement (pp. 1033, 1040–1042) | Strength / evidence | Vote |
|---|---|---|---|
| 18 | **Periampullary diverticulum (PAD)** → WEO **suggests** proceeding with cannulation and sphincterotomy **in the usual manner** using the techniques above | Conditional / Moderate | SA 75%, A 25% |
| 19 | **Billroth II gastrectomy** → WEO **suggests accessing the papilla with a forward-viewing endoscope with a distal cap, or a duodenoscope** | Conditional / Moderate | SA 66.7%, A 33.3% |
| 20 | **Billroth II gastrectomy** → WEO **suggests guidewire-assisted biliary cannulation with rotatable or Billroth II-dedicated sphincterotomes, or bendable cannulas** | Conditional / Very low | SA 27.3%, A 72.7% |
| 21 | **Other surgically altered anatomy where ductal access is difficult** (apart from Billroth II) → WEO **suggests ERCP with device-assisted enteroscopy** | Conditional / High | SA 27.3%, A 72.7% |
| 22 | **RYGB for obesity** → WEO **suggests laparoscopy-assisted ERCP (LA-ERCP) over enteroscopy-assisted ERCP (EA-ERCP)**, because it is more technically successful | Conditional / Moderate | SA 36.4%, A 63.6% |
| 23 | Alternatively, if expertise is available, WEO **suggests EUS-directed transgastric ERCP (EDGE)** | Conditional / Moderate | SA 72.7%, A 27.3% |
| 24 | **Inaccessible papilla due to duodenal obstruction** → WEO **suggests EUS- or percutaneous-guided access** according to local expertise | Conditional / Low | SA 75%, A 25% |

- **PAD (Statement 18, pp. 1040–1041):** 3 meta-analyses — lower cannulation success with PAD, mainly from older studies; significantly lower with an **intradiverticular papilla**. Two found more PEP and bleeding, one more perforation. No studies compare techniques in PAD. **Prudence with free-hand precut** when cannulation is difficult without unintentional PD cannulation — anatomy and positioning raise bleeding and perforation risk.
- **Billroth II (Statements 19–20, p. 1041):**
  - Gastroscope with or without a distal cap vs duodenoscope: comparable afferent-loop intubation, selective cannulation and AE rates (4 meta-analyses). A **distal cap** improves inspection behind the semilunar folds and scope stability.
  - **Balloon enteroscopy-assisted ERCP did not improve outcomes** in Billroth II → consider it after failure with standard scopes. Standard or pediatric colonoscopes or a short double-balloon enteroscope are options, especially with a **long afferent limb**.
  - The papilla is approached caudally; the **biliary orifice lies at 5 o'clock**, making cannulation and sphincterotomy orientation harder → rotatable / Billroth II-dedicated sphincterotomes and bendable cannulas.
  - Standard cannulation fails → rendezvous-assisted cannulation; **percutaneous rendezvous had higher technical success than EUS-guided rendezvous** in this setting.
  - Sphincter ablation options once cannulated — conventional ES, EPSBD, EPLBD, ES + EPLBD — have similar clinical success and AE rates.
- **Other altered anatomy (Statement 21, p. 1041):** Roux-en-Y hepaticojejunostomy, Whipple, pylorus-preserving pancreaticoduodenectomy (target = bilioenteric anastomosis); Roux-en-Y gastrectomy and RYGB (target = papilla). 7 meta-analyses of EA-ERCP (single-balloon, double-balloon, spiral): enteroscopic success **82.9–97.2%**, diagnostic (cannulation) **69.4–95.1%**, therapeutic **61.7–98%**, AEs **4–6.6%**. No basis to prefer one scope; **spiral EA-ERCP inferior to balloon EA-ERCP** in a recent meta-analysis.
- **RYGB (Statements 22–23, p. 1042):** difficulty from a long Roux limb, angulated jejunojejunostomy, frontal/caudal approach to the ampulla, few long accessories. 17 meta-analyses: **LA-ERCP and EDGE success >90%**, but AEs **14–24% (EDGE)** and **13–21% (LA-ERCP)**. EA-ERCP safer but less successful. LA-ERCP needs surgical–endoscopic coordination; EDGE needs specific expertise. Discussion: panel suggests **LA-ERCP or EDGE equally**, by local expertise and availability.
- **Duodenal obstruction (Statement 24, p. 1042):**
  - Gastric outlet obstruction (GOO) occurs in about **15–20%** of pancreatic cancer patients; may precede, accompany or follow biliary obstruction. Consider prognosis, resectability, timing, prior biliary or duodenal stents, and EUS-guided gastroenterostomy (longer patency than duodenal stents).
  - **Type 3:** papilla usually reachable → standard ERCP (watch for duodenal-biliary reflux, even after a duodenal stent).
  - **Type 1:** a duodenal SEMS can be placed and ERCP done through its lumen (technical success **87%** in a retrospective study) — only if the stent's distal end can presumptively sit **above the papilla**; not possible if GOO is treated with EUS-guided gastroenterostomy.
  - **Type 2:** ERCP through a duodenal stent has significantly lower success.
  - EUS-guided options: choledochoduodenostomy, hepaticogastrostomy, antegrade stenting, gallbladder drainage. EUS-guided drainage appears superior to percutaneous for safety and reintervention (mostly retrospective). A network meta-analysis of 5 RCTs found no treatment superior to percutaneous drainage for efficacy and safety, though percutaneous ranked slightly worse on safety. Hepaticogastrostomy or antegrade stenting may beat choledochoduodenostomy for stent dysfunction with concomitant GOO, but no RCTs — superiority of any EUS route not established.

### Table 3 — Future research topics (p. 1043)
- **PEP prophylaxis:** rectal diclofenac vs indomethacin · combined vs single interventions (effect, cost) · intentional PD stenting after biliary cannulation in high-risk patients.
- **Cannulation:** "mixed" vs guidewire-assisted technique · pancreatic guidewire-assisted techniques vs precut after unintentional PD cannulation · repeat ERCP vs percutaneous/EUS rendezvous · precut vs rendezvous.
- **Sphincterotomy/dilation:** PEP and bleeding with pure cut vs mixed current · AEs vs EPSBD duration · single-operator cholangioscopy vs ES + EPLBD for difficult choledocholithiasis · ES before FCSEMS across the papilla without chronic PD obstruction.
- **Special circumstances:** cannulation techniques in PAD · new enteroscopes in altered anatomy · LA-ERCP vs EDGE in RYGB · endoscopic approaches to concomitant GOO and malignant biliary obstruction.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

**Against US sources (US governs the wiki):**
- **Definition of difficult cannulation.** WEO: **>10 min** after papilla visualization, **>5 attempts**, or **≥2** unintended PD cannulations/opacification. [[asge-2026-ercp-adverse-events|ASGE 2026]]: most commonly **>5 papillary engagements, >5 minutes, >1 inadvertent pancreatic cannulation, and/or pancreatic opacification**. The time threshold differs (10 vs 5 min). [[acg-asge-2026-ercp-quality-indicators|ACG/ASGE 2026]] states no time limit for persistence. ASGE 2026 governs.
- **Aggressive hydration — population.** WEO Statement 1 restricts stand-alone hydration to **inpatients when rectal NSAIDs are unavailable** (strong); [[asge-2023-post-ercp-pancreatitis|ASGE 2023]] **suggests** aggressive hydration in **unselected** patients (conditional, moderate). Regimen identical (20 mL/kg, then 3 mL/kg/h × 8 h). ASGE 2023 governs.
- **Wire-guided cannulation — strength.** Same direction; WEO **strong** (Statement 5) vs ASGE 2023 **conditional** (Rec 3).
- **Prophylactic PD stent — when.** WEO (Statement 3, strong): high-risk patients **if PD access has already been achieved**. ASGE 2023: **strong** after repeated/deep PD access or ampullectomy (Rec 4a), and **conditional** in other high-risk groups **as long as PD access can be easily achieved** (Rec 4b) — i.e., ASGE also supports deliberate PD cannulation for stenting. ASGE 2023 governs.
- **PD stent specification.** WEO: **3–5 cm, 5F**, no internal flange, external flange/pigtail, **≤14 days**, X-ray **within 14 days**. ASGE 2023: **3F–5F (preferably 5F), 3–7 cm** (2–3 cm if the wire cannot pass the head), X-ray, **remove in 2–4 weeks if needed**. Length range and retention window differ; ASGE 2023 governs.
- **Rectal NSAID timing.** WEO Statement 2: **30 to 90 min before to immediately after** ERCP; ASGE 2023: **>30 min before or during**. Overlapping, not identical; ASGE 2023 governs.
- **Combination prophylaxis.** WEO Statement 4 (WEO grade: Conditional recommendation, Low quality) suggests NSAIDs + hydration and/or PD stent, citing the Elmunzer RCT (indomethacin alone inferior to indomethacin + stent). ASGE 2023 could not confirm combination benefit (trial then ongoing); [[acg-asge-2026-ercp-quality-indicators|ACG/ASGE 2026]] cites the same multicentre trial as favoring indomethacin + stent. No direct conflict with the newer US document.
- **RYGB access.** WEO suggests **LA-ERCP over EA-ERCP** (Statement 22), EDGE as the alternative (Statement 23), and in its discussion LA-ERCP or EDGE equally. The [[ercp]] page carries an ASGE 2024 position of **EDGE first** (over enteroscopy- or laparoscopy-assisted ERCP), with LA-ERCP preferred when near-term surgery is needed. The US position governs.
- **Billroth II.** WEO: forward-viewing scope with cap or duodenoscope first; balloon enteroscopy only after standard-scope failure. ASGE 2024 (Rec 4, conditional, low) **suggests enteroscopy-assisted ERCP as the initial approach** in patients **with biliary obstruction** after Roux-en-Y hepaticojejunostomy, pancreaticoduodenectomy or Billroth II, with EUS-guided or percutaneous drainage if unsuccessful. The US position governs; WEO adds the technique detail (cap, 5 o'clock orifice, rotatable sphincterotomes).
- **Sphincterotomy current.** No conflict: WEO Statement 12 recommends mixed current (WEO grade: Strong recommendation, High quality); [[aga-2026-electrosurgery|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2026]] notes newer blended settings have not raised PEP and may reduce bleeding, with technique the main PEP driver.

**Internal inconsistencies in the WEO document:**
- **Statement 4:** Table 2 reads "aggressive intravenous hydration **or** PD stenting"; the statement text (p. 1035) reads "**and/or**".
- **Statement 15:** the statement text gives the duration **(>1 to ≤5 min)**; Table 2 omits the numbers.
- **NSAID timing:** Statement 2 says up to **immediately after** ERCP; Figure 2 says "30–90 minutes before / **during** ERCP".
- **Repeat ERCP interval:** Statement 9 says **at least 48 h**; Figure 3 says **after 2–3 days**; the discussion reports 2–6 days in studies, with longer waits individualized.
- **Rendezvous:** Statement 10 (percutaneous) is conditional and Statement 11 (EUS-guided, "alternatively") is a strong recommendation, yet the discussion says the panel suggests the two **equally**.
- **Billroth II (tension, not a contradiction):** EUS-guided rendezvous carries the strong recommendation generally (Statement 11), but after Billroth II the discussion reports higher technical success with percutaneous rendezvous (p. 1041).
- **High-risk PEP criteria** are invoked (Statement 3) but not listed; the document defers to ESGE.
- **Statement 17** carries **high** quality evidence but only a conditional strength (strength is set by the vote, not the evidence).
