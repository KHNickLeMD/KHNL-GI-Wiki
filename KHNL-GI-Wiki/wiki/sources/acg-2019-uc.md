## Summary

The 2019 ACG guideline is the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE)-based reference for adult [[ulcerative-colitis|ulcerative colitis (UC)]], organized as diagnosis/assessment/prognosis, goals of management, induction and maintenance stratified by activity (mild vs moderate-to-severe), the hospitalized patient with acute severe ulcerative colitis (ASUC), and colorectal cancer (CRC) prevention.

**The document carries two separately numbered statement sets and the distinction is load-bearing.** *Recommendations* (Table 2, numbered 1–49) are GRADE-rated: strength is **Strong** ("benefits clearly outweigh the negatives and/or the result of no action") or **Conditional** ("uncertainty remains about the balance of benefits and potential harms"), and quality of evidence is High, Moderate, Low, or Very Low. *Key concept statements* (Table 3, numbered 1–54) are **ungraded** — statements not amenable to the GRADE process because of the structure of the statement or the available evidence, some extrapolated from evidence and/or expert opinion. Key concepts carry no strength or quality rating; the numbering of the two sets runs in parallel and does not interleave.

It set endoscopic healing (Mayo endoscopic subscore 0 or 1) as a treatment target, endorsed fecal calprotectin (FC) as an endoscopy surrogate when endoscopy is not feasible, and codified [[mesalamine-5-asa|5-aminosalicylate (5-ASA)]] strategy by extent: rectal 5-ASA for proctitis, rectal plus oral 5-ASA for left-sided disease, oral ≥2 g/d for extensive disease, and budesonide multi-matrix (MMX) as the next step. For moderate-to-severe disease it recommended [[anti-tnf-agents|anti-tumor necrosis factor (anti-TNF) agents]] (adalimumab, golimumab, infliximab — infliximab in combination with a [[thiopurines|thiopurine]]), [[vedolizumab]], and tofacitinib, with recommendations against thiopurine or [[methotrexate]] monotherapy for induction.

ASUC management is detailed: deep vein thrombosis (DVT) prophylaxis, [[clostridioides-difficile|*Clostridioides difficile*]] testing with vancomycin if positive, intravenous corticosteroids (IVCS), and rescue with infliximab or cyclosporine at day 3–5. Surveillance recommendations favor dye spray chromoendoscopy on standard-definition scopes and either narrow-band imaging or chromoendoscopy on high-definition scopes.

## Key Findings / Claims

### GRADE Recommendations (Table 2)

All 49, with the document's own strength and quality-of-evidence labels. Numbering is the document's.

**Diagnosis, assessment, and prognosis**

1. Stool testing to rule out *C. difficile* in patients suspected of having UC — **Strong, Very Low**
2. Against serologic antibody testing to establish or rule out a diagnosis of UC — **Strong, Very Low**
3. Against serologic antibody testing to determine the prognosis of UC — **Strong, Very Low**

**Goals for managing patients with UC**

4. Suggest treating to achieve mucosal healing (resolution of inflammatory changes; Mayo endoscopic subscore 0 or 1) to increase the likelihood of sustained steroid-free remission and prevent hospitalizations and surgery — **Conditional, Low**
5. Suggest FC as a surrogate for endoscopy when endoscopy is not feasible or available, to assess for mucosal healing — **Conditional, Very Low**

**Induction of remission — mildly active UC**

6. Mildly active ulcerative proctitis: rectal 5-ASA **1 g/d** for induction — **Strong, High**
7. Mildly active left-sided colitis: rectal 5-ASA enemas **≥1 g/d**, preferred over rectal steroids, for induction — **Strong, Moderate**
8. Mildly active left-sided UC: suggest rectal 5-ASA enemas **≥1 g/d** *combined with* oral 5-ASA **≥2 g/d** over oral 5-ASA alone — **Conditional, Low**
9. Mildly active left-sided UC intolerant of or nonresponsive to oral *and* rectal 5-ASA at appropriate doses (oral ≥2 g/d and rectal ≥1 g/d): oral budesonide MMX **9 mg/d** for induction — **Strong, Moderate**
10. Mildly active extensive colitis: oral 5-ASA **≥2 g/d** to induce remission — **Strong, Moderate**
11. UC of any extent failing to respond to 5-ASA: oral systemic [[corticosteroids-ibd|corticosteroids]] to induce remission — **Strong, Low**
12. Mildly active UC not reaching remission on appropriately dosed 5-ASA (≥2 g/d oral and/or ≥1 g/d rectal): suggest **against switching to an alternate 5-ASA formulation**; consider alternative therapeutic classes — **Conditional, Low**
13. Mildly active UC of any extent: suggest **low dose (2–2.4 g/d)** 5-ASA over **higher dose (4.8 g/d)** — no difference in remission rate — **Conditional, Very Low**
14. Mildly to moderately active UC not responding to oral 5-ASA: **add** budesonide MMX 9 mg/d to induce remission — **Strong, Moderate**
15. Mildly to moderately active UC of any extent using 5-ASA for induction: once-daily *or* more frequent oral 5-ASA by patient preference to optimize adherence — efficacy and safety no different — **Strong, Moderate**

**Maintenance of remission — previously mildly active UC**

16. Mildly active ulcerative proctitis: rectal 5-ASA **1 g/d** to maintain remission — **Strong, Moderate**
17. Mildly active left-sided or extensive UC: oral 5-ASA **≥2 g/d** for maintenance — **Strong, Moderate**
18. Against systemic corticosteroids for maintenance of remission in UC — **Strong, Moderate**

**Induction of remission — moderately to severely active UC**

19. Moderately active UC: oral budesonide MMX for induction — **Strong, Moderate**
20. Moderately to severely active UC of any extent: oral systemic corticosteroids to induce remission — **Strong, Moderate**
21. Moderately to severely active UC: **against monotherapy** with thiopurines or methotrexate for induction — **Strong, Low**
22. Moderately to severely active UC: anti-TNF therapy using **adalimumab, golimumab, or infliximab** for induction — **Strong, High**
23. Moderately to severely active UC who failed 5-ASA and in whom anti-TNF is used for induction: suggest **against** continuing 5-ASA for added clinical efficacy — **Conditional, Low**
24. When **infliximab** is used for induction: **combination therapy with a thiopurine** — **Strong, Moderate (for azathioprine)**
25. Moderately to severely active UC: vedolizumab for induction — **Strong, Moderate**
26. Moderately to severely active UC **previously failed anti-TNF**: vedolizumab for induction — **Strong, Moderate**
27. Moderately to severely active UC: [[jak-inhibitors|tofacitinib]] **10 mg orally twice daily (b.i.d.) for 8 wk** to induce remission — **Strong, Moderate**
28. Moderately to severely active UC **previously failed anti-TNF**: tofacitinib for induction — **Strong, Moderate**
29. Anti-TNF responders now losing response: suggest measuring **serum drug levels and antibodies** (antibodies if there is not a therapeutic level) to assess the reason for loss of response — **Conditional, Very Low**

**Maintenance of remission — previously moderately to severely active UC**

30. Achieved remission but previously failed 5-ASA and now on anti-TNF: against concomitant 5-ASA for efficacy of maintenance — **Conditional, Low**
31. Against systemic corticosteroids for maintenance of remission in UC — **Strong, Moderate**
32. In remission after corticosteroid induction: suggest thiopurines for maintenance vs no treatment or corticosteroids — **Conditional, Low**
33. Previously moderately to severely active UC now in remission: against methotrexate for maintenance — **Conditional, Low**
34. **Continue** anti-TNF (adalimumab, golimumab, infliximab) to maintain remission after anti-TNF induction — **Strong, Moderate**
35. **Continue** vedolizumab to maintain remission after vedolizumab induction — **Strong, Moderate**
36. **Continue** tofacitinib for maintenance after tofacitinib induction — **Strong, Moderate**

**Hospitalized patient with ASUC**

37. ASUC: DVT prophylaxis to prevent venous thromboembolism (VTE) — **Strong, Low**
38. ASUC: test for *C. difficile* infection (CDI) — **Strong, Moderate**
39. ASUC with concomitant CDI: treat CDI with **vancomycin instead of metronidazole** — **Strong, Low**
40. Against **routine** use of broad-spectrum antibiotics in the management of ASUC — **Strong, Low**
41. Suggest **against total parenteral nutrition for the purpose of bowel rest** in ASUC — **Conditional, Very Low**
42. ASUC: **a total of 60 mg/d of methylprednisolone, or hydrocortisone 100 mg 3 or 4 times per day**, to induce remission — **Strong, Low**
43. ASUC failing to adequately respond to IVCS **by 3–5 days**: medical rescue therapy with **infliximab or cyclosporine** — **Strong, Moderate**
44. ASUC achieving remission with infliximab: maintain remission with **the same agent** — **Strong, Moderate**
45. ASUC achieving remission with cyclosporine: suggest maintenance with **thiopurines** — **Conditional, Low**
46. ASUC achieving remission with cyclosporine: suggest maintenance with **vedolizumab** — **Conditional, Very Low**

**Colorectal cancer prevention**

47. Suggest colonoscopic screening and surveillance to identify neoplasia in UC of **any extent beyond the rectum** — **Conditional, Very Low**
48. With **standard-definition** colonoscopes: dye spray chromoendoscopy with **methylene blue or indigo carmine** to identify dysplasia — **Strong, Low**
49. With **high-definition** colonoscopes: suggest **white-light endoscopy with narrow-band imaging** *or* dye spray chromoendoscopy with methylene blue or indigo carmine — **Conditional, Low**

### Key Concept Statements (Table 3)

All 54, condensed. **Ungraded** — none of these carries a GRADE strength or quality rating. Numbering is the document's and is independent of the recommendation numbering above.

**Diagnosis, assessment, and prognosis**

1. Suspect UC in patients with hematochezia and urgency.
2. Exclude infectious etiologies at the time of diagnosis.
3. [[colonoscopy|Colonoscopy]] with intubation of the ileum plus biopsies of **affected and unaffected** areas to confirm the diagnosis, read by a pathologist with gastrointestinal pathology expertise when possible.
4. Disease extent categories: (i) **proctitis** — within **18 cm of the anal verge**, distal to the rectosigmoid junction; (ii) **left-sided colitis** — extending from the sigmoid to the splenic flexure; (iii) **extensive colitis** — beyond the splenic flexure.
5. If the terminal ileum is normal, further evaluation of stomach and small bowel by [[upper-endoscopy|upper endoscopy]] and cross-sectional imaging is **not** needed unless other symptoms/findings suggest proximal gastrointestinal involvement or [[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease (CD)]] rather than UC.
6. Severity definitions are needed to guide treatment and should be based on (i) patient-reported outcomes (PROs: bleeding, normalization of bowel habits), (ii) inflammatory burden (endoscopic extent and severity, markers of inflammation), (iii) disease course (hospitalization, steroid need, failure to respond to medications), and (iv) disease impact (functionality, quality of life [QoL]).
7. FC can be used as a noninvasive marker of disease activity and to assess response to therapy and relapse.

**Goals for managing patients with UC**

8. UC is chronic; therapy is required to induce *and* maintain remission. Categorize decisions as (i) induction and (ii) maintenance, with the goal of obtaining and maintaining **steroid-free remission**.
9. Management strategies should reflect patient's and provider's goals and recognize the chronic nature of the disease.
10. Corticosteroid-free remission may be defined by symptoms, endoscopic findings, or disease impact without ongoing corticosteroid use. **Symptomatic remission** = improvement in PROs; **endoscopic healing** = restoration of intact mucosa without friability; **deep remission** = both, and is the preferred goal of management.
11. Select induction and maintenance therapy by **disease extent, severity, and prognosis**.
12. Initial treatment focuses on restoring normal bowel frequency and controlling bleeding and urgency. An endoscopically healed mucosa is associated with sustained remission and reduced colectomy risk.
13. Histological healing is associated with some improved clinical outcomes but has **not yet been prospectively validated** as an end point of treatment.
14. Control of mucosal inflammation may reduce dysplasia risk.
15. Monitor for disease-related and drug-related complications; incorporate [[ibd-preventive-care|preventive strategies]] as outlined in a separate ACG guideline.
16. Routine visits are recommended to monitor for relapse and address health maintenance needs.
17. Screen for coexistent anxiety and depressive disorders and provide resources when identified.

**Induction of remission — mildly active UC**

18. Mildly active UC with **a number of poor prognostic factors** (increased risk of hospitalization or surgery; Table 8) should be treated with **moderate-to-severe** therapies. Each factor carries a **different weight** and must be discussed in shared decision-making — age alone is a weaker factor than severe endoscopic activity, but **young age combined with another factor** may be sufficient criteria to treat per the moderate-to-severe protocol.
19. Reassess mildly active UC for response to induction therapy **within 6 weeks**.
20. Fecal microbiota transplantation ([[fmt|FMT]]) requires more study and clarification of treatment before use as a therapy for UC.
21. Complementary therapies such as [[probiotics]] and curcumin require further study with adequate power and clarification of end points.

**Induction of remission — moderately to severely active UC**

22. Management of the nonhospitalized patient with moderately to severely active UC is similar throughout, except where data exist specifically for **moderately** active UC:
    - a. 5-ASA **could be used as monotherapy** for induction of **moderately but not severely** active UC.
    - b. In moderately active UC, consider **nonsystemic** corticosteroids such as budesonide MMX before systemic therapy.
    - c. In **severely** active UC, consider **systemic** corticosteroids rather than topical corticosteroids.
23. Robust data on combination anti-TNF + immunomodulator therapy in moderately to severely active UC exist **only for infliximab and thiopurines**.
24. **Primary nonresponders** to an anti-TNF (lack of therapeutic benefit after induction **despite adequate drug levels**) should be evaluated and considered for an **alternative mechanism of action** rather than cycling to another drug within the anti-TNF class.
25. **Initial response then loss of efficacy** to one anti-TNF: alternative anti-TNF therapy (**but not the biosimilar to the original brand**) compared with no treatment for induction of remission.
26. Nonresponse or loss of response should be assessed with [[therapeutic-drug-monitoring-ibd|therapeutic drug monitoring]] to identify the reason and whether to optimize the existing therapy or select an alternate.
27. Obtain surgical consultation and consider colectomy in moderately to severely active UC that is refractory to or intolerant of medical therapy.

**Maintenance of remission — previously moderately to severely active UC**

28. 5-ASA maintenance is **likely not as effective** in previously **severely** active UC as in previously **moderately** active UC.
29. Budesonide MMX **has not been studied** for maintenance of remission of previously moderately to severely active UC.
30. Most clinical trials and available data demonstrate benefit from using **the steroid-sparing therapy that induced remission** to maintain it.
31. There is **insufficient evidence** to support **proactive** therapeutic drug monitoring in all unselected patients with UC in remission.
32. Suggest **elective proctocolectomy** in patients with UC failing maximal medical management.

**Hospitalized patient with ASUC**

33. All patients admitted with ASUC should have stool testing to rule out CDI.
34. All patients with ASUC should undergo **flexible sigmoidoscopy within 72 hr, preferably within 24 hr** of admission — to assess endoscopic severity and obtain biopsies to evaluate for cytomegalovirus (CMV) colitis.
35. Assess for [[toxic-megacolon|toxic megacolon]] **on a regular basis** during the admission.
36. Monitor response using stool frequency, rectal bleeding, physical examination, vital signs, and **serial C-reactive protein (CRP)**.
37. **Avoid** nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), opioids, and medications with anticholinergic side effects in ASUC.
38. Failing to adequately respond to medical therapy **by 3–5 days**, or suspected toxicity → obtain **surgical consultation**.
39. Choice between infliximab and cyclosporine should be based on **provider experience with the agent, history of previous immunomodulator or anti-TNF failure, and serum albumin**.
40. Toxic megacolon, colonic perforation, severe refractory hemorrhage, and refractoriness to medical therapy are **indications for surgery** in ASUC.
41. Infliximab and cyclosporine do **not** increase postoperative complications of colectomy; **surgery should not be deferred** based on this exposure.

**Colorectal cancer prevention**

42. Screening and subsequent surveillance colonoscopy for dysplasia in UC of **extent greater than the rectum** should start **8 years after diagnosis**.
43. UC with [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis|primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)]]: screening colonoscopy **at the time of UC diagnosis**, then **annually**.
44. [[colonoscopy-surveillance|Surveillance colonoscopy]] at **1- to 3-year intervals**, the specific interval based on **combined CRC risk factors in UC and findings from previous examinations**.
45. At colonoscopy, identify raised lesions and abnormal pit patterns and perform **targeted biopsies**. Remove endoscopically discrete lesions, clearly **labeling and separating** distinct lesions and segments of the colorectum.
46. **Most neoplasia in UC is visible** with standard- or high-definition white-light examination.
47. It is **unclear** whether segmental random biopsies are still required during surveillance colonoscopy in UC.
48. Pathologic interpretation of UC-associated neoplasia should be done by a pathologist experienced in gastrointestinal pathology, and neoplastic findings **reviewed by a second experienced pathologist**.
49. Dysplasia of **any grade** that is **discrete and completely removed** → proctocolectomy may not be necessary; if surgery is not performed, subsequent surveillance colonoscopy should initially be at **shortened intervals**.
50. Dysplasia that is **not resectable or is multifocal** → refer for **proctocolectomy**.
51. Patients with **extensive inflammatory polyps** may not be able to have adequate surveillance; inform them of this and that more frequent surveillance or surgery may be required.
52. **No medical therapy** has demonstrated sufficient prevention of dysplasia or CRC to avoid colonoscopic surveillance in UC.
53. Patients with UC-associated dysplasia under ongoing active surveillance **may benefit** from augmented visualization by dye spray chromoendoscopy at their **first examination after** dysplasia was detected.
54. **Fecal DNA testing and computed tomography (CT) colonography are not recommended** for screening or surveillance of UC-associated neoplasia because of insufficient evidence.

- Extent follows the Montreal system: proctitis (E1), left-sided (E2), extensive (E3, proximal to the splenic flexure).

### Operative Detail Behind the Recommendations

**ASUC — definition and day-3 assessment**

- **ASUC** = **≥6 bowel movements daily** plus **≥1 systemic sign of toxicity**: tachycardia, fever, anemia (**hemoglobin <10.5 g/dL**), or elevated inflammatory markers (**ESR >30 mm/hr**). In children, Pediatric Ulcerative Colitis Activity Index (PUCAI) **≥65**.
- **Oxford index** at day 3 of IVCS: **>8 bowel movements**, *or* **3–8 bowel movements with CRP >45 mg/L** → colectomy in **85%**. Partial response **40%**, complete response **5%**.
- Children: **PUCAI >45 at day 3** or **>70 at day 5** predicted IVCS failure. Other failure predictors: hypoalbuminemia, colonic dilation, **ESR >75 mm/hr**, **temperature >38 °C**.
- **UCEIS ≥5** → 50% rescue therapy, 33% colectomy (vs 27% and 9% at UCEIS ≤4). **UCEIS ≥7** outperformed Mayo endoscopic subscore 3 for predicting colectomy.
- Plain film: thickened wall, loss of haustration, mucosal islands; **transverse colon diameter >5.5 cm** predicts worse outcome. CT only for suspected extraluminal complication/perforation or unclear CD vs UC.
- Toxic megacolon occurs in **<5%** of ASUC. Absolute indications for surgery in ASUC: toxic megacolon, perforation, uncontrolled severe hematochezia, multiorgan dysfunction. Preferred operation: **subtotal or total colectomy with end ileostomy**.
- VTE prophylaxis = **low-molecular-weight heparin**, apparently safe despite active bleeding.
- IVCS response usually by **3–5 days**; further response after **7 days** unlikely, so prolonging IVCS beyond that without rescue cannot be recommended. No benefit above **60 mg methylprednisolone**; single, divided, or continuous dosing equivalent.

**Dosing the source gives**

- **Cyclosporine** ASUC target **2 mg/kg** (comparable response and colectomy rate to 4 mg/kg, fewer adverse events); drug levels **200–400** — ACG 2019 gives the range without units. Infliximab **may be preferred** instead in patients who previously failed immunomodulator therapy and in those with **albumin <23 g/L**; low serum cholesterol or magnesium raises the risk of neurologic adverse events from cyclosporine.
- **Infliximab** rescue **5 mg/kg** at weeks **0, 2, 6**.
- Combination induction: infliximab **5 mg/kg** (0, 2, 6 wk) + **azathioprine 2.5 mg/kg orally** — superior to either alone at 16 wk in anti-TNF- and immunomodulator-naive patients with normal thiopurine methyltransferase activity.
- **Vedolizumab** maintenance **300 mg intravenously every 8 weeks**; **tofacitinib** maintenance **5 or 10 mg b.i.d.**; **methotrexate** parenteral **25 mg/wk** (not superior to placebo for maintenance).
- **[[tacrolimus|Tacrolimus]]** optimal target trough **10–15 ng/mL** in steroid-refractory UC; long-term outcome and colectomy data are limited. ACG 2019 discusses tacrolimus in the text but makes no recommendation or key concept statement on it.
- **CMV colitis** in up to one-third of steroid-refractory ASUC; biopsy the **ulcer base**; immunohistochemistry, rapid culture, or polymerase chain reaction preferred over hematoxylin-eosin. **Ganciclovir, intravenous then oral, 14-day course** — response rate around **70%**; oral **valganciclovir** may be appropriate in selected patients. Do not defer colectomy to finish the course in nonresponders.

**CRC, surgery, biomarkers**

- Cumulative CRC risk **1%, 2%, 5%** at **10, 20, >20 years**. Risk factors: longer duration, greater inflammatory activity, younger age at diagnosis, greater extent, PSC, first-degree relative with CRC. Inflammatory pseudopolyps may **not** be a risk factor on newer data.
- Colectomy indications: ASUC; chronic refractory UC; dysplasia/carcinoma. **Chronic refractory UC** = refractory to induction with biologics, corticosteroids, or small molecules, *or* corticosteroid dependent.
- Elective operation of choice for refractory UC: **restorative proctocolectomy with ileal pouch–anal anastomosis (IPAA)**, as a **2- or 3-stage** procedure — the pouch is not built at the first stage in a patient still on corticosteroids or other medical therapy, which minimizes complications and operating time. Stapled anastomosis gives better functional outcomes than handsewn with mucosectomy; reserve mucosectomy for neoplasia involving the rectum or higher recurrence risk.
- **The term DALM (dysplasia-associated lesion or mass) should no longer be used** — describe size, shape, and pit pattern instead. "Invisible" dysplasia = found on nontargeted biopsies. Colitis-associated lesions are more often flat or spreading than sessile or pedunculated. Bowel-sparing segmental or subtotal resection is an option in selected patients (deep remission, poor IPAA candidates, proximal dysplasia not involving the rectum).
- Postoperative mortality **1%**; early complications (≤30 d) 9%–65%, late (>30 d) 17%–55%. Preoperative anti-TNF raised infectious complications in CD but **not** UC.
- [[nutrition-in-ibd|Malnutrition]] definitions used: weight loss **>10%–15%** over 6 months, body mass index **<18.5 kg/m²**, albumin **<30 g/L**.
- FC vs endoscopic inflammation: sensitivity **87.3%**, specificity **77.1%**, area under the curve **0.91**; best sensitivity **90%** at **50 μg/g**, best specificity **78.2%** above **100 μg/g**. FC cutoffs for remission and the optimal target have not been studied prospectively and so are not amenable to the GRADE process.
- Perinuclear antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies occur in up to **70%** of UC, but pooled sensitivity is low — the basis of recommendations 2 and 3.

### Explicit Non-Recommendations

Positions stated as *unable to recommend* — neither for nor against:

- **Adalimumab, golimumab, vedolizumab, tofacitinib as ASUC rescue** — no data; cannot currently be recommended in this setting.
- **Accelerated or high-dose infliximab induction in ASUC** — may suit a subgroup; routine use cannot be recommended on existing evidence.
- **Routine medical salvage after infliximab or cyclosporine failure** — cannot be recommended; significant risk of adverse outcomes.
- **Histologic healing as a management end point** — not recommended at this time.
- **Probiotic VSL#3 as an adjunct to 5-ASA** — quality of evidence too low to recommend for or against in UC.
- ***Escherichia coli* Nissle for induction** — insufficient evidence to recommend for or against.
- **FMT** — variability in donors, delivery, duration, and end points makes the trials hard to interpret; not currently a recommended treatment option for UC.
- **Routine disability/QoL scores in practice**, and **genetic or serologic markers to predict severity and course** — insufficient data.
- Key concept 47 leaves **segmental random biopsies during surveillance** explicitly unresolved.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **Superseded by [[acg-2025-uc]]** for drug positioning. Agents licensed for UC after 2019 are absent: ustekinumab, ozanimod, etrasimod, upadacitinib, mirikizumab, risankizumab, guselkumab. The 2025 edition also prefers vedolizumab over adalimumab and adds IUS as a monitoring tool — neither position exists here.
- **Only tofacitinib** is named among Janus kinase inhibitors; later class safety restrictions are not addressed.
- Both editions define proctitis identically in their key concepts (within 18 cm of the anal verge, distal to the rectosigmoid junction); the ≤15 cm figure in the 2025 body text has no counterpart here.
- **Recommendation 30 reads "we recommend against … (conditional recommendation, low quality of evidence)"** — the document pairs *recommend* with *Conditional*. Reproduced as printed; the same pairing appears in recommendation 33.
- **Recommendations 18 and 31 are the same statement** (against systemic corticosteroids for maintenance), printed once in each maintenance section. Both are in the document.
- [[aga-2024-uc-pharm]] and [[aga-2023-biomarkers-uc]] post-date this edition and carry the current positions on sequencing and biomarker-based monitoring.
