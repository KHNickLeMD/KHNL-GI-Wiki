## Summary
AGA Expert Commentary reviewing the current evidence on blood-based tests for CRC screening and their implications, leveraging decision-analytic modeling to identify the test performance, interval, and uptake needed for blood tests to approach the effectiveness of stool tests and colonoscopy. As an Expert Commentary it has no numbered Best Practice Advice; the points below are its key clinical claims.

Two blood tests have population-based clinical-validation data. **Guardant Shield** (cell-free DNA, methylation) — validated in 22,877 adults aged 45–84; CRC sensitivity 83.1%, specificity 89.6%, advanced-precursor-lesion (APL) sensitivity 13.2%; stage 1 CRC sensitivity only 55%. It is FDA-approved (May 2024) and CMS-covered, commercially available. **Freenome** (cell-free DNA methylation) — validated in 48,995 adults aged 45–85; CRC sensitivity 79.2%, specificity 91.5%, APL sensitivity 12.5%; stage 1 sensitivity ~57%; expected to seek FDA/CMS approval. Both detect APLs and early-stage CRC poorly compared with stool tests, limiting cancer prevention and curative-stage detection.

Decision-analytic modeling (4 validated models) projects that triennial Guardant Shield yields substantially smaller reductions in CRC incidence (24%–47%) and mortality (53%–59%) than annual FIT (45%–77% / 67%–81%), triennial Cologuard, or 10-yearly colonoscopy. At its $1450 list price triennial Shield is cost-effective vs no screening ($74,800–$102,300/QALY) but is less effective and more costly than FIT or colonoscopy; even at $300 it would remain inferior to FIT/colonoscopy. For triennial blood-based screening to approximate annual FIT, its APL sensitivity would need to rise to ~50%–80% and cost fall to <$125–$140.

The main attraction is potential improved adherence (only 60%–65% of eligible U.S. adults are up to date; offering a blood test to those who decline other screening increased uptake by 17.5% in one study). But population outcomes are inferior if blood tests *substitute* for FIT/colonoscopy: to match FIT/colonoscopy, participation would need to be ≥35% higher; outcomes are similar only if for every 3 people substituting a blood test there are 2 new screenees. Blood testing requires a *programmatic* approach: abnormal tests must be followed by colonoscopy (almost 50% of patients fail to complete timely colonoscopy within 3–6 months of a positive stool test), and normal tests need repeat testing at appropriate intervals. Multicancer detection tests (e.g., Galleri) remain investigational. The bottom line: novel blood tests may improve participation but could cause net harm if substituted for more effective tests; for patients who decline all other screening, they are an acceptable option.

## Key Findings / Claims
- **Guardant Shield (Guardant):** cell-free DNA methylation blood test. Validation: 22,877 adults, ages 45–84, blood draw ≤60 days before screening colonoscopy; clinical-validation cohort 7861 (65 CRC, 1116 APLs). CRC sensitivity 83.1%, specificity 89.6%, APL sensitivity 13.2%; stage 1 CRC sensitivity 55%. FDA-approved May 2024, CMS-covered, commercially available. List price $1450.
- **Freenome:** cell-free DNA methylation blood test. Validation: 48,995 adults, ages 45–85, blood draw ≤120 days before screening colonoscopy; clinical-validation cohort 27,010 (72 CRC, 2567 APLs). CRC sensitivity 79.2%, specificity 91.5%, APL sensitivity 12.5%; stage 1 sensitivity 57.1%. Expected to seek FDA/CMS approval.
- **APL = advanced precancerous lesion:** adenoma or sessile serrated lesion ≥10 mm, villous histology, high-grade dysplasia, or carcinoma in situ.
- **Both blood tests:** lower sensitivity for stage 1 CRC and for APLs than stool tests → reduced potential for cancer prevention (poor APL detection) and curative treatment (suboptimal early-stage detection). Performance figures are one-time test performances; optimal repeat interval unknown.
- **CMS coverage:** acceptable CRC-screening blood tests covered **every 3 years**; CMS minimum performance threshold is CRC sensitivity 74% and specificity 90% (implies ~10% APL positivity rate).
- **Modeling — reductions in CRC incidence / mortality vs no screening (4 validated models):**
  - Annual FIT: 45%–77% / 67%–81%
  - Triennial MT-sDNA (Cologuard): 40%–73% / 64%–78%
  - Colonoscopy q10y: 57%–81% / 72%–88%
  - Triennial Guardant Shield: 24%–47% / 53%–59% (Freenome similar to Shield)
- **Cost-effectiveness:** Triennial Shield at $1450 = cost-effective vs no screening ($74,800–$102,300/QALY) but less effective and more costly than FIT/colonoscopy. At $300 it could be cost-saving vs no screening but still inferior to FIT/colonoscopy. Shorter intervals (biennial/annual) improve benefit but costs rise faster than benefits.
- **Threshold to match annual FIT:** triennial blood test would need APL sensitivity ~50%–80% AND cost <$125–$140.
- **Substitution vs supplementation:** If blood tests substitute for currently recommended stool tests/colonoscopy, CRC outcomes are inferior. To equal FIT/colonoscopy, participation must be ≥35% higher; population outcomes similar only if for every 3 substituters there are 2 net-new screenees.
- **Adherence:** Only 60%–65% of eligible U.S. adults up to date. In a study of patients who declined other screening, offering a blood test increased CRC screening by 17.5% vs usual care; an RCT showed adherence up to 11% higher for blood vs stool testing.
- **Programmatic requirement:** Abnormal blood test → must complete colonoscopy; normal → repeat at appropriate interval. ~50% of patients do not complete colonoscopy within 3–6 months of a positive stool test; delays raise CRC incidence/mortality. Whether those who decline screening colonoscopy will complete it after a positive blood test is unknown.
- **Multicancer detection tests (MCDs):** Galleri (Grail) — overall cancer sensitivity 51.5%, specificity 99.5%; large NHS validation underway. Investigational; confirmatory imaging imperfect, costs/harms substantial, mortality benefit unproven. Premature for clinical CRC screening.
- **Interval blood tests between screening colonoscopies:** premature to consider.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **Positioning of blood tests vs the existing "Not Recommended — Septin-9" note in [[colorectal-cancer-screening]]:** ACG 2021 recommended against the older Septin-9 blood test; this 2025 CPU addresses newer cfDNA tests (Shield FDA/CMS-approved) without endorsing them as equivalent to FIT/colonoscopy — it positions them as acceptable mainly for patients who would otherwise decline all screening. Not a direct contradiction; the newer test class is distinct.
- **USPSTF status:** As of this CPU, blood tests were not yet folded into USPSTF recommendations the way stool tests/colonoscopy are; the table in the screening page lists endorsement status.
- **Unknowns flagged by the source:** optimal repeat interval, real-world uptake, whether improved participation compensates for lower efficacy, and downstream colonoscopy completion after a positive blood test.
