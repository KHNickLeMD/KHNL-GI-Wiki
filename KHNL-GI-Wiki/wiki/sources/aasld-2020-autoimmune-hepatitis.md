## Summary

AASLD document on [[autoimmune-hepatitis|autoimmune hepatitis (AIH)]] in adults and children, in two parts:

- **Guideline recommendations (5)** — from Mayo Clinic Evidence-Based Practice Center systematic reviews/meta-analyses, rated by Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) as strong or conditional. Topics: first-line glucocorticoid + azathioprine (AZA) therapy, second-line drugs, glucocorticoids after liver transplantation (LT).
- **Guidance statements** — expert-panel consensus; quality of evidence and strength **not formally rated** (p. 673).

Diagnosis rests on compatible histology plus elevated aminotransferases, elevated immunoglobulin G (IgG) and/or autoantibodies, after excluding other liver diseases; there is no signature diagnostic marker. Two serologic types: type 1 (antinuclear antibody [ANA] and/or smooth muscle antibody [SMA]) and type 2 (anti–liver kidney microsome type 1 [anti-LKM1]). Long-term outcome of treated AIH: 10- and 20-year survival 91% and 70%.

First line: budesonide + AZA or prednisone/prednisolone + AZA (no cirrhosis, not acute severe); no budesonide in cirrhosis or acute severe AIH. Second line: mycophenolate mofetil (MMF) or tacrolimus (TAC), MMF tried first. Drug withdrawal is possible in a minority after ≥2 years of normal aminotransferases and IgG. Also covers acute severe AIH and AIH with [[acute-liver-failure|acute liver failure (ALF)]], drug-induced AIH-like injury, overlap with [[primary-biliary-cholangitis|primary biliary cholangitis (PBC)]] and [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis|primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)]], pregnancy, pretreatment evaluation, cancer surveillance, and recurrent/de novo AIH after LT.

**What's new since 2010** (p. 671): scoring systems only to support judgment in challenging cases and to define study cohorts; elastography for fibrosis; thiopurine methyltransferase (TPMT) testing before AZA encouraged in all; budesonide + AZA or predniso(lo)ne + AZA first line (no cirrhosis, acute severe hepatitis or ALF); AZA can continue in pregnancy, MMF contraindicated; liver tissue exam before withdrawal (≥2 years biochemical remission) preferred but not mandatory in adults and **required in children**; MMF or TAC second line; acute severe AIH → predniso(lo)ne, then LT if no improvement within 2 weeks; ALF → direct LT evaluation; glucocorticoids can be discontinued after LT with monitoring for recurrence.

## Guideline Recommendations (GRADE)

Numbered 1–5 by the document (pp. 692, 697, 701).

| # | Recommendation | Strength | Certainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Children and adults with AIH **without cirrhosis or acute severe AIH**: AASLD suggests budesonide + AZA **or** prednisone/prednisolone + AZA as first-line treatment (supporting trial regimen: budesonide 3 mg three times daily [TID] + AZA 1–2 mg/kg/day) | Conditional | Low |
| 2 | Children and adults with AIH **with cirrhosis or acute severe AIH**: AASLD suggests budesonide **not** be used (portosystemic shunting may reduce efficacy and let budesonide bypass the liver) | Conditional | Very low |
| 3 | Children or adults with treatment failure, incomplete response, or drug intolerance to first-line agents: AASLD suggests MMF or [[tacrolimus\|TAC]] to achieve and maintain biochemical remission | Conditional | Low |
| 4 | AASLD suggests a trial of MMF over TAC as the initial second-line agent (superior ease of use and side-effect profile) | Conditional | Very low |
| 5 | Limited data support long-term glucocorticoids after LT to prevent rejection, graft loss, recurrent AIH, or reduced survival in adults → AASLD suggests that a **gradual withdrawal of glucocorticoids be considered** after LT | Conditional | Very low |

## Guidance Statements (ungraded)

Consensus statements; the document does not rate or number them — the **G-numbers are the wiki's**. Page = journal page.

### Diagnosis (pp. 680–681)

- **G1** Diagnosis requires compatible **histology**, further supported by: elevated serum aminotransferases; elevated serum IgG and/or positive autoantibody(ies); **exclusion** of viral, hereditary, metabolic, cholestatic and drug-induced diseases that may resemble AIH.
- **G2** Initial serology: **ANA + SMA in adults; ANA + SMA + anti-LKM1 in children**; consider further autoantibody tests if needed to secure the diagnosis.
- **G3** Diagnostically challenging cases → review by, or referral to, an experienced liver center **before starting therapy**.
- **G4** Consider AIH in **all** patients with acute or chronic liver disease — including asymptomatic liver test abnormalities, ALF, and autoantibody-negative hepatitis.

### Concurrent Immune Diseases (p. 682)

- **G5** Screen for **celiac and thyroid disease at diagnosis** (no specific tests named).
- **G6** Assess for rheumatoid arthritis, [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]], autoimmune hemolytic anemia, diabetes and other extrahepatic autoimmune diseases **based on symptoms and provider concern**.

### Overlap Syndromes (pp. 682–683, 699)

- **G7** AIH + cholestatic laboratory/histologic findings consistent with PBC + **positive antimitochondrial antibody (AMA)** → consider AIH–PBC overlap.
- **G8** AIH + cholestatic laboratory findings + histologic bile duct injury or loss + concurrent chronic [[ulcerative-colitis|ulcerative colitis (UC)]] → evaluate for **large-duct PSC by cholangiography** (AIH–PSC overlap).
- **G9** The Paris criteria can aid the AIH–PBC diagnosis but may exclude patients with less severe cholestatic features.
- **G10** **Neither the revised nor the simplified International Autoimmune Hepatitis Group (IAIHG) score** should be used to assess overlap syndromes.
- **G11** **Consider adding [[ursodeoxycholic-acid|ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA)]]** to prednisone or prednisolone + AZA in adults and children with AIH and overlap syndromes.

### Drug-Induced AIH-Like Injury (p. 684)

- **G12** Always consider drug-induced AIH-like liver injury in the differential diagnosis of AIH.
- **G13** **Withdraw the offending agent**; monitor until laboratory resolution.
- **G14** Glucocorticoids for drug-induced AIH-like injury if symptoms or disease activity are **severe (e.g., Hy's law met)**, or if symptoms and laboratory tests fail to improve or worsen after stopping the drug.
- **G15** A laboratory flare **after glucocorticoid withdrawal** suggests underlying AIH and the need for immunosuppressive therapy.

### Noninvasive Fibrosis Assessment (p. 685)

- **G16** Serum biomarker panels for fibrosis are unestablished in AIH — **should not be used**.
- **G17** Vibration-controlled transient elastography (VCTE) can identify advanced fibrosis or cirrhosis with reasonable accuracy, but **defer it ≥6 months after successful treatment** (inflammation confounds stiffness).

### Pretreatment Evaluation (pp. 686–688)

- **G18** Consider screening for **absent or near-absent TPMT activity** before starting AZA.
- **G19** Vaccinate all susceptible patients per **age-specific Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) guidelines**.
- **G20** Patients unprotected against hepatitis A virus (HAV) and hepatitis B virus (HBV) → vaccinate, **preferably before immunosuppressive therapy**.
- **G21** Hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg)-negative / anti–hepatitis B core (anti-HBc)-positive at diagnosis → **periodic HBsAg and HBV DNA** during prednisone/prednisolone + AZA, to detect reactivation and the need for on-demand antiviral therapy.
- **G22** Prior HBV infection + **high-dose glucocorticoids or other immune modulators, especially B cell–depleting agents** → moderate reactivation risk; consider **preemptive antiviral therapy**.
- **G23** **Bone densitometry at baseline in adults with AIH who have risk factors for osteoporosis**; repeat **every 2–3 years of continuous glucocorticoid treatment**.
- **G24** Serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D **at diagnosis and annually**.
- **G25** On glucocorticoids: **elemental calcium 1,000–1,200 mg/day + vitamin D at least 400–800 IU/day**; supplement vitamin D as clinically indicated if insufficient.
- **G26** **Bisphosphonate** for documented osteoporosis.
- **G27** Assess all features of **metabolic syndrome** before and during therapy; may require individualized treatment adjustment and lifestyle change.
- **G28** Identify barriers to long-term adherence proactively at the start of treatment; monitor thereafter.
- **G29** Monitor **depression and quality of life** throughout; structured, validated questionnaires can be used.

### Pregnancy (p. 689)

- **G30** Family planning: aim for **biochemical remission for 1 year before conception**.
- **G31** Women of reproductive potential: prenatal counseling on the adverse effect of active AIH on pregnancy and on flare risk during and after pregnancy.
- **G32** **Continue maintenance glucocorticoids and/or AZA throughout pregnancy.**
- **G33** **MMF is contraindicated in pregnancy**; counsel on its adverse effects before starting it.
- **G34** Cirrhosis + pregnant or planning pregnancy within the next year → **endoscopic variceal screening** before conception or in the **second trimester**; treat with band ligation.
- **G35** Monitor closely for the **first 6 months postpartum** for an early flare.

### Acute Severe AIH and ALF (p. 694)

- **G36** Acute severe AIH → treatment trial of **prednisone or prednisolone alone**; AIH with ALF → **evaluate directly for LT**.
- **G37** Acute severe AIH that does not improve laboratory tests, or worsens clinically, **within 1–2 weeks** of glucocorticoids → evaluate for LT.

### Treatment Withdrawal and Relapse (p. 696)

- **G38** Drug withdrawal with long-term treatment-free remission is possible in **a minority**; consider it when serum aminotransferases and IgG have been **normal for ≥2 years**.
- **G39** Liver tissue examination before withdrawal helps exclude unsuspected inflammation and reduce relapse, but is **not mandatory in adults** (the summary on p. 671: preferred but not mandatory in adults, **required in children**).
- **G40** Monitor closely for relapse: **regular laboratory tests during the first 12 months** after withdrawal, then **annually, lifelong**.
- **G41** Relapse → **prompt reinstitution of the original treatment** until biochemical remission, then transition to long-term maintenance.

### Nonresponse and Salvage (p. 698)

- **G42** Nonresponse to first-line treatment (children or adults) → reevaluate the **accuracy of the diagnosis and medication adherence**.
- **G43** Anti–tumor necrosis factor (anti-TNF) and anti-CD20 are possible alternatives **after first- and second-line regimens have failed**; supporting data are limited.

### Cancer Surveillance (p. 700)

- **G44** **Hepatic ultrasonography ± serum alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) every 6 months in patients with cirrhosis**, plus standard guidelines for extrahepatic malignancy.

### After Liver Transplantation (p. 704)

- **G45** Suspect recurrent AIH, de novo AIH, or plasma cell hepatitis/rejection in LT recipients with laboratory changes of allograft injury.
- **G46** Obtain **liver biopsy, serum IgG and autoantibodies** to distinguish immune-mediated disease from other causes of allograft dysfunction.
- **G47** Recurrent or de novo AIH → **add predniso(lo)ne with AZA to the calcineurin inhibitor** to achieve biochemical remission.

## Key Data, Definitions and Tables

### Definitions (Table 2, p. 673)

| Condition | Definition |
|---|---|
| AIH | Lymphoplasmacytic interface hepatitis, elevated aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and total IgG, ≥1 characteristic autoantibody |
| Acute severe AIH | Jaundice, **international normalized ratio (INR) >1.5 <2**, no encephalopathy; no previously recognized liver disease |
| ALF | **INR ≥2**; hepatic encephalopathy **within 26 weeks of onset** of illness; no previously recognized liver disease |
| Biochemical remission | Normal serum AST, ALT and IgG (IgG may stay elevated in cirrhosis) |
| Histological remission | No inflammation in liver tissue after treatment |
| Treatment failure | Worsening laboratory or histologic findings despite compliance with standard therapy |
| Incomplete response | Improvement insufficient to meet remission criteria |
| Relapse | Exacerbation after induction of remission and drug withdrawal (or nonadherence) |
| Treatment intolerance | Unable to continue maintenance therapy because of drug side effects |

*AST = aspartate aminotransferase · ALT = alanine aminotransferase · INR = international normalized ratio*

### Type 1 vs Type 2 AIH (Table 3, p. 677)

| Feature | Type 1 | Type 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Autoantibodies | ANA; SMA/anti-actin; anti–soluble liver antigen (anti-SLA) | Anti-LKM1 (anti–liver cytosol type 1 [anti-LC1], anti-LKM3) |
| Frequency | 96% of US adults | 9–12% of US children; 38% of UK children |
| Age | Peripubertal and adults | Usually <14 y |
| Presentation | Acute 25–75%; acute severe 2–6%; asymptomatic 25–34% | Acute onset ~40%; ALF possible; relapse frequent |
| Remission after drug withdrawal | Possible | **Rare — usually need long-term immunosuppression** |

### Autoantibodies (pp. 676–678)

- White North American adults at presentation: ANA 80%, SMA 63%, anti-LKM1 3%. Anti-LKM1 sensitivity for AIH in North American adults 1% → test after ANA and SMA are negative.
- Anti-LKM1 in **13–38% of British and Canadian children** with AIH → test ANA, SMA and anti-LKM1 together at presentation in children.
- Anti-SLA: 7–22% of type 1 AIH; specificity 99%; **associated with severe disease and with relapse after drug withdrawal** (Table 4: predicts relapse after treatment; associated with poor outcome).
- Up to 20% are negative for ANA, SMA and anti-LKM1 (seronegative AIH) → further antibodies per Figure 2 (anti-SLA, atypical perinuclear antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody [pANCA], tissue transglutaminase, AMA).
- AMA in 8–12% of AIH without histologic bile duct injury/loss; these patients respond to glucocorticoids and do not evolve into PBC. AMA alone is insufficient for AIH–PBC overlap — liver tissue examination required.

### Histology (p. 679)

- Interface hepatitis = histologic hallmark; plasma cells in 66%, lobular hepatitis in 47%, centrilobular necrosis in 29%, emperipolesis in 65%, rosettes in 33%.
- **None of the individual findings is specific for AIH**; interface hepatitis with portal lymphocytic/lymphoplasmacytic cells extending into the lobule, emperipolesis and rosettes together are considered typical.
- Cirrhosis at presentation: 28–33% of adults, 38% of children.

### Scoring Systems (p. 680)

- Revised original IAIHG score: sensitivity 100% vs 95% (simplified). Simplified score: specificity 90% vs 73%, accuracy 92% vs 82%.
- **Revised score for complex or unusual features; simplified score for typical patients.** Low simplified score → consider reassessment with the revised score.
- Children (meta-analysis, simplified criteria): sensitivity 77%, specificity 95%.
- Most useful to define cohorts for clinical studies; aids diagnosis in challenging cases. Score tables are in the online supplement (Supporting Table S2), not in the article.

### Drugs Linked to AIH-Like Injury (Table 5, p. 683)

| Definite | Probable |
|---|---|
| Minocycline, nitrofurantoin, infliximab, alpha-methyldopa, adalimumab, halothane, oxyphenisatin\*, dihydralazine\*, **tienilic acid\*** | Propylthiouracil, isoniazid, diclofenac, etanercept, atorvastatin, rosuvastatin, clometacine |

*\*Removed from the market. "Possible" column (checkpoint inhibitors, herbal products and others): see Table 5.*

- Hy's law (p. 684): aminotransferases >3× upper limit of normal (ULN) + total bilirubin >2× ULN → death or LT in 9–12%; meeting it supports glucocorticoids.

### First-Line Treatment (Figure 4, p. 690)

|---|---|---|---|

| AZA | Check TPMT; **add AZA (50–150 mg/d) after 2 weeks** | **Not in decompensated cirrhosis**; compensated: check TPMT, add AZA (50–150 mg/d) after 2 weeks | — |
| Induction labs | Every 1–2 weeks | Every 1–2 weeks | Every 12–24 hours |

| Response | Taper prednisone to **5–10 mg daily** (budesonide 3 mg daily) **over the next 6 months**; maintain AZA; labs every 2–4 weeks | Taper prednisone to 5–10 mg daily over 6 months; maintain AZA if started; labs every 2–4 weeks | Cautiously reduce prednisone; consider AZA once cholestasis resolves (check TPMT first); labs every 1–2 weeks |

**Dosing detail (pp. 690–692):**
- Prednisone alone 40–60 mg/day (adults) or 1–2 mg/kg/day (children, max 40–60 mg/day); or 20–40 mg/day with AZA (US adult AZA 50–150 mg/day; Europe and children 1–2 mg/kg/day).
- After remission: reduce to 20 mg/day (labs every 2 weeks), then 2.5–5 mg every 2–4 weeks to 5–10 mg/day; may then stop, leaving AZA alone.
- Prednisone monotherapy suits treatment expected to last <6 months or AZA contraindication; prolonged monotherapy, especially >10 mg/day, should be avoided.
- Typical AZA start 50–100 mg/day (adults), 1–2 mg/kg/day (children); leukopenia/thrombocytopenia → dose reduction or withdrawal; stop if cytopenia does not recover in 1–2 weeks. No AZA in active malignancy.
- Budesonide trial regimen: 3 mg TID, reduced to twice daily after remission. Not for steroid-refractory AIH; role in acute severe AIH or ALF unknown → not recommended there.
- Acute severe AIH (p. 693): predniso(lo)ne usually 0.5–1 mg/kg/day in adults, up to 2 mg/kg in children; abandon ineffective treatment within 1–2 weeks. In ALF, glucocorticoids have not improved overall survival; survival was worse with Model for End-Stage Liver Disease (MELD) >40.

**Table 8 — monitoring on prolonged first-line drugs (p. 691):**
- Predniso(lo)ne: taper to lowest dose; eye exams (cataract, glaucoma); bone density monitoring; vitamin D and calcium; screen mood/mental health.
- Budesonide: do not prescribe in cirrhosis or acute severe AIH.
- AZA: check TPMT status before prescribing; **monitor cell counts at least every 6 months**; reduce dose for mild cytopenia, stop for severe cytopenia or gastrointestinal intolerance; sun avoidance and **yearly dermatologic skin-cancer screening**; not recommended in decompensated cirrhosis or acute severe AIH.

### Withdrawal and Relapse Data (pp. 694–696)

- Normal AST, ALT and IgG for ≥2 years proposed before withdrawal; cirrhosis with chronic IgG elevation does not exclude withdrawal if other tests are normal for ≥2 years.
- Normal liver tissue lowers subsequent relapse to 28%; with vs without prewithdrawal biopsy, relapse 30% vs 21% (P = 0.57). Prewithdrawal biopsy still strongly advised in children.
- Treatment-free remission at ≥3 years' follow-up: **19–40%**.
- Relapse after withdrawal: **adults 50–87%, children 60–80%**; after ≥2 years of normality: 46% adults, 80% children. Long-term remission in 20% of children with type 1 AIH, rarely in type 2.
- **50% of relapses within the first 3 months**; 90% within 28 months; after year 1, ~3%/year over the next 3 years; late relapse possible.
- Retreatment: laboratory resolution in **94%** (4 ± 1 months), histologic in 59% (8 ± 2 months). Adults should be treated **long-term after the first relapse**; repeated relapse → more cirrhosis (38% vs 10%) and liver death/LT (20% vs 3%).
- After relapse, AZA up to 2 mg/kg/day while predniso(lo)ne is minimized; AZA-intolerant → MMF, or in adults low-dose predniso(lo)ne ≤7.5 mg/day.

### Second-Line Data (pp. 696–697)

- Treatment failure 7–9% of adults; incomplete response ~15%; treatment-ending side effects 13%.
- MMF pooled response 58% (82% AZA intolerance vs 32% treatment failure). TAC normalized aminotransferases in 75–94% (meta-analyses); dose-limiting side effects ~25%.

### Overlap Syndromes

| | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **AIH–PBC (Paris criteria, p. 682)** | **2 of 3 PBC criteria:** alkaline phosphatase (ALP) ≥2× ULN or gamma-glutamyltransferase (GGT) ≥5× ULN · **presence of AMA** · florid bile duct lesions on histology. **Plus AIH criteria** (in addition to interface hepatitis): ALT ≥5× ULN **and** IgG ≥2× ULN or SMA present |
| **AIH–PSC (p. 682)** | Typical AIH features · **absence of AMA** · large-duct PSC on endoscopic or magnetic resonance cholangiography, **or small-duct PSC** on histology ("onion-skinning" periductal fibrosis) |

- Paris criteria: sensitivity 92%, specificity 97% (single center); never independently validated (IAIHG); may miss AIH–PBC with less pronounced cholestatic features.
- Consider AIH–PSC in all patients with AIH and chronic UC, unexplained cholestatic laboratory findings, or nonresponse to conventional glucocorticoids. Chronic UC in 16% of adults with AIH; 42% of those with AIH + UC have cholangiographic PSC changes.
- Laboratory features of cholestasis (raised ALP or GGT) are associated with incomplete or delayed response and may indicate an alternative diagnosis (p. 694).
- **Treatment (p. 699):** consider adding UDCA (G11). AIH–PBC meeting Paris criteria: prednisone or prednisolone 30 mg/day tapered over 4 weeks to 10 mg/day + UDCA 13–15 mg/kg/day beat either alone. AIH–PSC: predniso(lo)ne 0.5 mg/kg/day tapered to 10–15 mg/day + UDCA 13–15 mg/kg/day; children with autoimmune sclerosing cholangitis: UDCA 10 mg/kg twice daily (not exceeding 1.5–2 g/day) with predniso(lo)ne.
- AIH–PSC in adults vs AIH: laboratory resolution 22% vs 64%, treatment failure 33% vs 10%, liver death or LT 33% vs 8%.

### Cancer and Long-Term Outcomes (pp. 699–700)

- Treated AIH (nontransplant center): 10-year survival 91%, 20-year 70%; standardized mortality ratio 1.63 for all-cause death.
- Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) in 1–9% of AIH with cirrhosis (annual incidence 1.1–1.9%); risk factors: cirrhosis ≥10 years, portal hypertension, continuous inflammation, immunosuppression ≥3 years. Extrahepatic malignancy in 5% of treated patients, nonmelanoma skin cancer most common.

### Liver Transplantation (pp. 700–702, Table 13)

- **Recurrent AIH:** 8–12% within the first year, 36–68% after 5 years. Diagnostic criteria as for the original disease. Graft dysfunction at 2 months–12 years; may be detected only by liver biopsy.
- **Recurrent AIH treatment (Table 13):** predniso(lo)ne **30 mg daily + AZA 1–2 mg/kg daily**; reduce predniso(lo)ne to 5–10 mg daily in 4–8 weeks; predniso(lo)ne + AZA maintenance; continue the calcineurin inhibitor. Rescue (empiric): MMF for AZA, switch calcineurin inhibitor, rapamycin.
- **De novo AIH** (transplanted for another disease): 1–7% of pediatric and 1–3% of adult recipients. Children: predniso(lo)ne 1–2 mg/kg (<60 mg daily) + AZA 1–2 mg/kg daily; otherwise as for recurrent AIH. The Banff working group proposed "plasma cell–rich rejection" to replace "plasma cell hepatitis" and "de novo AIH" for a defined pattern of late graft dysfunction; separating de novo AIH from plasma cell hepatitis/rejection may be prudent.
- Glucocorticoid continuation vs withdrawal after LT: meta-analysis found no significant difference (Table 12) → Rec 5.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- All 5 recommendations are conditional with low or very low certainty; the first-line meta-analysis rests on a single randomized trial (Table 9); most guidance rests on retrospective series.
- The ≥2-year normal-test requirement before withdrawal is described as "proposed", not trial-derived.
- Prewithdrawal biopsy: "required in children" (p. 671 summary) vs "strongly advised in children" (p. 695 text).
- De novo AIH vs plasma cell–rich rejection after LT remains a nosologic question.
- Newer [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis|EASL 2025]] differs on several points (budesonide first line, acute severe AIH timing, withdrawal criteria); the comparison lives on [[autoimmune-hepatitis]].
