# HIV/AIDS-Related Diarrhea

Diarrhea is one of the most common GI complaints in people with HIV. The differential is driven almost entirely by **two questions**: (1) **What is the CD4 count?** — which determines the spectrum of opportunistic pathogens, and (2) **Is this infectious or non-infectious?** — since antiretroviral therapy (ART, especially boosted protease inhibitors) and HIV enteropathy are leading non-infectious causes in the ART era. Effective ART with immune reconstitution is the single most important intervention for most chronic infectious causes.

## Framework: CD4 Count Stratifies the Differential

| CD4 (cells/µL) | Risk tier | Key considerations |
|---|---|---|
| **>200** | Near-normal host | Same pathogens as immunocompetent hosts (bacterial, viral, *C. difficile*); consider ART side effects and non-infectious causes |
| **<200** | Opportunistic risk begins | *Cryptosporidium*, microsporidia become important; coccidian parasites |
| **<100** | High risk | *Cryptosporidium*, microsporidia, chronic/severe protozoal disease |
| **<50** | Highest risk | **CMV colitis** and **disseminated MAC** become major considerations |

> Most opportunistic enteric disease is not seen until CD4 <200, and usually <100. CMV and MAC cluster at **CD4 <50**.

## Differential by Category

### Infectious — Common to All Hosts (consider at any CD4)

- **Bacterial:** *Salmonella* ([[salmonella-infection]]), *Shigella* ([[shigellosis]]), *Campylobacter* ([[campylobacter-infection]]) — often more severe, bacteremic, and relapsing in HIV. *Clostridioides difficile* ([[clostridioides-difficile]]) is among the **most common** causes given frequent antibiotic and healthcare exposure.
- **Viral:** norovirus ([[norovirus]]) and routine viral gastroenteritis.

### Infectious — Opportunistic (advanced HIV, low CD4)

- **Cryptosporidium** ([[cryptosporidiosis]]) — watery, voluminous, secretory diarrhea; chronic and debilitating at low CD4. Diagnosis: stool antigen EIA, modified acid-fast stain, multiplex PCR.
- **Microsporidia** (*Enterocytozoon bieneusi*, *Encephalitozoon intestinalis*) — chronic watery diarrhea, malabsorption, wasting at CD4 <100; modified trichrome stain / PCR.
- **Cystoisospora belli** and **Cyclospora** — coccidian parasites; acid-fast oocysts.
- **CMV colitis** — CD4 <50; bloody diarrhea, abdominal pain, fever, can cause ulceration, hemorrhage, perforation; diagnosed by colonoscopy with biopsy (inclusion bodies, immunostain).
- **Mycobacterium avium complex (MAC)** — disseminated disease at CD4 <50; fever, weight loss, diarrhea, hepatosplenomegaly, cytopenias; AFB blood cultures, tissue biopsy.
- **Giardia** ([[giardiasis]]), **Entamoeba histolytica** ([[entamoeba-histolytica-infection]]), *Strongyloides* — also seen, exposure-dependent.

### Non-Infectious

- **ART / medication-induced diarrhea** — the **most common non-infectious cause** in the ART era; especially **boosted protease inhibitors** (e.g., lopinavir/ritonavir, ritonavir-boosted regimens). Secretory, watery, dose-related; improves with regimen switch or symptomatic therapy.
- **HIV (idiopathic AIDS) enteropathy** — pathogen-negative chronic diarrhea after thorough work-up; attributed to direct HIV mucosal effects, immune dysregulation, and villous atrophy. **Diagnosis of exclusion.**
- **Other:** non-infectious colitis (e.g., IBD), pancreatic insufficiency, IRIS (immune reconstitution after ART start).

## Diagnostic Approach

1. **History:** stool volume/frequency, **small- vs. large-bowel pattern** (watery/voluminous → small bowel/proximal; bloody/mucoid with tenesmus → colitis), nocturnal symptoms, fever, weight loss, abdominal pain; medication/ART history, antibiotic exposure, travel, food/water and sexual exposures.
2. **Check the CD4 count and viral load** — anchors the differential.
3. **Stool studies:** culture, *C. difficile* testing, ova & parasites with **special stains** (modified acid-fast for *Cryptosporidium*/*Cystoisospora*/*Cyclospora*; modified trichrome for microsporidia), antigen EIA / **multiplex GI PCR panel**. AFB blood cultures if MAC suspected.
4. **Endoscopy when stool work-up is non-diagnostic and CD4 is low:** for unexplained AIDS-related diarrhea with **CD4 <100**, **flexible sigmoidoscopy with biopsy** is generally sufficient to evaluate for CMV and other mucosal pathogens; colonoscopy/EGD with biopsy if proximal disease or CMV/MAC strongly suspected.
5. See [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] for the general acute-diarrhea diagnostic framework.

## Pathogen-Directed Treatment

| Pathogen | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Cryptosporidium** | **ART / immune reconstitution is the most important therapy**; supportive (rehydration, antidiarrheals); **nitazoxanide** for severe disease or slow immune recovery |
| **Microsporidia** | **Albendazole 400 mg BID × ~4 weeks** (esp. *E. intestinalis*); ART/immune reconstitution; fumagillin for *E. bieneusi* (limited availability) |
| **Cystoisospora / Cyclospora** | **TMP-SMX** |
| **CMV colitis** | **IV ganciclovir** (or valganciclovir); foscarnet if resistant/intolerant; plus ART |
| **MAC (disseminated)** | **Azithromycin (or clarithromycin) + ethambutol** ± rifabutin; plus ART |
| **Bacterial (Salmonella/Shigella/Campylobacter)** | Targeted antibiotics — lower threshold to treat than in immunocompetent hosts |
| ***C. difficile*** | Per standard guidance — see [[clostridioides-difficile]] |

## Non-Infectious / Symptomatic Management

- **Optimize / switch ART** if a protease inhibitor is the culprit.
- **Crofelemer** — FDA-approved antisecretory agent for **symptomatic relief of non-infectious diarrhea** in adults with HIV/AIDS on ART; minimally absorbed, well tolerated (ADVENT trial).
- **Loperamide** and other antidiarrheals for symptom control once infection is excluded/treated.
- **Initiate or optimize ART** — immune reconstitution resolves or prevents most opportunistic enteric disease.

## Key Mnemonics

- **CD4 <50 = "CMV and MAC"** (the two big opportunists at the lowest counts).
- **Watery + voluminous → small bowel** (*Cryptosporidium*, microsporidia); **bloody + tenesmus → colitis** (CMV, bacterial).
- **Bland watery diarrhea, all work-up negative, on a protease inhibitor → think drug; if off drug → HIV enteropathy** (diagnosis of exclusion).
- **The best "antidiarrheal" in HIV is effective ART.**
