Endoscopic eradication therapy (EET) is the minimally invasive, organ-sparing treatment for dysplastic [[barretts-esophagus|Barrett's esophagus]] and early esophageal neoplasia. It combines **endoscopic resection of visible lesions** with **ablation of the remaining flat Barrett's segment**, and has largely replaced esophagectomy for high-grade dysplasia (HGD) and intramucosal carcinoma (IMC/T1a esophageal adenocarcinoma [EAC]). The defining endpoint is **complete eradication of intestinal metaplasia (CE-IM)** — no endoscopically visible Barrett's esophagus (BE) and no intestinal metaplasia (IM) on surveillance biopsies.

## Indications

Per [[asge-2018-barretts-eet|American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2018]] (Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation [GRADE]) and concordant with [[aga-2024-barretts-eet|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2024]] and [[acg-2022-barretts|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2022]]. *Decision algorithm by histology: [[barretts-esophagus]].*

| Indication | Recommendation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmed **HGD** | EET over surveillance, and EET over esophagectomy | **Strong** (ASGE 2018 Recs 2b, 3; AGA 2024 Rec 1) |
| **IMC / T1a [[esophageal-adenocarcinoma\|EAC]]** | EET over esophagectomy — lymph node (LN) metastasis risk **0% in HGD, up to 2% in IMC** | **Strong** (ASGE 2018 Rec 3) |
| **T1b EAC, sm1 with all four low-risk features** | EET a reasonable alternative to esophagectomy, especially in poor surgical candidates | Best Practice Advice 5 ([[aga-2020-endoscopic-treatment-barretts-dysplasia\|AGA 2020]]); narrative ([[acg-2022-barretts\|ACG 2022]]) |
| Confirmed **low-grade dysplasia (LGD)** | EET over surveillance; shared decision making — patients valuing avoidance of procedural risk may choose surveillance | **Conditional** (ASGE 2018 Rec 2a; AGA 2024 Rec 2) |
| **Indefinite for dysplasia (IND)**, confirmed on expert review | **No EET** — annual endoscopy until the histologic grade changes | Implementation consideration ([[aga-2025-barretts-surveillance\|AGA 2025]]) |
| **nondysplastic BE (NDBE)** | **Against** routine EET | **Conditional** against (AGA 2024 Rec 3) |

**Dysplasia must be confirmed by ≥1 expert gastrointestinal (GI) pathologist or panel** before EET (ASGE 2018 Rec 1, conditional). Expert review changes the diagnosis in ~55% of cases (mostly downgrading).

**Repeat the endoscopy before committing to EET.** Confirmed LGD gets a repeat high-definition white-light endoscopy (HD-WLE) within **3–6 months** to find a visible lesion, which then goes to endoscopic resection ([[aga-2020-endoscopic-treatment-barretts-dysplasia|AGA 2020]] Best Practice Advice 1); AGA 2025 makes the same point for BE, IND and LGD — repeat within **6 months** on high-dose acid suppression, by an expert endoscopist, to rule out prevalent HGD or EAC.

**Depth and histology decide the T1b branch.** Submucosal cancer **T1b sm2-3**, poorly differentiated cancer, or lymphatic/vascular infiltration makes esophagectomy the treatment of choice — **any one** is sufficient, so adverse histology overrides depth (LN-metastasis risk at least 20%). The sm1 exception requires **all four** low-risk features (depth <500 µm, negative deep margin, well-to-moderate differentiation, no lymphovascular invasion); the criteria table, nodal risk by substage and the M1–M3 (mucosal) / SM1–SM3 (submucosal) depth levels live on [[esophageal-adenocarcinoma]] and [[endoscopic-submucosal-dissection]].

**The versions differ here.** ASGE 2018's algorithm routes *all* submucosal cancer to surgical referral; AGA 2020 and ACG 2022 opened the sm1 low-risk exception above.

## Technique

EET is sequential: **resect, then ablate.** Routine complete endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR) of the entire Barrett's segment (cEMR) is **strongly recommended against** (ASGE 2018 Rec 6) because it causes far more strictures (odds ratio [OR] 4.73), bleeding (OR 6.88), and perforation (OR 7.00) than focal EMR + radiofrequency ablation (RFA), with equivalent eradication — randomized data put the stricture rate at **88% with radical stepwise EMR vs 14%** with focal EMR + ablation (AGA 2020).

### Step 1 — Endoscopic Resection of Visible Lesions

- **Resect ALL visible lesions** (nodularity, ulceration, plaque, depression, mucosal discoloration), no matter how subtle — strong recommendation (ASGE 2018 Rec 4). Resection always precedes ablation.
- **Multiband EMR** (preferred for most cases — faster and cheaper) or cap-assisted EMR — equivalent specimens and safety.
- **[[endoscopic-submucosal-dissection|endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD)]]** for larger lesions unsuitable for en-bloc EMR, post-ablation scarring, or suspected submucosal invasion.
- **Do not resect >50% of the circumference** in one setting — stricture risk rises, so extensive resection of flat BE is avoided (AGA 2020 Best Practice Advice).
- Resection **changes management in 30–40%** ([[acg-2022-barretts]]); ASGE 2018 notes the management-change outcome was not consistently reported.
- **ESD vs EMR** ([[aga-2024-barretts-eet|AGA 2024]] Rec 5, conditional): most neoplastic lesions are managed with [[endoscopic-mucosal-resection|EMR]]; choose ESD for large bulky lesions, lesions highly suspicious for ≥T1b invasion (**depressed, Paris 0-IIc or 0-IIa+c**), or failed prior EMR. [[asge-2023-esd|ASGE 2023]] instead selects by size — ESD over EMR for well-differentiated, nonulcerated early EAC or Barrett's nodular dysplasia **>20 mm**, and **no recommendation either way at ≤20 mm** — see [[esophageal-cancer]]. They diverge on large flat, nondepressed lesions.

> [!pearl] Resection is the staging step, not the biopsy
> EMR reaches the muscularis mucosa and submucosa and **changes the histologic diagnosis in ~39%** of patients, usually upstaging ([[asge-2018-barretts-eet]]). That is why [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]] has no routine role in separating T1a from T1b in HGD/IMC or nodules — a **strong** recommendation against, on a T1a/T1b sensitivity of only 41% ([[asge-2019-barretts-screening-surveillance|ASGE 2019]] Rec 5).

### Step 2 — Ablation of Residual Flat Segment

After EMR of all visible lesions, **ablate the remaining flat Barrett's segment** (ASGE 2018 Rec 5, conditional). Lack of ablation predicts recurrence (relative risk [RR] 2.5); metachronous neoplasia 16.5% (EMR + ablation) vs 29.9% (EMR alone).

- **[[radiofrequency-ablation|RFA]]** — preferred primary ablative modality (widest randomized controlled trial (RCT)/safety evidence).
- **Cryotherapy** (spray liquid nitrogen or cryoballoon) — acceptable alternative, especially for RFA-refractory cases; no RCT vs RFA.
- **Hybrid [[argon-plasma-coagulation|argon plasma coagulation (APC)]]** — one of several ablation options, but **RFA carries the highest-quality and most extensive RCT evidence** ([[aga-2024-barretts-eet]]); [[asge-2018-barretts-eet|ASGE 2018]] notes APC is **not** the current standard even though photodynamic therapy (PDT)/APC were the modalities in the trial underpinning the "ablate the residual segment" recommendation. Settings, technique, and adverse events on [[argon-plasma-coagulation]].
- **Exception** ([[aga-2024-barretts-eet|AGA 2024]]): if only a small area of BE remains beyond the visible lesion, completion resection in the same session is acceptable and may be preferred over a second ablation procedure.

**Ablation ground rules** ([[aga-2020-endoscopic-treatment-barretts-dysplasia|AGA 2020]]):

- **Target: all visible columnar mucosa + 5–10 mm proximal to the squamocolumnar junction (SCJ) + 5–10 mm distal to the gastroesophageal junction (GEJ)** (top of the gastric folds), with a focal device, circumferentially.
- Ablate only flat BE without inflammation or visible abnormality; start every session with HD-WLE, preferably plus optical chromoendoscopy, to exclude a lesion needing resection.
- **No routine biopsies of flat BE before an ablation session** — blood inhibits energy transfer.
- Incomplete healing → **postpone ablation ≥6 weeks**, verify acid suppression, **avoid biopsies** (reactive change mimics dysplasia).
- Sessions every **2–3 months** until no columnar epithelium remains in the tubular esophagus; may extend beyond 1 year. **Twice-daily [[proton-pump-inhibitors|proton pump inhibitor (PPI)]]** throughout — this makes a baseline pH study unnecessary except in selected cases (poor squamous regeneration, refractory BE, persistent erosive esophagitis).

> [!pitfall] The GEJ is the hardest place to ablate
> Gastric folds and a widened hiatal hernia reduce balloon-electrode contact, endoscopy cannot reliably judge residual BE there, and it is a common site of neoplastic recurrence — treat the GEJ/neo-Z-line circumferentially with focal, targeted therapy ([[aga-2020-endoscopic-treatment-barretts-dysplasia]]).

## Outcomes and Goals

- **Primary endpoint: CE-IM** — absence of endoscopically visible BE plus no intestinal metaplasia on surveillance biopsies (defined by 1-2 consecutive negative endoscopies).
- Most patients achieve CE-IM within **~3 ablation sessions**.
- **EET vs esophagectomy (HGD/IMC):** equivalent complete eradication (RR .96) and 1-, 3-, 5-year survival (5-yr RR .88) and EAC-related mortality; fewer major adverse events (RR .38; 16 fewer/100). Neoplasia recurrence higher with EET (RR 9.5; ~2 more/100), but >95% of recurrences are re-treated endoscopically.
- **EET vs surveillance (LGD):** progression RR ~.14-.16; cumulative progression 12.6% (surveillance) vs 1.7% (RFA).
- **EET vs surveillance (HGD):** progression RR .22-.42.

## Adverse Events

RFA ± EMR pooled (37 studies, 9200 patients):

| Event | Rate |
|---|---|
| Overall serious adverse events | 8.8% (6.5%-11.9%) |
| [[esophageal-stricture\|Esophageal stricture]] (most common) | 5.6% (4.2%-7.4%) |
| Bleeding | 1% (.8%-1.3%) |
| Perforation | 0.6% (.4%-.9%) |

The burden falls on the *resection* step, not the ablation — the RFA-alone vs EMR + RFA split (RR 4.4) is on [[radiofrequency-ablation]].

**For consent, quote stricture at about 6%, with bleeding and perforation each <1%** (AGA 2020 Best Practice Advice 12).

## Post-CE-IM Surveillance

Continue surveillance after CE-IM (ASGE 2018 Rec 7, conditional) — recurrence persists; cessation is not recommended. Pooled recurrence: any 7.5/100 patient-years, IM 4.8/100 patient-years, dysplasia 2.0/100 patient-years; most within the first 3 years. Intervals run off the **worst pre-treatment histology** — schedule table on [[barretts-esophagus]].

Post-CE-IM biopsies are 4-quadrant, in separate containers: the **high cardia just below the Z line** (top of the gastric folds) and each of the **distal 2-3 cm of neosquamous epithelium**. Biopsies of normal-appearing mucosa above that range add little yield, even after long-segment disease ([[acg-2022-barretts]]).

**Read the first year differently.** Dysplasia detected in the first year after CE-IM carries RR **1.92** versus later years — [[aga-2020-post-endoscopy-esophageal-adenocarcinoma|AGA 2020]] reads it as **incompletely treated prevalent disease** rather than true recurrence, and residual intestinal metaplasia as a marker of prevalent dysplasia. The post-endoscopy esophageal adenocarcinoma (PEEC) framework is on [[barretts-esophagus]].

## Where to Perform EET

High-volume centers with access to expert GI pathology, advanced imaging, EMR/ESD expertise, and a multidisciplinary team (surgery, medical/radiation oncology) — [[acg-2022-barretts|ACG 2022]] Rec 20, conditional. A volume-outcome effect is demonstrated (more experience → fewer sessions to CE-IM, lower recurrence).

**Volume thresholds — check the denominator, they are not interchangeable:**

| Threshold | Denominator | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **≥10 new cases annually** | per **center** | [[aga-2020-endoscopic-treatment-barretts-dysplasia\|AGA 2020]] Best Practice Advice 8 |
| ≥10 new HGD/early EAC cases per year | per **endoscopist** | European Society of Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ESGE) threshold, cited by [[acg-2022-barretts]] |
| >10 ablation procedures annually vs <3 | per **facility** | AGA 2020 recurrence analysis — hazard ratio (HR) **0.19** (95% confidence interval [CI] 0.05–0.68) for BE recurrence at high- vs low-volume facilities |

**Competence in esophageal endoscopic resection is not reached quickly.** [[aga-2016-low-grade-dysplasia-barretts|AGA 2016]] reports a 5% perforation rate across the first 120 esophageal endoscopic resections performed by 6 participants despite an intensive structured program — evidence that 20 procedures are insufficient; increasing endoscopist/center experience (threshold of 30) was associated with fewer sessions to reach CE-IM, without a demonstrable case-volume effect on safety or efficacy outcomes.

**Quality indicators and the performance target for each** ([[acg-2022-barretts|ACG 2022]], a joint ACG/ASGE indicator set):

| Quality indicator | Type | Target (range) |
|---|---|---|
| Dysplasia read by a GI pathologist, or confirmed by a second pathologist, before starting EET | Process | **90%** (75-100) |
| Complete eradication of dysplasia (CE-D) achieved **by 18 months** | Outcome | **80%** (70-95) |
| CE-IM achieved **by 18 months** | Outcome | **70%** (50-80) |
| Adverse events tracked and documented after EET | Process | **90%** (50-100) |

**Audit is part of the job** (AGA 2016 Practice Advice 16): endoscopists performing EET should report audits of their **CE-D and CE-IM rates and adverse events** in clinical practice. AGA 2016 names four further indicators not in the table above — HD-WLE available at centers performing EET · the endoscopist performs both mucosal ablation and endoscopic resection · complete endoscopic resection is performed in patients with visible lesions · surveillance occurs after achieving CE-IM.
