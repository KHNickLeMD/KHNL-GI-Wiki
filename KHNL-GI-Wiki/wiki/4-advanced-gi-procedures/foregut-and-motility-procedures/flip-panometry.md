## Overview

**FLIP (Functional Lumen Imaging Probe) Panometry** is an endoscopic technique that simultaneously measures esophagogastric junction (EGJ) opening geometry and the esophageal body contractile response during controlled bag distension. Unlike [[high-resolution-manometry|high-resolution manometry (HRM)]], FLIP can be performed at the time of sedated endoscopy, providing an integrated motor assessment without a separate procedure.

FLIP panometry generates a 2-axis motility profile — EGJ opening versus contractile response — that maps to the FLIP Panometry Motility Classification v2.0 matrix ([[dallas-2025-flip-panometry]]).

**Scope limit (Dallas):** the classification is intended for patients **without previous foregut surgery and without mechanical obstruction or abnormal anatomy** (strictures, paraesophageal hernia) — these produce *secondary* motility abnormalities. In post-surgical or obstructed anatomy, findings must be read against that specific anatomy (see [[#FLIP After Foregut Intervention]]).

## Standard Protocol

**Equipment** ([[aga-2025-flip-esophageal]]):

| Catheter | Electrode pairs | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| **8-cm (325N)** | 16 pairs, 0.5 cm apart | EGJ metrics only |
| **16-cm (322N)** | 16 pairs, 1 cm apart | EGJ metrics **+ esophageal body contractile response** |

Panometry (the classification below) requires the **16-cm catheter** ([[dallas-2025-flip-panometry]], 94% agreement). AGA 8-cm protocol: EGJ at balloon center (waist at 20 mL); fill to 30, 40, and 50 mL, measuring EGJ diameter, pressure, and EGJ-DI at each; **record metrics from the 40-mL fill** ([[aga-2025-flip-esophageal]]).

**Setup:** calibrate to atmospheric pressure before transoral placement; set filter settings to **"off"** for optimal intraprocedural interpretation.

**Inflation steps** (94% agreement):

1. 50 mL — hold ≥30 seconds
2. 60 mL — hold ≥30 seconds (**primary EGJ distensibility index (EGJ-DI) measurement step**)
3. 70 mL — hold ≥30 seconds (**maximum diameter step**; 70 mL is the target maximum fill for a complete motility study)

Longer holds (60–90 s) are acceptable if needed to capture metrics, especially with sustained/spastic contractions. A **high-pressure alarm** in a correctly positioned FLIP does **not** increase procedural risk (88% agreement) — FLIP pressures are trivial next to balloon dilation (3–8 ATM = 2280–6080 mmHg).

**Positioning:** Dallas — **2 sensors (2 cm) below the crural diaphragm**, maintained throughout the entire study (100% agreement). American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) — position with **1–3 impedance sensor pairs distal to the EGJ** (16-cm catheter), confirmed by the EGJ "waist" on the real-time display at 30–40 mL. Resistance while positioning → withdraw and reposition (protects the distal pressure transducer); endoscopic visualization may help with a diverticulum, hiatus hernia, or prior foregut surgery.

**EGJ-DI:** median of **3** measurements taken during the 60-mL fill, at the times of **greatest** EGJ opening diameter; measure **>5 s after** active filling; avoid dry-catheter artifact; do not use measures taken during FLIP emptying for clinical decisions.

**Maximum EGJ diameter:** greatest opening during the 70-mL fill.

> **EGJ-DI is not reliable when the associated FLIP pressure is <15 mmHg** (Dallas, 88% agreement) — record the pressure alongside every EGJ-DI. Also document **EGJ–crural diaphragm separation**, which indicates [[hiatal-hernia|hiatal hernia]].

## EGJ Opening Classification

Assessed from two parameters measured at defined volumes ([[dallas-2025-flip-panometry]], 100% agreement for each):

| Category | EGJ-DI (at 60 mL) | Max EGJ Diameter (at 70 mL) |
|---|---|---|
| **REO** (Reduced EGJ opening) | <2.0 mm²/mmHg | **AND** <12 mm |
| **NEO** (Normal EGJ opening) | ≥2.0 mm²/mmHg | **AND** ≥16 mm |
| **Inconclusive** (IEO; formerly "borderline") | Anything in between | Anything in between |

*Both criteria (EGJ-DI **and** diameter) must be met for REO or NEO; everything else is inconclusive (median 8.0, 100% agreement). Thresholds are unchanged from the 2021 scheme — only "borderline" was renamed "inconclusive".*

![[flip-2025-dallas-egj-opening-07.png|700x337]]
*Figure 2 — FLIP EGJ opening classification by EGJ-DI at 60 mL (y-axis) and maximum EGJ diameter at 70 mL (x-axis): red = reduced, blue = normal, grey = inconclusive. ([[dallas-2025-flip-panometry]])*

**Negative predictive value (NPV) for achalasia/conclusive EGJ outflow obstruction (EGJOO) rises with the EGJ-DI threshold used** ([[aga-2025-flip-esophageal]]): 90% for EGJ-DI >2.0, **96%** for >2.8, **97%** for >3.0 mm²/mmHg. Using EGJ-DI **without** the maximum diameter carries a lower NPV.

**Caveats on the extremes:** REO is seen in **5%–11%** of symptomatic patients *without* an HRM disorder of EGJ outflow; inconclusive EGJ opening is reported in **25%–28%** of patients with normal EGJ outflow pressure on HRM. In healthy subjects (meta-analysis, 15 studies, 154 subjects) the 5th–95th percentile EGJ-DI at 60 mL was **3.1–8.1 mm²/mmHg**, and **no** healthy subject had EGJ-DI <2.0.

## Contractile Response (CR) Classification

Building blocks — the contraction patterns the categories are defined from ([[dallas-2025-flip-panometry]] Table 1):

| Pattern | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Distinct antegrade contraction** | Antegrade contraction extending **≥6 cm** of axial length with an associated **pressure increase of ≥10 mmHg** (100% agreement) |
| **RAC** (repetitive antegrade contraction) | "Rule-of-6s": **≥6 consecutive** antegrade contractions of **≥6 cm** axial length at **6 ± 3 contractions/min** at regular cadence (100% agreement) |
| **SOC** (sustained occluding contraction) | **Non-propagating** (retrograde or horizontal), **lumen-occluding — diameter <6 mm** — contraction of the esophageal body persisting **>10 s** with an associated FLIP pressure increase **>35 mmHg** (88% agreement) |
| **sLESC** (sustained lower esophageal sphincter [LES] contraction) | Transient reduction in diameter at the LES lasting **>5 s**, with a rise in FLIP pressure, **independent of** antegrade contractions, respiration, or crural contraction (100% agreement) |
| **RRC** (repetitive retrograde contraction) | **≥6 consecutive retrograde** contractions of **≥6 cm** axial length at **>9 contractions/min** (88% agreement). Clinical relevance uncertain |

Categories (contractile response [CR] reflects the **aggregate** across the 50-, 60-, and 70-mL fills, not a single volume):

| CR Category | Defining Features |
|---|---|
| **Absent** (ACR) | **No contractions in the body** |
| **Diminished** | Contractions present, not meeting absent (ACR) / normal (NCR) / spastic (SCR) criteria, with **low** FLIP pressure (**<40 mmHg** at 60 mL) |
| **Disordered** | Contractions present, not meeting ACR / NCR / SCR criteria, with **high** FLIP pressure (**≥40 mmHg** at 60 mL) |
| **Normal** (NCR) | **More than 1** distinct antegrade contraction. Does **not** require the RAC pattern |
| **Spastic** (SCR) | **SOCs or sustained LES contractions** |

- The **40 mmHg** pressure separating Diminished from Disordered is the pressure recorded **at the same moment as the 60-mL EGJ-DI**. It is a deliberately dichotomous cut: extremes shift probability — **<30 mmHg** favours hypomotility, **>55 mmHg** favours spasm.
- The spectrum is **non-linear**: *Disordered* carries a lower probability of a spastic disorder than *Spastic*; *Diminished* a lower probability of clinically relevant hypocontractility than *Absent*.
- **RRC was removed as a criterion for Spastic CR in v2.0** (non-specific — also seen in mechanical obstruction; low-volume RRCs may be passive respiratory propagation).
- Spastic or disordered CR **may be reactive** to subtle mechanical obstruction → prompts endoscopic re-inspection and consideration of dilation.
- **AGA 2025 cut differs at exactly 40 mmHg:** AGA text puts diminished at **≤40** and disordered at **>40 mmHg** (its Table 1: <40 vs >40 mmHg pressure increase); this page follows the Dallas <40 / ≥40 mmHg definition ([[aga-2025-flip-esophageal]]).
- "Vigorous antegrade contractions" (antegrade contraction of consistent width lasting ≥10 s with pressure rise >35 mmHg — definition did **not** reach 80% agreement) and other morphologies (isolated focal, sustained focal, triangle-, bridge-shaped) are **not** classification criteria — uncertain significance.

![[flip-2025-dallas-cr-hierarchy-09.png|650x333]]
*Figure 5 — Hierarchical approach to the contractile response category, assessed over the aggregate of the 50-, 60-, and 70-mL fills. SOCs = sustained occluding contractions; sLESCs = sustained LES contractions. The statements put diminished at <40 and disordered at ≥40 mmHg. ([[dallas-2025-flip-panometry]])*

![[flip-2025-aga-egj-cr-patterns-04.png|700x356]]
*Figure 1 (AGA) — Example FLIP panometry tracings: (A) NEO with RACs (normal); (B) REO with absent contractile response; (C) IEO with antegrade contractions; (D) REO with spasm. ([[aga-2025-flip-esophageal]])*

## FLIP Panometry Motility Classification v2.0

Matrix combining EGJ Opening × Contractile Response ([[dallas-2025-flip-panometry]] Figure 6; median 9.0, 88% agreement):

![[flip-2025-dallas-classification-v2-11.png|700x395]]
*Figure 6 — FLIP Panometry Motility Classification v2.0: EGJ opening (columns: normal, inconclusive, reduced) × contractile response (rows: spastic to absent). ([[dallas-2025-flip-panometry]])*

| Contractile Response | **NEO** | **IEO** | **REO** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Spastic** | Possible Spasm | Possible Obstruction* | Spastic Obstruction |
| **Disordered** | Possible Spasm | Possible Obstruction* | Spastic Obstruction |
| **Normal** | **Normal** | Possible Obstruction* | Obstruction with Normal Contractility |
| **Diminished** | Hypocontractility | Possible Obstruction* | Non-spastic Obstruction |
| **Absent** | Hypocontractility | Possible Obstruction* | Non-spastic Obstruction |

*\*With inconclusive EGJ opening the classification is "**possible obstruction**" and **must be further described by the contractile response category** (e.g. "possible obstruction with diminished contractile response"). Complementary testing — timed barium esophagram (TBE) with tablet and/or HRM — is typically required to resolve it.*

Clinical correlates:

| Classification | Correlate |
|---|---|
| **Normal** | Major motor disorder unlikely (median 9.0, 94% agreement) |
| **Hypocontractility** | Diminished CR carries a lower probability of clinically relevant hypocontractility than absent CR; consider HRM if hypocontractility suspected (AGA) — e.g. [[ineffective-esophageal-motility\|ineffective esophageal motility]] |
| **Possible Spasm** | May support HRM to define the type and extent of spastic activity (e.g. [[distal-esophageal-spasm\|distal esophageal spasm (DES)]], [[hypercontractile-esophagus\|hypercontractile esophagus]]); spastic or disordered CR may also be reactive to subtle mechanical obstruction |
| **Spastic Obstruction** | SOCs are associated with spastic disorders such as [[achalasia]] type III; HRM to define type and extent of spastic activity — AGA: to tailor therapy to the length of the spastic segment. AGA Table 1: REO + spastic or disordered CR → **spastic achalasia is likely** |
| **Obstruction with Normal Contractility** | Suggests **mechanical** obstruction — re-inspect for subtle stricture/ring, consider dilation (AGA Table 1: mechanical obstruction is possible) |
| **Non-spastic Obstruction** | Supports a disorder of [[esophagogastric-junction-outflow-obstruction\|EGJ outflow]] (median 8.5, 94% agreement); may suffice for a definitive diagnosis with supportive clinical data, even if HRM alone is inconclusive; discordant tests → more testing. AGA Table 1: REO + absent or diminished CR → **nonspastic achalasia is likely** |
| **Possible Obstruction** (IEO) | AGA Table 1: with any CR pattern, **motor and mechanical obstruction remain possible** — additional evaluation needed to define the mechanism |

AGA 2025: **EGJ metrics drive the final diagnosis** of obstruction vs no obstruction; the contractile response characterizes secondary peristalsis (full AGA Table 1: [[aga-2025-flip-esophageal#Table 1 — Use of FLIP Findings in Making Obstructive Structural and Motor Diagnoses]]). Spastic responses may be primary or **secondary** to hiatus hernia or mucosal inflammation (e.g., [[eosinophilic-esophagitis|eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE)]]).

**A spastic or inconclusive FLIP classification should prompt further workup — HRM and/or TBE with tablet** (median 9.0, 81% agreement).

## Evidence-Based Clinical Applications

### Normal FLIP

- In the largest cohort (**722 subjects** with manometry), **86** had a normal FLIP (NCR + NEO). Of those: **92% normal motility**, **6%** absent contractility/IEM, **2%** DES or hypercontractile esophagus, and **no achalasia or EGJOO** on HRM.
- In 687 symptomatic patients, NEO was found in **<1%** of the 243 with HRM achalasia or TBE-confirmed conclusive EGJOO (AGA); Dallas reports the same cohort as **99% of 203 with NEO** having normal EGJ outflow on HRM. In another cohort, **0 of 23** patients with achalasia had NEO.
- In a cohort of **706** patients, **none of 108** with a normal contractile response had achalasia or absent contractility.
- Among 17 patients with HRM-only EGJOO and NEO on FLIP, **none** had a ≥5-cm barium column at 5 minutes on TBE, and 9 of 10 followed longitudinally did well on conservative treatment (AGA); Dallas: **90%** of NEO + inconclusive-EGJOO patients given conservative non-achalasia therapy reported good outcomes.
- **Caution:** absent secondary peristalsis on FLIP is reported in **23%–43%** of patients with normal primary peristalsis on HRM — so an absent CR alongside NEO does not by itself define a motor disorder.

### Non-spastic Obstruction (REO + Absent/Diminished CR)

- Among **202** patients with diminished/absent CR **and** REO, **92% (185/202)** had HRM findings consistent with **achalasia or conclusive (TBE-confirmed) EGJOO**; **77%** had abnormal emptying on TBE.
- Positive predictive value (PPV) **>75%** for a manometric EGJ outflow disorder when EGJ-DI <2 **and** diameter <12 mm; NPV **>90%** when those criteria are not met.
- One study: EGJ-DI <2 mm²/mmHg had **sensitivity 80%**, diameter <12 mm had **specificity 90%**, for an abnormal TBE.
- Prospective data: **odds ratio (OR) 22.5 (95% confidence interval [CI] 2.5–206.7)** for symptomatic response to LES botulinum toxin in patients with diminished/absent CR.
- Among 139 patients meeting manometric EGJOO criteria, **72%** with conclusive FLIP for an EGJ outflow disorder had diminished/absent CR — and these had more abnormal TBE emptying and higher success with sphincter-directed therapy.

### Obstruction with Normal Contractility (REO + Normal CR)

- Suggests a **mechanical** rather than primary motor etiology.
- **Prompt endoscopic re-inspection for a subtle stricture or ring, and consider dilation**, before manometry-directed therapy.

### Spastic Classifications

- SOCs and sustained LES contractions are associated with spastic motility disorders such as achalasia type III (Dallas).
- A spastic or inconclusive FLIP classification → **HRM and/or TBE with tablet** (median 9.0, 81%); HRM characterizes type and extent of spastic activity, which AGA uses to tailor therapy to the length of the spastic segment.

## CARS Score — Endoscopic Pretest Probability of Achalasia

The AGA algorithm branches on whether the **endoscopy** is suggestive of achalasia, quantified by the **CARS** score ([[aga-2025-flip-esophageal]] Supplementary Table 1). Scored during a high-quality [[upper-endoscopy|upper endoscopy]] **after excluding strictures and other mechanical causes of dysphagia**:

| Points | **C**ontents | **A**natomy | **R**esistance (to scope passage across EGJ) | **S**tasis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | none | non-dilated lumen | no resistance | no stasis changes or [[infectious-esophagitis\|Candida esophagitis]] |
| **1** | liquid content | dilated lumen | mild resistance | chronic mucosal stasis changes · Candida esophagitis (each listed as its own 1-point item) |
| **2** | solid content | severely dilated lumen | severe resistance | — |

- Sum of the 4 components; total score **0–8**.
- **Optimal threshold for achalasia ≥4.0** (receiver operating characteristic [ROC]-derived): sensitivity **68%**, specificity **99%**, PPV **93%**, NPV **94%** (sensitivity reported as 68%–72% across studies).
- **CARS 0–1 + NEO** on FLIP → NPV **100%** for achalasia spectrum disorders.
- **CARS ≥4 + REO** on FLIP → PPV **97%** for **non-spastic** achalasia.
- **Best Practice Advice (BPA) 5:** achalasia with **absent or diminished** CR can be diagnosed when the clinical presentation is complemented by an abnormal CARS score and REO. Diagnostic uncertainty or FLIP suggesting **spasm** → HRM and/or TBE **before irreversible LES disruption**.
- Endoscopy also supplies pretest probability for separating idiopathic achalasia from **pseudoachalasia**.

## AGA Clinical Algorithm

Practical use of FLIP, keyed to the Best Practice Advice numbers ([[aga-2025-flip-esophageal]] Figure 2):

![[flip-2025-aga-clinical-algorithm-05.png|700x372]]
*Figure 2 (AGA) — Practical clinical algorithm for using and interpreting FLIP: indications (left), high-quality EGD first with mechanical obstruction treated accordingly (right), then FLIP read as NEO, REO, or IEO against whether the EGD is suggestive of achalasia (CARS score ≥4) or not. ([[aga-2025-flip-esophageal]])*

**Who gets FLIP**

- Symptoms suspicious for achalasia (BPA 2)
- Obstructive symptoms with **inconclusive** HRM/TBE (BPA 2)
- Persistent [[dysphagia]] in treated achalasia (BPA 10)
- Persistent dysphagia in treated EoE (BPA 7)
- Dysphagia after [[antireflux-surgery|antireflux surgery (ARS)]] or bariatric intervention (BPA 11)
- Index esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD) for dysphagia, after exclusions, when expertise and equipment are readily available (BPA 2)
- Also when HRM fails or is likely to: catheter cannot traverse the LES, discomfort/gagging prevents an adequate study, or other tests show a dilated or tortuous esophagus
- Rationale: test discordance is common — concordance of HRM, FLIP, and TBE for LES obstruction was only **57%** (126 patients); concordant findings on >1 test raise confidence before **irreversible LES disruption**, and conservative treatment is used if a confident achalasia spectrum diagnosis cannot be made. FLIP is typically done **before** any dilation.

**First — high-quality EGD (BPA 1, 3).** Withholding [[proton-pump-inhibitors|proton pump inhibitors (PPIs)]] 2–4 weeks raises the yield for erosive reflux change and EoE. If it shows **mechanical obstruction, Los Angeles (LA) grade B–D erosive esophagitis (BPA 8), stricture, EoE, or hiatus hernia >3 cm** → treat accordingly and FLIP may be cancelled (consider FLIP in EoE, BPA 7).

- The **Los Angeles grade A–D mucosal-break criteria** are shown in [[acg-2025-egd-quality|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG)/American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2025]] **Figure 2, "Los Angeles classification scheme for erosive esophagitis"** (and [[aga-2024-upper-endoscopy-quality|AGA 2024]] Figure 2); see [[reflux-testing]] for the LA grade.

Then read FLIP against the endoscopy:

| FLIP | **EGD not suggestive of achalasia** (e.g. hiatus hernia; CARS <4) | **EGD suggestive of achalasia** (CARS ≥4) |
|---|---|---|
| **NEO** | High NPV for achalasia (BPA 4). Test/treat for [[gerd\|gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)]] or a [[disorders-of-gut-brain-interaction\|disorder of gut-brain interaction (DGBI)]]; consider HRM if spasm or hypocontractility suspected | High NPV for achalasia (BPA 4). Consider HRM for a disorder of peristalsis; consider TBE for anatomy/mechanical obstruction |
| **REO** | Achalasia or obstructive disorder **possible** (BPA 6). Endoscopic re-evaluation, dilate a stricture if found; consider HRM and/or TBE | Achalasia or obstructive disorder **confirmed** (BPA 5). Absent/diminished CR → LES-directed therapy; spastic CR → HRM to tailor therapy to the length of the spastic segment; consider intra-procedure FLIP (BPA 9) |
| **IEO** | Achalasia or obstructive disorder **less likely** (BPA 6). Endoscopic re-evaluation, dilate a stricture if found; consider HRM and/or TBE | Achalasia or obstructive disorder **possible** (BPA 6). Endoscopic re-evaluation, dilate a stricture if found; consider HRM and/or TBE |

- Standard endoscopy sedation (propofol/fentanyl/midazolam) does **not** meaningfully alter FLIP findings (BPA 3). **Avoid inhalation anesthetics** when assessing motility: sevoflurane can raise EGJ diameter and EGJ-DI vs propofol and impair secondary peristalsis; desflurane/isoflurane effects unknown. See [[endoscopy-sedation]].
- **Do not use FLIP to diagnose [[gerd|GERD]]** or to decide on antireflux intervention (BPA 8) — no discriminative EGJ-DI threshold separates symptomatic GERD from controls, and EGJ-DI does not correlate with acid exposure time. Need for antireflux intervention is set by symptoms and reflux burden; FLIP can identify achalasia and obstructive syndromes that refute reflux disease in PPI nonresponders.
- **EoE (BPA 7):** FLIP measures esophageal remodeling — *distensibility plateau* (static diameter despite rising distension volume/pressure) is lower in patients with prior food impaction than dysphagia alone, and low DI/plateau tracks with symptom duration and diagnostic delay. FLIP **should not be used to diagnose EoE**; use it when the mechanism of persisting dysphagia is unclear on endoscopy/histology. An **achalasia-like pattern** can occur in EoE (possibly fibrostenotic pseudoachalasia) → irreversible LES-disruptive treatment only cautiously and only if supported by complementary tests.
- **Contraindicated with actively bleeding [[variceal-upper-gi-bleeding|varices]].**
- FLIP identifies hiatal hernia with **84% correlation** to HRM, but **elicits or enlarges** a hernia in up to **25%**.

## FLIP After Foregut Intervention

Where the numbers actually change management ([[aga-2025-flip-esophageal]]):

| Setting | Metric | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **Intra-procedural**, during [[poem\|peroral endoscopic myotomy (POEM)]]/[[heller-myotomy\|laparoscopic Heller myotomy (LHM)]] (BPA 9) | EGJ-DI **<2–3 mm²/mmHg** | May predict persisting symptoms → **may prompt additional myotomy** |
| Intra-procedural | **High** EGJ-DI | Associated with post-op reflux / erosive esophagitis after LHM or POEM |
| **Persistent obstructive symptoms after achalasia treatment** (BPA 10) | EGJ-DI **<2–3 mm²/mmHg** | Higher **Eckardt** scores (score structure and bands: [[achalasia]]), suboptimal emptying → ineffective/incomplete LES disruption → may support **additional LES-directed therapy** |
| Same setting | **NEO** | Look for alternate explanations — esophageal body anatomy, GERD |
| **After ARS** (BPA 11) | EGJ-DI **<2 mm²/mmHg** | Associated with dysphagia symptoms (cohort of 175) |
| Intra-operative target during ARS | **Toupet 2.6–3.7 mm²/mmHg**; **Nissen >2.2 mm²/mmHg** | *Proposed* ranges — may guide deferring fundoplication, changing ARS type, or loosening/tightening the wrap |

- EGJ-DI rises dramatically during/immediately after POEM or LHM, then falls by 12 months but stays above pre-op values. Ideal EGJ-DI targets after LES myotomy, and FLIP's role in guiding myotomy length (e.g., type III achalasia), are not yet defined.
- During [[pneumatic-dilation|pneumatic dilation]], data are less robust, but FLIP has been found to assess adequacy of dilation.
- **Post-ARS obstructive symptoms:** provided EGJ outflow disorders were ruled out **before** the intervention, FLIP can guide management, including **EGJ dilation when symptoms are confirmed by REO**.
- Tightness of **hiatal closure** affects DI more than the fundoplication itself.
- Among patients undergoing FLIP for symptoms after ARS, abnormal secondary peristalsis was found in **84%**, worsening with longer post-ARS duration and/or EGJ outflow resistance.
- After **Roux-en-Y gastric bypass (RYGB) or sleeve gastrectomy** ([[bariatric-surgery]]), secondary dysmotility is more common than in obese patients with native anatomy — most often **absent contractility**.
- Magnetic sphincter augmentation (limited data): lower diameter and EGJ-DI than ARS with similar symptomatic outcomes in one study; more postoperative dysphagia than partial or complete fundoplication despite similar EGJ-DI in another.
- Proposed ARS ranges are not uniformly predictive: another study found no FLIP metric differences between patients with and without suboptimal outcomes at up to 2 years.

## FLIP vs. HRM

| Feature | FLIP Panometry | HRM |
|---|---|---|
| Performed when | During sedated endoscopy | Separate awake/lightly sedated procedure |
| EGJ opening | Directly measured (EGJ-DI, max diameter) | Inferred from integrated relaxation pressure (IRP) and intrabolus pressure (IBP) |
| Motor pattern | Contractile response to distension | Peristaltic response to wet swallows |
| Achalasia exclusion | Normal FLIP: major motor disorder unlikely; NPV >90% for EGJ outflow disorder when REO criteria not met | Defines achalasia subtypes ([[chicago-classification-v4\|Chicago Classification]]) |
| EGJOO | PPV >75% with REO | Defined by IRP + IBP criteria |
| Expertise required | FLIP panometry interpretation | Chicago Classification 4.0 interpretation |
| Complementarity | Often used together; FLIP adds at-scope information | Provides subtype detail FLIP cannot |

## Key Clinical Rules

1. **FLIP does not replace HRM for achalasia subtyping** — HRM ([[chicago-classification-v4|Chicago Classification v4.0]]) defines Type I/II/III and guides [[poem|POEM]] vs. [[pneumatic-dilation|pneumatic dilation]] vs. [[heller-myotomy|LHM]] selection
2. **Normal FLIP (NEO + normal CR): major motor disorder unlikely** (94% agreement) — high NPV for achalasia; direct management toward GERD or a functional disorder
3. **REO + absent/diminished CR supports an EGJ outflow disorder** (94% agreement) — may be enough for a definitive diagnosis with supportive clinical data; discordant endoscopy/HRM/esophagram → more testing
4. **Inconclusive EGJ opening = possible obstruction** — describe by CR category; HRM and/or TBE with tablet (81% agreement)
5. **REO + normal CR = obstruction with normal contractility** — suggests mechanical obstruction; re-inspect endoscopically for subtle stricture or ring and consider dilation
