## Platforms

Device-assisted enteroscopy (DAE), also called deep enteroscopy (DE), is the technique of choice for tissue acquisition and therapy in the **midgut** — the gastrointestinal (GI) tract between the ampulla of Vater and the ileocecal valve ([[asge-2015-deep-enteroscopy]]). Three platforms are in use; the figures below come from the American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2015 deep enteroscopy guideline and the American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2015 small bowel bleeding guideline.

| Platform | How it advances | Routes | Depth of insertion ([[asge-2015-deep-enteroscopy\|ASGE 2015]]) | Total enteroscopy rate ([[asge-2015-deep-enteroscopy\|ASGE 2015]]) | Mean antegrade procedure time ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding\|ACG 2015]]) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Double-balloon enteroscopy (DBE)** | Balloon on both scope tip and overtube; push-and-pull advancement cycles | Antegrade + retrograde | 240–360 cm past the ligament of Treitz; 102–140 cm past the ileocecal valve | **0%–86%** across series (highest in Asian populations); pooled **44%** | **79 ± 15 min** (10 studies) |
| **Single-balloon enteroscopy (SBE)** | Silicone balloon on the overtube only; the scope tip hooks a fold during reduction | Antegrade + retrograde | 133–256 cm past the ligament of Treitz; 73–163 cm past the ileocecal valve | **15%–25%** | **65 ± 16 min** (5 studies) |
| **Spiral enteroscopy (SE)** | Raised spiral ridge on the overtube; clockwise rotation draws the scope forward; needs an enteroscope <9.4 mm in diameter | Antegrade (a retrograde overtube exists, for limited ileoscopy) | Mean 176–250 cm | **Very low** — the main limitation, from difficult retrograde passage | **35 ± 6 min** (4 trials) |

- **Any method of DAE can be used** when endoscopic evaluation and therapy are required, based on similar diagnostic yields *(ACG 2015: strong recommendation, high level of evidence)* [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]].
- **DE is an effective and safe technique** for small-bowel examination *(ASGE 2015: recommend; High)*.
- **DBE is the most effective platform for achieving total enteroscopy** *(ASGE 2015: recommend; Moderate)* — a prospective randomized study confirmed a significantly higher total-enteroscopy rate for DBE than SBE ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]).
- **Either DBE or SBE** is acceptable for **retrograde** enteroscopy *(ASGE 2015: suggest; Low)*.
- SBE's practical advantages over DBE: shorter setup, one balloon cycle instead of two, simpler control panel, non-latex balloon ([[asge-2015-deep-enteroscopy]]).
- **Short DBE model:** difficult ileocolonoscopy, [[ercp|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]] in surgically altered anatomy, and proximal small-bowel endoscopy — standard-length accessories can be used ([[asge-2015-deep-enteroscopy]]).
- **NaviAid** (through-the-scope balloon on a standard adult colonoscope): limited data — ASGE 2015 makes **no recommendation**.

> [!pitfall] Latex allergy rules out double-balloon
> The DBE scope-tip and overtube balloons are **latex**; the SBE overtube balloon is **silicone**. For patients with latex allergy, SBE should be performed ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]).

## Indications

### Small-Bowel Bleeding

Indications for DBE in suspected small-bowel bleeding ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]):

- **Positive [[capsule-endoscopy|video capsule endoscopy (VCE)]]** — for tissue diagnosis and therapeutics
- **VCE contraindicated**
- **Negative VCE but high clinical suspicion** of a small-bowel lesion
- **Active bleeding**

- **ASGE 2017 adds:** DAE or push enteroscopy when VCE is **unavailable or nondiagnostic** in overt small-bowel bleeding; a positive VCE is followed by push enteroscopy if the lesion is within reach, otherwise DAE *(suggest; Low)* [[asge-2017-small-bowel-bleeding]].
- **DAE as the initial small-bowel procedure** in select circumstances — high suspicion of small-bowel [[angioectasia|angiectasias]] or surgically altered anatomy *(ASGE 2017 and ASGE 2015: suggest; Low)*.
- **VCE before DAE, whenever possible.** VCE-guided ("targeted") DAE raises diagnostic yield to **73–93%** and therapeutic yield to **57–73%** ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]). The graded recommendation on VCE-before-DAE sequencing and its exceptions lives on [[capsule-endoscopy]].
- **Treat what you find:** a source found by VCE and/or deep enteroscopy that is associated with significant ongoing anemia or active bleeding should be managed with endoscopic therapy *(ACG 2015: strong recommendation, low level of evidence)*.

**Timing — earlier appears better, on limited data** ([[asge-2017-small-bowel-bleeding]], ungraded):

- **Early DBE (within 24 h)** of overt small-bowel bleeding: 90% diagnostic and therapeutic yield (10 patients).
- **Urgent DBE (within 72 h of the last visible GI bleeding)** vs non-urgent: diagnostic yield **70% vs 30%** (P < .05; retrospective, 120 patients), with lower recurrent bleeding.
- ACG 2015 concurs that emergency DBE is technically feasible in massive overt small-bowel hemorrhage, and that **repeat DBE from the same direction** may help when the prior DBE was positive.

> [!pearl] A negative capsule does not end the workup
> VCE has a false-negative rate of **11%** for all small-bowel findings and **up to 19% for neoplasms**. With a negative VCE but high clinical suspicion, pursue DBE anyway — including consideration of total enteroscopy ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]). Both of the guideline's false-negative figures, and how they differ, are set out on [[capsule-endoscopy]].

### Small-Bowel Tumors

- DE for **tissue diagnosis and therapy** when a tumor is detected by another test (eg, VCE), or when suspicion is high despite negative initial testing *(ASGE 2015: suggest; Low)*.
- DE also permits **tattoo placement** to localize a small-bowel tumor for surgery ([[asge-2015-deep-enteroscopy]]).

### Crohn's Disease

- DE when imaging shows abnormalities **within reach of the enteroscope** — allows endoscopic and histologic evaluation plus therapeutics (hemostasis, stricture dilation, foreign-body retrieval) *(ASGE 2015: suggest; Low)*. Therapeutic DE can delay or prevent surgery in [[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease]] with small-bowel strictures.
- The 2025 ASGE interventional inflammatory bowel disease consensus **could not agree** on a statement recommending push or device-assisted enteroscopy in suspected small-bowel Crohn's disease when ileocolonoscopy and small-bowel imaging are not feasible or inconclusive ([[asge-2025-ibd-endoscopy-consensus]]).

### Polyposis Syndromes

- **[[familial-adenomatous-polyposis|Familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP)]]:** jejunal or ileal polyps **>1 cm** found on capsule endoscopy or magnetic resonance enterography (MRE) → DBE or SBE for [[polypectomy]] *(ASGE 2020: Very low)*. Deep enteroscopy is **not** recommended for routine small-bowel screening; reserve it for therapeutic intent — a suspected advanced jejunal or ileal polyp on capsule endoscopy or MRE, symptomatic patients, and preoperative screening before duodenal surgery ([[asge-2020-fap]]). The FAP small-bowel surveillance schedule lives on [[capsule-endoscopy]].
- **[[peutz-jeghers-syndrome|Peutz-Jeghers syndrome]]:** DE allows endoscopic resection of polyps **>1 cm** ([[asge-2015-deep-enteroscopy]]).

### Beyond the Reach of Enteroscopy

- **Fixed small bowel from prior resections, or large polyps** → laparoscopy-assisted DBE resects multiple polyps in one session, reducing small-bowel resections and the risk of [[short-bowel-syndrome|short bowel syndrome]] ([[asge-2015-deep-enteroscopy]]).
- **Lesion beyond maximal insertion depth, or DAE impossible without lysis of adhesions** → intraoperative enteroscopy, which is highly sensitive but invasive; its use should be limited to scenarios where enteroscopy cannot be performed, such as prior surgeries and intestinal adhesions *(ACG 2015: strong recommendation, low level of evidence)*.

See [[small-bowel-bleeding]] for the full small-bowel bleeding algorithm.

## Route Selection and Depth

The scope reaches only part of the small bowel from either end, so the route is chosen to put the target lesion within reach — **capsule transit times** on the preceding VCE are what localize it ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]], [[asge-2015-deep-enteroscopy]]).

| Route | Reach with DBE ([[asge-2015-deep-enteroscopy\|ASGE 2015]]) | Choose it for |
|---|---|---|
| **Antegrade (oral)** | **240–360 cm** past the ligament of Treitz | Lesions in the **proximal two-thirds** of the small bowel (ASGE 2015) — ACG 2015 says **proximal 75%**. Deeper intubation and a higher success rate make this the **preferred** route |
| **Retrograde (rectal)** | **102–140 cm** past the ileocecal valve | **More distal** lesions — the **distal one-third** (ASGE 2015) |

For comparison, ACG 2015 puts push enteroscopy at **90–150 cm** and ileoscopy at **50–80 cm**.

> [!pitfall] Same number, two different landmarks
> ASGE 2015 gives the antegrade depth as 240–360 cm past the **ligament of Treitz**; ACG 2015 gives the same 240–360 cm **distal to the pylorus**. The landmarks are not interchangeable — this page follows ASGE 2015, whose subject is deep enteroscopy. Both agree on 102–140 cm proximal to the ileocecal valve retrograde.

**Total enteroscopy** = intubation of the entire small bowel by one or both routes ([[asge-2015-deep-enteroscopy]]).

- **Attempt total deep enteroscopy if there is a strong suspicion of a small-bowel lesion based on clinical presentation** *(ACG 2015: strong recommendation, moderate level of evidence)*.
- Also indicated for multiple small-bowel lesions, a negative initial DBE, or high clinical suspicion after a nondiagnostic VCE ([[asge-2015-deep-enteroscopy]]).
- Pooled DBE total-enteroscopy rate **44%** by combined antegrade-plus-retrograde or antegrade-only approach (systematic review, 66 articles / 12,823 DBE procedures; overall diagnostic yield 68.1%, vascular lesions the most common finding at 66%) ([[asge-2015-deep-enteroscopy]]).

## Technique

### Setup

- Enteroscope working length **200 cm**, outer diameter **9.4 mm**; overtube **140 cm**. The **2.8 mm** accessory channel passes virtually all standard-caliber through-the-scope diagnostic and therapeutic instruments ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]).
- **General anesthesia is often used for antegrade** procedures; **retrograde** procedures are usually done under **moderate sedation** ([[asge-2015-deep-enteroscopy]]).
- **Additional personnel are required** to handle the overtube. Spiral enteroscopy is a two-person procedure — an assistant rotates the overtube while the endoscopist keeps the small-bowel lumen in view ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]).
- **Carbon dioxide insufflation:** post-procedure bloating and abdominal pain were once common but are now rarely reported, because CO₂ diffuses rapidly across the intestinal mucosa ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]).

### Advancement Cycle

> [!howto] Push-and-pull cycle (DBE)
> 1. Pass the enteroscope and overtube into the small bowel, typically past the ampulla; inflate the **overtube** balloon.
> 2. Advance the enteroscope further into the small bowel.
> 3. Inflate the **enteroscope** balloon (DBE). With SBE there is no distal balloon — hook the flexed scope tip over a fold instead.
> 4. Advance the overtube over the enteroscope.
> 5. Draw both back together, with both balloons inflated — the small bowel plicates over the enteroscope.
> 6. Repeat; each cycle traverses more bowel than conventional endoscopy ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]).

### Troubleshooting

- **Duodenojejunal transition** is the technical obstacle for spiral enteroscopy — the sharp angulation may stop the overtube from safely engaging the proximal jejunum for forward passage ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]).
- **Deep mucosal tears or perforation with SBE** are more likely if the endoscope tip is flexed while the overtube is advanced, particularly with adhesions from prior abdominal surgery or anastomotic strictures. Continuous **"power suction"** may reduce injury from the inverted-tip technique ([[asge-2015-deep-enteroscopy]]).
- **Perforation is more common** in patients with an intestinal anastomosis and after small-bowel [[polypectomy]] ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]).

## Therapeutics Deliverable at Enteroscopy

What makes DAE worth doing rather than repeating imaging — it is the only modality that can both see and treat the midgut ([[asge-2015-deep-enteroscopy]], [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]):

- Biopsy, mucosal injection, and tattoo placement
- **[[polypectomy]]** and endoscopic mucosal resection
- **Stricture dilation** (eg, Crohn's strictures within reach)
- **Hemostasis** — [[argon-plasma-coagulation|argon plasma coagulation (APC)]], electrocoagulation, hemoclips (used for small-bowel angiectasias)
- **Palliative stenting** (for small-bowel tumors)
- **Foreign-body / retained-capsule retrieval**

## Adverse Events

| Platform / setting | Rate | Source |
|---|---|---|
| DBE, **diagnostic** procedures | **0.8%** | [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding\|ACG 2015]] |
| DBE, **with therapeutics** (electrocoagulation, polypectomy, dilation) | up to **4%** | [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding\|ACG 2015]] |
| DBE, overall (large prospective database) | **1.2%** | [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding\|ACG 2015]] |
| DBE, severe adverse events (AEs) with therapeutic DBE | **3%–4%** (overall DBE AE rate ~1%) | [[asge-2015-deep-enteroscopy\|ASGE 2015]] |
| DBE, mortality | **0.05%** | [[asge-2015-deep-enteroscopy\|ASGE 2015]] |
| [[acute-pancreatitis\|Pancreatitis]] — most common complication of **peroral diagnostic** DBE | at least **0.3%** | [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding\|ACG 2015]] |
| SBE, diagnostic | ~**1%**, equivalent to diagnostic DBE | [[asge-2015-deep-enteroscopy\|ASGE 2015]] |
| SE — severe AEs / small-bowel perforation | **0.34%** / **0.27%** (retrospective registry, 1750 patients) | [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding\|ACG 2015]] |
| Deep enteroscopy, all indications — **minor** / **major** AEs | **9.1%** / **0.72%** (systematic review, 12,823 DBE procedures) | [[asge-2015-deep-enteroscopy\|ASGE 2015]] |

- Main DBE complications: **ileus, pancreatitis, and perforation**, the last usually associated with large polypectomies ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]).
- Adding therapy multiplies the DBE complication rate severalfold (0.8% → up to 4%) — a reason to have a VCE-localized target before booking the procedure.
