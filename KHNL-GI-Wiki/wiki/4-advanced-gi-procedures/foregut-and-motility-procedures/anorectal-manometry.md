## Overview

- Measures **rectal sensation** and **anorectal pressures** at rest, during anal/pelvic-floor contraction (squeeze), during evacuation, and on cough or Valsalva. ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]])
- Anorectal manometry (ARM) **plus** a [[#Balloon Expulsion Test (BET)|balloon expulsion test (BET)]] is the standard functional workup for [[defecation-disorders|defecation disorders (DD)]] — American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) key concept: *"ARM and balloon expulsion are required to diagnose DD."*
- **ARM with BET is generally sufficient to diagnose a DD**, and also **predicts who will benefit from [[biofeedback-therapy|biofeedback]]** — American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2026. The same document applies a stricter bar where the decision is to call constipation *refractory* and move toward surgery: **2 of 3 abnormal tests (ARM, BET, defecography)**. ([[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]])
- **No single test is a gold standard.** ARM, BET, and magnetic resonance (MR) defecography are concordant at levels of agreement **>70%**, but individual patients may disagree across tests.
- **How many abnormal tests it takes has changed — and the two live rules disagree.** Rome IV required **≥2 abnormal tests**; **[[rome-v-2026-dgbi|Rome V]] (2026) requires difficult-evacuation symptoms (e.g., straining, digital maneuvers) plus only 1 of 3 abnormal tests** — balloon expulsion, manometry, **or** imaging — and has **dropped anal electromyography (EMG)** as a criterion. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders|ACG 2021]] still requires **ARM *and* BET** (key concept above). Same tier; Rome V is the newer publication, so this page follows the looser 1-of-3 bar, while ACG's both-tests rule remains the stricter operating standard in an ACG-following lab. Version table: [[defecation-disorders]].
- Sequencing, the DD diagnostic algorithm, and defecography indications live on [[defecation-disorders]]; incontinence workup on [[fecal-incontinence]]. Do not read this page as the algorithm — it covers the test itself.
- **Digital rectal examination should be performed before referral for ARM.** ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]])

## Indications

| Indication | What the source says |
|---|---|
| Suspected DD / refractory constipation | ARM + BET **required** to diagnose DD ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]) |
| [[irritable-bowel-syndrome\|Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)]] with pelvic-floor symptoms | *"We suggest that anorectal physiology testing be performed in patients with IBS and symptoms suggestive of a pelvic floor disorder and/or refractory constipation not responsive to standard medical therapy."* **Consensus recommendation; unable to assess using Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE)** ([[acg-2020-ibs]]). Anorectal dysfunction occurs in **all IBS subtypes**, prevalence estimated **up to 40%** in tertiary care |
| [[fecal-incontinence\|Fecal incontinence (FI)]] | ARM, rectal BET, and rectal sensation **should be performed in patients who fail to respond to conservative measures**; anal canal imaging and anal EMG added for anal weakness ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]) |
| Levator syndrome ([[proctalgia-syndromes]]) | ARM and BET **should be performed** in levator syndrome to identify patients eligible for biofeedback — **strongly recommended despite low-quality evidence**; *not* indicated in idiopathic chronic proctalgia syndrome ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]) |
| Before labelling constipation **refractory** | *"Most patients with CC [chronic constipation] should be evaluated with anorectal manometry (ARM) with balloon expulsion testing (BET) and complete a course of pelvic floor biofeedback therapy, when indicated, before being labeled as having RC [refractory constipation]."* **Best practice advice (BPA) 2** ([[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]]) — i.e. ARM+BET is a gate on the refractory-constipation diagnosis itself, not just a workup option. Pathway: [[chronic-constipation]] |
| Suspected Hirschsprung disease | *"Suspected Hirschsprung's disease requires anorectal manometry and deep biopsy to examine for the absence of myenteric neurons."* ([[asge-2014-constipation]]) |

**Findings on anorectal physiology testing that suggest a pelvic floor disorder** (Table 6, [[acg-2020-ibs]]) — note the guideline's own caveat that many of these are seen across **all IBS subtypes and are not specific** to dyssynergia:

- Uncoordinated abdominal, rectoanal, and pelvic floor muscles
- Rectal hyposensitivity
- Paradoxical increase in sphincter/puborectalis pressure during relaxation or simulated evacuation
- Prolonged balloon expulsion time
- Inadequate anal relaxation during push
- Inadequate abdominorectal propulsive forces

## Technique

### Catheters

| Type | Sensors | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Conventional | Water-perfused, air-charged, or solid-state | Requires a **pull-through maneuver** to profile the anal canal |
| High-resolution (HRM) / high-definition (3D) | **More closely spaced sensors straddling the entire anal canal** | Better spatial resolution; **no pull-through needed** |

- Measurements with conventional and HRM catheters are **comparable**, but **absolute values are greater with HRM/high-definition than with conventional catheters**. See [[high-resolution-manometry]] for the manometric platform generally.

### Patient Position

- Recent studies suggest **seated manometry may discriminate healthy people from DD better than left-lateral**; confirmatory studies awaited, but ACG states performance in the **seated position might be considered** if feasible.
- The **International Anorectal Physiology Working Group (IAPWG)** protocol instead specifies **left lateral, hips and knees flexed**, for both manometry and rectal sensory testing. ([[london-2020-anorectal-classification]])

### IAPWG Standardized Protocol

*The sequence as a whole reached level 1 consensus (>90% unanimity). Total study time for manometry + rectal sensory test (RST) + BET is expected to be **15–20 minutes**. Maneuver durations and the recovery intervals between maneuvers are themselves part of the protocol.* All from [[london-2020-anorectal-classification]].

| Order | Maneuver | Duration / number | Recovery interval | What it measures |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stabilization** | 3 min | — | Lets anal tone return to baseline after intubation; **no maneuver before it** |
| 2 | **Rest** | 60 s | — | Basal anal tone; patient reminded to relax and stay quiet (movement artifact). Ultraslow waves 0.5–2 cycles/min may appear |
| 3 | **Short squeeze** | 3 × 5 s | 30 s between squeezes | Voluntary anal/pelvic-floor contraction; **best of three** analyzed |
| 4 | **Long (endurance) squeeze** | 1 × 30 s | 60 s after | **Fatigue over time**, not contractile strength |
| 5 | **Cough** | 2 | 30 s between | Reflex anal pressure rise on abrupt intra-abdominal pressure change. Each must be a **single** cough; **best = greatest rise in rectal pressure** |
| 6 | **Push** | 3 × 15 s | 30 s between | Simulated defecation. Rectal balloon insufflation **not mandated**. Because false positives are common, the **most qualitatively normal** attempt is analyzed |
| 7 | **Rectal sensory testing** | — | — | FCSV, DDV, MTV (see below) |
| 8 | **RAIR** | 1, starting volume **≥30 mL** | — | Reflex anal pressure fall on rapid rectal distension. **If megarectum is suspected, repeat with increasing volumes** — a low distending volume in a large-capacity rectum can fail to elicit it |

- **BET is performed immediately before or after** this manometry/RST block.
- Push duration (3 × 15 s) is the **one element that reached no consensus** (<75% unanimity); everything else above is level 1 or 2.
- **Digital rectal examination before intubation** (also an ACG recommendation, above) — assess pelvic floor structure/function/sensitivity, exclude local pathology and fecal loading, and confirm the patient understands "squeeze" and "push".
- **Preparation:** continue all medications; eat and drink up to the time of the test; bowel preparation optional; a pre-procedure enema is **not contraindicated but must be documented** because it can alter function. Document any medication that affects anorectal function, **including analgesics**.

### Equipment Specifications

*IAPWG minimum standards ([[london-2020-anorectal-classification]]).*

| Component | Specification |
|---|---|
| Manometry sensors | **Circumferential, not unidirectional**, recording. **Minimum 6 cm longitudinal recording length.** Thin flexible catheters; rigid high-definition catheters **may** be used but are not standard |
| Manometry system | Recommendations are based on **solid-state systems — the single element that reached no consensus (<75%)** — and also apply to water-perfused high-resolution systems (moderate consensus) |
| Rectal balloon placement | Base **3–5 cm above the upper border of the anal canal**, so it cannot impinge on the anal canal during inflation; **most distal sensor outside the anal verge** |
| RST balloon | Capacity **≥400 mL** (maximum tolerated volume in health is no greater than 350 mL); **latex-free** |
| RST distension | Ramp **1–5 mL/s** or phasic **10 mL/s**, always **with air**. ⚠ **Ramp and phasic results are not interchangeable** |
| BET catheter | Flexible, **up to 16 Fr**, non-latex compliant balloon; **50 mL tepid water**; inserted left lateral, then **seated, ideally on a toilet in privacy** |

## Measurements Reported

*The IAPWG minimum reporting set ([[london-2020-anorectal-classification]]). Values are compared against the lab's own limits — see below.*

| Test | Measurement | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| ARM — rest | **Anal resting pressure** | Mean maximum pressure across the whole anal canal over the 60-s recording (mmHg) |
| ARM — rest | **Ultraslow waves** | Repeated anal pressure oscillations at 1–2/min (present/absent) |
| ARM — squeeze | **Anal squeeze pressure** | Maximum incremental pressure during the 5-s short squeeze (mmHg) |
| ARM — long squeeze | **Endurance squeeze pressure** | Time the patient sustains an anal pressure increase **>50% of maximum incremental squeeze pressure** over the 30-s squeeze (s) |
| ARM — push | **Rectal and anal pressure change during push** | Maximum pressure change in rectum and in anal canal during push (mmHg) |
| ARM — cough | **Rectal and anal pressure during cough** | Maximum pressure in rectum and anal canal during cough (mmHg) |
| ARM | **RAIR** | Reflex fall in maximum anal pressure on rapid rectal distension (present/absent) — **also record the volume that elicited it** |
| BET | **Balloon expulsion time** | Seconds to expel the balloon — **also record whether a desire to defecate was present** |
| RST | **FCSV** — first constant sensation volume | Minimum insufflation volume eliciting a sensory response (mL) |
| RST | **DDV** — desire to defecate volume | Volume eliciting a sustained desire to defecate (mL) |
| RST | **MTV** — maximum tolerated volume | Volume causing an intolerable desire to defecate (mL) |

- A fourth sensory threshold, **sustained urgency volume** (volume eliciting a sense of fecal urgency), is **optional**.

## Interpretation — Reference Values and Pitfalls

**This is the section that decides whether a study is abnormal. Every pitfall below is an ACG-stated reason not to call DD on manometry alone.**

- **Compare pressures only against reference values obtained with the same technique.** Because HRM/3D values run higher than conventional, cross-technique comparison invalidates the result.
- **Reference data are thin** — characterized in relatively few individuals, more in women than men; the largest cohort is **96 women** studied with the Medtronic HRM device.
- **Anal sphincter pressures decline with age and are lower in women** → interpret anal canal pressure **adjusted for age and sex**.
- **A negative rectoanal gradient is not by itself abnormal.** Intuitively normal evacuation should need rectal pressure > anal pressure, but many healthy people have a negative gradient: in **women <50 y**, the **10th-percentile reference value is −70 mm Hg** (Manoscan HRM).
- **37% of asymptomatic women fail to relax or paradoxically contract the pelvic floor during evacuation.**
- The same caution applies to decreased rectal pressures, paradoxical anal contraction, high anal pressure during evacuation, and high anal resting pressure.
- **Interpret with the clinical features** — false-positive and false-negative results are not uncommon.

### Assigning a Pattern — the London Classification

- **[[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders|ACG 2021]] and [[rome-v-2026-dgbi|Rome V]] say whether a study is abnormal; the [[london-classification|London Classification]] says what to call the pattern.** Neither guideline contains a manometric classification scheme, so the IAPWG consensus is where the named diagnoses come from. ([[london-2020-anorectal-classification]])
- Four parts — rectoanal inhibitory reflex, anal tone and contractility, rectoanal coordination, rectal sensation — and **one study may return a diagnosis under more than one**. Findings are tiered **major** (not seen in controls), **minor** (seen in patients and controls) or **inconclusive**. Full decision logic: [[london-classification]].
- Two consequences that change how this page's numbers are used:
  - **The classification publishes no reference values at all.** Every decision point is "above the upper limit of normal" or "below the lower limit of normal", referred to the lab's own data or to published data acquired with **identical equipment and protocol**, adjusted for sex, parity and age. This is the same technique-specific caution stated above, now a formal rule.
  - **Cough and long squeeze are recorded but excluded from the classification.** Both remain in the protocol; the consensus did not consider them justifiable as diagnostic criteria. An abnormal cough response may mark a more severe phenotype and a preserved one a biofeedback target — neither is a criterion.
- **Dyssynergia never rises above a minor finding** in this scheme, and an abnormal manometric coordination pattern with a *normal* balloon expulsion is only **inconclusive** — the practical statement of the false-positive problem listed above. It is the **BET**, not the manometric pattern, that carries the weight.
- The numbered **Rao dyssynergia types I–IV** are not part of the London Classification and are not in any ingested source; this page can assign a London diagnosis but not a Rao type.

## Balloon Expulsion Test (BET)

- **Measures the time to evacuate a balloon filled with 50 mL of warm water in the seated position.**

| Balloon used | Upper limit of normal | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Party or **commercial balloon** (Mui Scientific) — *most widely used and preferred* | **1 minute** | The reference standard technique |
| **Foley catheter** inflated to 50 mL | **2 minutes** | 50 mL is **above the manufacturer's recommended 30 mL limit**; even at the 2-min cutoff **25% of healthy people are misclassified as abnormal** → **ACG discourages Foley balloons** |

- **AGA 2026 uses the same bar** — a **prolonged BET (≥1 minute)** is one of its three abnormal tests for DD, alongside a reduced rectoanal gradient on ARM and inadequate rectal evacuation on defecography. ([[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]])
- ⚠ The cutoff is meaningless without its balloon type — a 1-minute threshold applied to a Foley study, or a 2-minute threshold applied to a commercial balloon, misclassifies patients.
- **Performance** (vs defecography, 106 functional constipation + 24 DD patients): sensitivity and specificity **~88%**; **positive predictive value (PPV) 64%, negative predictive value (NPV) 97%**.
- **Alternative filling:** rather than a fixed volume, inflate until the patient feels the desire to defecate (**averaged 183 mL**) — may compensate for the reduced rectal sensation seen in some DD patients.

## Defecography — Barium vs Magnetic Resonance (MR)

*Indications and findings: [[defecation-disorders]]. This section is the modality choice.* All from [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]].

- Order either modality when **clinical features suggest a structural abnormality**, or when **ARM and BET are inconclusive**.
- Each abnormal anorectal test — lower rectoanal gradient on manometry, prolonged BET, reduced evacuation on defecography — is **highly specific** for DD.

| | Barium defecography | MR defecography |
|---|---|---|
| Position | **Seated** — preferable when the primary question is rectal evacuation | **Supine** |
| Radiation | Yes | **None** |
| Strengths | Real-time evacuation assessment | More precise pelvic organ prolapse and pelvic floor motion; **especially useful for pelvic floor dysfunction in patients with clinical DD features and a normal BET** — a group that includes **>90% of patients with a large rectocele, enterocele and/or peritoneocele** |
| Availability / cost | Wider | **Less widely available, more expensive** |

## Adjunct — Anal EMG

- Average anal EMG activity recorded from electrodes on an acrylic anal plug or taped to perianal skin.
- **≥20% reduction in anal EMG activity during evacuation is considered normal** (data limited); **<20% reduction correlates with dyssynergic defecation** as identified by manometry and abnormal BET.
- ⚠ **EMG is an adjunct only, not a diagnostic criterion** — Rome V removed it from the dyssynergic-defecation criteria (see [[#Overview]]).

## Parameters That Matter, by Indication

| Setting | Key ARM parameters |
|---|---|
| **[[defecation-disorders\|Defecation disorder]]** | Rectal and anal pressures on evacuation, anal relaxation, **rectoanal gradient**, rectal sensation |
| **[[fecal-incontinence\|Fecal incontinence]]** | **Anal sphincter resting and squeeze pressures are the key parameters.** Resting pressure largely reflects **internal** anal sphincter; squeeze pressure reflects **external** anal sphincter. FI patients typically show reduced squeeze pressure, reduced squeeze duration, and/or reduced rectal capacity with rectal hypersensitivity; incontinent patients have lower resting pressures. Rectal sensation in FI may be **normal, increased, or decreased**. The **anal cough reflex** is qualitatively useful for lower-motor-neuron integrity of the external anal sphincter |

- Baseline ARM + BET **predict outcome and guide biofeedback therapy** ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]] key concept 7).

## Limitations

- Testing should be performed by **laboratories with more training and experience**, even though ARM is widely available.
- Normative data are limited and technique-specific (see above).
- ARM alone does not differentiate DD from IBS — it identifies abnormalities that may respond to biofeedback. ([[acg-2020-ibs]])
