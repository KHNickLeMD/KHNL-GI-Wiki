## Overview

- Anorectal manometry (ARM) measures **rectal sensation** and **anorectal pressures** — at rest, on anal/pelvic-floor contraction (squeeze), during attempted evacuation, and on cough or Valsalva ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]).
- **ARM plus a [[#Balloon Expulsion Test (BET)|balloon expulsion test (BET)]] is the standard functional workup for [[defecation-disorders|defecation disorders (DD)]]** — American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) key concept: *"ARM and balloon expulsion are required to diagnose DD."* The pair is generally **sufficient** to diagnose a DD and also **predicts who benefits from [[biofeedback-therapy|biofeedback]]** (American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2026). AGA applies a stricter bar — **2 of 3 abnormal tests (ARM, BET, defecography)** — only where the decision is to call constipation *refractory* and move toward surgery ([[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]]).
- **No single test is a gold standard.** ARM, BET, and magnetic resonance (MR) defecography agree at **>70%**, but individual patients may disagree across tests.
- **How many abnormal tests it takes has changed — and the live rules disagree.** Rome IV required **≥2**; **[[rome-v-2026-dgbi|Rome V]] (2026) requires a difficult-evacuation symptom (e.g., straining, digital maneuvers) plus only 1 of 3 abnormal tests** — balloon expulsion, manometry, **or** imaging — and has **dropped anal electromyography (EMG)** as a criterion. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders|ACG 2021]] still requires **ARM *and* BET** (key concept above). Same tier; Rome V is newer, so this page follows the 1-of-3 bar while ACG's both-tests rule remains the stricter operating standard in an ACG-following lab. Version table: [[defecation-disorders]].
- **Digital rectal examination should be performed before referral for ARM** ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]).
- **This page covers the test.** The criteria built on its measurements live elsewhere: dyssynergic defecation on [[defecation-disorders]] (which also holds the diagnostic algorithm and defecography indications), incontinence workup on [[fecal-incontinence]], and the rectal-sensation thresholds on [[anorectal-sensory-dysfunction]].

## Indications

| Indication | What the source says |
|---|---|
| Suspected DD / refractory constipation | ARM + BET **required** to diagnose DD ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]) |
| [[irritable-bowel-syndrome\|Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)]] with pelvic-floor symptoms and/or refractory constipation | Anorectal physiology testing **suggested** — **consensus recommendation, unable to assess using Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE)** ([[acg-2020-ibs]]). Anorectal dysfunction occurs in **all IBS subtypes**, prevalence **up to 40%** in tertiary care |
| [[fecal-incontinence\|Fecal incontinence (FI)]] | ARM, rectal BET, and rectal sensation **in patients who fail conservative measures**; add anal canal imaging and anal EMG for anal weakness ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]) |
| Levator ani syndrome ([[proctalgia-syndromes]]) | ARM and BET **should be performed** to identify patients eligible for biofeedback — **strongly recommended despite low-quality evidence**; *not* indicated in idiopathic chronic proctalgia syndrome ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]) |
| Before labelling constipation **refractory** | ARM + BET **and** a completed course of pelvic floor biofeedback, when indicated, before the refractory label — **best practice advice (BPA) 2** ([[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]]). A gate on the diagnosis itself, not just a workup option. Pathway: [[chronic-constipation]] |
| Suspected Hirschsprung disease | ARM **and** deep biopsy, to examine for the absence of myenteric neurons ([[asge-2014-constipation]]) |

- **Findings that suggest a pelvic floor disorder** — uncoordinated abdominal/rectoanal/pelvic floor muscles, rectal hyposensitivity, paradoxical rise in sphincter or puborectalis pressure on relaxation or simulated evacuation, prolonged balloon expulsion time, inadequate anal relaxation during push, inadequate abdominorectal propulsive force. The guideline's own caveat: these occur across **all IBS subtypes and are not specific** to dyssynergia (Table 6, [[acg-2020-ibs]]).

### Graded Evidence for the Test

[[rome-v-2026-anorectal-disorders|Rome V]] grades the anorectal diagnostic tests and issues **no graded treatment recommendations**. Its grade vocabulary, as the chapter defines it:

| Grade | Definition |
|---|---|
| **A1** | **Excellent** evidence in favor of the test, based on high specificity, sensitivity, accuracy and positive predictive values |
| **B2** | **Good** evidence, with some evidence on those measures |
| **B3** | **Fair** evidence, with some evidence on those measures |
| **C** | **Poor** evidence, with some evidence on those measures |

| Test | Indication | Grade | What it is credited with |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ARM** | Fecal incontinence | **A1** | Facilitates diagnosis of FI, DD and rectal sensory disorders |
| **ARM** | Constipation with dyssynergic defecation | **A1** | Identifies DD, weak or hypertonic sphincter/puborectalis, rectal hypo- and hypersensitivity, rectal compliance, Hirschsprung disease |
| **BET** | Constipation with dyssynergic defecation | **A1** | Identifies DD and impaired evacuation |

## Technique

### Catheters

| Type | Sensors | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Conventional | Water-perfused, air-charged, or solid-state | Requires a **pull-through maneuver** to profile the anal canal |
| High-resolution manometry (HRM) / high-definition (3D) | **More closely spaced sensors straddling the entire anal canal** | Better spatial resolution; **no pull-through needed** |

- Measurements with conventional and HRM catheters are **comparable**, but **absolute values are greater with HRM/high-definition than with conventional catheters**. See [[high-resolution-manometry]] for the manometric platform generally.

### Patient Position

- **Rectoanal pressures and the rectoanal gradient during evacuation are greater seated than left lateral**, and **seated HRM distinguishes healthy individuals from DD patients better than left lateral**. Attempted defecation should ideally be performed **seated** — though catheter displacement during evacuation makes this challenging, and **in practice manometry is generally performed left lateral** ([[rome-v-2026-anorectal-disorders]]). ACG likewise states the **seated position might be considered** if feasible.
- The **International Anorectal Physiology Working Group (IAPWG)** protocol specifies **left lateral, hips and knees flexed**, for both manometry and rectal sensory testing ([[london-2020-anorectal-classification]]).

### IAPWG Standardized Protocol

*The sequence as a whole reached level 1 consensus (>90% unanimity). Total study time for manometry + rectal sensory test (RST) + BET is expected to be **15–20 minutes**. Maneuver durations and the recovery intervals between maneuvers are themselves part of the protocol.* All from [[london-2020-anorectal-classification]].

| Order | Maneuver | Duration / number | Recovery interval | What it measures |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stabilization** | 3 min | — | Lets anal tone return to baseline after intubation; **no maneuver before it** |
| 2 | **Rest** | 60 s | — | Basal anal tone; patient reminded to relax and stay quiet (movement artifact). Ultraslow waves 0.5–2 cycles/min may appear |
| 3 | **Short squeeze** | 3 × 5 s | 30 s between squeezes | Voluntary anal/pelvic-floor contraction; **best of three** analyzed |
| 4 | **Long (endurance) squeeze** | 1 × 30 s | 60 s after | **Fatigue over time**, not contractile strength |
| 5 | **Cough** | 2 | 30 s between | Reflex anal pressure rise on abrupt intra-abdominal pressure change. Each must be a **single** cough; **best = greatest rise in rectal pressure** |
| 6 | **Push** | 3 × 15 s | 30 s between | Simulated defecation. Rectal balloon insufflation **not mandated**. Because false positives are common, the **most qualitatively normal** attempt is analyzed |
| 7 | **Rectal sensory testing** | — | — | Sensory thresholds — see [[#Measurements Reported]] |
| 8 | **Rectoanal inhibitory reflex (RAIR)** | 1, starting volume **≥30 mL** | — | Reflex anal pressure fall on rapid rectal distension. **If megarectum is suspected, repeat with increasing volumes** — a low distending volume in a large-capacity rectum can fail to elicit it |

- **BET is performed immediately before or after** this manometry/RST block.
- Push duration (3 × 15 s) is the **one element that reached no consensus** (<75% unanimity); everything else above is level 1 or 2.
- **Digital rectal examination before intubation** (also an ACG recommendation, above) — assess pelvic floor structure/function/sensitivity, exclude local pathology and fecal loading, and confirm the patient understands "squeeze" and "push".
- **Preparation:** continue all medications; eat and drink up to the time of the test; [[bowel-preparation|bowel preparation]] optional; a pre-procedure enema is **not contraindicated but must be documented** because it can alter function. Document any medication that affects anorectal function, **including analgesics**.

### Equipment Specifications

*IAPWG minimum standards ([[london-2020-anorectal-classification]]).*

| Component | Specification |
|---|---|
| Manometry sensors | **Circumferential, not unidirectional**, recording. **Minimum 6 cm longitudinal recording length.** Thin flexible catheters; rigid high-definition catheters **may** be used but are not standard |
| Manometry system | Recommendations are based on **solid-state systems — the single element that reached no consensus (<75%)** — and also apply to water-perfused high-resolution systems (moderate consensus) |
| Rectal balloon placement | Base **3–5 cm above the upper border of the anal canal**, so it cannot impinge on the anal canal during inflation; **most distal sensor outside the anal verge** |
| RST balloon | Capacity **≥400 mL** (maximum tolerated volume in health is no greater than 350 mL); **latex-free** |
| RST distension | Ramp **1–5 mL/s** or phasic **10 mL/s**, always **with air**. ⚠ **Ramp and phasic results are not interchangeable** |
| BET catheter | Flexible, **up to 16 Fr**, non-latex compliant balloon; **50 mL tepid water**; inserted left lateral, then **seated, ideally on a toilet in privacy** |

### Balloon Expulsion Test (BET)

- **Method:** the patient attempts to expel a water-filled rectal balloon (e.g. a commercial balloon) — filled with **50 mL of water**, *or* inflated until the individual's **defecatory desire** — **in privacy, seated on a commode** ([[rome-v-2026-anorectal-disorders]]). Filling to the desire threshold (**averaged 183 mL**) may compensate for the reduced rectal sensation of some DD patients.
- **Normal expulsion time ranges from 20 seconds to 2 minutes, although a 1-minute cutoff is recommended.** Results are **reproducible in 90% of individuals across days**.

| Balloon used | Upper limit of normal | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Party or **commercial balloon** (Mui Scientific) — *most widely used and preferred* | **1 minute** | The reference standard technique |
| **Foley catheter** inflated to 50 mL | **2 minutes** | 50 mL is **above the manufacturer's recommended 30 mL limit**; even at the 2-min cutoff **25% of healthy people are misclassified as abnormal** → **ACG discourages Foley balloons** |

- ⚠ The cutoff is meaningless without its balloon type — a 1-minute threshold applied to a Foley study, or a 2-minute threshold applied to a commercial balloon, misclassifies patients.
- **AGA 2026 uses the same bar** — a **prolonged BET (≥1 minute)** is one of its three abnormal tests for DD, alongside a reduced rectoanal gradient on ARM and inadequate rectal evacuation on defecography ([[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]]).
- **Performance** (vs defecography, 106 functional constipation + 24 DD patients): sensitivity and specificity **~88%**; **positive predictive value (PPV) 64%, negative predictive value (NPV) 97%**.
- ⚠ **A screening test, not a mechanism test.** It does not reveal DD pathophysiology, and results **may be normal in some patients who have constipation with features of DD on defecography or manometry** ([[rome-v-2026-anorectal-disorders]]).

### Rectal Sensory Testing

*All from [[rome-v-2026-anorectal-disorders]] unless noted. Equipment minimums are in the table above.*

- **Method:** a **handheld syringe** or an **electronic barostat** distends a rectal balloon; record threshold volumes for **first sensation**, **desire or urgency to defecate**, and **maximum tolerance**. Higher-than-normal values indicate hyposensitivity.
- ⚠ **Specify the method.** Technique — inflation rate, increments, volumes — varies between laboratories, so the thresholds are not interchangeable.
- **Rectal compliance, sex, age and body mass index may affect results.**
- The **barostat method** (infinitely compliant balloon on a computer-driven pump) gives **reproducible thresholds** and **accurately measures rectal compliance**.
- **Familiarise the patient with the potential sensations first, ideally via sensory charts.**
- Threshold criteria defining **rectal hyposensitivity and hypersensitivity**: [[anorectal-sensory-dysfunction]].

## Measurements Reported

*The IAPWG minimum reporting set ([[london-2020-anorectal-classification]]). Values are compared against the lab's own limits — see below.*

| Test | Measurement | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| ARM — rest | **Anal resting pressure** | Mean maximum pressure across the whole anal canal over the 60-s recording (mmHg) |
| ARM — rest | **Ultraslow waves** | Repeated anal pressure oscillations at 1–2/min (present/absent) |
| ARM — squeeze | **Anal squeeze pressure** | Maximum incremental pressure during the 5-s short squeeze (mmHg) |
| ARM — long squeeze | **Endurance squeeze pressure** | Time the patient sustains an anal pressure increase **>50% of maximum incremental squeeze pressure** over the 30-s squeeze (s) |
| ARM — push | **Rectal and anal pressure change during push** | Maximum pressure change in rectum and in anal canal during push (mmHg) |
| ARM — cough | **Rectal and anal pressure during cough** | Maximum pressure in rectum and anal canal during cough (mmHg) |
| ARM | **RAIR** | Reflex fall in maximum anal pressure on rapid rectal distension (present/absent) — **also record the volume that elicited it** |
| BET | **Balloon expulsion time** | Seconds to expel the balloon — **also record whether a desire to defecate was present** |
| RST | **FCSV** — first constant sensation volume | Minimum insufflation volume eliciting a sensory response (mL) |
| RST | **DDV** — desire to defecate volume | Volume eliciting a sustained desire to defecate (mL) |
| RST | **MTV** — maximum tolerated volume | Volume causing an intolerable desire to defecate (mL) |

- A fourth sensory threshold, **sustained urgency volume** (volume eliciting a sense of fecal urgency), is **optional**.

## Interpretation — Reference Values and Pitfalls

**This is the section that decides whether a study is abnormal. Every pitfall below is an ACG-stated reason not to call DD on manometry alone.**

- **Compare values only against age- and sex-appropriate normal values for that particular manometry system.** Normal values differ considerably **between HRM and non-HRM catheters, and among different HRM catheter types** ([[rome-v-2026-anorectal-disorders]]) — cross-system comparison invalidates the result.
- **Reference data are thin** — characterized in relatively few individuals, more in women than men; the largest cohort is **96 women** studied with the Medtronic HRM device.
- **Anal sphincter pressures decline with age and are lower in women.**
- **37% of asymptomatic women fail to relax or paradoxically contract the pelvic floor during evacuation.** The same caution applies to decreased rectal pressures, paradoxical anal contraction, high anal pressure during evacuation, and high anal resting pressure.
- **Interpret with the clinical features** — false-positive and false-negative results are not uncommon.

### Rectoanal Gradient (RAG)

- **Rome V: assessment of rectoanal coordination, such as the rectoanal gradient (RAG), is the best manometry measure of abnormal evacuation** ([[rome-v-2026-anorectal-disorders]]).
- ⚠ **A negative RAG is not by itself abnormal.** Intuitively, normal evacuation should need rectal pressure > anal pressure — but many healthy people have a negative gradient. Among healthy people with a **normal balloon expulsion time**, the 10th–90th percentile RAG during **left lateral HRM**:

| Sex | 10th–90th percentile RAG |
|---|---|
| **Women** | **−70 to 12 mm Hg** |
| **Men** | **−128 to 1 mm Hg** |

- **The RAG is abnormal only when it falls below the lower limit of normal** for sex and, in women, age.
- **Performance:** an abnormal RAG measured with the Manoscan (Medtronic) catheter is **36% sensitive and 85% specific** for predicting a prolonged balloon expulsion time.
- **Combine it with the BET** — probability of reduced rectal evacuation in constipated patients: **14%** when both are normal, **45%** when either is abnormal, **75%** when both are abnormal. An abnormal BET and an abnormal gradient are **81%** and **86% specific** respectively for reduced rectal evacuation, which is why **defecography may not be required**.

### Historical Dyssynergia Types I–IV

*Descriptive patterns from an early study of 35 constipated patients — **not Rome V diagnostic criteria**. Rome V declines to subclassify DD, and the types are not part of the London Classification either; assign a London diagnosis rather than a numbered type.*

| Type | Propulsion (rectal pressure on push) | Anal response |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Adequate, **≥45 mm Hg** | Paradoxical anal contraction |
| **II** | Inadequate, **<45 mm Hg** | Paradoxical contraction **or** insufficient relaxation |
| **III** | Adequate, **≥45 mm Hg** | Absent or insufficient anal relaxation (**<20%**) |
| **IV** | Inadequate, **<45 mm Hg** | Inadequate relaxation (**<20%**) |

**Why Rome V dropped the subclassification** ([[rome-v-2026-anorectal-disorders]]):

- A controlled study found **87% of healthy participants had "abnormal" patterns** suggestive of DD — an inference drawn from HRM data read against older non-HRM, seated normal values, which is itself the argument for system-specific reference values.
- **Interobserver agreement** is **substantial for types I and IV**, **moderate** for normal vs dyssynergic patterns, and **only fair for types II and III**.
- The cut-points for inadequate propulsion and paradoxical contraction **vary across techniques**, do not reliably discriminate healthy people from DD patients, and **do not predict response to biofeedback**.

### Assigning a Pattern — the London Classification

- **ACG 2021 and Rome V say whether a study is abnormal; the [[london-classification|London Classification]] says what to call the pattern.** Neither guideline contains a manometric classification scheme, so the IAPWG consensus is where the named diagnoses come from ([[london-2020-anorectal-classification]]).
- Four parts — RAIR, anal tone and contractility, rectoanal coordination, rectal sensation — and **one study may return a diagnosis under more than one**. Findings are tiered **major** (not seen in controls), **minor** (seen in patients and controls) or **inconclusive**. Full decision logic: [[london-classification]].
- **The classification publishes no reference values at all** — every decision point is "above/below the limit of normal", referred to the lab's own data or to published data acquired with **identical equipment and protocol**, adjusted for sex, parity and age.
- **Cough and long squeeze are recorded but excluded from the classification** — the consensus did not consider them justifiable as diagnostic criteria. An abnormal cough response may mark a more severe phenotype and a preserved one a biofeedback target; neither is a criterion.
- **Dyssynergia never rises above a minor finding**, and an abnormal manometric coordination pattern with a *normal* balloon expulsion is only **inconclusive**. It is the **BET**, not the manometric pattern, that carries the weight.

## Defecography — Barium vs Magnetic Resonance (MR)

*Indications and findings: [[defecation-disorders]]. This section is the modality choice.*

- Order either modality when **clinical features suggest a structural abnormality**, or when **ARM and BET are inconclusive** ([[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]]). Rome V grades **fluoroscopic defecography B2** and **MR defecography B2** ([[rome-v-2026-anorectal-disorders]]).

| | Barium defecography | MR defecography |
|---|---|---|
| Position | **Seated** — preferable when the primary question is rectal evacuation | **Supine**; open magnet and seated assessment are ideal but not widely available |
| Radiation | Yes | **None** |
| Strengths | Real-time evacuation assessment | More precise pelvic organ prolapse and pelvic floor motion; **especially useful for pelvic floor dysfunction in patients with clinical DD features and a normal BET** — a group that includes **>90% of patients with a large rectocele, enterocele and/or peritoneocele** |
| Availability / cost | Wider | **Less widely available, more expensive** |

## Adjunct — Anal EMG

- Average anal EMG activity recorded from electrodes on an acrylic anal plug or taped to perianal skin.
- **≥20% reduction in anal EMG activity during evacuation is considered normal** (data limited); **<20% reduction correlates with dyssynergic defecation** as identified by manometry and abnormal BET.
- Rome V grades both forms **B3** in FI: **needle EMG** — research laboratories only, painful; **surface EMG** — largely used for biofeedback ([[rome-v-2026-anorectal-disorders]]).
- ⚠ **EMG is an adjunct only, not a diagnostic criterion** — Rome V removed it from the dyssynergic-defecation criteria (see [[#Overview]]).

## Parameters That Matter, by Indication

| Setting | Key ARM parameters |
|---|---|
| **[[defecation-disorders\|Defecation disorder]]** | Rectal and anal pressures on evacuation, anal relaxation, **rectoanal gradient**, rectal sensation |
| **Fecal incontinence** | **Anal resting and squeeze pressures are the key parameters** — resting reflects the **internal** sphincter, squeeze the **external** sphincter. Typical findings: reduced squeeze pressure, reduced squeeze duration, and/or reduced rectal capacity with rectal hypersensitivity; lower resting pressure in passive incontinence. Rectal sensation may be **normal, increased, or decreased**. The **anal cough reflex** is qualitatively useful for lower-motor-neuron integrity of the external sphincter |

- Baseline ARM + BET **predict outcome and guide biofeedback therapy** ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]] key concept 7).

## Limitations

- Testing should be performed by **laboratories with more training and experience**, even though ARM is widely available.
- Normative data are limited and technique-specific (see above).
- ARM alone does not differentiate DD from IBS — it identifies abnormalities that may respond to biofeedback. ([[acg-2020-ibs]])
