Radiofrequency ablation (RFA) delivers controlled thermal energy to destroy a thin layer of diseased mucosa — or, in solid organs, tumor tissue — while sparing deeper layers. **Depth is the whole point:** settings are chosen to ablate epithelium and muscularis mucosae without injuring the submucosa ([[asge-2019-radiation-proctopathy]]); in the bile duct, bipolar electrodes produce coagulative necrosis only in the portion of the stricture in direct contact with the catheter ([[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]]).

In luminal gastrointestinal (GI) practice it is the **preferred primary ablative modality** within [[endoscopic-eradication-therapy|endoscopic eradication therapy (EET)]] for flat dysplastic [[barretts-esophagus|Barrett's esophagus (BE)]] (widest evidence base — [[acg-2022-barretts|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2022]]). It is also used for bleeding chronic [[radiation-proctopathy]], intraductally for malignant biliary obstruction, and as a thermal ablation modality for small [[hepatocellular-carcinoma|hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)]].

## Indications

| Setting | Role | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Flat dysplastic BE (low-grade dysplasia [LGD], high-grade dysplasia [HGD]); residual flat segment after [[endoscopic-mucosal-resection\|endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR)]] | Preferred ablative modality within [[endoscopic-eradication-therapy\|EET]] | [[asge-2018-barretts-eet]], [[acg-2022-barretts]], [[aga-2024-barretts-eet]] |
| Non-dysplastic BE | **Not** routinely indicated (Rec 3, conditional) | [[aga-2024-barretts-eet]] |
| Bleeding chronic radiation proctopathy | Suggested option alongside [[argon-plasma-coagulation\|argon plasma coagulation (APC)]], bipolar electrocoagulation, heater probe — no modality shown superior (Rec 1) | [[asge-2019-radiation-proctopathy]] |
| Malignant perihilar [[cholangiocarcinoma]], not a candidate for resection or transplantation | **Adjuvant intraductal RFA (or photodynamic therapy [PDT]) + plastic stent** over plastic stent alone — survival advantage (Rec 10, conditional, low) | [[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]] |
| Early-stage HCC ≤3 cm, ineligible for/declining surgery | Thermal ablation (RFA or microwave) is the treatment of choice (Level 1, strong); RFA ≈ resection for tumors <2 cm, resection superior for larger (Guidance Statement [GS] 39) | [[aasld-2023-hcc]] |

*Not suited to RFA in the liver: HCC >3 cm, or tumors next to large vessels, diaphragm, heart or central bile ducts — use another locoregional modality ([[aasld-2023-hcc]]). The American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD) does not advise one thermal ablative modality over another (GS 39a); microwave may be less susceptible to heat-sink near large vessels.*

*Evidence caveat: intraductal RFA trial data come predominantly from **extrahepatic** stricture populations — the perihilar evidence is less robust than for PDT ([[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]]). Stent choice is a separate question: ACG 2023 found the evidence **insufficient** to recommend for or against plastic stents vs uncovered self-expandable metal stents (SEMS) in perihilar strictures (Rec 9); because an uncovered SEMS cannot be removed, planned repeat ablation is one of the considerations that favors a replaceable plastic stent.*

## Technique

### Esophagus

- **Sequence: resect, then ablate.** All visible lesions are resected first ([[endoscopic-mucosal-resection|EMR]] or [[endoscopic-submucosal-dissection|endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD)]]); RFA treats the **remaining flat segment**.
- **Catheter types:**
  - **Balloon-based (circumferential)** — long or circumferential segments.
  - **Focal** — short segments, tongues, islands, residual disease at follow-up sessions.
- **Sessions:** most patients reach **complete eradication of intestinal metaplasia (CE-IM)** within **3 ablation sessions** ([[asge-2018-barretts-eet]]).
- Ablation target at the squamocolumnar and gastroesophageal junctions, session scheduling, acid suppression and the rest of the workflow: [[endoscopic-eradication-therapy]].

### Rectum

- Bleeding chronic [[radiation-proctopathy]] — focal ablation catheter applied to the distal rectum, stopping short of the dentate line to spare anal sensory mucosa, and over a short segment only.
- Energy and power density, applications per site, electrode cleaning, margins and session intervals: [[radiation-proctopathy]] ([[asge-2019-radiation-proctopathy]]).

### Bile Duct

- Bipolar radiofrequency catheter passed at [[ercp|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]] across the malignant stricture, then **plastic stent** placement (stent must be removable if repeat ablation is planned).
- **Repeat ablation is usually delivered at the time of routine stent exchange** — the way most supporting studies applied it. RFA as a one-time treatment before metal stenting improved survival in a small observational study but not in a small phase II randomized controlled trial (RCT) ([[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]]).
- Used with systemic therapy for unresectable perihilar disease; drainage strategy on [[biliary-stricture]].

> [!pitfall] Confirm inoperability before you ablate
> Full-thickness thermal injury from RFA causes edema and scarring that confound subsequent preoperative imaging and cloud the assessment of surgical candidacy. Start PDT or RFA only after multidisciplinary evaluation has confirmed inoperability ([[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]]).

## Outcomes

- **BE progression vs surveillance (LGD and HGD), and EET vs esophagectomy:** [[endoscopic-eradication-therapy]]. Recurrence rates after CE-IM, where recurrence appears (75% at the gastroesophageal junction, most of those endoscopically invisible) and surveillance intervals: [[barretts-esophagus]].
- **Subsquamous ("buried") BE:** random biopsies of normal-appearing neosquamous epithelium yield only **1% for any recurrence and 0.2% for dysplastic recurrence** — consistent with a low rate of subsquamous BE after successful RFA, so their value is largely confined to the distal 2–4 cm of the tubular esophagus ([[acg-2022-barretts]]).
- **Chronic radiation proctopathy:** 3 small case series (66 patients), **no comparative studies or randomized trials**. No modality is proven superior to APC (pooled clinical success 87%) or bipolar electrocoagulation (88%); per-series RFA success rates and session counts are on [[radiation-proctopathy]] ([[asge-2019-radiation-proctopathy]]).

## Adverse Events

*Pooled EET safety (RFA ± EMR; 37 studies, 9,200 patients) is tabulated on [[endoscopic-eradication-therapy]]. The RFA-specific split:*

- **RFA alone 5%** (95% confidence interval [CI] 2.9–8.3) vs **EMR + RFA 22.2%** (95% CI 16.4–29.4) — **relative risk (RR) 4.4, P=.015** ([[asge-2018-barretts-eet]]). Adding resection to the ablation, not the ablation itself, drives the adverse-event burden.
- **Intraductal RFA:** abdominal pain; **hemobilia ~4%** (proximity to hepatic vasculature) ([[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]]).
- RFA is treated as a **high-bleeding-risk** procedure for periprocedural antithrombotic management — see [[anticoagulation-gi-bleeding]].

## Alternatives

- **Cryotherapy** (spray liquid nitrogen or cryoballoon) — may be considered in patients **unresponsive to RFA** (ACG 2022 key concept, ungraded); no RCT vs RFA. Stricture rate **9–12%**, and a non-randomized multicenter comparison found comparable eradication rates for RFA and cryoballoon but **more strictures in the cryoballoon arm** ([[acg-2022-barretts]]).
- **APC** — the most-used endoscopic modality for radiation proctopathy; note reported **colonic gas explosion** with inadequate bowel prep ([[asge-2019-radiation-proctopathy]]).
- **Complete EMR of the entire BE segment** — **strongly recommended against** in favor of focal EMR + RFA ([[asge-2018-barretts-eet]] Rec 6); the comparative stricture, bleeding and perforation odds ratios are on [[endoscopic-eradication-therapy]].

*Cryotherapy eradication rates in dysplastic BE ([[acg-2022-barretts]]):*

| Cryotherapy | Complete eradication of dysplasia (CED) | CE-IM |
|---|---|---|
| Spray cryotherapy (cohort studies) | 81–88% | 57–61% |
| Cryoballoon (prospective cohort, intention to treat) | 76% | 72% |
| Spray cryotherapy **after failed RFA** (systematic review) | 76% | 46% |
