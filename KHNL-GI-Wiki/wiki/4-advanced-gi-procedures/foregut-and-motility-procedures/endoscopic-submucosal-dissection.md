En-bloc endoscopic resection of early gastrointestinal (GI) neoplasia using electrosurgical knives rather than a snare: it circumvents the size limit of [[endoscopic-mucosal-resection|endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR)]], removes lesions far larger than a snare allows in one piece, and so permits accurate R0 assessment and a low recurrence rate ([[asge-2023-esd]]). First-line over surgery for many early-stage esophageal and gastric cancers. The principles below are generic to endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD); **colorectal indications, hybrid ESD and early-T1 colorectal cancer (CRC) resection are on [[colorectal-esd]]** — the colon has a narrower indication set and a thinner wall.

## Definitions
- **Early-stage cancer:** malignant tumor confined to mucosa and/or submucosa, no deeper involvement, no locoregional or distant spread.
- **En-bloc resection:** entire neoplastic tissue removed in **1 piece**. EMR en-bloc is capped by snare diameter — **generally 1.5–2 cm** ([[aga-2018-esd-united-states]]); larger lesions need piecemeal EMR, which cannot reliably confirm R0.
- **R0 resection:** resection margins microscopically disease-free.

## Depth of Invasion
| Level | Depth |
|---|---|
| **M1** | Intraepithelial noninvasive carcinoma / carcinoma in situ (CIS) |
| **M2** | Microinvasive into the lamina propria |
| **M3** | Microinvasive into the muscularis mucosa |
| **SM1** | Upper third of submucosa |
| **SM2** | Middle third of submucosa |
| **SM3** | Lower third of submucosa |

Deeper invasion → higher lymph-node-metastasis (LNM) risk → favors surgery. **In the esophagus the SM1 ceiling is histology-specific — 200 µm for squamous cell carcinoma, 500 µm for adenocarcinoma** — and the nodal risk at each level differs sharply by cell type: [[esophageal-cancer#T1b Substaging and Nodal Risk]] ([[aga-2019-esd-t1b-esophageal-cancer]]).

## Lesion Selection — ESD vs EMR vs Surgery

All recommendations are *conditional*, low quality (Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation [GRADE] "suggest"). **Differentiation and ulceration qualifiers are not uniform — read them per row.** [[asge-2023-esd]]

| Lesion (all early-stage) | ESD over EMR | Either ESD or EMR \* | Surgery |
|---|---|---|---|
| Esophageal squamous dysplasia / **esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC)** — well differentiated, nonulcerated, no submucosal invasion | >15 mm | ≤15 mm | **Against surgery** (same population) |
| **[[esophageal-adenocarcinoma\|Esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC)]]** (T1) or nodular [[barretts-esophagus\|Barrett's]] dysplasia — well differentiated, nonulcerated | >20 mm | ≤20 mm | No comparative ESD-vs-surgery evidence |
| **[[gastric-adenocarcinoma\|Gastric adenocarcinoma (GAC)]]** — well or moderately differentiated, intestinal type, nonulcerated | 20–30 mm ⚠ *see ulceration note* | <20 mm | **Against surgery** for intestinal type ≤30 mm (this row carries *no* ulceration qualifier); **poorly differentiated, any size → surgical evaluation** |

\* Summary table wording is "we do not make a recommendation for or against either ESD or EMR"; the recommendation text in the body reads "suggest either ESD or EMR".

- The GAC decision turns on **4 factors**: differentiation (well/moderate vs poor), morphology (ulcerated vs nonulcerated), type (intestinal vs diffuse), and size. The surgery decision turns on **differentiation, diffuse type, or clear ulceration — not on size**.
- ⚠ **[[asge-2023-esd|American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2023]] is internally inconsistent on ulceration in the 20–30 mm GAC row** — abstract and summary table say "**nonulcerated**", the Question 3a text and the gastric algorithm say "**with or without ulceration**", with no reconciliation. Conservative reading: *nonulcerated*, since the document also states that ulceration "indicates a high likelihood of invasion into the submucosa". Ulcerated intestinal-type lesions ≤3 cm do sit inside the expanded criteria ([[#Stomach]]).
- Lesions exceeding size/depth criteria (e.g. deep submucosal invasion) → **surgical evaluation**.
- Applies to early [[esophageal-cancer|ESCC/EAC]], early GAC, and their precursor lesions (Barrett's dysplasia, gastric [[gastric-intestinal-metaplasia|intestinal metaplasia]]/dysplasia).

## Indications by Organ

Size alone does not decide the indication. Sources: [[aga-2018-esd-united-states]] (all organs), [[asge-2023-esd]] (esophagus, stomach).

### Esophagus

**Squamous neoplasia — indications**
- ESD is the **primary modality** for squamous dysplasia and cancer confined to superficial mucosa (best practice advice [BPA] 5). Aim is **always en-bloc, ESD first-line** — ESCC metastasizes to nodes at earlier stages than Barrett's neoplasia, so accurate histology matters more.
- **Absolute:** high-grade dysplasia (HGD) to G1–G2, Paris 0-II, **m1–m2**, **≤ two thirds of the esophageal circumference**.
- **Expanded:** **m3 or sm <200 µm**, **any size**, clinically N0.

**Barrett's neoplasia — indications**
- EMR is first-line for [[barretts-esophagus|Barrett's esophagus (BE)]] overall; ESD is chosen for **lesion features**, not size alone (BPA 4): large or bulky area of **nodularity**; high likelihood of **superficial submucosal invasion**; **recurrent dysplasia**; **EMR specimen showing invasive carcinoma with positive margins**; **equivocal preprocedural histology**; **intramucosal carcinoma**. Also HGD to G1–G2 **T1a (m1–m3) lesions ≥15 mm** not amenable to en-bloc EMR.
- **Paris 0-Is and 0-IIc lesions where submucosal invasion may exceed 500 µm → triage to ESD** ([[asge-2023-esd]]).
- **Why features, not size:** preresection and postresection histology disagree in **30%–60% of cases** → unrecognised invasive component is common. **Deep margin positive on an EMR specimen → assume residual neoplasia**; ESD is the best-suited modality for the additional therapy.
- ⚠ **Size threshold differs between sources:** [[aga-2018-esd-united-states]] uses **≥15 mm** for Barrett's T1a lesions; the newer [[asge-2023-esd]] uses **>20 mm** for EAC — the threshold this page asserts ([[#Lesion Selection — ESD vs EMR vs Surgery]]).

**After resection**
- **ESCC: m3 or any submucosal invasion is noncurative** ([[#Depth Thresholds by Organ]]).
- **EAC/Barrett's, curative:** wait **2–3 months** for the post-ESD ulcer to heal, on high-dose [[proton-pump-inhibitors|proton pump inhibitor (PPI)]], then ablate any residual BE (typically [[radiofrequency-ablation|radiofrequency ablation]]), repeating **every 2–3 months until complete eradication of intestinal metaplasia** ([[aga-2021-post-esd-surveillance]]); ASGE 2023 likewise stresses early initiation of ablation. Detail: [[endoscopic-eradication-therapy]].
- **Noncurative, either histology:** multidisciplinary tumor board — surgery, repeat endoscopic therapy, imaging surveillance, systemic or radiation therapy.

> [!pearl] Expanded indication ≠ curative resection
> m3/sm1 ESCC may be resected for excisional staging, but the pathology is noncurative. Fit for surgery → esophagectomy; unfit → multidisciplinary review with close endoscopic follow-up, [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]] and computed tomography (CT) of chest and abdomen or positron emission tomography (PET)-CT, ± chemoradiotherapy ([[aga-2021-post-esd-surveillance]]).

**Notes**
- **Very large ESCC (>5 cm) or ≥ two thirds of the circumference → multidisciplinary discussion**; surgery may still be the safest option. Ulceration, suspected submucosal invasion or poor differentiation → multidisciplinary review regardless of size ([[asge-2023-esd]]).
- ⚠ Nodal risk at SM1 ESCC: ASGE 2023 estimates **~5%**; [[aga-2019-esd-t1b-esophageal-cancer]] reports **8%–26.5%** ([[esophageal-cancer#T1b Substaging and Nodal Risk]]).
- **Stricture prevention:** resection of **>75% of the esophageal circumference** → high stricture risk. Inject steroid into the submucosa of the ESD defect or give ingested viscous budesonide slurry; then upper endoscopy **at 4 weeks** — not for surveillance but to start [[esophageal-dilation|dilation]] before a severe [[esophageal-stricture|stricture]] forms — repeating dilation **every 1–2 weeks until resolution** ([[aga-2021-post-esd-surveillance]]).

### Stomach

**Indications**
- **Absolute:** mucosal adenocarcinoma (or HGD), **intestinal type**, G1/G2, **≤2 cm**, nonulcerated.
- **Expanded:** intestinal-type G1/G2 of any size without ulceration; intestinal-type G1/G2 with submucosal invasion <500 µm; intestinal-type G1/G2 ≤3 cm with ulceration; diffuse-type G3/G4 ≤2 cm without ulceration; en-bloc resection for lesions at risk for submucosally invasive cancer.
- **The LNM risk each set accepts — the whole justification for resecting endoscopically instead of gastrectomy:** **0.2%** absolute, **0.7%** expanded (meta-analyses), both judged practically acceptable with comparable outcomes. Within the expanded set, **undifferentiated mucosal (2.6%) and submucosal (2.5%) lesions carry the higher risk** → weigh against surgery.
- A 2021 Japanese guideline reclassified T1a early gastric cancers with **<1% LNM risk** from expanded to **absolute** criteria ([[aga-2021-post-esd-surveillance]]).

**After resection**
- **Noncurative findings** — each prompts **multidisciplinary review** rather than automatic gastrectomy: positive lateral **or** deep margin; submucosal infiltration **>500 µm**; poorly differentiated or undifferentiated pathology; **ulcerated tumor >3 cm**; lymphovascular invasion (LVI). **LVI, SM2-or-deeper invasion and a positive deep margin are the findings that favour referral for surgery.**
- **Positive lateral margin as the *only* noncurative criterion:** mounting evidence that **additional endoscopic therapy within 3–6 months** suffices in lieu of surgery, with long-term remission; the long-term strategy is formally unresolved.
- **Mixed histology** ([[aga-2021-post-esd-surveillance]]): T1a **<3 cm** with ulceration stays curative if **predominantly differentiated** (metastatic risk <1%); T1a **>2 cm without ulcer is noncurative if the undifferentiated region is >2 cm**; a predominantly differentiated T1b with **<500 µm** invasion is **not curative if undifferentiated histology focally invades the submucosa** → consider surgery.

**Notes**
- Test for and eradicate [[helicobacter-pylori-infection|*Helicobacter pylori*]] — associated with lower rates of metachronous gastric cancer, which develops in **5.9% within 3 years** of curative ESD ([[aga-2021-post-esd-surveillance]]).

### Duodenum
- The duodenal wall is **highly vascular**, has a **thin muscularis propria**, and is exposed to **gastric acid, bile, and pancreatic juice** → high rates of immediate and delayed bleeding and of perforation.
- **Restrict to endoscopists with extensive ESD experience in other locations**, and **refrain during the early phase of an ESD practice** ([[aga-2018-esd-united-states]] BPA 6). Lesion selection and perforation rates are on [[nonampullary-duodenal-adenoma]].

### Colorectum
- Indications, hybrid ESD, early-T1 CRC resection and colorectal post-ESD surveillance are on [[colorectal-esd]].

## Curative Resection Criteria
Clinical success = **curative resection** requires ALL of the following on histology of the resected specimen:
1. Circumferential (lateral/horizontal) **and** deep (vertical) margins microscopically free of malignant cells (R0)
2. Well (G1) or moderate (G2) differentiation
3. No lymphovascular invasion
4. **Low-grade (grade 1) or absent tumor budding** ([[aga-2021-post-esd-surveillance]])
5. No deep invasion — organ-specific depth limits below

Failure of any criterion → not curative → consider surgery / adjuvant therapy. Organ-specific noncurative findings and what to do about them are under each organ in [[#Indications by Organ]].

### Depth Thresholds by Organ

Depth of submucosal invasion below the muscularis mucosae compatible with a **curative** ESD ([[aga-2021-post-esd-surveillance]], [[asge-2023-esd]]):

| Cancer type / organ | Curative if submucosal invasion is |
|---|---|
| Esophageal and gastric **adenocarcinoma** | **<500 µm** |
| Colorectal **adenocarcinoma** | **<1000 µm** (colorectal detail on [[colorectal-esd]]) |
| Esophageal **squamous cell carcinoma** | **No depth is curative once the muscularis mucosae is reached** — m3 or any submucosal invasion is noncurative, given the increased LNM risk |

### What Counts as R0 in a Field of Premalignant Mucosa

Per [[aga-2021-post-esd-surveillance]]:

- **No minimal distance** is required between tumor cells and the resection margin for ESD — unlike surgical R0 resection.
- Lesion sitting in a field of lesser dysplasia (Barrett's, severe [[atrophic-gastritis]]) → consider the resection **R0 if the highest histologic grade of the target neoplasm is not present at the margins**. Example: a large HGD nodule in BE removed completely, flat low-grade dysplasia (LGD) present at the specimen margin = R0 for HGD. ASGE 2023 agrees for a **positive lateral margin only** when every other curative criterion is met.
- **R1 horizontal margin** (microscopic) with otherwise low-risk histology → additional endoscopic therapy by ESD or [[endoscopic-mucosal-resection|EMR]] is possible.
- **R1 deep/vertical margin** after ESD of a malignant neoplasm → **prompt referral for surgery**.
- Western pathology does not uniformly use the Japanese protocol (2–3 mm serial sectioning, lymphovascular-invasion stains), and no definition of a negative R0 margin is universally accepted — the reason the surveillance intervals below are tighter than Asian protocols require.

## Technique

> [!howto] ESD sequence
> 1. **Mark the borders** before lifting — mandatory in the stomach, where margins become indistinct after injection; extra peripheral marks orient the specimen for pathology.
> 2. **Lift** with dynamic submucosal injection (agents: [[endoscopic-mucosal-resection#Submucosal Lifting Agents]]).
> 3. **Incise** circumferentially, then **dissect** the submucosa with an electrosurgical knife.
> 4. **Coagulate** exposed vessels, then close as needed and **pin the specimen**.
> ([[aga-2018-esd-united-states]])

- **Energy settings** — which current for dissection vs fibrosis, the 10 W knife-shaft technique for preemptive vessel sealing, when to switch to coagulation forceps, and underwater settings: **[[electrosurgery#Third-Space Endoscopy (ESD, POEM)]]** ([[aga-2026-electrosurgery]] BPA 13).
- **Fibrosis** (prior tattoo, extensive biopsies, partial snare resection) lowers en-bloc rates and raises bleeding and perforation risk → **traction**: a clip tied with line and anchored to the opposite wall gives pulley traction. **Tunnel and pocket methods and traction devices** make dissection faster and safer.
- **Coagulate exposed nonbleeding visible vessels** (little or no overlying submucosa) in the bed to reduce delayed bleeding — established in the **upper GI tract**, **equivocal** after large colorectal resections. Use **low-voltage ("soft") coagulation**: less perforation and postpolypectomy burn syndrome.
- **Closure** of the post-ESD site may be considered based on patient factors, procedural factors and lesion location (BPA 11); device choice by defect size and organ is on [[endoscopic-management-of-perforation]].

## Specimen Handling and Pathology

- **Pin the specimen flat** (e.g. to cork board) **before immersing in formalin** — unpinned specimens curl and distort the muscularis mucosae.
- An **expert GI pathologist** must report: **margin involvement, degree of differentiation, lymphovascular invasion, depth of submucosal invasion, and tumor budding**.
- Interobserver and intraobserver variability in grading dysplasia and early cancer is documented throughout the GI tract → **strongly consider a second expert GI pathologist**. ([[aga-2018-esd-united-states]] BPA 13)

## Outcomes vs EMR

Meta-analysis of 8 observational studies (821 ESD vs 1306 EMR), [[asge-2023-esd]]:

| Outcome | ESD | EMR |
|---|---|---|
| Clinical success (curative resection) | **93.3%** | 72.1% |
| Local recurrence | **0.5%** | 5.2% |
| Perforation | **5.7%** | 0.8% |
| Stricture | 9.2% | 7.4% |
| Bleeding · distant recurrence | No significant difference | No significant difference |

- Over **90% of perforations are managed endoscopically**; ESD takes roughly **47 minutes longer** than EMR.
- Technically demanding; often available only at tertiary/quaternary referral centers.

## Surveillance After Curative Resection

Schedules proposed by the [[asge-2023-esd]] panel (algorithm figures); comparative data on post-ESD surveillance tools and intervals were **notably lacking**, so these are expert opinion, not graded evidence.

| Resected pathology | Endoscopy | Imaging |
|---|---|---|
| Esophageal LGD or HGD (CIS) | q6 mo × 2 y, then annually | — |
| Esophageal **intramucosal carcinoma (squamous)** | q3–6 mo for the first year, then q6–12 mo × 2 y, then annually | May consider CT annually × 3–5 y |
| **T1a EAC (m1–m3)** or Barrett's LGD/HGD | q6 mo × 2 y, then annually | — |
| **T1b (Sm1) EAC** | at 3, 6 and 12 mo, then q6 mo × 2 y, then annually | Consider EUS to exclude nodal metastasis; consider CT chest/abdomen annually × 3–5 y |
| Gastric HGD or **T1a** | q6 mo × 3 y, then annually | — |
| Gastric **T1b**, negative deep margin | q3–6 mo × 1 y, then annually | Consider CT every 6–12 mo × 3–5 y |

- **After piecemeal resection or a positive margin:** higher recurrence risk → **take biopsy samples** at surveillance rather than relying on inspection alone.
- **Technique:** high-definition endoscopy with dye-based or electronic chromoendoscopy, optical magnification where available; biopsy the scar centrally and peripherally and inspect the rest of the organ for metachronous lesions ([[aga-2021-post-esd-surveillance]]).
- **Long-term surveillance is warranted in all cases:** synchronous/metachronous gastric cancer **10%–20%**; metachronous esophageal cancer after resection of squamous cell carcinoma **2.2%–9% per year**. Also follow lesion-/organ-specific schedules ([[barretts-esophagus]], [[gastric-adenocarcinoma]], [[colorectal-cancer]]).
- **Recurrent/metachronous early gastric cancer:** no confident ESD-vs-surgery conclusion (studies did not separate true recurrence from incomplete resection); EMR-vs-ESD data clearly favour **ESD**. Decide at a **multidisciplinary meeting**.
- ⚠ [[aga-2021-post-esd-surveillance]] proposes close but not identical schedules (first endoscopy at 6 mo for T1a early gastric cancer, 3–6 mo for T1b-Sm1, 6–12 mo for esophageal LGD) and offers EUS with each endoscopy for T1b lesions; where they differ, the newer ASGE 2023 schedule above governs. **None of these intervals apply to hereditary cancer syndromes** — they may need surgery or more intense surveillance.

## Training and Referral Practice

- **Which lesion to start on:** well-established indications of **lowest technical complexity**, in patients **at least fair surgical candidates**. US first lesions are typically **rectal** (early gastric cancer is uncommon) — **colorectal ESD is safe without previous gastric ESD experience**, unlike Japanese practice.
- **Educational model:** the master–apprentice pathway does not transfer; the stepwise US approach is **didactic self-study → courses of increasing complexity → animal models → observation of expert live cases**.
- **Quality metrics** to track: **en-bloc, R0 and curative resection rates** plus acceptable adverse-event rates; Asian benchmarks may not transfer. ([[aga-2018-esd-united-states]] BPA 9, 14–16)

> [!pitfall] Three referral habits that make the lesion unresectable
> Tattooing beneath or immediately adjacent to a lesion (tattoo away from it), extensive biopsies, and partial snare resection for histopathology all induce submucosal fibrosis. **Refer large complex colorectal polyps** to a high-volume specialized center instead of sampling or partially resecting them ([[aga-2018-esd-united-states]] BPA 16).
