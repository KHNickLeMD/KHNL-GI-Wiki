Pneumatic dilation (PD) is the forceful, balloon-mediated disruption of the **muscularis propria** of the lower esophageal sphincter (LES) — the only dilation that works in [[achalasia|achalasia]]. **Standard through-the-scope (TTS) and bougie dilators are not effective**, because they do not disrupt the muscularis propria ([[acg-2020-achalasia]]). A true achalasia dilation uses a dedicated **achalasia balloon ≥30 mm**; a TTS balloon dilation is a different procedure and does not count as PD ([[sages-2024-poem]]).

Which of the definitive achalasia therapies a given patient should get — PD, [[heller-myotomy|laparoscopic Heller myotomy (LHM)]], [[poem|peroral endoscopic myotomy (POEM)]], or [[botulinum-toxin|botulinum toxin]] — is a treatment-selection decision and lives on **[[achalasia#Therapeutics|achalasia]]**. This page owns the **procedure**: candidacy, technique, recovery, adverse events, and retreatment.

## Indications and Candidacy

**Indication:** [[achalasia|achalasia]] in a patient who is a candidate for definitive therapy. Where the guidelines place PD among the options:

| Setting | Guidance | Strength / certainty |
|---|---|---|
| Any candidate for definitive therapy | PD and LHM are "both effective and equivalent short- and long-term procedures" ([[acg-2020-achalasia]]) | Strong; High |
| Types I and II | POEM and PD give comparable symptomatic improvement ([[acg-2020-achalasia]]) | Conditional; Low |
| Types I and II | LHM and PD are comparable options; choose by shared decision-making ([[asge-2020-achalasia]]) | "We recommend" (strong) |
| Type III | Tailored POEM or LHM **over PD** — more efficacious disruptive therapy at the LES ([[acg-2020-achalasia]]) | Strong; Moderate |
| Adults, any subtype | **POEM over PD** — the newest guidance ([[sages-2024-poem]]) | Conditional; moderate certainty |

- **Subtype is assigned only on [[high-resolution-manometry|high-resolution manometry (HRM)]] by the [[chicago-classification-v4|Chicago Classification v4.0]]** — the integrated relaxation pressure (IRP) thresholds and type I/II/III criteria live on that page.
- The [[sages-2024-poem|Society of American Gastrointestinal and Endoscopic Surgeons (SAGES) 2024]] preference for POEM rests on **less dysphagia** (risk ratio [RR] 0.45, 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.24–0.83) and **fewer reinterventions for failure** (RR 0.27, 95% CI 0.14–0.54) in one randomized controlled trial (RCT) of 125 patients — **not** on safety: perforation was comparable (odds ratio [OR] 0.99, 95% CI 0.58–1.69; 7 observational studies, 5,244 patients).
- **Where PD is still the better choice** ([[sages-2024-poem]]): elderly or frail patients; patients unable to tolerate a prolonged procedure or general anesthesia; repeated failure after prior intervention; as a **bridge** for symptoms while awaiting POEM or LHM; and where no POEM or LHM capability exists locally. Prior PD did not make a later POEM or LHM more difficult in the panel's experience.

> [!pitfall] Never dilate a patient who could not go to surgery
> Esophageal [[endoscopic-management-of-perforation|perforation]] needing operative repair occurs in **1.9% (range 0%–10%)**, so every patient considered for PD must also be a surgical candidate and consent must state it explicitly. The American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) names **operator expertise and institutional surgical backup** the most important determinants of a safe PD ([[acg-2020-achalasia]]).

- **Predictors of a favorable response** — use these to decide whether to start with PD at all ([[acg-2020-achalasia]]):
  - Age **>45 years**
  - **Female** sex
  - **Narrow (non-dilated)** esophagus
  - Post-PD LES pressure **<10 mm Hg**
- **Young men (<45 y) respond worst** to a 3.0-cm start, thought to reflect thicker LES musculature. In this group, start at **3.5 cm**, or consider [[heller-myotomy|LHM]] or [[poem|POEM]] as the initial approach instead.
- **Periprocedural antithrombotics:** pneumatic and bougie dilation are **high-bleeding-risk** procedures (30-day major bleeding >2%) — management on [[anticoagulation-gi-bleeding]] ([[acg-cag-2022-anticoag-gi-bleeding]]).

## Technique

- **Dilator:** non-radiopaque **graded-size polyethylene balloon (Rigiflex)**. Three sizes: **3.0, 3.5, 4.0 cm** (30, 35, 40 mm).
- **[[endoscopy-sedation|Sedation]]:** always performed under sedation, **with or without fluoroscopy**; fluoroscopic guidance is the most common approach ([[asge-2020-achalasia]]).
- **Positioning — the step that determines effectiveness:** accurate placement of the balloon **across the LES**, confirmed either fluoroscopically or endoscopically by seating the balloon's **two rings at the esophagogastric junction (EGJ)**.
- **Inflation:** pressure sufficient to **obliterate the fluoroscopic waist** (or reach maximum balloon dilation endoscopically) — usually **10–15 psi of air, held 15–60 seconds**.
- ⚠ **No standardized protocol exists.** ACG 2020 states there is substantial practice variability in predilation setup, the dilation protocol itself, and postdilation recovery.

*See Figure 4 in [[acg-2020-achalasia]] for fluoroscopic and endoscopic images of correct two-ring balloon positioning at the EGJ.*

### Balloon Sizing and the Graded Protocol

| Step | Balloon | When to advance |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **3.0 cm** — the default start for most patients | Reassess **symptomatically and objectively at 4–6 weeks** |
| 2 | **3.5 cm** | If still symptomatic at reassessment |
| 3 | **4.0 cm** | If still symptomatic |

*Exception: young men (<45 y) may start at 3.5 cm — see [[#Indications and Candidacy]].*

### Reassessment Between Dilations

- **Objective test: [[achalasia#Timed Barium Esophagram (TBE)|timed barium esophagram (TBE)]]**, at 4–6 weeks alongside symptom assessment, before advancing to the next balloon size.
- **Success = complete esophageal emptying at 1 minute** after the barium bolus ([[acg-2020-achalasia]]).
- **Do not use the Eckardt score or [[high-resolution-manometry|HRM]] alone to define treatment failure**; TBE is the first-line test for continued or recurrent symptoms after definitive therapy ([[acg-2020-achalasia]], Strong/Very Low).
- **Symptoms improved but barium column still >5 cm at 5 minutes** → follow closely, and treat if retention worsens or esophageal dilatation increases.

## Efficacy

Cumulative good-to-excellent symptom relief, average follow-up **1.6 years** (range 0.1–6 y) ([[acg-2020-achalasia]]):

| Cumulative through | Good-to-excellent relief |
|---|---|
| 3.0 cm | **74%** |
| 3.5 cm | **86%** |
| 4.0 cm | **90%** |

- Across all series, good-to-excellent relief after PD ranges **50%–93%**.
- Pooled clinical success (**Eckardt score ≤3** — the score's four components and bands live on [[achalasia]]) is **83% (95% CI 79–85%)** across 52 uncontrolled studies / 4,166 patients at 3–6 months, with mean LES pressure falling **34.5 → 20.8 mm Hg** ([[asge-2020-achalasia]]).

> [!pearl] Judge PD on the completed graded course, not one dilation
> Trials that scored progression to a larger balloon as a failure understate PD; the [[asge-2020-achalasia|American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2020]] guideline calls the response to **graded** dilation the only outcome relevant to practice. In the POEM-versus-PD RCT, PD was capped at 1–2 dilations with 30- or 35-mm balloons and scored **54% at 2 years**, rising to **76%** in the post hoc analysis that included the 14 patients taken to 40 mm — against **85%–90% at 2–5 years** in series that dilated sequentially up to 40 mm ([[acg-2020-achalasia]]).

## Post-Procedure Care

- **Observe in recovery** for signs of perforation: **pain, crepitus, fever**.
- ⚠ **Do NOT perform routine post-dilation gastrografin/barium esophagram** ([[acg-2020-achalasia]], Strong/Low). Reserve contrast esophagram and/or computed tomography (CT) chest/abdomen for **clinical suspicion** of perforation. In the cited retrospective series (n=119), none of the 49 who had a routine esophagram had a perforation.
- **Discharge** with antiemetics and explicit instructions to call for **severe chest pain ± fever** — **delayed perforation can occur after discharge**.

## Adverse Events

### Perforation

- The **most serious** complication of PD.
- **Rate:** **1.9% (range 0%–10%)** overall; median **1.9% (range 0%–16%) in experienced hands** (operators with >100 patients treated).
- *Contradiction surfaced:* the [[asge-2020-achalasia|ASGE 2020]] meta-analysis — same year, different study set — pools perforation at **2.8% (95% CI 2.3–3.5%)** and bleeding requiring intervention at **2% (95% CI 1–4%)**. Quote both figures rather than either alone.
- **Management is size-dependent:**
  - **Small perforation** → conservative therapy may suffice: **antibiotics, parenteral nutrition, stent placement**.
  - **Large perforation with extensive mediastinal contamination** → **surgical repair via thoracotomy** is the best approach.
- **Early recognition drives outcome.**

### GERD

- [[gerd|gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)]] occurs in **15%–35%** after PD → [[proton-pump-inhibitors|proton pump inhibitor (PPI)]] therapy is indicated in those affected ([[acg-2020-achalasia]]).
- [[asge-2020-achalasia|ASGE 2020]] pools **symptomatic** GERD lower, at **9% (95% CI 5–16%)** at ~6 months — the two figures use **different GERD definitions**, not different populations.
- **Recurrent [[dysphagia|dysphagia]] after PD is not automatically recurrent achalasia** — consider a **GERD-related distal [[esophageal-stricture|esophageal stricture]]** as a contributor.

## Retreatment and Salvage

- **Re-evaluate before retreating.** About **10%–15%** of patients have persistent or recurrent symptoms after an initial myotomy; the minimum workup is repeat [[upper-endoscopy|upper endoscopy]], **HRM**, and **TBE** ([[aga-2024-poem-advances]]).
- **PD after failed [[heller-myotomy|LHM]]: 89% success** — systematic review of 87 post-LHM patients, mean **2.5** dilations (range 1–3) at mean 26-month intervals, with extremely low complication rates. The graded statement is *"PD is an appropriate and safe treatment option for patients with achalasia post-initial surgical myotomy or POEM in need of retreatment"* (*Strong; Moderate*, [[acg-2020-achalasia]]).
  - *Contradiction surfaced:* [[asge-2020-achalasia|ASGE 2020]] puts the post-LHM PD response at **50%–75%** from retrospective series. ACG 2020 is the later of the two 2020 documents, so its 89% leads the page; counsel within that range.
- **[[poem|POEM]] after failed PD or LHM: 94%–95.7%** — uncontrolled single-arm figures (a 51-patient prospective multicentre registry and a 46-patient case series; both Eckardt score ≤3). ACG's graded statement covers **safety only**: *"POEM is a safe option in patients with achalasia who have previously undergone PD or LHM"* (*Strong; Low*).
- ⚠ **PD vs POEM head to head, after failed LHM: PD 27% vs POEM 62%** — the **only RCT** in this setting, reported by the newer [[aga-2024-poem-advances|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2024]] Clinical Practice Update (CPU) (Best Practice Advice [BPA] 12); no difference in esophagitis, reflux symptoms, PPI use, or serious adverse events. **This is the number to counsel from when choosing between PD and POEM for a failed myotomy** — the 89% and 94%–95.7% above are single-arm rates from different cohorts and cannot be compared with each other. The decision stays shared, weighing post-procedural reflux, need for repeat intervention, local expertise, and preference.
- After a failed initial myotomy (POEM or LHM), ASGE 2020 suggests **either PD or a redo myotomy** by the same or the alternative technique.
- PD can also be used after failed POEM — retrospective observational data only; ACG expects it to remain effective by analogy to the post-LHM experience.
