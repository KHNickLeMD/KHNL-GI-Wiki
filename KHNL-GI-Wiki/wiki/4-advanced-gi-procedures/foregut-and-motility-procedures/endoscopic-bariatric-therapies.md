## Overview

**Endoscopic bariatric therapies (EBTs)** are devices and techniques that occupy the gap between lifestyle/pharmacologic management of [[obesity]] and [[bariatric-surgery|bariatric/metabolic surgery]].

- Rationale per [[aga-2017-endoscopic-bariatric-therapies|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2017]]: surgery induces **20%–40% total body weight loss (TBWL)** and reduces obesity-related morbidity and mortality, but at the cost of increased morbidity and rare mortality. EBTs are less invasive, lower cost, and applicable to a larger segment of the population with moderate obesity.
- Obesity affects **35% of US adults** (body mass index [BMI] ≥30 kg/m²); obesity with its comorbidities accounts for **~21% of US health expenditure**.
- **Four EBTs were US Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved** as of the 2017 review.

> [!pearl] Plan for weight regain before the device goes in
> The AGA 2017 message is **programmatic, not device-specific**: weight loss after an EBT is variable and can be temporary — especially with removable, anatomy-preserving devices — so weight recidivism should be anticipated and managed. Obesity is a chronic disease and **no single intervention, device, or operation cures it** ([[aga-2017-endoscopic-bariatric-therapies]]).

> **The AGA 2017 Clinical Practice Update is ungraded.** Its 7 Best Practice Advice statements carry no evidence grade, strength rating, or GRADE assessment; the authors describe it as a consensus summary of expert opinion without a formal systematic evidence review — not graded evidence. The balloon *recommendations* on this topic are governed by the newer, formally graded [[aga-2021-intragastric-balloons|AGA 2021 intragastric balloon guideline]], which graded **endoscopically placed balloons only**, leaving swallowable (non-endoscopic) balloons ungraded (scope and eligibility detail on [[intragastric-balloon]]).

## Classification and Mechanism

| Site | Devices / techniques | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Gastric** | space-occupying balloons and devices ([[intragastric-balloon\|intragastric balloon (IGB)]], TransPyloric Shuttle, Full Sense), endoscopic suturing/plication (endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty, primary obesity surgery endoluminal), aspiration therapy | Restriction, plus altered gastric mechanical/chemical receptor signaling, gastric emptying, and orexigenic hormone levels |
| **Small bowel** | duodenojejunal bypass sleeve, gastroduodenojejunal bypass sleeve, duodenal mucosal resurfacing, self-assembling magnets | Exploits the proximal small intestine's role in glucose homeostasis and incretin signaling |

## Patient Selection and Program Requirements

*AGA 2017 Best Practice Advice (BPA) 1–5, ungraded.*

- **Indication (BPA 1):** consider EBTs in patients with obesity **unsuccessful in losing or maintaining weight loss with lifestyle interventions**.
- **Bridge indication (BPA 2):** EBTs can be used in **severe obesity as a bridge to traditional bariatric surgery**, and as a bridge to unrelated interventions blocked by weight limits (orthopedic surgery, organ transplantation).
- **Structured program (BPA 3):** use EBTs as part of a structured weight-loss program with **dietary intervention, exercise therapy, and behavior modification — in both the active weight-loss phase and the long-term maintenance phase**.
- **Pre-enrollment screening (BPA 4):** screen all potential candidates with a **comprehensive evaluation for medical conditions, comorbidities, and psychosocial or behavioral patterns** contributing to the condition.
- **Prospective follow-up (BPA 5):** follow patients prospectively to capture the program's impact on weight, weight-related comorbidities, and all related adverse outcomes. **Poor responders should be identified and offered a detailed evaluation and alternative therapy.**
- **Multidisciplinary team:** gastroenterologists adopting EBTs should develop expertise in obesity as a disease alongside medical obesity specialists, behavioral therapy professionals, registered dietitians, and bariatric surgeons.

**AGA 2017 gives no BMI cut-points, no contraindication list, and no age criteria for EBTs** — eligibility is framed only by failure of lifestyle intervention and by the bridge indication, and "severe obesity" in BPA 2 is not defined by a BMI. (The BMI 30–40 kg/m² range of the US balloon trials is on [[intragastric-balloon]]; surgical BMI thresholds are on [[bariatric-surgery]].)

## Gastric Devices

Balloon-by-balloon fill volumes, dwell times, efficacy, and adverse-event rates live on **[[intragastric-balloon]]**, which also carries the AGA 2021 graded recommendations ([[proton-pump-inhibitors|proton pump inhibitor (PPI)]] prophylaxis, antiemesis, supplementation, post-removal strategy).

### Space-Occupying and Pylorus-Modulating Devices

- **TransPyloric Shuttle** — large spherical silicone bulb tethered to a smaller cylindrical bulb; the small bulb advances into the duodenum with peristalsis, creating an **intermittent pyloric seal that delays gastric emptying** and prolongs satiety. The large bulb's size prevents migration out of the stomach.
- **Full Sense Device** — modified fully covered gastroesophageal stent: cylindrical esophageal component plus a gastric disk on struts applying pressure to the cardia to induce satiety. **No outcome data are reported for this device** in AGA 2017.

### Endoscopic Suturing and Plication

- **Endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty (ESG)** — series of endoluminal **full-thickness** sutures through the gastric wall from the prepyloric antrum to the gastroesophageal junction, reducing capacity of the whole stomach along the greater curvature (a "sleeve").
- **Primary obesity surgery endoluminal (POSE)** — transmural tissue-anchor plications restricting accommodation of the gastric fundus, plus **3 additional plications in the distal gastric body** to delay emptying.

> [!pitfall] POSE's sham-controlled result is far below its uncontrolled data
> Open-label single-arm and nonblinded trials reported **%TBWL 12.7%–15.5%**; the pivotal blinded sham-controlled randomized controlled trial (RCT) found only **4.94% ± 7 vs 1.38% ± 5.6 at 12 mo**. Weigh uncontrolled EBT series accordingly ([[aga-2017-endoscopic-bariatric-therapies]]).

### Aspiration Therapy

- **A-Tube / AspireAssist** — a silicone percutaneous gastrostomy tube with a skin port, through which the patient disposes of part of each ingested meal.
- **Rate of spontaneous fistula closure after A-Tube removal is not yet clear.**

## Small-Bowel Devices

- **EndoBarrier duodenojejunal bypass sleeve (DJBS)** — impermeable polymer liner anchored in the duodenal bulb by a barbed nitinol crown, extending **65 cm** into the small bowel; endoscopic removal at 12 months. Food bypasses the duodenum and proximal jejunum without mixing with pancreaticobiliary secretions until further downstream, manipulating the entero-insulin system.
  - Glycemic effect: hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) **−0.7** (95% confidence interval [CI] −1.76 to 0.2; *P* = .16) at 12 wk; **−1.7** (95% CI −2.5 to −0.86; *P* < .001) at 24 wk; **−1.5** (95% CI −2.2 to −0.78; *P* < .001) at 52 wk — an additional **−1%** (95% CI −1.67 to −0.4; *P* = .001) vs controls.
  - Mean percent excess weight loss (%EWL) difference vs control across 4 RCTs: **9.4%** (95% CI 8.26–10.65).
  - **Second-generation DJBS** devices with atraumatic anchoring and retrieval systems were in human trials at the time of review.

> [!pitfall] Hepatic abscess stopped the US EndoBarrier trial
> The pivotal multicenter double-blinded sham-controlled US trial was **terminated early after 325 of 500 enrollments because of a 3.5% incidence of hepatic abscess** ([[aga-2017-endoscopic-bariatric-therapies]]). The meta-analysis of earlier series put liver abscess at only 0.13%.

- **Earlier-development small-bowel EBTs:**
  - *Gastroduodenojejunal bypass sleeve.*
  - *Duodenal mucosal resurfacing* — thermal ablation of superficial duodenal mucosa by a catheter delivering hot water after a submucosal lift, hypothesized to reset diseased duodenal enteroendocrine cells and restore incretin signaling, with only a minimal transient fall in body weight.
  - *Self-assembling magnets* — create a dual-path enteral bypass between proximal jejunum and ileum, diverting bile and nutrients to the terminal ileum.

## Outcomes and Adverse Events

Best available evidence per non-balloon device ([[aga-2017-endoscopic-bariatric-therapies|AGA 2017]] Table 1; balloon-by-balloon data on [[intragastric-balloon]]). Devices were not compared head to head — study designs, populations, and time points differ.

| Device | Best evidence | Weight loss | Key adverse events |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TransPyloric Shuttle** | Prospective single-arm, n = 10 | %TBWL **14.5% ± 5.8 at 6 mo** | Gastric erosion **75%**, gastric ulcer **50%**, intolerance/early removal 10%, nausea 45% |
| **Full Sense** | None reported | — | — |
| **ESG** | Multicenter prospective, 242 patients at 3 centers | %TBWL **16.8% ± 6.4 at 6 mo** (n = 137) · **18.2% ± 10 at 12 mo** (n = 53) · **19.8% ± 11.6 at 18 mo** (n = 30) | **5 (2%)**, all recovered fully without surgery: 2 perigastric fluid collections drained percutaneously, 1 self-limited hemorrhage from splenic laceration, 1 pulmonary embolism at 72 h, 1 pneumoperitoneum + pneumothorax needing a chest tube |
| **POSE** | Pivotal US blinded sham-controlled RCT, 221 vs 111 | %TBWL at 12 mo **4.94% ± 7 vs 1.38% ± 5.6** | Serious adverse events **4.7%** — vomiting prolonging admission 1.9%, nausea 1.6%, pain 0.4%, extragastric bleeding requiring open surgery 0.4%, hepatic abscess requiring drainage 0.4%. Abdominal pain 45.2%, nausea 21.3%, vomiting 19.5%, [[gerd\|gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)]] 8.1% |
| **Aspiration therapy** | Pivotal US open-label RCT, 111 vs 60 | %TBWL among completers at 12 mo **14.2% ± 9.8% (n = 82) vs 4.9% ± 7% (n = 31)**; **≥25% EWL in 68.3% vs 25.8%**, **≥10% TBWL in 69.5% vs 19.4%** | Stoma granulation tissue 40.5%, stoma infection 14.4%, peritonitis 0.9%, gastric ulcer 0.9%; intolerance/early removal **26% before 52 wk, 27% after** |
| **EndoBarrier DJBS** | Meta-analysis (n = 105) + pivotal blinded sham-controlled RCT (213 vs 107), stopped early | Meta-analysis **%EWL 35.3% at 12 mo** (95% CI 24.6–46.1); RCT %TBWL **7.7% ± 9.6% vs 2.1% ± 5.4%** (*P* < .0001) | RCT: **hepatic abscess 3.5%**, early retrieval 10.9%. Meta-analysis: abdominal pain 58.7%, nausea/vomiting 39.4%, intolerance/early removal 18%, migration 5%, gastrointestinal bleeding 4%, device obstruction 3.5% |

## Technique

Balloon placement and removal technique is on [[intragastric-balloon]].

- **ESG** — double-channel therapeutic gastroscope with a commercially available endoscopic suturing device. A **tissue helix** captures the target site on the gastric wall and retracts it into the suturing arm, enabling full-thickness suture placement; sutures run from the prepyloric antrum to the gastroesophageal junction.
- **POSE** — a large overtube-style peroral incisionless operating platform with **4 working channels**: a slim endoscope plus three instruments — a jawed grasper that creates and approximates full-thickness **serosa-to-serosa** tissue folds, a helical-tipped tissue grasper that helps capture target tissue for the miniplication, and a needle-tipped suture-anchor delivery catheter that installs a pair of tissue anchors joined by suture, holding the plication **until serosal fusion**.
- **Aspiration therapy** — A-Tube inserted like a standard [[enteral-access|percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy (PEG)]] tube. **2 weeks later** the external portion is shortened and a valved skin port is attached flush with the skin. The AspireAssist device connects to the port; an attached water reservoir flushes boluses of tap water into the stomach to facilitate aspiration cycles. Aspiration is by **siphon, ideally 20 min after the meal**, removes **about a third of the meal**, and takes **~10–15 min**.
- **EndoBarrier DJBS** — sleeve and anchoring crown restrained in a delivery capsule, advanced to the duodenal bulb **over a stiff wire under endoscopic and fluoroscopic guidance**; sleeve then advanced to the proximal jejunum and the anchoring crown deployed in the bulb. Removal at 12 mo: grasp the polypropylene drawstring with a custom device that collapses the crown into a **foreign-body retrieval hood**, avoiding gastric or esophageal trauma on withdrawal.
- **TransPyloric Shuttle** — delivery system advanced through an **overtube** into the stomach, where the device is deployed and self-assembles.
- **Full Sense** — deployed and removed endoscopically.

## Training and Credentialing

- **Knowledge base (BPA 6):** clinicians should have comprehensive knowledge of the **indications, contraindications, risks, benefits, and outcomes of individual EBTs**, plus practical knowledge of the risks and benefits of alternative obesity therapies.
- **Privileging (BPA 7):** institutions should establish **specific guidelines applied consistently across disciplines** for granting EBT privileges, reflecting the knowledge and technical skill required before privileges are granted.
- Clinicians also managing the complications of bariatric/metabolic surgery endoscopically carry a separate, more demanding skill set — fluoroscopy, stent deployment and retrieval, stenosis management, percutaneous drain management; see [[bariatric-surgery-leak]].

## Open Questions

- **Matching patient to device is unsolved** — AGA 2017 calls for research identifying the physiologic, behavioral, and clinical predictors of success for each EBT.
- **Combination and sequencing untested** — tandem and sequential use of EBTs with obesity pharmacotherapy, on top of a comprehensive lifestyle program, is called the key to durable weight loss but has not been studied.
- **Training gap** — obesity-management principles should be better incorporated into the gastroenterology fellowship curriculum.
