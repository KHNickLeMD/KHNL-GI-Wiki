## Overview

**Endoscopic bariatric therapies (EBTs)** are devices and techniques that occupy the gap between lifestyle/pharmacologic management of [[obesity]] and [[bariatric-surgery|bariatric/metabolic surgery]].

- Rationale per [[aga-2017-endoscopic-bariatric-therapies|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2017]]: surgery induces **20%–40% total body weight loss (TBWL)** and reduces obesity-related morbidity and mortality, but at the cost of increased morbidity and rare mortality. EBTs are less invasive, lower cost, and applicable to a larger segment of the population with moderate obesity.
- Obesity affects **35% of US adults** (body mass index [BMI] ≥30 kg/m²); obesity with its comorbidities accounts for **~21% of US health expenditure**.
- **Four EBTs were US Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved** as of the 2017 review.
- Central message is **programmatic, not device-specific**: weight loss after an EBT is variable and can be temporary — especially with removable, anatomy-preserving devices — so weight recidivism should be anticipated and managed. Obesity is a chronic disease and **no single intervention, device, or operation cures it**.

> **The AGA 2017 Clinical Practice Update is ungraded.** Its 7 Best Practice Advice statements carry no evidence grade, strength rating, or GRADE assessment; the authors describe it as a consensus summary of expert opinion without a formal systematic evidence review. Do not cite it as graded evidence. The balloon *recommendations* on this topic are governed by the newer, formally graded [[aga-2021-intragastric-balloons|AGA 2021 intragastric balloon guideline]] — which graded **endoscopically placed balloons only**, leaving swallowable (non-endoscopic) balloons ungraded (scope and eligibility detail on [[intragastric-balloon]]).

## Classification and Mechanism

| Site | Devices / techniques | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Gastric** | space-occupying balloons and devices ([[intragastric-balloon\|intragastric balloon (IGB)]], TransPyloric Shuttle, Full Sense), endoscopic suturing/plication (endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty, primary obesity surgery endolumenal), aspiration therapy | Restriction, plus altered gastric mechanical/chemical receptor signaling, gastric emptying, and orexigenic hormone levels |
| **Small bowel** | duodenojejunal bypass sleeve, gastroduodenojejunal bypass sleeve, duodenal mucosal resurfacing, self-assembling magnets | Exploits the proximal small intestine's role in glucose homeostasis and incretin signaling |

## Patient Selection and Program Requirements

*AGA 2017 Best Practice Advice (BPA) 1–5, ungraded.*

- **Indication (BPA 1):** consider EBTs in patients with obesity **unsuccessful in losing or maintaining weight loss with lifestyle interventions**.
- **Bridge indication (BPA 2):** EBTs can be used in **severe obesity as a bridge to traditional bariatric surgery**, and as a bridge to unrelated interventions blocked by weight limits (orthopedic surgery, organ transplantation).
- **Structured program (BPA 3):** use EBTs as part of a structured weight-loss program with **dietary intervention, exercise therapy, and behavior modification — in both the active weight-loss phase and the long-term maintenance phase**.
- **Pre-enrollment screening (BPA 4):** screen all potential candidates with a **comprehensive evaluation for medical conditions, comorbidities, and psychosocial or behavioral patterns** contributing to the condition.
- **Prospective follow-up (BPA 5):** follow patients prospectively to capture the program's impact on weight, weight-related comorbidities, and all related adverse outcomes. **Poor responders should be identified and offered a detailed evaluation and alternative therapy.**
- **Multidisciplinary team:** gastroenterologists adopting EBTs should develop expertise in obesity as a disease alongside medical obesity specialists, behavioral therapy professionals, registered dietitians, and bariatric surgeons.

**AGA 2017 gives no BMI cut-points, no contraindication list, and no age criteria for EBTs** — eligibility is framed only by failure of lifestyle intervention and by the bridge indication. (The BMI 30–40 kg/m² range of the US balloon trials is on [[intragastric-balloon]].)

## Gastric Devices

Balloon-by-balloon fill volumes, dwell times, efficacy, and adverse-event rates live on **[[intragastric-balloon]]**, which also carries the AGA 2021 graded recommendations ([[proton-pump-inhibitors|proton pump inhibitor (PPI)]] prophylaxis, antiemesis, supplementation, post-removal strategy).

### Space-Occupying and Pylorus-Modulating Devices

- **TransPyloric Shuttle** — large spherical silicone bulb tethered to a smaller cylindrical bulb; the small bulb advances into the duodenum with peristalsis, creating an **intermittent pyloric seal that delays gastric emptying**.
  - Prospective single-arm (n = 10): **%TBWL 14.5% ± 5.8 at 6 mo**; gastric erosion 75%, gastric ulcer 50%.
- **Full Sense Device** — modified fully covered gastroesophageal stent: cylindrical esophageal component plus a gastric disk on struts applying pressure to the cardia to induce satiety.

### Endoscopic Suturing and Plication

- **Endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty (ESG)** — endoluminal full-thickness sutures from the prepyloric antrum to the gastroesophageal junction reduce capacity along the greater curvature.
  - Multicenter study, 242 patients at 3 centers: **%TBWL 16.8% ± 6.4 at 6 mo** (n = 137), **18.2% ± 10 at 12 mo** (n = 53), **19.8% ± 11.6 at 18 mo** (n = 30).
  - Adverse events in **5 (2%)**, all recovering fully without surgery.
- **Primary obesity surgery endolumenal (POSE)** — transmural tissue-anchor plications restricting fundic accommodation, plus 3 distal gastric body plications to delay emptying.
  - Pivotal US blinded sham-controlled randomized controlled trial (RCT) (221 vs 111): **%TBWL at 12 mo 4.94% ± 7 vs 1.38% ± 5.6**; serious adverse event rate **4.7%**.

### Aspiration Therapy

- **A-Tube / AspireAssist** — percutaneous gastrostomy tube; aspiration by siphon **~20 min after a meal** removes **about a third of the meal**, taking 10–15 min.
- Pivotal US open-label RCT (111 vs 60): **%TBWL among completers at 12 mo 14.2% ± 9.8%** (n = 82) **vs 4.9% ± 7%** (n = 31).
- Adverse events: stoma granulation tissue 40.5%, stoma infection 14.4%, peritonitis 0.9%, gastric ulcer 0.9%.
- **Rate of spontaneous fistula closure after A-Tube removal is not yet clear.**

## Small-Bowel Devices

- **EndoBarrier duodenojejunal bypass sleeve (DJBS)** — impermeable polymer liner anchored in the duodenal bulb by a barbed nitinol crown, extending **65 cm** into the small bowel; endoscopic removal at 12 months.
  - Meta-analysis: **35.3% excess weight loss (%EWL) at 12 mo** (95% confidence interval [CI] 24.6–46.1). Across 4 RCTs the mean %EWL difference vs control was **9.4%** (95% CI 8.26–10.65).
  - Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c): **−0.7** (95% CI −1.76 to 0.2; *P* = .16) at 12 wk; **−1.7** (95% CI −2.5 to −0.86; *P* < .001) at 24 wk; **−1.5** (95% CI −2.2 to −0.78; *P* < .001) at 52 wk — an additional **−1%** (95% CI −1.67 to −0.4; *P* = .001) vs controls.
  - ⚠ **US multicenter double-blinded sham-controlled trial terminated early after 325 of 500 enrollments because of a 3.5% incidence of hepatic abscess.** At termination: %TBWL 7.7% ± 9.6% vs 2.1% ± 5.4% (*P* < .0001); HbA1c −1.1% ± 1.5% vs −0.3% ± 1.6%; early device retrieval for adverse events in **10.9%**.
- **Earlier-development small-bowel EBTs:**
  - *Gastroduodenojejunal bypass sleeve.*
  - *Duodenal mucosal resurfacing* — thermal ablation of superficial duodenal mucosa after submucosal lift, hypothesized to reset diseased duodenal enteroendocrine cells.
  - *Self-assembling magnets* — create a dual-path jejunoileal bypass.

## Training and Credentialing

- **Knowledge base (BPA 6):** clinicians should have comprehensive knowledge of the **indications, contraindications, risks, benefits, and outcomes of individual EBTs**, plus practical knowledge of the risks and benefits of alternative obesity therapies.
- **Privileging (BPA 7):** institutions should establish **specific guidelines applied consistently across disciplines** for granting EBT privileges, reflecting the knowledge and technical skill required before privileges are granted.
- Clinicians also managing the complications of bariatric/metabolic surgery endoscopically carry a separate, more demanding skill set — fluoroscopy, stent deployment and retrieval, stenosis management, percutaneous drain management; see [[bariatric-surgery-leak]].

## Open Questions

- **Durability is unresolved** — weight loss after EBT is variable and can be temporary. The authors call for research matching a patient to the EBT most likely to succeed, and for study of **combined and sequential EBT plus obesity pharmacotherapy**.
- **Two devices carry unresolved safety signals:** EndoBarrier DJBS (3.5% hepatic abscess, trial stopped early) and aspiration therapy (unknown rate of spontaneous fistula closure).
- No eligibility thresholds are supplied by AGA 2017 (see above).
