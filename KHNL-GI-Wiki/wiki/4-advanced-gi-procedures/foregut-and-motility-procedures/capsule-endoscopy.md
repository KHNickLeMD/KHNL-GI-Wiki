## Overview

- Swallowed wireless camera capsule measuring **26 × 11 mm**, imaging at **2 frames/s over an 8–12 h** period; allows noninvasive evaluation of the **entire small bowel in 79–90%** of patients. ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]])
- Sequencing with [[device-assisted-enteroscopy|device-assisted enteroscopy (DAE)]], computed tomography (CT)/magnetic resonance (MR) enterography, and angiography lives on [[small-bowel-bleeding]] — this page covers the test.

## Indications

### Small-Bowel Bleeding

| Society · statement | Statement | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding\|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2015]] Rec 5 | *"VCE [video capsule endoscopy] should be considered a **first-line procedure** for small bowel evaluation after upper and lower GI [gastrointestinal] sources have been excluded, including second-look endoscopy when indicated."* | **Strong recommendation, moderate** level of evidence |
| ACG 2015 Rec 10 | *"VCE should be performed **before** deep enteroscopy to increase diagnostic yield. Initial deep enteroscopy can be considered in cases of **massive hemorrhage** or when VCE is contraindicated."* | **Strong recommendation, high** level of evidence |
| ACG 2015 Rec 6 | *"Owing to the **lower detection rate of lesions in the duodenum and proximal jejunum** with VCE, push enteroscopy should be performed if proximal lesions are suspected."* | **Strong recommendation, very low** level of evidence |
| [[asge-2017-small-bowel-bleeding\|American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2017]] Rec 2 | *"We **suggest** VCE as the initial test for patients with overt or occult small-bowel bleeding. Positive VCE results should be followed with push enteroscopy if within reach or DAE."* | Weaker ("we suggest"); **low** quality evidence |
| ASGE 2017 Rec 3 | *"We suggest DAE or push enteroscopy if VCE is **unavailable or nondiagnostic**"* in overt small-bowel bleeding | Weaker ("we suggest"); **low** quality evidence |
| ASGE 2017 Rec 4 | In select circumstances (high suspicion of small-bowel [[angioectasia\|angiectasias]], surgically altered anatomy), DAE may be considered as the **initial** small-bowel procedure | Weaker ("we suggest"); **low** quality evidence |

- **Terms ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]):** after a normal [[upper-endoscopy|upper]] and lower endoscopic examination and **before** capsule endoscopy = **"potential small bowel bleeding"**; **small bowel bleeding** = source found distal to the ampulla of Vater and/or proximal to the ileocecal valve. **Overt** = hematochezia or melena; **occult small bowel bleeding** = [[iron-deficiency-anemia|iron-deficiency anemia]] **with or without guaiac-positive stools**, with a small bowel source found (the older obscure-bleeding definition keyed on a positive fecal occult blood test).

### Crohn's Disease

- **Key concept 27 ([[acg-2025-crohns]]):** *"Video capsule endoscopy is a useful **adjunct** in the diagnosis of patients with small bowel CD [Crohn's disease] in patients in whom there is a **high index of suspicion** of disease."*
- **Key concept 28:** *"Patients with **obstructive symptoms** should have small bowel imaging and/or **patency capsule** evaluation before video capsule endoscopy to decrease the risk of capsule retention."*
- Incremental diagnostic yield of VCE over comparators in suspected CD: **32% vs barium studies, 47% vs CT enterography (CTE), 22% vs [[colonoscopy|ileocolonoscopy]].**
- Capsule endoscopy may also identify a site for **directed biopsy** to establish the diagnosis ([[acg-2025-crohns]]).
- **Lewis score** — scores **3 endoscopic parameters: villous appearance, ulcers, and strictures**; built into some capsule software platforms; quantifies small-bowel inflammatory burden and assists CD diagnosis ([[acg-2025-crohns]]).

**Lewis score — how it is computed** ([[gralnek-2008-lewis-score]]). Divide the small bowel into **three tertiles by transit time** (if the capsule never reaches the colon, use the last image). Score each tertile:

| Parameter | Number | Longitudinal extent | Descriptor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Villous appearance** | Normal **0** · Oedematous **1** | Short **8** · Long **12** · Whole tertile **20** | Single **1** · Patchy **14** · Diffuse **17** |
| **Ulcer** | None **0** · Single **3** · Few **5** · Multiple **10** | Short **5** · Long **10** · Whole tertile **15** | <¼ **9** · ¼–½ **12** · >½ **18** |

Then score **stenosis once for the whole study**: number (none **0** · single **14** · multiple **20**) × ulcerated (**24**) or non-ulcerated (**2**) × traversed (**7**) or not traversed (**10**).

> **Total = (worst tertile) + (stenosis).** Per tertile: (villous number × extent × descriptor) + (ulcer number × extent × size).

| Score | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| **<135** | Normal / clinically insignificant |
| **135–790** | **Mild** |
| **≥790** | **Moderate to severe** |

- **Definitions:** oedema = **villous width ≥ villous height**, distinct from (not contiguous with) an ulcer · ulcer = mucosal break with **white or yellow base and a red or pink collar** · ulcer size = **largest ulcer in that tertile, collar included**, as % of the capsule image · **few = 2–7, multiple = ≥8** · extent **short ≤10%, long 11–50%, whole >50%** of the tertile.

> [!pitfall] Do not add the three tertiles together
> The Lewis score carries **only the most severe tertile**, plus the stenosis score — the authors were concerned that adding the tertile scores in mild disease "could provide a total number greater than one tertile of severe disease." ([[gralnek-2008-lewis-score]])

- ⚠ **The bands overlap in the derivation data** — no disease ranged 0–271, mild 204–4050, moderate/severe 331–13,019; the authors note significant overlap between the cut-offs, which are crossover points of relative-frequency curves ([[gralnek-2008-lewis-score]]).
- ⚠ **Reader agreement is moderate** — κ **0.48** villous appearance, **0.66** ulcer, **0.58** stenosis. Erythema (κ **0.02** in the initial evaluation) was among the parameters eliminated from the final index ([[gralnek-2008-lewis-score]]).
- **Derivation:** 44 de-identified studies — Crohn's disease 22, [[nsaid-enteropathy|nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) enteropathy]] 12, other mucosal breaks 10. Weights were adjusted until the score reproduced the expert panel's **severity ranking** with zero error (error = a lower-ranked case scoring higher than a higher-ranked one) ([[gralnek-2008-lewis-score]]).
- ⚠ Specificity is questioned — **no consensus exists on which capsule findings constitute a diagnosis of CD** ([[acg-2025-crohns]]).

### Polyposis Surveillance

| Syndrome | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| [[peutz-jeghers-syndrome\|Peutz-Jeghers]] | *"Baseline small bowel surveillance using **video capsule endoscopy or MRE [magnetic resonance enterography]** performed between ages **8–10 years** or earlier if symptomatic. If no polyps at the initial examination, surveillance resumes at **age 18**. Because of the risk of small bowel intussusception, small bowel surveillance in adulthood continues **throughout life every 2–3 years**."* **Strong recommendation, low quality of evidence** ([[aga-2022-hamartomatous-polyposis]]). VCE is **more sensitive than MRE for small-bowel polyps** and is an alternative for patients unable to undergo MRE. |
| [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis\|Familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP)]] | Capsule endoscopy (CE) or MRE for **index small-bowel screening in Spigelman stage III–IV or before duodenectomy**, surveillance every **2–4 y** (Table 6; very low quality); jejunal/ileal polyps **>1 cm** found on CE or MRE → double- or single-balloon enteroscopy for [[polypectomy]] (very low quality). Deep enteroscopy is **not** for routine small-bowel screening. CE is the **preferred imaging modality** (false-negative rates up to **42%** for polyps >10 mm with contrast studies) and is **more sensitive than MRE for smaller polyps**; the two perform **equally for polyps >15 mm**, but MRE is more reliable for polyp location/size and images outside the GI tract. ([[asge-2020-fap]]) |

### Esophageal Capsule — not a recommended screening test

- **[[barretts-esophagus|Barrett's esophagus]] screening:** pooled **sensitivity 77%, specificity 86%** across 9 studies and 618 patients; specificity varies with the reference standard (**90%** against endoscopic confirmation, **73%** against histologic). Dedicated screening studies gave sensitivity **78%** and **60%**. ([[aga-2018-barretts-screening-techniques]])
- ⚠ **Disadvantages as a screening tool:** **no biopsy capability**, limited control of the device even when tethered, and **esophageal capsule retention in 0.7%–2.2%** of patients — usually from an **unsuspected [[esophageal-stricture|esophageal stricture]]**. Its sedation-free cost advantage is **negated by the cost of the capsule and its equipment**.
- American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2018 Rec 2: *"We do not recommend the use of any alternative test to screen for Barrett's esophagus at this time"* — this covers esophageal capsule endoscopy ([[aga-2018-barretts-screening-techniques]]). The swallowed sponge (Cytosponge) is a different device; see [[barretts-esophagus]].

## Diagnostic Performance

| Metric | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Diagnostic yield, suspected small-bowel bleeding | **38–83%** | [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]] |
| **Positive predictive value** | **94–97%** | [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]] |
| **Negative predictive value** | **83–100%** | [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]] |
| Negative predictive value, [[crohns-disease\|Crohn's disease]] | **96%** | [[acg-2025-crohns]] |
| False-negative rate, **general** | **10–36%** | [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]] (limitations of VCE) |
| False-negative rate, **all small-bowel findings** | **11%** | [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]] (VCE-guided DAE) |
| False-negative rate, **neoplasms** | **up to 19%** | [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]] (VCE-guided DAE) |
| Incidental findings in healthy volunteers (lack of specificity) | **14%** | [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]] |
| Findings leading to endoscopic/surgical intervention or change in medical management | **37–87%** | [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]], [[asge-2017-small-bowel-bleeding]] |
| Remain transfusion-free without rebleed after VCE-directed intervention | **50–66%** | [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]] |
| Rebleeding after a **negative** capsule study | **6–27%** (ACG 2015) — but **6–11%** per ASGE 2017 | ⚠ see below |

- ⚠ **Rebleeding after a negative VCE — guidelines differ:** ACG 2015 **6–27%**; the newer ASGE 2017 "generally low (**6%–11%**)." The newer figure leads.
- **Two false-negative rates, both printed by ACG 2015 and not reconciled:** **10–36%** (limitations section) and **11% for all small bowel findings, up to 19% for neoplasms** (VCE-guided enteroscopy section). Neither is "the" false-negative rate.
- Hence a negative VCE with **high clinical suspicion** still goes on to double-balloon enteroscopy ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]; pathway on [[small-bowel-bleeding]]).

## What Raises the Yield

Predictors of a positive capsule study ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]):

- **Hemoglobin <10 g/dL**
- **Duration of bleeding >6 months**
- **More than one bleeding episode**
- **Overt vs occult bleeding — 60% vs 46%**
- **VCE performed within 2 weeks of the bleeding episode — 91% vs 34%**
- **VCE within 48–72 h of overt bleeding has the greatest yield for lesion detection**
- Also: male sex, age >60 y and inpatient status (independent predictors); cardiac and renal comorbidities

## Limitations

- **No therapeutic capability**; cannot control capsule movement; difficult to localize the lesion precisely ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]).
- **Fails to identify the major papilla in a majority of cases** → may miss important duodenal lesions from rapid transit through the duodenal loop. Improves to **60%** with a dual-camera capsule, but VCE still misses clinically important duodenal and proximal jejunal lesions and **cannot be relied on alone to exclude bleeding there** — push enteroscopy if proximal lesions are suspected (ACG Rec 6, above; [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]).
- In FAP, CE **underestimates duodenal polyps and cannot reliably visualize the ampulla**, so it does **not** replace direct endoscopic evaluation of the duodenum and ampulla. ([[asge-2020-fap]])
- **[[endoscopic-management-of-perforation|Perforation]]** is the most serious reported complication — **exceedingly rare** ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]).

## Capsule Retention and the Patency Capsule

| Population | Retention rate |
|---|---|
| Evaluation for potential small-bowel bleeding | **~1.5%** ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]); **1.4%** in one large study ([[asge-2017-small-bowel-bleeding]]) |
| **Crohn's disease** | **up to 13%** ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]) |
| **Suspected** Crohn's disease | **0%–5.4%**, higher in **known** CD ([[acg-2025-crohns]]) |
| CD, pooled analysis — **overall** | **3.32%** (95% confidence interval [CI] 2.62–4.2) ([[asge-2025-ibd-endoscopy-consensus]]) |
| CD, pooled — **suspected** vs **established** | **2.35%** (1.31–4.19) vs **4.63%** (3.42–6.25) ([[asge-2025-ibd-endoscopy-consensus]]) |
| CD, pooled — **adult** vs **pediatric** | **3.49%** vs **1.64%** ([[asge-2025-ibd-endoscopy-consensus]]) |

**Who needs imaging or a patency capsule first:**

| Setting | Before VCE | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Suspected obstruction or strictures | Retention risk limits use **until patency is documented**; screening small-bowel radiographs have not eliminated the problem, the patency capsule may be useful | [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding\|ACG 2015]] |
| Small-bowel bleeding with established [[inflammatory-bowel-disease\|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]], prior radiation therapy, previous small-bowel surgery and/or suspected small-bowel stenosis | CTE **could be considered before VCE** (Rec 14 · strong recommendation, very low level of evidence) | [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding\|ACG 2015]] |
| Small-bowel bleeding with suspected stricture, bowel obstruction or IBD ("potential reasons for capsule retention") | Multiphase CTE or MRE **first** | [[asge-2017-small-bowel-bleeding\|ASGE 2017]] |
| CD with **obstructive symptoms** | Small-bowel imaging and/or **patency capsule** (key concept 28) | [[acg-2025-crohns\|ACG 2025]] |
| Suspected small-bowel CD **without** obstructive symptoms, ileocolonoscopy and/or small-bowel imaging (CTE, MRE or [[intestinal-ultrasound\|intestinal ultrasound]]) **not conclusive** | Capsule endoscopy **may be performed after a passed patency capsule** (Statement 10 · consensus: strongly agree 42.9%, agree 50.0%) | [[asge-2025-ibd-endoscopy-consensus\|ASGE 2025]] |
| CD with obstructive symptoms, inconclusive abdominal imaging, or prior bowel surgery | Patency capsule or CT and/or magnetic resonance imaging before VCE **may be used** as a safety precaution | [[asge-2025-ibd-endoscopy-consensus\|ASGE 2025]] |

- The ASGE 2025 panel did **not** reach consensus on push or device-assisted enteroscopy for suspected small-bowel CD when ileocolonoscopy and imaging are not feasible or inconclusive ([[device-assisted-enteroscopy]]).
- A **failed patency capsule study** is itself associated with **worse long-term clinical outcomes** than successful passage, regardless of CD phenotype. ([[acg-2025-crohns]])
- In FAP, CE can be performed successfully even after prior bowel surgery, though **capsule retention in the pouch** has been reported. ([[asge-2020-fap]])

## Repeat VCE

- Repeat VCE **increases diagnostic yield even when the first study is negative**; particularly beneficial when bleeding **changes from occult to overt** or there is a **hemoglobin drop ≥4 g/dL**. ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]])
- Second-look VCE after an initial nondiagnostic study was **positive in 49%** in one prospective series of persistent small-bowel bleeding; in [[iron-deficiency-anemia|iron-deficiency anemia (IDA)]], **35%** of second studies showed positive/suspected findings and **10%** changed management. ([[asge-2017-small-bowel-bleeding]])
