## Overview

- Swallowed wireless camera capsule (26×11 mm) imaging at ~2 frames/s over 8–12 h; evaluates 79–90% of the small bowel.
- **First-line small-bowel test** after exclusion of upper/lower sources. *(ASGE 2017: initial test for overt or occult small-bowel bleeding; ACG 2015: Strong/Moderate)* [[asge-2017-small-bowel-bleeding]]
- Diagnostic yield for suspected [[small-bowel-bleeding|small bowel bleeding]] 38–83%; sensitivity 94–97%, specificity 83–100%; NPV 83–100% (negative VCE is reassuring).
- **Timing:** highest yield within 48–72 h of overt bleeding (overt ~60% vs occult ~46%).

## Indications

- [[small-bowel-bleeding]] (overt or occult, after negative [[upper-endoscopy|EGD]]/[[colonoscopy]]).
- Suspected small-bowel [[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease]].
- Iron-deficiency anemia with negative bidirectional endoscopy.
- Polyposis surveillance ([[peutz-jeghers-syndrome]], [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis]]).

## Limitations / Contraindications

- **Capsule retention** with known/suspected obstruction or stenosis — ~1.5% overall, up to 13% in Crohn's. Use patency capsule or CTE/MRE first if obstruction suspected.
- No therapeutic capability; misses the duodenal papilla in most cases; lower yield for proximal duodenal/jejunal lesions.
- Positive VCE → push enteroscopy (if within reach) or [[device-assisted-enteroscopy|DAE]] for therapy. *(ASGE 2017)*

See [[small-bowel-bleeding]] for the full diagnostic algorithm and sequencing with [[device-assisted-enteroscopy]].
