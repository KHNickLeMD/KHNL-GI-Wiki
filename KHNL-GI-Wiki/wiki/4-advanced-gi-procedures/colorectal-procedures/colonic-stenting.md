# Colonic Stenting (SEMS for Malignant Colonic Obstruction)

Endoscopic placement of a self-expanding metal stent (SEMS) across a **malignant colonic obstruction (MCO)** — either as a **bridge to surgery (BTS)**, converting an emergency operation into a one-stage elective resection, or as **definitive palliation** in a patient who will not be resected. Initial assessment and benign strictures: [[colonic-stricture]]. Upper-tract counterpart: [[gastric-outlet-obstruction]].

## ASGE 2026 Recommendations

[[asge-2026-colonic-strictures]] is an American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) guideline built on Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE). **All four malignant-obstruction recommendations are conditional**; the numbering is the wiki's (the document numbers none).

| # | Recommendation | Strength / certainty |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Obstruction from highly suspected or confirmed [[colorectal-cancer\|colorectal cancer (CRC)]] that appears surgically resectable → **endoscopic colonic stent, when feasible, as BTS over emergency surgery** without stent placement | Conditional / **low** |
| 2 | Obstruction from CRC **not curable by surgical resection** → **palliative colonic stent over palliative surgery**. Currently receiving or may receive **bevacizumab** → **shared decision-making** with surgeons, oncologists and patients | Conditional / **very low** |
| 3 | Obstruction from **extracolonic malignancy (ECM)** → guide the stent-vs-surgery decision by **specific tumor biology and multidisciplinary discussion**; stenting is feasible but has **higher risk of clinical and technical failure than intraluminal malignancy**, to be openly discussed with patients | Conditional / **very low** |
| 4 | CRC obstruction undergoing stent placement → **uncovered SEMS over covered SEMS** | Conditional / **very low** |

- **Palliation: ASGE supersedes the older ungraded stance.** American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2021 best practice advice (BPA) 12 declined to choose between SEMS and a diverting colostomy ([[aga-2021-malignant-alimentary-tract-obstruction]]); ASGE 2026 is newer and GRADE-based, and **suggests the stent**.
- **Who the randomized trials behind Rec 1 enrolled:** symptomatic **left-sided** obstruction (splenic flexure to rectosigmoid), potentially resectable tumors; excluded a mass **<8 cm from the anal verge**, unresectable cancer, ischemia, perforation, peritonitis. Right-sided BTS is **more technically challenging and less feasible**, and its evidence is observational (below).
- **Tissue diagnosis before stenting is preferred but often unavailable** — none of the trials required confirmatory histopathology; the decision may rest on the degree of radiologic suspicion.
- **Synchronous lesions:** decompression allows a preoperative staging workup that emergency surgery forecloses — complete [[colonoscopy]] through the SEMS, or computed tomography (CT) / magnetic resonance (MR) colonography.

### Outcomes behind recommendation 1 — stent as BTS vs emergency surgery

| Outcome | Data | Odds ratio (OR) (95% confidence interval [CI]) | Certainty | Judgment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Permanent stoma** | 7 randomized controlled trials (RCTs) | **0.51 (0.34–0.76)** | **Moderate** | Favors stent |
| Primary anastomosis | 7 RCTs | 2.35 (1.47–3.76) | Low | Favors stent |
| Postoperative adverse events | 8 RCTs | 0.44 (0.23–0.85) | Low | Favors stent |
| **Cancer recurrence** | 6 RCTs | **1.84 (1.05–3.22)** | **Very low** | Favors emergency surgery |
| 60-d mortality | 6 RCTs | 0.93 (0.49–1.79) | Low | Neither |
| 3-y survival | 4 RCTs | 1.66 (0.93–2.96) | Low | Neither |
| Quality of life | 1 RCT | P = .36 | Very low | Neither |

> [!pitfall] The cancer-recurrence signal has a fragility index of 2
> Very low certainty, and not associated with higher 3-year mortality; proposed mechanism is microperforation with lymphovascular seeding. Discuss it and set it against the reduction in permanent stoma; a patient who values recurrence risk over a permanent ostomy may reasonably choose emergency surgery ([[asge-2026-colonic-strictures]]).

### Outcomes behind recommendations 2–4

- **Palliative stent vs palliative surgery** (RCTs + observational, all very low certainty): permanent stoma **OR 0.15 (0.08–0.28)**, adverse events **OR 0.56 (0.36–0.89)**, length of stay **mean difference −4.85 days (−6.02 to −3.68)** — all favoring the stent; 30-day mortality OR 0.60 (0.30–1.18), neither.
- **ECM vs intracolonic malignancy** (7–8 observational studies, very low): clinical failure **OR 2.88 (1.58–5.25)** and technical failure **OR 2.92 (1.13–5.54)**, both favoring stenting in *intracolonic* disease; adverse events OR 1.26 (0.81–1.96), neither. Pooled stenting for ECM: technical success **89%** (95% CI 83–96), clinical success **74%** (63–86), adverse events **29%** (16–42).
- **Uncovered vs covered SEMS** (1 RCT + observational): stent patency **18.5 days longer** (95% CI 10.5–26.5 in the text; Table 4 prints 19 d, 11–48; low certainty), need for reinsertion **OR 0.38 (0.17–0.86)**, adverse events **OR 0.57 (0.44–0.74)** — all favoring uncovered; clinical and technical success no different.

## Indications — the two distinct uses

**The two indications have different decision rules — do not merge them.** In BTS every patient goes on to resection, so the stent buys staging, [[bowel-preparation|bowel preparation]] and an elective operation. In palliation the stent *is* the treatment, and the comparator is a stoma.

| Indication | AGA 2021 (ungraded BPA) |
|---|---|
| **BTS** — resectable MCO | SEMS is *"a reasonable choice"* to allow one-stage, elective resection (BPA 11) — now graded as ASGE Rec 1 |
| **Palliation** — not a resection candidate | SEMS or diverting colostomy, by patient goals and functional status (BPA 12) — superseded by ASGE Rec 2 |
| **Proximal / right-sided** (proximal to the splenic flexure) | SEMS *"a reasonable option"* both as BTS and palliatively (BPA 13) |
| **ECM**, not a surgical candidate | SEMS *"a reasonable alternative"*, but more technically challenging, more variable clinical success, more frequent complications incl. migration (BPA 14) |

- **Every decision is multidisciplinary** — oncologists, surgeons, endoscopists — weighing the obstruction, patient expectations, prognosis, expected subsequent therapies and functional status (BPA 1).
- Surgical candidates: weigh stenting **against local surgical and endoscopic expertise** ([[aga-2021-malignant-alimentary-tract-obstruction]]).

## Contraindications and Cautions

- **Absolute:** signs of **ischemia, perforation or peritonitis**. **Relative:** rectal stricture with its distal end **within 5 cm of the anal verge** — pain and incontinence ([[asge-2026-colonic-strictures]]).
- ⚠ **Bevacizumab — unresolved.** Older meta-analysis: stent-related perforation **12.7% with vs 7% without**; more recent observational studies: similar risk either way. AGA 2021 cited large retrospective studies showing no association, with the caveat that many patients received bevacizumab outside 6 weeks of the stent placement; ASGE 2026 calls it still debated and answers with **shared decision-making** (Rec 2), stressing perforation and complicated surgical management. The newer source governs.
- ⚠ **Perforation and tumor seeding.** One single-centre retrospective South Korean study: perforation during SEMS placement → **OR 46** for later metastasis from seeding — small, retrospective, with a higher perforation rate than prior meta-analyses. A signal, not a risk estimate ([[aga-2021-malignant-alimentary-tract-obstruction]]).

## Bridge to Surgery

### Left-sided and unspecified-site data

AGA 2021 series ([[aga-2021-malignant-alimentary-tract-obstruction]]); comparative outcomes vs emergency surgery are in the Rec 1 table above.

| Metric | Result |
|---|---|
| Technical success | **91.9%** (systematic review, 21 studies, 407 patients); **98%** (two international registries, 182 patients) |
| Clinical success | **94%** (registries) |
| Proceeded to one-stage surgery | **71.7%** |
| Perforation | **1.7%** (7/407); **3%** (registries) |
| Stent migration | **3.9%** (16 cases); **1.2%** (registries) |
| Overall adverse events | **7.8%** (registries) |

### Right-sided / proximal obstruction

Traditionally managed by emergency resection (ER) with primary anastomosis, assuming stenting is too difficult there and ER is safe. AGA BPA 13 rejects the first; [[kanaka-2022-colonic-stent-bts-right-sided]] argues against the second — emergency right hemicolectomy **30-day mortality 6.0%** vs **0.7%** elective (Japanese National Clinical Database). Right-sided disease is **32–54%** of malignant large bowel obstruction but only **~5%** of reported BTS cases.

**Meta-analysis of 7 non-randomized comparative studies, 5136 patients (BTS 1662 / ER 3474)** — ASGE 2026 cites it for lower postoperative adverse events and mortality with right-sided BTS:

| Outcome | BTS | ER | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Postoperative complications | **19.3%** | 31.3% | OR 0.78 (0.66–0.92) |
| Postoperative mortality | **0.9%** | 5.2% | OR 0.51 (0.28–0.92) |
| **Severe complications** (Clavien–Dindo ≥III / reintervention) | — | — | **OR 0.98 (0.68–1.41) — not significant** |
| Primary anastomosis | **97.8%** | 85.9% | — |
| Stoma construction | **2.0%** | 11.0% | — |
| Laparoscopic surgery | **48.5%** | 15.7% | — |
| Anastomotic leakage | **2.6%** | 5.6% | — |
| Surgical site infection | **4.6%** | 5.8% | — |
| Ileus | 6.7% | 3.1% | Not significant — the one signal favoring ER |

- ⚠ **"Reduces complications" must carry the severity caveat** — severe complications did **not differ significantly** (table).
- **"Right-sided" was defined inconsistently** — 5 of 7 studies included the **entire transverse colon**.
- **Timing:** the complication benefit held on both sides of the median **20-day** stent-to-surgery interval — **<20 days** OR 0.45 (0.22–0.92); **≥20 days** OR 0.81 (0.68–0.96). Across studies the interval ranged from about **7 to 28.1 days**.
- ⚠ **The benefit may be selection, not treatment.** Right-colon tumor site independently predicted technical difficulty (**OR 2.5, 1.61–4.01**), so stented patients may be the easy-to-stent subset of non-randomized studies. Propensity matching in the largest study (n=3000) is the main counterweight; **excluding it, the mortality benefit lost statistical significance.**

## Palliative Stenting

- **Default (ASGE Rec 2): stent over palliative surgery.** Stent not technically feasible, or fails clinically → **palliative surgery** ([[asge-2026-colonic-strictures]]).
- ⚠ **Performance status can favor surgery.** **Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) 0 or 1:** palliative *surgery* had improved overall survival vs SEMS; **ECOG 2 or 3:** no difference ([[aga-2021-malignant-alimentary-tract-obstruction]]).
- Why AGA 2021 left it open: SEMS gave **earlier oral intake and shorter stay** but a **trend favoring colostomy** for overall survival and readmission, and meta-analyses disagreed on clinical success and survival; SEMS carried **higher perforation risk and more late adverse events**.

## Extracolonic Malignancy

- **Different mechanism:** CRC obstructs by space-occupying growth; ECM by **extrinsic compression, mesenteric infiltration and dysmotility**. Commonest ECM causes of MCO: **gynecological malignancies** (AGA); ASGE lists ovarian and prostate cancer, peritoneal carcinomatosis and lymphoma.
- No study compares stent with surgery. Patients often have poor performance status, and surgery is usually a **diverting ostomy** rather than resection. ASGE individualizes by **tumor biology, life expectancy, stricture characteristics, available resources, multidisciplinary discussion and patient preference** ([[asge-2026-colonic-strictures]]).
- **Avoid stents in multifocal colonic obstruction from ECM** — no clear established benefit (ASGE sustainability advice).
- As BTS in ECM, one small AGA-cited series (9 patients): **100%** technical success, **77.8%** proceeded to elective surgery.

## Stent Selection

- **Uncovered over covered (Rec 4)** — main advantage is **lower migration**, hence longer patency and fewer reinterventions. **Covered colonic stents are not approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) or commercially available in the US**; most covered-stent data are from abroad or off-label use ([[asge-2026-colonic-strictures]]).
- **Uncovered SEMS can become permanently embedded by tissue ingrowth** — reserve for **confirmed or highly suspected malignancy**:
  - Malignancy **potentially curable with chemotherapy or radiation** (e.g. lymphoma) → uncovered SEMS is **not a good option** (removal becomes difficult).
  - Unfavorable prognosis (e.g. peritoneal carcinomatosis) and not a good surgical candidate → uncovered SEMS **reasonable**.

> [!pitfall] Covered vs uncovered is opposite in the colon and esophagus
> Colon: uncovered (fewer adverse events, longer patency, fewer reinterventions). Esophagus: fully or partially covered, per AGA BPA 5. Early colonic studies had shown uncovered stents with lower migration but more tumor ingrowth ([[aga-2021-malignant-alimentary-tract-obstruction]]).

## Technique

- **Success** ([[asge-2026-colonic-strictures]]): malignant colonic stricture — technical **96%**, clinical **99%**; lower for ECM (above).
- **Consent:** individualized — technical and clinical success, possible need for emergency surgery after technical or clinical failure, and adverse events.
- **Before the stricture:** avoid air insufflation; advance mainly with **waterjet** and minimal carbon dioxide — gas proximal to a stricture creates a closed loop at risk of proximal perforation. **Fluoroscopy is necessary** when stenting is planned. Sedation, bowel preparation, biopsy and tattoo rules: [[colonic-stricture]].
- **Equipment:** extraction balloon catheter, long wire (0.035″ or 0.025″), fluoroscopy, water-soluble contrast, compatible endoscope (therapeutic endoscope or adult colonoscope; newer smaller-diameter upper endoscopes with large working channels also take through-the-scope devices). **Through-the-scope SEMSs are ideal in the colon.**
- **Sizing:** diameter **18 to 30 mm**, chosen from the healthy lumen diameter; length leaving **at least 2 cm of stent on each side** of the stricture.

> [!howto] Deploying a colonic SEMS
> 1. Advance a wire across the stricture and **coil it in the upstream colon**.
> 2. Advance the stent across and deploy **under fluoroscopy**, with the **proximal end at least 2 cm proximal to the stricture**.
> 3. Deploy the **distal end under endoscopic view**, at least **2 cm from the stricture end** and at least **2 cm upstream of the anal verge** (limits tenesmus and pain).
> 4. **Immediate stool passage through the stent confirms transluminal placement.**
> 5. Afterwards: diet as tolerated **up to low residue**; **laxatives titrated to keep stools soft**.

> [!pitfall] Don't balloon-dilate the stent after deployment
> It is **unnecessary and may increase the risk of perforation** ([[asge-2026-colonic-strictures]]).

- **[[lumen-apposing-metal-stents|Lumen-apposing metal stents (LAMS)]] in the colon (benign setting — temporary remodeling of short anastomotic strictures refractory to balloon dilation):** most used diameters **15 and 20 mm**, lengths **10 and 15 mm**; **rarely possible in the right colon** (catheter too short for adult colonoscopes). Distal flange **≥2 cm from the anal verge**; balloon dilation to maximal diameter may be considered after deployment for immediate relief; removal or exchange **at 2 to 3 months**.

## Adverse Events

Colonic SEMS for malignant obstruction ([[asge-2026-colonic-strictures]]):

| Event | Rate |
|---|---|
| **Any adverse event** | **24%** |
| Stent-induced perforation | 5% |
| Procedure-related perforation | 3% |
| Stent migration | 7% |
| Stent occlusion | 8% |
| Ulceration | 2% |

- **Perforation elsewhere:** 13% in one BTS RCT (9% stent-related, 4% procedure-related; intraoperative microperforation in 7%); **7.5%** in a meta-analysis of 4086 patients, mostly in the first few days. BTS series: [[#Left-sided and unspecified-site data]].
- **Perforation often requires surgery** ([[endoscopic-management-of-perforation]]); the other adverse events can usually be managed endoscopically.
- Associated with **early** perforation: **sigmoid location, age >70**. With **delayed** perforation: **flexure location, absence of peritoneal carcinomatosis** ([[aga-2021-malignant-alimentary-tract-obstruction]]).
- **Migration** is more frequent with covered stents. AGA BPA 14 also called complications incl. migration more frequent in ECM; ASGE 2026's newer meta-analysis found **no difference** in perforation or migration between ECM and intracolonic malignancy.

## Evidence Quality

- [[aga-2021-malignant-alimentary-tract-obstruction]] is a tier-1 AGA Clinical Practice Update with **no formal rating** of evidence or strength — every statement is BPA.
- [[kanaka-2022-colonic-stent-bts-right-sided]] is tier-2, rates itself **very low to low by GRADE**, and reports short-term outcomes only. **No RCT of right-sided BTS exists**, and the authors doubt one is feasible.
- **Long-term oncologic outcome after BTS is unsettled:** ASGE's cancer-recurrence signal is very low certainty, and it calls for longer follow-up including disease-specific survival.
