*Instrument-based pelvic floor retraining — the cornerstone of [[defecation-disorders|dyssynergic defecation]] management and a Strong-recommendation therapy for [[fecal-incontinence|fecal incontinence]] and levator ani syndrome. Delivered as a course of supervised sessions, not a single procedure.*

## Indications and Evidence

| Indication | Recommendation | Strength / Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Dyssynergic defecation** ([[defecation-disorders\|defecation disorders]]) | Instrumented anorectal biofeedback to manage symptoms | **Strong / Moderate** ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]) |
| **[[fecal-incontinence\|Fecal incontinence]]** not responding to education + conservative measures | Biofeedback = pelvic floor rehabilitative techniques with visual or auditory feedback | **Strong / Moderate** |
| **Levator ani syndrome with abnormal [[anorectal-manometry\|anorectal manometry (ARM)]]** (chronic proctalgia, see [[proctalgia-syndromes]]) | Biofeedback to teach pelvic floor muscle reconditioning — the **only** effective therapy | **Strong / Very Low** |
| Levator ani syndrome with abnormal ARM, biofeedback **unavailable** | Electrical (galvanic) stimulation | Conditional / Very Low |

- **Course: 4–6 sessions, each several weeks apart, with well-trained therapists** ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]). This is the guideline's ungraded key concept — the graded recommendation above specifies no session count.
- **The ARM qualifier is the decision.** Both proctalgia recommendations apply to levator syndrome *with abnormal ARM* — **not** to idiopathic chronic proctalgia syndrome. ARM + balloon expulsion test (BET) are performed specifically to sort which proctalgia patient is a biofeedback candidate ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]).

### Dyssynergic Defecation

- **The mainstay of management**, not an adjunct — it **restores bowel function by improving rectoanal coordination**, and **a multicomponent biofeedback is preferred** ([[rome-v-2026-anorectal-disorders]]). Management begins with explanation, education and lifestyle measures.
- **Superior to sham feedback, pelvic floor exercises, laxatives and muscle relaxant drugs** in large randomized controlled trials (RCTs), **short- and long-term** ([[rome-v-2026-anorectal-disorders]]); also superior to **sham biofeedback, diazepam, and PEG (polyethylene glycol)** ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]).
- **Not** a substitute for surgery in proven dyssynergia with a structural abnormality — surgery is reserved for biofeedback failure **and** a structural lesion (e.g. significant rectocele). Counsel carefully before any surgery for a defecatory disorder: potential risks often outweigh benefits.
- Two RCTs found **surgery superior to biofeedback**, but one enrolled patients whose symptoms were due to **structural** pelvic floor abnormalities and the other used a **suboptimal biofeedback protocol** ([[rome-v-2026-anorectal-disorders]]).

> [!pitfall] Does not fix isolated slow-transit constipation
> Biofeedback is ineffective when the problem is slow transit alone and there is no defecation disorder ([[rome-v-2026-anorectal-disorders]]). It is still tried first when both coexist — transit often normalises once the dyssynergia is corrected ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]).

### Fecal Incontinence

- Patients are taught to **selectively contract the external anal sphincter and puborectalis**, to improve the **strength and duration of anal squeeze**, and to **correct dyssynergia when present** ([[rome-v-2026-anorectal-disorders]]).
- **Feedback** is given via **visual tracings of balloon volume and/or anorectal pressure**, reinforced by the therapist and by **balloon distention with progressively smaller volumes**.
- **Intact baseline sensation and improved sensory discrimination predict greater success.**
- **More than half maintain benefit at a 7-year median follow-up.**
- **Conservative measures come first — and they decide the yield.** Up to **20%** need no further treatment when conservative measures (education + antidiarrheals/laxatives + daily pelvic floor exercises) are properly taught and followed up. Among those who then still fail medications, education and behavioral therapy, **76% reported adequate relief at 3 months with biofeedback vs 41% with pelvic floor exercises alone** ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]).

> [!pitfall] A failed first course rarely works the second time
> Patients who fail initial biofeedback rarely respond to it later — refer them for alternative therapies rather than repeating the course ([[rome-v-2026-anorectal-disorders]]).

### Levator Ani Syndrome

- In an RCT, **87% of patients with levator tenderness reported adequate pain relief after biofeedback**, vs **45% after electrical stimulation** and **22% after massage** ([[rome-v-2026-anorectal-disorders]]).
- **Electrogalvanic stimulation** may be considered if biofeedback is unavailable.

> [!pitfall] No levator tenderness, no benefit from any therapy
> Patients with chronic proctalgia but **without** levator tenderness benefited from none of the three therapies tested ([[rome-v-2026-anorectal-disorders]]). Tenderness on digital rectal examination is the selector — the pain alone is not.

### Rectal Sensory Disorders

- **Sensory biofeedback** (repeated balloon inflations with stepwise smaller volumes) is particularly useful in **rectal hyposensitivity**; **sensory adaptation therapy** is the barostat-mediated protocol for **rectal hypersensitivity** ([[rome-v-2026-anorectal-disorders]]).
- Volumes, pressure increments, session counts and the comparative trial data: [[anorectal-sensory-dysfunction]].

## Technique

Suggested treatment protocol for anorectal biofeedback — American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2021 Table 3 ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]):

| Component | Details |
|---|---|
| **Education** | Diagrams to teach anatomy, explain what dyssynergia is, explain the rectoanal gradient |
| **Correct toileting position and behavior** | Foot stool with **knees higher than hips**, lean forward; consider limiting time per toilet visit and number of visits |
| **Abdominal breathing technique** | Taught in detail; patient practices at home |
| **Manometric biofeedback (BF) — generating sufficient rectal pressure** | Rectal pressure on push recorded at the rectal port and shown to the patient; patient taught to tighten abdominal muscles and lower the diaphragm to raise pressure appropriately |
| **Manometric BF — anal relaxation, then rectoanal coordination** | Visual feedback of anal manometry or electromyography (EMG) on the push maneuver teaches relaxation instead of paradoxical contraction; patient then views rectal *and* anal tracings and learns to raise rectal pressure and drop anal pressure **simultaneously** |
| **Balloon expulsion retraining** | Simulated defecation with a rectal balloon during sessions |
| **Sensory retraining** | For hyposensitivity: rectal balloon inflated, patient told the volume in mL and trained to detect progressively smaller volumes |

- **Initial measures come first** — normalize stool form and give toileting position/behavior advice before or alongside the course.
- **Correction is stepwise:** push effort improves before anal relaxation does — paradoxical contraction can still be present at 2 weeks and fully corrected by 6 weeks ([[rome-v-2026-anorectal-disorders]]).
- **Time to benefit: often up to 3–6 months of consistent practice** before benefit manifests. ([[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]])
- **Confirming response: normalisation of the BET after biofeedback suggests the defecatory disorder (DD) has resolved.** ([[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]])
- **In fecal incontinence (FI) the same protocol is re-aimed:** increase strength and coordination of the external anal sphincter without contracting the abdominal wall, and correct rectal sensation — in rectal hypersensitivity the goal is to *reduce* sensation so defecation can be postponed. Tailor to the specific anorectal dysfunction.

## Patient Selection

- Requires objective anorectal physiology testing first: **ARM + BET** are required to diagnose a defecation disorder and to identify biofeedback candidates in levator ani syndrome ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]).
- **What "abnormal ARM" means in levator syndrome** — the single trial behind the Strong recommendation selected patients by **failure to evacuate a 50 mL water-filled balloon** *plus* **manometric inability to relax the pelvic floor during simulated defecation**, in the setting of levator tenderness. Exclude overlapping pelvic conditions (chronic prostatitis in men, chronic pelvic pain syndrome in women) before a conservative trial.
- Digital rectal examination is recommended in all patients with suspected defecation disorder — **75% sensitivity, 87% specificity** for dyssynergia. It also identifies structural abnormalities ([[anal-fissure|anal fissures]], [[hemorrhoids]], fecal impaction, descending perineum syndrome, anorectal cancer) and grades sphincter function.
- **Symptoms that suggest a defecation disorder:** excessive straining, sense of anorectal blockage, manual maneuvers to facilitate evacuation, sense of incomplete evacuation.
- **Baseline ARM + BET predict the outcome** and guide how the course is aimed.
- **Do not subtype before referring.** [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]] and [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]] recommend biofeedback for dyssynergic defecation without subtyping it, and [[rome-v-2026-anorectal-disorders]] abolished the historical types I–IV because the patterns have limited utility on their own. What to use instead for selecting and aiming the course is on [[anorectal-manometry]] (rectoanal gradient, anal relaxation on push, rectal sensation, BET).
- Distinguish from slow-transit constipation: colonic transit study if BET is normal **or** biofeedback fails. Up to **50%** of defecation-disorder patients have delayed colonic transit (usually secondary).
- **Predictors of a favorable outcome** in dyssynergic defecation ([[rome-v-2026-anorectal-disorders]]): **anal digitation**, **hard stools**, **greater patient motivation**, **undergoing ≥5 sessions**, and a **prolonged balloon expulsion time**. **Psychological distress adversely affects outcomes.**

> [!pitfall] How many abnormal tests it takes is version-dependent
> ACG 2021 requires **both** ARM and BET to be abnormal; **[[rome-v-2026-dgbi|Rome V]] (2026) requires difficult-evacuation symptoms plus only 1 of 3 abnormal tests** (balloon expulsion, manometry, **or** imaging) and has dropped anal EMG. Rome V is the newer publication, so this page follows the 1-of-3 bar. Version table: [[defecation-disorders]]; test detail: [[anorectal-manometry]].

## Delivery — Office, Home, and Pelvic Floor Physical Therapy

*All from [[rome-v-2026-anorectal-disorders]].*

- **Home-based biofeedback achieves success rates comparable with office biofeedback — 68% vs 70% — at lower cost.**
- **Barriers to delivery:** labor-intensiveness, lack of dedicated equipment and of a standardized protocol, limited therapist availability, poor insurance coverage.
- **Pelvic floor physical therapy** is a widely used alternative, but **evidence and standardization are lacking and it differs significantly from biofeedback** — it is not interchangeable.

## Role in the Constipation Pathway

Biofeedback is a **gate** that must be passed before the "refractory" label ([[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]]):

- **Best practice advice (BPA) 2:** most patients with [[chronic-constipation|chronic constipation]] should be evaluated with ARM + BET and **complete a course of pelvic floor biofeedback therapy, when indicated, before being labeled as having refractory constipation.**
- **BPA 1:** refractory constipation is defined by infrequent and/or unsatisfactory bowel habits, with or without abdominal pain, **despite an adequate trial of lifestyle, dietary, medical, and/or pelvic floor biofeedback therapy when indicated** in adults meeting criteria for chronic constipation or constipation-predominant [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)]].
- **BPA 10:** surgical therapy for refractory constipation only after confirming slow-transit constipation **and excluding pelvic floor dysfunction** — i.e. an untreated defecation disorder must be excluded by ARM/BET ± biofeedback before an operation is considered.

- **A biofeedback trial is required before colectomy.** It may normalise colonic transit in many patients with a DD; there is ample evidence and strong consensus for addressing the DD before colectomy with ileorectal anastomosis. Failure to address it first gives poor functional outcomes — persistent abdominal pain and constipation — and low satisfaction.

See [[chronic-idiopathic-constipation]] for the full refractory pathway.

## Limitations

- **Levator ani syndrome:** the Strong recommendation rests on a **single key RCT** with no independent replication in 10 years — the guideline itself flags this. It stands because biofeedback carries no significant risk and there is no effective alternative ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]).
- Galvanic stimulation is the named fallback for levator syndrome only, and was **less effective than biofeedback but superior to conservative treatment**.
- **Predictors of poor response (FI):** short-term memory loss from dementia, depression, and central nervous system (CNS) etiologies (spinal cord or head injury). The course requires a motivated patient, reinforcement over time, and a skilled therapist.
- In FI trials, biofeedback was **not superior to conservative measures / pelvic floor exercises alone or to education** — a likely explanation is that trial patients had not first been treated with conservative measures (contrast the 76% vs 41% result above, where they had).
- **Not recommended** for dyssynergia: [[botulinum-toxin|botulinum toxin]] (poor evidence, short-lived, impractical long-term).
- **[[sacral-nerve-stimulation|Sacral nerve stimulation (SNS)]] is not recommended for constipation** (no benefit in RCTs) — biofeedback does not compete with SNS in this indication.
