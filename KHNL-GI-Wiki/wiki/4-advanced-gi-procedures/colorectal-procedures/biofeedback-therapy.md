*Instrument-based pelvic floor retraining — the cornerstone of [[defecation-disorders|dyssynergic defecation]] management and a Strong-recommendation therapy for [[fecal-incontinence|fecal incontinence]] and levator ani syndrome. Delivered as a course of supervised sessions, not a single procedure.*

## Indications and Evidence

| Indication | Recommendation | Strength / Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Dyssynergic defecation** ([[defecation-disorders\|defecation disorders]]) | Instrumented anorectal biofeedback, **4–6 sessions** with well-trained therapists | **Strong / Moderate** ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]) |
| **Levator ani syndrome with abnormal [[anorectal-manometry\|ARM]]** (chronic proctalgia, see [[proctalgia-syndromes]]) | Biofeedback to teach pelvic floor muscle reconditioning — the **only** effective therapy | **Strong / Very Low** |
| Levator ani syndrome with abnormal ARM, biofeedback **unavailable** | Electrical (galvanic) stimulation | Conditional / Very Low |
| **[[fecal-incontinence\|Fecal incontinence]]** not responding to education + conservative measures | Biofeedback = pelvic floor rehabilitative techniques with visual or auditory feedback | **Strong / Moderate** |

- **The ARM qualifier is the decision.** Both proctalgia recommendations apply to levator syndrome *with abnormal ARM* — **not** to idiopathic chronic proctalgia syndrome. ARM + BET are performed specifically to sort which proctalgia patient is a biofeedback candidate ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]).
- For dyssynergic defecation, biofeedback is **superior to sham biofeedback, diazepam, and PEG (polyethylene glycol)** in RCTs — it is the cornerstone of management, not an adjunct.
- In fecal incontinence, up to **20%** need no further treatment when conservative measures (education + antidiarrheals/laxatives + daily pelvic floor exercises) are properly taught and followed up.
- **Not** a substitute for surgery in proven dyssynergia with a structural abnormality — surgery is reserved for biofeedback failure **and** a structural lesion (e.g. significant rectocele). Counsel carefully before any surgery for a defecatory disorder: potential risks often outweigh benefits.

## Protocol

Suggested treatment protocol for anorectal biofeedback — ACG 2021 Table 3 ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]):

| Component | Details |
|---|---|
| **Education** | Diagrams to teach anatomy, explain what dyssynergia is, explain the rectoanal gradient |
| **Correct toileting position and behavior** | Foot stool with **knees higher than hips**, lean forward; consider limiting time per toilet visit and number of visits |
| **Abdominal breathing technique** | Taught in detail; patient practices at home |
| **Manometric BF — generating sufficient rectal pressure** | Rectal pressure on push recorded at the rectal port and shown to the patient; patient taught to tighten abdominal muscles and lower the diaphragm to raise pressure appropriately |
| **Manometric BF — anal relaxation, then rectoanal coordination** | Visual feedback of anal manometry or EMG on the push maneuver teaches relaxation instead of paradoxical contraction; patient then views rectal *and* anal tracings and learns to raise rectal pressure and drop anal pressure **simultaneously** |
| **Balloon expulsion retraining** | Simulated defecation with a rectal balloon during sessions |
| **Sensory retraining** | For hyposensitivity: rectal balloon inflated, patient told the volume in mL and trained to detect progressively smaller volumes |

- **Initial measures come first** — normalize stool form and give toileting position/behavior advice before or alongside the course.
- **In FI the same protocol is re-aimed:** increase strength and coordination of the external anal sphincter without contracting the abdominal wall, and correct rectal sensation — in rectal hypersensitivity the goal is to *reduce* sensation so defecation can be postponed. Tailor to the specific anorectal dysfunction.

## Patient Selection

- Requires objective anorectal physiology testing first: **ARM + balloon expulsion test (BET)** are required to diagnose a defecation disorder and to identify biofeedback candidates in levator ani syndrome ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]).
- **What "abnormal ARM" means in levator syndrome** — the single trial behind the Strong recommendation selected patients by **failure to evacuate a 50 mL water-filled balloon** *plus* **manometric inability to relax the pelvic floor during simulated defecation**, in the setting of levator tenderness. Exclude overlapping pelvic conditions (chronic prostatitis in men, chronic pelvic pain syndrome in women) before a conservative trial.
- Digital rectal examination is recommended in all patients with suspected defecation disorder — **75% sensitivity, 87% specificity** for dyssynergia. It also identifies structural abnormalities ([[anal-fissure|anal fissures]], [[hemorrhoids]], fecal impaction, descending perineum syndrome, anorectal cancer) and grades sphincter function.
- **Symptoms that suggest a defecation disorder:** excessive straining, sense of anorectal blockage, manual maneuvers to facilitate evacuation, sense of incomplete evacuation.
- **Baseline ARM + BET predict the outcome** and guide how the course is aimed.
- ⚠ **Dyssynergia subtypes (types I–IV) are not defined in any ingested source.** [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]] recommends biofeedback for dyssynergic defecation without subtyping it, and neither it nor [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]] reproduces the type I–IV scheme — so the wiki does not state it. What the guideline *does* give for selecting and aiming the course is on [[anorectal-manometry]] (rectoanal gradient, anal relaxation on push, rectal sensation, BET). Closing this needs a source that defines the subtypes; do not supply them from memory.
- Distinguish from slow-transit constipation: colonic transit study if BET is normal **or** biofeedback fails. Up to **50%** of defecation-disorder patients have delayed colonic transit (usually secondary).

## Role in the Constipation Pathway

Biofeedback is a **gate** that must be passed before the "refractory" label ([[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]]):

- **BPA 2:** most patients with [[chronic-constipation|chronic constipation]] should be evaluated with ARM + BET and **complete a course of pelvic floor biofeedback therapy, when indicated, before being labeled as having refractory constipation.**
- **BPA 1:** refractory constipation is defined by infrequent and/or unsatisfactory bowel habits, with or without abdominal pain, **despite an adequate trial of lifestyle, dietary, medical, and/or pelvic floor biofeedback therapy when indicated** in adults meeting criteria for chronic constipation or constipation-predominant [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|IBS]].
- **BPA 10:** surgical therapy for refractory constipation only after confirming slow-transit constipation **and excluding pelvic floor dysfunction** — i.e. an untreated defecation disorder must be excluded by ARM/BET ± biofeedback before an operation is considered.

See [[chronic-idiopathic-constipation]] for the full refractory pathway.

## Limitations

- **Levator ani syndrome:** the Strong recommendation rests on a **single key RCT** with no independent replication in 10 years — the guideline itself flags this. It stands because biofeedback carries no significant risk and there is no effective alternative ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]).
- Availability of trained therapists and manometric equipment limits delivery; galvanic stimulation is the named fallback for levator syndrome only, and was **less effective than biofeedback but superior to conservative treatment**.
- **Predictors of poor response (FI):** short-term memory loss from dementia, depression, and CNS etiologies (spinal cord or head injury). The course requires a motivated patient, reinforcement over time, and a skilled therapist.
- In FI trials, biofeedback was **not superior to conservative measures / pelvic floor exercises alone or to education** — a likely explanation is that trial patients had not first been treated with conservative measures. Labor intensive and highly expertise-dependent.
- **Not recommended** for dyssynergia: botulinum toxin (poor evidence, short-lived, impractical long-term).
- **[[sacral-nerve-stimulation|SNS]] is not recommended for constipation** (no benefit in RCTs) — biofeedback does not compete with SNS in this indication.
