# Interventional EUS for Vascular Investigation and Therapy

[[endoscopic-ultrasound|EUS]]-guided access to vessels in or near the GI wall. Two prerequisites for clinical utility: (1) a vascular target in/near the GI wall where endoscopic access beats percutaneous, and (2) efficacy/safety comparable or superior to current alternatives. Clinically available today: **gastric variceal injection therapy** and **EUS-guided portosystemic pressure gradient (EUS-PPG)**. ([[aga-2023-interventional-eus-vascular]])

## Landscape — maturity of each intervention

| Intervention | Status | Evidence base |
|---|---|---|
| Gastric variceal coil ± glue | Clinically available; **strongest case for adoption at centers of expertise** | 3 retrospective series, 2 small RCTs, meta-analyses |
| EUS-PPG | Clinically available (FDA-approved device) | Case series to 83 patients; no reported AEs |
| Ectopic/rectal varices | Emerging | Case reports + 1 small series |

## EUS-Guided Gastric Variceal Therapy

- Gastric variceal hemorrhage is less prevalent than esophageal but often more severe and higher-mortality. **Cardiofundal varices (IGV1, GOV2)** are the hardest endoscopic targets — large, awkwardly located, requiring a clear retroflexed field of view; intravascular needle placement during direct endoscopic injection (DEI) may be inaccurate.
- **Why EUS guidance:** better injection precision (including during active bleeding when direct visualization fails), expands options to **hemostatic coils**, and gives **real-time Doppler feedback of hemostasis**.
- Coils: soft platinum wire with spaced synthetic fibers (IR embolization coils — Nester or MReye, Cook); intravascular placement is postulated to initiate thrombus and act as a scaffold for subsequently injected cyanoacrylate, minimizing postprocedure embolic events.
- **Not FDA approved.** Treatment decisions should be multidisciplinary with hepatology and interventional radiology (which offers BRTO and [[tips|TIPS]] for the same lesions — see [[variceal-upper-gi-bleeding]]).

### Technique — gastric variceal coiling

([[aga-2023-interventional-eus-vascular]])

1. Patient intubated, left lateral decubitus.
2. Instill **100–200 cm³ water** through the echoendoscope and retain it in the gastric fundus — delineates intramural vessels (gastric varices) from extramural collaterals and **perforator veins** (identified by tracing extramural vessels across the muscularis propria into the intramural varix).
3. Access the varix with a **22-gauge or 19-gauge FNA needle**, transesophageal or transgastric approach.
4. Place **1–3 coils** sequentially into the target vessel; use **Doppler interrogation during placement to decide how many coils are needed**.
5. When Doppler flow is significantly reduced or near-absent, inject an adjunct such as **cyanoacrylate** (or absorbable gelatin sponge/Gelfoam).
6. Repeat for other varices as required.

- **Fluoroscopy ± varicealography** helpful early in the learning curve but **not required**.
- **No definitive data on whether to target the varix itself or the perforator vein(s).**
- The multi-step technique requires adequate training of nurses and technicians.

### Outcomes — coil vs glue vs combination

- EUS-based therapies overall: ~**99% technical success and control of bleeding**; rebleeding **0%–16%**; adverse events **0%–7%**.
- **Meta-analysis, EUS-guided vs DEI:** treatment efficacy 94% vs 91%, but **gastric variceal obliteration 84% vs 63% (P = .02)**.
- **Combination (coil + glue) is the preferred strategy:**

| Regimen | Hemostasis | Adverse events | Recurrence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coil + cyanoacrylate | 96%–98% | 10% | 5% (significantly fewer than monotherapy) |
| Cyanoacrylate alone | 96% | 21% | — |
| Coils alone | 90% | 3% | — |

- Coil + **Gelfoam** in lieu of cyanoacrylate: similar low rebleeding and reintervention rates. EUS-guided **thrombin** injection: small series only.
- At least one retrospective comparative study shows EUS-guided cyanoacrylate injection superior to DEI.

## EUS-Guided Portal Pressure Gradient (EUS-PPG)

- Standard **HVPG** (interventional radiology, transjugular) measures the portal gradient **indirectly** — free hepatic vein pressure minus wedged hepatic vein pressure. Drawbacks: requires radiation and contrast; being indirect it can **misdiagnose noncirrhotic/presinusoidal [[portal-hypertension|portal hypertension]]**.
- Reference thresholds cited: **HVPG >10 mm Hg** correlates with development of esophageal varices; **HVPG >12 mm Hg** with increased risk of bleeding esophageal varices. (Full HVPG staging is on [[portal-hypertension]].)
- **EUS-PPG measures the hepatic vein–portal vein gradient directly**, using the FDA-approved EchoTip Insight system (Cook).

### Technique — EUS-PPG

([[aga-2023-interventional-eus-vascular]])

1. Patient **supine**, preferably under **general anesthesia** to minimize movement.
2. **One dose of prophylactic antibiotics** (e.g., ciprofloxacin or cefazolin).
3. **Level the compact manometer at the midaxillary line / level of the right heart** and keep it there for the entire procedure.
4. Linear echoendoscope: identify hepatic veins anatomically, confirm by Doppler; target the **middle or left hepatic vein with a 25-gauge needle via a transgastric approach**.
5. Take **≥3 consecutive pressure measurements** (one-needle-stick approach) and average them.
6. Identify the **intrahepatic portal vein**, confirm by Doppler, repeat the measurement sequence.
7. **EUS-PPG = mean portal pressure − mean hepatic vein pressure.**

### Indications and evidence

**Where EUS-PPG is indicated or clinically helpful** ([[aga-2023-interventional-eus-vascular]]):

- **Discordant data** on the presence/absence of portal hypertension — isolated thrombocytopenia, isolated splenomegaly, normal HVPG but possible presinusoidal portal hypertension
- **Supplementing preoperative clearance** (including pretransplant workup) in known or suspected [[cirrhosis]]
- **Monitoring response** to medications or other intervention
- Strongest argument for use: **when there is already another indication for endoscopy** — variceal screening or [[liver-biopsy|liver biopsy]] — allowing [[upper-endoscopy|EGD]], EUS, PPG, biopsy, and assessment/treatment of portal-hypertensive endoscopic features (varices, portal hypertensive gastropathy) in one session

**Evidence:** first human 2014; 28-patient series — excellent correlation between EUS-PPG and endoscopic findings of portal hypertension in suspected cirrhosis; 64-patient retrospective series with concurrent liver biopsy — excellent correlation with **histologic fibrosis stage**, clinical portal hypertension, thrombocytopenia, splenomegaly, **APRI >2**, and **FIB-4 >3.25**, with **no reported adverse events**; 83-patient series — correlated with thrombocytopenia and varices, again no AEs.

## EUS-Guided Portal Vein Sampling

- The portal circulation carries venous drainage of the entire GI tract and ~**70% of vascular input to the liver**; portal blood may be enriched with metabolites, tumor cells, and xenobiotics filtered out by the liver before reaching peripheral blood.
- Small clinical series show EUS-guided portal venous sampling is **safe and feasible**. In an 18-patient single-center cohort with suspected pancreaticobiliary cancers, **100% of portal samples contained circulating tumor cells vs <25% of peripheral blood samples** — supporting "portal liquid biopsy" for studying metastasis.
- **Should be performed as part of a research protocol.** ([[aga-2023-interventional-eus-vascular]])

## Emerging Interventions

- **Ectopic and rectal varices:** EUS targeting of rectal submucosal or penetrating vessels with sclerosants, coils, or cyanoacrylate — limited to single case reports. One series described glue ± coils for duodenal (n=3) and choledochal (n=5) varices. Larger series needed.
- **Splenic artery embolization** (cirrhosis with hypersplenism + recent variceal bleeding): 5 patients treated with combinations of endoscopic variceal ligation and EUS-guided cyanoacrylate and/or coils — **all had postprocedure fever and abdominal pain**, no splenic abscesses; post-procedure CT portal venography showed reduced vein diameter, improved cytopenias, and a **mean splenic embolization rate of 65%**. Optimal anatomy/indications and endoscopic-vs-radiologic choice undefined.
- **Arterial bleeding and pseudoaneurysms** (visceral arteries inaccessible to IR due to difficult localization or vessel tortuosity — salvage role):

| Series | Target | n | Technical success | Adverse events | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coil + glue | Splenic artery pseudoaneurysm, 2.5–6.5 cm, after failed radiologic embolization | 6 | 100% | None | All 6 occluded at 3 months |
| Thrombin injection | Visceral artery aneurysms 1.9–5.0 cm — splenic (5), left hepatic (2), gastroduodenal (1); unable to undergo embolization | 8 | 100% | 25% postprocedure abdominal pain requiring IV analgesia | Lumen obliteration 7/8 (88%), recurrence 1/8 (12%) at 12 weeks |

## Experimental

- **EUS-guided intrahepatic portosystemic shunt:** preclinical porcine studies deployed a self-expanding metal stent via EUS to bridge hepatic and portal vein, functioning as a portosystemic shunt (the [[tips|TIPS]] concept). Clinical translation requires a dedicated EUS-deliverable endovascular stent and identification of scenarios where EUS beats the IR approach.

## Open Questions

([[aga-2023-interventional-eus-vascular]])

- **Gastric variceal therapy:** technique is heterogeneous (varix vs perforator target, coil size/number, injectate) — consensus technique needed; larger multicenter data for technical success, safety, durability, generalizability; RCTs vs IR endovascular therapies (BRTO, TIPS); role in **primary prophylaxis** vs acute bleeding/secondary prophylaxis only; training pathways given how rare bleeding gastric varices are outside referral centers.
- **EUS-PPG:** whether the thresholds for portal hypertension (>5 mm Hg) and clinically significant portal hypertension (≥10 mm Hg) hold when acquired by EUS rather than percutaneous endovascular approach; whether general anesthesia (vs conscious sedation used for transjugular HVPG) shifts absolute hepatic and portal vein pressures; head-to-head EUS-PPG vs HVPG trials warranted.
- Endosonographers should be trained in interventional EUS and operate as part of a **multidisciplinary team**.
