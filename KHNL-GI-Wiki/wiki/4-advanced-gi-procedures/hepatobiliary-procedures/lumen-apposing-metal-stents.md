# Lumen-Apposing Metal Stents (LAMS)

A **lumen-apposing metal stent (LAMS)** is a short, fully covered, saddle-shaped metal stent with wide bilateral anchoring flanges that holds two apposed lumens open across a transmural tract. It is the enabling device for [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]]-guided transmural drainage — [[pancreatic-fluid-collection-drainage|pancreatic fluid collection drainage]], [[eus-guided-gallbladder-drainage|EUS-guided gallbladder drainage (EUS-GBD)]], [[eus-guided-biliary-drainage|EUS-guided biliary drainage]], EUS-guided gastroenterostomy (EUS-GE), and EUS-directed transgastric [[ercp|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]] (EDGE). This page holds the device: specifications, delivery systems, deployment, adverse events, and the competency step. **Each procedure keeps its own indication and route decision on its own page.**

## Device Design and Specifications

**Why LAMS replaced the earlier transmural constructs** ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]):

- Pigtail plastic stents and biliary **self-expandable metal stents (SEMS)** had no flanges — failure modes were leak, contralateral wall injury, and migration.
- LAMS answer all three with a **short saddle + large bilateral anchoring flanges + large inner diameter**: the flanges hold apposition, and the lumen is wide enough to pass **stones** and the **endoscope itself** (therapeutic cholecystoscopy, direct endoscopic necrosectomy).
- Device-family diameters span **6–20 mm**.

**Against a covered esophageal SEMS** ([[asge-2016-pancreatic-fluid-collections]]):

- LAMS length is **~1 cm**; covered esophageal SEMS are **no shorter than 6–7 cm**.
- Broad flanges + large inner diameter mean **no separate anchoring stent is needed**, and through-the-stent necrosectomy is easy.

### US-Available Device

Three LAMS designs are marketed in Europe and Asia; **one is commercially available in the United States** (Axios, Boston Scientific) ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]):

| Parameter | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Covering** | Fully covered |
| **Saddle lengths** | **10 and 15 mm** |
| **Inner (lumen) diameters** | **10, 15, 20 mm** |
| **Flange diameters** | **21, 24, 29 mm** |
| **Delivery catheter** | **10.5F** |
| **Scope requirement** | Therapeutic echoendoscope with a **3.7-mm working channel** |

## Delivery Systems

| | **Non–cautery-enhanced** | **Cautery-enhanced (electrocautery-tipped)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Steps** | 19-gauge **fine-needle aspiration (FNA)** puncture → guidewire → tract dilation → stent placement | Direct freehand entry and deployment through the enteral wall — **no guidewire, no device exchange** |
| **Imaging** | Fluoroscopic guidance | Entirely EUS-guided; fluoroscopy only if a salvage maneuver is needed |
| **Advantage** | Wire access is retained if the stent misdeploys | Avoids tract dilation, removes exchanges, shortens the procedure |

Sources: [[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]] (both techniques), [[asge-2016-pancreatic-fluid-collections]] (cautery-enhanced delivery avoids tract dilation).

## Indications and Regulatory Status

- **US Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved indication: drainage of pancreatic fluid collections.** ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])
- **Gallbladder drainage is off-label** — disclose it in consent; FDA approval awaits further outcome and safety data. ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])
- **EUS-GE: no dedicated FDA-approved device exists**, and the technique is expertise-limited and not widely available. ([[aga-2021-malignant-alimentary-tract-obstruction]] Best Practice Advice 10) Using a LAMS to create the gastroenterostomy is explicitly **off-label** ([[asge-2021-gastroduodenal-obstruction]]).
- **Benign gastroduodenal stricture is off-label too.** Every dedicated enteral stent available in the US is uncovered and non-removable, so covered LAMS is the off-label workaround described for **short benign pyloroduodenal strictures** ([[asge-2021-gastroduodenal-obstruction]]).
- **Benign colonic anastomotic stricture <15 mm long and refractory to endoscopic balloon dilation** — suggested by [[asge-2026-colonic-strictures]] (conditional, very low certainty). In the one comparative study against balloon dilation there was **no significant difference** in technical success, clinical success, recurrence or adverse events, and **migration was 46%**. Rarely possible in the right colon (adult colonoscopes exceed the catheter length); detail on [[colonic-stricture]].
- Procedure-specific indications and route choice: [[pancreatic-fluid-collection-drainage]], [[eus-guided-gallbladder-drainage]], [[eus-guided-biliary-drainage]], [[gastric-outlet-obstruction]].

## Technique

### Deployment Sequence

> [!howto] Cautery-enhanced LAMS deployment
> 1. Confirm the target lumen sits **<10 mm** from the planned enteral puncture site.
> 2. Puncture directly with the cautery-tipped delivery catheter (or 19-gauge FNA needle → guidewire → tract dilation if non-cautery).
> 3. Deploy the **distal flange** inside the target lumen under endosonographic guidance.
> 4. **Pull back until the flange is snug** against the far wall — this is what creates apposition.
> 5. Deploy the **proximal (enteral) flange** under EUS or endoscopic view.
>
> No fluoroscopy is needed unless a salvage maneuver becomes necessary ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]).

### Puncture-to-Lumen Distance

- **The enteral puncture site must be <10 mm from the target lumen** for safe deployment. ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])
- A target farther away than that is a reason to choose a different drainage route, not a longer stent — e.g. a gallbladder >10 mm from the enteral wall goes to transpapillary drainage ([[acute-cholecystitis]]).

### Troubleshooting

- **Prefer an over-the-wire technique** — it keeps access if the stent misdeploys — when the target is **fibrotic or contracted**, a **percutaneous drain is already in place**, or the **scope position is difficult**. ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])
- **Coaxial double-pigtail plastic stent through the LAMS** mitigates bleeding, stent occlusion by debris, contralateral wall injury, and LAMS migration — routine practice for gallbladder drainage, and used by some after pancreatic fluid collection drainage. ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]], [[asge-2016-pancreatic-fluid-collections]])

## Adverse Events and Prevention

| Procedure | **Adverse events (AEs)** |
|---|---|
| **EUS-guided pancreatic fluid collection drainage with LAMS** | Overall **13%–15%**, led by **bleeding** and **stent misdeployment/dysfunction** |
| **EUS-GE / EDGE** | **Stent misdeployment with perforation** is the most common AE, **~10%** |

([[acg-2025-eus-quality]])

### Stent Misdeployment

Misdeployment types described for EUS-GE, with observed frequencies ([[acg-2025-eus-quality]]):

| Type | Where the stent ends up | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Distal flange in the peritoneum, **no enterotomy** | **63%** |
| **II** | Distal flange in the peritoneum **with an enterotomy** | **30.4%** |
| **III** | In the small bowel with the **proximal flange in the peritoneum** | **2.2%** |
| **IV** | Distal flange in the **colon** (gastrocolic anastomosis) | **4.4%** |

> [!pitfall] A misdeployed LAMS comes out, and the patient stays fasted
> Type I — and often type II — is managed by **stent removal plus closure** of the defect. Keep the patient **fasted ≥24 hours** after such an adverse event ([[acg-2025-eus-quality]]). Closure techniques: [[endoscopic-management-of-perforation]].

### Bleeding and Dwell Time

- **Bleeding ~7% cumulative** after pancreatic fluid collection drainage with LAMS, **associated with stent dwell >4 weeks**. ([[acg-2025-eus-quality]])
- **Do not leave a LAMS, or any SEMS, in a pancreatic fluid collection long-term** — delayed bleeding is the concern beyond several weeks. ([[asge-2016-pancreatic-fluid-collections]])
- **After EDGE, dwell drives fistula persistence:** LAMS dwell **≥40 days** carried an **odds ratio of 4.5** for a persistent fistula, with an added **9.5% per extra 7 days**. ([[acg-2025-eus-quality]])

## Competency and Training

- **Master LAMS deployment in pancreatic fluid collections first — the FDA-approved indication — with ≥10 independent successful deployments before progressing to EUS-GBD.** ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])
- Deployment belongs to endoscopists trained in advanced therapeutic EUS at high-volume centers; procedure-level learning-curve numbers are on [[eus-guided-gallbladder-drainage]]. ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])
