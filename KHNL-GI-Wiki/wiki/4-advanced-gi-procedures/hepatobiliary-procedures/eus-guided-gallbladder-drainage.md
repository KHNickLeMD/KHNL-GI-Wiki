# EUS-Guided Gallbladder Drainage (EUS-GBD)

EUS-guided gallbladder drainage (EUS-GBD) is transmural gallbladder decompression: a stent (now standardly a [[lumen-apposing-metal-stents|lumen-apposing metal stent (LAMS)]]) is placed under [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]] guidance from the duodenum or stomach into the gallbladder. Primary alternative to percutaneous transhepatic gallbladder drainage (PT-GBD) and endoscopic transpapillary drainage (ET-GBD) in high-surgical-risk [[acute-cholecystitis]]. ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])

## Indications

([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])

> [!pearl] In unfit-for-surgery acute cholecystitis, EUS-GBD is now the preferred drainage route
> **"EUS-GBD should be preferred over ET-GBD and/or PT-GBD in unfit-for-surgery patients with acute cholecystitis"** — the **only Strong recommendation, on the only moderate-quality evidence, at 100% agreement**, in the whole i-EUS consensus ([[i-eus-2025-eus-gallbladder-drainage]], Statement 4). Everything else in that document is conditional.

- **Drain in the first place** when the unfit patient has **moderate to severe** acute cholecystitis (Statement 2), and **do not** offer EUS-GBD to **fit-for-surgery** patients (Statement 21) — cholecystectomy remains the gold standard.
- **Timing: early, <48 hours**, in patients not responding to conservative therapy (Statement 3) — but see the certainty caveat under [[#Outcomes]].

1. **Gallbladder drainage in high-surgical-risk patients with [[acute-cholecystitis|acute cholecystitis]]** (the core indication)
2. **Conversion of percutaneous cholecystostomy to internal drainage** in patients who will never be cholecystectomy candidates (removes the external drain)
3. **Rescue drainage of [[biliary-stricture|malignant biliary obstruction]]** in select patients after failed [[ercp|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]] *and* failed [[eus-guided-biliary-drainage|EUS-guided biliary drainage (EUS-BD)]]

## Patient Selection

- Requirements: high surgical risk, **no evidence of free gallbladder perforation**, able to tolerate [[endoscopy-sedation|anesthesia]] and a therapeutic endoscopic procedure. ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])
- **What counts as "high surgical risk"?** The American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) Clinical Practice Update (CPU) defines it only qualitatively — **"significant medical comorbidities that would make laparoscopic cholecystectomy excessively risky"**; patients "may be deemed inoperable because of comorbidities." Method selection then turns on **comorbidities, gallbladder position, cystic duct characteristics, an in-dwelling metal biliary stent, presence of ascites, future surgical candidacy, and local endoscopic/radiographic/surgical expertise**.
  - ⚠ **No numeric risk threshold is given, and that is now a deliberate position rather than an omission.** [[i-eus-2025-eus-gallbladder-drainage|i-EUS 2025]] (Statement 1) **declines to prefer any one validated score over another** and recommends instead a **multidisciplinary evaluation of surgical risk** to stratify prognosis, assess preoperative complication risk and set the treatment strategy. The scores it names, and what each was built for: **ASA Physical Status** and **P-POSSUM** for postoperative morbidity and mortality (P-POSSUM had the highest postoperative accuracy, **optimal cutoff 25** for high risk, in 1429 patients), **Charlson Comorbidity Index** for comorbidity-based survival, **APACHE** for critical-illness severity, and the 9-point **Clinical Frailty Score** for overall fitness. **Age >80** independently predicts postoperative complications. Newer models: **ACME** (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, dementia, age >80, preoperative vasoactive amines — 92% mortality prediction accuracy across surgical and non-surgical candidates) and **Chole-Risk**. ⚠ The **Tokyo Guidelines and ASA showed lower predictive accuracy for morbidity** in acute cholecystitis than P-POSSUM in a 344-patient comparison.
  - Neither [[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]] nor [[asge-2024-therapeutic-eus-biliary]] gives an American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) class, Charlson score, or other cutoff; American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2024 says only "not surgical candidates at the time of intervention." The AGA CPU defers severity assessment and drainage strategy to the Tokyo Guidelines cholecystitis papers — Mori 2018 (*TG18: management strategies for gallbladder drainage in acute cholecystitis*) and Hirota 2007 / Yokoe 2018 (*Diagnostic criteria and severity assessment of acute cholecystitis: Tokyo Guidelines*). [[tg18-2018-cholangitis-flowchart]] grades **cholangitis only**; do not carry the cholangitis grades across to cholecystitis (see [[acute-cholecystitis]]).
- **Prefer ET-GBD first** when the patient: already needs ERCP for another reason ([[choledocholithiasis]], suspected [[acute-cholangitis|cholangitis]]); is a **potential future cholecystectomy candidate** (ET-GBD preserves normal anatomy for surgery); has **coagulopathy**, **large-volume [[ascites]]**, or comorbidities that would preclude optimal percutaneous drain placement *or* EUS-GBD; or needs drainage only as a **temporizing measure**.
- **Prefer EUS-GBD** when: **in-dwelling uncovered metal biliary stent obstructing the cystic duct takeoff**; malignant cystic duct obstruction; **large cholelithiasis burden** or other indication for per-oral cholecystoscopy; poor candidate for repeat interventions.
- **Discussion with the surgical team before EUS-GBD is imperative** — later cholecystectomy requires fistula repair (postoperative leak risk); clarify future surgical candidacy and the surgeon's comfort with a transmural stent. Cholecystectomy after EUS-GBD appears safe/feasible in limited data.
- [[asge-2024-therapeutic-eus-biliary|ASGE 2024]] (conditional recommendation): in nonsurgical candidates, **EUS-GBD favored over percutaneous cholecystostomy** where expertise exists. Route-selection algorithm (incl. failure cascades) lives on [[acute-cholecystitis]].

> ⚠ **The two documents pull in different directions on ET-GBD, and both positions are shown.** [[i-eus-2025-eus-gallbladder-drainage|i-EUS 2025]] issues a **Strong** recommendation preferring EUS-GBD over ET-GBD *and* PT-GBD in unfit-for-surgery acute cholecystitis; [[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage|AGA 2023]] lists four situations above for trying **ET-GBD first**. Being newer and the document dedicated to this exact question, **i-EUS governs the general case**. The AGA situations are not contradicted so much as narrowed, and one is positively reinforced: i-EUS **Statement 6** suggests **same-session ERCP and ET-GBD** when acute cholecystitis and [[choledocholithiasis|choledocholithiasis]] coexist (~15% of cases), a 1-stage approach with similar efficacy and safety to 2-stage plus organizational benefit. **Future surgical candidacy** remains a real reason to protect the anatomy — i-EUS itself says EUS-GBD is not for fit-for-surgery patients.

- **Conversion of an existing percutaneous drain to EUS-GBD** should follow the patient's condition and predicted risk of recurrence (Statement 5). The case for it: cholecystitis recurs in **22%–45%** after percutaneous catheter removal, and in a 238-patient comparative study conversion cut recurrence vs conservative management after removal (**5% vs 18%, P=0.012**). **Time PT-GBD catheter removal to the expected efficacy of the EUS-GBD** rather than to a fixed interval (Statement 17).
- **Rescue in distal malignant biliary obstruction** after ERCP failure when EUS-BD is not feasible (Statement 19) — but **not prophylactically** in patients receiving a self-expandable metal stent for distal malignant biliary obstruction with **tumour across the cystic duct orifice** (Statement 20).

## Contraindications

([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])

- Gallbladder perforation
- Biliary peritonitis
- Large-volume ascites
- Significant coagulopathy
- Intolerance to anesthesia (contraindicates all endoscopic GBD)

## Informed Consent

- LAMS use for gallbladder drainage is currently **off-label** (FDA-approved indication is pancreatic fluid collection drainage) — patients must be told. ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])
- Procedure-specific adverse events: puncture-induced hemorrhage, bile leak with peritonitis, gallbladder perforation, stent migration; **late**: relapsing cholecystitis from stent migration/occlusion.
- Future cholecystectomy may be **more difficult or not possible** after EUS-GBD.

## Technique

([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])

**Stent:** LAMS — device specifications, the two delivery systems, the deployment sequence, the puncture-to-lumen distance rule, and deployment troubleshooting live on [[lumen-apposing-metal-stents]]. Use here is off-label (see [[#Informed Consent]]).

**Access route — duodenum vs stomach:** no significant adverse event (AE) difference reported; authors prefer the **duodenal bulb** (prepyloric antral LAMS occlude with food more easily; higher inward-migration risk from antral contraction), but the deciding factor is the point of **closest gallbladder–gastrointestinal (GI) wall apposition**. Distal stomach is easier for surgeons to access if the drainage site later needs closure. **Either route is acceptable; personalize it** to the EUS window, any potential future surgical indication, and the **expected duration of LAMS indwelling** ([[i-eus-2025-eus-gallbladder-drainage]] Statement 11).

**More i-EUS technical positions** ([[i-eus-2025-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]):

- **Use a LAMS** (Statement 7, 100% agreement). Across 21 studies (166 patients), technical and clinical success were similar for double-pigtail plastic stents, self-expandable metal stents (SEMS) and LAMS, but **adverse events were 18% / 12% / 10%** respectively. Plastic pigtails leak and cause peritonitis through small caliber and poor fistula sealing; bare SEMS seal better but migrate and can injure the opposite wall (**antimigratory SEMS**: clinical success OR 2.16, 1.07–4.36; adverse events OR 0.36, 0.14–0.98).
- **≥15 mm diameter could be preferred *when cholecystoscopy is scheduled*** (Statement 8) — the condition is planned access for lithotomy, clearance or polyp removal, **not** stone burden. A 102-patient comparison of 10 mm vs 15 mm found **no significant difference in clinical success or adverse events**.
- **Perform in a fluoroscopy-equipped room** (Statement 10).
- **Contracted gallbladder or poor lumen visualization → saline injection** may be attempted to distend the lumen before puncture (Statement 12).

> [!pitfall] Do not balloon-dilate the LAMS after deploying it
> **Routine dilation is not suggested** (Statement 9, 100% agreement). LAMS do not reach full caliber immediately — the metal mesh **expands gradually over the following days** and that is enough. Immediate dilation is routine in *other* LAMS applications ([[pancreatic-fluid-collection-drainage|pancreatic fluid collection drainage]], EUS-directed transenteric ERCP), which is exactly why it gets done here by habit; **no dedicated study supports it in EUS-GBD**, one long-term series found **specific adverse events in 14%** of immediately dilated patients, and series that deliberately did not dilate reported technical success 95.5%–100%, clinical success 100% and adverse events 0%–4.5% ([[i-eus-2025-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]).

## Outcomes

([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])

- **Technical success 90%–98.7%; clinical success 89%–98.4%.**
- **Adverse events 4.8%–22%:** bleeding, recurrent cholecystitis, stent migration, occlusion; meta-analysis morbidity 20.4% (dislodgement, blockage, leak, peritonitis, pneumoperitoneum, abscess, recurrent cholecystitis); mortality 3.9%, predominantly ongoing sepsis in this comorbid population.
- **vs PT-GBD** (5 comparative studies, 488 patients, incl. the DRAC-1 international randomized controlled trial [RCT]): comparable technical/clinical success; PT-GBD associated with longer hospital stay, longer time to clinical resolution, higher reintervention rates, higher postprocedure pain scores, trend to more AEs at 30 d/1 y; **recurrent cholecystitis 20% (PT) vs 2.6% (EUS), P = .029**; PT-GBD readmissions driven by drain leaks/obstruction/dislodgement.
> [!pitfall] Two findings that complicate the "EUS-GBD is best" headline
> **Mortality.** A pooled analysis found EUS-GBD had higher clinical success than ET-GBD and PT-GBD but an **all-cause mortality of 26% vs 16.6% and 11.2% (P=0.001)**. i-EUS attributes this to **selection bias** — EUS-GBD being done in patients with lower overall survival — but it is **not refuted by comparative data**, and the Strong recommendation rests on adverse events, recurrence and reintervention, **not on mortality**.
> **No modality wins outright.** On network meta-analysis EUS-GBD led on recurrent cholecystitis, **ET-GBD on reintervention, unplanned readmission and mortality**, and PT-GBD on technical success, with **adverse-event risk comparable across all three** ([[i-eus-2025-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]).

- **The <48 h timing rule is extrapolated.** **No study addresses the timing of EUS-GBD specifically** — the five comparing early vs late drainage studied *percutaneous* drainage, and even there "early" meant <24 h in three, <48 h in one and <72 h in another; two found fewer drainage-related complications and three a shorter stay, but **two found no clinical benefit and no reduction in morbidity or mortality**. i-EUS downgraded the statement to **very low** certainty for this reason ([[i-eus-2025-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]).
- **A second RCT reinforces drainage over surgery in the high-risk:** a multicentre trial in 142 high-risk patients was **terminated early** for major complications of **65% with PT-GBD vs 12%** in the surgical arm (RR 0.19, 0.10–0.37; P<0.001), with 66% requiring reintervention.
- **vs ET-GBD:** ET-GBD has lower technical (**84% vs 98%**) and clinical (**91% vs 97%**) success — cystic duct occlusion by tumor, stone, or uncovered metal stent is the dominant failure mode; ET-GBD cannot treat gallstones and may need stent exchanges. Network meta-analysis: **recurrent cholecystitis lowest after EUS-GBD** (relative risk [RR] EUS 1.089 vs PT 2.02 vs ET 2.891) but **mortality lowest after ET-GBD** (RR ET 1.29 vs PT 2.09 vs EUS 2.62).

## Follow-Up / Stent Management

Two options after cholecystitis resolves ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]):

1. **Per-oral cholecystoscopy at 4–6 weeks** through the LAMS: complete stone clearance (basket ± laser lithotripsy) and **exchange of the LAMS for double-pigtail plastic stents** to maintain the fistula long-term. Technical success 93.1%; 56% of patients pass stones spontaneously; overall stone clearance 88% after a mean 1.25 sessions.
2. **Leave the LAMS permanently** (frail patients, refusal of a second procedure): delayed AEs 7.1% at median 275 days; **3-year stent patency 86%** — reasonable in select very-high-risk patients.

**Periprocedural and follow-up positions** ([[i-eus-2025-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]):

- **No routine antibiotic prophylaxis** before EUS-GBD (Statement 13).
- **Antithrombotics: manage as for a high-risk-for-bleeding endoscopic procedure** (Statement 14). But the drainage need can override: **in patients at high bleeding risk, or when antithrombotic therapy cannot be stopped, EUS-GBD can still be considered** on the clinical need for drainage.
- **No routine medical therapy afterwards** (Statement 15) — nothing is given as standard once the stent is in.
- **Early oral refeeding (<48 h)** after an uncomplicated procedure (Statement 16, 100% agreement).
- **LAMS permanent indwelling is an individualized decision** (Statement 18) — corroborating the two options above. **Exchange for double-pigtail plastic stents when long-term life expectancy is predicted.**
- **EUS-GBD does not make a later cholecystectomy more complex** than PT-GBD does, and the **LAMS should be removed before surgery** (Statement 22).

## Quality Benchmarks

From [[acg-2025-eus-quality|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG)/ASGE 2025 EUS quality indicators]]:

- **Technical success in EUS-GBD: target >90%** (Grade 2B)
- **Overall adverse-event rate after EUS-GBD: <20%** (Grade 2C)

## Training

([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])

- Limited to high-volume centers with expertise in biliary disease and interventional EUS; requires advanced EUS training.
- Published learning-curve estimates: competency at ~**19–25 procedures** — but AGA 2023 declines to set a required case number, citing prior experience, model training and the individual trainee.
- Authors' stepwise pathway: complete the LAMS competency step in pancreatic fluid collections first ([[lumen-apposing-metal-stents]]) → first EUS-GBD cases in significantly distended gallbladders with no intervening tissue. Prior experience, model training, and individual learning curves all modify progression.
