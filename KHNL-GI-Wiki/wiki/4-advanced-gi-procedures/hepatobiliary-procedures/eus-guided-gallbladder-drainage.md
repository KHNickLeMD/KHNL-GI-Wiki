# EUS-Guided Gallbladder Drainage (EUS-GBD)

EUS-guided gallbladder drainage (EUS-GBD) is transmural gallbladder decompression: a stent (now standardly a [[lumen-apposing-metal-stents|lumen-apposing metal stent (LAMS)]]) is placed under [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]] guidance from the duodenum or stomach into the gallbladder. Primary alternative to percutaneous transhepatic gallbladder drainage (PT-GBD) and endoscopic transpapillary drainage (ET-GBD) in high-surgical-risk [[acute-cholecystitis]]. ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])

## Indications

> [!pearl] In unfit-for-surgery acute cholecystitis, EUS-GBD is the preferred drainage route
> **"EUS-GBD should be preferred over ET-GBD and/or PT-GBD in unfit-for-surgery patients with acute cholecystitis"** — the **only Strong recommendation, on the only moderate-quality evidence, at 100% agreement**, in the whole i-EUS consensus ([[i-eus-2025-eus-gallbladder-drainage]], Statement 4). Everything else in that document is conditional.

| Indication | Who / when | Qualifiers |
|---|---|---|
| **[[acute-cholecystitis\|Acute cholecystitis]] in the unfit-for-surgery patient** (core indication) | **Moderate to severe** disease, **not responding to conservative therapy** — drain **early, <48 h** (Statements 2, 3) | **Not** for fit-for-surgery patients (Statement 21); cholecystectomy remains the gold standard |
| **Conversion of a percutaneous cholecystostomy to internal drainage** | Patient who will never be a cholecystectomy candidate; decide on the patient's condition and **predicted risk of recurrent cholecystitis** (Statement 5) | Removes the external drain; time catheter removal to the expected efficacy of the EUS-GBD, not a fixed interval (Statement 17) |
| **Rescue drainage in distal malignant [[biliary-stricture\|biliary obstruction]]** | After **failed [[ercp\|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]]** when **[[eus-guided-biliary-drainage\|EUS-guided biliary drainage (EUS-BD)]] is not feasible** (Statement 19) | **Not routine prophylaxis** when a self-expandable metal stent is placed for distal malignant biliary obstruction with **tumour across the cystic duct orifice** (Statement 20) |

Statement numbers throughout are the consensus document's own ([[i-eus-2025-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]). The severity grading that defines "moderate to severe": [[acute-cholecystitis]].

- **The <48 h rule is extrapolated, not tested.** No study addresses the timing of EUS-GBD itself; all five early-vs-late studies used percutaneous drainage, where "early" meant <24 h in three, <48 h in one and <72 h in another, and **two found no clinical benefit and no reduction in morbidity or mortality** (two others found fewer drainage-related complications, three a shorter stay). Hence Statement 3's **very low** certainty.
- **Why conversion of a percutaneous drain is worth doing:** cholecystitis recurs in **22%–45%** after percutaneous catheter removal without surgery; in a 238-patient retrospective comparison, endoscopic conversion cut recurrence to **5% vs 18% (P = 0.012)**.
- **Prophylaxis — the numbers behind "not routine".** Post-ERCP cholecystitis after a self-expandable metal stent runs **4.1%–9.7%**, and **9.6%–16.8%** when tumour crosses the cystic duct orifice. A 44-patient randomized controlled trial (RCT) of prophylactic EUS-GBD in that subgroup cut it from **22.7% (5/22) to 0%**, but adverse events of EUS-GBD in a *normal* gallbladder run ~**13%** — so it stays **case-by-case** (consider it with multiple risk factors; otherwise drain later if cholecystitis develops).

## Patient Selection

### Surgical Risk

- **"High surgical risk" has no numeric definition.** The American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) Clinical Practice Update (CPU) gives it qualitatively — **"significant medical comorbidities that would make laparoscopic cholecystectomy excessively risky"** ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]); [[asge-2024-therapeutic-eus-biliary|American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2024]] says only "not surgical candidates at the time of intervention."
- **i-EUS prefers no one score and asks for a multidisciplinary evaluation** of surgical risk instead — to stratify prognosis, assess the preoperative risk of complications and decide the treatment strategy (Statement 1). The scores it names:

| Score | What it estimates | Detail given |
|---|---|---|
| **American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) Physical Status** | Anesthesiology risk | — |
| **Portsmouth Physiological and Operative Severity Score for the enumeration of Mortality and Morbidity (P-POSSUM)** | Morbidity and mortality after surgery | **Highest postoperative predictive accuracy** of the existing scores; **optimal cutoff 25** identifies high risk |
| **Charlson Comorbidity Index** | Survival based on comorbidities | — |
| **Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation (APACHE)** | Severity of critical illness | — |
| **Clinical Frailty Score** | Overall fitness | **9-point scale**; used to judge eligibility for laparoscopic cholecystectomy vs minimally invasive drainage |
| **Acute Cholecystitis Mortality Estimation** | Mortality | Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease + dementia + **age >80 y** + preoperative vasoactive amines; **92% accuracy**, in surgical and non-surgical candidates |
| **Chole-Risk** | In-hospital and 30-day mortality and major morbidity | Derived in **1429 patients** |

- All were validated primarily preoperatively and are only now being studied in non-surgical candidates; **age >80 y independently predicts postoperative complications and mortality**, worse than at 65–79 y. ([[i-eus-2025-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])
- Also required for the transmural route: **no evidence of free gallbladder perforation**, able to tolerate [[endoscopy-sedation|anesthesia]] and a therapeutic endoscopic procedure. ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])
- **Multidisciplinary discussion whenever a later cholecystectomy is possible** — the cholecystogastrostomy or cholecystoduodenostomy may complicate it. ([[acg-2025-eus-quality]])

> [!pitfall] High surgical risk is not the same as inoperable
> A multicentre trial randomizing **142 high-risk patients** was **terminated early because major complications were higher with percutaneous drainage — 65% vs 12% with surgery** (relative risk [RR] 0.19, 95% confidence interval 0.10–0.37; P < 0.001), 66% of them needing reintervention. Surgery is still feasible in selected high-risk patients, which is why the risk assessment is multidisciplinary ([[i-eus-2025-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]).

### Route Choice

*Three-route comparison and the full route-selection algorithm, incl. failure cascades: [[acute-cholecystitis]].*

- **Prefer ET-GBD first** when the patient already needs ERCP for another reason ([[choledocholithiasis]], suspected [[acute-cholangitis|cholangitis]]); is a **potential future cholecystectomy candidate** (it preserves normal anatomy for surgery); has **coagulopathy**, **large-volume [[ascites]]**, or comorbidities that would preclude optimal percutaneous drain placement *or* EUS-GBD; or needs drainage only as a **temporizing measure**. ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])
- **Prefer EUS-GBD** when there is an **in-dwelling uncovered metal biliary stent obstructing the cystic duct takeoff** or malignant cystic duct obstruction; a **large cholelithiasis burden** or other indication for per-oral cholecystoscopy; or a poor candidate for repeat interventions. ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]) [[asge-2024-therapeutic-eus-biliary|ASGE 2024]] (conditional recommendation) likewise favors it over percutaneous cholecystostomy in nonsurgical candidates where expertise exists.
- **Acute cholecystitis plus [[choledocholithiasis|choledocholithiasis]] (~15% of cases): same-session ERCP and ET-GBD** (Statement 6) — 1-stage, with efficacy and safety similar to 2-stage plus organizational benefit. If ET-GBD fails, EUS-GBD combined with ERCP is a rescue with success comparable to EUS-GBD alone.
- **Discussion with the surgical team before EUS-GBD is imperative** — later cholecystectomy requires fistula repair (postoperative leak risk); clarify future candidacy and the surgeon's comfort with a transmural stent. ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])

> ⚠ **The documents pull in different directions on ET-GBD, and all positions are shown.** [[i-eus-2025-eus-gallbladder-drainage|i-EUS 2025]] recommends EUS-GBD over ET-GBD *and* PT-GBD here (Strong); [[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage|AGA 2023]] lists the four situations above for trying **ET-GBD first**. Being newer and dedicated to this exact question, **i-EUS governs the general case** — the AGA situations are narrowed rather than contradicted, and one is reinforced, since i-EUS itself keeps ET-GBD for concomitant choledocholithiasis and excludes fit-for-surgery patients. Older, and so outranked here, [[tg18-2018-gallbladder-drainage|Tokyo Guidelines 2018]] makes **percutaneous drainage the standard** and permits EUS-GBD only in high-volume institutes with skilled pancreaticobiliary endoscopists — the operative position where that expertise is absent ([[acute-cholecystitis]]).

## Contraindications

- Gallbladder perforation · biliary peritonitis · large-volume ascites · significant coagulopathy. Intolerance to anesthesia contraindicates **all** forms of endoscopic gallbladder drainage. ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])
- **Extra caution, not absolute exclusions:** emphysematous cholecystitis, contained gallbladder perforation associated with cholecystitis, large amounts of ascites. ([[acg-2025-eus-quality]])

## Informed Consent

- **Regulatory status changed between the two documents, and the newer governs.** [[acg-2025-eus-quality|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG)/ASGE 2025]] states that electrocautery-enhanced LAMS **gained US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval for this indication**; [[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage|AGA 2023]] called gallbladder drainage an **off-label** use of LAMS that the patient had to be told about.
- Procedure-specific adverse events: puncture-induced hemorrhage, bile leak with peritonitis, gallbladder perforation, stent migration; **late**: relapsing cholecystitis from stent migration or occlusion. ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])
- Future cholecystectomy may be **more difficult or not possible** after EUS-GBD. ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])

## Technique

**Device and deployment** — LAMS specifications, the two delivery systems, the deployment sequence, the **<10 mm** puncture-to-lumen distance rule, over-the-wire troubleshooting and the coaxial double-pigtail stent live on [[lumen-apposing-metal-stents]]. ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])

**Access route — duodenum vs stomach.** **Either is acceptable; personalize it** to the EUS window, any potential future surgical indication, and the **expected duration of LAMS indwelling** ([[i-eus-2025-eus-gallbladder-drainage]], Statement 11); no significant adverse event (AE) difference between routes has been shown. AGA authors prefer the **duodenal bulb** (prepyloric antral LAMS occlude with food more easily; antral contraction raises inward-migration risk), but the deciding factor is the point of **closest gallbladder–gastrointestinal (GI) wall apposition**; the distal stomach is easier for a surgeon to reach if the site later needs closure. ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])

**Stent choice — use a LAMS** (Statement 7, 100% agreement). Across a systematic review of 21 studies (166 patients) technical and clinical success were similar (**90%–100%**) for all three constructs; the adverse events and failure modes were not:

| Stent | Adverse events | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| Double-pigtail plastic | **18%** | Bile leak and peritonitis — small caliber, inadequate fistula sealing |
| Self-expandable metal stent (SEMS) | **12%** | Seals the fistula better, but migrates and can injure the contralateral wall |
| **LAMS** (preferred) | **10%** | Large bore, wide flanges and an electrocautery tip give single-step deployment |

- In a meta-analysis of 27 studies (1004 patients), **antimigratory SEMS and LAMS** improved clinical success (odds ratio [OR] 2.16, 95% CI 1.07–4.36) and reduced adverse events (OR 0.36, 0.14–0.98). ([[i-eus-2025-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])
- **Diameter: ≥15 mm could be preferred when cholecystoscopy is scheduled** (Statement 8) — i.e. when access for lithotomy, gallbladder clearance or polyp removal is planned. A 102-patient comparison of 10 mm (n = 78) vs 15 mm (n = 24) found **no significant difference in clinical success or adverse events**; the DRAC 1 trial and an international consensus suggest 10 mm for small and 15 mm for large gallbladder stones.
- **Perform in a fluoroscopy-equipped room** (Statement 10), with access to interventional radiology and pancreatobiliary surgery. Fluoroscopy matters most when converting a percutaneous drain and for prophylactic EUS-GBD, and is mandatory for the Seldinger multistep technique.
- **Contracted gallbladder or poor lumen visualization →** inject **saline mixed with iodine contrast through a 19-gauge fine-needle aspiration needle** to distend the lumen before puncture (Statement 12); injection through an existing percutaneous tube does the same when converting.

> [!pitfall] Do not balloon-dilate the LAMS after deploying it
> **Routine dilation is not suggested** (Statement 9, 100% agreement). LAMS do not reach full caliber immediately — the metal mesh **expands gradually over the following days** and that is enough. Immediate dilation is routine in *other* LAMS applications ([[pancreatic-fluid-collection-drainage|pancreatic fluid collection drainage]], EUS-directed transenteric ERCP), which is exactly why it gets done here by habit; **no dedicated study supports it in EUS-GBD**, and series that deliberately did not dilate reported technical success 95.5%–100%, clinical success 100% and adverse events 0%–4.5% ([[i-eus-2025-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]).

## Outcomes

- **Technical success 90%–98.7%; clinical success 89%–98.4%** ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]). Pooled across studies and meta-analyses: technical success **95.3%**, with the **highest clinical success of the three routes at 96.7%** ([[acg-2025-eus-quality]]).
- **Adverse events 4.8%–22%** — bleeding, recurrent cholecystitis, stent migration, occlusion; meta-analytic morbidity **20.4%** (dislodgement, blockage, leak, peritonitis, pneumoperitoneum, abdominal abscess, recurrent cholecystitis); **mortality 3.9%**, predominantly from ongoing sepsis in this comorbid population. ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])
- **vs PT-GBD** (5 comparative studies, 488 patients, incl. the DRAC 1 international RCT): comparable success, but percutaneous drainage brings a longer stay, slower resolution, more reintervention, more postprocedure pain and **recurrent cholecystitis 20% vs 2.6% (P = .029)**. **vs ET-GBD:** ET-GBD has lower technical (**84% vs 98%**) and clinical (**91% vs 97%**) success, its dominant failure mode being cystic duct occlusion by tumor, stone or uncovered metal stent; it also cannot treat gallstones. ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]) Route-by-route relative risks: [[acute-cholecystitis]].

> [!pitfall] Two findings that complicate the "EUS-GBD is best" headline
> **Mortality.** A pooled analysis found EUS-GBD had higher clinical success than ET-GBD and PT-GBD but an **all-cause mortality of 26% vs 16.6% and 11.2% (P = 0.001)**, which i-EUS attributes to **selection bias** (EUS-GBD done in patients with lower overall survival). The Strong recommendation rests on adverse events, recurrence and reintervention, **not on mortality**.
> **No modality wins outright.** On network meta-analysis EUS-GBD led on recurrent cholecystitis, **ET-GBD on reintervention, unplanned readmission and mortality**, and PT-GBD on technical success, with **adverse-event risk comparable across all three** ([[i-eus-2025-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]).

## Follow-Up / Stent Management

Two options once cholecystitis resolves; **which one is an individualized decision** (Statement 18):

1. **Per-oral cholecystoscopy at 4–6 weeks** through the LAMS: complete stone clearance (basket ± laser lithotripsy) and **exchange of the LAMS for double-pigtail plastic stents** to maintain the fistula long-term — the choice **when long-term life expectancy is predicted**. Technical success 93.1%; 56% of patients pass stones spontaneously; overall stone clearance 88% after a mean 1.25 sessions ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]). In 128 patients, replacing the LAMS with two 7F double-pigtail stents at 4 weeks gave a mean stent patency of **421 days with no recurrent cholecystitis** ([[i-eus-2025-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]).
2. **Leave the LAMS permanently** — the frail patient, or one unwilling to undergo a second procedure: delayed adverse events 7.1% at median 275 days follow-up, **3-year stent patency 86%** ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]). Taking the stent out after the gallbladder is cleared carries a **40% risk of recurrent cholelithiasis and cholecystitis** ([[i-eus-2025-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]).

**Periprocedural and follow-up positions** ([[i-eus-2025-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]):

- **No routine antibiotic prophylaxis** before EUS-GBD (Statement 13).
- **Antithrombotics: manage as for a high-risk-for-bleeding endoscopic procedure** (Statement 14). But the drainage need can override: **in patients at high bleeding risk, or when antithrombotic therapy cannot be stopped, EUS-GBD can still be considered** on the clinical need for drainage.
- **No routine medical therapy afterwards** (Statement 15) — nothing is given as standard once the stent is in; provide analgesia, for which no standardized protocol exists.
- **Early oral refeeding (<48 h)** after an uncomplicated procedure (Statement 16, 100% agreement). Food impaction causing recurrent cholecystitis has been reported only weeks later.
- **Removing the percutaneous catheter after conversion: individualize it** to the expected efficacy of the EUS-GBD (Statement 17). Judge tract maturity on percutaneous cholangiography — **well-formed = an intact column of contrast from gallbladder to skin; poorly formed = contrast spillage**; a tract is mature ~3–4 weeks after the original placement. Practice runs from same-session removal to a median of 7–13 days, catheter clamped meanwhile.
- **EUS-GBD does not make a later cholecystectomy more complex** than PT-GBD does, and the **LAMS should be removed before surgery** (Statement 22).

## Quality Benchmarks

From [[acg-2025-eus-quality|ACG/ASGE 2025 EUS quality indicators]]:

- **Technical success in EUS-GBD: target >90%** (indicator 13, level of evidence 2B)
- **Overall adverse-event rate after EUS-GBD: <20%** (indicator 20, level of evidence 2C)
- Both targets sit **deliberately below published success rates**: the literature comes from expert centers and early experience with a novel procedure.

## Training

([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]])

- Limited to high-volume centers with expertise in biliary disease and interventional EUS; requires advanced EUS training.
- Published learning-curve estimates: competency at ~**19–25 procedures** — but AGA 2023 sets no required case number, citing prior experience, model training and the individual trainee.
- Stepwise pathway: clear the LAMS competency step in pancreatic fluid collections first ([[lumen-apposing-metal-stents]]) → first EUS-GBD cases in **significantly distended gallbladders with no intervening tissue**.
