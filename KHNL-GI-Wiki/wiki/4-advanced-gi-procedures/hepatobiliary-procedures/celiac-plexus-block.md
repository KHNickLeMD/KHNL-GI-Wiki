# Celiac Plexus Block and Neurolysis (EUS-CPB / EUS-CPN)

Injection of pharmaceuticals into and/or around the region of the **celiac ganglia** to treat upper abdominal pain of pancreatic origin ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]]).

- **Target anatomy:** the celiac plexus is a network of interconnected neural fibers surrounding the celiac artery as it branches from the aorta; it transmits visceral sensory input — including pain signals — from most of the upper abdominal organs, including the pancreas ([[aga-2022-recurrent-pancreatitis-endoscopy]]).
- **Routes:** endoscopic (under [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]] guidance), interventional radiologic (percutaneous, under fluoroscopic or computed tomography [CT] guidance), or surgical ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]], [[asge-2024-chronic-pancreatitis-endoscopy]]).
- Listed among the accepted indications for EUS ([[acg-2025-eus-quality]]).

## Block vs Neurolysis

Two different injections with two different goals — the agent, not the route, is what distinguishes them.

| | **Celiac plexus block (CPB)** | **Celiac plexus neurolysis (CPN)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Injectate** | Local anesthetic + steroid — most commonly **bupivacaine and triamcinolone** ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]]); local anesthetic **with or without** a steroid in the trials underpinning [[asge-2024-chronic-pancreatitis-endoscopy\|American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2024]] | **Cytolytic agent** — alcohol or phenol ([[asge-2016-solid-pancreatic-neoplasia]]) |
| **Mechanism** | Interrupts neural signaling in response to intra-abdominal pain ([[aga-2022-recurrent-pancreatitis-endoscopy]]) | **Ablates the neurons** of the celiac ganglia ([[asge-2016-solid-pancreatic-neoplasia]]) |
| **Usual population** | Chronic abdominal pain from [[chronic-pancreatitis\|chronic pancreatitis (CP)]] | Chronic abdominal pain from [[pancreatic-cancer\|pancreatic cancer]] ([[acg-2025-eus-quality]]) |

- ACG 2020 names bupivacaine and triamcinolone as the usual block agents but does not give a dose or an injectate volume; the ingested guidelines give injectate volume only for neurolysis (see [[#Technique]]).

## Indications

### Chronic Pancreatitis Pain

**[[asge-2024-chronic-pancreatitis-endoscopy|ASGE 2024]], Recommendation 2 — *conditional recommendation / low quality of evidence*:** in patients with painful CP in whom a decision is made to proceed with a CPB, **use an EUS-guided rather than a percutaneous approach**.

Who the block is for (ASGE 2024 general concepts + panel discussion):

- CP **nonresponsive to medical therapy**, with all other options exhausted.
- **Nonobstructive (small-duct) CP** — no endoscopic or surgical decompression option available.
- Patients with **opioid side effects**, or who wish to avoid opioids.
- ⚠ Set the expectation at consent: **fewer than 60% of patients get pain relief, and it is not sustained (<6 months).**
- A patient with an obstructed main pancreatic duct belongs in the decompression pathway instead — the stepwise pain ladder and the endoscopy-vs-surgery decision live on [[chronic-pancreatitis]].

**[[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2020]], Recommendation 10 — *conditional recommendation, very low quality of evidence*:** suggests **considering celiac plexus block for treatment of pain in CP**. ACG frames it as a relatively low-risk, opioid-free way to reduce refractory pain in **selected** patients — "selected" chosen deliberately, because numerous studies have shown no significant benefit in CP, and it is not clear which patients derive the most benefit.

**[[aga-2022-recurrent-pancreatitis-endoscopy|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2022]], Best Practice Advice (BPA) 8 (ungraded):** CPB **should not be routinely performed** for pain due to CP. Proceeding in **selected patients with debilitating pain in whom other therapeutic measures have failed** can be considered **case-by-case**, but only after discussing the unclear outcomes of the intervention and its procedural risks.

### Pancreatic Cancer Pain

**[[asge-2024-solid-pancreatic-masses|ASGE 2024 (solid pancreatic masses)]], Recommendation 7 — *conditional recommendation / low quality of evidence*:** in patients with **unresectable** pancreatic cancer and abdominal pain, **CPN as an adjunct to medical analgesic therapy**.

- **Trigger:** abdominal pain **refractory to medical therapy**, or **opioid adverse effects not well tolerated**.
- **Sequence:** given the modest benefit in pain severity and the potential undesirable effects, **analgesic therapy alone may be preferred first-line**; CPN is an add-on, not a substitute.
- **Resectable or borderline-resectable disease:** effects are not well known — the recommendation does not extend there.
- **Route:** EUS or percutaneous. The two have never been compared directly, and performed similarly in the supporting meta-analysis. **Prefer the endoscopic route when an upper endoscopy or EUS is already being done for another purpose, or when percutaneous CPN is not available.**

**[[asge-2016-solid-pancreatic-neoplasia|ASGE 2016]] (older, superseded on this topic by ASGE 2024):** suggests EUS-guided CPN be considered in patients with pancreatic-cancer-related pain (*conditional, ⊕⊕⊕◯*), calling CPN the preferred therapy for cancer-related pain. It adds that **early** EUS-CPN reduced pain and may moderate morphine consumption in newly diagnosed, painful, inoperable pancreatic cancer in a double-blind controlled trial.

## Technique

**EUS-guided CPN for pancreatic cancer pain** ([[asge-2024-solid-pancreatic-masses]]):

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| Needle | **At least 22-gauge** fine-needle aspiration (FNA) needle, or larger caliber — **not the same needle used for EUS-guided tissue acquisition (EUS-TA)** |
| Injection | **Central or bilateral**, **10–20 mL of 99% alcohol** |
| Fluids | **1 L intravenous (IV) normal saline** periprocedurally |
| Monitoring | **~2 hours** postprocedure, with vital signs and orthostatic parameters |

- **Access:** transgastric ([[asge-2016-solid-pancreatic-neoplasia]]).
- **Laterality for CPB in CP:** in a subgroup analysis, **bilateral injections of the plexus gave better pain relief than unilateral** ([[asge-2024-chronic-pancreatitis-endoscopy]]).
- **Percutaneous comparator:** performed under fluoroscopic or CT guidance ([[asge-2024-chronic-pancreatitis-endoscopy]]).
- EUS may outperform the percutaneous route in CP because of **better localization and targeting of the plexus** ([[asge-2024-chronic-pancreatitis-endoscopy]]).
- Needle caliber, alcohol volume, and the monitoring interval above are given for neurolysis; no ingested guideline specifies an equivalent needle size or injectate volume for the block.

## Outcomes

### Efficacy in Chronic Pancreatitis

**EUS-guided vs percutaneous CPB** — meta-analysis of **2 randomized controlled trials (RCTs), 74 patients** with intractable abdominal pain unresponsive to medical therapy; trials excluded CP complicated by abscess, pseudocyst, or biliary stricture ([[asge-2024-chronic-pancreatitis-endoscopy]]):

| Outcome | EUS-CPB vs percutaneous |
|---|---|
| Pain relief at **4 weeks** | Odds ratio (OR) **8.11** (95% confidence interval [CI] 2.77–23.75), I² 0% — favors EUS |
| Pain relief at **12 weeks** | OR **4.33** (95% CI 1.24–15.08), I² 0% — favors EUS |
| Pain intensity (median visual analog scale [VAS]) | Significantly lower after EUS-CPB |
| Adverse events | **No difference** |

**Single-arm EUS-CPB data** — 8 noncomparative studies, 283 CP patients: **modest pain relief in 59.5%** (95% CI 54.51–64.30). Relief is **not durable — most patients return to baseline pain in under 6 months** ([[asge-2024-chronic-pancreatitis-endoscopy]]).

**Observational data** ([[aga-2022-recurrent-pancreatitis-endoscopy]]): pain relief in **50%–60%**, duration of benefit **6 months or less**; evidence quality weak (no placebo control, short follow-up, inconsistent definitions). **Almost all patients still require analgesics after CPB.**

**[[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis|ACG 2020]] meta-analysis figures:** reported pain relief ranges widely, **25%–96%**; **median response rate 68% for EUS-guided and 61% for percutaneous** procedures. A single treatment can potentially give pain reduction or relief for **3–6 months** and may reduce or eliminate the need for oral analgesia.

*⚠ Guidelines disagree on whether the route matters in CP. ACG 2020 holds that CPB is similarly effective whether performed by an endoscopist, a radiologist, or a surgeon, with the choice made on experience and availability. The newer ASGE 2024 guideline suggests the EUS-guided route over the percutaneous one — that is what this page recommends.*

*⚠ No study has compared CPB with placebo or sham, which leaves the decision on when to use it not evidence-based and the efficacy of the block itself open ([[asge-2024-chronic-pancreatitis-endoscopy]]).*

### Efficacy in Pancreatic Cancer

**CPN added to medical analgesic therapy vs medical analgesic therapy alone** — meta-analysis of **8 RCTs, 602 patients** with unresectable pancreatic cancer and abdominal pain (CPN percutaneous in 5 studies, EUS in 2, surgical in 1) ([[asge-2024-solid-pancreatic-masses]]):

| Outcome | Result with added CPN |
|---|---|
| Pain severity at **4 weeks** | Mean difference (MD) **−0.37** points on a 10-point VAS (95% CI −0.65 to −0.09), I² 35% — **improved** |
| Pain severity at **6 months** | MD **−1.2** points VAS (95% CI −1.47 to −0.93), I² 0% — **improved** |
| **Constipation** | OR **0.23** (95% CI 0.07–0.74), I² 63% — **reduced** |
| Pain severity at 8 weeks | MD −0.7 (95% CI −1.57 to 0.17), I² 99% — no difference |
| Survival | MD 0.11 months (95% CI −1.13 to 1.35) — no difference |
| Opioid use at 4 weeks | MD −34.91 morphine milligram equivalents (95% CI −79.01 to 9.19) — no difference |
| Opioid use at 8 weeks | MD −38.31 morphine milligram equivalents (95% CI −93.57 to 16.95) — no difference |
| Quality of life (0–10) at 4 / 8 weeks | MD 0.57 (95% CI −0.43 to 1.57) / 0.57 (95% CI −0.45 to 1.6) — no difference |
| Diarrhea | OR 3.73 (95% CI 0.99–14.12) — no difference |
| Drowsiness | OR 0.92 (95% CI 0.09–9.03) — no difference |

- **Subgroup by approach** (EUS-guided vs percutaneous or surgical): effects similar to the overall result.
- **Older single-arm estimate:** meta-analysis of 5 studies, 199 patients — EUS-CPN relieved abdominal pain in **72%** ([[asge-2016-solid-pancreatic-neoplasia]]).

## Repeat and Serial Injection

- **Repeat on an "as-needed" basis, generally separating procedures by 3 months or more**, if the patient had clinical benefit from the initial celiac intervention ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]]).
- Patients who benefited from a single CPB may benefit from a **subsequent injection once relief has subsided** ([[asge-2024-chronic-pancreatitis-endoscopy]]).
- The role of **serial** CPB is even less well understood; consider only in selected patients with demonstrated benefit ([[aga-2022-recurrent-pancreatitis-endoscopy]]).

## Adverse Events

**Rates by procedure** ([[acg-2025-eus-quality]]):

| Adverse event (AE) | EUS-CPB | EUS-CPN |
|---|---|---|
| Transient diarrhea | **2%** | **10%** |
| Hypotension | **2%** | **5%** |

- Overall risk profile considered **acceptable**, but severe AEs are reported in case reports/series: **retroperitoneal abscess, empyema, paraplegia, and fatal AEs** — all deserving cautious consideration.
- **CP series** ([[asge-2024-chronic-pancreatitis-endoscopy]]): nonserious AEs in **1.6% of 220 procedures** in 158 patients — transient hypotension, diarrhea, retroperitoneal abscess, postprocedural pain.
- **CP, by category** ([[aga-2022-recurrent-pancreatitis-endoscopy]]): diarrhea and orthostatic hypotension most common; **major AEs — abscess formation, intravascular injection, paralysis — in <1%**.
- **Pancreatic cancer** — systematic review of 16 studies, 867 patients ([[asge-2024-solid-pancreatic-masses]]): **spinal cord infarction with paralysis 0.2%**, transient diarrhea 9%, pain exacerbation 8%, hypotension 6%. Add the AEs inherent to an invasive procedure — [[endoscopy-sedation|sedation]] events and bleeding from needle puncture.
- **Reported major AEs of CPN** ([[asge-2016-solid-pancreatic-neoplasia]]): reversible and permanent paralysis, organ puncture, gastric necrosis.
- Procedural risks of the block itself include bleeding and allergic reaction, plus the risks of and need for sedation ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]]).
- ⚠ Active alcohol consumption is a relative contraindication to elective interventional procedures in CP — see [[chronic-pancreatitis]].
