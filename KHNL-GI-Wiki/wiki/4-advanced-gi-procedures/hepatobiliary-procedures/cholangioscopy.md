**Cholangioscopy** is direct endoscopic visualization of the biliary (or pancreatic) ductal lumen, most commonly performed as single-operator peroral cholangioscopy through the working channel of a duodenoscope during [[ercp|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]]. It permits targeted, image-directed mini-forceps biopsy of biliary lesions, visual characterization of strictures, and intraductal lithotripsy of stones that resist conventional extraction.

## Indications

### Indeterminate Biliary Strictures

*Full multimodality sampling strategy and the comparative sensitivity table across modalities: [[biliary-stricture]].*

- **[[asge-2023-indeterminate-biliary-strictures|ASGE 2023]] (Rec 2 — conditional, very low quality):** cholangioscopic-guided biopsy sampling is suggested for [[biliary-stricture|biliary strictures]] of undetermined etiology that are
  - **(a) nondistal with a high probability of adequate drainage of the critical liver segment, *or* (b) a previous nondiagnostic ERCP done without cholangioscopy** — **and (c) at centers with clinical expertise and easy access to the equipment.** Otherwise the American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) suggests ERCP with *or* without cholangioscopy.
- **Yield:** ERCP **with** cholangioscopy is **0.72** sensitive for malignancy (95% confidence interval [CI] 0.66–0.77) vs **0.61** (0.57–0.66) without (*P* = .001); incremental yield **27%** across 4 observational studies and **41%** in the single randomized controlled trial (RCT).
- Cholangioscopy-directed biopsy is **60–75%** sensitive in patients with negative prior sampling — superior to [[brush-cytology|brush cytology]] or fluoroscopy-directed forceps biopsy alone, which is why ≥2 sampling modalities are used at the index ERCP ([[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]]).
- ⚠ **Why "nondistal":** in the very distal common bile duct (CBD) the cholangioscope is unstable and the mini-forceps hard to pass. In one study fluoroscopy-directed intraductal biopsy beat cholangioscopic biopsy for distal strictures (**76% vs 50%**), with no difference for proximal strictures — distal strictures are an [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]] sampling indication instead.
- **Cost and time:** adds **~14 min** (95% CI 10–20) and **~$2637** when done during an ERCP with stent placement. Still cost-effective — modeling in [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis|primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)]] gave a gain of 0.22 quality-adjusted life-years (QALYs) for an extra $8562.44, base-case incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) **$39,277.25 per QALY**, below the $50,000/QALY willingness-to-pay threshold.
- **Adjunctive [[confocal-laser-endomicroscopy|probe-based confocal laser endomicroscopy (pCLE)]]** — the Miami and Paris criteria and the pooled performance figures live on that page. ASGE panel position: pCLE is **difficult to master and expensive**, so widespread adoption is unlikely in the near future.

### Difficult Bile Duct Stones

- **[[asge-2015-ercp-benign-biliary|ASGE 2015]] (Rec 6 — *low quality*):** cholangioscopy is an **adjunctive technique for difficult bile duct stones not amenable to removal after sphincterotomy with or without balloon dilation or mechanical lithotripsy.**
- **[[bsg-2017-common-bile-duct-stones|BSG 2017]] (Rec 15 — *low quality, strong*):** cholangioscopy-guided **electrohydraulic lithotripsy (EHL) or laser lithotripsy (LL)** when other endoscopic options fail to clear the duct.
- **Position among the options** for large and difficult [[choledocholithiasis|choledocholithiasis]] (stone-clearance sequence: [[ercp]]): [[asge-2019-choledocholithiasis|ASGE 2019]] Rec 4 suggests **intraductal therapy *or* conventional therapy with papillary dilation** — alternatives chosen by local expertise, cost and preference, not a fixed order (Conditional, Very low). Large-balloon dilation = **≥12 mm** ([[asge-2015-ercp-benign-biliary]]).
- **What actually makes extraction difficult:** in most cases **size, shape and number** of stones; **altered anatomy from previous surgery** independently lowers the chance of success ([[bsg-2017-common-bile-duct-stones]]).
- ⚠ **"Difficult" means two different things in two tier-1 guidelines, and the difference is which rung of the ladder you are on.** Neither definition rests on size, impaction, intrahepatic location or duct taper — both are **functional**, and they sit one rung apart:

| Source | A stone is "difficult" when… | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| [[asge-2015-ercp-benign-biliary\|ASGE 2015]] (Rec 6) | it is **not removable after sphincterotomy ± balloon dilation or mechanical lithotripsy** — i.e. *before* cholangioscopy | **Cholangioscopy-guided lithotripsy** |
| [[bsg-2017-common-bile-duct-stones\|BSG 2017]] | standard extraction **plus mechanical lithotripsy, plus endoscopic papillary balloon dilation (EPBD) after sphincterotomy, plus cholangioscopy** (or extracorporeal shock wave lithotripsy where available) **have all failed** — "difficult **stone disease**" | **Percutaneous radiological extraction** (major complications **3.6%–6.8%**) **or open duct exploration** |

- **Duct clearance with cholangioscopy-guided lithotripsy** — each row is its own population, so read across rows with care:

| Source | Population | Clearance |
|---|---|---|
| [[asge-2015-ercp-benign-biliary\|ASGE 2015]] | difficult **extrahepatic** stones | **83%–100%** |
| [[bsg-2017-common-bile-duct-stones\|BSG 2017]] | all had already failed mechanical lithotripsy **and** EPBD with prior sphincterotomy | **73%–97%** |
| [[acg-asge-2026-ercp-quality-indicators\|ACG/ASGE 2026]] | CBD stones >10 mm; meta-analysis of 19 RCTs (2,752 patients) comparing 5 approaches | **100%** with single-operator cholangioscopy — the highest of the five; mechanical lithotripsy the lowest at **57%** |

### Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis

- **[[aga-2019-psc-cancer-surveillance|AGA 2019]] (Best Practice Advice 4):** investigate [[cholangiocarcinoma|cholangiocarcinoma]] with ERCP and brush cytology, with or without [[fish|fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH)]] and/or cholangioscopy, in PSC patients with **worsening clinical symptoms, worsening cholestasis, or a dominant stricture**. ERCP with brush cytology is *not* a routine surveillance test in PSC.
- **Reaches lesions nothing else reaches:** in a prospective study of 47 PSC patients, **4 target lesions could not have been reached without cholangioscopic visualization** of the duct.
- Cholangioscopy can **differentiate [[igg4-related-cholangitis|immunoglobulin G4 (IgG4)–related sclerosing cholangitis]] from PSC**.
- ⚠ **Cannulation failure is more frequent in PSC — 15% vs 2% in controls (*P* = .015)** — even though sensitivity, specificity and accuracy for cancer diagnosis are similar in PSC and non-PSC patients.

## Visual Characterization of Malignant Strictures

Malignant strictures take one of **three cholangioscopic morphologies** — knowing which one you are looking at is what targets the mini-forceps bite ([[asge-2023-indeterminate-biliary-strictures]]):

| Malignant morphology | Distinguishing features |
|---|---|
| **Nodular** | Irregular mucosa with **severe neovascularization**; the mass can obstruct the lumen |
| **Papillary** | **Numerous papillary projections**, **less** neovascularization than nodular |
| **Infiltrative** | Luminal narrowing **without a discrete mass**; more **whitish mucosal discoloration** plus neovascularization |

## Technique

- **Two platforms:**
  - **Single-operator cholangioscopy** — disposable cholangioscope passed through the duodenoscope; current digital platforms give one-operator use, improved imaging and a broader range of interventional accessories. The older two-operator "mother–baby" systems were technically demanding and broke easily, which restricted use of the technique. ([[bsg-2017-common-bile-duct-stones]], [[asge-2026-ercp-adverse-events]])
  - **Direct peroral cholangioscopy** — an ultra-slim video endoscope steered through the sphincterotomy into the duct; image quality is excellent, and the limitation is scope stability against the duodenal loop. ([[bsg-2017-common-bile-duct-stones]])
- **Drain what you fill.** Cholangioscopy requires instillation of water or saline solution, which risks introducing infection into a proximal biliary tree that is not adequately drained — so ensure drainage of the critical segment. The ASGE panel was split on whether to do it at the index ERCP or wait until the proximal ducts are decompressed. ([[asge-2023-indeterminate-biliary-strictures]])
- **Biopsy:** align and advance the forceps carefully into the CBD under fluoroscopic guidance, and discuss the result with a dedicated gastrointestinal (GI) pathologist (ASGE 2023 general concepts, ungraded).
- **Lithotripsy energy:** EHL generates a shock wave from the rapid thermal expansion of fluid around a high-voltage spark; LL focuses pulsed laser energy that the water in the stone absorbs, expanding it. **Both must be delivered under direct vision** for safety and precise targeting. ([[bsg-2017-common-bile-duct-stones]])

> [!pitfall] Turn the gas off for direct peroral cholangioscopy
> In **direct** peroral cholangioscopy, **the air or carbon dioxide (CO₂) supply is switched off while the duct is being inspected, to reduce the risk of gas embolism**. ([[bsg-2017-common-bile-duct-stones]])

## Adverse Events

Cholangioscopy is considered safe overall, but its profile is **distinct from routine ERCP — notably infection and pancreatitis** ([[asge-2026-ercp-adverse-events]]). Rates for ERCP as a whole, and the cross-technique comparison: [[ercp]].

- **Post-ERCP pancreatitis (PEP) 2%** (95% CI 2–3), within an overall adverse-event rate of **7%** (6–9) — meta-analysis of 49 studies of cholangioscopy for difficult stones or indeterminate strictures. **7.1%** in a prospective PSC cohort.
- **[[acute-cholangitis|Cholangitis]] is the signature risk.** Infectious adverse events **1%–12%**; bacteremia of uncertain significance **13.9%**. Against conventional ERCP in ~4000 chronic pancreatitis patients, cholangitis was **1.0% vs 0.2% (odds ratio 4.98)**.
  - ⚠ **Give periprocedural antibiotics.** One retrospective study found cholangitis in **12% without prophylaxis vs 1% with**; the evidence is conflicting, but cholangioscopy patients are considered high risk and cholangioscopy is one of the three scenarios the published literature most strongly supports for prophylaxis at ERCP ([[acg-asge-2026-ercp-quality-indicators]]). Agents, timing and the full indication list: [[antibiotic-prophylaxis-endoscopy]].
- **Bleeding:** no compelling data showing more clinically significant bleeding than standard ERCP, despite the plausible mechanism of direct duct-wall trauma during lithotripsy. **Perforation** rates appear similar to conventional ERCP.
