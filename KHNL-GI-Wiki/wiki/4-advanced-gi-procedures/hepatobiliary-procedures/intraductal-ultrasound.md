## What It Is

- **Intraductal ultrasound (IDUS)** = high-frequency ultrasound probe advanced through the [[ercp|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]] scope **into the biliary tree**, giving a cross-sectional view of the stricture and the surrounding duct ([[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]]).
- **It images; it cannot sample.** Any tissue diagnosis still requires [[brush-cytology|brush cytology]], fluoroscopic-guided biopsy, or [[cholangioscopy]]-directed biopsy — IDUS earns its place by telling those tools *where* to sample ([[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]], [[esge-2021-ampullary-tumors]]).
- Distinct from [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]], which images the duct from outside it (stomach/duodenum). The two answer different questions and have opposite blind spots: EUS is strongest for **distal** strictures and for nodes ([[asge-2023-indeterminate-biliary-strictures]]); IDUS for **intraductal extent** and the duct wall itself.
- **Three-dimensional IDUS reconstruction** may add accuracy over standard IDUS and EUS ([[asge-2013-biliary-neoplasia]]).
- These guidelines do not specify probe caliber or frequency, and none describes the insertion technique beyond passing the probe through the duodenoscope into the duct.

## Where It Changes Management

### Indeterminate Biliary Stricture

*Full sampling algorithm: see [[biliary-stricture]].*

- IDUS increases the sensitivity of diagnosing malignant strictures compared with ERCP alone ([[asge-2023-indeterminate-biliary-strictures]]).
- **Not used alone** — combine with another diagnostic modality such as forceps biopsy ([[esge-2021-ampullary-tumors]]).

> [!pearl] IDUS tells you where to biopsy
> Its proposed role is not standalone diagnosis but **localizing the malignant-appearing region for targeted biopsy sampling** — the probe finds the abnormal segment, the forceps or brush go there ([[asge-2023-indeterminate-biliary-strictures]], [[esge-2021-ampullary-tumors]]).

### Hilar Cholangiocarcinoma

- IDUS at ERCP may add useful information in suspected [[cholangiocarcinoma|cholangiocarcinoma (CCA)]], and is **more accurate than ERCP-guided cytology and transpapillary biopsy** for identifying CCA ([[asge-2013-biliary-neoplasia]]).
- **More accurate than standard EUS for staging hilar CCA**; the reported staging accuracy in the table below is for IDUS combined with [[cholangioscopy]] ([[asge-2013-biliary-neoplasia]]).

### Ampullary Tumor

*Full workup, resection and intraductal-extension criteria: see [[ampullary-adenoma]].*

- Assesses **depth of invasion relative to the muscularis propria**, **intraductal extension**, and periampullary nodal involvement — the findings that triage a patient to endoscopic papillectomy versus pancreaticoduodenectomy or surgical ampullectomy ([[asge-2013-biliary-neoplasia]]); both EUS and IDUS can identify malignancy and its extension beyond the muscularis propria ([[asge-2015-ampullary-duodenal-adenomas]]).
- **Selected patients only** — routine use is weighed against training, cost and pancreatitis risk ([[esge-2021-ampullary-tumors]]).
- The graded pre-resection ultrasound recommendation is for **EUS**, not IDUS: EUS for large lesions or those with features concerning for malignancy. Lesions <1 cm without obvious signs of malignancy (ulceration, induration, bleeding) may not require ultrasound evaluation at all before endoscopic removal ([[asge-2015-ampullary-duodenal-adenomas]]).
- Read across the whole literature, IDUS yields are **slightly higher than or comparable to EUS** ([[esge-2021-ampullary-tumors]]) — a narrower margin than the single-study gaps in the table below.

## Malignant Criteria

IDUS findings suggestive of malignancy in a biliary stricture ([[asge-2023-indeterminate-biliary-strictures]]):

- Intraluminal mass with an **irregular margin**
- **Wall thickness >9 mm**
- Heterogeneous lesion with an **uneven mucosal surface**
- **Eccentric wall thickening**
- **Destruction of the wall layers**
- Mass **invading surrounding tissue**

ASGE 2023 does not grade or weight these features, require a minimum number of them, or illustrate them. For ampullary tumors specifically, ESGE 2021 states the morphological criteria predicting malignancy are unclear.

## Limitations and Adverse Events

- **Cannot acquire tissue** — never a substitute for sampling ([[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]]).
- **Post-ERCP pancreatitis is the stated harm**, and the reason routine use must be balanced against training and cost ([[esge-2021-ampullary-tumors]]). No adverse-event rate for IDUS is reported in these guidelines.
- ⚠ **The two 2023 guidelines disagree on availability, and it is the practical determinant of whether you can use it.** ASGE 2023 calls IDUS "more widely available now" and on that basis suggests it could be considered for targeted sampling; ACG 2023 describes **declining availability**. Neither recommends it, so the disagreement does not change a recommendation — but expect the probe not to be on the shelf.

> [!pitfall] Never stage on IDUS alone
> It **may overestimate tumor stage** in ampullary neoplasms; magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) beats EUS for nodal staging, and CT and positron emission tomography (PET) detect small metastases that neither EUS nor IDUS sees ([[asge-2015-ampullary-duodenal-adenomas]]). IDUS is a local-extent tool, not a staging workup on its own.

## Guideline Position

- **ESGE 2021** (ampullary tumors) carries the only graded statement on IDUS, unnumbered in the document: *"ESGE suggests that intraductal ultrasound (IDUS) can be useful in selected patients with ampullary tumors; however, routine use must be balanced against training, costs, and risk of pancreatitis."* — **Weak recommendation, low quality evidence**.
- **ASGE 2023** (biliary strictures of undetermined etiology) reviewed IDUS under "other considerations" and **made no recommendation** — utility needs further study before any statement on widespread adoption.
- **ACG 2023** names it an adjunct only; the inability to acquire tissue and its declining availability limit its impact in practice.
- **ASGE 2013** and **ASGE 2015** make no graded recommendation naming IDUS.
