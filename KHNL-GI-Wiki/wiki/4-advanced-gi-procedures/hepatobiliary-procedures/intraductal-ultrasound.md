## What It Is

- High-frequency ultrasound probe passed at the time of [[ercp|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]] **into the bile duct itself**, imaging the duct wall and periductal tissue from the lumen.
- **It images; it cannot sample.** Any tissue diagnosis still requires [[brush-cytology|brush cytology]], fluoroscopic-guided biopsy, or [[cholangioscopy]]-directed biopsy — intraductal ultrasound (IDUS) earns its place by telling those tools *where* to sample ([[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]]).
- Distinct from [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]], which images the duct from outside it (stomach/duodenum). The two answer different questions and have opposite blind spots: EUS is strongest for **distal** strictures and for nodes; IDUS for **intraductal extent** and the duct wall itself.
- **Three-dimensional IDUS reconstruction** may add accuracy over standard IDUS and EUS ([[asge-2013-biliary-neoplasia]]).

## Where It Changes Management

### Indeterminate Biliary Stricture

*Full sampling algorithm: see [[biliary-stricture]].*

- IDUS increases the sensitivity of diagnosing malignant strictures compared with ERCP alone.
- Its proposed role is **not** standalone diagnosis but **localizing the malignant-appearing region for targeted biopsy sampling** — the probe finds the abnormal segment, the forceps go there ([[asge-2023-indeterminate-biliary-strictures]]).

### Hilar Cholangiocarcinoma Staging

- IDUS at ERCP may add useful information in suspected [[cholangiocarcinoma|cholangiocarcinoma (CCA)]], and is **more accurate than ERCP-guided cytology and transpapillary biopsy** for identifying CCA.
- **More accurate than standard EUS for staging hilar CCA**; combined with [[cholangioscopy]], accuracy for staging hilar lesions is **95%–100%** ([[asge-2013-biliary-neoplasia]]).

### Ampullary Adenoma — Staging Before Resection

*Full workup and resection criteria: see [[ampullary-adenoma]].*

- EUS and IDUS both assess **depth of invasion relative to the muscularis propria**, **intraductal extension**, and periampullary nodal involvement — the findings that triage a patient to endoscopic papillectomy versus pancreaticoduodenectomy or surgical ampullectomy.
- Reserved for **large lesions or features concerning for malignancy**; lesions <1 cm without obvious malignant signs (ulceration, induration, bleeding) may not need ultrasound staging before endoscopic removal.
- **<1 cm of extension into the common bile duct or pancreatic duct does not by itself preclude endoscopic therapy** ([[asge-2015-ampullary-duodenal-adenomas]]).

## Malignant Criteria

IDUS findings suggestive of malignancy ([[asge-2023-indeterminate-biliary-strictures]]):

| Finding |
|---|
| Intraluminal mass with an **irregular margin** |
| **Wall thickness >9 mm** |
| Heterogeneous lesion with an **uneven mucosal surface** |
| **Eccentric wall thickening** |
| **Destruction of the wall layers** |
| Mass **invading surrounding tissue** |

> No ingested source grades or weights these features, gives a required number of them, or provides the endoscopic/ultrasonographic images illustrating each.

## Diagnostic Performance

| Setting | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Indeterminate stricture, targeted sampling | IDUS-guided transpapillary biopsy **90.8%** vs transpapillary biopsy alone **76.9%** diagnostic accuracy (*P* = .028) | [[asge-2023-indeterminate-biliary-strictures]] |
| Indeterminate stricture, overall | Accuracy **up to 92%** | [[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]] |
| Hilar CCA staging | **95%–100%** when combined with cholangioscopy | [[asge-2013-biliary-neoplasia]] |
| Ampullary neoplasm, tumor visualization | IDUS **100%** vs EUS **59%** vs computed tomography (CT) **30%** | [[asge-2015-ampullary-duodenal-adenomas]] |
| Ampullary neoplasm, tumor diagnosis | Overall accuracy IDUS **88.9%** vs EUS **56.3%** (*P* = .05) | [[asge-2015-ampullary-duodenal-adenomas]] |

## Limitations and Why It Is Not Recommended

- **No society recommendation exists either way.** ASGE 2023 reviewed IDUS under "other considerations" and deliberately **made no recommendation** — utility needs further study before any statement on widespread adoption ([[asge-2023-indeterminate-biliary-strictures]]).
- **Cannot acquire tissue** — never a substitute for sampling ([[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]]).
- **May overestimate tumor stage** in ampullary neoplasms ([[asge-2015-ampullary-duodenal-adenomas]]).
- CT and positron emission tomography (PET) detect small metastases that neither EUS nor IDUS sees — IDUS is a local-extent tool, not a staging workup on its own.
- ⚠ **The two 2023 guidelines disagree on availability, and it is the practical determinant of whether you can use it.** ASGE 2023 calls IDUS "more widely available now" and on that basis suggests it could be considered for targeted sampling; ACG 2023 describes **declining availability**. Neither recommends it, so the disagreement does not change a recommendation — but expect the probe not to be on the shelf.
