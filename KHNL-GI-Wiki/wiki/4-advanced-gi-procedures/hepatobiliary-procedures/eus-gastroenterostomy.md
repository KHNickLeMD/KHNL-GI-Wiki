**Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)-guided gastroenterostomy (EUS-GE)** creates a stapler-free anastomosis between the stomach and a distended loop of small bowel using an electrocautery-enhanced **lumen-apposing metal stent (LAMS)**, bypassing an obstruction without surgery. *Which* patient gets it rather than a duodenal stent or a surgical bypass is decided on [[gastric-outlet-obstruction]]; this page is *how it is done and what goes wrong*.

## Indications

### Malignant gastric outlet obstruction

The two comparative recommendations of [[i-eus-2026-eus-gastroenterostomy|the i-EUS international consensus (2026)]], both for **palliating malignant gastric outlet obstruction (GOO)**:

| Comparison | Direction | Why | Strength / certainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| vs **duodenal (enteral) stenting** | **EUS-GE preferred** | Superior efficacy, comparable safety | Statement 1.3: **Strong**, low quality (98% agreement) |
| vs **surgical bypass** | **EUS-GE suggested** | Comparable efficacy, **lower incidence of adverse events** | Statement 1.4: Conditional, low quality (98%) |

> [!pearl] The durability argument, not the success rate, is what favours EUS-GE over a duodenal stent
> Clinical success differs modestly (risk ratio [RR] 1.10, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.05–1.15). **Reintervention is where the gap is: RR 0.18 (0.11–0.29)**, and 4% vs 29% at 6 months in the first randomized trial. The anastomosis sits **remote from the tumour**, so it is not threatened by ingrowth or overgrowth ([[i-eus-2026-eus-gastroenterostomy]]).

- **Overall adverse events are not increased** vs enteral stenting (RR 0.58, 0.34–1.00), but complications, when they occur, **may be more severe**.
- Against surgery in the **malignant-only** subgroup: clinical success comparable (odds ratio [OR] 1.14, 0.63–2.09), **total adverse events markedly lower (OR 0.25, 0.13–0.46)**; no clear difference in severe adverse events or reintervention.
- **Statement 1.4 rests on observational studies only** (search to 30 April 2025). The two multicentre randomized trials against surgery — **ENDURO** ([[kastelijn-2023-enduro-protocol|protocol]]) and Bang et al. — came later, were not pooled or graded, and were consistent with its direction (ENDURO: faster resumption of solid intake, noninferior for reintervention) ([[i-eus-2026-eus-gastroenterostomy]]).
- **Planned curative resection is not a bar.** EUS-GE may be considered in candidates for potentially curative resection, after careful multidisciplinary evaluation (Statement 1.7, 89%) — e.g. temporary palliation as a bridge to surgery during neoadjuvant chemotherapy.
- **US guideline status:** the [[asge-2021-gastroduodenal-obstruction|American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2021]] guideline discussed EUS-GE outside its formal questions and made no recommendation on it, calling for randomized trials against both surgical gastrojejunostomy and enteral stents; LAMS use to create the gastroenterostomy was **off-label** at the time of drafting.

### Benign and expanded indications

All conditional, all requiring multidisciplinary discussion ([[i-eus-2026-eus-gastroenterostomy]]). **EDGE** = EUS-directed transgastric [[ercp|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]]; **EDGI** = EUS-directed transgastric intervention (non-ERCP procedures through the same gastrogastrostomy); **EDEE** = EUS-directed transenteric ERCP.

| Indication | Statement | Strength / certainty |
|---|---|---|
| **Benign GOO**, selected patients | Treatment option after multidisciplinary discussion | 4.1: Conditional, very low (93%) |
| **[[bariatric-surgery\|Roux-en-Y gastric bypass (RYGB)]]** needing endoscopic intervention | **EDGE or EDGI suggested** over alternative approaches | 4.2a: Conditional, low (95%) |
| **Afferent limb syndrome** with underlying malignancy | EUS-guided anastomosis using a LAMS | 4.4: Conditional, low (98%) |

- In **RYGB**, EDGE matches laparoscopy-assisted ERCP and beats [[device-assisted-enteroscopy|enteroscopy-assisted]] ERCP: technical success **96% and 93% vs 77%**, ERCP completion **93% and 92% vs 64%** (both P<0.01), with no significant difference in overall adverse events (19%, 20% and 13%; P=0.49). **Laparoscopy-assisted ERCP remains preferable in selected cases when a concomitant cholecystectomy is required.**
- Benign GOO is most often from [[peptic-ulcer-disease|peptic ulcer disease]] or [[chronic-pancreatitis|chronic pancreatitis]]; balloon dilation is still first line, but recurs and needs repeat sessions.

### Contraindications and the questions left open

- **Relative contraindications** ([[asge-2021-gastroduodenal-obstruction|ASGE 2021]]): tumour or blood vessels — including perigastric varices — involving the intended transgastric access site; large-volume [[ascites]]; peritoneal carcinomatosis; bowel obstruction distal to the intended gastroenterostomy site.
- **Ascites — i-EUS 2026:** **not a technical contraindication**, though it increases procedural complexity. **Preprocedural drainage may be considered for moderate or severe ascites** (Statement 1.5, 87% agreement). The grades are pragmatic, not validated: **mild** = small-volume, asymptomatic, usually found intraprocedurally; **moderate** = clinically relevant or drainable; **severe** = tense, recurrent or extensive, requiring drainage before any attempt.
  - Ascites may also be a marker of more advanced disease — carcinomatosis, portal vein thrombosis — associated with poorer survival and more recurrent obstruction.

Five questions fell below the consensus threshold (at least 85% agreement) and are **not a recommendation either way** ([[i-eus-2026-eus-gastroenterostomy]]):

| Question | Agreement | Panel's practical note |
|---|---|---|
| Is **peritoneal carcinomatosis** a contraindication? | 80% | Case by case after multidisciplinary discussion — weigh disease burden, expected survival and especially **obstruction at multiple levels**, which can defeat a technically successful anastomosis |
| Is **orotracheal intubation** required? | 67% | Reflects differing anaesthetic practice, not conflicting data; choose airway protection from aspiration risk, cardiorespiratory status and expected complexity |
| Is **[[antibiotic-prophylaxis-endoscopy\|antibiotic prophylaxis]]** required? | 80% | Many centres give a single prophylactic broad-spectrum dose — complex procedures, higher infection risk, ascites; individualize |
| **EDEE** in non-RYGB surgically altered anatomy | 82% | Highly selected cases only, after multidisciplinary evaluation |
| **EUS-guided coloenterostomy** for small- or large-bowel obstruction | 82% | Exceptional cases only, after multidisciplinary discussion |

## Setting and Training

- Perform **in centres with a multidisciplinary team where acute adverse events can be readily managed** (Statement 1.1, 93%) — the procedure can require luminal stent placement and endoscopic closure of a gastrointestinal wall defect at short notice.
- **Perform in a fluoroscopy-equipped room** (Statement 2.5, 96%). Every published series did; fluoroscopy aids prompt recognition and management of misdeployment and guidewire problems.
- Operators should already have **experience in EUS-guided LAMS placement and in managing its adverse events**, then undergo **a period of supervised training** in EUS-GE (Statement 1.2, 85%).
- **Learning curve, not a credentialing number:** adverse events and misdeployments cluster early and fall after approximately 40 procedures; the ~25–40 figure is an estimate of proficiency development. The panel judged the evidence **insufficient for any universal credentialing threshold or minimum institutional case volume** ([[i-eus-2026-eus-gastroenterostomy]]).

## Technique

### Distending the target loop

Adequate distension of the target jejunal loop is the prerequisite for the whole procedure.

> [!howto] Standardized distension (Statement 2.1a, 100% agreement)
> 1. Advance an **enteric catheter** — nasojejunal feeding tube or nasobiliary catheter — across the stricture and **beyond the ligament of Treitz**.
> 2. Infuse **saline** continuously or intermittently.
> 3. Stop when a **stable fluid-filled loop with adequate luminal apposition** is seen on EUS.
> 4. **Visualize the catheter inside the jejunal lumen on EUS** before puncturing — may help identify the right loop and reduce the risk of **colonic mispuncture**.

- **Saline, not water** — minimizes electrolyte disturbance, notably hyponatraemia. **Dye (e.g. methylene blue)** may be added to confirm intraluminal deployment of the proximal flange endoscopically; **radiopaque contrast** to delineate small-bowel anatomy fluoroscopically (Statement 2.1b, 89%). No fluid composition has shown clinically meaningful superiority.
- **Antiperistaltic agents** — intravenous butylscopolamine or glucagon — may be used to reduce bowel motility.
- **Direct needle puncture with saline instillation is the fallback, not the default** — reserve it for when a catheter or endoscope cannot be advanced past the obstruction, such as a tight or serrated stricture.

### Access and stent deployment

- **Freehand (wireless) placement of the LAMS is recommended** (Statement 2.2, 98%): puncture the distended loop directly with the electrocautery-enhanced LAMS, **without a guidewire**. Fewer device exchanges, shorter procedure time, better target-loop stability. Formalized as the **Wireless EUS-GE Simplified Technique (WEST)**.
- **Avoid routine over-the-wire access** (19-gauge needle puncture, guidewire into the jejunum, LAMS delivered over the wire) — guidewire manipulation can **displace the jejunal loop** during advancement of the delivery system, degrading EUS visualization. Three retrospective comparisons suggest lower technical success and more adverse events with over-the-wire access, with **stent misdeployment** the main cause of its complications.
- **Two operators, deep sedation:** in the ENDURO trial, EUS-GE was performed preferably by **two advanced endoscopists** under **[[endoscopy-sedation|deep sedation]] with propofol**, using a 20 × 10-mm electrocautery-enhanced LAMS ([[kastelijn-2023-enduro-protocol]]).

Step sequence as protocolized in ENDURO ([[kastelijn-2023-enduro-protocol]]):

1. Gastroscope proximal to the stenosis; remove residual gastric contents; place a feeding tube or nasobiliary drain distal to the stenosis through the working channel. Gastric residue too great for safe sedation → reschedule after extensive gastric decompression.
2. Exchange for a linear-array echoendoscope; identify the post-stenotic loop by flushing saline mixed with a small amount of indigo carmine. Fluoroscopy optional to check the planned puncture site.
3. Puncture the loop directly with the electrocautery-enhanced delivery system.
4. Deploy the distal flange and pull it against the small-bowel wall; deploy the proximal flange, pushing it out of the working channel during gentle echoendoscope retraction.
5. Confirm position by **backflow of indigo-coloured saline into the stomach**; let the stent expand naturally, with no balloon dilation; remove all tubes.

> [!pitfall] Do not balloon-dilate the LAMS at the end of the case
> **Routine intraprocedural dilation is not recommended** (Statement 2.4, 100% agreement). Spontaneous gradual expansion is sufficient, outcomes without dilation are excellent, and dilation has been associated with **stent dislodgement and perforation**. No study has compared routine dilation against none ([[i-eus-2026-eus-gastroenterostomy]]).

### Stent choice

- Both **15-mm and 20-mm** diameter LAMS are safe and effective. A **20-mm LAMS** was suggested for its potential clinical advantages — better dietary tolerance and less reintervention (Statement 2.3; conditional, very low quality; 88%):
  - Multicentre retrospective comparison: comparable technical and clinical success; more 20-mm patients tolerated a soft-solid or regular diet (Gastric Outlet Obstruction Scoring System [GOOSS] ≥2); reintervention 4.2% (20 mm) vs 8.1% (15 mm), not significant.
  - 2023 meta-analysis: no difference in technical success, clinical success or overall adverse events; reintervention 10.3% (15 mm) vs 3.5% (20 mm), pooled OR 3.59 for the 15-mm stent (95% CI 1.40–9.18; P=0.008).

> [!pitfall] The 20-mm recommendation is overtaken by a device recall — read the footnote
> Statement 2.3 was voted **before** recall/removal notices affecting selected **20 × 10-mm AXIOS / Hot AXIOS configurations and batches**, issued for deployment and expansion problems during stent delivery. The document states that the notices do **not** show a class-wide disadvantage of the 20-mm diameter, that **affected devices must not be used**, and that **the statement must not be read as making the 20-mm LAMS a currently preferred standard**. Select by regulatory notice, device availability, anatomy, indication and operator experience ([[i-eus-2026-eus-gastroenterostomy]]).

## Periprocedural Care

- **Antithrombotics:** temporarily withdraw [[anticoagulation-gi-bleeding|anticoagulants]] and **shift dual antiplatelet therapy to aspirin monotherapy** before the procedure (Statement 3.2, 98%) — interrupt oral anticoagulants based on individual thrombotic risk; de-escalate dual antiplatelet therapy when feasible. Therapeutic EUS is classed a **high-risk intervention for bleeding**; GOO is usually an elective setting, which allows time to optimize. Bleeding occurs in fewer than 5%, usually mild-to-moderate and amenable to standard endoscopic hemostasis.
- **Refeeding:** a **same-day liquid diet may be considered**, advancing to soft or solid as tolerated (Statement 3.3, 92%); published series advanced to soft or solid diet as early as day 2. Structured nutritional assessment and individualized dietary counselling are recommended, particularly in malignant GOO.
- **No routine post-procedural imaging** to check LAMS position before resuming oral intake (Statement 3.4, 98%) — position is already confirmed intraprocedurally by endoscopy and fluoroscopy, and by **blue-dyed fluid draining into the stomach** when dye was used. Image only if clinical signs or symptoms suggest a complication.
- **Early re-endoscopy:** the ENDURO trial barred re-endoscopy through the LAMS within the first 6 weeks after placement, as the fistula tract may not be fully mature ([[kastelijn-2023-enduro-protocol]]).

## Adverse Events

Pooled across 36 studies and 1846 patients undergoing EUS-GE for GOO (overall success rates under [[#Outcomes]]) ([[i-eus-2026-eus-gastroenterostomy]]):

| Adverse event | Rate |
|---|---|
| Any adverse event | **13.0%** |
| **LAMS misdeployment** | **4.6%** (the majority of adverse events) |
| Serious adverse events | **1.2%** |
| Procedure-related mortality | **0.3%** |

### LAMS misdeployment

The **main cause of technical failure and of adverse events**. Management is tailored to **misdeployment subtype and the timing of recognition**, with surgery considered for bowel injury (Statement 3.6, 93%). The Ghandour classification, from a multicentre cohort of 467 procedures, sorts events by **flange position and whether an enterotomy was made**:

| Type | What happened | Management |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Distal flange in the peritoneum, **no bowel injury**. **Most frequent.** | Usually endoscopic — LAMS removal, redo-anastomosis, or rescue enteral stenting, **with closure of the gastric defect** to prevent leakage. |
| **II** | Distal flange released into the peritoneum **despite successful bowel puncture**. | Stent-in-stent or natural orifice transluminal endoscopic surgery (NOTES)-based rescue feasible in selected cases; **urgent surgery needed in up to 7%**. |
| **III** | **Proximal** flange deployed in the peritoneum. | Prevented by intrachannel release; **most often requires surgery**. |
| **IV** | **Gastrocolonic anastomosis** — stomach to colon. ~**4%** of misdeployments. | Prevented by careful loop identification and controlled fluid instillation. Managed **deliberately late**: allow the fistula tract to mature, then endoscopic LAMS removal and defect closure, often with over-the-scope clips. |

> [!pitfall] Misdeployment: antibiotics immediately, diagnosis early
> **Start broad-spectrum systemic antibiotics as soon as misdeployment is recognized.** Early recognition with prompt endoscopist–surgeon–radiologist evaluation is critical — **delayed diagnosis is associated with significantly worse outcomes** ([[i-eus-2026-eus-gastroenterostomy]]).

### Recurrent obstruction

- **Manage recurrent GOO endoscopically first when the LAMS is dysfunctional** (Statement 3.5, 91%). Reintervention for stent dysfunction is uncommon — 0% to 3% across large series.
- **Most recurrent symptoms are not stent occlusion.** In one series only 2 of 8 reinterventions were for occlusion (managed by food-impaction removal or a coaxial tubular stent); the rest were **[[gastroparesis]] or downstream obstruction**. In another, recurrent obstruction in 16% was **entirely downstream [[small-bowel-obstruction|small-bowel obstruction]] from carcinomatosis**, not stent failure.
- So: **look for a second level of obstruction and for impaired gastric motility before blaming the anastomosis.**

## Outcomes

| Setting | Technical success | Clinical success | Adverse events |
|---|---|---|---|
| **GOO** (36 studies, 1846 patients) | 96.9% | 90.6% | breakdown under [[#Adverse Events]] |
| **Benign GOO** (376 patients, 15 studies) | 95.8% | 93.4% | 11.6%; **recurrence 11.6%** at median 14 mo |
| **EDGE** in RYGB | 96% (92–97.6) | 91% (85–95) | ~17% (14–24) |
| **EDGI** (non-ERCP interventions) | 98% (97–100) | 99% (99–100) | ~10% (6–13) |
| **Afferent limb syndrome** | 96% (93–100) | 95% (91–99) | 7% (3–11) |

- The most frequent adverse event of **EDGE is LAMS dislodgement**; available evidence suggests the risk may be reduced by larger-diameter (20 mm) stents and by **stent fixation techniques**.
- **Long-term benign-GOO data are limited** — no prospective or randomized studies, and the timing and indications for LAMS revision or removal (scheduled or on demand) vary between centres.
