**Endoscopic ultrasound-guided gastroenterostomy (EUS-GE)** creates a stapler-free anastomosis between the stomach and a distended loop of small bowel using an electrocautery-enhanced **lumen-apposing metal stent (LAMS)**, bypassing an obstruction without surgery. *Which* patient gets it rather than a duodenal stent or a surgical bypass is decided on [[gastric-outlet-obstruction]]; this page is *how it is done and what goes wrong*.

## Indications

### Malignant gastric outlet obstruction

The two comparative recommendations of [[i-eus-2026-eus-gastroenterostomy|the i-EUS international consensus (2026)]], both for **palliating malignant gastric outlet obstruction (GOO)**:

| Comparison | Direction | Why | Strength / certainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| vs **duodenal (enteral) stenting** | **EUS-GE preferred** | Superior efficacy, comparable safety | **Strong**, low quality (98% agreement) |
| vs **surgical bypass** | **EUS-GE suggested** | Comparable efficacy, **lower incidence of adverse events** | Conditional, low quality (98%) |

> [!pearl] The durability argument, not the success rate, is what favours EUS-GE over a duodenal stent
> Clinical success differs modestly (RR 1.10, 95% CI 1.05–1.15). **Reintervention is where the gap is: RR 0.18 (0.11–0.29)**, and 4% vs 29% at 6 months in the first randomized trial. The anastomosis sits **remote from the tumour**, so it is not threatened by ingrowth or overgrowth ([[i-eus-2026-eus-gastroenterostomy]]).

- **Overall adverse events are not increased** vs enteral stenting (RR 0.58, 0.34–1.00), but complications, when they occur, **may be more severe**.
- Against surgery in the **malignant-only** subgroup: clinical success comparable (OR 1.14, 0.63–2.09), **total adverse events markedly lower (OR 0.25, 0.13–0.46)**; no clear difference in severe adverse events or reintervention.
- **Planned curative resection is not a bar.** EUS-GE may be considered in patients who are candidates for potentially curative surgical resection, after careful multidisciplinary evaluation (89% agreement) — relevant now that neoadjuvant chemotherapy creates patients needing temporary palliation as a bridge to surgery.

### Benign and expanded indications

All conditional, all requiring multidisciplinary discussion ([[i-eus-2026-eus-gastroenterostomy]]):

| Indication | Statement | Strength / certainty |
|---|---|---|
| **Benign GOO**, selected patients | Treatment option after multidisciplinary discussion | Conditional, very low (93%) |
| **Roux-en-Y gastric bypass** needing endoscopic intervention | **EDGE or EDGI preferred** over alternative approaches | Conditional, low (95%) |
| **Afferent limb syndrome** with underlying malignancy | EUS-guided anastomosis using a LAMS | Conditional, low (98%) |

- **EDGE** = EUS-directed transgastric [[ercp|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]]; **EDGI** = EUS-directed transgastric intervention (non-ERCP procedures through the same gastrogastrostomy); **EDEE** = EUS-directed transenteric ERCP.
- In **Roux-en-Y gastric bypass (RYGB)**, EDGE matches laparoscopy-assisted ERCP and beats enteroscopy-assisted ERCP: technical success **96% and 93% vs 77%**, ERCP completion **93% and 92% vs 64%** (both P<0.01), with no significant difference in overall adverse events. **Laparoscopy-assisted ERCP remains preferable when a concomitant cholecystectomy is needed.**
- Benign GOO is most often from [[peptic-ulcer-disease|peptic ulcer disease]] or [[chronic-pancreatitis|chronic pancreatitis]]; balloon dilation is still first line, but recurs and needs repeat sessions.

### Contraindications and the questions left open

- **Ascites is not a technical contraindication**, though it increases procedural complexity. **Preprocedural drainage may be considered for moderate or severe ascites** (87% agreement). The grades are pragmatic, not validated: **mild** = small-volume, asymptomatic, usually found intraprocedurally; **moderate** = clinically relevant or drainable; **severe** = tense, recurrent or extensive, requiring drainage before any attempt.
  - Ascites may also be a marker of more advanced disease — carcinomatosis, portal vein thrombosis — which predicts poorer survival and more recurrent obstruction.

> [!pitfall] Five questions the consensus deliberately did not answer
> Each fell below the 85% threshold and is **not a recommendation either way** ([[i-eus-2026-eus-gastroenterostomy]]): whether **peritoneal carcinomatosis** is a contraindication (80%), whether **orotracheal intubation** is required (67% — the document's lowest, reflecting differing anaesthetic practice rather than conflicting data), whether **antibiotic prophylaxis** is required (80%), **EDEE in non-RYGB altered anatomy** (82%), and **EUS-guided coloenterostomy** for bowel obstruction (82%). Many centres do give a single prophylactic broad-spectrum dose for complex cases, higher infection risk or ascites — the panel simply declined to recommend it.

- With peritoneal carcinomatosis, decide case by case after multidisciplinary discussion, weighing disease burden, expected survival, and especially **the presence of obstruction at multiple levels** — downstream obstruction is what defeats a technically successful anastomosis.

## Setting and Training

- Perform **in centres with a multidisciplinary team where acute adverse events can be readily managed** (93%) — the procedure can require luminal stent placement and endoscopic closure of a gastrointestinal wall defect at short notice.
- **Perform in a fluoroscopy-equipped room** (96%). Every published series did; fluoroscopy is the back-up read for misdeployment and guidewire problems.
- Operators should already have **experience in EUS-guided LAMS placement and in managing its adverse events**, then undergo **a period of supervised training** in EUS-GE (85%).

> [!pitfall] ~40 procedures is a learning-curve estimate, not a credentialing threshold
> Adverse events and misdeployments cluster in the early phase and fall off after roughly 40 cases, but the panel judged the evidence **insufficient to set any universal case-volume or credentialing number** and framed the statement around prior experience and supervised training instead ([[i-eus-2026-eus-gastroenterostomy]]).

## Technique

### Distending the target loop

Adequate distension of the target jejunal loop is the prerequisite for the whole procedure.

> [!howto] Standardized distension (Statement 2.1a, 100% agreement)
> 1. Advance an **enteric catheter** — nasojejunal feeding tube or nasobiliary catheter — across the stricture and **beyond the ligament of Treitz**.
> 2. Infuse **saline** continuously or intermittently.
> 3. Stop when a **stable fluid-filled loop with adequate luminal apposition** is seen on EUS.
> 4. **Visualize the catheter inside the jejunal lumen on EUS** before puncturing — this confirms the right loop and is the guard against **colonic mispuncture**.

- **Saline, not water** — minimizes electrolyte disturbance, notably hyponatraemia. **Dye (e.g. methylene blue)** may be added to confirm intraluminal deployment of the proximal flange endoscopically; **radiopaque contrast** to delineate small-bowel anatomy fluoroscopically (89%). No fluid composition has shown clinically meaningful superiority.
- **Antiperistaltic agents** — intravenous hyoscine butylbromide or glucagon — may be used to quiet the bowel.
- **Direct needle puncture with saline instillation is the fallback, not the default** — reserve it for when a catheter or endoscope cannot be advanced past the obstruction, such as a tight or serrated stricture.

### Access and stent deployment

- **Freehand (wireless) placement of the LAMS is recommended** (98%): puncture the distended loop directly with the electrocautery-enhanced LAMS, **without a guidewire**. Fewer device exchanges, shorter procedure time, better target-loop stability. Formalized as the **Wireless EUS-GE Simplified Technique (WEST)**.
- **Avoid routine over-the-wire access** — guidewire manipulation can **displace the jejunal loop** during advancement of the delivery system, degrading EUS visualization. Across three retrospective comparisons the over-the-wire approach had lower technical success and more adverse events, driven specifically by **stent misdeployment**.
- **Release the proximal flange intrachannel** — this is what prevents Type III misdeployment.

> [!pitfall] Do not balloon-dilate the LAMS at the end of the case
> **Routine intraprocedural dilation is not recommended (100% agreement)** — the strongest consensus in the document. Spontaneous gradual expansion is sufficient, outcomes without dilation are excellent, and dilation has been associated with **stent dislodgement and perforation**. No study has compared routine dilation against none ([[i-eus-2026-eus-gastroenterostomy]]).

### Stent choice

- A **20-mm diameter LAMS** was suggested for its potential clinical advantages — better dietary tolerance and less reintervention (reintervention 4.2% vs 8.1% vs 15-mm in one series; pooled OR 3.59, 95% CI 1.40–9.18, P=0.008 favouring 20 mm). Conditional, very low quality (88%).

> [!pitfall] The 20-mm recommendation is overtaken by a device recall — read the footnote
> Statement 2.3 was voted **before** recall/removal notices affecting selected **20 × 10-mm AXIOS / Hot AXIOS configurations and batches**, issued for deployment and expansion problems during stent delivery. The document states that the notices do **not** show a class-wide disadvantage of the 20-mm diameter, that **affected devices must not be used**, and that **the statement must not be read as making the 20-mm LAMS a currently preferred standard**. Select by regulatory notice, device availability, anatomy, indication and operator experience ([[i-eus-2026-eus-gastroenterostomy]]).

## Periprocedural Care

- **Antithrombotics:** temporarily withdraw anticoagulants and **de-escalate dual antiplatelet therapy to aspirin monotherapy** before the procedure (98%). EUS-GE is classified a **high-risk intervention for bleeding**; GOO is usually an elective setting, which allows time to optimize. Bleeding occurs in **<5%**, usually mild-to-moderate and amenable to standard hemostasis.
- **Refeeding:** a **same-day liquid diet may be considered**, advancing to soft or solid as tolerated (92%). Structured nutritional assessment and individualized dietary counselling matter in malignant GOO, where malnutrition is the rule.
- **No routine post-procedural imaging** to check LAMS position before resuming oral intake (98%) — position is already confirmed intraprocedurally by endoscopy and fluoroscopy, and by **blue-dyed fluid draining into the stomach** when dye was used. Image only if clinical signs or symptoms suggest a problem.
- **Do not re-endoscope through the LAMS within the first 6 weeks** — the fistula tract may not be mature ([[kastelijn-2023-enduro-protocol]]).

## Adverse Events

Pooled across 36 studies and 1846 EUS-GE procedures for GOO ([[i-eus-2026-eus-gastroenterostomy]]):

| Outcome | Rate |
|---|---|
| Technical success | **96.9%** |
| Clinical success | **90.6%** |
| Overall adverse events | **13.0%** |
| **LAMS misdeployment** | **4.6%** (the majority of adverse events) |
| Severe adverse events | **1.2%** |
| Procedure-related mortality | **0.3%** |

### LAMS misdeployment

The **main cause of technical failure and of adverse events**. Management is tailored to **misdeployment subtype and the timing of recognition**, with surgery considered for bowel injury (Statement 3.6, 93%). The Ghandour classification, from a multicentre cohort of 467 procedures, sorts events by **flange position and whether an enterotomy was made**:

| Type | What happened | Management |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Distal flange in the peritoneum, **no bowel injury**. **Most frequent.** | Usually endoscopic — LAMS removal, redo-anastomosis, or rescue enteral stenting, **with closure of the gastric defect** to prevent leakage. |
| **II** | Distal flange released into the peritoneum **despite successful bowel puncture**. | Stent-in-stent or NOTES-based rescue feasible in selected cases; **urgent surgery needed in up to 7%**. |
| **III** | **Proximal** flange deployed in the peritoneum. | Prevented by intrachannel release; **most often requires surgery**. |
| **IV** | **Gastrocolonic anastomosis** — stomach to colon. ~**4%** of misdeployments. | Prevented by careful loop identification and controlled fluid instillation. Managed **deliberately late**: allow the fistula tract to mature, then endoscopic LAMS removal and defect closure, often with over-the-scope clips. |

> [!pitfall] Misdeployment: antibiotics immediately, diagnosis early
> **Start broad-spectrum systemic antibiotics as soon as misdeployment is recognized.** Early recognition with prompt endoscopist–surgeon–radiologist evaluation is critical — **delayed diagnosis is associated with significantly worse outcomes** ([[i-eus-2026-eus-gastroenterostomy]]).

### Recurrent obstruction

- **Manage recurrent GOO endoscopically first when the LAMS is dysfunctional** (91%). Stent dysfunction is uncommon — 0% to 3% across large series.
- **Most recurrent symptoms are not stent occlusion.** In one series only 2 of 8 reinterventions were for occlusion (managed by food-impaction removal or a coaxial tubular stent); the rest were **gastroparesis or downstream obstruction**. In another, recurrent obstruction in 16% was **entirely downstream small-bowel obstruction from carcinomatosis**, not stent failure.
- So: **look for a second level of obstruction and for impaired gastric motility before blaming the anastomosis.**

## Outcomes

| Setting | Technical success | Clinical success | Adverse events |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Malignant GOO** (overall EUS-GE) | 96.9% | 90.6% | 13.0% |
| **Benign GOO** (376 patients, 15 studies) | 95.8% | 93.4% | 11.6%; **recurrence 11.6%** at median 14 mo |
| **EDGE** in RYGB | 96% (92–97.6) | 91% (85–95) | ~17% (14–24) |
| **EDGI** (non-ERCP interventions) | 98% (97–100) | 99% (99–100) | ~10% (6–13) |
| **Afferent limb syndrome** | 96% (93–100) | 95% (91–99) | 7% (3–11) |

- The most frequent adverse event of **EDGE is LAMS dislodgement**; risk is reduced by larger-diameter (20 mm) stents and by **stent fixation techniques**.
- **Long-term benign-GOO data are thin** — the timing and indications for LAMS revision or removal vary widely between centres, and durability beyond ~14 months is largely unknown.

> [!pearl] The strong recommendation rests on evidence that excludes the randomized trials
> The pooled analyses are **entirely observational**: literature was searched to **30 April 2025**, and the two multicentre randomized trials — **ENDURO** ([[kastelijn-2023-enduro-protocol]]) and the trial by Bang et al. — were published after that cutoff, so they were **not pooled and not GRADE-assessed**. The panel reports both as consistent with its direction but did not let them change the wording, strength or certainty ([[i-eus-2026-eus-gastroenterostomy]]).
