**Brush cytology** is transpapillary sampling of a [[biliary-stricture|biliary stricture]] during [[ercp|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]]. The American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) calls it the "traditional cornerstone" of initial stricture sampling because of its relative technical ease and widespread availability, including standard cytopathological handling and interpretation ([[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]]). Its sensitivity is low, so it is paired with other sampling modalities, and it is not the first test when a pancreatic mass is present.

## When Brushing Is — and Is Not — the Right Test

The indication depends on **stricture location and whether a mass is present** ([[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]]). Full location-by-location workup: [[biliary-stricture]].

| Setting | Where brushing sits |
|---|---|
| **Extrahepatic stricture due to an apparent or suspected pancreatic mass** | **Not first-line:** [[endoscopic-ultrasound\|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]] with fine-needle sampling (aspiration or biopsy; FNA/B) is recommended over ERCP (Rec 1; strong, moderate-quality evidence). In patients undergoing combined EUS and ERCP (for concurrent decompression), brushing is indicated when FNA/B is nondiagnostic or real-time cytological evaluation is not available; the combination appears to have the greatest yield |
| **Extrahepatic stricture without an associated mass** | Same approach as a suspected malignant perihilar stricture |
| **Suspected malignant perihilar stricture** | **Multimodality sampling over brush cytology alone** at the index ERCP (Rec 3; strong, low-quality evidence) |

> [!pitfall] Brushing plus a plastic stent where EUS is unavailable
> In asymptomatic or minimally symptomatic patients with an extrahepatic stricture from an apparent or suspected pancreatic mass, ACG suggests single-session EUS and ERCP over ERCP alone (Key Concept 2, ungraded). Brush cytology yield is very low and most patients with a pancreatic mass are not resected without a tissue diagnosis, so refer or transfer to a center that can do both in one anesthesia session. Acute cholangitis or significant symptoms → expedient ERCP is justified ([[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]]).

- **Pre-test probability of malignancy is high:** ~**55%** in strictures without an obvious mass on cross-sectional imaging ([[asge-2023-indeterminate-biliary-strictures]]); **73%** in one large series of extrahepatic strictures referred for EUS-guided FNA, and **74%–87%** in 2 systematic reviews of suspected malignant strictures ([[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]]).

## Test Characteristics

American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) meta-analysis of 21 observational studies (2,726 patients) in strictures of undetermined etiology ([[asge-2023-indeterminate-biliary-strictures]]):

| Strategy | Sensitivity for malignancy | Miss rate |
|---|---|---|
| **Brush cytology alone** | **.4** (95% confidence interval [CI] .37–.43) | **58%** (95% CI 46–71) |
| Fluoroscopic-guided biopsy alone | **.52** (95% CI .49–.56); *P* = .006 vs brushing | **41%** (95% CI 31–52) |
| **Brushing + fluoroscopic-guided biopsy** | **.66** (95% CI .63–.69); *P* < .001 vs brushing | — |

- **Incremental yield** of adding intraductal biopsy to brushing: **20%** (95% CI 9–31).
- Subgroups (proximal vs distal strictures; biliary vs pancreatic masses): no difference in sensitivity of brushings vs biopsy sampling.
- Specimen adequacy higher with brush cytology, but this was an intention-to-treat analysis and likely reflects the **technical difficulty and failures of fluoroscopic-guided biopsy**, not specimen quality.
- No difference in technical success or **adverse events** (odds ratio .53; 95% CI .14–2.05); events were few (2 brushing vs 5 biopsy patients, >500 per group), but the 2 severe events (prolonged bleeding; perforation requiring surgical choledochotomy) were both in the biopsy group.

**Why reported sensitivity varies** ([[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]]):

| Evidence | Sensitivity for malignancy |
|---|---|
| 2013 meta-analysis, 1,556 patients (33% [[pancreatic-cancer\|pancreatic cancer]], 36% [[cholangiocarcinoma]]) | **41.6%** composite |
| Subsequent studies | clustering around **50%–60%** |
| Outliers | as low as **6%**, or ~**75%** (one of these studies used rapid on-site evaluation [ROSE] of the brush specimens) |
| 2015 meta-analysis, 9 studies / 730 indeterminate strictures | brushings **45%**, forceps biopsies **48%**, **both 59%** |

ACG: *"the performance characteristics of this diagnostic modality are inadequate to drive clinical decision making."*

## Interpreting the Result

- **The problem is false negatives.** In malignant perihilar strictures the primary diagnostic challenge is falsely negative results (low sensitivity); falsely positive samples (low specificity) are uncommon ([[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]]). Neither guideline gives a numeric specificity for biliary brush cytology.
- **A negative brushing does not exclude malignancy:** brush cytology alone misses **58%** of malignancies ([[asge-2023-indeterminate-biliary-strictures]]).
- A stricture still undiagnosed despite initial ERCP with intraductal sampling is **indeterminate**; further options are deployed selectively by clinical context, stricture characteristics and resource availability (ACG Key Concept 4, ungraded) → see [[biliary-stricture]].

## Adding Other Sampling Modalities

- **ACG Rec 3** (perihilar; principles also apply to extrahepatic strictures without a mass):
  - Use **≥2 diagnostic sampling modalities** at ERCP-based evaluation.
  - Rationale exists for **3–4 modalities** at the initial evaluation if they can be done safely; trimodality sampling is supported by retrospective data.
  - The safety of adding a modality and the low yield of brushing justified a strong recommendation despite low-quality evidence.
  - Options that appear to add value to brushing: fluoroscopy-directed forceps biopsy, [[cholangioscopy]]-directed biopsy, and [[fish|fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH)]] (its favorable performance is attenuated in [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis|primary sclerosing cholangitis]]).
- **ASGE Rec 1** (Conditional recommendation, very low quality of evidence): in strictures of undetermined etiology undergoing ERCP, add **fluoroscopic-guided biopsy sampling to brush cytology** rather than brush cytology alone.
  - Intraductal biopsy is more technically challenging, more time-consuming and caused more severe adverse events → the panel acknowledged it should be done **at tertiary care centers or where endoscopic expertise exists**.
  - If that expertise is available, ASGE suggests brushing + fluoroscopic-guided biopsy for **any stricture location**.
  - General concepts: review all cross-sectional imaging; discuss the patient in a multidisciplinary board or committee.
