## Definition / Scope

The adult presenting with **infrequent and/or unsatisfactory defecation** — how to separate primary (gut–brain) constipation from a secondary cause, and then which of the three physiologic subtypes is driving it. Not a defined diagnosis; management endpoints live on [[chronic-idiopathic-constipation]] and [[defecation-disorders]].

- **Prevalence:** chronic constipation (CC) affects **8%–12% of the US population**; **3 million patients per year** seek clinical evaluation [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]]
- **Nomenclature:** *chronic constipation*, *chronic idiopathic constipation*, and *functional constipation* are **interchangeable terms** [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]]. [[rome-v-2026-dgbi|Rome V]] (2026) formalised this — "chronic constipation" (bowel disorder of gut–brain interaction [DGBI] category **C2**) replaced Rome IV's "functional constipation", dropping the word *functional*
- **Separate Rome V entities that are not C2:** **C6 [[opioid-induced-constipation|opioid-induced constipation]]**; **F3 dyssynergic defecation** (an anorectal disorder). Rome V **removed the umbrella category "functional defecation disorders"** as too broad — it swept in structural conditions such as [[rectal-prolapse|rectal prolapse]] and perineal descent [[rome-v-2026-dgbi]]
- **Refractory constipation (RC)** = infrequent and/or unsatisfactory bowel habits, with or without abdominal pain, **despite an adequate trial of lifestyle, dietary, medical, and/or pelvic floor [[biofeedback-therapy|biofeedback]] therapy when indicated**, in an adult who satisfies criteria for CC or constipation-predominant irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) (American Gastroenterological Association [AGA] 2026 best practice advice [BPA] 1) [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]]
  - **Operational criteria.** International consensus guidelines quoted by [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]] define RC as "infrequent and/or unsatisfactory bowel habits" **plus any one** of:
    1. Inadequate number of bowel movements most of the time, **and complete bowel movements on fewer than 3 days/week**
    2. **Straining on most occasions**, or worsening straining
    3. **No improvement in stool consistency on current therapy, and [[bristol-stool-form-scale|Bristol stool scale]] score <3**
    4. Insufficient improvement of another sign or symptom of CC on current treatment
    5. **Poor tolerability** of the current treatment makes the relief it provides unacceptable
  - RC is what opens the refractory arm of the algorithm below — transit testing, defecography, and the surgical question

**Rome V criteria for chronic constipation (C2)** ([[rome-v-2026-bowel-disorders]]) — fulfilled for the past 3 months, symptom onset ≥6 months before diagnosis:

> Must include:
> 1. **Two or more** of:
>    a. Straining during **>25%** of defecations
>    b. Lumpy or hard stools (Bristol 1–2) in **>25%** of defecations
>    c. Sensation of incomplete evacuation in **>25%** of defecations
>    d. Sensation of anorectal obstruction/blockage in **>25%** of defecations
>    e. Manual maneuvers to facilitate **>25%** of defecations (digital evacuation, support of the pelvic floor)
>    f. Fewer than **3 spontaneous bowel movements (SBMs) per week**
> 2. Loose stools are rarely present without the use of laxatives
> 3. **Insufficient criteria for IBS**

- **CC vs IBS with constipation (IBS-C)** turns on whether abdominal pain/discomfort is the *predominant* feature. Pain may occur in CC — it just does not dominate; a patient with CC must not meet IBS criteria ([[rome-v-2026-bowel-disorders]]).
- **Occasional constipation** is a separate Rome Foundation Working Team label for the patient who does *not* meet CC: "at least 1 functional constipation symptom, in the absence of alarm signs or symptoms, occurring at irregular and infrequent intervals, which is bothersome enough to induce a patient to seek management" ([[rome-v-2026-bowel-disorders]]).

### What counts as abnormal

Many patients hold misconceptions about normal bowel habits; normative data guide treatment goals (BPA 4) [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]]:

| Parameter | Normal range in the US population |
|---|---|
| Stool frequency | **~95%** of individuals fall between **3 bowel movements/day and 3 bowel movements/week** |
| Stool consistency | **90%** have an average **Bristol 3–5 (men)** and **Bristol 2–6 (women)** |

### The three physiologic subtypes

| Subtype | What is wrong | How it is proven | Where it is managed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Normal-transit** | Neither transit nor evacuation is measurably impaired | Normal [[anorectal-manometry\|anorectal manometry (ARM)]] + balloon expulsion test (BET), and normal colonic transit | [[chronic-idiopathic-constipation]] |
| **Slow-transit** | Delayed colonic transit ("colonic inertia") | **Colonic transit testing** once a defecatory disorder is excluded or treated [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]; in RC **off treatment**, ideally repeated **on a maximal laxative regimen** to document refractoriness [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]] | [[chronic-idiopathic-constipation]] |
| **Defecatory disorder** | Inadequate rectal propulsive forces and/or impaired relaxation or paradoxical contraction of the external anal sphincter and/or puborectalis [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]] | ARM + BET (± defecography) — see [[defecation-disorders]] | [[biofeedback-therapy\|Biofeedback]] [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]] |

**Approximately 37%** of CC patients who undergo pelvic floor testing have dyssynergic defecation [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]]; slow transit in a defecatory disorder often normalizes with successful biofeedback [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]] ([[chronic-idiopathic-constipation#Anorectal Physiology Testing (if outlet dysfunction suspected)|treat the pelvic floor first]]).

## Differential Diagnosis

**The first fork is IBS-C vs chronic constipation, and Rome V moved it** [[rome-v-2026-dgbi]]:

- **Frequency:** abdominal pain *or discomfort* **≥3 days per month in the last 3 months** qualifies for [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|IBS]] — Rome IV required **1 day per week** over the previous 3 months
- **Discomfort re-included** (removed in Rome IV)
- **Not continuous:** continuous pain points to [[centrally-mediated-abdominal-pain-syndrome|centrally mediated abdominal pain syndrome]]

| Alternative | Discriminating feature |
|---|---|
| [[irritable-bowel-syndrome\|IBS-C]] | Recurrent, not continuous, pain/discomfort on average ≥3 days/month in the past 3 months, with ≥2 of: related to defecation, change in stool frequency, change in stool form (Rome V C1) [[rome-v-2026-bowel-disorders]] |
| Opioid-induced constipation (Rome V **C6**) | **New or worsening** constipation symptoms **when initiating, changing, or increasing opioid therapy** [[rome-v-2026-bowel-disorders]] — criteria and management on [[opioid-induced-constipation]] |
| [[defecation-disorders\|Defecatory disorder]] | Excessive straining, sense of anorectal blockage, manual maneuvers, sense of incomplete evacuation [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]; confirmed on anorectal testing |
| [[colorectal-cancer\|Colorectal cancer (CRC)]] / benign colonic stricture | Alarm features (below); [[colonoscopy]] indicated |
| [[colon-ischemia\|Ischaemic]], post-surgical, or [[inflammatory-bowel-disease\|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]]-related stricture | Endoscopically dilatable; identified at colonoscopy [[asge-2014-constipation]] |
| [[rectal-prolapse\|Rectal prolapse]] / [[rectal-prolapse#Solitary Rectal Ulcer Syndrome\|solitary rectal ulcer syndrome]] | Solitary rectal ulcer at endoscopy suggests underlying prolapse [[asge-2014-constipation]] |
| Hirschsprung disease | Suspected cases need [[anorectal-manometry\|ARM]] **plus deep biopsy** for absent myenteric neurons [[asge-2014-constipation]] |

### Secondary causes to exclude before labelling it primary

Patients with RC should be **thoroughly evaluated for secondary causes** (BPA 3) [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]]:

- **Medications** — among the most common iatrogenic causes. Opioid-induced or opioid-exacerbated constipation; **anticholinergic agents such as antipsychotics**; **iron supplements**. In many patients these *exacerbate* pre-existing constipation rather than cause it
  - **Opioids — first steps** [[aga-2019-opioid-induced-constipation]]: confirm the indication is appropriate, that the patient is in a pain management program (ideally with a pain specialist), and that the **minimum necessary dose** is being taken. **Opioid switching** to an **equianalgesic dose of a less-constipating opioid** may help — oral/parenteral morphine preparations may constipate more than **transdermal opioids such as fentanyl**; combination opioid agonist/antagonist agents (e.g. oxycodone + naloxone) carry a lower risk of constipation
- **Disordered eating** — inadequate caloric intake; eating disorders such as anorexia nervosa may be undiagnosed or unrecognized
- **Comorbid neurological disease** — rarely undiagnosed or early-stage Parkinson's disease or multiple sclerosis without typical neurologic signs; consider autonomic function testing with orthostatic symptoms (off-label pyridostigmine option on [[chronic-idiopathic-constipation]])
- **Endometriosis** occasionally coexists with CC
- **Metabolic/organic conditions excluded from the chronic idiopathic constipation (CIC) evidence base** — hypothyroidism and [[celiac-disease|celiac disease]] were explicit exclusions when the CIC pharmacologic recommendations were derived, as were opioid-induced constipation and IBS-C [[aga-acg-2023-constipation]]

## Key Tests

### Which test answers which question

| Test | Question it answers | When to order |
|---|---|---|
| **[[colonoscopy]]** | Is there organic colorectal disease? | **Not** in the initial evaluation absent alarm features or suspicion of organic gastrointestinal (GI) disease (ASGE Rec 1, moderate quality). Indications: [[#Red Flags / Alarm Features]] [[asge-2014-constipation]] |
| **[[anorectal-manometry\|ARM]] + BET** | Is this a defecatory disorder? | **Most patients with CC** should have ARM + BET (and complete a biofeedback course when indicated) **before being labelled refractory** (BPA 2) [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]]; all patients refractory to standard therapy [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]. Careful rectal examination in every patient first [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]]. Rome V: not at the initial evaluation unless the digital rectal examination strongly suggests a defecatory disorder [[rome-v-2026-bowel-disorders]] |
| **Colonic transit testing** (radiopaque markers, scintigraphy, or wireless motility capsule) | Is transit slow, and is it slow *despite* treatment? | Only after no response to biofeedback, or with normal anorectal testing [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]. In RC: **off treatment** to document slow transit and **ideally again on a maximal laxative regimen** to document refractoriness (BPA 5); wireless motility capsules also measure **regional** gut transit [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]] |
| **Defecography (barium or magnetic resonance [MR])** | Is there a structural cause, or does imaging resolve a discordant ARM/BET? | Clinical features and initial tests disagree, ARM and BET disagree, and/or pelvic organ prolapse is suspected [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]; should be considered in RC (BPA 6) [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]] |
| **Gastric emptying / regional gut transit** | Is there upper-gut dysmotility that predicts a poor surgical outcome? | **Before surgery for RC** (BPA 11): regional gut transit testing; gastric emptying in patients with clinically significant upper GI symptoms. Recurrent constipation and poor quality of life after colectomy were more common with pre-existing upper GI dysmotility [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]]. Rome V likewise recommends upper gut transit evaluation, to exclude a diffuse GI motility disorder, when surgery is considered [[rome-v-2026-bowel-disorders]] |
| **Colonic manometry ± barostat** | Is colonic motility truly impaired? | Before colectomy, if available — especially when colonic transit is equivocal [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]]. Rome V: not widely available outside academic centers; relevance to CC management uncertain [[rome-v-2026-bowel-disorders]] |

**Testing needed to diagnose a defecatory disorder differs by guideline** (ACG 2021: ARM and BET both required; AGA 2026: typically 2 of 3 tests abnormal; Rome V: any 1 of 3 abnormal) — criteria table on [[defecation-disorders]].

**Defecography:** barium vs MR choice on [[anorectal-manometry#Defecography — Barium vs Magnetic Resonance (MR)]]; findings and when they are clinically relevant on [[defecation-disorders]].

**Labs and imaging (Rome V)** [[rome-v-2026-bowel-disorders]]:

- **Labs:** complete blood count (CBC) to exclude anemia (an alarm feature); **thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) and serum calcium only when clinically indicated** — e.g. suspected hypothyroidism or a disease inducing hypercalcemia.
- **Imaging** (barium enema, ultrasound, computed tomography, magnetic resonance) has **minimal diagnostic value in CC** absent alarm features or relevant comorbidities — not generally recommended.

### Test conditions that change the answer

- **Colonic transit:** stop **laxatives for ≥7 days** and **medications that delay colon transit for ≥2 weeks** before testing. Day-to-day reproducibility is poor in dyssynergic defecation and colonic inertia, so **repeat testing may be necessary** — in some patients while taking laxatives [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]]
- **Each abnormal anorectal test finding is highly specific** for a defecatory disorder — a lower rectoanal gradient on manometry, a prolonged BET, and reduced evacuation on defecography. In select patients defecography may help confirm the diagnosis, particularly with an **abnormal gradient on ARM and a normal BET** [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]]
- **Normalisation of the BET after biofeedback** suggests the defecatory disorder has resolved [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]]
- **[[bowel-preparation|Bowel preparation]]:** chronic constipation is an independent risk factor for inadequate prep — consider a more aggressive cleansing regimen [[asge-2014-constipation]]

## Red Flags / Alarm Features

**Colonoscopy to exclude organic disease** (ASGE Rec 2, high quality) [[asge-2014-constipation]]:

- **Rectal bleeding**
- **Heme-positive stool**
- **Iron-deficiency anaemia** — see [[iron-deficiency-anemia]]
- **Weight loss**
- **Obstructive symptoms** (ungraded text)
- **Before surgery** for chronic constipation
- Younger patients: flexible sigmoidoscopy may be sufficient to exclude distal disease (ungraded text)

**Screening-driven, not alarm-driven:** ASGE 2014 recommends colonoscopy for patients **>50 years** presenting with constipation who have not previously had CRC screening (Rec 3, high quality) [[asge-2014-constipation]]. **[[acg-2021-crc-screening|ACG 2021]] later suggested average-risk screening at ages 45–49 as well** (Rec 2, conditional, very low-quality evidence) — a general screening recommendation, not specific to constipation; start/stop ages and modalities on [[colorectal-cancer-screening]].

Therapeutic colonoscopy (stricture dilation, percutaneous cecostomy; no role in disimpaction): [[chronic-idiopathic-constipation#Role of Endoscopy (ASGE 2014)]].
