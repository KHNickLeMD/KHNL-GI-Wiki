## Definition / Scope

*Workup lives here; the gut–brain framework — and the **Rome Clinical Criteria**, which Rome V prefers over the research criteria below in everyday practice (qualitative features met + symptoms bothersome, a lower frequency allowed, 8-week rather than 6-month duration) — live on [[disorders-of-gut-brain-interaction]].*

Three prevalent, often-overlapping symptoms; when frequent/severe enough to impair daily activity they are **disorders of gut-brain interaction (DGBIs)** ([[aga-2023-bloating]]).

- **Belching** (eructation) — audible escape of air from the esophagus or stomach into the pharynx. Two mechanisms, plus one mirror-image complaint:
  - **Gastric belching** (Rome V **B3b**) — physiologic: proximal gastric distension triggers a transient relaxation of the lower esophageal sphincter → gastric air vented; often associated with [[gerd|gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)]].
  - **Supragastric belching** (Rome V **B3a**) — a *learned behavior*: air sucked (less often injected) into the esophagus from the pharynx and immediately expelled, independent of peristalsis; associated with anxiety; **stops during sleep, distraction, or speaking**.
  - **Inability to belch syndrome** — **new in Rome V**; see [[#Inability to belch syndrome]].
- **Aerophagia** — excess air swallowing → air reaches intestine/colon → bloating and flatulence rather than belching; not a Rome V category.

**Rome V criteria — belching disorders (B3)** ([[rome-v-2026-gastroduodenal-disorders]]) — fulfilled for the last 3 months, onset ≥6 months before diagnosis:

> **Bothersome** (severe enough to impact usual activities) **belching from the esophagus or stomach >3 days a week.**
>
> **B3a. Supragastric belching** (from esophagus) · **B3b. Gastric belching** (from stomach)
>
> Supportive criteria:
> 1. Supragastric belching is strongly supported by observing frequent, repetitive belching
> 2. Supragastric belching can be interrupted by maneuvers or distraction
> 3. Excessive gastric belching has **no defining clinical correlate**
> 4. Intraluminal esophageal impedance measurement (± concomitant manometry) objectively differentiates supragastric from gastric belches

- **Upper limit of normal: >13 supragastric belches per 24 hours.** No solid data define an upper limit for gastric belches.
- Very high frequency (**10–20 times per minute**) points to supragastric belching. **Distraction reduces the frequency; drawing attention to the behavior increases it.**
- **GERD overlap:** supragastric was the most common belch type in both excessive-belching and GERD patients (**93% vs 87%** of belches). Supragastric belches can provoke reflux, and treating them with cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) **reduces acid reflux**.

- **Abdominal bloating** — *subjective* sensation of fullness, pressure, or trapped gas in any abdominal region.
- **Abdominal distention** — *objective, measurable* increase in abdominal girth.
- The two are **distinct phenomena classified together** as Rome V **functional abdominal bloating (C4)**: there is insufficient research to separate them ([[rome-v-2026-bowel-disorders]]). Rome IV's *functional abdominal bloating and distention* was renamed because bloating is the DGBI symptom and distention co-occurs without adding diagnostic value ([[rome-v-2026-dgbi]]).

**Rome V criteria — functional abdominal bloating (C4)** ([[rome-v-2026-bowel-disorders]]) — fulfilled for the past 3 months, onset ≥6 months before diagnosis:

> Must include **both**:
> 1. Recurrent abdominal bloating and/or visible distension on average **at least 1 day per week**, **predominant over other symptoms**
> 2. **Insufficient criteria** for [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)]], chronic constipation, [[functional-diarrhea|functional diarrhea]], and the **postprandial distress syndrome** subtype of [[functional-dyspepsia|functional dyspepsia]] ([[dyspepsia]])
>
> Mild pain or discomfort related to the bloating, and minor bowel movement abnormalities, may be present but must not predominate.

- Primary functional bloating is uncommon (~3.5% globally; 4.6% women, 2.4% men — [[aga-2023-bloating]]), but bloating/distention accompany most other DGBIs: **96.6%** of patients with IBS and **88.9%** with functional dyspepsia report them, vs 53.8% of people without a DGBI.
- Evaluation follows the principles applied to IBS and chronic constipation; **evidence-based guidance for testing in functional abdominal bloating specifically is lacking**.

## Differential Diagnosis

- **Primary (functional) abdominal bloating** — the Rome V C4 criteria above, once the overlap syndromes below are excluded.
- **Overlapping DGBIs** — [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|IBS]] (esp. IBS with constipation [IBS-C]), [[chronic-idiopathic-constipation|functional constipation]], functional dyspepsia ([[dyspepsia]]), [[gastroparesis]].
- **Food intolerance / malabsorption** — carbohydrate enzyme deficiency (lactase, sucrase), artificial sweeteners/sugar alcohols, fructans. In the largest DGBI cohort, **fructose intolerance was the most common (60%)**, above **lactose intolerance (51%)**, with similar prevalence across DGBI types except IBS-C.
- **[[celiac-disease]]** and nonceliac gluten sensitivity.
- **[[small-intestinal-bacterial-overgrowth|Small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO)]]** / **[[intestinal-methanogen-overgrowth|intestinal methanogen overgrowth (IMO)]]** (methane, *Methanobrevibacter smithii*, esp. IBS-C) — pursue on the basis of **risk factors and accompanying diarrhea**, not the bloating itself (see the pitfall below).
- **Pelvic floor / defecatory dysfunction** — dyssynergic defecation with retained stool ([[defecation-disorders]]).
- **Abdominophrenic dyssynergia (APD)** — paradoxical diaphragm descent + anterior abdominal wall relaxation producing visible distention.
- **Structural / alarm** — [[ascites]], intra-abdominal mass, bowel obstruction/ileus/[[acute-colonic-pseudo-obstruction|pseudo-obstruction]], ovarian cancer (bloating a presenting symptom, esp. women ≥50), [[chronic-pancreatitis]] (bloating despite enzyme replacement).

## Diagnostic Algorithm

### Belching

![[bloating-2023-belching-algorithm-05.png|840x530]]
*Excessive belching: clinical clues, impedance findings, and management for supragastric belching, gastric belching, and aerophagia*
*Source: figure 4 of the AGA 2023 Clinical Practice Update, open access (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0)*
*[[aga-2023-bloating|AGA Clinical Practice Update on Evaluation and Management of Belching, Abdominal Bloating, and Distention]]*
*CBT = cognitive behavioral therapy · GERD = gastroesophageal reflux disease · HRM = high resolution manometry · PPI = proton pump inhibitor · TRLES = transient relaxation of lower esophageal sphincter*

- Supragastric belching **before** a reflux event does **not** respond to a [[proton-pump-inhibitors|proton pump inhibitor (PPI)]]; **after** reflux it may.
- Still uncertain → **manometry-impedance with prolonged registration, often after a meal challenge**, or **24-hour ambulatory pH-impedance monitoring** ([[reflux-testing]], [[ambulatory-reflux-monitoring]]).
- A plain abdominal radiograph may show gastric/bowel gaseous distension in gastric belching, but its sensitivity and specificity are uncertain ([[rome-v-2026-gastroduodenal-disorders]]).

> [!pitfall] Excessive supragastric belching is mistaken for hiccups
> The difference is invisible unless you watch an episode — **ask the patient for a smartphone video**. Hiccups are not associated with abnormal air transport in the esophagus ([[rome-v-2026-gastroduodenal-disorders]]).

### Inability to belch syndrome

Also called **retrograde cricopharyngeal dysfunction**. Mechanism: **ineffective relaxation of the upper esophageal sphincter (UES) in response to gastroesophageal gas reflux** — gas injection fails to relax the UES and instead produces common-cavity pressurization, oscillation of the esophageal air load, and secondary peristalsis clearing air back into the stomach ([[rome-v-2026-gastroduodenal-disorders]]). Rome V labels it **B3c** in its classification list ([[rome-v-2026-dgbi]]) and **B4** in the gastroduodenal chapter's criteria box.

**Rome V criteria — inability to belch syndrome** — fulfilled for the last 3 months, onset ≥6 months before diagnosis:

> Must include **both**:
> 1. **Bothersome inability, or impaired ability, to belch at least 3 days per week**
> 2. **No evidence of underlying major esophageal disease/dysfunction** explaining the symptom (e.g. [[achalasia]], [[antireflux-surgery|fundoplication]], [[esophagogastric-junction-outflow-obstruction|esophagogastric junction outflow obstruction]], GERD)
>
> Supportive criteria:
> 1. Associated symptoms that may be present: chest pain · gurgling noises in the chest · bloating · flatulence · epigastric pain
> 2. Supportive esophageal impedance manometry findings — air entrapment/oscillations, gas reflux episodes not followed by adequate UES relaxation

- **Diagnosis is clinical**, from the reported inability to belch plus the associated symptoms. [[high-resolution-manometry|High-resolution impedance manometry]] with **belch provocation by carbonated water** shows abnormalities with high prevalence and specificity, largely eliminated by [[botulinum-toxin|botulinum toxin]] — but its added value when the symptoms are already typical is undetermined.
- Probably **under-diagnosed** (recent entity, low awareness) and simultaneously **over-diagnosed** — most patients are self-referred and objective sphincter confirmation is rarely done.

### Bloating and distention

![[bloating-2023-bloating-algorithm-06.png|840x625]]
*Stepwise workup of abdominal bloating and distention, with the treatment that follows each diagnostic correlate*
*Source: figure 5 of the AGA 2023 Clinical Practice Update, open access (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0)*
*[[aga-2023-bloating|AGA Clinical Practice Update on Evaluation and Management of Belching, Abdominal Bloating, and Distention]]*
*APD = abdominophrenic dyssynergia · ARM = anorectal manometry · CBC = complete blood count · CD = celiac disease · CIP = chronic idiopathic intestinal pseudoobstruction · CMP = comprehensive metabolic profile · CT = computed tomography · FHx = family history · FODMAP = fermentable oligosaccharides, disaccharides, monosaccharides, and polyols · GI = gastrointestinal · IBD = inflammatory bowel disease · IBS-C = irritable bowel syndrome with constipation · KUB = kidney, ureter, and bladder X-ray · MRI = magnetic resonance imaging · NCGS = nonceliac gluten sensitivity · SIBO = small intestinal bacterial overgrowth · VH = visceral hypersensitivity*

Diaphragmatic breathing, central neuromodulators, and brain–gut behavioral therapies may be used **whichever diagnostic correlate is found** ([[aga-2023-bloating]]). Testing is symptom-driven, not routine. Detail behind the branches:

- Confirm the Rome V C4 criteria above and exclude the overlap DGBIs. The AGA update's best practice advice (BPA) 3 names the Rome IV criteria; Rome V (2026) is the current version.
- Suspected carbohydrate intolerance → **2-week dietary restriction** (simplest and cheapest; symptom resolution = a positive result) → breath testing only if refractory.
- Bloating + IBS with diarrhea (IBS-D) features → **celiac serology** (tissue transglutaminase [tTG]-IgA + total immunoglobulin A [IgA]); if positive → small-bowel biopsy.
- Constipation/difficult evacuation → **anorectal physiology testing + balloon expulsion** to rule out a pelvic floor disorder (BPA 8); abdominal x-ray may show stool burden.
- **SIBO risk factors** (the branch above, spelled out) — cystic fibrosis · Parkinson disease · scleroderma · diabetes · connective tissue disease · post-surgical motility disorders · IBS and functional dyspepsia · opioid and steroid use. **High-risk** (test or treat empirically): chronic watery diarrhea, malnutrition/weight loss, or a systemic/structural disease causing small-bowel dysmotility or delayed transit. Test = glucose or lactulose hydrogen breath test, or small-bowel aspirate and culture (reference standard, but costly and technically difficult).
- Nausea/vomiting present → consider a gastric emptying study (not routine for bloating alone, BPA 7); see [[nausea-and-vomiting]].
- Refractory upper-gastrointestinal (GI) symptoms + weight loss / suspected neuromyopathy → [[small-bowel-motility|antroduodenal manometry]], wireless motility capsule, whole-gut scintigraphy, radiopaque markers (tertiary referral).

> [!pitfall] Bloating alone is not a reason to test for SIBO
> Diarrhea, not bloating, has the strongest association with small intestinal bacterial overgrowth, and in IBS diarrhea or "gas" are the predictive symptoms ([[aga-2020-sibo]]) — [[acg-2020-sibo]] still calls bloating its most common symptom. Thresholds for a positive breath test vary widely between guidelines and **lactulose over-calls** relative to glucose or culture, so a lactulose-positive result in a bloating-only patient is weak evidence.

## Key Tests

| Test | Use in bloating/distention/belching |
|---|---|
| Impedance-pH ± high-resolution manometry | Differentiate gastric vs supragastric belching vs aerophagia |
| 2-week dietary restriction | First-line, low-cost test for carbohydrate/food intolerance |
| H₂/CH₄ breath testing (glucose/lactulose) | Carbohydrate intolerance; SIBO/IMO in selected at-risk patients |
| tTG-IgA + total IgA | Screen for [[celiac-disease]] (biopsy if positive) |
| [[anorectal-manometry\|Anorectal manometry]] + balloon expulsion | Pelvic floor dyssynergia when constipation/evacuation difficulty |
| Gastric emptying scintigraphy | Only if nausea/vomiting present — not routine |
| Computed tomography (CT)/ultrasound (US) abdomen | Only with alarm features/abnormal exam (ascites, mass, obstruction) |

## Red Flags / Alarm Features

Order labs/imaging/endoscopy (BPA 6) when present:
- Recent worsening dyspepsia or abdominal pain, esp. new onset
- Vomiting, GI bleeding
- Unintentional weight loss >10% body weight
- [[chronic-diarrhea|Chronic diarrhea]], signs of malnutrition
- Family history of GI malignancy, celiac disease, or [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]]
- Abnormal abdominal exam (mass, tympany, abnormal bowel sounds, succussion splash) → CT/US
- Woman ≥50 with new bloating → consider ovarian cancer

## Therapeutics

**Belching**

- **Supragastric belching / aerophagia** — [[brain-gut-behavioral-therapy|brain–gut behavioral therapy]] (CBT, diaphragmatic breathing), speech therapy, central neuromodulators; **not acid suppression** (the air is nonacidic). Dedicated CBT improves symptoms *and* reduces objectively measured supragastric belches.
- **Gastric belching** — dietary modification (avoid sucking candies and chewing gum, eat slowly, small swallows, avoid carbonated beverages); commonly recommended but **not rigorously tested**. [[baclofen|Baclofen]] only when gastric belching is driven by GERD; not for supragastric belching alone.
- Two negatives worth knowing: **gum chewing has no effect** on either belch type, and **no evidence supports surface-tension-reducing drugs** (dimethicone, simethicone) for belching ([[rome-v-2026-gastroduodenal-disorders]]).
- Reassurance and mechanism explanation matter: some clinicians belch intentionally to demonstrate, or **interrupt the patient's belching by having them bite on a pencil**, to show the behavior is learned.

> [!pearl] Speech therapy is the highest-yield belching treatment
> A dedicated, well-informed speech therapist achieved an **83% positive symptom response rate**. Step one is making the patient **aware of the behavior** that draws air into the esophagus, then abdominal and open-mouth breathing exercises ([[rome-v-2026-gastroduodenal-disorders]]).

**Inability to belch syndrome**

- **Botulinum toxin 50–100 IU injected into the upper esophageal sphincter** is the best-established treatment, usually given by ear-nose-throat specialists using anatomical landmarks or electromyographic confirmation.
- **No sham-controlled trial exists**, so the responder rate is unclear; effects appear to last **≥6 months in most patients**, and sustained remission also occurs. Occasional reports of hypnosis and of cricopharyngeal myotomy ([[rome-v-2026-gastroduodenal-disorders]]).

**Bloating and distention**

- **Dietary** — [[low-fodmap-diet|low–fermentable oligosaccharides, disaccharides, monosaccharides, and polyols (FODMAP) diet]], fructan/gluten avoidance where indicated, with a **GI dietitian** (BPA 9).
- **Pelvic floor disorder present** → **[[biofeedback-therapy|biofeedback]]** (BPA 11); treat coexisting constipation (BPA 13; see [[chronic-idiopathic-constipation]]).
- **Central neuromodulators** (antidepressants) — reduce visceral hypersensitivity, raise sensation threshold (BPA 12).
- **Brain–gut behavioral therapies** — hypnotherapy, CBT (BPA 14).
- **Abdominophrenic dyssynergia** — diaphragmatic breathing + central neuromodulators (BPA 15).
- **[[probiotics|Probiotics]] — not recommended** (BPA 10). Rome V agrees the certainty of benefit is low and adds that **some patients report bloating worsening** on probiotics ([[rome-v-2026-bowel-disorders]]).
- **Abdominophrenic biofeedback** for meal-triggered *visible* distension — thoracoabdominal wall motion–guided biofeedback using inductance plethysmography improved distension scores in a randomized trial (n = 42), with no effect in the placebo arm ([[rome-v-2026-bowel-disorders]]). Distinct from the pelvic floor biofeedback above.

**Drug options with trial data** ([[rome-v-2026-bowel-disorders]]):

| Drug | Dose | Population / evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [[rifaximin]] | 400 mg twice daily × 7 d | Predominant bloating + excessive flatulence with a **negative** lactulose breath test |
| Rifaximin | 400 mg 3× daily × 10 d | Reduced bloating severity over 10-week follow-up |
| Rifaximin | 550 mg 3× daily × 14 d | Nonconstipated IBS — adequate relief of bloating 40% vs 30% placebo |
| [[prucalopride]] | 2 mg once daily | Chronic constipation with moderate–severe bloating (post hoc: 62% vs 50%) |
| Secretagogues | — | Superior to placebo for bloating on meta-analysis of randomized trials |
| Peppermint oil | 2–4× daily before meals | May improve bloating and distention |
| Simethicone · α-galactosidase | Over the counter | Gas/bloating; α-galactosidase for high-oligosaccharide meals |
