Noninvasive cross-sectional imaging — no ionizing radiation, no instrumentation.

- **Magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP)** — heavily T2-weighted sequences image the pancreatic and bile ducts **without contrast or instrumentation**.
- **Contrast-enhanced magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)** — characterizes solid and cystic pancreatic lesions, focal liver lesions, and bowel/perianal inflammation. A liver observation in a patient at high risk for [[hepatocellular-carcinoma|hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)]] is reported with [[li-rads|Liver Imaging Reporting and Data System (LI-RADS)]] ([[acr-2018-li-rads-ct-mri-core]]); outside that at-risk population it is worked up as a [[focal-liver-lesions|focal liver lesion]].

## Technique Standards

| Parameter | Specification | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Field strength | **1.5-T minimum**; 3-T may add advantage for **small pancreatic lesions** (superior soft-tissue resolution) | [[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening]], [[aasld-2022-psc]] |
| Coils | Phased-array | [[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening]] |
| MRCP sequence | **T2-weighted 3-dimensional (3D) MRCP with 1-mm slices preferred over 2-dimensional (2D) MRCP** | [[aasld-2022-psc]] |
| Axial sequences | T1-weighted + T2-weighted | [[aasld-2022-psc]] |
| Contrast | Extracellular **or** hepatobiliary agent at diagnosis, and when imaging for a change in clinical status or concern for [[cholangiocarcinoma\|cholangiocarcinoma (CCA)]] — insufficient evidence to favor either agent type. Pancreatic surveillance: with **and** without intravenous contrast | [[aasld-2022-psc]], [[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening]] |
| Adequate study | No artifact or blurring; **third-order biliary branches and beyond** delineated | [[aasld-2022-psc]] |

## Indications

*Criteria, risk strata, surveillance intervals and size thresholds live on the linked page — not duplicated here.*

| Setting | When MRI/MRCP is the test |
|---|---|
| [[choledocholithiasis]] | **Intermediate probability (10%–50%)** of a common bile duct (CBD) stone → **[[endoscopic-ultrasound\|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]] *or* MRCP** to confirm before [[ercp\|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]]; choose by patient preference, local expertise and availability (conditional, low quality). The criteria that assign the stratum live on that page. [[asge-2019-choledocholithiasis]] |
| [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis\|Primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)]] | **Preferred diagnostic modality** — ERCP avoided for diagnosis. Also the modality for **annual CCA surveillance** (± CA 19-9), and it defines a **high-grade stricture: >75% lumen reduction in the CBD or hepatic ducts**. The diagnostic algorithm lives on that page. [[aasld-2022-psc]] |
| [[liver-transplantation\|Post-transplant]] [[biliary-stricture\|biliary stricture]] | **First-line diagnostic tool** for anastomotic and non-anastomotic strictures (Strong, Level 2). [[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-graft-complications]] ASGE calls it an **acceptable diagnostic test**, of greatest use when the diagnosis is unclear or the probability of stricture is intermediate (Conditional, moderate-to-high quality). [[asge-2023-post-transplant-biliary-strictures]] |
| Indeterminate biliary stricture | Anatomy and planning only — it **cannot confirm or exclude malignancy**; tissue is required. Contrasted computed tomography (CT) or MRI–MRCP should be obtained and reviewed **before** ERCP for malignant perihilar obstruction, to plan which sectors to drain. [[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]] |
| [[acute-pancreatitis\|Acute pancreatitis]] | CBD stone suspected **without** [[acute-cholangitis\|cholangitis]] or jaundice → MRCP or EUS to screen **before** ERCP; diagnostic ERCP avoided. Not improving at 48–72 h → CT **or** MRI for local complications; MRI is favored with contrast allergy or renal insufficiency (necrosis visible on non-gadolinium T2-weighted images). [[acg-2024-acute-pancreatitis]] |
| Idiopathic / [[recurrent-acute-pancreatitis\|recurrent acute pancreatitis]] | Additional evaluation with repeat abdominal ultrasound, MRI and/or EUS (conditional, very low quality) — looking for microlithiasis and for a mass obstructing the pancreatic or bile duct. Suspect [[pancreatic-cancer\|pancreatic cancer]] over age 40, especially with a prolonged or recurrent course. [[acg-2024-acute-pancreatitis]] |
| Suspected [[acute-cholecystitis\|acute cholecystitis]] or cholangitis | **Third step in nonpregnant adults**: ultrasound first, then CT; if both are equivocal/nondiagnostic and suspicion persists → MRI/MRCP or hepatobiliary iminodiacetic acid (HIDA) scan for cholecystitis (conditional; knowledge gap for MRI/MRCP), and MRI/MRCP is a reasonable option when cholangitis is suspected. **Pregnancy:** ultrasound or MRI can be considered first, no preference (knowledge gap). [[idsa-2024-ciai-imaging-cholecystitis-cholangitis]] |
| [[pancreatic-cysts\|Pancreatic cysts]] | **Tests of choice for diagnosis** — non-invasive, no radiation, better at showing communication between the main pancreatic duct and the cyst. **MRCP is the preferred surveillance modality.** Pancreatic-protocol CT or EUS are excellent alternatives when MRI cannot be done (conditional, very low quality). Surveillance intervals live on that page. [[acg-2018-pancreatic-cysts]] |
| [[chronic-pancreatitis\|Chronic pancreatitis]] | CT **or** MRI is first-line for diagnosis (strong, low quality). **Secretin-enhanced MRCP** when CT/MRI or EUS leaves the diagnosis unconfirmed and suspicion stays high (conditional, low quality) — it distends the main and side-branch ducts and grades duodenal filling. [[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]] |
| [[pancreatic-cancer-screening\|Pancreatic cancer surveillance]] (genetic susceptibility) | Accepted modality — alone or **alternating with EUS** (conditional, very low quality). MRI preferred for higher anesthesia/procedural risk, for patients who value avoiding invasive testing, and where it can be combined with other imaging (e.g. enterography in [[peutz-jeghers-syndrome\|Peutz-Jeghers syndrome]]). [[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening]] |
| [[focal-liver-lesions\|Focal liver lesion]] of uncertain etiology | **Multiphasic contrast-enhanced imaging, preferably MRI or CT, with late arterial, portal venous and delayed phases** (strong). MRI preferred over standard cross-sectional imaging to separate [[hepatocellular-adenoma\|hepatocellular adenoma]] from other lesions, and **multiphase MRI with a hepatobiliary-specific agent** for suspected [[focal-nodular-hyperplasia\|focal nodular hyperplasia]] (both conditional). Suspected liver metastases → MRI with hepatobiliary contrast **plus diffusion-weighted imaging**. [[acg-2024-focal-liver-lesions]] |
| [[crohns-disease\|Crohn's disease]] | Magnetic resonance enterography is **used preferentially under age 35 y** and whenever serial examinations are likely, because it avoids radiation; CT enterography is comparable for detecting small-bowel disease. Pelvic MRI and/or EUS further characterize [[perianal-fistulizing-crohns-disease\|perianal disease]] and perirectal abscesses (key concepts, ungraded). [[acg-2025-crohns]] |

> [!pitfall] Don't interpose MRCP when ERCP is already needed
> High pretest probability of a stricture, or cholangitis → go straight to ERCP; MRCP is diagnostic, not therapeutic, and only delays drainage. ERCP is equally warranted **despite a negative MRCP** when clinical suspicion stays high ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-graft-complications]], [[asge-2023-post-transplant-biliary-strictures]]).

## Test Performance

| Setting | Measure | MRI/MRCP | Comparator | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Intermediate-probability CBD stone (5 prospective comparative studies, 272 patients) | Pooled sensitivity | **0.87** (95% confidence interval [CI] 0.80–0.93) | EUS 0.97 (0.91–0.99) | [[asge-2019-choledocholithiasis]] |
| Same | Pooled specificity | **0.92** (0.87–0.96) | EUS 0.90 (0.83–0.94) | [[asge-2019-choledocholithiasis]] |
| Post–liver transplant biliary stricture (20 studies, 758 patients; ERCP = reference) | Pooled sensitivity / specificity | **94.9% / 90.3%** | — | [[asge-2023-post-transplant-biliary-strictures]] |
| Same (accuracy 13 studies, 508 patients) | Accuracy (positive / negative predictive value) | **92.4%** (90.6% / 93.7%) | — | [[asge-2023-post-transplant-biliary-strictures]] |
| Pancreatic cyst | Accuracy for **cyst type** | **40%–50%** | CT and EUS without fine-needle aspiration (FNA) similar | [[acg-2018-pancreatic-cysts]] |
| Pancreatic cyst | Accuracy for **benign vs malignant** | **55%–76%** | CT and EUS without FNA similar | [[acg-2018-pancreatic-cysts]] |
| Biliary stricture | Accuracy for **malignancy** | **60%–80%** (CT, MRI or EUS without FNA) | — | [[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]] |

- At a 50% pretest probability of post-transplant stricture, out of 1,000 patients MRCP correctly identifies 475 with a stricture and 452 without, calls 48 falsely positive, and misses 25 ([[asge-2023-post-transplant-biliary-strictures]]).

## Limitations

- **EUS is more sensitive than MRI for small solid pancreatic lesions.** [[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening]] In the two choledocholithiasis studies with the widest EUS–MRCP gap, the false-negative MRCPs were all **small stones (6 mm)**. [[asge-2019-choledocholithiasis]]
- **Malignancy in a biliary stricture cannot be confirmed or excluded noninvasively** — a definitive tissue diagnosis is needed in most strictures not resectable at presentation. [[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]]
- **Cyst type and benign-vs-malignant calls are unreliable on imaging alone** (accuracies above); an indeterminate cyst may need a second modality or cyst-fluid analysis via EUS. [[acg-2018-pancreatic-cysts]]
- **Chronic pancreatitis should not be diagnosed on secretin-enhanced MRCP findings alone**; its expense also restricts it to cases first-line testing left unresolved. [[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]]
- **Patient/hardware constraints:** MRI-incompatible metallic implants, claustrophobia, contrast allergy, renal failure. [[asge-2023-post-transplant-biliary-strictures]], [[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening]] Overall procedural risk is low, especially compared with ERCP. [[asge-2023-post-transplant-biliary-strictures]]

> [!pitfall] A negative MRCP does not exclude PSC
> With a high pretest probability, a **30% probability of PSC remains despite a negative MRCP** — proceed to [[liver-biopsy|liver biopsy]] for small-duct disease, and repeat a suboptimal or equivocal study at an experienced center using 3D reconstruction ([[aasld-2022-psc]]).
