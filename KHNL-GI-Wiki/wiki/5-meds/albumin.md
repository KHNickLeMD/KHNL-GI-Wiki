## Overview

- Intravenous (IV) albumin is a **plasma volume expander**. In [[cirrhosis]] it raises effective arterial blood volume, which falls progressively in the vasodilatory–hyperdynamic circulatory state that drives decompensation ([[aga-2024-vasoactive-albumin-cirrhosis]]).
- Different mechanism from the vasoconstrictors ([[terlipressin]], [[somatostatin-analogs|octreotide]], norepinephrine, midodrine), which lower [[portal-hypertension|portal pressure]] by splanchnic vasoconstriction. Adverse effects of the **vasoactive** drugs are tabled on [[somatostatin-analogs]].
- **Evidence grade:** the American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) Clinical Practice Updates (CPUs) cited here issue ungraded **Best Practice Advice (BPA)** statements — expert review, with no formal rating of evidence quality or strength. American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD) 2021 guidance statements likewise carry no grade, and the American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2026 paracentesis statement is a key concept the guideline says it could not grade because of heterogeneity in the evidence.
- **Preparation:** **20%–25% (hyperoncotic) albumin** is what the guidelines specify for paracentesis ([[aga-2025-ascites-cirrhosis]] BPA 6; [[acg-2026-preventive-care-cirrhosis|ACG 2026]] writes 25%). **5% albumin** — or crystalloid, preferentially lactated Ringer's — is used for resuscitating hypovolemia ([[aasld-2021-ascites-sbp-hrs]]). No concentration is specified for the other indications below.
- **Core roles:** at [[paracentesis|large-volume paracentesis (LVP)]], in [[spontaneous-bacterial-peritonitis|spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP)]], and as the volume expander of choice for [[aki-in-cirrhosis|acute kidney injury (AKI) in cirrhosis]]. **Not** for uncomplicated [[ascites]].

## Indications and Dosing

| Indication | Advice | Albumin regimen |
|---|---|---|
| **Paracentesis >5 L (LVP)** | Give IV albumin (AGA 2024 BPA 4, AGA 2025 BPA 6, AASLD 2021, ACG 2026 key concept 1) | **6–8 g per litre of ascites removed**, as 20%–25% albumin (expert opinion; AGA 2025 calls both the 5-L cutoff and the 6–8 g/L dose "somewhat arbitrary"). **Lower doses (4 g/L) may be sufficient** |
| **Paracentesis ≤5 L** | Not routine — incremental benefit is less certain, and smaller taps cause no significant hemodynamic change | Consider the same 6–8 g/L where the risk of circulatory compromise is highest: **systolic blood pressure <90 mm Hg, serum sodium <130 mmol/L, and/or AKI** (AASLD 2021). AGA 2025 and ACG 2026 frame the same triggers as hypotension, renal insufficiency/dysfunction, or electrolyte abnormalities |
| **LVP in [[acute-on-chronic-liver-failure\|acute-on-chronic liver failure (ACLF)]]** | Give albumin during paracentesis | **6–8 g/L regardless of the volume removed** |
| **SBP** | AGA 2024: albumin *may be considered* (BPA 5). AASLD 2021: treat with IV albumin **in addition to** antibiotics | **1.5 g/kg body weight on day 1 + 1 g/kg on day 3** — the dose was chosen arbitrarily in the pivotal trial and has remained the standard. Highest-benefit subgroup: table below |
| **AKI in cirrhosis with ascites (hospitalized)** | Albumin is the **volume expander of choice** (BPA 8) | **1 g/kg body weight daily × 2 consecutive days, capped at 100 g/day**, in patients with evidence of intravascular volume depletion. No response after 2 days of diuretic withdrawal plus this albumin challenge is itself a diagnostic criterion for hepatorenal syndrome–AKI (HRS-AKI; full algorithm on [[aki-in-cirrhosis]]) |
| **HRS-AKI** | Concurrent albumin with the vasoconstrictor, accounting for volume status; terlipressin is the vasoactive drug of choice (BPA 10) | With terlipressin: **1 g/kg on day 1, then 40–50 g/day for the duration of therapy** (AASLD 2021). With norepinephrine: albumin titrated to a central venous pressure of **4–10 mm Hg** |
| **Hyponatremia in cirrhosis** | Hypovolemic: part of fluid resuscitation. Hypervolemic and either severe (serum sodium <120 mEq/L) or symptomatic: albumin alongside sodium/water restriction and holding diuretics and laxatives | Hypovolemic: **5% albumin** or crystalloid. Hypervolemic: hyperoncotic albumin, dosed on volume assessment — **no dose is specified**. Full treatment ladder on [[ascites]] |

- Vasoactive-drug dosing for HRS-AKI lives on [[aki-in-cirrhosis]] and is not reproduced here.

### Highest-Benefit Subgroup in SBP

| Marker | AGA 2024 | AASLD 2021 |
|---|---|---|
| Bilirubin | **>4 mg/dL** | **>5 mg/dL** |
| Creatinine | >1.0 mg/dL | >1.0 mg/dL |
| Blood urea nitrogen (BUN) | >30 mg/dL | >30 mg/dL |
| How the markers combine | Bilirubin **and** baseline AKI (creatinine **and** BUN) — all three together | Renal dysfunction (BUN **or** creatinine) **or** severe hepatic decompensation (bilirubin) |
| Looser phrasing each ends on | Society guidelines specify that patients with AKI and/or [[jaundice]] are more likely to benefit | Patients with AKI and/or jaundice at the time of SBP diagnosis are more likely to benefit |

- **Neither source restricts albumin to this subgroup** — the criteria mark who gains most, not who qualifies.
- ⚠ **The two guidelines disagree on the bilirubin cut-off.** Use **>4 mg/dL** per AGA 2024, the newer source; the discrepancy changes management only between 4 and 5 mg/dL, where AGA would treat and AASLD would not.

> [!pitfall] Fixed-dose albumin can overshoot
> In AKI and HRS-AKI a routine fixed dose may be insufficient or may precipitate volume overload and pulmonary edema. Tailor it to volume status — while accepting that the best way to assess volume is unknown ([[aga-2024-vasoactive-albumin-cirrhosis]]).

## Where Albumin Is Not Indicated

- **Uncomplicated ascites, hospitalized or not — do not use** (AGA 2024 BPA 6).
- **Infections other than SBP — not advised**, unless there is concomitant AKI. Three randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and a meta-analysis found no reduction in AKI or mortality, and more pulmonary edema (AGA 2024).
- **Long-term weekly or biweekly infusions — no recommendation can be made.** AASLD 2021 calls the data controversial in diuretic-responsive ascites and insufficient in refractory ascites outside LVP.
- **Targeting a serum albumin level** in inpatients not admitted specifically for volume overload does not reduce infection, kidney dysfunction, or death; the role of concurrent IV albumin with IV loop diuretics for inpatient volume overload is unclear (AGA 2025).
- **Routine initial resuscitation of shock in cirrhosis or ACLF — not supported.** Use **balanced crystalloids** (lactated Ringer's or Plasma-Lyte, preferred over 0.9% saline), adding albumin only for its established indications — LVP, SBP, HRS-AKI (Statement 46, B1). RCTs show no consistent survival benefit and more pulmonary oedema and fluid overload, especially with 20% albumin; selected septic-shock patients may benefit ([[aasld-easl-2026-aclf-global-consensus]]). ⚠ Narrows the older AASLD 2021 framing of 5% albumin as an option for resuscitating hypovolemia (*Overview*); in shock the 2026 consensus governs.
- **Vasoconstrictors are not a substitute** — do not use them for uncomplicated ascites, after LVP, or in SBP (AGA 2024 BPA 7).

## Evidence

### Paracentesis

- A single 5-L tap does **not** cause deleterious hemodynamic or renal effects.
- Daily 5-L paracenteses **without** albumin → AKI in 21% (**0% with albumin**); plasma renin and aldosterone rose at 48 h only in the no-albumin group — "post-paracentesis circulatory dysfunction" (PCD).
- Albumin vs dextran-70 vs polygeline (RCT, n=289): PCD equal for volumes **<5 L**; for **>5 L**, PCD **18% (albumin)** vs 34% and 38% — confirmed in a meta-analysis of 8 trials (n=694).
- A Cochrane meta-analysis found **no mortality or renal-dysfunction difference** for volume expansion after LVP, so robust-outcome data are still lacking.

### Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis

- AKI is the strongest predictor of death in SBP, and may progress to HRS-AKI even after the infection resolves.
- RCT (n=105): antibiotics **+ albumin** → AKI **10% vs 33%**, death **10% vs 29%**.
- **Lower doses may also work:** a small RCT giving only **10 g/day on days 1–3** still showed less renal dysfunction (**7% vs 20%**) and lower in-hospital mortality (**27% vs 40%**) than control, consistent with another small trial. Four of the five RCTs in the most recent meta-analysis used the standard 1.5 g/kg + 1 g/kg regimen.
- Albumin beat hydroxyethyl starch for systemic hemodynamics in a 20-patient RCT.

### Other Settings

- Albumin did **not** enhance the diuretic effect of furosemide (crossover RCT); a 126-patient unblinded RCT showed faster ascites resolution and less recurrence but **no survival benefit, and was not cost-effective**.
- Chronic weekly albumin: ANSWER (n=431, ascites persisting despite diuretics; 40 g twice weekly for 2 weeks then 40 g weekly for 18 months) lowered 18-month mortality, LVP need, hyponatremia, SBP and HRS-AKI — but was unblinded and not placebo-controlled. The placebo-controlled MACHT trial (albumin every 15 days plus midodrine in patients listed for transplant) found **no difference** in mortality or ascites complications.
- ATTIRE (n=777 inpatients with new or worsening ascites; albumin dosed to a serum albumin target throughout admission): **no effect** on infection, AKI, or death, and **more pulmonary edema** in the albumin arm, which received 10× the albumin of controls.

> [!pearl] Benefit is confined to three settings
> Outside LVP, SBP and AKI, no outpatient or inpatient population has been shown to benefit from albumin ([[aga-2024-vasoactive-albumin-cirrhosis]]).

## Cautions

- Adverse events, all reported in <10% of patients: **fluid overload, pulmonary edema, rigors, hypotension/tachycardia, pyrexia, nausea/vomiting, rash/pruritus.**
- Generally dose-dependent, but they still occur at lower doses.
