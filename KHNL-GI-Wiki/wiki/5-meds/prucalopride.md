## Class

- Selective, high-affinity 5-hydroxytryptamine type 4 (5-HT4) receptor agonist; prokinetic. Promotes neurotransmission by enteric neurons → stimulates the peristaltic reflex, intestinal secretion, and gastrointestinal (GI) motility ([[aga-acg-2023-constipation]]).
- Induces and increases the amplitude of colonic **high-amplitude propagated contractions**, which propel colonic contents — at least part of the clinical effect.
- **More selective for the 5-HT4 receptor than other 5-HT4 agonists** ([[rome-v-2026-bowel-disorders]]); action is **purely prokinetic, with no direct antiemetic effect** ([[aga-2025-gastroparesis]]).

## Dosing

| Population | Dose |
|---|---|
| Adults, normal renal function | **2 mg orally (PO) once daily** — starting dose 1–2 mg daily, titrated to symptom response; **maximum 2 mg daily** |
| Severe renal impairment — creatinine clearance (CrCl) <30 mL/min | **1 mg daily** |
| Age ≥65 y | **No reduction** — efficacy comparable to the overall study population |

- **Duration:** trials ran **4–24 weeks**; the drug label sets no limit. Can be used as a replacement for, or an adjunct to, over-the-counter (OTC) agents ([[aga-acg-2023-constipation]]).
- **Why dosing stops at 2 mg:** 1, 2 and 4 mg all relieved constipation in phase 3 randomized controlled trials (RCTs), but **4 mg was not significantly more effective than 2 mg** for most endpoints and caused more GI side effects — so only the 1 mg and 2 mg doses are approved ([[rome-v-2026-bowel-disorders]]).

## Indications

### Chronic Idiopathic Constipation

- **Recommended** in adults with [[chronic-idiopathic-constipation|chronic idiopathic constipation (CIC)]] who do not respond to OTC agents — **strong recommendation, moderate certainty of evidence** ([[aga-acg-2023-constipation]] **Recommendation 10**). This is the **only approved indication** ([[aga-2025-gastroparesis]]).
- Beyond stool frequency, it improved constipation symptoms, abdominal symptoms, quality of life, and treatment satisfaction vs placebo; the guideline notes it **may have additional benefit for abdominal pain** ([[aga-acg-2023-constipation]]). For bloating, see [[abdominal-bloating-and-distention]].
- **Cost ~$563/month** — the most expensive of the prescription agents for CIC, against **<$50/month** for every OTC agent ([[aga-acg-2023-constipation]]).

Pooled from **5 twelve-week placebo-controlled RCTs of prucalopride 2 mg daily** (United States, Europe, Asia-Pacific) ([[aga-acg-2023-constipation]]):

| Outcome | Effect vs placebo | Certainty |
|---|---|---|
| Complete spontaneous bowel movements (CSBMs) per week | mean difference (MD) **+0.96** (95% confidence interval [CI] 0.64–1.29) | High |
| Responder (**≥3 CSBMs/wk**) | relative risk (RR) **2.37** (1.97–2.85) — **165 more responders per 1,000** (117–222) | High |
| Alternative endpoint A (**≥3 CSBMs/wk *and* ≥1 CSBM over baseline for ≥75% of study weeks**) | RR **2.51** (1.97–3.21) — **109 more per 1,000** (70–160) | High |
| Global relief (extremely / quite a bit effective) | RR **2.09** (0.15–3.0) | Moderate |
| Patient Assessment of Constipation–Quality of Life (PAC-QOL; lower = better) | MD **0.32 lower** (0.41–0.23 lower) | Moderate |
| **Diarrhea leading to discontinuation** | RR **3.00** (1.89–4.78) | Moderate |

- Spontaneous bowel movements (SBMs) per week were **not studied** in any included trial. **Overall certainty for prucalopride: moderate.**

### Refractory Constipation

- Prucalopride is one of the Food and Drug Administration (FDA)–approved agents to be trialled — alone *and* in combination — before off-label agents or surgery ([[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]] Best Practice Advice [BPA] 7, ungraded).
- Prucalopride and [[linaclotide]] are the two agents shown **effective in chronic constipation unresponsive to OTC laxatives**, though the American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) cautions those trials **lacked rigorous evaluation of prior OTC therapy or colonic transit**.
- **Combination across mechanisms** (e.g. polyethylene glycol plus prucalopride) is common when monotherapy fails, but the data are **anecdotal**. Full pathway: [[chronic-idiopathic-constipation]].

### Gastroparesis

- **AGA suggests *against* prucalopride as first-line treatment of [[gastroparesis]]** — conditional recommendation, **very low** certainty ([[aga-2025-gastroparesis]] Recommendation 5). Use here is outside the approved indication.
- Evidence: 2 crossover RCTs, **49 patients** with scintigraphy-confirmed gastroparesis (one idiopathic-only, one diabetic/mixed connective tissue disease). Reductions in the composite nausea/vomiting score (−0.18; 95% CI −0.53 to 0.17), early satiety/postprandial fullness (−0.2; −0.58 to 0.18), and symptom severity (−0.22; −0.50 to 0.07) **did not reach the threshold for clinical importance**; abdominal pain and quality of life likewise improved sub-threshold. Serious adverse effects (RR 2.8; 95% CI 0.12–66.05) and discontinuation for adverse events (AEs) (RR 1.87; 0.18–19.47) were increased but both very imprecise.
- **Who may still reasonably elect it** (shared decision making):
  - Patients placing higher value on possible symptom improvement and lower value on AE risk — **particularly idiopathic gastroparesis** (the idiopathic trial showed favorable results; the predominantly diabetic trial did not).
  - Patients with **gastroparesis + CIC**, for efficiency of treating both conditions with one drug given its established CIC efficacy.
- **Caveat:** gastroparesis attributed to **diabetes or connective tissue disease** may be less likely to respond; benefit depends on relieving gastric retention rather than on antiemesis.

### Opioid-Induced Constipation

- The AGA makes **no recommendation** for prucalopride in [[opioid-induced-constipation|opioid-induced constipation (OIC)]] — **an evidence gap, not a recommendation against** ([[aga-2019-opioid-induced-constipation]] Statement 4a). Quality of evidence was rated down for suspected publication bias and imprecision; trial detail sits on [[opioid-induced-constipation]].
- Phase 2 studies showed benefit, but **large phase 3 RCTs have not been performed** ([[rome-v-2026-bowel-disorders]]).

### Irritable Bowel Syndrome with Constipation

- **Not widely evaluated** in [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|irritable bowel syndrome with constipation (IBS-C)]] ([[rome-v-2026-bowel-disorders]]).

## Contraindications

Intestinal perforation or obstruction; [[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease]]; [[ulcerative-colitis|ulcerative colitis]]; [[toxic-megacolon|toxic megacolon]]/megarectum ([[aga-acg-2023-constipation]]).

## Adverse Effects

- Headache (most common, often transient), nausea, diarrhea, abdominal pain — typically within the first week, resolving in a few days.
- **Diarrhea in 11%–19%** of treated patients vs **3%–5%** on placebo ([[rome-v-2026-bowel-disorders]]).
- **5%** discontinued prucalopride because of side effects. Serious adverse events were uncommon, but the CI around the estimate was imprecise and included a possible increased risk ([[aga-acg-2023-constipation]]).
- **No cardiovascular signal:** cardiovascular adverse events were no more common than placebo, and as a selective 5-HT4 agonist prucalopride has **no appreciable cardiac adverse effects**, unlike cisapride and perhaps [[tegaserod]] ([[aga-2025-gastroparesis]]).
- No drug-associated risk of miscarriage, major birth defects, or adverse maternal/fetal outcomes identified ([[aga-acg-2023-constipation]]).

> [!pitfall] Counsel and watch for mood change in the first weeks
> Reports of new-onset or worsening depression and suicidal thoughts/behavior cluster in the **initial weeks** of treatment, and the label asks clinicians to be alert to unusual changes in mood, behavior, and suicidal ideation — **no causal association has been demonstrated** ([[aga-2025-gastroparesis]]).
> The underlying signal: in a safety database of **4,476 subjects, 4 attempted and 2 completed suicides** — both completed suicides had stopped prucalopride **more than 1 month before** the event ([[aga-acg-2023-constipation]]).
