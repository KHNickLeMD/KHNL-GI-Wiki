Glucagon-like peptide-2 (GLP-2) agonists are **intestinotrophic** peptides used to enhance **intestinal adaptation** and facilitate weaning from long-term **[[nutrition-in-hospitalized-patients|parenteral nutrition (PN)]]** in **[[short-bowel-syndrome|short bowel syndrome]] (SBS) / intestinal failure**, including [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]]-associated (typically post-resection [[crohns-disease|Crohn's]]) intestinal failure. Distinct from **[[glp-1-receptor-agonists|glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) agonists]]** (e.g. [[semaglutide]]), which are used for weight/glycemic management — GLP-2 is trophic to the gut, not an incretin weight-loss agent.

## Mechanism
- Native GLP-2 is secreted postprandially from **L cells in the distal ileum and right colon**; its very short half-life was extended in the recombinant molecule, allowing **once-daily subcutaneous injection**. ([[aga-2022-short-bowel-syndrome]])
- Binds GLP-2 receptors on intestinal enteroendocrine cells, subepithelial myofibroblasts and enteric neurons → local release of insulin-like growth factor-1, nitric oxide and keratinocyte growth factor. GLP-2 also **increases intestinal and portal blood flow** and **inhibits gastric acid secretion**. ([[gattex-2025-teduglutide-label]])
- Net effect: **increases villous height and crypt depth**, with effects on gastrointestinal (GI) motility → enhances **intestinal adaptation** (often 1–2 y to maximum effect after resection) and reduces the PN/intravenous (IV) support required. ([[aga-2024-diet-nutrition-ibd]], [[aga-2023-ostomies]])
- Dose-ranging data: every dose except 0.03 mg/kg/d enhanced GI fluid (wet weight) absorption by ~**750–1000 mL/d**. ([[gattex-2025-teduglutide-label]])

## Agents
- **Teduglutide (Gattex)** — recombinant GLP-2 analogue; initial United States (US) approval 2012. All dosing and safety detail below is teduglutide's. ([[gattex-2025-teduglutide-label]])
- Other GLP-2 analogues named as adaptation-promoting options for high ostomy output: **elsiglutide, glepaglutide, apraglutide** — the American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) ostomy update names them but gives no dosing. ([[aga-2023-ostomies]])

## Indications
- **Labeled indication:** adults and children **≥1 y** with SBS who are **dependent on parenteral support**. ([[gattex-2025-teduglutide-label]])
- **Positioning — late line.** Significant side effects and cost mean teduglutide is used **only after diet and the conventional SBS treatments are optimized**, in carefully selected SBS/intestinal-failure patients. It is **not "one more antidiarrheal."** ([[aga-2022-short-bowel-syndrome]])
- **Weaning from long-term PN.** The AGA advises that in IBD with SBS, long-term PN be transitioned toward **customized hydration** (IV electrolyte support and/or oral rehydration solutions) and oral intake whenever possible, to reduce long-term PN complications; **GLP-2 agonists can facilitate this transition** (Best Practice Advice 8). Roughly **50% of adults and 73% of children** with SBS can wean off PN support. ([[aga-2024-diet-nutrition-ibd]])
- **Chronic high [[ostomy-management|ostomy]] output (HOO)** — persistent or new HOO **>3 wk after** stoma creation; more likely with a jejunostomy, **short bowel (<200 cm small intestine remaining)**, or small bowel that fails to adapt after surgery. GLP-2 analogues are the **adaptation-promoting** category of the AGA treatment table for HOO, listed after bulking agents, antimotility, antisecretory and anti-inflammatory agents and before surgical reversal of the ostomy. ([[aga-2023-ostomies]])
- **Off-label:** the labeled indication requires dependence on parenteral support — treating HOO in a patient who is not parenteral-support–dependent falls outside it.
- **Co-manage with a registered dietitian**, especially in SBS and with complex nutrition therapy. ([[aga-2024-diet-nutrition-ibd]])

## Dosing

*The AGA clinical practice updates give no teduglutide dose; the figures below are from the US prescribing information ([[gattex-2025-teduglutide-label]]).*

- **0.05 mg/kg once daily, subcutaneous** — same dose in adults and in children ≥1 y. **Not** intravenous or intramuscular.
- **Renal impairment: halve it to 0.025 mg/kg once daily** when estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) is **<60 mL/min/1.73 m²** (moderate, severe, or end-stage renal disease). Exposure otherwise rises 1.5–2.6× (area under the curve).
- **Hepatic impairment: no adjustment** at [[cirrhosis|Child-Pugh]] A or B. **Not studied in Child-Pugh C.**
- **0.1 mg/kg/d (twice the recommended dose) is not a recommended dosage** — its trial's primary endpoint was not statistically significant, and obstruction was more frequent.
- **No contraindication in the label.** Safety and effectiveness are not established **below 1 y** of age; the 5 mg kit is not recommended under **10 kg**.
- Missed dose → take it as soon as possible that day; **never 2 doses in one day**. Alternate injection sites — 1 of the 4 abdominal quadrants, or alternating thighs or arms.
- **Preparation ceiling:** the 5 mg vial reconstitutes with 0.5 mL sterile water to **10 mg/mL**, and **a maximum of 0.38 mL (3.8 mg) can be withdrawn per vial** — a dose above 3.8 mg (body weight above 76 kg at 0.05 mg/kg) needs more than one vial. Give within **3 hours** of reconstitution; do not shake or freeze.
- **Stopping also destabilizes fluid balance** — monitor fluid and electrolyte status after discontinuation.

## Monitoring

Teduglutide is intestinotrophic, so the label builds an endoscopic and laboratory program around it. Polyps reported in the trials and postmarketing include **colorectal, gastric and small-intestinal (duodenum, ileum, jejunum)**.

| When | Adults | Children |
|---|---|---|
| **Within 6 mo before starting** | [[colonoscopy]] **and** [[upper-endoscopy]] with removal of any polyps; baseline bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase, lipase, amylase | Fecal occult blood test — scope only if new/unexplained blood in the stool; same baseline labs |
| **End of year 1** | Repeat colonoscopy and upper endoscopy (or alternate imaging) | Colonoscopy/sigmoidoscopy; consider upper endoscopy (or alternate imaging) |
| **Thereafter** | No polyp found → repeat **no less frequently than every 5 y**; polyp found → standard [[colonoscopy-surveillance\|polyp surveillance]] intervals | Fecal occult blood **annually**; colonoscopy/sigmoidoscopy **every 5 y** |
| **Every 6 mo on treatment** | Bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase, lipase, amylase | Same |

- Clinically meaningful change in those labs → **image the gallbladder/biliary tract, liver or pancreas** and reassess continuing the drug. ([[gattex-2025-teduglutide-label]])

## Safety

### Malignancy
- **Do not start teduglutide in active GI malignancy** — it is a growth factor that can enhance the growth of colonic and other GI polyps and accelerate cancer growth. ([[aga-2022-short-bowel-syndrome]])
- **Expert-review opinion goes further:** avoid teduglutide with **active or recent (arbitrarily, 5 years) malignancy of any location** — 1 liver cancer and 2 lung cancers occurred among teduglutide-treated subjects in the confirmatory phase 3 trial report to the Food and Drug Administration. ([[aga-2022-short-bowel-syndrome]])
- **Label:** no formal contraindication, but at **increased risk for malignancy** use only if benefits outweigh risks. Active **GI malignancy (GI tract, hepatobiliary, pancreatic)** arising on therapy → **discontinue**. Active **non-GI** malignancy → continue or stop on benefit–risk. ([[gattex-2025-teduglutide-label]])

### Adverse Reactions

> [!pitfall] Obstruction and fluid overload are the two that change management
> **Intestinal or stomal obstruction** → temporarily hold teduglutide while the patient is clinically managed; it may be restarted when the obstruction resolves, if clinically indicated. **Fluid overload / congestive heart failure** follows from the enhanced absorption the drug is given for → **adjust parenteral support** and reassess the drug rather than reflexively stopping, especially with underlying cardiovascular disease. ([[gattex-2025-teduglutide-label]])

Adults, reactions ≥10% on teduglutide in the two 24-wk placebo-controlled trials:

| Reaction | Teduglutide 0.05 mg/kg/d (n=77) | Placebo (n=59) |
|---|---|---|
| Abdominal pain | 30% | 22% |
| Nausea | 23% | 20% |
| Upper respiratory tract infection | 21% | 12% |
| Abdominal distension | 20% | 2% |
| Injection-site reaction | 13% | 12% |
| Vomiting | 12% | 10% |
| Fluid overload | 12% | 7% |
| Hypersensitivity | 10% | 7% |

- **Intestinal obstruction/stenosis:** 4% (3/77) at the recommended dose, 9% (3/32) at twice it, **0% on placebo**; onset 1 day to 6 mo. Across the extension studies, 2 patients needed endoscopic dilation and 1 needed surgery.
- **Stoma complications 42% (13/31) vs 14% (3/22) on placebo** among patients who had a stoma.
- **Congestive heart failure:** 2 cases (3%) on teduglutide, 1 serious.
- **Biliary and pancreatic disease:** cholecystitis, cholelithiasis, cholangitis, pancreatitis — the reason for the 6-monthly labs.
- **Enhanced absorption of concomitant oral drugs** — monitor any oral drug requiring titration or with a narrow therapeutic index and consider reducing *its* dose. One patient on prazepam deteriorated to coma during the first week of teduglutide (prazepam level >300 mcg/L), resolving 5 days after both drugs were stopped.
- **Anti-teduglutide antibodies** reach **48% by month 30** in adults (**54% at month 12** in children), with no association with loss of efficacy, adverse events or altered pharmacokinetics — a rising titre is not itself a reason to stop.
- **Breastfeeding is not recommended.** Pregnancy: available data identify no drug-associated risk, set against the real malnutrition risk of untreated SBS.

## Prescribing and Consent
- Intestinal peptide hormones should be prescribed **only by clinicians experienced in diagnosing and managing SBS**, with the ability and facilities to objectively balance benefits against detriments. ([[aga-2022-short-bowel-syndrome]])
- **Informed consent must cover:** probability of weaning from PN; probability of quality-of-life improvement; expected duration of treatment; expected course after cessation; potential adverse effects and risks; cost; and the need for careful, regular monitoring.
- The AGA update and the label give **no treatment duration and no stopping rule for responders** — trial patients stayed on teduglutide even after coming off parenteral support entirely.

## Efficacy

Adults — pivotal 24-wk placebo-controlled trial, parenteral support measured as weekly PN/IV volume:

| Endpoint | Teduglutide 0.05 mg/kg/d (n=43) | Placebo (n=43) |
|---|---|---|
| ≥20% reduction in weekly parenteral support at wk 20 **and** 24 (primary) | 63% (27/43) | 30% (13/43) |
| Mean reduction in weekly parenteral support | 4.4 L/wk (baseline 12.9 L/wk) | 2.3 L/wk (baseline 13.2 L/wk) |
| ≥1 fewer infusion day per week | 54% | 23% |

- **30 mo (open-label extension, 30 completers):** mean reduction **7.55 L/wk (66%)**; **28/30 (93%)** achieved ≥20% reduction; **10 patients came off parenteral support entirely**, after 1.2–15.5 y of dependence.
- **Children, 24 wk (n=26 at 0.05 mg/kg/d):** **69%** achieved ≥20% reduction in parenteral support, **38%** dropped ≥1 infusion day/wk, **12%** achieved enteral autonomy; mean change −23 mL/kg/d (−42%).
