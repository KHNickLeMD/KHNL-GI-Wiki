Glucagon-like peptide-2 (GLP-2) agonists are **intestinotrophic** peptides used to enhance **intestinal adaptation** and facilitate weaning from long-term **[[nutrition-in-hospitalized-patients|parenteral nutrition (PN)]]** in **[[short-bowel-syndrome|short bowel syndrome]] (SBS) / intestinal failure**, including [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]]-associated (typically post-resection [[crohns-disease|Crohn's]]) intestinal failure. Distinct from **[[glp-1-receptor-agonists|glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) agonists]]** (e.g. [[semaglutide]]), which are used for weight/glycemic management — GLP-2 is trophic to the gut, not an incretin weight-loss agent.

## Mechanism
- Native GLP-2 is secreted postprandially from **L cells in the distal ileum and right colon**; its very short half-life was extended in the recombinant molecule, allowing once-daily dosing. ([[aga-2022-short-bowel-syndrome]])
- GLP-2 is trophic to intestinal mucosa → **increases villous height and crypt depth** and **slows gastrointestinal (GI) transit**, augmenting the absorptive surface. ([[aga-2024-diet-nutrition-ibd]])
- Net effect: promotes **intestinal adaptation** (the mucosal remodeling that occurs over ~1–2 years after resection) and reduces the volume of PN/intravenous (IV) support required. ([[aga-2024-diet-nutrition-ibd]], [[aga-2023-ostomies]])

## Agents
- **Teduglutide** — the prototype GLP-2 analogue.
- Other GLP-2 analogues named in the class: **elsiglutide, glepaglutide, apraglutide**. ([[aga-2023-ostomies]])

## Indications
- **Short bowel syndrome / intestinal failure on long-term PN.** The American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) advises that in IBD with SBS, long-term PN be transitioned toward **customized hydration** (IV electrolyte support and/or oral rehydration solutions) and oral intake whenever possible to reduce long-term PN complications; **GLP-2 agonists can facilitate this transition** (Best Practice Advice 8). Intestinal adaptation lets ~50% of adults and ~73% of children eventually wean off PN. ([[aga-2024-diet-nutrition-ibd]])
- **Chronic high [[ostomy-management|ostomy]] output (HOO).** In persistent HOO (>3 wk after stoma creation), GLP-2 analogues sit late in the stepwise medical ladder: bulking → antimotility → antisecretory → anti-inflammatory → **GLP-2 analogues (adaptation-promoting agents)** → surgical reversal. Chronic HOO is more likely with a jejunostomy, **short bowel (<200 cm small intestine remaining)**, or failed post-surgical adaptation. ([[aga-2023-ostomies]])
- Management should be **co-managed with a registered dietitian**, especially in SBS/intestinal failure. ([[aga-2024-diet-nutrition-ibd]])

## Dosing & Monitoring

*Teduglutide only — it is the one GLP-2 analogue approved in the United States (US). The AGA clinical practice updates give no dose; the figures below are from the US prescribing information ([[gattex-2025-teduglutide-label]]).*

### Dose
- **0.05 mg/kg once daily, subcutaneous** — same dose in adults and in children ≥1 y. **Not** intravenous or intramuscular.
- **Renal impairment: halve it to 0.025 mg/kg once daily** when estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) is **<60 mL/min/1.73 m²** (moderate, severe, or end-stage renal disease). Exposure otherwise rises 1.5–2.6×.
- **Hepatic impairment: no adjustment** at [[cirrhosis|Child-Pugh]] A or B. **Not studied in Child-Pugh C.**
- **No contraindication in the label** — but the AGA advises against use in active GI malignancy (see [[#Malignancy]]). Approved ≥1 y of age; not established below 1 y, and the 5 mg kit is not recommended under 10 kg.
- Missed dose → take it the same day; **never two doses in one day**. Rotate injection sites (abdominal quadrants, thighs, arms).
- **Preparation ceiling:** the 5 mg vial reconstitutes with 0.5 mL sterile water to **10 mg/mL**, and **a maximum of 0.38 mL (3.8 mg) can be withdrawn** — so a patient over ~76 kg needs more than one vial per dose. Use within **3 hours**; do not shake or freeze.

### Surveillance — the gastroenterologist's job
Teduglutide is intestinotrophic, so the label builds an endoscopic program around it.

| When | Adults | Children |
|---|---|---|
| **Within 6 mo before starting** | [[colonoscopy]] **and** [[upper-endoscopy]] with removal of any polyps | Fecal occult blood test; scope only if new/unexplained blood |
| **End of year 1** | Repeat both | Colonoscopy/sigmoidoscopy; consider upper endoscopy |
| **Thereafter** | If no polyp found, **no less frequently than every 5 y**; if a polyp was found, standard [[colonoscopy-surveillance\|polyp surveillance]] intervals | Fecal occult blood **annually**; colonoscopy/sigmoidoscopy **every 5 y** |
| **Every 6 mo, all ages** | Bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase, lipase, amylase | Same |

- **A gastrointestinal (GI) cancer diagnosed on therapy → stop the drug.** Non-GI malignancy → continue or stop on benefit–risk.
- A clinically meaningful rise in the 6-monthly labs → image biliary tract, liver or pancreas and reassess continuing.

> [!pitfall] Obstruction and fluid overload are the two that stop the drug
> **Intestinal or stomal obstruction** occurred in **4%** at the recommended dose (**9%** at twice it, **0%** on placebo) — hold teduglutide, manage, restart if it resolves. **Fluid overload** (12% vs 7%) and **congestive heart failure (2 cases, 3%)** follow from the enhanced absorption the drug is given for — **adjust parenteral support** rather than reflexively stopping, and reassess in cardiovascular disease. ([[gattex-2025-teduglutide-label]])

### Malignancy

- **Do not start teduglutide in active GI malignancy** — it is a growth factor that can enhance growth of colonic and other GI polyps and accelerate cancer growth. ([[aga-2022-short-bowel-syndrome]])
  - The label itself lists **no formal contraindication**; it requires **discontinuation** if a GI cancer is diagnosed on therapy, and a benefit–risk decision for **non-GI** malignancy. ([[gattex-2025-teduglutide-label]])
  - **Expert-review opinion goes further:** avoid teduglutide with **active or recent (arbitrarily, 5 years) malignancy of any location** — the confirmatory phase 3 STEPS trial FDA report included **1 liver cancer and 2 lung cancers** among teduglutide-treated subjects. ([[aga-2022-short-bowel-syndrome]])

### Prescribing and Consent

- Intestinal peptide hormones should be prescribed **only by clinicians experienced in SBS management**, with the facilities to objectively evaluate benefit vs detriment. ([[aga-2022-short-bowel-syndrome]])
- **Informed consent must cover:** probability of weaning from PN; probability of quality-of-life improvement; expected duration of treatment; expected course after cessation; potential adverse effects and risks; cost; and the need for careful, regular monitoring.

### Other safety
- **Enhanced absorption of concomitant oral drugs** — monitor any drug needing titration or with a narrow therapeutic index and consider reducing *its* dose. A patient on prazepam progressed to coma in the first week, resolving 5 days after both drugs stopped.
- **Biliary and pancreatic disease:** cholecystitis, cholelithiasis, cholangitis, pancreatitis — the reason for the 6-monthly labs.
- **Stopping also destabilizes fluid balance** — monitor fluid and electrolytes after discontinuation.
- Most common reactions (≥10%, vs placebo): abdominal pain 30/22%, nausea 23/20%, upper respiratory infection 21/12%, **abdominal distension 20/2%**, injection-site reaction 13/12%, vomiting 12/10%, fluid overload 12/7%, hypersensitivity 10/7%. **Stoma complications 42% vs 14%** in patients with a stoma.
- **Anti-teduglutide antibodies reach 48% by month 30** (54% at 12 mo in children) with **no observed loss of efficacy** — a rising titre is not a reason to stop.
- **Breastfeeding is not recommended.** Pregnancy: no drug-associated risk identified, against a real malnutrition risk from untreated SBS.

### Magnitude of benefit
- 24 wk: **63% vs 30%** achieved ≥20% reduction in weekly parenteral support; mean **−4.4 vs −2.3 L/wk**; **54% vs 23%** dropped ≥1 infusion day per week.
- 30 mo: mean reduction **7.55 L/wk (66%)**, and **10 of 30 completers came off parenteral support entirely** after 1.2–15.5 y of dependence.
- The higher 0.1 mg/kg/day dose **missed its primary endpoint** and is not a recommended dosage.
