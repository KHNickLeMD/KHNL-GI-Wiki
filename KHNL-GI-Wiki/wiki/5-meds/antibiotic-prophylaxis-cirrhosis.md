Prophylactic antibiotics in cirrhotic patients with [[upper-gi-bleeding|upper GI bleeding]] aim to prevent bacterial translocation-driven infections ([[spontaneous-bacterial-peritonitis|spontaneous bacterial peritonitis]], bacteremia) that independently worsen short-term mortality.

## Standard Agents

- **Ceftriaxone** 1g IV daily — preferred in Child-Pugh C, hospitalized patients, or settings with high quinolone resistance; 5–7 days per current guidelines
- **Norfloxacin** 400 mg PO BID — alternative in outpatient-tolerant, Child-Pugh A/B patients in low-resistance regions; 7 days per current guidelines

## Duration Controversy

Current guidelines recommend 5–7 days, but a 2025 systematic review ([[albhaisi-2025-prophylactic-antibiotics-cirrhosis]]) found the evidence supporting this duration is low quality. Shorter courses (2–3 days) may be non-inferior for mortality, though bacterial infection rates are 15.2% higher with shorter durations. An individualized approach is reasonable pending higher-quality RCTs.

See [[variceal-upper-gi-bleeding]] for full clinical context.
