Prophylactic antibiotics in [[cirrhosis|cirrhotic]] patients with [[upper-gi-bleeding|upper gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding]] aim to prevent bacterial translocation-driven infections ([[spontaneous-bacterial-peritonitis|spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP)]], bacteremia) that independently worsen short-term mortality. In the American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD) framework this is a form of **SBP primary prophylaxis** ([[aasld-2021-ascites-sbp-hrs]]).

## Indications

### Acute Upper GI Bleeding

- **Give to every patient with cirrhosis and upper GI bleeding, from admission**, whether or not [[upper-endoscopy|endoscopy]] is performed.
  - **Variceal or nonvariceal, with or without [[ascites|ascitic fluid]]** — the risk is bacterial translocation across the whole population, and the infections prevented in trials were not confined to peritonitis *([[acg-2026-preventive-care-cirrhosis|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2026]] Rec 5, conditional, low certainty)*.
  - Other statements agree for the bleeding population at large: *[[asge-2015-antibiotic-prophylaxis|American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2015]] Rec 9 (high quality)*, *[[baveno-vii-2022-portal-hypertension|Baveno VII]] 6.7 (A.1)*, and *[[asge-2026-esophageal-gastric-varices|ASGE 2026]] R3 (conditional, very low certainty)*. *[[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension|Baveno VIII]] 5.10* recommends it at presentation with **acute variceal bleeding (AVB)** (level of evidence [LoE] 1; strength of recommendation [SoR]: strong).
- **Rule out SBP and other active infection first** — prophylaxis is for the uninfected patient; an established infection is treated, not prophylaxed ([[aasld-2021-ascites-sbp-hrs]]).
- **Child-Pugh A — the one subgroup where omission is now on the table.** Baveno VIII 5.10 allows **duration and need to be individualized** by infection risk and cirrhosis severity, noting Child-Pugh A patients **may require only a shorter course or no therapy** (LoE 2; SoR: weak). That is a change: Baveno VII 6.8 agreed the risk of infection and mortality in Child-Pugh A is very low but held that **more prospective studies were needed** before prophylaxis could be avoided (B.2). ACG 2026 still recommends treating all comers and calls for further research in Child-Pugh A, citing an open-label randomized controlled trial (RCT) in Child-Pugh A upper GI bleeding that found **no difference in infections, bleeding control or mortality** with versus without prophylaxis. Child-Turcotte-Pugh (CTP) point table: [[cirrhosis]].

### Non-Bleeding SBP Prophylaxis

*A separate indication — criteria, agents and stopping rules live on [[spontaneous-bacterial-peritonitis]].*

- **Primary** (no prior SBP): ascitic fluid protein **<1.5 g/dL** *plus* renal dysfunction or liver failure ([[aasld-2021-ascites-sbp-hrs]]). Baveno VIII 4.12 **does not recommend long-term primary prophylaxis after a first decompensating event** to prevent further decompensation (LoE 2; SoR: strong), reversing Baveno VII 4.6, which had recommended it in selected high-risk patients (GI haemorrhage, Child-Pugh C with low-protein ascites; B.1).
- **Secondary** (prior SBP): daily norfloxacin, or **oral ciprofloxacin 500 mg/day** where norfloxacin is unavailable ([[aasld-2021-ascites-sbp-hrs]]); stop on recompensation (Baveno VIII 6.22).
- **Neither substitutes for intravenous (IV) ceftriaxone in acute hemorrhage** — AASLD notes direct evidence for the ciprofloxacin regimen is lacking, and quinolones are less effective in patients colonized with multidrug-resistant organisms.

## Regimen

| Item | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Agent | **Ceftriaxone 1 g IV every 24 h** — the antibiotic of choice / most commonly used agent | [[aasld-2021-ascites-sbp-hrs\|AASLD 2021]] Guidance Statement; [[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension\|AASLD 2023]] Guidance Statement (GS) 27; Baveno VII 6.9 (A.1) |
| Class | **Third-generation cephalosporin** generally preferred; no dose given | Baveno VIII 5.11 (LoE 2; SoR: strong) |
| When to start | **Immediately on presentation / from admission** | AASLD 2023; Baveno VII 6.7; Baveno VIII 5.10 |
| Why not a quinolone | **High rates of quinolone resistance**; ceftriaxone also preferred in advanced cirrhosis and in patients already on quinolone prophylaxis | AASLD 2023; Baveno VII 6.9 |
| Allergy / intolerance | "An antibiotic with equivalent microbial coverage" — ASGE 2015 names oral **norfloxacin**, withdrawn from the US market in **2014**. None of these guidelines gives a dose for a substitute in the bleeding setting; take it from local stewardship policy | ASGE 2015 Rec 9; AASLD 2021 |
| Mandatory tailoring | Choice **must** follow local resistance patterns, antimicrobial stewardship policy, and patient allergies | AASLD 2023 GS 27; Baveno VII 6.9 (D.2); Baveno VIII 5.11 |

## Duration

| Source | Duration |
|---|---|
| **[[acg-2026-preventive-care-cirrhosis\|ACG 2026]] — governs (newest)** | **Short course, 2–3 days**, preferred over a longer course *or no antibiotics* (Rec 5; conditional, low certainty) |
| [[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension\|AASLD 2023]] | **2–5 days**; IV antimicrobials until **stability for discharge or 5 days, whichever is shorter**, absent active infection. Ceftriaxone 1 g/24 h **up to 5 days**; stop **once bleeding is controlled** and no active infection (GS 27) |
| [[aasld-2021-ascites-sbp-hrs\|AASLD 2021]] | Until the hemorrhage has resolved **and vasoactive drugs are discontinued** — **maximum 7 days** (Guidance Statement) |
| [[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension\|Baveno VIII]] | **Individualize** duration and need by infection risk and cirrhosis severity; Child-Pugh A may need a shorter course or none (5.10) |
| [[albhaisi-2025-prophylactic-antibiotics-cirrhosis\|Prosty 2025]] (meta-analysis, RCT-tier) | 2–3 d vs 5–7 d: **97.9% probability of noninferiority** for mortality |

- **Default to 2–3 days.** ACG 2026 is the most recent tier-1 statement, and it lands **inside** the AASLD 2023 range rather than contradicting it — the practical effect is to move the default from the middle of 2–5 days to its floor. Between the two AASLD statements, follow the newer 2023 [[portal-hypertension|portal hypertension]] guidance.
- **"5–7 days" is a trial duration, not a recommended course.** It is what the trials AASLD 2021 cited used; AASLD 2021 itself sets **7 days as a ceiling**, not a target, and no current document recommends a 5–7 day course.
- **Keep the AASLD 2021 qualifier** where you use its stopping rule: antibiotics run until the hemorrhage has resolved **and vasoactive drugs are stopped** — not simply until bleeding stops.

> [!pitfall] A shorter course is not a step toward no course
> ACG 2026 Rec 5 compares 2–3 days with "a longer course **or no antibiotics**," so the short course is favoured over giving none. In the same meta-analysis, **1–10 days versus no prophylaxis was not noninferior** ([[acg-2026-preventive-care-cirrhosis]]).

## Evidence for Prophylaxis

**[[asge-2026-esophageal-gastric-varices|ASGE 2026]] (R3) still suggests prophylaxis — conditionally, on very low certainty — and rests the recommendation on *infection*, not survival.** Both ASGE 2026 and ACG 2026 assessed the same 2025 Bayesian meta-analysis (1,322 patients, trials 1995–2023), which is really **two different comparisons**: 12 trials of prophylaxis versus none, and only **2** of a short versus a long course.

| Outcome | No prophylaxis vs prophylaxis (12 trials) | Shorter vs longer cephalosporin (2 trials) | All trials — shorter, incl. none, vs longer (14 trials) |
|---|---|---|---|
| All-cause mortality | **+2.1%** (−1.9 to 6.6); 89.8% | −3.4% (−11.4 to 4.7); 97.9% | +0.9% (−2.6 to 4.9); 97.3% |
| Bacterial infection | **+18.8%** (8.6 to 29.4); 0.5% | −5.0% (−29.1 to 19.0); 81.0% | +15.2% (5.0 to 25.9); 2.4% |
| Early rebleeding | +4.0% (−4.2 to 12.3); 60.6% | −1.5% (−18.2 to 15.2); 79.8% | +2.9% (−4.2 to 10.0); 73.8% |

- Each cell: **risk difference (RD), 95% credible interval, then the probability of noninferiority** against a prespecified **5% margin**. A **positive RD is worse with the first-named (shorter or absent) strategy** ([[albhaisi-2025-prophylactic-antibiotics-cirrhosis]]).
- **ASGE 2026 assessed the prophylaxis-vs-none comparison** (first column): certainty **low** for mortality, **very low** for infection and rebleeding; judgment favours **prophylaxis** for infection, and **neither** direction for mortality or rebleeding.
- **Only infection survives scrutiny.** Omitting prophylaxis had a **0.5%** probability of noninferiority for bacterial infection — that is the effect holding the recommendation up. Across all 14 trials a *shorter* course also cost infections (+15.2%), which ACG weighed against cost and antimicrobial resistance and judged acceptable, noting clinicians **may extend therapy in patients at higher infection risk**.
- **The mortality signal is fading with time, not holding.** Noninferiority of *no* prophylaxis for mortality was **89.8%** across those 12 trials; splitting the meta-analysis by era of publication gives **98.9% after 2004 versus 72.4% before 2004** — driven by both a lower baseline mortality (**15.5% → 4.3%**) and smaller between-group differences (**3.4% → −1.4%**) — and **the most recent RCT (2023) showed no mortality benefit at all**. ASGE judged the evidence insufficient to materially alter current practice, which is a different statement from judging the practice effective.
- **Why the certainty is so low:** several included trials were unblinded, several did not exclude patients with active infection at randomization, a priori sample-size calculations were inadequate in all of them, and none reported loss to follow-up ([[asge-2026-esophageal-gastric-varices]]).

> [!pitfall] Carry the *C difficile* counterweight
> Prophylaxis is now usually a third-generation cephalosporin, a class tied to **increasing rates of *[[clostridioides-difficile|Clostridioides difficile]]* infection**. Weigh the possible decreased benefit against that risk in each patient ([[asge-2026-esophageal-gastric-varices]]).

- **Bottom line:** keep giving prophylaxis at presentation — every current guideline still says so — but the honest framing is *fewer infections, no demonstrated survival benefit in the modern era, and a real antibiotic-harm counterweight*. That is also why Baveno VIII now permits individualizing duration and need by Child-Pugh class. Whether prophylaxis helps this population at all is on ASGE's own future-directions list.
