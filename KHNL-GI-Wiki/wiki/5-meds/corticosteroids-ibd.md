Corticosteroids in [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]] are **induction agents only**. Every ingested guideline that addresses maintenance recommends **against** them — systemic, budesonide, and topical alike. The practical decision is *which* formulation, and that is set by **disease (Crohn's disease [CD] vs ulcerative colitis [UC]), disease extent, and severity**, because the two budesonide products deliver drug to different segments of bowel.

## Formulations — which steroid for which disease

| Formulation | Delivery | Use | Dose | Maintenance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Budesonide CIR** (controlled ileal release) | Terminal ileum / right colon | **Mildly to moderately active ileocecal [[crohns-disease\|CD]]** — induction | **9 mg orally (PO) once daily × 8–12 wk** | **No** |
| **Budesonide MMX** (multimatrix) | Colon | **Mildly to moderately active [[ulcerative-colitis\|UC]]** — induction, any extent, and left-sided UC failing 5-aminosalicylate (5-ASA) | **9 mg PO daily** | **No** |
| **Prednisone** (oral systemic) | Systemic | Moderate–severe CD; UC failing 5-ASA; moderate–severe UC | **40–60 mg/day**, then **taper 5 mg/wk** | **No** |
| **Methylprednisolone intravenous (IV)** | Systemic | Severe/fulminant CD (hospitalized); acute severe UC (ASUC) | CD **40–60 mg/day**; ASUC **60 mg/day total** | **No** |
| **Hydrocortisone IV** | Systemic | Acute severe UC | **100 mg three times daily (TID) or four times daily (QID)** | **No** |
| **Rectal corticosteroid** (suppository, foam, enema) | Rectum / left colon | UC proctitis or left-sided colitis | Route by extent — suppository for proctitis, foam/enema for left-sided | **No** |

- Budesonide CIR's advantage is **first-pass hepatic metabolism** — systemic bioavailability ≈**10%–20%**, so fewer systemic steroid effects than prednisone. The trade-off is that its release site makes it **ineffective for colonic CD**. [[acg-2025-crohns]]
- **The two budesonides are not interchangeable.** CIR releases in the ileum (ileocecal CD); MMX releases across the colon (UC). Neither is a systemic steroid substitute in severe disease.

## Crohn's Disease

*Recommendation numbers, strength, and evidence quality below are [[acg-2025-crohns|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2025]]'s own unless another source is named.*

| Recommendation | Strength | Quality |
|---|---|---|
| **Rec 5** — Budesonide CIR **9 mg/day for induction** in **mildly to moderately active ileocecal** CD | Strong | Moderate |
| **Rec 6** — **Against budesonide for maintenance** of remission in ileocecal CD | Strong | Low |
| **Rec 7** — **Oral corticosteroids for short-term induction** in **moderate–severe** CD | Strong | Low |
| **Rec 9** — [[thiopurines\|azathioprine (AZA)/6-mercaptopurine (6-MP)]] for **maintenance after steroid induction** | Conditional | Low |
| **Rec 11** — [[methotrexate\|Methotrexate]] **≤25 mg subcutaneously (SQ)/intramuscularly (IM) weekly** for maintenance after steroid induction | Conditional | Moderate |

**[[acg-2018-crohns|ACG 2018]] adds (older guideline, non-conflicting):**

- Conventional corticosteroids **do not consistently achieve mucosal healing and should be used sparingly** *(conditional, low)* — the reason steroids are a bridge and not a strategy.
- **IV corticosteroids** for **severe or fulminant** CD *(conditional, moderate)*.
- Once remission is induced with corticosteroids, a thiopurine or methotrexate **should be considered** *(strong, moderate)*.
- **Budesonide should not be used to maintain remission beyond 4 months** *(strong recommendation)* — a specific stop-date for the drug most often continued by drift.
- Corticosteroids are **not effective for maintenance** of medically induced remission and should not be used long-term *(strong, moderate)*.

**Sequencing:** steroids induce, something else maintains. [[acg-2025-crohns|ACG 2025]] Rec 3 suggests **against requiring failure of conventional therapy before advanced therapy** *(conditional, low)* — a steroid course is not a prerequisite for a biologic. Full positioning on [[crohns-disease]].

**Course length:** the prednisone taper should bring the **total course to ≤3 months** in moderate–severe CD. [[acg-2018-crohns]]

**Ineffective in fistulizing CD** — mesalamine and corticosteroids do not treat perianal fistulas. [[acg-2025-crohns]]

**Before surgery:** if on steroids preoperatively, taper toward **≤prednisone 20 mg/day** to reduce postoperative infection. [[acg-2025-crohns]]

## Ulcerative Colitis

*Recommendation numbers, strength, and evidence quality below are [[acg-2025-uc|ACG 2025]]'s own unless another source is named.*

| Recommendation | Strength | Quality |
|---|---|---|
| **Rec 8** — **Topical (rectal) corticosteroids** for **proctitis or left-sided colitis** | Conditional | Very low |
| **Rec 9** — Rectal [[mesalamine-5-asa\|5-ASA]] enemas **≥1 g/day preferred over rectal steroids** for induction in proctitis/left-sided colitis | Strong | Moderate |
| **Rec 11** — **Budesonide MMX 9 mg/day** for **left-sided** UC intolerant of or nonresponsive to **oral + rectal 5-ASA** | Strong | Moderate |
| **Rec 13** — **Oral systemic corticosteroids** for UC **failing 5-ASA** therapy | Strong | Low |
| **Rec 16** — **Add budesonide MMX 9 mg/day** for **mild–moderate UC of any extent** not responding to 5-ASA | Strong | Moderate |
| **Rec 20** — **Against systemic, budesonide MMX, *or* topical corticosteroids for maintenance** | Strong | Moderate |
| **Rec 21** — **Oral budesonide MMX** for **moderately active** UC | Strong | Moderate |
| **Rec 22** — **Oral systemic corticosteroids** for **moderate–severe** UC induction | Strong | Low |
| **Rec 35** — **Against systemic corticosteroids for maintenance** | Strong | Moderate |
| **Rec 36** — [[thiopurines\|Thiopurines]] for maintenance **after steroid induction** | Conditional | Low |
| **Rec 50** — **IV methylprednisolone 60 mg/day OR hydrocortisone 100 mg TID/QID** for acute severe UC | Strong | Low |

**The severity gate for budesonide MMX:** it is recommended for **mildly to moderately** active UC. In **severely active** disease use systemic corticosteroids instead — budesonide MMX is not a substitute.

**What "nonresponsive to 5-ASA" means:** [[acg-2019-uc|ACG 2019]] Rec 9 quantifies the adequate 5-ASA trial that must fail before budesonide MMX — **oral at least 2 g/day and rectal at least 1 g/day**. [[acg-2025-uc|ACG 2025]] Rec 11 states the same recommendation without repeating the doses.

**Steroids may be skipped:** oral prednisone is a bridge only and **may be omitted entirely** when another effective induction strategy is planned. [[acg-2025-uc]]

### Acute Severe UC — intravenous corticosteroids

- **IV methylprednisolone 60 mg/day total, or hydrocortisone 100 mg three or four times daily** *(Rec 50 — Strong, Low)*. [[acg-2025-uc]]
- **Day 3 is the decision point.** Inadequate response to IV corticosteroids → **medical rescue with [[anti-tnf-agents\|infliximab]] or [[calcineurin-inhibitors\|cyclosporine]]**. [[acg-2019-uc|ACG 2019]] Rec 43 gives the window as **3–5 days** *(strong, moderate)*. The objective day-3 nonresponder criteria (Oxford/Travis index), the endoscopic predictors, and the rescue algorithm live on [[ulcerative-colitis]].
- **Adequate response:** continue IV steroids, then transition to an oral steroid plus advanced therapy.
- **AGA 2026 dose ceiling and stop rule** ([[aga-2026-inpatient-ibd]]): methylprednisolone **40–60 mg/day IV or equivalent** (bolus, split, or infusion); **>60 mg/day adds no benefit and increases adverse events**. Continuing IV corticosteroids **beyond 7 days in a nonresponder** adds no benefit and increases complications and mortality.
- **C-reactive protein (CRP) trigger:** hospitalized, **≥6 bloody bowel movements/day + ≥1 systemic sign**, and **CRP ≥1.2 mg/dL (≥12 mg/L)** → start IV corticosteroids ([[aga-2026-inpatient-ibd]]).
- Response can be tracked earlier than day 3 by [[intestinal-ultrasound|intestinal ultrasound]] — a **≥20% reduction in bowel wall thickness within 48 h of IV steroids** predicts steroid response. [[acg-2025-uc]]

## Induction Only — the not-for-maintenance rule

The "against maintenance" recommendations are among the strongest in both guidelines, and they cover **every route**:

- **UC:** against **systemic, budesonide MMX, and topical** corticosteroids for maintenance *(Rec 20 — Strong, Moderate; restated as Rec 35 for systemic steroids)*. [[acg-2025-uc]]
- **CD:** against **budesonide** for maintenance of ileocecal CD *(Rec 6 — Strong, Low)*; conventional corticosteroids are not effective for maintenance and should not be used long-term *(strong, moderate)*; **budesonide not beyond 4 months** *(strong)*. [[acg-2025-crohns]] [[acg-2018-crohns]]
- The corollary is that a steroid course **obligates a maintenance plan** — thiopurine or methotrexate after steroid induction in CD *(Recs 9, 11)*, thiopurines after steroid induction in UC *(Rec 36)*, or an advanced therapy.
- **The endpoint is steroid-free:** [[acg-2025-uc|ACG 2025]] Rec 4 recommends treating to endoscopic improvement (Mayo endoscopic subscore 0–1) **specifically to increase steroid-free remission** and prevent hospitalization and surgery *(Strong, Moderate)*. **Corticosteroid-free remission** = symptomatic + endoscopic remission with **no corticosteroid for a sustained period, usually >12 weeks** *(Key Concept 11)*.

## Steroid Dependence and Steroid Refractoriness

| Term | Definition as given | What it triggers |
|---|---|---|
| **Steroid-dependent (CD)** | **Unable to taper without recrudescence of symptoms** | Start a [[thiopurines\|thiopurine]] or methotrexate **with or without [[anti-tnf-agents\|anti-tumor necrosis factor (TNF)]] therapy** — [[acg-2018-crohns\|ACG 2018]] Rec 45 *(strong, moderate)*; in practice, advance to a biologic |
| **Steroid-refractory (acute severe UC)** | **Inadequate response to IV corticosteroids by day 3** (window stated as **3–5 days** in [[acg-2019-uc\|ACG 2019]]) | Medical rescue with **infliximab or [[calcineurin-inhibitors\|cyclosporine]]** *(strong, moderate)* |
| **Corticosteroid-free remission (UC)** | Symptomatic + endoscopic remission with **no corticosteroid for a sustained period, usually >12 weeks** | The treatment target |

- The ACG Crohn's and UC guidelines define steroid dependence by **behaviour on taper**, not by a number of weeks of exposure or a threshold daily dose; no day-count or milligram criterion is attached to the term in these documents, and there is **no ACG definition of steroid refractoriness in Crohn's disease** parallel to the ASUC day-3 rule. In CD, disease persisting **despite conventional corticosteroids and/or advanced therapies** is classified as **severe/fulminant** (see [[crohns-disease]]).

**The numeric criteria come from ECCO** ([[ecco-2017-crohns-part-1]]) — the only ingested source that attaches doses and time windows to these terms in Crohn's disease:

| Term | ECCO criterion |
|---|---|
| **Steroid-refractory CD** | **Active disease despite prednisolone up to 1 mg/kg/day for 4 weeks** |
| **Steroid-dependent CD** | **Either** (i) **unable to reduce below prednisolone 10 mg/day — or budesonide 3 mg/day — within 3 months of starting steroids**, without recurrent active disease, **or** (ii) **relapse within 3 months of stopping steroids** |

- **Combination rule:** either limb alone makes the patient steroid-dependent. The definition requires that **total steroid duration not exceed 3 months** before the prednisolone-10 mg/day threshold is reached. **The aim is complete withdrawal.**
- **Exclude disease-specific complications first** — assess refractoriness or dependence only after an abscess, stricture or infection has been ruled out.
- **"Chronic active disease" is to be avoided** as ambiguous; use steroid-refractory or steroid-dependent.
- **When is a steroid-sparing agent due?** ECCO cannot define "infrequently relapsing disease" from trial evidence, but offers **more than one full course of systemic steroids per year** as the threshold for introducing one.

## Adverse Effects and Monitoring

**Bone — the best-quantified harm.** [[acg-2025-ibd-preventive-care]]

| Exposure | Fracture risk |
|---|---|
| **Long-term corticosteroid use** | **30%–50%** of patients develop fractures; risk tracks **daily** dose, not cumulative dose |
| **Prednisone 3–10 mg/day** | Already raises fracture risk |
| **As low as prednisone 2.5 mg/day** | **1.55-fold** **spinal** fracture risk |
| **>7.5 mg/day** | **5-fold** higher spine/hip fracture risk |
| **Onset** | Fractures appear **3–6 months** after starting |
| **After discontinuation** | Risk falls as early as **3 months** after stopping; back to baseline within **1–2 years** |

- Background IBD risk (independent of steroids): overall fracture **relative risk (RR) 1.38 (95% confidence interval [CI] 1.11–1.73)**; vertebral fracture **RR 2.26 (95% CI 1.04–4.90)**.
- **Rec 11 — adults with IBD and conventional bone mineral density (BMD) risk factors: dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA) at the time of diagnosis and periodically** *(Conditional, very low)*.
- **Osteoprotection is advisable once therapy is likely to last >6 weeks**; most advocate calcium and vitamin D for all patients. **Budesonide costs less bone than prednisolone** — mean BMD change **−1.04% vs −3.84% over 2 years** (p = 0.0084). [[ecco-2017-crohns-part-1]]

**Adverse effects fall into three groups by timing** ([[ecco-2017-crohns-part-1]]) — which group you are in decides what to look for:

| Timing | Effects |
|---|---|
| **Early** (supraphysiologic induction doses) | Acne, moon face, oedema, skin striae, sleep and mood disturbance, dyspepsia, glucose intolerance |
| **Prolonged use** (usually >12 weeks, sometimes less) | Posterior subcapsular cataract, osteoporosis, osteonecrosis of the femoral head, myopathy, susceptibility to infection |
| **Withdrawal** | Acute adrenal insufficiency from sudden cessation, **pseudo-rheumatism**, raised intracranial pressure |

> [!pitfall] A post-taper flare that is really steroid withdrawal
> Steroid withdrawal produces **myalgia, malaise and arthralgia that mimic a recrudescence of Crohn's disease**. Confirm objective inflammation before calling it a relapse and re-escalating ([[ecco-2017-crohns-part-1]]).

- **Postoperative sepsis** is increased by steroids — odds ratio **3.7 (95% CI 1.2–11.0)** in IBD; **not seen with [[thiopurines|thiopurines]]** (OR 1.7, 95% CI 0.7–9.6). Steroids combined with other immunosuppressants increase serious infections.

**Infection and vaccination.** Corticosteroids are immune-modifying therapy: **no live vaccines** (live attenuated influenza, measles-mumps-rubella [MMR], dengue, yellow fever) while on immune-modifying therapy, and **vaccinate before starting** it when possible. Household contacts may receive live vaccines with precautions. [[acg-2025-ibd-preventive-care]]

**Perioperative.** Taper toward **≤prednisone 20 mg/day** before surgery to reduce postoperative infection. [[acg-2025-crohns]]

**Mucosal healing.** Conventional corticosteroids **do not consistently achieve mucosal healing** — symptomatic response on steroids does not equal remission by the [[acg-2025-uc|treat-to-target]] endpoint. [[acg-2018-crohns]]

## Pregnancy and Lactation

*Preconception optimization, disease-activity monitoring, trimester plan, mode of delivery and the full drug grid: [[ibd-in-pregnancy]]. All statements here from [[global-consensus-2025-pregnancy-ibd]].*

- **Use corticosteroids in pregnancy when clinically necessary, with appropriate monitoring** (Conditional, low). Appropriate from preconception through lactation — but **minimize use and employ steroid-sparing therapy**, the same induction-only logic as outside pregnancy.
- **Why to minimize:** registry data link corticosteroid exposure in pregnancy to gestational diabetes, preterm birth, low birth weight, small size for gestational age, intrauterine growth restriction and neonatal intensive care unit admission; **late (second- or third-trimester) use was associated with serious infant infections at 9 and 12 months**.
- **Give routine calcium and vitamin D to every pregnant woman on corticosteroids** to prevent steroid-associated osteopenia (the general bone rules are in [[#Adverse Effects and Monitoring]]).
- **Budesonide MMX 9 mg once daily may be considered** in pregnancy for mild-moderate UC not responding to oral plus topical 5-ASA — only **4 reported pregnancy cases**, so the evidence is anecdotal.
- **Breastfeeding is compatible.** Worst-case prednisone/prednisolone milk amounts are **<100 mcg/day even at maternal doses up to 120 mg/day**, so **the old advice to wait 4 hours after a dose is unnecessary**; budesonide's systemic relative infant dose is **~0.27%**.

## Corticosteroids in Autoimmune Hepatitis

The same two drugs, a different rule set — and the one place where **budesonide is explicitly contraindicated by liver physiology**. [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis|European Association for the Study of the Liver (EASL) 2025]] is the newer guideline and governs; full regimens and response definitions on [[autoimmune-hepatitis]].

| Setting in [[autoimmune-hepatitis\|autoimmune hepatitis (AIH)]] | EASL 2025 | Strength, level of evidence (LoE) |
|---|---|---|
| **Induction** | Predniso(lo)ne **≥0.5 mg/kg/day**, up to **1 mg/kg/day** in more severe and advanced disease, with **[[thiopurines\|AZA]]** or **mycophenolate mofetil 1.5–2 g/day** | Strong, LoE 2 |
| **Taper** | Individualised by complete biochemical response; rapid tapering (e.g. 5–10 mg every 1–2 weeks) is desirable but must follow response | Strong, LoE 4 |
| **Maintenance** | AZA or mycophenolate mofetil alone or with **predniso(lo)ne ≤5 mg/day**. Low-dose predniso(lo)ne **monotherapy** only in mild disease with complete biochemical response and intolerance of **both** AZA and mycophenolate mofetil | Strong, LoE 2 / weak, LoE 3 |
| **Budesonide** | **Not first line; contraindicated in [[cirrhosis\|cirrhosis]]** (also because of the high risk of portal vein thrombosis). A switch **may be suggested** for corticosteroid side effects in **non-cirrhotic, predniso(lo)ne-dependent** patients | Strong, LoE 2 / weak, LoE 3 |
| **Acute severe AIH** (icteric, international normalized ratio [INR] ≥1.5 but <2, no [[hepatic-encephalopathy\|hepatic encephalopathy (HE)]], no chronic lesions on liver biopsy) without acute liver failure or acute-on-chronic liver failure | Predniso(lo)ne **0.5–1 mg/kg/day** or IV methylprednisolone at an equivalent dose; **0.5 mg/kg/day** is the pragmatic start. **No improvement after 3–7 days → refer to a liver transplant centre** | Strong, LoE 3 |
| **Decompensated cirrhosis** with activity (raised aminotransferases and/or modified hepatic activity index ≥4) | Consider corticosteroids (median predniso(lo)ne 20–30 mg/day in reported series) | Strong, LoE 4 |
| **Bone and eyes** | DEXA in all adults at treatment start; calcium and vitamin D on long-term corticosteroids; intraocular pressure at baseline and periodically | Strong, LoE 3 (DEXA; calcium/vitamin D) |

**[[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis|American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD) 2020]]** (older; differences flagged):

| Recommendation | Strength | Certainty |
|---|---|---|
| **Rec 1** — In children and adults with AIH **without cirrhosis or acute severe AIH**: **budesonide 3 mg TID + AZA** (adults 50–150 mg/day; children 1–2 mg/kg/day) **OR prednisone/prednisolone + AZA** are **equally acceptable** first-line therapies — ⚠ superseded by EASL 2025 (budesonide not first line) | Conditional | Low |
| **Rec 2** — In patients **with cirrhosis OR acute severe AIH** (INR >1.5 <2, no HE): do **NOT** use budesonide | Conditional | Very low |

- **Why budesonide fails in cirrhosis:** portal-systemic shunting **bypasses the first-pass effect**, eliminating the systemic side-effect advantage that is its entire rationale.
- **Acute severe AIH** (jaundice + INR >1.5 <2, no HE, no previously recognized liver disease): treatment trial of **prednisone or prednisolone alone**; no improvement in laboratory tests, or clinical worsening, within **1–2 weeks** → evaluate for [[liver-transplantation|liver transplantation]]. ⚠ EASL 2025 makes the call earlier — **3–7 days** (table above).
- **AIH-related [[acute-liver-failure|acute liver failure (ALF)]]** (INR ≥2 + HE within 26 weeks of onset): evaluate directly for transplantation. Budesonide's role in acute severe AIH or ALF is unknown → not recommended.
- After relapse following drug withdrawal, retreatment achieves laboratory resolution in **94%**; treatment-free remission at ≥3 years' follow-up is **19–40%**.
- **Monitoring on glucocorticoids:** **DEXA at baseline in adults with osteoporosis risk factors, repeated every 2–3 years of continuous glucocorticoids**; **25-hydroxyvitamin D** at diagnosis and annually; elemental calcium 1,000–1,200 mg/day + vitamin D at least 400–800 IU/day while on glucocorticoids. Check **thiopurine methyltransferase (TPMT)** before AZA, **hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg)/hepatitis B core antibody (anti-HBc)** before immunosuppression, and give vaccines beforehand.
- AASLD 2020 states the prednisone/prednisolone regimen as a drug pairing without printing a starting dose or taper schedule; the budesonide dose (3 mg TID) is given.
- Both guidelines contraindicate budesonide in cirrhosis; only AASLD 2020 keeps it first line.
