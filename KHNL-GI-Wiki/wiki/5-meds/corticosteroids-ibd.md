Corticosteroids in [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|IBD]] are **induction agents only**. Every ingested guideline that addresses maintenance recommends **against** them — systemic, budesonide, and topical alike. The practical decision is *which* formulation, and that is set by **disease (CD vs UC), disease extent, and severity**, because the two budesonide products deliver drug to different segments of bowel.

## Formulations — which steroid for which disease

| Formulation | Delivery | Use | Dose | Maintenance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Budesonide CIR** (controlled ileal release) | Terminal ileum / right colon | **Mildly to moderately active ileocecal [[crohns-disease\|CD]]** — induction | **9 mg PO once daily × 8–12 wk** | **No** |
| **Budesonide MMX** (multimatrix) | Colon | **Mildly to moderately active [[ulcerative-colitis\|UC]]** — induction, any extent, and left-sided UC failing 5-ASA | **9 mg PO daily** | **No** |
| **Prednisone** (oral systemic) | Systemic | Moderate–severe CD; UC failing 5-ASA; moderate–severe UC | **40–60 mg/day**, then **taper 5 mg/wk** | **No** |
| **Methylprednisolone IV** | Systemic | Severe/fulminant CD (hospitalized); acute severe UC | CD **40–60 mg/day**; ASUC **60 mg/day total** | **No** |
| **Hydrocortisone IV** | Systemic | Acute severe UC | **100 mg TID or QID** | **No** |
| **Rectal corticosteroid** (suppository, foam, enema) | Rectum / left colon | UC proctitis or left-sided colitis | Route by extent — suppository for proctitis, foam/enema for left-sided | **No** |

- Budesonide CIR's advantage is **first-pass hepatic metabolism** — systemic bioavailability ≈**10%–20%**, so fewer systemic steroid effects than prednisone. The trade-off is that its release site makes it **ineffective for colonic CD**. [[acg-2025-crohns]]
- **The two budesonides are not interchangeable.** CIR releases in the ileum (ileocecal CD); MMX releases across the colon (UC). Neither is a systemic steroid substitute in severe disease.

## Crohn's Disease

*Recommendation numbers, strength, and evidence quality below are [[acg-2025-crohns|ACG 2025]]'s own unless another source is named.*

| Recommendation | Strength | Quality |
|---|---|---|
| **Rec 5** — Budesonide CIR **9 mg/day for induction** in **mildly to moderately active ileocecal** CD | Strong | Moderate |
| **Rec 6** — **Against budesonide for maintenance** of remission in ileocecal CD | Strong | Low |
| **Rec 7** — **Oral corticosteroids for short-term induction** in **moderate–severe** CD | Strong | Low |
| **Rec 9** — [[thiopurines\|AZA/6-MP]] for **maintenance after steroid induction** | Conditional | Low |
| **Rec 11** — Methotrexate **≤25 mg SQ/IM weekly** for maintenance after steroid induction | Conditional | Moderate |

**[[acg-2018-crohns|ACG 2018]] adds (older guideline, non-conflicting):**

- Conventional corticosteroids **do not consistently achieve mucosal healing and should be used sparingly** *(conditional, low)* — the reason steroids are a bridge and not a strategy.
- **IV corticosteroids** for **severe or fulminant** CD *(conditional, moderate)*.
- Once remission is induced with corticosteroids, a thiopurine or methotrexate **should be considered** *(strong, moderate)*.
- **Budesonide should not be used to maintain remission beyond 4 months** *(strong recommendation)* — a specific stop-date for the drug most often continued by drift.
- Corticosteroids are **not effective for maintenance** of medically induced remission and should not be used long-term *(strong, moderate)*.

**Sequencing:** steroids induce, something else maintains. [[acg-2025-crohns|ACG 2025]] Rec 3 suggests **against requiring failure of conventional therapy before advanced therapy** *(conditional, low)* — a steroid course is not a prerequisite for a biologic. Full positioning on [[crohns-disease]].

**Ineffective in fistulizing CD** — mesalamine and corticosteroids do not treat perianal fistulas. [[acg-2025-crohns]]

**Before surgery:** if on steroids preoperatively, taper toward **≤prednisone 20 mg/day** to reduce postoperative infection. [[acg-2025-crohns]]

## Ulcerative Colitis

*Recommendation numbers, strength, and evidence quality below are [[acg-2025-uc|ACG 2025]]'s own unless another source is named.*

| Recommendation | Strength | Quality |
|---|---|---|
| **Rec 8** — **Topical (rectal) corticosteroids** for **proctitis or left-sided colitis** | Conditional | Very low |
| **Rec 9** — Rectal [[mesalamine-5-asa\|5-ASA]] enemas **≥1 g/day preferred over rectal steroids** for induction in proctitis/left-sided colitis | Strong | Moderate |
| **Rec 11** — **Budesonide MMX 9 mg/day** for **left-sided** UC intolerant of or nonresponsive to **oral + rectal 5-ASA** | Strong | Moderate |
| **Rec 13** — **Oral systemic corticosteroids** for UC **failing 5-ASA** therapy | Strong | Low |
| **Rec 16** — **Add budesonide MMX 9 mg/day** for **mild–moderate UC of any extent** not responding to 5-ASA | Strong | Moderate |
| **Rec 20** — **Against systemic, budesonide MMX, *or* topical corticosteroids for maintenance** | Strong | Moderate |
| **Rec 21** — **Oral budesonide MMX** for **moderately active** UC | Strong | Moderate |
| **Rec 22** — **Oral systemic corticosteroids** for **moderate–severe** UC induction | Strong | Low |
| **Rec 35** — **Against systemic corticosteroids for maintenance** | Strong | Moderate |
| **Rec 36** — [[thiopurines\|Thiopurines]] for maintenance **after steroid induction** | Conditional | Low |
| **Rec 50** — **IV methylprednisolone 60 mg/day OR hydrocortisone 100 mg TID/QID** for acute severe UC | Strong | Low |

**The severity gate for budesonide MMX:** it is recommended for **mildly to moderately** active UC. In **severely active** disease use systemic corticosteroids instead — budesonide MMX is not a substitute.

**What "nonresponsive to 5-ASA" means:** [[acg-2019-uc|ACG 2019]] Rec 9 quantifies the adequate 5-ASA trial that must fail before budesonide MMX — **oral at least 2 g/day and rectal at least 1 g/day**. [[acg-2025-uc|ACG 2025]] Rec 11 states the same recommendation without repeating the doses.

**Steroids may be skipped:** oral prednisone is a bridge only and **may be omitted entirely** when another effective induction strategy is planned. [[acg-2025-uc]]

### Acute Severe UC — intravenous corticosteroids

- **IV methylprednisolone 60 mg/day total, or hydrocortisone 100 mg three or four times daily** *(Rec 50 — Strong, Low)*. [[acg-2025-uc]]
- **Day 3 is the decision point.** Inadequate response to IV corticosteroids → **medical rescue with [[anti-tnf-agents\|infliximab]] or [[calcineurin-inhibitors\|cyclosporine]]**. [[acg-2019-uc|ACG 2019]] Rec 43 gives the window as **3–5 days** *(strong, moderate)*. The objective day-3 nonresponder criteria (Oxford/Travis index), the endoscopic predictors, and the rescue algorithm live on [[ulcerative-colitis]].
- **Adequate response:** continue IV steroids, then transition to an oral steroid plus advanced therapy.
- Response can be tracked earlier than day 3 by [[intestinal-ultrasound|intestinal ultrasound]] — a **≥20% reduction in bowel wall thickness within 48 h of IV steroids** predicts steroid response. [[acg-2025-uc]]

## Induction Only — the not-for-maintenance rule

The "against maintenance" recommendations are among the strongest in both guidelines, and they cover **every route**:

- **UC:** against **systemic, budesonide MMX, and topical** corticosteroids for maintenance *(Rec 20 — Strong, Moderate; restated as Rec 35 for systemic steroids)*. [[acg-2025-uc]]
- **CD:** against **budesonide** for maintenance of ileocecal CD *(Rec 6 — Strong, Low)*; conventional corticosteroids are not effective for maintenance and should not be used long-term *(strong, moderate)*; **budesonide not beyond 4 months** *(strong)*. [[acg-2025-crohns]] [[acg-2018-crohns]]
- The corollary is that a steroid course **obligates a maintenance plan** — thiopurine or methotrexate after steroid induction in CD *(Recs 9, 11)*, thiopurines after steroid induction in UC *(Rec 36)*, or an advanced therapy.
- **The endpoint is steroid-free:** [[acg-2025-uc|ACG 2025]] Rec 4 recommends treating to endoscopic improvement (Mayo endoscopic subscore 0–1) **specifically to increase steroid-free remission** and prevent hospitalization and surgery *(Strong, Moderate)*. **Corticosteroid-free remission** = symptomatic + endoscopic remission with **no corticosteroid for a sustained period, usually >12 weeks** *(Key Concept 11)*.

## Steroid Dependence and Steroid Refractoriness

| Term | Definition as given | What it triggers |
|---|---|---|
| **Steroid-dependent (CD)** | **Unable to taper without recrudescence of symptoms** | Start a [[thiopurines\|thiopurine]] or methotrexate **with or without [[anti-tnf-agents\|anti-TNF]] therapy** — [[acg-2018-crohns\|ACG 2018]] Rec 45 *(strong, moderate)*; in practice, advance to a biologic |
| **Steroid-refractory (acute severe UC)** | **Inadequate response to IV corticosteroids by day 3** (window stated as **3–5 days** in [[acg-2019-uc\|ACG 2019]]) | Medical rescue with **infliximab or [[calcineurin-inhibitors\|cyclosporine]]** *(strong, moderate)* |
| **Corticosteroid-free remission (UC)** | Symptomatic + endoscopic remission with **no corticosteroid for a sustained period, usually >12 weeks** | The treatment target |

- The ACG Crohn's and UC guidelines define steroid dependence by **behaviour on taper**, not by a number of weeks of exposure or a threshold daily dose; no day-count or milligram criterion is attached to the term in these documents.
- There is **no ACG definition of steroid refractoriness in Crohn's disease** parallel to the ASUC day-3 rule; in CD, disease that persists **despite conventional corticosteroids and/or advanced therapies** is classified as **severe/fulminant** (see [[crohns-disease]]).

## Adverse Effects and Monitoring

**Bone — the best-quantified harm.** [[acg-2025-ibd-preventive-care]]

| Exposure | Fracture risk |
|---|---|
| **Long-term corticosteroid use** | **30%–50%** fracture risk |
| **As low as prednisone 2.5 mg/day** | Increased **spinal** fracture risk |
| **High-dose, >7.5 mg/day** | **5-fold** higher spine/hip fracture risk |
| **After discontinuation** | Risk decreases **3–6 months** after stopping |

- Background IBD risk (independent of steroids): overall fracture **RR 1.38 (95% CI 1.11–1.73)**; vertebral fracture **RR 2.26 (95% CI 1.04–4.90)**.
- **Rec 11 — adults with IBD and conventional BMD risk factors: DEXA at the time of diagnosis and periodically** *(Conditional, very low)*.

**Infection and vaccination.** Corticosteroids are immune-modifying therapy: **no live vaccines** (live attenuated influenza, MMR, dengue, yellow fever) while on immune-modifying therapy, and **vaccinate before starting** it when possible. Household contacts may receive live vaccines with precautions. [[acg-2025-ibd-preventive-care]]

**Perioperative.** Taper toward **≤prednisone 20 mg/day** before surgery to reduce postoperative infection. [[acg-2025-crohns]]

**Mucosal healing.** Conventional corticosteroids **do not consistently achieve mucosal healing** — symptomatic response on steroids does not equal remission by the [[acg-2025-uc|treat-to-target]] endpoint. [[acg-2018-crohns]]

## Corticosteroids in Autoimmune Hepatitis

The same two drugs, a different rule set — and the one place where **budesonide is explicitly contraindicated by liver physiology**. [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

| Recommendation | Strength | Certainty |
|---|---|---|
| **Rec 1** — In children and adults with [[autoimmune-hepatitis\|AIH]] **without [[cirrhosis\|cirrhosis]] or acute severe AIH**: **budesonide 3 mg TID + [[thiopurines\|AZA]]** (adults 50–150 mg/day; children 1–2 mg/kg/day) **OR prednisone/prednisolone + AZA** are **equally acceptable** first-line therapies | Conditional | Low |
| **Rec 2** — In patients **with cirrhosis OR acute severe AIH** (INR 1.5–<2.0 without [[hepatic-encephalopathy\|HE]]): do **NOT** use budesonide | Conditional | Very low |

- **Why budesonide fails in cirrhosis:** portal-systemic shunting **bypasses the first-pass effect**, eliminating the systemic side-effect advantage that is its entire rationale.
- **Acute severe AIH** (jaundice + INR 1.5–<2, no HE): initiate a **prednisone trial** and reassess at **1–2 weeks**; no biochemical improvement → evaluate for [[liver-transplantation|liver transplantation]].
- **AIH-related acute liver failure** (INR ≥2 + HE): evaluate directly for transplantation — do not delay listing while awaiting a steroid response. Budesonide is contraindicated here as well.
- **94%–95%** of AIH patients are steroid-responsive; only **~30%** can discontinue all immunosuppression long-term.
- **Monitoring on glucocorticoids:** **DEXA at baseline, repeated every 2–3 years**; **25-hydroxyvitamin D** at diagnosis and annually, supplement if deficient. Check **TPMT** before AZA, **HBsAg/anti-HBc** before immunosuppression, and give vaccines beforehand.
- AASLD 2020 states the prednisone/prednisolone regimen as a drug pairing without printing a starting dose or taper schedule; the budesonide dose (3 mg TID) is given.
