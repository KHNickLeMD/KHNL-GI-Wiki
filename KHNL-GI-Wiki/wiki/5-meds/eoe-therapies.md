## Choosing a Therapy

- **One agent at a time.** [[acg-2025-eoe|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2025]]: select a single anti-inflammatory therapy — there are no data on combination therapy ([[proton-pump-inhibitors|proton pump inhibitor (PPI)]] + topical steroid, drug + diet).
- **No head-to-head comparative efficacy across classes** → choice is by shared decision-making (burden, cost, coverage, patient preference).
- [[esophageal-dilation|Esophageal dilation]] treats stricture, not inflammation, and is always paired with an anti-inflammatory therapy (see [[eosinophilic-esophagitis]]).
- Ratings below are from ACG 2025 and from the [[aga-2020-eoe|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) / Joint Task Force 2020]] guideline.

| Therapy | ACG 2025 | AGA 2020 |
|---|---|---|
| PPI | Suggested as a treatment (conditional; low) | Suggested over no treatment (conditional; very low) |
| Swallowed topical corticosteroid (STC) | Recommended (**strong**; moderate) | Recommended over no treatment (**strong**; moderate) |
| Fluticasone **or** budesonide — either acceptable | Suggested (conditional; low) | — |
| Topical rather than oral systemic steroid | — | Suggested (conditional; moderate) |
| Empiric food elimination diet (FED) | Suggested (conditional; low) | Suggested, 6-food version (conditional; low) |
| Elemental formula | — | Suggested over no treatment (conditional; moderate) |
| Allergy test–directed elimination diet | **Not** suggested (conditional; very low) | Suggested over no treatment (conditional; very low) |
| Dupilumab, ≥12 y, PPI-nonresponder | Suggested (conditional; moderate) | Anti–interleukin-4 receptor alpha (IL-4Rα) only in a trial (no recommendation) |
| Dupilumab, 1–11 y, PPI-nonresponder | Suggested (conditional; low) | — |
| Maintenance of effective therapy | Recommended (**strong**; low) | Suggested, for STC (conditional; very low) |

> **Two contradictions, resolved in favor of the newer guideline.** (1) AGA 2020 suggested an allergy test–directed elimination diet over no treatment; ACG 2025 does **not** suggest allergy testing to direct the diet, because skin prick testing identified only 13% of confirmed food triggers in one prospective series and testing-directed diets have the lowest response rates in meta-analysis. Follow ACG 2025. (2) AGA 2020 confined anti-IL-4Rα therapy to clinical trials; dupilumab was approved after that guideline and ACG 2025 now suggests it.

## Proton Pump Inhibitors

- Acts beyond acid suppression in EoE — decreases eotaxin-3 expression, improves esophageal barrier function, maintains epithelial transcriptional homeostasis. Explain this so patients are not confused by the reflux indication.
- **No specific agent can be recommended** — trials mixed omeprazole, esomeprazole, rabeprazole, and lansoprazole at varying doses, frequencies, and durations.

| Population | Dose |
|---|---|
| Adults | **Double the approved reflux dose per day** — e.g. omeprazole 20 mg twice daily, or 40 mg daily, or other PPI equivalent |
| Children | **2 mg/kg/day** (or 1 mg/kg twice daily) |

- **Divided dosing is the higher-yield choice.** In 305 newly diagnosed patients (overall histologic remission 42.3%), remission was **53%–54% with omeprazole 20–40 mg twice daily vs 10%–12% with 20–40 mg once daily** (P<0.0001). ACG 2025 still permits once-daily or divided dosing before meals on adherence grounds because efficacy data are variable.
- Once-daily may suffice with a PPI minimally metabolized by CYP2C19 — 61% histologic response to once-daily esomeprazole in 51 patients.
- **Induction duration: 8–12 weeks**, then [[upper-endoscopy|upper endoscopy]] with biopsies.
- Pooled efficacy (2016 meta-analysis, 33 studies, 188 children + 431 adults): clinical response 60.8% (95% confidence interval [CI] 48.4%–72.2%); histologic remission (<15 eosinophils per high-power field [eos/hpf]) 50.5% (95% CI 42.2%–58.7%). AGA 2020's 23 observational studies gave an unweighted histologic response rate of 42%.
- **Predictors of nonresponse:** rapid CYP2C19 metabolism (may be overcome by dose escalation), *STAT6* polymorphisms, allergic rhinitis, younger age, lower body mass index, higher blood eosinophil count. Abnormal pH monitoring does **not** predict PPI response, and the presence of [[gerd|gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)]] does not predict it either.
- Partial response to PPI in a subgroup of patients happens after successful steroid therapy in initial PPI responders — lack of initial response does not guarantee permanent nonresponse.
- **[[potassium-competitive-acid-blockers|Potassium-competitive acid blockers (PCABs)]]:** preliminary only. Largest study — retrospective, 118 patients on [[vonoprazan]] or a PPI (rabeprazole 10 mg, rabeprazole 20 mg, esomeprazole 20 mg): PCAB group 72.7% symptomatic response, a 2-point reduction in the EoE Endoscopic Reference Score (EREFS), 39.4% complete histologic remission — similar to the PPI results. No data support histamine-2 receptor blockers in EoE.

## Swallowed Topical Corticosteroids

Histologic response rates of 60%–70% across meta-analyses; every STC trial, budesonide or fluticasone, beat placebo histologically. **Either budesonide or fluticasone is a reasonable first STC** — the one randomized comparison found no significant difference (see below).

### Formulations and Dosing

| Agent / formulation | Children | Adults |
|---|---|---|
| **Budesonide** (any formulation) | 1–2 mg/day (by age, height, weight; may be divided twice daily) | 2–4 mg/day (may be divided twice daily) |
| Budesonide oral suspension (BOS) | — | **2 mg twice daily** (approved dose; US Food and Drug Administration [FDA]-approved for EoE 2024) |
| Budesonide orodispersible tablet (BOT) | — | **1 mg twice daily** (European Medicines Agency [EMA]-approved for EoE 2018) |
| **Fluticasone**, swallowed from a metered-dose inhaler (MDI) — off-label | 110–880 µg/day in a divided dose | **1,760 µg/day in a divided dose** |

- Fluticasone doses above are for a multidose inhaler; adjust if a disk device is used, depending on whether it is a 100- or 250-µg device.
- Phase 3 histologic efficacy (≤15 eos/hpf): 62% with BOS 2 mg twice daily; 95% with BOT 1 mg twice daily.
- Fluticasone propionate orally disintegrating tablet (phase 3, not yet available): histologic response (<15 eos/hpf) 75% at 3 mg at bedtime to 95% at 1.5 mg twice daily in a phase 2b trial.
- **Induction duration: 8–12 weeks** before the response endoscopy (STC trials ran 6–12 weeks).

### Delivery and Vehicle

- Fluticasone is **swallowed from the inhaler, not inhaled** — the MDI is actuated into the mouth and swallowed.
- Fluticasone powder (Diskus packets) did **not** show histologic superiority over the MDI in a retrospective comparison, despite being the more logical vehicle for esophageal delivery.
- **Compounding viscous budesonide** when an asthma preparation is adapted for EoE: mix the aqueous budesonide to a **syrup-like consistency** using sucralose, honey, maple syrup or similar, to a **goal total volume of ~10 mL**. The concentration of budesonide as well as volume and viscosity properties of the vehicle may affect effectiveness and have not been compared in trials.
- Off-label asthma preparations adapted for esophageal delivery have comparable histologic efficacy to the purpose-built preparations (BOS, BOT), though no direct comparison exists.
- **Head-to-head, 8 weeks, newly diagnosed and mostly PPI-nonresponsive:** oral viscous budesonide (OVB) 1 mg twice daily (n=56) vs fluticasone MDI 880 µg twice daily (n=55) — histologic response (<15 eos/hpf) 71% vs 64%, not significant; post-treatment dysphagia scores and EREFS also similar.
- ACG 2025 does not state how long to avoid eating or drinking after a dose.

### Topical vs Systemic Steroid

- **Use topical, not oral systemic, steroid** (AGA 2020). The single randomized comparison, 80 children: prednisone 1 mg/kg twice daily vs fluticasone 2 puffs 4 times daily (110 µg/puff if <10 y; 220 µg/puff if 11–18 y) for 4 weeks, then tapered over 8 weeks.
  - Histologic improvement statistically greater with prednisone at 4 weeks, but symptom response was similar at week 24 (72% prednisone vs 65% fluticasone) and relapse was 45% in both arms.
  - **Systemic complications in 40% of the prednisone arm** (weight gain, cushingoid appearance) vs a 15% rate of oral candidiasis with fluticasone.
- Benefit of systemic steroids in patients refractory to STC is unknown.

## Dietary Elimination

*Step-up sequence and the per-diet response rates are on [[eosinophilic-esophagitis]].*

- **Start less restrictive** (single-food elimination diet [1FED] or 2-food elimination diet [2FED]) and escalate on nonresponse — ACG 2025. This is a change from AGA 2020, which framed the empiric recommendation around the 6-food elimination diet (6FED); the step-up approach reduced endoscopic procedures and trigger-identification time by 20% while reaching similar response rates, so follow ACG 2025.
- **Elemental (amino acid–based) formula** is effective but rarely first choice — poor palatability may require percutaneous gastrostomy, insurance coverage exists in a minority of US states, and nutritional monitoring by a registered dietitian is needed. Reserve for infants and for patients with severe or refractory EoE without other options.
- **Trigger identification after response:** add back one food or food group every **6–8 weeks** with an endoscopy after each.
  - **Histologic flare (≥15 eos/hpf) defines a trigger** → re-eliminate that food indefinitely, then add back the next one.
  - If a patient has been nonadherent or had known food contamination, perform a **washout of 2–6 weeks** (depending on amount and nature of contamination) and push the endoscopy back accordingly.
  - Add foods back systematically starting with those **least likely to trigger EoE**.
- Food-related symptoms during reintroduction are not always EoE.
- **Do not order serum immunoglobulin E (IgE) or immunoglobulin G (IgG) food panels, skin prick tests, or patch tests to direct the diet.** Consult an allergist when there is concern for loss of tolerance on prolonged food avoidance (risk of de novo IgE-mediated reactions including anaphylaxis on reintroduction), or when oral immunotherapy is being considered.
- Work with a registered dietitian or nutritionist with EoE knowledge; add a feeding therapist if avoidant-restrictive food intake disorder or feeding dysfunction is present.
- **"Diet holidays"** — relax the restriction and bridge with medical therapy for travel, holidays, or special events; may improve long-term adherence, though no data yet support the approach. Harder in children, for whom resuming a trigger food may be difficult to reverse.

## Dupilumab

Monoclonal antibody against IL-4Rα; blocks the signaling of both interleukin-4 and interleukin-13, cytokines central to the type 2 inflammatory cascade. Given by subcutaneous injection.

- **Indication (ACG 2025):** EoE **nonresponsive to PPI therapy** — ages ≥12 y (recommendation 10) and 1–11 y (recommendation 11).
- **FDA approval:** 2022 for ≥12 y and ≥40 kg; February 2024 for ages 1–11 y weighing ≥15 kg.

| Weight | Dose | Interval |
|---|---|---|
| 15–<30 kg | 200 mg subcutaneously | every other week |
| 30–<40 kg | 300 mg subcutaneously | every other week |
| ≥40 kg | 300 mg subcutaneously | **every week** |

- **Weekly, not every-other-week, in adults/adolescents.** In the phase 3 randomized controlled trial (RCT), weekly dosing gave 60% histologic remission (≤6 eos/hpf) vs 5% placebo and a 21.9-point drop in the Dysphagia Symptom Questionnaire vs 9.6 (P<0.001); **every-other-week dosing achieved similar histologic remission but no significant symptom benefit**, which is why weekly 300 mg was the dose approved for ≥12 y and ≥40 kg.
- Pediatric (1–11 y, n=102, 16 weeks): histologic response 68% higher dose, 58% lower dose, 3% placebo (P<0.001), with improved endoscopic severity and growth; responses maintained to 52 weeks.
- **Severe fibrostenotic disease:** 46 patients refractory to all previous treatments — ~60% histologic response with concomitant endoscopic and symptom improvement, and the largest dilation size reached improved. A phase 2 subanalysis showed a 2–3 mm gain in esophageal diameter vs placebo in the absence of dilation.
- **No tuberculosis, human immunodeficiency virus, or hepatitis screening before starting**; no increase in infection risk with the anti-IL-4Rα mechanism.
- **No therapeutic drug monitoring** — clinically important immunogenicity has not been reported.
- Most common adverse effects: injection-site discomfort, erythema, or reactions; arthralgias reported in non-EoE trials.
- **Cost and insurance coverage are the main barriers.** If a patient has another atopic condition that independently meets a dupilumab indication, it can be considered earlier in the algorithm, with the relevant specialist.
- Trial population caveat: mean EoE duration ~5 years, ~40% had required dilation, all were PPI nonresponders, ~70% had prior topical steroid exposure with about half nonresponsive or intolerant.

## Agents Not Recommended

| Agent / class | Stance |
|---|---|
| Omalizumab (anti-IgE) | **Against** — ACG 2025 (conditional; low) and AGA 2020 (conditional; very low). An RCT showed no change in esophageal eosinophilia or symptoms; EoE is not IgE-mediated |
| Montelukast (leukotriene receptor antagonist) | **Against** — ACG 2025 (conditional; very low). In the one blinded placebo-controlled trial (n=41 after 6 weeks of swallowed topical steroid), dysphagia responses were similar to placebo |
| Cromolyn sodium | **Against** — ACG 2025 (conditional; very low). Randomized trial (n=16) showed no significant reduction in esophageal eosinophilia or symptoms |
| Cendakimab (anti–interleukin-13) | **No recommendation** — phase 3 ongoing; phase 2 met its endpoint for eosinophil count and endoscopic severity |
| Benralizumab, lirentelimab, mepolizumab, reslizumab (eosinophil-depleting / anti–interleukin-5) | **No recommendation** — profound histologic response without significant symptom benefit. AGA 2020: >90% of anti–interleukin-5–treated patients failed histologic remission (risk ratio 0.92; 95% CI 0.84–1.00) |
| [[anti-tnf-agents\|Infliximab]], [[vedolizumab]] | Case reports only — not to be used clinically outside research |
| Immunomodulators (azathioprine, 6-mercaptopurine), losartan, etrasimod, IRL1104 | Early-phase data only; AGA 2020 placed immunomodulators in the clinical-trial-only category |

## Assessing Response

| Therapy started | First assessment |
|---|---|
| PPI, STC, or FED | **8–12 weeks** |
| Dupilumab | **12–24 weeks** |

Assess all three domains at the same endoscopy — symptoms, endoscopic appearance, histology (ACG 2025, strong recommendation):

- **Histologic goal: <15 eos/hpf** (<60 eos/mm²) — a reasonable target for most patients; quantify the peak count rather than reporting ">15".
- **Endoscopic goal: EREFS ≤2** on the 0–9 scale (complete normalization is EREFS 0).
- **Symptoms:** ask about dysphagia and about adaptive eating behaviors, not just "are you better" — symptoms correlate only modestly with biologic activity, and they improve falsely when patients have modified eating or had dilation.
- **"Deep remission"** = response in all domains. Achieved in only about 1 in 10 — in a Swiss cohort of 351 patients, 33 (9.4%); relapse was noted at a median of 22 weeks after stopping.
- Persistent symptoms despite histologic response → look for an undetected stricture (barium esophagram), esophageal infection complicating STC, hypervigilance/visceral hypersensitivity, or feeding dysfunction.
- No validated noninvasive biomarker replaces biopsies. Unsedated transnasal endoscopy, the Cytosponge (sensitivity/specificity 75%/86% vs biopsy), the Esophageal String Test, mucosal impedance, and [[flip-panometry|the functional lumen imaging probe (EndoFLIP)]] are alternatives used mainly at specialty centers.
- Stable, uncomplicated patient with an adequate response → regular clinical follow-up; moderate-to-severe disease or complications (food impaction, perforation, stricture requiring dilation, malnutrition) → individualized, shorter intervals.

## Maintenance and Dose Reduction

- **Continue the effective therapy** — ACG 2025 recommendation 16 (strong, despite low-quality evidence). Off treatment, **78% relapse histologically (>15 eos/hpf) within 1 year** and 94% have some eosinophilic infiltration; fibrostenotic progression follows treatment interruption, and EoE does not remit spontaneously.
- **A gap in care of ≥2 years** (no visits or endoscopies) carries increasing risk of fibrostenosis with longer gaps.

**Maintenance doses with randomized support:**

| Regimen | Outcome |
|---|---|
| BOT 1 mg twice daily or 0.5 mg twice daily × 48 weeks | Remission maintained in 75% and 73.5% vs 4.4% on placebo; placebo median time to relapse 87 days vs >350 days |
| BOS 2 mg twice daily × 36 weeks | 43.5% vs 24% without relapse (P=0.13); per-protocol 50% vs 16.7% (P=0.038) |
| Budesonide 0.25 mg twice daily × 50 weeks | Only 35.7% in histologic remission vs 0% on placebo — AGA 2020 judged this dose likely too low versus the 1 mg twice-daily induction dose |
| PPI, long term | 70%–85% maintain response at 1 year; >60% of adults at mean follow-up 3.6 years |

- **Dose reduction is reasonable after remission** — use the lowest effective dose, confirmed by endoscopy and biopsy. Evidence is strongest for BOT.
- **Every-other-day dosing may not be effective**, and loss of response to STC occurs over time.
- **PPI step-down:** 30% of initial responders relapsed when reduced to once daily, but regained histologic remission on re-escalation to twice daily; a prospective cohort stepping omeprazole from 40 mg twice daily → 40 mg daily → 20 mg daily held remission in >80%.
- **Any change in therapy or dose → repeat endoscopy with biopsies** to confirm the response is maintained.
- Diet maintenance is limited by adherence: roughly half cannot maintain the diet long term even after triggers are identified.

## Nonresponse — What to Change

1. Confirm adherence and technique (how the inhaler or slurry is actually taken; diet compliance and contamination).
2. **Switch within or across classes** — change or modify prior treatment; if a medication was used, try the other medication class; if diet was used, escalate to a more restrictive diet or switch to pharmacologic therapy.
3. **Step to dupilumab** after PPI nonresponse — or earlier if another atopic indication applies.
4. **Fibrostenotic component with persistent dysphagia** → add esophageal dilation, continuing the anti-inflammatory therapy ([[eosinophilic-esophagitis]]).
5. Consider a clinical trial; refer to a specialty center.

## Safety and Monitoring

**Swallowed topical corticosteroids**

- **Esophageal/oral candidiasis is the most common adverse effect** — 3.8% with BOS to 23.7% with BOT in the phase 3 trials (many suspected cases were not confirmed histologically, and most patients were asymptomatic).
  - Management: intermittent topical clotrimazole or oral fluconazole, dose reduction of the STC, or cessation — according to symptoms and severity.
- **Adrenal insufficiency is uncommon** — identified in ≤5% in the BOS and BOT induction trials and in a large pediatric observational study; a systematic review across varying exposure durations reported 15.8%, but definitions of adrenal dysfunction varied (morning cortisol vs stimulation testing) and symptomatic adrenal insufficiency is rarer still.
  - **Testing is not necessary for short-duration therapy.** Test for continuous long-term administration, or when the patient is on multiple corticosteroid formulations (e.g. concurrent inhaled or intranasal steroid).
  - In children, STC monotherapy is not typically associated with clinical adrenal insufficiency, and growth failure from topical steroids is unusual — more likely with systemic steroids; growth typically accelerates once remission is achieved.
- Short-term studies (≤3 months) showed no increased risk of adverse events overall versus placebo.

**PPIs**

- Long-term safety: a randomized trial over 10 years in 17,598 participants found no increased risk of chronic kidney disease, dementia, pneumonia, bone fracture, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, or diabetes mellitus. The **only increased risk was [[clostridioides-difficile|*Clostridioides difficile*]] infection**.
- Benefit outweighs the risks in EoE, but address patient concerns explicitly — unaddressed concerns undermine adherence.

**Dupilumab** — injection-site reactions; no infection screening and no drug monitoring required (see above).

**Pediatric nutrition** — monitor height, weight, and body mass index on any therapy; involve a pediatric dietitian whenever food exclusion is part of the plan, and a pediatric endocrinologist for a child not meeting growth milestones.
