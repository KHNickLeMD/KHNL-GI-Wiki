## Class Overview

**Peripherally acting μ-opioid receptor antagonists (PAMORAs)** block μ-opioid receptors **in the gut** without entering the **central nervous system (CNS)**, thereby restoring the function of the enteric nervous system [[aga-2019-opioid-induced-constipation]].

- Rationale: [[opioid-induced-constipation|opioid-induced constipation (OIC)]] arises primarily from enteric μ-receptor activation, so blocking those receptors reverses the constipating effect **without reversing central analgesia**.
- A distinct drug class in the **American Gastroenterological Association (AGA)** scheme for OIC, separate from traditional laxatives, intestinal secretagogues ([[lubiprostone]]) and selective 5-hydroxytryptamine (5-HT) agonists ([[prucalopride]]).
- Three PAMORAs — **naldemedine, naloxegol, methylnaltrexone** — are **US Food and Drug Administration (FDA)**-approved for OIC and carry AGA recommendations in laxative-refractory OIC. **Alvimopan** is approved for a different problem entirely.

> [!pitfall] Avoid when the blood–brain barrier is compromised
> Conditions that compromise the blood–brain barrier let a PAMORA reach the CNS — potential for **serious withdrawal or reversal of anesthesia** ([[aga-2019-opioid-induced-constipation]]).

## Dosing and Monitoring

| Agent | Approved indication | Route / formulation | Dose | Monitoring |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Naldemedine** | OIC — approved March 2017 | Oral | **0.2 mg**, the dose used in both 12-week phase 3 trials in noncancer pain | — |
| **Naloxegol** | OIC in adults with chronic non-cancer pain — approved 2014 | Oral | **12.5 mg or 25 mg**; **25 mg** met the primary endpoint in both 12-week phase 3 trials | **Adverse cardiovascular events** — an explicit FDA condition of approval |
| **Methylnaltrexone** | OIC in chronic non-cancer pain — the **12 mg subcutaneous injection** is the approved form | Subcutaneous injection **and** oral tablet | Subcutaneous **8 mg or 12 mg**, daily or every other day; oral tablet **150 mg**, with a recommended dosage of **450 mg daily** | — |
| **Alvimopan** | **Postoperative ileus after bowel resection** — not OIC | — | not printed in these sources | — |

- Doses are from [[rome-v-2026-bowel-disorders|Rome V 2026]]; approvals from [[aga-2017-opioids-in-gastroenterology|AGA 2017]] and [[aga-2019-opioid-induced-constipation|AGA 2019]], which prints no PAMORA doses.
- **Dosing frequency for naldemedine and naloxegol is not stated consistently.** AGA 2017 prints naloxegol as "12.5 or 25 mg 4 times a day orally," while the trials it tabulates gave naloxegol once daily; Rome V prints the strengths without a frequency.
- Neither AGA document nor Rome V gives a **renal or hepatic dose adjustment**, a **titration schedule**, or a **treatment duration** for any PAMORA.

## Agents

### Naldemedine

- **Derivative of naltrexone**, to which it is structurally related.
- The **latest PAMORA approved** for management of OIC (March 2017).
- The **only** prescription OIC agent evaluated in the AGA guideline with **long-term (52-week) safety data**.

### Naloxegol

- **Pegylated derivative of naloxone** — pegylation increases **oral bioavailability** and enhances **peripheral selectivity**. Also a **substrate for the P-glycoprotein transporter**, which limits entry of the drug into the CNS; only negligible amounts reach the CNS, so it does not reduce opioid pain relief.
- The **first PAMORA approved** for management of OIC in adults with chronic non-cancer pain (2014).
- Antagonized morphine's effect on orocecal transit **with no effect on miosis or opioid withdrawal symptoms** — evidence of exclusively peripheral action.
- Improved spontaneous bowel movements (SBMs) **from the first week** of treatment in the large phase 2 and phase 3 studies, **including in patients with an inadequate response to laxatives**; also improved stool consistency, complete SBMs, percentage of days with straining, and constipation quality-of-life scores.
- **Over 52 weeks** in OIC with non-cancer pain: most common side effects abdominal pain, diarrhea, nausea, headache, flatulence; **no QT/corrected QT (QTc) interval prolongation and no serious adverse events** occurred.

### Methylnaltrexone

- **Quaternary N-methyl derivative of naltrexone** — the methyl group decreases lipid solubility and increases polarity, so the drug **cannot cross the blood–brain barrier**.
- Opioid antagonist effects **throughout the body** — **reverses itching as well as constipation**.
- The only agent in the class available in **both oral and subcutaneous injection** forms.
- ⚠ **Perforation reports — 7 cases of gastric or intestinal perforation** reported to the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System during the **first 18 months after approval** [[aga-2017-opioids-in-gastroenterology]]. **Causation has not been established:** the affected patients had either a coincidental gastric ulcer or severe constipation, itself a risk factor for perforation.

### Alvimopan

- A μ-opioid antagonist that is also a **δ (delta)-opioid receptor antagonist**.
- Approved in the United States for **postoperative ileus in patients after bowel resection** — not for OIC [[aga-2017-opioids-in-gastroenterology]].
- Accelerates recovery of **gastrointestinal (GI)** function (especially the **lower** GI tract), shortens length of hospital stay and reduces postoperative ileus-related morbidity **without compromising opioid analgesia** in an enhanced recovery setting.
- **Meta-analysis of 9 randomized controlled trials, 4,075 patients:** significantly decreased time to first passage of stool and lowered the chance of serious side effects.

> [!pearl] Alvimopan treats ileus, not OIC
> Same class, different problem — alvimopan is the PAMORA used after **bowel resection** to speed return of lower GI function ([[aga-2017-opioids-in-gastroenterology]]).

## Clinical Use

- **Indication: laxative-refractory OIC.** PAMORAs are **not** first-line — laxatives are, and the AGA panel favored **scheduled** laxatives from **at least 2 classes** before escalating [[aga-2019-opioid-induced-constipation]].
- The graded AGA statements (**2a–2c**), the laxative-refractory definition with its Bowel Function Index trigger, and the pooled efficacy and safety data for each agent are on [[opioid-induced-constipation]].
- **Response target:** an improvement in SBM frequency **by ≥3 per week** was associated with consistent improvement in patient-reported outcomes [[aga-2017-opioids-in-gastroenterology]].
