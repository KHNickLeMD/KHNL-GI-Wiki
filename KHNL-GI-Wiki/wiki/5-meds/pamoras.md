## Class Overview

**Peripherally acting μ-opioid receptor antagonists (PAMORAs)** — drugs that **do not enter the central nervous system** but **block the μ-opioid receptors in the gut**, thereby effectively restoring the function of the enteric nervous system [[aga-2019-opioid-induced-constipation]].

- The therapeutic logic: [[opioid-induced-constipation|opioid-induced constipation]] arises primarily from **enteric μ-receptor activation**, so blocking those receptors reverses the constipating effect **without reversing central analgesia**.
- Distinguished in [[aga-2019-opioid-induced-constipation]] from traditional laxatives, intestinal secretagogues ([[lubiprostone|lubiprostone]]), and selective 5-HT agonists ([[prucalopride|prucalopride]]).

## Agents

Three PAMORAs carry AGA recommendations in laxative-refractory OIC.

| Agent | Chemistry / pharmacology | FDA approval | Formulations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Naloxegol** | **Pegylated derivative of naloxone.** Pegylation allows increased **oral bioavailability** and enhanced **peripheral selectivity**. Also a **substrate for the P-glycoprotein transporter**, which limits entry of the medication into the CNS | **2014** — the **first** PAMORA approved for management of OIC in adult patients with **chronic non-cancer pain** | Oral |
| **Methylnaltrexone** | **Quaternary ammonium cation** that **cannot cross the blood–brain barrier**. Has opioid antagonist effects throughout the body — **reverses itching as well as constipation** | — | **Oral and subcutaneous injection** |
| **Naldemedine** | **Structurally related to naltrexone** | **March 2017** — the latest PAMORA approved for management of OIC | Oral |

*Dosing is not printed in [[aga-2019-opioid-induced-constipation]] for any of the three agents.*

## Safety

- **Avoid PAMORAs in conditions that compromise the blood–brain barrier**, due to the potential for **serious withdrawal or reversal of anesthesia** [[aga-2019-opioid-induced-constipation]].
- **Naldemedine** — adverse events leading to discontinuation were more common than placebo (**RR 1.44; 95% CI 1.03–2.03**): infection, abdominal pain, diarrhea, flatulence, nausea, back pain; the absolute increase was low (**2 more per 100 treated patients**). It is the **only prescription OIC agent in the guideline with long-term (52-week) safety data**.
- **Naloxegol** — generalized or upper abdominal pain, diarrhea, nausea, headache, flatulence (**RR 2.33; 95% CI 1.62–3.35**). Two deaths in the safety extension study, **one in each study arm**; neither was considered related to study medication.
- **Methylnaltrexone** — no statistically significant increase in adverse events leading to discontinuation.
- **Cost** is a practical limit on the whole class: it may limit patient and provider use of naldemedine, naloxegol use "should be judicious," and methylnaltrexone is high-cost relative to other agents — though its subcutaneous form may be an advantage in some situations.

## Clinical Use

- **Indication:** **laxative-refractory** OIC — i.e. after scheduled laxatives from at least 2 classes have failed. PAMORAs are **not** first-line; laxatives are.
- **Recommendations and efficacy data live on [[opioid-induced-constipation]]** — naldemedine (strong, high-quality evidence), naloxegol (strong, moderate), methylnaltrexone (conditional, low) — alongside the laxative-refractory criteria and the Bowel Function Index cutoff that triggers escalation.
