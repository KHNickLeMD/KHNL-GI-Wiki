## Overview

Fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT): transfer of stool-derived microbiota to restore a disrupted gut microbiome. Delivered as **conventional FMT** (donor stool, via [[colonoscopy]], capsule, or enema) or **US Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved live biotherapeutic products** — fecal microbiota live-jslm (RBL, rectal) and fecal microbiota spores live-brpk (VOS, oral). Established evidence-based role: **recurrent [[clostridioides-difficile|C. difficile]] infection (CDI)** after standard-of-care antibiotics ([[aga-2024-fmt]]).

## Indications & AGA 2024 Recommendations

All American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2024 recommendations are **conditional** (suggestions).

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Immunocompetent** adults, recurrent CDI | FOR fecal microbiota–based therapy after completing standard-of-care antibiotics (Rec 1) |
| **Mildly/moderately immunocompromised** adults, recurrent CDI | FOR **conventional FMT** (Rec 2) |
| **Severely immunocompromised** adults, recurrent CDI | **AGAINST** any fecal microbiota–based therapy (Rec 2) |
| Adults hospitalized with **severe/fulminant CDI not responding** to antimicrobials | FOR **conventional FMT** (Rec 3) |
| [[ulcerative-colitis\|ulcerative colitis (UC)]], [[crohns-disease\|Crohn's]], [[pouchitis]], [[irritable-bowel-syndrome\|irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)]] | **AGAINST** conventional FMT **except within a clinical trial** (Recs 4–7) |

- **Key gate:** severe immunocompromise contraindicates fecal microbiota–based therapy even for recurrent CDI.
- Outside recurrent/refractory CDI, use is investigational — not routine care.
- **Recs 4–7 are about treating the underlying disease, not CDI in that patient.** Recurrent, severe, or fulminant CDI *occurring in* UC, Crohn's, pouchitis, or IBS is still managed by Recs 1–3. For UC, conventional FMT may also be reasonable under **expanded access** when no comparable or satisfactory alternative therapy is available (Rec 4).

### Immunocompromise — what counts as "severe" (AGA 2024)

The stratum decides Rec 2, so the criteria are the decision. **Severely immunocompromised** = any of ([[aga-2024-fmt]]):

- Active cytotoxic therapy for solid tumors or hematologic malignancies
- Chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy or hematopoietic cell transplant — **only when neutropenic**
- **Any neutropenia**
- Severe primary immunodeficiency
- Advanced or untreated human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) — CD4 <200/mm³, acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS)-defining illness without immune reconstitution, or clinical manifestations of symptomatic HIV

**Mildly/moderately immunocompromised** = immunocompromised but **not** meeting the severe definition. In this group use **conventional FMT only** — there is insufficient evidence for the FDA-approved products (fecal microbiota live-jslm, fecal microbiota spores live-brpk) in any immunocompromised patient.

- **AGA 2026 working triggers in recurrent CDI** ([[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]): cytotoxic chemotherapy, recent stem cell transplant, absolute neutrophil count <500/µL, advanced or untreated HIV (CD4 <200/mm³). Severe immunocompromise is usually **transient** — **defer** microbiota therapy and continue suppressive vancomycin rather than abandon it.

## Administration (AGA 2024 implementation considerations)

*Recurrent CDI (nonsevere, nonfulminant, outpatient):*
- Give **on completion** of a standard-of-care antibiotic course — these therapies **prevent recurrence, they do not treat active CDI**.
- **Bridge** with suppressive anti-CDI antibiotics (e.g. vancomycin) until the therapy is given.
- Stop CDI antibiotics **1–3 days before conventional FMT** for washout: **1 day** if a bowel purge is given, **3 days** if not. Rapid recurrence within 1–2 days of stopping antibiotics is a risk when setting the window.
- Screened donor stool required; **no specific delivery route is recommended** (insufficient evidence to favor one).
- **Administration schedule for the two FDA-approved products** ([[aga-2026-cdiff-ibd|AGA 2026, CDI in IBD]]) — the only schedules any ingested guideline prints:
  - **RBL** — a broad consortium given as a **single-dose rectal enema, 1–3 days after completing antimicrobials**. Its label narrows this to **24–72 hours after the last antibiotic dose** ([[rebyota-2022-live-jslm-label]]); see [[#RBL — product dosing and administration (label)]].
  - **VOS** — an encapsulated selective group of *Firmicutes* spores, **4 capsules once daily for 3 consecutive days, started 2–4 days after antimicrobials and after a bowel lavage**. Its label confirms the regimen and prints the preparation sequence ([[vowst-2025-live-brpk-label]]); see [[#VOS — product dosing and administration (label)]].
  - Both prevent recurrence rather than treat active infection, so both follow the antibiotic course and its washout.
- **Product choice when the patient has [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|IBD]]:** RBL has IBD-specific data (phase 3 open-label study including 74 patients with IBD — **treatment success 78.9% at 8 weeks**, **sustained clinical response 91.1% at 6 months**, comparable with non-IBD participants); **there are no VOS data in CDI with IBD**, but either product is reasonable on mild-to-moderate immunocompromising medications given the similar mechanism and response. RBL and VOS are now the main routes to microbiota restoration because major United States stool banks no longer supply specimens.
- Consider after the **second recurrence (third episode)**, or earlier in selected patients at high risk of recurrent or morbid recurrence (recovered from severe/fulminant/treatment-refractory CDI, significant comorbidities).
  - **[[aga-2026-cdiff-adults|AGA 2026]] names the high-risk criteria that license earlier use** (after an initial infection or first recurrence): **age >65 years**, **immunocompromised**, or **resident of a skilled nursing facility**. Per product labeling, both FDA-approved products are approved to prevent CDI **regardless of the number of recurrences**.
  - [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults|AGA 2026]] gives the washout as **1–4 days** off antibacterial therapy before microbiota restoration, and specifies the bridge as suppressive oral vancomycin **at least 125 mg daily** until the therapy is approved and accessible.
- Caution in patients needing frequent or long-term antibiotics — ongoing antibiotics may diminish efficacy.
- **ACG 2021 delivery and repeat dosing** ([[acg-2021-cdiff]]): FMT for a second or further recurrence is a strong recommendation (moderate quality). Deliver by **colonoscopy or oral capsules** (both strong; equivalent — 96.2% vs 96.2% in one randomized trial); **enema** only if these are unavailable (conditional; lower efficacy in meta-analyses). Recurrence **within 8 weeks** of FMT → **repeat FMT** (conditional); most failures occur within 4 weeks, and <5% fail a second FMT. Before repeating, restart anti-CDI antibiotics to control symptoms; consider colonoscopic delivery if the first FMT was by enema or capsule.
- **Guidelines differ — route.** [[aga-2024-fmt|AGA 2024]] finds insufficient evidence to recommend any route; [[acg-2021-cdiff|ACG 2021]] prefers colonoscopy or capsules over enema.
- **VOS evidence — ECOSPOR III** ([[feuerstadt-2022-ser109-cdiff]]): purified *Firmicutes* spores, 4 capsules daily × 3 days after standard antibiotics resolve symptoms; 8-week recurrence **12% vs 40%** with placebo (relative risk 0.32, 95% CI 0.18–0.58), consistent across age and prior antibiotic (vancomycin or fidaxomicin); safety similar to placebo (mostly mild GI events); sustained clinical response **88% vs 60%**, number needed to treat **3.6**; the trial reports the dose as **≈3 × 10⁷ spore colony-forming units (CFU)**. The **rationale for the magnesium citrate purge** is here: vancomycin and fidaxomicin persist 5–7 days and inactivate the dose species — the volume and timing are on the label, below. [[aga-2024-fmt]] lists fecal microbiota spores, live-brpk among the fecal microbiota–based therapies for recurrent CDI.
- **Pregnancy:** avoid FMT during pregnancy; defer to postpartum ([[acg-2021-cdiff]]).
- Alternatives for patients who decline: [[cdiff-antibiotics|vancomycin taper or tapered-pulsed fidaxomicin]]. *(AGA 2024 also lists bezlotoxumab; [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults|AGA 2026]] reports it is **no longer commercially available**.)*

*Severe/fulminant CDI not responding to antimicrobials:*
- **Which patients qualify** — AGA defines severe CDI by a white blood cell count (WBC) and creatinine threshold and fulminant CDI as severe disease plus shock, ileus, or [[toxic-megacolon|megacolon]]; the full criteria table (and how it changed between the 2010 and 2018 Infectious Diseases Society of America (IDSA) versions) lives on [[clostridioides-difficile]]. Read it before invoking Rec 3.
- Consider generally **within 2–5 days** of initiating CDI treatment; multidisciplinary care (critical care, surgery, gastroenterology [GI], infectious diseases [ID]).
- **First dose via colonoscopy or flexible sigmoidoscopy** — confirms the diagnosis and lets you grade severity. Insufficient evidence for enema or capsule here, and **no evidence** for the FDA-approved products in this setting.
- **Nasoenteric tube is discouraged** — increased risk of fecal aspiration.
- If a **bowel purge is not feasible or safe**, perform FMT **without** bowel preparation.
- **Stop other nonessential antibiotics** wherever possible (an ID-facing reason multidisciplinary care matters).
- **Follow-up:** assess response by **stool output, WBC, and C-reactive protein (CRP)**. Most patients need repeat FMT; anti-CDI antibiotics may need to be continued or resumed around dosing. Repeat-dosing interval and the pseudomembrane-driven sequential protocol are on [[clostridioides-difficile]].
- **At discharge:** after colitis resolves, continue **suppressive vancomycin** and perform a **final fecal microbiota–based therapy as an outpatient** to prevent recurrence — that last dose may be given by colonoscopy, capsule, or enema.
- **Not advised** with bowel perforation or obstruction (**or high-grade colonic stricture**, [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]), or in severe immunocompromise. Colectomy is the standing alternative for severe CDI refractory to antibiotics, or fulminant CDI.
- **Dose count and donor source** ([[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]): **multi-dose** FMT from **fresh directed donors or institutional stool banks**; most patients need **2–3 administrations** of liquid product via lower endoscopy to clear the acute infection. Oral vancomycin may be **held on the day of FMT** but is otherwise continued.
- **Mortality benefit (the evidence behind Rec 3):** 5 observational studies, 647 patients with severe/fulminant CDI — conventional FMT vs standard of care, mortality **relative risk (RR) 0.37 (95% confidence interval [CI] 0.23–0.59)**, **number needed to treat (NNT) 4** to prevent 1 death, with no difference in serious adverse events. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]
- **Regulatory:** the FDA prohibits stool-bank donor material outside an investigational new drug application; locally procured FMT falls under **FDA enforcement discretion** and requires an explicit risk–benefit discussion. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]

### Dosing — what AGA 2024 does and does not specify

- **Conventional FMT:** no dose, volume, or route is specified — the guideline states there is **insufficient evidence to recommend a specific route**.
- **FDA-approved products:** AGA gives **no dosing at all** and directs the reader to the **manufacturer's package insert** for fecal microbiota spores live-brpk and fecal microbiota live-jslm. The only quantitative statement in the guideline is descriptive: per its package insert, each **150-mL dose of fecal microbiota live-jslm contains 1×10⁸–5×10¹⁰ CFU/mL**, a microbial content **less than that in 1 g of stool**, whereas all published conventional-FMT studies used far greater dosing.
- Product dosing is absent from the 2024 guideline; the **administration schedules above** come from [[aga-2026-cdiff-ibd|AGA 2026]], and the per-dose microbial content from the package insert — now ingested for RBL as [[rebyota-2022-live-jslm-label]].

### RBL — product dosing and administration (label)

From [[rebyota-2022-live-jslm-label]]. **Indication is narrower than "FMT"**: prevention of recurrence in adults **≥18 y following antibiotic treatment for recurrent CDI**, with an explicit Limitation of Use — **not indicated to treat CDI**.

- **Single 150 mL dose, rectal only**, given **24–72 hours after the last CDI antibiotic dose**.
- **Only contraindication: history of severe allergic reaction (e.g. anaphylaxis) to a product component.** Have treatment for anaphylaxis immediately available.
- Made from human fecal matter → may transmit infectious agents, and **may contain food allergens** (donors have no dietary restrictions; the risk is unknown). Tell the patient both.
- **Avoid oral antibiotics for up to 8 weeks afterwards** unless specifically directed.
- Counsel the patient to report returning **persistent diarrhea — ≥3 loose stools in 24 h for 2 consecutive days**.
- Each dose: **1×10⁸–5×10¹⁰ CFU/mL** (>1×10⁵ CFU/mL *Bacteroides*) in PEG 3350 and saline, **≤5.97 g PEG 3350**. **Mechanism of action is not established**, by the label's own statement.

> [!howto] Giving an RBL enema
> 1. Thaw in a **refrigerator (2–8 °C) over ~24 h** — never with heat; do not refreeze. Usable **≤5 days refrigerated, thaw time included**.
> 2. Patient empties bladder and bowel; position **left side or knee-chest** on an underpad.
> 3. Close the pinch clamp fully, then spike the bag. **Do not** bleed air from the tubing — product is lost.
> 4. Lubricate and insert **~12 cm (5 in)**, angled slightly **toward the umbilicus**.
> 5. Hold the tube steady, open the clamp, **raise the bag — gravity flow only**.
> 6. Clamp, withdraw slowly, then keep the position **up to 15 min**. Restroom use is unrestricted.

> [!pitfall] Three ways to lose the dose
> **Do not** let the tube sag or loop (the full dose will not run in), **do not** squeeze the bag (painful), and **do not** hang it from an IV stand. Some product always remains in the tube — that is expected, not an incomplete dose ([[rebyota-2022-live-jslm-label]]).

- **Storage:** ultracold freezer **−60 °C to −90 °C**, or refrigerator **2–8 °C for up to 5 days including thaw time**. The **administration set** is stored at **10–34 °C and never frozen**.
- **Adverse reactions** (8 weeks, vs placebo): abdominal pain 8.9% vs 6.9%, diarrhea 7.2% vs 3.4%, distension 3.9% vs 2.3%, flatulence 3.3% vs 0, nausea 3.3% vs 1.1% — **mostly in the first 2 weeks**, mild to moderate, none life-threatening. Serious adverse events 10.1% vs 7.2% at 6 months, **none judged related**.
- **Not established under 18 y.** Not absorbed systemically, so pregnancy and lactation exposure is not expected. Nearly half of recipients were ≥65 y, but the data cannot say whether they respond differently.

> **Efficacy is proven at 8 weeks and unproven at 6 months.** Treatment success **70.6% vs 57.5%** (difference 13.1 points, 95% credible interval 2.3–24.0; posterior probability of superiority 0.991 against a 0.975 threshold). **Sustained clinical response at 6 months was 65.5% vs 56.5% — difference 9.1%, 95% CI −3.6% to 21.7%, not significant.** The randomized trials also **excluded** celiac disease, [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|IBD]], [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|IBS]] and chronic diarrhea, so those patients are not described by these numbers.

> [!pitfall] A label contraindication is not the whole gate
> RBL's label lists **no immunocompromise restriction** — its only contraindication is component allergy; **VOS's label lists no contraindication at all**. **[[aga-2024-fmt]]'s restriction still governs patient selection**: conventional FMT only in mild/moderate immunocompromise, and no microbiota-based therapy in severe immunocompromise. Label silence is not permission.

### VOS — product dosing and administration (label)

From [[vowst-2025-live-brpk-label]]. Same narrow indication as the rectal product: prevention of recurrence in individuals **≥18 y following antibacterial treatment for recurrent CDI**, with the same explicit Limitation of Use — **not indicated to treat CDI**.

- **4 capsules orally once daily for 3 consecutive days**, each dose **on an empty stomach before the first meal of the day**. Swallow whole — **do not crush, chew, or break**.
- **Contraindications: none** (the label's own word).
- **Antibacterials must not be given concurrently** — the capsules contain live spores. Counsel the patient directly.
- Made from human fecal matter → may transmit infectious agents, and **may contain food allergens** (donors have no dietary restrictions; risk unknown). Tell the patient both.
- **Each capsule holds 1×10⁶–3×10⁷ *Firmicutes* spore CFU** in 92 ± 4% glycerol in saline — a **per-capsule** figure, not the per-dose figure the trial reports. **Mechanism of action is not established**, by the label's own statement.

> [!howto] Preparing a patient for VOS
> 1. **Finish the antibacterial course 2–4 days before** the first capsule.
> 2. The **day before**, and **≥8 h before** the first dose, drink **296 mL (10 oz) magnesium citrate**.
> 3. **Nothing by mouth but small sips of water for ≥8 h** before the first dose.
> 4. Day 1 dose on the empty stomach; **Days 2 and 3 in the morning before breakfast**.

> [!pitfall] Magnesium citrate when the kidneys will not take it
> The label prescribes magnesium citrate and prints **no approved alternative**. In the trials, participants with **impaired kidney function** received **250 mL polyethylene glycol electrolyte solution instead — explicitly not approved for this use**. No ingested guideline addresses the substitution ([[vowst-2025-live-brpk-label]]).

- **Storage is ordinary: 2–25 °C (36–77 °F), refrigerator or room temperature, never the freezer** — no ultracold chain, unlike RBL. Supplied as bottles of 12 capsules imprinted "SER109".
- **Adverse reactions** (8 weeks, vs placebo): abdominal distension 31.1% vs 29.3%, fatigue 22.2% vs 21.7%, constipation 14.4% vs 10.9%, chills 11.1% vs 7.6%, diarrhea 10.0% vs 4.3%. **Median duration ≤5 days** each; most begin **within 10 days** of starting and are mild to moderate. Across 349 recipients, **no serious adverse event was judged related**.
- **Not established under 18 y.** **No pregnancy data and no animal reproductive studies**; excretion in human milk unknown. Half of recipients were ≥65 y, but the data cannot say whether they respond differently.

> **Recurrence separation narrows but does not close.** **8 weeks 12.4% vs 39.8%** (relative risk 0.32, 95% CI 0.18–0.58) · **12 weeks 18.0% vs 46.2%** (0.40, 0.24–0.65) · **24 weeks 21.3% vs 47.3%** (0.46, 0.30–0.73). Entry required **≥3 CDI episodes in 12 months** and symptom resolution after 10–21 days of vancomycin or fidaxomicin. The trials **excluded neutropenia (absolute neutrophil count <500/mm³), [[toxic-megacolon|toxic megacolon]] and small bowel ileus**, so those patients are not described by these numbers.

## Place in Therapy

- Anchored within [[clostridioides-difficile|C. difficile]] management for recurrent disease after appropriate antibiotics, and for severe/fulminant disease refractory to antimicrobials.
- Stronger evidence than [[probiotics]] for established recurrent CDI.
