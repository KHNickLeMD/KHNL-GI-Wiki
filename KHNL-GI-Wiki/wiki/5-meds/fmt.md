## Overview

- Fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT): transfer of stool-derived microbiota to restore a disrupted gut microbiome.
- Three forms — **conventional FMT** (screened donor stool via [[colonoscopy]], capsule, or enema) and two **US Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved live biotherapeutics**: fecal microbiota live-jslm (RBL, rectal) and fecal microbiota spores live-brpk (VOS, oral).
- Established role is **only** [[clostridioides-difficile|C. difficile]] infection (CDI): recurrence prevention after standard-of-care antibiotics, and severe/fulminant disease refractory to antimicrobials ([[aga-2024-fmt]]). Everything else is investigational.
- **All three prevent recurrence; none treats active CDI** — they follow the antibiotic course, never replace it.
- Conventional FMT is now minimally available (major United States stool banks no longer supply specimens), so RBL and VOS are the main routes to microbiota restoration ([[aga-2026-cdiff-ibd]]).
- **Regulatory:** the FDA prohibits stool-bank donor material outside an investigational new drug (IND) application; locally procured FMT falls under **FDA enforcement discretion** and requires an explicit risk–benefit discussion ([[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]).
- CDI diagnosis, severity definitions, antibiotic regimens and the overall pathway: [[clostridioides-difficile]], [[cdiff-antibiotics]].

## Indications

All [[aga-2024-fmt|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2024]] recommendations are **conditional** (suggestions).

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Immunocompetent** adults, recurrent CDI | FOR fecal microbiota–based therapy on completion of standard-of-care antibiotics (Rec 1) |
| **Mildly/moderately immunocompromised** adults, recurrent CDI | FOR **conventional FMT** (Rec 2) |
| **Severely immunocompromised** adults, recurrent CDI | **AGAINST** any fecal microbiota–based therapy (Rec 2) |
| Adults hospitalized with **severe/fulminant CDI not responding** to antimicrobials | FOR **conventional FMT** (Rec 3) |
| [[ulcerative-colitis\|ulcerative colitis (UC)]], [[crohns-disease\|Crohn's]], [[pouchitis]], [[irritable-bowel-syndrome\|irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)]] | **AGAINST** conventional FMT **except within a clinical trial** (Recs 4–7) |

- **Recs 4–7 concern treating the underlying disease, not CDI in that patient.** Recurrent, severe, or fulminant CDI *occurring in* UC, Crohn's, pouchitis, or IBS is still managed by Recs 1–3. In UC, conventional FMT may also be reasonable under **expanded access** when no comparable or satisfactory alternative therapy exists (Rec 4).
- Outside recurrent and refractory CDI, use is investigational — not routine care.

### Immunocompromise Strata

The stratum decides Rec 2, so the criteria are the decision. **Severely immunocompromised** = any of ([[aga-2024-fmt]]):

- Active cytotoxic therapy for solid tumors or hematologic malignancies
- Chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy or hematopoietic cell transplant — **only when neutropenic**
- **Any neutropenia**
- Severe primary immunodeficiency
- Advanced or untreated human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) — CD4 <200/mm³, acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS)-defining illness without immune reconstitution, or clinical manifestations of symptomatic HIV

**Mildly/moderately immunocompromised** = immunocompromised but not meeting the severe definition → **conventional FMT only**; evidence is insufficient for either FDA-approved product in any immunocompromised patient.

- **AGA 2026 working triggers for severe immunocompromise** ([[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]): cytotoxic chemotherapy, recent stem cell transplant, absolute neutrophil count <500/µL, advanced or untreated HIV (CD4 <200/mm³). Severe immunocompromise is usually **transient** — **defer** microbiota therapy and continue suppressive vancomycin rather than abandon it.

> [!pitfall] A label contraindication is not the whole gate
> RBL's label lists **no immunocompromise restriction** — its only contraindication is component allergy; **VOS's label lists no contraindication at all**. **[[aga-2024-fmt]]'s restriction still governs patient selection**: conventional FMT only in mild/moderate immunocompromise, nothing in severe immunocompromise. Label silence is not permission.

## Products

[[aga-2024-fmt|AGA 2024]] specifies **no dose, volume, or route for conventional FMT** and **no dosing at all** for the FDA-approved products, directing the reader to the package insert.

| | Conventional FMT | RBL | VOS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Route / dose | Screened donor stool; no dose or volume specified | Single **150 mL rectal** enema | **4 capsules orally, once daily × 3 days**, each dose on an empty stomach before the first meal |
| Microbial content | All published studies used far greater dosing than RBL | **1×10⁸–5×10¹⁰ colony-forming units (CFU)/mL** (>1×10⁵ CFU/mL *Bacteroides*); less than in 1 g of stool | **1×10⁶–3×10⁷ *Firmicutes* spore CFU per capsule** |
| Timing off antibiotics | **1–3 days** (1 day if a bowel purge is given, 3 days if not); AGA 2026 gives 1–4 days | **24–72 h after the last CDI antibiotic dose** | **2–4 days** after completing antibacterials |
| Bowel prep | Purge optional | None | **Magnesium citrate required** |
| Labeled indication | Not FDA-approved | Prevention of recurrence, adults ≥18 y, after antibiotic treatment for recurrent CDI — **not indicated to treat CDI** | Same, individuals ≥18 y — **not indicated to treat CDI** |
| Contraindications | — | History of **severe allergic reaction (e.g. anaphylaxis) to a component**; have anaphylaxis treatment available | **None** (the label's own word) |
| Immunocompromise | Only option in mild/moderate | Insufficient evidence | Insufficient evidence |
| Severe/fulminant CDI | Yes — first dose by lower endoscopy | Not studied, not approved | Not studied, not approved |
| Storage | — | Ultracold **−60 °C to −90 °C**, or **2–8 °C ≤5 days including thaw time**; administration set at **10–34 °C, never frozen** | **2–25 °C** (refrigerator or room temperature), **never the freezer**; bottles of 12 capsules |

*Dose/route/timing from [[rebyota-2022-live-jslm-label]], [[vowst-2025-live-brpk-label]] and [[aga-2024-fmt]]; neither product's mechanism of action is established, by each label's own statement.*

### Conventional FMT

- Appropriately **screened donor stool** required; special donor testing may be needed in immunocompromised recipients ([[aga-2024-fmt]]).
- **Multi-dose** material from **fresh directed donors or institutional stool banks** for fulminant disease ([[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]).
- Pregnancy: **avoid, defer to postpartum** ([[acg-2021-cdiff]]).

### Live-jslm (RBL)

- **Avoid oral antibiotics for up to 8 weeks afterwards** unless specifically directed.
- Made from human fecal matter → may transmit infectious agents and **may contain food allergens** (donors have no dietary restrictions; risk unknown). Tell the patient both.
- Counsel the patient to report returning **persistent diarrhea — ≥3 loose stools in 24 h for 2 consecutive days**.
- Not established under 18 y; not absorbed systemically, so pregnancy and lactation exposure is not expected.
- Only product with data in [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]]: a phase 3 open-label study including 74 patients with IBD found safety and efficacy consistent with conventional FMT ([[aga-2026-cdiff-ibd]]).

> [!howto] Giving an RBL enema
> 1. Thaw in a **refrigerator (2–8 °C) over ~24 h** — never with heat; do not refreeze.
> 2. Patient empties bladder and bowel; position **left side or knee-chest** on an underpad.
> 3. Close the pinch clamp fully, then spike the bag. **Do not bleed air from the tubing** — product is lost.
> 4. Lubricate and insert **~12 cm (5 in)**, angled slightly **toward the umbilicus**.
> 5. Hold the tube steady, open the clamp, **raise the bag — gravity flow only**. Never squeeze the bag or hang it from an intravenous pole; do not let the tube sag or loop.
> 6. Clamp, withdraw slowly, then keep the position **up to 15 min**. Restroom use is unrestricted.

- Product always remaining in the tube is expected, not an incomplete dose ([[rebyota-2022-live-jslm-label]]).

### Spores Live-brpk (VOS)

- **Antibacterials must not be given concurrently** — the capsules contain live spores. Counsel the patient directly.
- Swallow capsules whole — **do not crush, chew, or break**.
- Made from human fecal matter → same infectious-agent and **food allergen** counseling as RBL.
- Not established under 18 y; **no pregnancy data and no animal reproductive studies**; excretion in human milk unknown.
- **No data in CDI with IBD**, but either product is reasonable on mild-to-moderate immunocompromising medications, given the similar mechanism and response ([[aga-2026-cdiff-ibd]]).

> [!howto] Preparing a patient for VOS
> 1. **Finish the antibacterial course 2–4 days before** the first capsule — vancomycin and fidaxomicin persist 5–7 days and inactivate the dose species ([[feuerstadt-2022-ser109-cdiff]]).
> 2. The **day before**, and **≥8 h before** the first dose, drink **296 mL (10 oz) magnesium citrate**.
> 3. **Nothing by mouth but small sips of water for ≥8 h** before the first dose.
> 4. Day 1 dose on the empty stomach; **Days 2 and 3 in the morning before breakfast**.

> [!pitfall] Magnesium citrate when the kidneys will not take it
> The label prescribes magnesium citrate and prints **no approved alternative**. In the trials, participants with **impaired kidney function** received **250 mL polyethylene glycol electrolyte solution instead — explicitly not approved for this use**. No ingested guideline addresses the substitution ([[vowst-2025-live-brpk-label]]).

## Administration

### Recurrent CDI

*Nonsevere, nonfulminant, outpatient.*

- Give **on completion** of a standard-of-care antibiotic course; **bridge** with suppressive oral vancomycin **≥125 mg daily** until the therapy is approved and accessible ([[aga-2024-fmt]], [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]).
- Washout before dosing: see [[#Products]]. Rapid recurrence within 1–2 days of stopping antibiotics is a risk when setting the window.
- **When in the course:** after the **second recurrence (third episode)**, or earlier (after an initial infection or first recurrence) in patients at high risk of recurrent or morbid recurrence — **age >65 years**, **immunocompromised**, **resident of a skilled nursing facility**, a prior severe/fulminant/treatment-refractory episode, or significant comorbidities. Per product labeling, both FDA-approved products are approved to prevent CDI **regardless of the number of recurrences** ([[aga-2024-fmt]], [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]).
- **Route — guidelines differ.** [[aga-2024-fmt|AGA 2024]]: insufficient evidence to recommend any route. [[acg-2021-cdiff|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2021]]: **colonoscopy or oral capsules** (both strong; equivalent, 96.2% vs 96.2% in one randomized trial), **enema** only if neither is available (conditional; lower efficacy in meta-analyses). FMT for a second or further recurrence is an ACG strong recommendation, moderate quality.
- **Recurrence within 8 weeks of FMT → repeat FMT** (ACG, conditional); most failures occur within 4 weeks and <5% fail a second FMT. Restart anti-CDI antibiotics to control symptoms first, and consider colonoscopic delivery if the first dose was by enema or capsule ([[acg-2021-cdiff]]).
- **Caution** in patients needing frequent or long-term antibiotics — ongoing antibiotics may diminish efficacy.
- Patients who decline: vancomycin taper or tapered-pulsed fidaxomicin ([[cdiff-antibiotics]]). *(AGA 2024 also lists bezlotoxumab; [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults|AGA 2026]] reports it is **no longer commercially available**.)*

### Severe or Fulminant CDI

*Hospitalized, not responding to antimicrobials — **conventional FMT only**; the FDA-approved products are unstudied and unapproved here, and may be underdosed or lack the needed microbial diversity ([[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]).*

- **When:** generally **within 2–5 days** of initiating CDI treatment, with multidisciplinary care (critical care, surgery, gastroenterology, infectious diseases).
- **First dose via colonoscopy or flexible sigmoidoscopy** — confirms the diagnosis and lets you grade severity. Insufficient evidence for enema or capsule. **Nasoenteric tube is discouraged** (fecal aspiration risk).
- If a **bowel purge is not feasible or safe**, perform FMT **without** [[bowel-preparation|bowel preparation]].
- Most patients need **2–3 administrations** of liquid product via lower endoscopy to clear the acute infection; repeat interval and the pseudomembrane-driven sequential protocol are on [[clostridioides-difficile]].
- **Stop other nonessential antibiotics** where possible. Oral vancomycin may be **held on the day of FMT** but is otherwise continued, and anti-CDI antibiotics are usually resumed or continued around dosing.
- **Follow-up:** stool output, white blood cell count (WBC), and C-reactive protein (CRP).
- **At discharge:** after colitis resolves, continue **suppressive vancomycin** and perform a **final fecal microbiota–based therapy as an outpatient** to prevent recurrence — that dose may be given by colonoscopy, capsule, or enema.
- **Not advised** with bowel perforation or obstruction, **high-grade colonic stricture** ([[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]), or severe immunocompromise. **Colectomy** is the standing alternative.
- Severity definitions (leukocyte and creatinine thresholds; fulminant = severe plus shock, ileus, or [[toxic-megacolon|megacolon]]) are on [[clostridioides-difficile]] — read them before invoking Rec 3.

## Place in Therapy

- Anchored within [[clostridioides-difficile|C. difficile]] management: recurrent disease after appropriate antibiotics, and severe/fulminant disease refractory to antimicrobials.
- Stronger evidence than [[probiotics]] for established recurrent CDI.
- Published efficacy excludes groups commonly seen in gastroenterology — the RBL trials excluded [[celiac-disease|celiac disease]], IBD, IBS and chronic diarrhea; the VOS trials excluded neutropenia (absolute neutrophil count <500/mm³), toxic megacolon and small bowel ileus ([[rebyota-2022-live-jslm-label]], [[vowst-2025-live-brpk-label]]).
