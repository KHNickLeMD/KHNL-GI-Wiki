**Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT-2) inhibitors** are oral agents increasingly used to treat diabetes mellitus, for glycemic control and cardiovascular risk reduction. They are not gastrointestinal (GI) drugs, but they carry one decision that belongs to the endoscopist: **when to stop them before a procedure, and what to do if they were not stopped** ([[asge-2025-glp1-sglt2-periendoscopic]]).

## Why They Matter in GI

- The periprocedural risk is **not aspiration** — that is the [[glp-1-receptor-agonists|glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist (GLP-1 RA)]] problem. It is **euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA)**, triggered by the fasting and fluid shifts of procedure preparation.
- The US Food and Drug Administration warning on this class is specifically for **ketoacidosis in patients undergoing surgery or a prolonged period of fasting with fluid shifts**.
- **[[colonoscopy]] is the higher-risk procedure**, because of the long relative fasting period that [[bowel-preparation|bowel preparation]] requires. Several reported cases involve patients who did **not** withhold the drug before colonoscopy.
- **[[upper-endoscopy|Upper endoscopy]]** requires only a short fast and **has not been reported to increase euDKA risk**, except rarely in an emergent case — though data are limited.
- Whether these patients need **extended bowel preparation** is unresolved.

## Hold Before Endoscopy

The American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) **suggests holding SGLT-2 inhibitors 3 to 4 days before elective endoscopy** — **at least 72 hours** before a scheduled endoscopic procedure ([[asge-2025-glp1-sglt2-periendoscopic]], Statement 11; 12/12 panel agreement, no evidence grading applied).

| Drug | Route | Half-life | Hold before endoscopy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Empagliflozin | Oral | 12.4 h | **3 days** |
| Dapagliflozin | Oral | 12.9 h | **3 days** |
| Canagliflozin | Oral | 10.6 h | **3 days** |
| Ertugliflozin | Oral | 16.6 h | **4 days** |

> [!howto] The patient arrives still taking it
> Statement 11 gives two options for anyone who did not hold the drug as recommended:
> 1. **Reschedule** the procedure, **or**
> 2. **Send a basic metabolic panel** and check for an **anion-gap acidosis** — before the procedure per the statement, before *and* after it per the statement's discussion.

- Holding these agents for a few days may cause **hyperglycemia**; the position statement emphasizes that **coordination with endocrine specialists is imperative**.
- Statements 4–10 of the position statement cover GLP-1 RAs only; it sets **no SGLT-2–specific rule for urgent, emergent, or time-sensitive procedures**. There, management is individualized by shared decision-making weighing the risk of euDKA against the risk of delaying the endoscopy.

## Euglycemic Diabetic Ketoacidosis

> [!pitfall] A normal glucose does not exclude it
> euDKA presents with **elevated *or near-normal* blood glucose** — that is what makes it easy to miss in a patient who looks metabolically stable. Check the **anion gap**, not just the fingerstick ([[asge-2025-glp1-sglt2-periendoscopic]]).

- **Mechanism:** reduced glucose availability and production during a fasting state (typically with some stressor) and/or increased urinary glucose excretion from excess counter-regulatory hormones.
- **Risk factors:** fasting, **low-carbohydrate diet**, intercurrent infection or illness, dehydration, relative insulin deficiency, surgical stress, excessive alcohol intake, insulin omission or dose reduction, severe acute illness, extensive exercise. **Lower body mass index** may raise risk.
- Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) is a **rare** adverse event of this class overall.
