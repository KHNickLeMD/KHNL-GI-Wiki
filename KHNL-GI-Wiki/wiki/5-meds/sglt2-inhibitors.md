**Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT-2) inhibitors** ("flozins") are oral antidiabetic agents used for type 2 diabetes mellitus, with additional cardiovascular and renal indications. They are not gastrointestinal (GI) drugs, but they carry one decision that belongs to the endoscopist: **when to stop them before a procedure, and what to do if they were not stopped** ([[asge-2025-glp1-sglt2-periendoscopic]]).

## Why They Matter in GI

- The periprocedural risk is **not aspiration** — that is the [[glp-1-receptor-agonists|glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist (GLP-1 RA)]] problem. It is **euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA)**, triggered by the fasting and fluid shifts of procedure preparation.
- **[[colonoscopy]] is the higher-risk procedure**, because of the long relative fasting period that [[bowel-preparation|bowel preparation]] requires. Several reported cases involve patients who did **not** withhold the drug before colonoscopy.
- **[[upper-endoscopy|Upper endoscopy]]** requires only a short fast and **has not been reported to increase euDKA risk**, except rarely in an emergent case — though data are limited.

## Hold Before Endoscopy

**Hold 3–4 days before elective endoscopy** ([[asge-2025-glp1-sglt2-periendoscopic]], Statement 11; unanimous panel agreement, no evidence grading applied).

| Drug | Route | Half-life | Hold before procedure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Empagliflozin | Oral | 12.4 h | **3 days** |
| Dapagliflozin | Oral | 12.9 h | **3 days** |
| Canagliflozin | Oral | 10.6 h | **3 days** |
| Ertugliflozin | Oral | 16.6 h | **4 days** |

*The hold is far longer than the half-lives suggest because the target is the drug's ongoing urinary glucose excretion and ketogenic state, not simply its clearance.*

> [!howto] The patient arrives still taking it
> 1. **Reschedule** the procedure, **or**
> 2. **Send a basic metabolic panel before the procedure** and check for an **anion-gap acidosis**.
>
> Either is acceptable per [[asge-2025-glp1-sglt2-periendoscopic]]; proceeding without one of the two is not.

- Holding these agents for several days may cause **hyperglycemia**; the position statement emphasises that **coordination with the endocrine or prescribing clinician is imperative**.

## Euglycemic Diabetic Ketoacidosis

> [!pitfall] A normal glucose does not exclude it
> euDKA presents with **elevated *or near-normal* blood glucose** — that is what makes it easy to miss in a patient who looks metabolically stable. Check the **anion gap**, not just the fingerstick ([[asge-2025-glp1-sglt2-periendoscopic]]).

- **Mechanism:** reduced glucose availability and production during a fasting state (usually with some stressor) and/or increased urinary glucose excretion from excess counter-regulatory hormones, on a background of relative insulin deficiency.
- **Risk factors:** fasting, **low-carbohydrate diet**, intercurrent infection or illness, dehydration, relative insulin deficiency, surgical stress, excessive alcohol intake, insulin omission or dose reduction, severe acute illness, extensive exercise. **Lower body mass index** may raise risk.
- DKA is a **rare** adverse event of this class overall; the US Food and Drug Administration issued a warning specifically about ketoacidosis in patients undergoing surgery or a prolonged fast with fluid shifts.
