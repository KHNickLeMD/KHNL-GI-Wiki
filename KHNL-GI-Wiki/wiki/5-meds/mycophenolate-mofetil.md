Mycophenolate mofetil (MMF) is an antimetabolite immunosuppressant — a potent, non-competitive, reversible inhibitor of **isoform type II of inosine-5′-monophosphate dehydrogenase** ([[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]). In gastroenterology and hepatology its main use is [[autoimmune-hepatitis|autoimmune hepatitis (AIH)]], where it became a **first-line alternative to [[thiopurines|azathioprine]]** in 2025.

## Dosing in Autoimmune Hepatitis

| Setting | Dose |
|---|---|
| **First-line induction**, with predniso(lo)ne ≥0.5 mg/kg/day | **Start 1,000 mg/day** at the same time as the corticosteroid → **1,500–2,000 mg/day (0.75–1.0 g twice daily) at week 2** |
| **Maintenance** | **1.5–2 g/day** as monotherapy, or with low-dose predniso(lo)ne **≤5 mg/day**, adapted to sustain complete biochemical response |
| **Second-line**, for thiopurine intolerance | **1.5–2.0 g/day** |

- Unlike azathioprine, MMF is started **at the same time as** the corticosteroid, not 2 weeks later, and has **no bilirubin restriction** on starting ([[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]])
- Full treatment duration, tapering schedule and withdrawal criteria are on [[autoimmune-hepatitis]]

## Where MMF Sits in the AIH Pathway

- **First-line alternative to azathioprine** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation*) — on the CAMARO randomised controlled trial in treatment-naive AIH: **complete biochemical response at 6 months 72.2% with MMF vs 32.3% with azathioprine (p = 0.004)**, severe adverse events **0% vs 12.9%** (p = 0.03), treatment withdrawal **5.1% vs 25.8%**
- **Second-line agent of choice for thiopurine intolerance or side effects** (*LoE 2, strong*) — complete response **62–92%** (pooled 82%, 95% confidence interval 77–87%); discontinuation **0–8%**
- **Before third-line therapy** after azathioprine optimisation fails (*LoE 2, weak*) — rescues **32–68%** of azathioprine insufficient responders
- **Insufficient response to first-line MMF → switch to a thiopurine** before escalating to third-line agents. Insufficient response occurs in **3–28%** on first-line MMF (vs 22–68% on first-line azathioprine)
- **Low-dose predniso(lo)ne monotherapy is reserved for patients intolerant to both azathioprine *and* MMF** with mild disease in complete biochemical response (*LoE 3, weak*)

*All from [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]. Response definitions: [[autoimmune-hepatitis#Severity Assessment]].*

## Monitoring

- **Aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), prothrombin time, fasting glucose and full blood count at 4 weeks** — a single check suffices, because MMF-related toxicity is rare. (Azathioprine by contrast needs the same panel **weekly for the first month**, its toxicity peaking in the first 6 weeks.)
- Then labs **including immunoglobulin G (IgG) every 2–3 months** to call complete biochemical response at 6 and 12 months; **every 3–6 months** once it is attained
- **Metabolite levels are not measured** — there is no MMF equivalent of the thiopurine 6-TGN/6-MMP panel ([[thiopurines]])

## Teratogenicity and Contraception

> [!pitfall] MMF is teratogenic — counsel men as well as women
> **Withdraw MMF at least 3 months before conception** (*LoE 2, strong*), and never use it for a first presentation of AIH during pregnancy — use standard regimens **excluding MMF** (*LoE 2, strong*). While on treatment: a **negative pregnancy test** and **two methods of birth control** (hormonal contraceptive, partner condom, diaphragm, copper intrauterine device, sponge, spermicide), continued **to at least 12 weeks after** the drug is stopped. Similar precautions are advisable for **male** patients. ([[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]])

- Counselling of **both female and male** patients is a strong recommendation in its own right
- Thiopurines, not MMF, are what continue through pregnancy in AIH — see [[liver-disease-in-pregnancy]]

## Paediatric Dosing

**5–10 mg/kg twice daily, increasing to 20 mg/kg twice daily (max 1,000 mg twice daily)** — the preferred **second/third-line** agent in refractory paediatric AIH, ahead of [[calcineurin-inhibitors|calcineurin inhibitors]] (*LoE 3, strong*). Estimated adverse events **≈48%**; metabolites are not routinely measured; teratogenicity needs explicit monitoring. Full paediatric regimen: [[autoimmune-hepatitis#Paediatric AIH]]. ([[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]])

## Cautions

- **Lowers the platelet count independently of liver disease** — so a platelet-based portal hypertension rule can mislead in a patient on MMF. This is one reason the Baveno cut-offs are not validated in AIH ([[liver-stiffness-measurement]], [[portal-hypertension]])
- **MMF use in the first year after liver transplantation is a risk factor for AIH recurrence** — though use beyond the first year is not ([[liver-transplantation]])
- Patients escalated to third-line immunosuppression after MMF should be **evaluated for opportunistic infection risk** with prophylaxis or vaccination as appropriate
