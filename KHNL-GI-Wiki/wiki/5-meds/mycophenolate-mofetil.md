Mycophenolate mofetil (MMF) is an antimetabolite immunosuppressant — a potent, non-competitive, reversible inhibitor of **isoform type II of inosine-5′-monophosphate dehydrogenase** ([[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]). Its main use in gastroenterology and hepatology is [[autoimmune-hepatitis|autoimmune hepatitis (AIH)]], where it became a **first-line alternative to [[thiopurines|azathioprine]]** in 2025; it is also the antimetabolite used to spare the [[calcineurin-inhibitors|calcineurin inhibitor]] after [[liver-transplantation|liver transplantation]].

## Dosing in Autoimmune Hepatitis

### Adults

| Setting | Dose |
|---|---|
| **First-line induction**, with predniso(lo)ne ≥0.5 mg/kg/day | **1,000 mg/day started at the same time as the corticosteroid** → **1,500–2,000 mg/day (0.75–1.0 g twice daily) from week 2** |
| **Maintenance** | **1.5–2 g/day** as monotherapy or with low-dose predniso(lo)ne **≤5 mg/day**, adapted to sustain stable complete biochemical response (CBR) |
| **Second line**, for thiopurine intolerance or side effects | **1.5–2.0 g/day** |

> [!pearl] MMF starts with the steroid — azathioprine does not
> The 2-week delay and the **bilirubin <6 mg/dL** gate before starting belong to azathioprine (they keep its hepatotoxicity from being read as non-response). MMF begins on day 1 alongside predniso(lo)ne and reaches full dose at week 2 ([[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]).

- Treatment duration, steroid taper and withdrawal criteria: [[autoimmune-hepatitis]]

### Children

**5–10 mg/kg twice daily, increased to 20 mg/kg twice daily (max 1,000 mg twice daily)** — it replaces azathioprine in refractory paediatric AIH and is the preferred second/third-line agent ahead of [[calcineurin-inhibitors|calcineurin inhibitors]] (*level of evidence [LoE] 3, strong recommendation*). Adverse events ≈48%; metabolites are not routinely measured; teratogenicity requires explicit counselling and monitoring. Full paediatric regimen: [[autoimmune-hepatitis#Paediatric AIH]]. ([[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]])

## Where MMF Sits in the AIH Pathway

| Line | Role of MMF | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| **First line** | Alternative to azathioprine, with predniso(lo)ne | *LoE 2, strong* |
| **Second line** | Agent of choice for thiopurine **intolerance** or side effects | *LoE 2, strong* |
| **Before third line** | After unsuccessful intensification/optimisation of azathioprine (**insufficient response**) | *LoE 2, weak* |
| **Children** | Preferred second/third-line agent, ahead of calcineurin inhibitors | *LoE 3, strong* |

- **Insufficient response to first-line MMF → switch to a thiopurine** before escalating to third-line agents
- **Intolerant to both azathioprine and MMF**, mild disease, CBR attained → low-dose predniso(lo)ne monotherapy (*LoE 3, weak*)

*All from [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]. Response rates behind these positions, the CAMARO comparison with azathioprine, and the response definitions: [[autoimmune-hepatitis#Intolerance, Insufficient Response and Rescue]] and [[autoimmune-hepatitis#Severity Assessment]].*

## Monitoring

- **Aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), prothrombin time, fasting glucose and full blood count at 4 weeks**, to look for non-response — not weekly through the first month as on azathioprine (whose toxicity peaks in the first 6 weeks), because MMF-related toxicity is rare
- After that the schedule is the disease's, not the drug's — immunoglobulin G (IgG) and aminotransferases to call CBR: [[autoimmune-hepatitis#Monitoring on Treatment]]
- **No metabolite levels and no trough target** — unlike thiopurines, where 6-thioguanine nucleotide (6-TGN) and 6-methylmercaptopurine (6-MMP) levels guide optimisation ([[thiopurines]], [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]; no trough is monitored in transplant use, [[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-graft-complications]])

## Adverse Effects and Interactions

- **Better tolerated than azathioprine** — discontinuation **0–8%** across AIH cohorts
- Reported events: **gastrointestinal (GI) — nausea, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, weight loss**; **leukopenia and other cytopenias**; rash, fatigue, alopecia, headache; **infections**; skin cancer ([[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]). In transplant use the profile is given as cytopenias, GI, infections and teratogenicity ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-graft-complications]])
- **May further lower the platelet count** — a competing cause of thrombocytopenia to weigh before acting on platelet-based [[portal-hypertension|portal hypertension]] rules in AIH, where the Baveno cut-offs are unvalidated ([[liver-stiffness-measurement]])
- **Villous atrophy** — mycophenolate is a reported medication cause, and so a mimic of refractory [[celiac-disease|celiac disease]] ([[aga-2021-seronegative-enteropathies]]); workup on [[seronegative-villous-atrophy]]

> [!pitfall] Acid suppression blunts mycophenolate exposure
> Antacids and [[proton-pump-inhibitors|proton pump inhibitors (PPIs)]] **decrease systemic exposure** to mycophenolate, as do calcium-free phosphate binders and drugs that interfere with enterohepatic recirculation ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-graft-complications]], Table 4).

## Teratogenicity and Contraception

> [!pitfall] MMF is teratogenic — counsel men as well as women
> **Withdraw MMF at least 3 months before conception** (*LoE 2, strong*), and never use it for a first presentation of AIH during pregnancy — use standard regimens **excluding MMF** (*LoE 2, strong*). While on treatment: a **negative pregnancy test** and **two methods of birth control** (hormonal contraceptive, partner condom, diaphragm, copper intrauterine device, sponge, spermicide), continued **to at least 12 weeks after** the drug is stopped. Similar precautions are advisable for **male** patients. ([[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]])

- Counselling of **both female and male** patients is a strong recommendation in its own right (*LoE 2*)
- Risks to quote to pubertal girls and their families: **birth defects (24% incidence)** and spontaneous abortion
- **After liver transplantation the interval is written differently:** mycophenolic acid products are contraindicated in pregnancy and should be stopped **≥6 weeks before conception in women and ≥90 days in men** (*Strong, Level 1* — the US Food and Drug Administration [FDA] recommendation), shorter than the 3 months given for AIH above ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]])
- In AIH it is **thiopurines (± corticosteroids), not MMF**, that continue through pregnancy — see [[liver-disease-in-pregnancy]]

## Other Uses

- [[liver-transplantation]] — antimetabolite (off-label) added to reduce or replace the calcineurin inhibitor, including for renal sparing and for chronic ductopenic rejection on tacrolimus monotherapy (*Weak, Level 4*); dose table on [[calcineurin-inhibitors]]. **Mycophenolate monotherapy is not recommended** to slow progression of chronic kidney disease after transplant — strong association with acute cellular rejection (*Strong, Level 2*) ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-graft-complications]], [[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]])
- [[antibody-mediated-rejection-liver-transplant]] — one of the agents added when immunosuppression is augmented in acute and chronic rejection ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-graft-complications]])
- **Recurrent AIH after transplant** — MMF use **in the first year** after transplantation is a risk factor for recurrence (use beyond the first year is not); low-dose MMF on a [[tacrolimus]] backbone has been proposed as long-term recurrence prophylaxis but is unvalidated ([[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]])
- [[immune-checkpoint-inhibitor-hepatitis]] — second-line immunomodulator when hepatitis fails to improve within **3–5 days** of glucocorticoids ([[aga-2021-ici-colitis-hepatitis]])
- [[autoimmune-pancreatitis]] — immunomodulator combined with prednisone in the maintenance and relapse regimens ([[aga-2019-autoimmune-pancreatitis]])
