Botulinum toxin injected into a gastrointestinal (GI) sphincter or muscle to relax it. One drug, one technique, and a **sharply different recommendation at every site** — strongly recommended against at the pylorus, reserved for the unfit at the lower esophageal sphincter (LES), a graded second-line option at the anal sphincter.

## Where It Is Recommended — and Where It Is Not

| Site / indication | Stance | Strength · Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LES — [[achalasia\|achalasia]]** | First-line **only** in patients **unfit for definitive therapy** (pneumatic dilation, myotomy) | Strong · Moderate | [[acg-2020-achalasia]] |
| **LES — achalasia, vs pneumatic dilation** | **Pneumatic dilation is superior** for long-term relief; **laparoscopic Heller myotomy preferred** in patients fit for surgery | Strong · Moderate | [[acg-2020-achalasia]] |
| **Pylorus — [[gastroparesis\|gastroparesis]]** | **NOT recommended** — negative randomized controlled trials (RCTs) | **Strong · Moderate** | [[acg-2022-gastroparesis]] (Rec 19) |
| **Pylorus — refractory gastroparesis** | Suggests **against** routine use | — | [[aga-2025-gastroparesis]] |
| **Internal anal sphincter — chronic [[anal-fissure\|anal fissure]]** | May be attempted **after topical calcium channel blocker (CCB) fails**, or as an alternative to CCB | Conditional · Low | [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]] (Table 9, Rec 2) |
| **[[defecation-disorders\|Defecation disorders]]** | Evidence poor, results short-lived; **unlikely to be a practical long-term solution** | Key concept 13 (ungraded) | [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]] |
| **Chronic proctalgia syndromes** | **No evidence** to support it | Ungraded statement | [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]] (Table, statement 3) |
| **Nonachalasia spastic disorders** — [[distal-esophageal-spasm\|distal esophageal spasm]], [[hypercontractile-esophagus\|hypercontractile esophagus]], [[esophagogastric-junction-outflow-obstruction\|EGJOO]] | One of the **less invasive options to exhaust before myotomy**, alongside medical therapy and endoscopic dilation | Best practice advice 3 (ungraded) | [[aga-2024-poem-advances]] |
| **Upper esophageal sphincter — inability to belch syndrome (B3c)** | Successful treatment **has been shown** | Narrative, no grade | [[rome-v-2026-dgbi]] |
| **Cricopharyngeus — myositis-related [[dysphagia\|dysphagia]]** | One of the cricopharyngeus-directed options alongside dilation and myotomy | Narrative, no grade | [[aga-2024-esophageal-immunity-infection]] |

## Achalasia and the Lower Esophageal Sphincter

### Dose and technique

- **100 units**, injected **above the squamocolumnar junction (SCJ)** in **0.5–1 mL aliquots** using a sclerotherapy needle. ([[acg-2020-achalasia]])

### Efficacy and its decay

**The decay is the whole clinical story** — it is why the drug is reserved for patients who cannot have definitive therapy. ([[acg-2020-achalasia]])

| Time after injection | Symptom relief |
|---|---|
| 30 days | 78.7% |
| 3 months | 70% |
| 6 months | 53% |
| **12 months** | **40.6%** |

- **46.6% require additional injections**; **30% go on to need more definitive therapy.**
- **Pneumatic dilation beats it at both 6 and 12 months, and the gap widens with time** — the pooled head-to-head numbers are on [[achalasia]]. ([[asge-2020-achalasia]])
- Where botulinum toxin sits in the unfit-patient sequence: it comes **before** pharmacotherapy. Calcium channel blockers and nitrates are reserved for patients who can undergo neither pneumatic dilation, Heller myotomy nor peroral endoscopic myotomy **and** have already failed botulinum toxin. ([[acg-2020-achalasia]])

### Effect on later definitive therapy

- **Prior botulinum toxin does not significantly affect [[poem|peroral endoscopic myotomy (POEM)]] outcomes**, but **may increase fibrosis risk with laparoscopic Heller myotomy** — the data conflict. ([[acg-2020-achalasia]])

### Predicting who responds

- On [[flip-panometry|functional lumen imaging probe (FLIP) panometry]], a **diminished or absent contractile response** predicts symptomatic response to LES botulinum toxin: **odds ratio 22.5 (95% confidence interval 2.5–206.7)** in prospective data. ([[dallas-2025-flip-panometry]])

## Gastroparesis — Intrapyloric Injection

- **Do not use it.** [[acg-2022-gastroparesis|ACG 2022]] recommends **against** intrapyloric botulinum toxin on the basis of **negative RCTs** — and it does so at **Strong strength, Moderate evidence**, the firmest grading botulinum toxin carries anywhere in the ingested guidelines. [[aga-2025-gastroparesis|AGA 2025]] likewise suggests against routine use, grouping it with gastric peroral endoscopic myotomy (G-POEM), gastric electrical stimulation and routine initial surgical pyloromyotomy/pyloroplasty.
- Note the asymmetry within the same guideline: ACG 2022 recommends **against** botulinum toxin (Strong) while recommending **G-POEM** over no treatment in medically refractory disease (Conditional, Low). Pyloric-directed therapy is not rejected as a concept — this agent is.
- AGA 2025 reasoning: published trials were **not specific to refractory gastroparesis**; retreatment as often as **every 3 months** limits cost-effectiveness and raises concern for diminishing effect; **repeat injections may scar the pylorus and complicate later [[g-poem|G-POEM]]** ([[aga-2025-gastroparesis]]).
- French multicenter observational data: reduced pyloric distensibility index (<10 mm²/mm Hg) on [[flip-panometry|EndoFLIP]] may predict benefit (symptom score improved at 3 mo) — not validated in RCTs; does not override the negative RCTs ([[acg-2022-gastroparesis]]).

## Anal Fissure

- **Second-line, after a topical calcium channel blocker.** [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders|ACG 2021]] suggests botulinum toxin A injection where CCB fails, or as an alternative to CCB (Conditional, Low).
- **Healing 60–80%** — higher than placebo — but **recurrence up to 42%** and **temporary incontinence ~18%**.
- It does not displace surgery: **lateral internal sphincterotomy remains the surgical treatment of choice** for chronic fissures that fail nonsurgical measures (Strong, High; healing 94–98%).
- **[[ascrs-2023-anal-fissures|ASCRS 2023]] rates it first-line**, equivalent to topical therapy, with a modest healing gain second-line after topical failure (Strong/moderate, 1B) — a higher position than ACG's. Decision detail lives on [[anal-fissure]].
- ⚠ **Dose does not buy healing here, and may cost continence.** Two meta-analyses: no dose dependency across 1,577 patients (healing 33%–96%, 5% transient incontinence); and with rising dose, healing fell slightly while **incontinence rose (RR 1.02) and recurrence rose (RR 1.04)** across 1,158 patients. Comparative trials used **20–60 units**; 20 units matched topical 2% diltiazem. ([[ascrs-2023-anal-fissures]])

## Where the Evidence Does Not Support It

- **Defecation disorders:** evidence is poor and results short-lived; **combined adverse effects 14.2%** in a systematic review. Unlikely to be a practical long-term solution. ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]], key concept 13)
- **Chronic proctalgia syndromes** (levator ani syndrome, idiopathic chronic proctalgia): **no evidence** supports botulinum toxin or digital massage — biofeedback is what ACG recommends for levator syndrome with abnormal anorectal manometry. ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]])

No ingested source gives a dose, dilution, or number of injection sites for any indication other than achalasia, or a repeat-injection interval for any indication.
