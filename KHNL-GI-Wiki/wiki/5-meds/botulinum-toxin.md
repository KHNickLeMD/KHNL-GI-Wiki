Botulinum toxin injected into a gastrointestinal (GI) sphincter or muscle to relax it. One drug, one technique, and a **sharply different recommendation at every site** — strongly recommended against at the pylorus, reserved for the unfit at the lower esophageal sphincter (LES), a graded second-line option at the anal sphincter.

## Where It Is Recommended — and Where It Is Not

| Site / indication | Stance | Strength · Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LES — [[achalasia\|achalasia]]** | First-line **only** in patients **unfit for definitive therapy** ([[pneumatic-dilation\|pneumatic dilation]], myotomy), compared with other less-effective pharmacological therapies | Strong · Moderate (Rec 4) | [[acg-2020-achalasia]] |
| **LES — achalasia, as definitive therapy** | **Recommend against**; may be reserved for patients who are not candidates for other definitive therapies | Recommend against · Moderate quality (Rec 2) | [[asge-2020-achalasia]] |
| **LES — vs [[heller-myotomy\|laparoscopic Heller myotomy (LHM)]]** | **LHM preferred** in patients fit for surgery | Strong · Moderate | [[acg-2020-achalasia]] |
| **LES — vs pneumatic dilation** | **Pneumatic dilation preferred** (ASGE); ACG states pneumatic dilation is superior for long-term symptom relief | Recommend · High quality (ASGE Rec 6); narrative, ungraded (ACG) | [[asge-2020-achalasia]], [[acg-2020-achalasia]] |
| **LES — before later myotomy** | Prior injection **does not significantly affect** performance and outcomes of myotomy | Conditional · Low | [[acg-2020-achalasia]] |
| **Pylorus — [[gastroparesis\|gastroparesis]]** | **NOT recommended**, on the basis of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) | **Strong · Moderate** (Rec 19) | [[acg-2022-gastroparesis]] |
| **Pylorus — medically refractory gastroparesis** | Suggests **against routine use** | Conditional · Very low (Rec 9) | [[aga-2025-gastroparesis]] |
| **Internal anal sphincter — chronic [[anal-fissure\|anal fissure]]** | May be attempted **after topical calcium channel blocker (CCB) fails**, or as an alternative to CCB | Conditional · Low (Table 9, Rec 2) | [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]] |
| **Internal anal sphincter — chronic anal fissure** | **Similar results to topical therapy as first-line**; modest healing gain as second-line after topical failure | Strong · Moderate, 1B (Rec 4) | [[ascrs-2023-anal-fissures]] |
| **Internal anal sphincter — fissure recurrent after sphincterotomy** | Good short-term healing, low risk of fecal incontinence (FI), but data limited | Weak · Low, 2C (Rec 9) | [[ascrs-2023-anal-fissures]] |
| **Nonachalasia spastic disorders** — [[distal-esophageal-spasm\|distal esophageal spasm]], [[hypercontractile-esophagus\|hypercontractile esophagus]], [[esophagogastric-junction-outflow-obstruction\|esophagogastric junction outflow obstruction (EGJOO)]] | One of the **less invasive options to exhaust before myotomy**, alongside medical therapy and endoscopic dilation | Best practice advice 3 (ungraded) | [[aga-2024-poem-advances]] |
| **Upper esophageal sphincter (UES) — inability to belch syndrome (B3c)** | Successful treatment **has been demonstrated** | Narrative, no grade | [[rome-v-2026-dgbi]] |
| **Cricopharyngeus — myositis-related [[dysphagia\|dysphagia]]** | One of the cricopharyngeus-directed options alongside dilation and myotomy | Narrative, no grade | [[aga-2024-esophageal-immunity-infection]] |
| **[[defecation-disorders\|Defecation disorders]]** | Evidence poor, results short-lived; **unlikely to be a practical long-term solution** | Key concept 13 (ungraded) | [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]] |
| **Chronic [[proctalgia-syndromes\|proctalgia syndromes]]** | **No evidence** to support it | Table 7, statement 3 (ungraded) | [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]] |

## Dose and Technique by Site

| Site | Dose | Technique | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LES (achalasia)** | **100 units**; escalating doses above 100 units have not been shown to give superior benefit | Injected **above the squamocolumnar junction (SCJ)** in **0.5–1 mL aliquots**, sclerotherapy needle | [[acg-2020-achalasia]] |
| **Pylorus (gastroparesis — recommended against)** | Trials used **100 units** diluted in 4 mL saline, or **200 units**; one retrospective dose comparison suggested 200 units gave a more favorable response than 100 units | **Four-quadrant** injections (1 mL per quadrant; 23-gauge or sclerotherapy needle) | [[aga-2025-gastroparesis]] |
| **Internal anal sphincter (chronic fissure)** | Reported range **5–100 units**; comparative trials used **20–60 units**, and **20 units** matched topical 2% diltiazem (43% healing each at 3 months) | **No consensus** on dose, precise injection site, or number of injections — neither guideline specifies a protocol | [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]], [[ascrs-2023-anal-fissures]] |
| **UES (inability to belch syndrome)** | **50–100 IU** | Mainly injected by ear-nose-throat specialists, targeted by anatomical landmarks or during confirmatory electromyography | [[rome-v-2026-gastroduodenal-disorders]] |

*No ingested source gives a dose for the cricopharyngeus in myositis or for nonachalasia spastic disorders.*

## Lower Esophageal Sphincter — Achalasia

**Stance:** first-line **only** for patients unfit for pneumatic dilation, LHM or [[poem|peroral endoscopic myotomy (POEM)]] — not a definitive therapy ([[acg-2020-achalasia]], [[asge-2020-achalasia]]).

> [!pearl] It paralyzes only half the sphincter
> Botulinum toxin blocks presynaptic acetylcholine release, interrupting the **neurogenic** component of the LES but not the **myogenic** basal tone — hence only an approximate **50% fall in basal LES pressure** (pooled: 38.23 → 23.30 mm Hg), enough for emptying when esophageal pressure overcomes the partly paralyzed sphincter, and never durable ([[acg-2020-achalasia]], [[asge-2020-achalasia]]).

**Efficacy and durability** — the decay is the whole clinical story, and it is why the drug is reserved for patients who cannot have definitive therapy ([[acg-2020-achalasia]]):

| Time after injection | Symptom relief |
|---|---|
| 30 days | 78.7% |
| 3 months | 70% |
| 6 months | 53.3% |
| **12 months** | **40.6%** |

- **46.6% require additional injections**; **30% needed additional treatment** (repeat injection, dilation or surgery). ([[acg-2020-achalasia]])
- Pooled ASGE meta-analysis of 22 uncontrolled studies (730 patients): clinical success (Eckardt score ≤3) **77% (95% confidence interval [CI] 72%–81%)** over 1–6 months of follow-up. ([[asge-2020-achalasia]])
- **Pneumatic dilation beats it at both 6 and 12 months, and the gap widens with time** — the pooled head-to-head numbers are on [[achalasia]]. ([[asge-2020-achalasia]])

**Harms:** generally very safe; serious events (mediastinitis, allergic reaction) are exceedingly rare. Chest pain in **11% (95% CI 7%–15%)** of the pooled cohort, and transient chest pain after **4.4% of 661 injections** in a multicenter review of 386 patients with esophageal motor disorders. ([[asge-2020-achalasia]])

**Sequencing in the unfit patient:** botulinum toxin comes **before** pharmacotherapy — CCBs and nitrates are reserved for patients who can undergo neither pneumatic dilation, LHM nor POEM **and** have already failed botulinum toxin. ([[acg-2020-achalasia]])

**Effect on later definitive therapy:** ACG suggests prior injection **does not significantly affect myotomy** performance or outcomes (Conditional, Low). The underlying data conflict: in responders the LES became fibrotic with less robust dysphagia relief after LHM, and complications were **10.4% vs 5.4%** in patients with versus without prior endoscopic treatment; prior botulinum toxin did not increase POEM complications, and operative time, length of stay and post-POEM dysphagia scores were similar. ([[acg-2020-achalasia]])

**Predicting who responds:** among patients with **reduced esophagogastric junction (EGJ) opening** on [[flip-panometry|functional lumen imaging probe (FLIP) panometry]], a **diminished or absent contractile response** (vs normal or borderline) was associated with symptomatic response to LES botulinum toxin — **odds ratio 22.5 (95% CI 2.5–206.7)** in prospective data. ([[dallas-2025-flip-panometry]])

## Pylorus — Gastroparesis

**Stance: do not use it routinely.** [[acg-2022-gastroparesis|ACG 2022]] recommends **against** intrapyloric injection on the basis of negative RCTs, at **Strong strength, Moderate evidence**. [[aga-2025-gastroparesis|AGA 2025]] suggests against routine use (Conditional, Very low), as it does for [[g-poem|gastric peroral endoscopic myotomy (G-POEM)]] and gastric electrical stimulation; on surgical pyloromyotomy/pyloroplasty it makes **no recommendation** (knowledge gap). ACG adds that pyloric interventions are reasonable to consider **in a clinical trial**.

- **Shared decision making (AGA 2025):** patients and clinicians who place a higher value on endoscopic treatment and a lower value on chronic medical therapy may reasonably attempt it.
- **Efficacy:** against saline injection, no clinically important change in symptom severity at 1 month (pooled mean difference 0.17, 95% CI −0.34 to 0.67; 2 trials, 44 patients). In a 40-patient trial of medically refractory gastroparesis it was **comparable to G-POEM** in nausea/vomiting, early satiety/postprandial fullness and symptom severity at 3 and 12 months. ([[aga-2025-gastroparesis]])
- **Why AGA 2025 still suggests against it:** the published trials were **not specific to refractory gastroparesis**, and retreatment as often as **every 3 months** (3–6 months) limits cost-effectiveness and raises concern for diminishing effect.

> [!pitfall] Repeat pyloric injections can burn the G-POEM bridge
> Repeat treatment may cause **pyloric scarring**, complicating later attempts at G-POEM or other pyloric intervention ([[aga-2025-gastroparesis]]).

- **Note the asymmetry within one guideline:** ACG 2022 recommends **against** botulinum toxin (Strong) while suggesting **pyloromyotomy** over no treatment in medically refractory disease (Conditional, Low). Pyloric-directed therapy is not rejected as a concept — this agent is.
- **Patient selection (not validated):** reduced pyloric distensibility index (<10 mm²/mm Hg) on [[flip-panometry|EndoFLIP]] — 19 of 35 such patients improved (total symptom score at 3 months), with no benefit in those with normal distensibility; does not override the negative RCTs ([[acg-2022-gastroparesis]]). A beneficial response has also been suggested in **postfundoplication** gastroparesis. Using the response to botulinum toxin to **predict G-POEM responders** is common practice but lacks robust supporting evidence. ([[aga-2025-gastroparesis]])

## Internal Anal Sphincter — Anal Fissure

**Stance:** the two guidelines place it differently — [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders|ACG 2021]] makes topical CCB first-line (Strong, Low) and suggests botulinum toxin where CCB fails or as an alternative to it (Conditional, Low); [[ascrs-2023-anal-fissures|ASCRS 2023]] rates it **first-line, with results similar to topical therapy** (Strong, Moderate, 1B). Decision detail lives on [[anal-fissure]].

- **Efficacy:** healing **60%–80%** of fissures, higher than placebo (ACG); **67%** healing vs **71%** with topical nitroglycerin or nifedipine in prospective comparisons (ASCRS). A Cochrane review found only **marginal** improvement over placebo (ACG).
- **Harms:** temporary incontinence of **flatus in up to 18%** and of **stool in 5%**; **recurrence in up to 42%**, though patients may be retreated with results similar to the first treatment. ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]])

> [!pitfall] Dose does not buy healing here, and may cost continence
> Two meta-analyses: no dose dependency across **1,577 patients** (healing 33%–96%, 5% transient FI); and with rising dose, healing fell slightly (−0.34%, 95% CI 0%–0.68%) while **FI rose (relative risk 1.02) and recurrence rose (relative risk 1.04)** across **1,158 patients** in 18 trials ([[ascrs-2023-anal-fissures]]).

- **Combination:** topical nitrates may **potentiate** the effect in refractory fissure — 60% vs 20% healing with combined nitroglycerin plus botulinum toxin vs botulinum toxin alone in a 30-patient trial. Adding an anocutaneous flap to injection may reduce postoperative pain and allow primary wound healing (Weak, Low, 2C). ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]], [[ascrs-2023-anal-fissures]])
- **Predictors of success:** female sex and lower body mass index; ACG adds satisfaction with the first procedure.
- **It does not displace surgery:** lateral internal sphincterotomy (LIS) remains the surgical treatment of choice for chronic fissures that fail nonsurgical measures (Strong, High; healing 94%–98%). For fissures **recurrent after LIS**, injection gave 74% healing with 10% temporary flatus incontinence (Weak, Low, 2C). ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]], [[ascrs-2023-anal-fissures]])
- **In [[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease]]:** treat refractory fissures with a CCB first; the role of botulinum toxin is uncertain, and LIS is used with great caution. ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]])

## Other Sites

- **UES — inability to belch syndrome (Rome V category B3c, "retrograde cricopharyngeal dysfunction"):** the **best-established treatment**; manometric abnormalities are largely eliminated or improved by injection, and clinical effects seem to **last ≥6 months** in most patients, though with no sham-controlled trial the responder rate and long-term outcome are unclear. Criteria and workup: [[abdominal-bloating-and-distention]]. ([[rome-v-2026-dgbi]], [[rome-v-2026-gastroduodenal-disorders]])
- **Cricopharyngeus — myositis-related dysphagia** (polymyositis, dermatomyositis, inclusion body myositis): cricopharyngeus-directed dilation, botulinum toxin injection or myotomy often have important roles. ([[aga-2024-esophageal-immunity-infection]])
- **Nonachalasia spastic disorders and EGJOO:** evaluate comprehensively with symptom correlation, and exhaust the less invasive options — medical therapy, botulinum toxin injection, endoscopic dilation — before considering POEM in very selected cases. ([[aga-2024-poem-advances]])

## Where the Evidence Does Not Support It

- **Defecation disorders** (injected into the anal sphincter complex, after failed medical management and [[biofeedback-therapy|biofeedback]] with dyssynergia from puborectalis dysfunction): evidence poor and results short-lived — the paralytic effect is reversible, with new nerve fibril growth in **2–3 months** — and **combined adverse effects 14.2%** (flatus and FI, occasionally more serious) in a systematic review of 3 small RCTs and 8 uncontrolled studies. Unlikely to be a practical long-term solution. ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]], key concept 13)
- **Chronic proctalgia syndromes** (levator ani syndrome, idiopathic chronic proctalgia): **no evidence** supports botulinum toxin or digital rectal massage — biofeedback is what ACG recommends for levator syndrome with abnormal [[anorectal-manometry|anorectal manometry (ARM)]] (Strong, Very low). ([[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]])
