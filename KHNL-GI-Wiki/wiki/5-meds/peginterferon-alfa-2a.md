## Place in Therapy

Pegylated interferon alfa-2a (peg-IFN-α-2a): once-weekly subcutaneous (SC) injection. US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) label covers two indications only — **chronic hepatitis C (CHC)** and **chronic hepatitis B (CHB)**, both with compensated liver disease ([[pegasys-2023-peginterferon-alfa-2a-label]]).

| Use | Label status | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **[[chronic-hepatitis-b\|CHB]]** — adults hepatitis B e antigen (HBeAg)-positive or -negative with viral replication and liver inflammation; children ≥3 y HBeAg-positive, non-cirrhotic, with raised alanine aminotransferase (ALT) | Labelled | **Rarely used** — limited applicability, safety and tolerability vs [[hbv-nucleoside-analogs\|nucleos(t)ide analogues (NAs)]]; functional cure (hepatitis B surface antigen [HBsAg] loss) infrequent, though higher than with NAs ([[aasld-idsa-2025-chb-treatment]]) |
| **[[hepatitis-d\|Chronic hepatitis delta virus (HDV)]]** | **Off-label** — the label has no hepatitis D indication | The **only treatment available in the US** for chronic HDV (peg-IFN-α-2a or 2b) per the AGA clinical practice update (CPU), whose table lists peg-IFN-α as US-approved ([[aga-2025-hepatitis-delta]]). Regimen and response → [[hepatitis-d]] |
| **[[hepatitis-c\|CHC]]** — adults; children ≥5 y with ribavirin | Labelled | With other hepatitis C virus (HCV) antiviral drugs; **monotherapy only** if those are contraindicated or not tolerated. Regimen choice → [[direct-acting-antivirals]] |

- **CHB, immune-tolerant phase:** NA plus peg-IFN for 1–1.5 y lowered hepatitis B virus (HBV) DNA but **had limited effect on HBeAg and HBsAg loss/seroconversion**. Peg-IFN add-on or switch from an NA to reach functional cure is not addressed by AASLD/IDSA 2025 ([[aasld-idsa-2025-chb-treatment]]).
- **CHC, label limitations of use:** not recommended alone or with ribavirin only (no other HCV antiviral) after a prior interferon-alfa failure; not recommended after solid organ transplantation.

## Dosing

**SC injection into the thigh or abdomen, once weekly.** Supplied as a 180 mcg/mL single-dose vial and a 180 mcg/0.5 mL prefilled syringe (graduated at 90, 135, 180 mcg). Vial: **1 mL = 180 mcg, 0.75 mL = 135 mcg, 0.5 mL = 90 mcg**; syringe: 0.5 / 0.375 / 0.25 mL. Pediatric doses are drawn from the vial with a 1 mL tuberculin syringe ([[pegasys-2023-peginterferon-alfa-2a-label]]).

| Setting | Dose and duration |
|---|---|
| **Adults, CHB** | **180 mcg weekly × 48 weeks** |
| Adults, CHC genotypes 1–4, with other HCV antiviral drugs | 180 mcg weekly; duration per the companion drug's label |
| Adults, CHC, with ribavirin only | 180 mcg weekly — **48 wk** genotypes 1 and 4, **24 wk** genotypes 2 and 3 |
| Adults, CHC genotypes 5 and 6 | **Insufficient data for a dose recommendation** |
| Adults, CHC monotherapy | 180 mcg weekly × **48 weeks** |
| Adults, CHC with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) coinfection | 180 mcg weekly; with ribavirin only, **48 weeks regardless of genotype** |
| Pediatric CHB (HBeAg-positive, ≥3 y) | **180 mcg/1.73 m² × body surface area (BSA) weekly, max 180 mcg, × 48 wk** |
| Pediatric CHC (≥5 y, with ribavirin) | 180 mcg/1.73 m² × BSA weekly, max 180 mcg — 24 wk genotypes 2/3, 48 wk others |

- A patient turning 18 during therapy **keeps the pediatric dose** to completion.
- **CHC genotype 1 futility stop** (with ribavirin or alone): discontinue if HCV RNA has not fallen **at least 2 log₁₀ from baseline by 12 weeks**, or is not undetectable **after 24 weeks**.

### Dose Modification

**Cytopenias (adults)** — after improvement, consider re-escalating to the prior dose.

| Value | Dose |
|---|---|
| Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) **500 to <750/mm³** | **135 mcg** weekly |
| ANC **<500/mm³** | Hold until **>1,000/mm³**, restart at **90 mcg** weekly, monitor ANC |
| Platelets **25,000 to <50,000/mm³** | **90 mcg** weekly |
| Platelets **<25,000/mm³** | **Discontinue** |

**ALT elevation (adults)**

- **Any indication — discontinue immediately** if ALT rises progressively despite dose reduction, **or** the rise comes with increased bilirubin or evidence of hepatic decompensation.
- **CHC, progressive rise above baseline** → reduce to **135 mcg** with more frequent liver-function testing.
- **CHB, ALT >5 × upper limit of normal (ULN)** → monitor liver function more often and consider **either reducing to 135 mcg or temporarily holding**.
- **CHB, persistent severe flare (ALT >10 × ULN)** → **consider discontinuation**.
- After a reduction or hold, therapy can resume once the ALT flare subsides.

> [!pitfall] Hepatitis B flares on peg-IFN are common
> ALT flares **>10 × ULN** occurred in **12% (HBeAg-negative) and 18% (HBeAg-positive)** on treatment and **7% and 12%** after stopping; marked flares have come with other liver-test abnormalities. Monitor liver function more often during a flare and apply the stop rule above ([[pegasys-2023-peginterferon-alfa-2a-label]]).

**Renal impairment (adults)** — estimate creatinine clearance before the first dose.

| Creatinine clearance | Dose |
|---|---|
| **30–50 mL/min** | 180 mcg weekly |
| **<30 mL/min, including hemodialysis** | **135 mcg weekly** |

- On the reduced dose, severe reactions or laboratory abnormalities → **90 mcg weekly until they abate**; **discontinue if intolerance persists**. Any degree of renal impairment → watch closely for laboratory abnormalities (especially falling hemoglobin). **No pediatric renal dosing exists.**

**Depression (adults and children)** — doses below are adult; children get the same steps as mcg/1.73 m² × BSA.

| Severity | Initial 4–8 wk: dose | Initial 4–8 wk: visits | After 8 wk: stable | After 8 wk: improves | After 8 wk: worsens |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mild** | No change | Weekly, visit and/or phone | Continue weekly visits | Resume normal visit schedule | Consider psychiatric consultation; **discontinue, or reduce to 135 mcg or 90 mcg** weekly |
| **Moderate** | **Reduce to 135 mcg or 90 mcg** weekly | Weekly (office visit at least every other week) | **Consider psychiatric consultation**; continue reduced dose | Improved and stable 4 wk → may resume normal visits; continue reduced dose or return to normal | **Immediate psychiatric consultation; discontinue permanently** |
| **Severe** | **Discontinue permanently** | **Immediate** psychiatric consultation | Psychiatric therapy necessary | Psychiatric therapy necessary | Psychiatric therapy necessary |

**Cytopenias and ALT (children)** — doses are per 1.73 m² × BSA, weekly.

| Finding | CHC | CHB |
|---|---|---|
| ANC 750–999/mm³ | Wk 1–2: 135 mcg immediately · wk 3–48: no change | No change |
| ANC 500–749/mm³ | Wk 1–2: hold until ANC >750, resume at 135 mcg; check ANC three times weekly to confirm >750 · wk 3–48: 135 mcg immediately | 135 mcg immediately |
| ANC 250–499/mm³ | Wk 1–2: hold until ANC >750, resume at **90 mcg** · wk 3–48: hold until ANC >750, resume at 135 mcg | Interrupt until ANC ≥1,000, resume at **90 mcg** and monitor |
| ANC <250/mm³ or febrile neutropenia | **Discontinue** | **Discontinue** |
| Platelets 25,000 to <50,000/mm³ | 90 mcg | 90 mcg |
| Platelets <25,000/mm³ | **Discontinue** | **Discontinue** |
| ALT persistently or increasingly ≥5 but <10 × ULN | 135 mcg; monitor weekly, reduce further if needed until stable or ALT falls | 135 mcg; monitor ALT weekly until stable or falling |
| ALT persistently ≥10 × ULN | **Discontinue** | **Discontinue** |

## Contraindications

**Contraindicated** ([[pegasys-2023-peginterferon-alfa-2a-label]]):
- **[[autoimmune-hepatitis|Autoimmune hepatitis]].**
- **Hepatic decompensation before treatment in [[cirrhosis|cirrhotic]] patients — Child-Pugh score >6 (class B and C)**; **≥6** in cirrhotic CHC patients coinfected with HIV.
- Known hypersensitivity to alpha interferons or any component (urticaria, angioedema, bronchoconstriction, anaphylaxis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome).
- **Neonates and infants** — the product contains benzyl alcohol.
- With ribavirin: **pregnant women, and men whose female partners are pregnant**.
- AGA CPU (HDV context): contraindicated in **advanced liver disease** or **major extrahepatic diseases** ([[aga-2025-hepatitis-delta]]).

**Decompensation during treatment** → discontinue immediately. The label's threshold differs by section: Child-Pugh **>6** (CHC dosing, contraindications) vs **≥6** (hepatic impairment section, cirrhotic CHC).

**Not recommended / not established:**
- **Solid organ transplant recipients** (CHC: not recommended); liver and renal **graft rejection** reported.
- CHB coinfected with HCV or HIV; CHC coinfected with HBV, or with HIV and CD4+ count <100/mm³.
- Pediatric CHB with cirrhosis; children below 3 y (CHB) / 5 y (CHC).
- **Significant or unstable cardiac disease** — should not receive peg-IFN/ribavirin; caution with any pre-existing cardiac disease.

## Monitoring

- **Before starting:** hematology and chemistry for all · **pregnancy test** in females of reproductive potential · **electrocardiogram** if pre-existing cardiac abnormality · **baseline eye examination for every patient**.
- **After starting:** hematology at **2 and 4 weeks**, chemistry at **4 weeks**, then periodically (adult trials: 1, 2, 4, 6, 8 weeks, then every 4–6 weeks). **Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) every 12 weeks** in trials. **Monthly pregnancy tests** during combination therapy with ribavirin and **for at least 6 months after**.
- **Eyes:** **periodic eye exams** for pre-existing ophthalmologic disorders (e.g., diabetic or hypertensive retinopathy); **prompt complete exam** for any new ocular symptoms.
- **Acceptable baseline values** (the trials' entry criteria): platelets **≥90,000/mm³** (≥75,000 in HCV cirrhosis, ≥70,000 in CHC/HIV) · ANC **≥1,500/mm³** · creatinine **<1.5 × ULN** · normal or controlled thyroid function · hemoglobin **≥12 g/dL women, ≥13 g/dL men** (≥11 / ≥12 with HIV coinfection) · CD4 **≥200/mm³**, or ≥100 but <200 with HIV-1 RNA <5,000 copies/mL.

## Adverse Effects

> **Boxed warning.** Alpha interferons may cause or aggravate **fatal or life-threatening neuropsychiatric, autoimmune, ischemic, and infectious disorders.** Monitor with periodic clinical and laboratory evaluation; **withdraw therapy** for persistently severe or worsening signs. In many but not all cases these resolve after stopping.

- **Most common (>40%): fatigue/asthenia, pyrexia, myalgia, headache.** Pediatric profile is similar.
- **Neuropsychiatric** — suicide, suicidal and homicidal ideation, relapse of addiction, overdose, in patients **with and without** prior psychiatric illness. Extreme caution with any depression history.
- **Bone marrow suppression** — severe cytopenias, rarely aplastic anemia. Caution if baseline neutrophils <1,500/mm³, platelets <90,000/mm³ or hemoglobin <10 g/dL. Severe lymphopenia (<500/mm³) in ~5% on monotherapy and 14% on combination therapy; severe anemia (<10 g/dL) in 13% on combination therapy.
- **Endocrine** — causes or aggravates hypo- and hyperthyroidism, hyperglycemia, hypoglycemia, diabetes. **Do not start** if not controllable with medication at baseline; uncontrollable on treatment → may need discontinuation. Hypo-/hyperthyroidism needing treatment, dose change or discontinuation (hepatitis C studies): **4% / 1% on monotherapy, 4% / 2% with ribavirin**; about **half still abnormal** in follow-up.
- **Ophthalmologic** — decreased or lost vision, retinopathy including macular edema, retinal artery or vein thrombosis, retinal hemorrhages, cotton wool spots, optic neuritis, papilledema, serous retinal detachment. **Discontinue** for new or worsening disease.
- **Autoimmune** — myositis, hepatitis, thrombotic and idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura, psoriasis, rheumatoid arthritis, interstitial nephritis, thyroiditis, systemic lupus erythematosus. Use with caution in autoimmune disorders.
- Also cardiovascular, cerebrovascular, pulmonary (including bronchiolitis obliterans and interstitial pneumonitis), serious infections, severe skin reactions, and **peripheral neuropathy with telbivudine**.
- **Pediatric growth inhibition** — in CHB patients 3–17 y, **13% were >15 percentiles below their baseline weight curve and 6% below their height curve at week 48**; at 5 years post-treatment, **29% and 18%**.
- **Postmarketing:** pure red cell aplasia, hearing impairment and loss, tongue pigmentation, liver and renal graft rejection, seizures.

> [!pitfall] Two gastrointestinal toxicities that stop the drug outright
> **Colitis** — ulcerative and hemorrhagic/[[colon-ischemia|ischemic]], sometimes fatal, **within 12 weeks of starting**; abdominal pain, bloody diarrhea and fever → **discontinue immediately**, and it usually resolves in **1–3 weeks**. **[[acute-pancreatitis|Pancreatitis]]** — sometimes fatal; **suspend** for suggestive symptoms, **discontinue** once diagnosed ([[pegasys-2023-peginterferon-alfa-2a-label]]).

## Drug Interactions

- **Inhibits cytochrome P450 1A2** → **theophylline** exposure (area under the curve) rises 25%; monitor levels and adjust. No effect on CYP2C9, 2C19, 2D6 or 3A4 substrates.
- **Methadone** levels 10–15% above baseline; monitor for toxicity.
- **HIV nucleoside analogues** — hepatic decompensation (some fatal) in cirrhotic CHC/HIV patients; monitor closely and consider reducing or stopping peg-IFN, ribavirin or both if toxicity worsens. **Zidovudine:** more severe neutropenia and anemia — consider stopping zidovudine.
- **[[thiopurines|Azathioprine]]** — pancytopenia and marrow suppression **3–7 weeks** into concomitant pegylated interferon/ribavirin plus azathioprine. For pancytopenia stop all three; **do not reintroduce peg-IFN/ribavirin with concomitant azathioprine**.
