## Place in Therapy

Pegylated interferon alfa-2a (peg-IFN-α-2a): weekly subcutaneous (SC) immunomodulator. Two labelled indications — **chronic hepatitis C (CHC)** and **chronic hepatitis B (CHB)** ([[pegasys-2023-peginterferon-alfa-2a-label]]).

- **[[chronic-hepatitis-b|CHB]]:** **rarely used.** Limited applicability, poorer safety and tolerability than nucleos(t)ide analogues; functional cure is higher than with [[hbv-nucleoside-analogs|NAs]] but still infrequent ([[aasld-idsa-2025-chb-treatment]]).
- **[[hepatitis-d|HDV]]:** the **only US-available** therapy — but **off-label**; the label does not mention hepatitis D. Indication and duration are on [[hepatitis-d]].
- **[[hepatitis-c|CHC]]:** historical. Direct-acting antiviral regimens have displaced it; labelled monotherapy only where other hepatitis C virus (HCV) drugs are contraindicated or not tolerated.

## Dosing

**Subcutaneous injection into the thigh or abdomen, once weekly.** Supplied as a 180 mcg/mL single-dose vial and a 180 mcg/0.5 mL prefilled syringe (graduated at 90, 135, 180 mcg). From the vial: **0.75 mL = 135 mcg, 0.5 mL = 90 mcg**.

| Setting | Dose and duration |
|---|---|
| **Adults, CHB** | **180 mcg weekly × 48 weeks** |
| Adults, CHC, with other HCV drugs | 180 mcg weekly; duration per the companion drug |
| Adults, CHC, with ribavirin only | 180 mcg weekly — **48 wk** genotypes 1 and 4, **24 wk** genotypes 2 and 3 |
| Adults, CHC monotherapy, or CHC/HIV with ribavirin | 180 mcg weekly × **48 weeks** (any genotype in coinfection) |
| Pediatric CHB (HBeAg-positive, ≥3 y) | **180 mcg/1.73 m² × body surface area (BSA) weekly, max 180 mcg, × 48 wk** |
| Pediatric CHC (≥5 y, with ribavirin) | 180 mcg/1.73 m² × BSA weekly, max 180 mcg — 24 wk genotypes 2/3, 48 wk others |

- **Genotypes 5 and 6: insufficient data for a dose.**
- A patient turning 18 during therapy **keeps the pediatric dose** to completion.
- **CHC genotype 1 futility stop:** discontinue if HCV RNA has not fallen **≥2 log₁₀ from baseline by 12 weeks**, or is still detectable **after 24 weeks**.

### Dose Modification

**Cytopenias (adults)** — after recovery, consider re-escalating to the prior dose.

| Value | Dose |
|---|---|
| Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) **500 to <750/mm³** | **135 mcg** weekly |
| ANC **<500/mm³** | Hold until **>1,000/mm³**, restart at **90 mcg** weekly, monitor ANC |
| Platelets **25,000 to <50,000/mm³** | **90 mcg** weekly |
| Platelets **<25,000/mm³** | **Discontinue** |

**Renal impairment** — evaluate renal function before the first dose.

| Creatinine clearance | Dose |
|---|---|
| **30–50 mL/min** | 180 mcg weekly (no change) |
| **<30 mL/min, including hemodialysis** | **135 mcg weekly** |

- On the reduced dose, severe reactions or laboratory abnormalities → cut to **90 mcg weekly until they abate**; **discontinue if intolerance persists**. **No pediatric renal dosing exists.**

**Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) elevation**

- **CHB, ALT >5 × upper limit of normal (ULN)** → monitor liver function more often and **either reduce to 135 mcg or hold**.
- **CHB, persistent severe flare (>10 × ULN)** → **consider discontinuation**.
- **CHC, progressive rise above baseline** → reduce to **135 mcg** with more frequent liver-function testing.
- **Any setting — discontinue immediately** if ALT rises progressively despite reduction, **or** is accompanied by rising bilirubin or evidence of hepatic decompensation. Therapy may restart once a flare subsides.

**Depression** — the label grades the response by severity and by whether it improves after 8 weeks.

| Severity | Initial (4–8 wk) | After 8 weeks |
|---|---|---|
| **Mild** | No dose change; review **weekly** by visit or phone | Worsens → consider psychiatric consultation; **discontinue, or reduce to 135 mcg, then 90 mcg** weekly |
| **Moderate** | **Reduce to 135 mcg or 90 mcg** weekly; review weekly (office visit ≥ every other week) | Improves and stable 4 wk → resume normal visits, continue reduced or normal dose · **Worsens → immediate psychiatric consultation, permanent discontinuation** |
| **Severe** | **Discontinue permanently**; **immediate** psychiatric consultation | Psychiatric therapy necessary |

> [!pitfall] A hepatitis B flare is expected — bilirubin tells you which kind
> Transient ALT flares **>10 × ULN** hit **12% (HBeAg-negative) and 18% (HBeAg-positive)** on treatment, and **7% and 12%** after stopping. The ALT value alone does not separate an immune flare from incipient decompensation — **rising bilirubin, prolonged prothrombin time and falling albumin** do. Those accompany the rise that means stop ([[pegasys-2023-peginterferon-alfa-2a-label]]).

## Contraindications

- **[[autoimmune-hepatitis|Autoimmune hepatitis]].**
- **Hepatic decompensation — Child-Pugh score >6 (class B and C)** in [[cirrhosis|cirrhotic]] patients before treatment (**≥6** in cirrhotic CHC patients coinfected with HIV).
- Known hypersensitivity to alpha interferons or any component (urticaria, angioedema, bronchoconstriction, anaphylaxis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome).
- **Neonates and infants** — the product contains benzyl alcohol.
- With ribavirin: **pregnant women, and men whose female partners are pregnant**.
- Not recommended in **solid organ transplant recipients** (CHC), where liver and renal **graft rejection** have been reported.
- **Not established** in CHB coinfected with HCV or HIV, in pediatric CHB with cirrhosis, or below 3 y (CHB) / 5 y (CHC).

> **Boxed warning.** Alpha interferons may cause or aggravate **fatal or life-threatening neuropsychiatric, autoimmune, ischemic, and infectious disorders.** Monitor with periodic clinical and laboratory evaluation; **withdraw therapy** for persistently severe or worsening signs. In many but not all cases these resolve after stopping.

## Monitoring

- **Before starting:** hematology and chemistry for all · **pregnancy test** in females of childbearing potential · **electrocardiogram** if pre-existing cardiac abnormality · **eye examination for every patient**.
- **After starting:** hematology at **2 and 4 weeks**, chemistry at **4 weeks**, then periodically (trials: 1, 2, 4, 6, 8 weeks, then every 4–6 weeks). **Thyroid-stimulating hormone every 12 weeks.** **Monthly pregnancy tests** during combination therapy and **for 6 months after**.
- **Acceptable baseline values** (the trials' entry criteria): platelets **≥90,000/mm³** (≥75,000 in HCV cirrhosis, ≥70,000 in CHC/HIV) · ANC **≥1,500/mm³** · creatinine **<1.5 × ULN** · normal or controlled thyroid function · hemoglobin **≥12 g/dL women, ≥13 g/dL men** (≥11 / ≥12 with HIV coinfection) · CD4 **≥200/mm³**, or ≥100 but <200 with HIV-1 RNA <5,000 copies/mL.

## Adverse Effects

- **Most common (>40%): fatigue/asthenia, pyrexia, myalgia, headache.** Pediatric profile is similar.
- **Neuropsychiatric** — suicide, suicidal and homicidal ideation, relapse of addiction, overdose, in patients **with and without** prior psychiatric illness. Extreme caution with any depression history.
- **Bone marrow suppression** — severe cytopenias, rarely aplastic anemia. Caution if baseline neutrophils <1,500/mm³, platelets <90,000/mm³ or hemoglobin <10 g/dL. Severe lymphopenia (<500/mm³) in ~5% on monotherapy and 14% on combination therapy; severe anemia (<10 g/dL) in 13% on combination therapy.
- **Endocrine** — causes or aggravates hypo- and hyperthyroidism, hyperglycemia, hypoglycemia, diabetes. **Do not start** if uncontrollable at baseline; about **half of thyroid abnormalities persist** into follow-up.
- **Ophthalmologic** — retinopathy, macular edema, retinal artery or vein thrombosis, optic neuritis, serous retinal detachment. **Discontinue** for new or worsening disease.
- **Autoimmune** — myositis, hepatitis, thrombotic and idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura, psoriasis, rheumatoid arthritis, interstitial nephritis, thyroiditis, systemic lupus erythematosus.
- Also cardiovascular, cerebrovascular, pulmonary (including bronchiolitis obliterans and interstitial pneumonitis), serious infections, severe skin reactions, and **peripheral neuropathy with telbivudine**.
- **Pediatric growth inhibition** — in CHB patients 3–17 y, **13% were >15 percentiles below their baseline weight curve and 6% below their height curve at week 48**; at 5 years post-treatment, **29% and 18%**.
- **Postmarketing:** pure red cell aplasia, hearing impairment and loss, tongue pigmentation, liver and renal graft rejection, seizures.

> [!pitfall] Two gastrointestinal toxicities that stop the drug outright
> **Colitis** — ulcerative and hemorrhagic/ischemic, sometimes fatal, **within 12 weeks of starting**; abdominal pain, bloody diarrhea and fever → **discontinue immediately**, and it usually resolves in **1–3 weeks**. **Pancreatitis** — sometimes fatal; **suspend** for suggestive symptoms, **discontinue** once diagnosed ([[pegasys-2023-peginterferon-alfa-2a-label]]).

## Drug Interactions

- **Inhibits cytochrome P450 1A2** → **theophylline** exposure rises ~25%; monitor levels and adjust. No effect on CYP2C9, 2C19, 2D6 or 3A4 substrates.
- **Methadone** levels 10–15% above baseline; monitor for toxicity.
- **Nucleoside analogues** — monitor closely; reduce or stop peg-IFN, ribavirin or both if toxicity worsens. **Zidovudine:** watch for worsening neutropenia and anemia.
- ⚠ **Azathioprine** — pancytopenia and marrow suppression **3–7 weeks** into concomitant pegylated interferon/ribavirin plus [[thiopurines|azathioprine]]. Stop all three; **do not reintroduce peg-IFN/ribavirin with concomitant azathioprine**.
