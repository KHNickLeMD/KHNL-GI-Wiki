Minimally absorbed, **peripherally acting mixed μ-/κ-opioid receptor agonist and δ-opioid receptor antagonist** — the δ-antagonism is designed to reduce constipation and increase analgesic potency vs a pure μ-agonist. US Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved for men and women with [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|diarrhea-predominant irritable bowel syndrome (IBS-D)]].

- [[aga-2022-ibs-d|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2022]]: **suggests** eluxadoline in IBS-D — *conditional recommendation, moderate certainty*.
- [[acg-2020-ibs|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2020]]: **suggests** mixed opioid agonists/antagonists to treat global IBS-D symptoms — *conditional recommendation, moderate quality of evidence*.

## Dosing

| Situation | Dose |
|---|---|
| Standard — generally recommended in most patients | **100 mg orally (PO) twice daily (BID)** |
| Cannot tolerate 100 mg | 75 mg PO BID |
| Mild or moderate hepatic impairment | 75 mg PO BID |
| Concomitant organic anion transporting polypeptide 1B1 (OATP1B1) inhibitor | 75 mg PO BID |
| Severe hepatic impairment — Child-Pugh class C | **Do not use** |

*Eluxadoline dose by clinical situation*
*Source: built from the AGA 2022 dosing statements, with the Child-Pugh class C cut-off from ACG 2020; neither guideline tabulates this*
*[[aga-2022-ibs-d|AGA 2022 Clinical Practice Guideline: Pharmacological Management of IBS-D]] · [[acg-2020-ibs|ACG 2020 Clinical Guideline: Management of IBS]]*
*PO = orally · BID = twice daily · OATP1B1 = organic anion transporting polypeptide 1B1*

- Benefit at 75 mg is **very similar** to 100 mg ([[aga-2022-ibs-d]]); sphincter of Oddi spasm events clustered at the 100 mg dose ([[aga-2017-opioids-in-gastroenterology]]).
- Neither guideline names which drugs are OATP1B1 inhibitors.
- Child-Pugh point table: [[cirrhosis]].

## Contraindications

- **No gallbladder** (post-cholecystectomy) — risk of [[acute-pancreatitis|pancreatitis]].
- **>3 alcohol-containing beverages per day**, or a history of alcoholism, alcohol abuse or addiction.
- **History of pancreatitis.**
- **Biliary duct obstruction.**
- **[[sphincter-of-oddi-dysfunction|Sphincter of Oddi disease or dysfunction]]**, including prior sphincter of Oddi spasm (SOS).
- **Severe hepatic impairment** (see [[#Dosing]]).

Basis: in the 2 phase 3 trials all **5 pancreatitis cases and all 8 SOS events** occurred in patients **without a gallbladder or with a history of alcohol abuse**, and none occurred on placebo ([[aga-2022-ibs-d]]); history of pancreatitis, SOS, alcohol abuse and post-cholecystectomy biliary pain were trial **exclusion criteria**.

> [!pitfall] No gallbladder — not even a single dose
> FDA safety communication, 15 March 2017: two deaths in post-cholecystectomy patients on eluxadoline — one from pancreatitis with **symptom onset within 1 hour of a single dose**, one from sphincter of Oddi spasm with severe abdominal pain and vomiting **shortly after the first dose** ([[aga-2017-opioids-in-gastroenterology]]). There is no safe trial period in this population.

## Efficacy

**Responder definition (FDA composite endpoint):** ≥30% decrease from baseline in the daily average worst abdominal pain score **and**, on the same day, a daily stool consistency score <5 on the [[bristol-stool-form-scale|Bristol Stool Form Scale]], on ≥50% of days evaluated ([[acg-2020-ibs]]).

| Dose | Responder, wk 1–12 | Responder, wk 1–26 | Number needed to treat (NNT), wk 1–12 | NNT, wk 1–26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 75 mg BID (n = 806) | 26.2% | 26.7% | 10 | 14 |
| 100 mg BID (n = 809) | 27.0% | 31.0% | 10 | 9 |
| Placebo (n = 808) | 16.7% | 19.5% | — | — |

*Eluxadoline vs placebo on the FDA composite responder endpoint, by dose and treatment window*
*Source: data from the 2 pooled phase 3 trials in adults meeting Rome III criteria for IBS-D, as reported in ACG 2020 (P < 0.001 vs placebo at both time points)*
*[[acg-2020-ibs|ACG 2020 Clinical Guideline: Management of IBS]]*
*NNT = number needed to treat · BID = twice daily*

At 100 mg BID over the first 12 weeks, [[aga-2022-ibs-d|AGA 2022]] also reports (relative risk [RR] for **failure** of symptom relief, 95% confidence interval [CI]):

- Adequate relief of IBS-D symptoms for ≥6 of the first 12 weeks: **38.4% vs 29.2%** (RR 0.87, 0.81–0.93).
- Stool consistency responder RR **0.84** (0.80–0.88); ≥50% urgency-free days RR **0.84** (0.78–0.90); clinically meaningful improvement in IBS quality of life (IBS-QOL) RR **0.84** (0.74–0.95).
- Abdominal pain RR **0.92** (0.84–1.00) — the weakest outcome; [[aga-2017-opioids-in-gastroenterology|AGA 2017]] notes abdominal pain scores were **not significant for either the 75 or the 100 mg dose** in the pivotal trials.
- Similar results for all outcomes at week 26. Overall certainty **moderate**, rated down for imprecision.

> [!pearl] Pick it for diarrhea and urgency, not for pain
> Eluxadoline improves stool consistency and urgency more than abdominal pain, so it suits IBS-D with predominant, bothersome diarrhea better than IBS-D whose dominant complaint is severe pain ([[aga-2022-ibs-d]]).

## Adverse Effects / Monitoring

| Adverse event | Eluxadoline | Placebo | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Constipation | 8% | 2.5% | [[acg-2020-ibs\|ACG 2020]] |
| Nausea | 7.7% | 5% | [[acg-2020-ibs\|ACG 2020]] |
| Abdominal pain | ~7% | not reported | [[aga-2022-ibs-d\|AGA 2022]] |
| Discontinuation for adverse events (AEs) | ~8% | ~4% | [[aga-2022-ibs-d\|AGA 2022]] |
| Pancreatitis | 0.4% | not reported | [[acg-2020-ibs\|ACG 2020]] |
| Sphincter of Oddi spasm | 0.5% — **all in patients without a gallbladder** | not reported | [[acg-2020-ibs\|ACG 2020]] |

*Adverse events on eluxadoline vs placebo*
*Source: data from the phase 3 program (common AEs, n = 1,666 on eluxadoline) and the pooled safety analysis (n = 807 at 75 mg BID, n = 1,032 at 100 mg BID, n = 975 placebo) as reported in ACG 2020, plus the AGA 2022 evidence summary*
*[[acg-2020-ibs|ACG 2020 Clinical Guideline: Management of IBS]] · [[aga-2022-ibs-d|AGA 2022 Clinical Practice Guideline: Pharmacological Management of IBS-D]]*
*AE = adverse event · BID = twice daily*

- Constipation tends to occur **within the first 3 months**; cardiac events were few and no different from placebo ([[acg-2020-ibs]]).
- Number needed to harm (NNH), based on AEs prompting discontinuation: **25 for 75 mg and 23 for 100 mg** ([[acg-2020-ibs]]).
- **Pancreatitis and SOS are the signal events** — counsel every patient to stop the drug and report severe abdominal pain. Lipase normalizes within days of stopping ([[acg-2020-ibs]]).
- Risk is dose-related: in a study of nearly 2,000 patients with IBS-D, 10 developed sphincter of Oddi spasm, **most on the higher 100 mg dose**; in the 10 months after US release the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System received **99 reports of pancreatitis and 39 of sphincter of Oddi spasm** ([[aga-2017-opioids-in-gastroenterology]]).
