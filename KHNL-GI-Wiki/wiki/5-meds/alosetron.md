## Class / Mechanism
- Selective **5-hydroxytryptamine type 3 (5-HT3) receptor antagonist**; mechanism believed to be **both centrally and peripherally mediated** ([[aga-2022-ibs-d|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2022]]).
- Primary action in [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|diarrhea-predominant irritable bowel syndrome (IBS-D)]]: **slowing of intestinal transit** ([[acg-2020-ibs|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2020]]).
- Other 5-HT3 antagonists also relieve IBS-D symptoms — ramosetron, and ondansetron, which **does not improve abdominal pain** ([[rome-v-2026-bowel-disorders|Rome V 2026]]).

## Indication & Population Restriction
*Place of alosetron among the IBS-D drug options: see [[irritable-bowel-syndrome]].*

- **ACG 2020** — recommends alosetron to relieve **global IBS-D symptoms in women with severe symptoms who have failed conventional therapy**; *Conditional recommendation; low quality of evidence*.
- **AGA 2022** — suggests using alosetron in patients with IBS-D; *Conditional recommendation, moderate certainty*. Implementation remark: **restricted for use in women with severe IBS-D under a risk-management program**.
- **"Severe" = ≥1 of** ([[aga-2022-ibs-d|AGA 2022]]):
  - Frequent and severe abdominal pain/discomfort
  - Frequent bowel urgency or [[fecal-incontinence|fecal incontinence]]
  - Disability or restriction of daily activities due to IBS
- **Restricted access.** Originally US Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved in 2000 for IBS-D in women ([[aga-2022-ibs-d|AGA 2022]]); **voluntarily withdrawn 28 November 2000** after postmarketing reports of **[[colon-ischemia|ischemic colitis]]**, **complicated constipation (obstruction or perforation)** and **death**; **reintroduced June 2002** under a **risk evaluation and mitigation strategy (REMS)** ([[acg-2020-ibs|ACG 2020]]).
- **REMS eligibility — all three required** ([[acg-2020-ibs|ACG 2020]]):
  - Woman
  - **Chronic (>6 months), severe IBS-D symptoms**
  - **Previous lack of response to traditional therapies**
- ACG 2020 notes that **"traditional therapies" has not been further defined**, and that multiple agents have been FDA-approved for IBS-D in the years since the REMS protocol was established.
- Beyond this restriction, ACG 2020 and AGA 2022 give no contraindications and no laboratory monitoring requirements.

## Dosing
The REMS caps prescribing at **0.5–1.0 mg twice daily**, which [[acg-2020-ibs|ACG 2020]] credits for the low postmarketing event rates below.

| Step | Dose | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Start | 0.5 mg twice daily | — |
| Escalate | 1 mg twice daily | Symptoms not controlled after 4 weeks on the starting dose |
| Discontinue | — | Symptoms persist after a further 4 weeks at 1 mg twice daily |

*Alosetron dose steps and their 4-week decision points*
*built from the dosing guidance under AGA 2022 Statement 3 (the guideline prints no dosing table)*
*[[aga-2022-ibs-d|AGA Clinical Practice Guideline: Pharmacological Management of IBS-D (2022)]]*

> [!pitfall] Constipation means stop, not dose through
> Stop alosetron until symptoms resolve; it may then be restarted at **0.5 mg once daily**. If constipation **recurs at the lower dose, discontinue** ([[aga-2022-ibs-d|AGA 2022]]).

## Efficacy
- Evidence base: **8 randomized controlled trials (RCTs), 4227 patients** (alosetron n=2517; placebo n=1710) in nonconstipating IBS — seven 12-week trials plus one 48-week trial ([[aga-2022-ibs-d|AGA 2022]]).
- Superior to placebo for **global symptoms** (relative risk [RR] 0.60; 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.54–0.67) and **IBS pain and discomfort** (RR 0.83; 0.79–0.88); individual studies also showed improved **urgency, stool consistency and IBS quality of life (IBS-QOL)** ([[aga-2022-ibs-d|AGA 2022]]).
- Pooled **RR of symptom persistence 0.79** (0.69–0.90) across 8 RCTs, **number needed to treat (NNT) 7.5** ([[acg-2020-ibs|ACG 2020]]).
- Overall certainty in the evidence: **moderate** ([[aga-2022-ibs-d|AGA 2022]]).
