Oral small-molecule inhibitors of the **Janus kinase (JAK)–signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT) pathway**, used as advanced therapy in [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]]: **tofacitinib** ([[ulcerative-colitis|ulcerative colitis (UC)]]), **upadacitinib** (UC and [[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease (CD)]]), **filgotinib** (UC, Europe only). Upadacitinib holds the highest UC induction grade bar infliximab (Strong / High), yet the class is the only one in IBD that is *regulatorily gated* — the US label reserves it for patients who failed or cannot tolerate a tumor necrosis factor (TNF) antagonist. The gate traces to **ORAL Surveillance**, a rheumatoid arthritis (RA) trial whose entry criteria are the risk criteria to apply at the bedside.

## Class Structure and Mechanism

| Agent | JAK selectivity | US IBD indication | Route |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tofacitinib** | **Nonselective** — JAK **1, 2, 3** | UC | Oral (PO) |
| **Upadacitinib** | **Selective JAK-1** | **UC and CD** | PO |
| **Filgotinib** | JAK-1 selective | **None** — UC in **Europe**; *not available in the United States* | PO |

- JAK-STAT inhibition = **systemic immunosuppression**; may blunt vaccine response ([[acg-2025-ibd-preventive-care]]).
- **Small molecules, not proteins** — no antidrug antibodies, no **protein loss** through inflamed mucosa (a driver of infliximab non-response in severe colitis); the mechanistic argument for the class in acute severe disease ([[acg-2025-uc]]). Patient prefers **oral** → sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P) receptor modulator or JAK inhibitor ([[aga-2024-uc-pharm]]).

## Agents and Dosing

### Ulcerative Colitis dosing
*Per [[acg-2025-uc]].*

| Agent | Induction | Maintenance |
|---|---|---|
| **Tofacitinib** | **10 mg PO twice daily (BID) × 8 wk** (extend 8 more wk → **16 wk total** if no wk-8 response; in the US label) | **5 mg PO BID** or **10 mg PO BID** |
| **Upadacitinib** | **45 mg PO once daily × 8 wk** (8 more wk benefits some wk-8 non-responders — [[aga-2024-uc-pharm]]) | **15 mg** or **30 mg PO once daily** |

### Crohn's Disease dosing
*Per [[acg-2025-crohns]]. Upadacitinib is the only JAK inhibitor with a CD indication.*

| Agent | Induction | Maintenance |
|---|---|---|
| **Upadacitinib** | **45 mg PO once daily × 12 wk** | **15 mg** or **30 mg PO once daily** — **30 mg preferred**, particularly in more progressive, debilitating or treatment-refractory (e.g. anti-TNF–experienced) disease |

> [!pitfall] Don't carry the UC clock into a CD patient
> Upadacitinib 45 mg daily runs **8 weeks in UC** but **12 weeks in CD** — same drug, same dose, different endpoint. Twelve weeks is what the CD trials assessed ([[acg-2025-crohns]]).

### Extended induction

Non-response at the end of standard induction is **not** automatic failure, and delayed responders hold their response.

| Agent (UC) | Extension in wk-8 non-responders | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Tofacitinib** | 10 mg BID × **8 more weeks** | **51–52%** respond by wk 16; of those, **56.1%** still in remission at **36 mo**, **45.8%** corticosteroid-free remission at 1 y |
| **Upadacitinib** | 45 mg/day × **8 more weeks** | **48%** respond; **over half** maintain response at **1 y** |

*([[acg-2025-uc]], [[aga-2024-uc-pharm]])*

### Maintenance dose selection and de-escalation

An efficacy-vs-safety trade; the guidelines pull both ways.

**Higher dose:**
- **Prior anti-TNF exposure is the main indication.** Tofacitinib 10 mg BID benefits prior failures in response, remission, corticosteroid-free remission *and* endoscopic improvement; upadacitinib's higher dose is more effective in prior-exposed maintenance → **most patients on 30 mg/day** ([[acg-2025-uc]]). Also favored with **high inflammatory burden** or **more severe disease** ([[aga-2025-crohns-pharm]]).

**Lower dose:**
- Upadacitinib US label: **lowest effective maintenance dose** ([[acg-2025-uc]]).
- **Shingles and venous thromboembolism (VTE) are dose-dependent** — higher at tofacitinib 10 mg BID → monitor actively and use preventive strategies if kept there ([[aga-2024-uc-pharm]]).

> [!pearl] De-escalation is not freely reversible
> ~**29%** reduced to tofacitinib 5 mg BID had to go back up to 10 mg BID, and response was recaptured in only **63%**; in OCTAVE **25%** could not stay in remission on the lower dose ([[aga-2024-uc-pharm]]). Because relapsers are not reliably recaptured, the American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) calls it "prudent to consider 10 mg BID for maintenance in most patients" ([[acg-2025-uc]]).

No renal or hepatic dose adjustment is given, and no dose-escalation or re-induction protocol for secondary loss of response.

## Indications at a Glance

*All rows require **moderate-to-severe** activity (acute severe UC [ASUC] row excepted); criteria on [[ulcerative-colitis]] and [[crohns-disease]], endoscopic indices on [[ibd-endoscopic-scoring]].*

| Setting | Agent | Recommendation | Strength / Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [[ulcerative-colitis\|UC]] — induction | **Tofacitinib** (Rec 30) · **Upadacitinib** (Rec 31) | Recommend | **Strong / Moderate** · **Strong / High** | [[acg-2025-uc]] |
| UC — maintenance after induction with the same agent | Tofacitinib **or** upadacitinib | Continue | **Strong / Moderate** | [[acg-2025-uc]] Rec 43 |
| [[crohns-disease\|CD]] **with prior anti-TNF exposure** — induction **+** maintenance | **Upadacitinib** | Recommend | **Strong / Moderate** | [[acg-2025-crohns]] Rec 23 |
| [[perianal-fistulizing-crohns-disease\|Perianal fistulizing CD]] — induction | **Upadacitinib** | Suggest | Conditional / **Very low** | [[acg-2025-crohns]] Rec 29 |
| UC — advanced therapy vs no treatment | Tofacitinib, upadacitinib | Recommend | **Strong / Moderate-to-high** | [[aga-2024-uc-pharm]] Rec 1 |
| UC — same comparison | **Filgotinib** | Suggest | Conditional / **Moderate** | [[aga-2024-uc-pharm]] Rec 2 |
| CD — same comparison | Upadacitinib | Recommend | **Strong / Moderate-to-high** | [[aga-2025-crohns-pharm]] Rec 1 |
| ASUC after intravenous (IV) corticosteroid or infliximab failure | Tofacitinib or upadacitinib | **Insufficient data to routinely recommend** | Key concept (KC) 56, ungraded | [[acg-2025-uc]] |

**Only the CD recommendation carries a prior-exposure precondition in its own text.** ACG UC Recs 30/31 impose none — in UC the anti-TNF-first requirement is **regulatory, not the guideline's**.

## Positioning — the FDA and EMA Gates

**United States (US Food and Drug Administration [FDA] label) — a hard gate.**
- Use **after failure or intolerance of a TNF antagonist** — **tofacitinib, filgotinib and upadacitinib**, in **both** UC ([[aga-2024-uc-pharm]]) and CD ([[aga-2025-crohns-pharm]]).
- Terms as extended to upadacitinib: after inadequate response or intolerance to **one or more** TNF inhibitors, **caution with cardiovascular risk factors**, **lowest effective maintenance dose** ([[acg-2025-uc]]). ORAL Surveillance is the origin, and the tofacitinib label change was **United States only** ([[acg-2025-crohns]]).

**Europe (European Medicines Agency [EMA]) — a *caution*, not a bar.** Cautious first-line use if at risk of adverse cardiovascular outcomes, by four named criteria ([[aga-2024-uc-pharm]]):

| # | Criterion |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Age 65 years or older** |
| 2 | **Current or previous long-term smoker** |
| 3 | **History of cardiovascular disease** (e.g. heart attack, stroke) |
| 4 | **History of cancer** |

- Outside the US label, where first-line use is allowed, the American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) rates **upadacitinib HIGH-efficacy**, **tofacitinib and filgotinib MODERATE-efficacy** in UC ([[aga-2024-uc-pharm]]).
- **High major adverse cardiovascular event (MACE) risk → use cautiously** ([[aga-2024-uc-pharm]]).

**Right pick when:** concomitant **inflammatory arthritis**, not arthralgias alone (anti-TNF **or** JAK inhibitor) · **oral route preferred** · upadacitinib = most efficacious UC agent in trials. **Pick something else when:** **higher infection risk** → [[vedolizumab]] or an [[il-23-and-il-12-23-inhibitors|anti–interleukin-23 (IL-23)]] strategy ([[acg-2025-uc]] KC 46–47; full comorbidity table on [[ulcerative-colitis]]).

## Sequencing and Efficacy Tier

Full AGA efficacy-tier tables live on [[ulcerative-colitis]] ([[aga-2024-uc-pharm]] Recs 3–4) and [[crohns-disease]] ([[aga-2025-crohns-pharm]] Recs 3–4). Class points:

- **UC:** upadacitinib higher-efficacy in both positions; **tofacitinib moves up a tier after anti-TNF exposure**. Every JAK row carries the US-label restriction, so the "naïve" tier is off-label in US practice.
- **CD: the tier inverts on prior exposure** — upadacitinib **lower** efficacy when naïve, **higher** after anti-TNF.
- **First advanced therapy works best**; response falls with each failure ([[acg-2025-uc]] KC 42).

> [!pearl] Colonic CD responds, ileal CD may not
> The phase 3 upadacitinib CD trials showed "striking differences" by location — **highly effective in isolated colonic disease, no significant efficacy signal in ileal CD**; meta-analyses suggest the same split class-wide ([[aga-2025-crohns-pharm]]). Likely explains the inverted naïve tier, and is a decision input on its own.

## Acute Severe UC

*Full ASUC pathway, day-3 indices and rescue choice: [[ulcerative-colitis]]. Complication pathway: [[toxic-megacolon]].*

- **Rescue after IV corticosteroid or infliximab failure:** [[acg-2025-uc]] KC 56 finds **insufficient data to routinely recommend** a JAK inhibitor (studies uncontrolled, anti-TNF–naïve or off-label dosed); the newer [[aga-2026-inpatient-ibd]] lists it as one of three rescue options, with **protein-losing colopathy favoring a small molecule**.
- **Rescue doses** ([[aga-2026-inpatient-ibd]]; optimal dose unknown): **standard** tofacitinib 10 mg BID, upadacitinib 45 mg daily; **off-label high-intensity inpatient** tofacitinib 10 mg three times daily × 9 doses, upadacitinib 30 mg BID. Give the recombinant zoster vaccine first if possible — **do not delay treatment** for it. Role and dosing as *initial* inpatient therapy are an explicit open question.

> [!pitfall] Don't stack immunosuppression in ASUC
> Avoid **high JAK doses plus corticosteroids**, and avoid a JAK inhibitor as **rescue immediately after infliximab** — over-immunosuppression and opportunistic infection ([[acg-2025-uc]]). ASUC is itself a VTE risk state; all hospitalized IBD patients get **prophylactic anticoagulation**.

## Safety — ORAL Surveillance and the Class Warning

### The risk criteria and what ORAL Surveillance showed

Everything restricting this class comes from one trial in **RA, not IBD** — RA active despite methotrexate, **age 50 years or older**, **≥1 additional cardiovascular (CV) risk factor**, randomized to tofacitinib 5 mg BID, 10 mg BID or a TNF inhibitor ([[acg-2025-crohns]]).

**The named CV risk factors are the gating criteria** ([[aga-2024-uc-pharm]]): prior cardiovascular disease · **cigarette smoker, current or past** · hypertension · **high-density lipoprotein (HDL) cholesterol <40 mg/dL** · diabetes mellitus · family history of premature coronary heart disease · extra-articular RA · history of coronary artery disease.

**Result — noninferiority to the TNF inhibitor was not shown** for either coprimary endpoint: **MACE** (CV death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, nonfatal stroke) **3.4% vs 2.5%**, hazard ratio **1.33 (95% confidence interval 0.91–1.94)**; **cancer excluding nonmelanoma skin cancer (NMSC) 4.2% vs 2.9%**, hazard ratio **1.48 (1.04–2.09)**.

- **Independent MACE risk factors, whatever drug the patient received: current smoking, aspirin use, age over 65, male sex** ([[acg-2025-crohns]]).
- **Serious infection** significantly elevated only at **tofacitinib 10 mg BID**; cancer incidence was higher in **all** groups in patients aged 65 and older.
- **Thrombosis is dose-graded.** Even **5 mg BID vs TNF inhibitor** gave higher deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism and VTE rates; rates rise consistently at **10 mg BID** (not an approved RA dose), where adjudicated VTE and **all-cause death** exceeded TNF inhibitor ([[acg-2025-uc]]).

> [!pitfall] The signal sits in the older or ever-smoking patient
> Stratified by age and smoking, patients **younger than 65 who had never smoked** had **no** increased MACE, malignancy, myocardial infarction or death on tofacitinib vs TNF inhibitors ([[acg-2025-crohns]]). Use it for the individual risk conversation — then apply the gate anyway, because it is regulatory.

**Caveats ACG itself raises:** RA on methotrexate, over half also on corticosteroids — not IBD monotherapy; **these events have not appeared in the pivotal IBD tofacitinib trials or real-world follow-up**; **no significant MACE or VTE with upadacitinib** in the UC phase 3 program, so its risk is "less clear but mandated" ([[acg-2025-crohns]], [[acg-2025-uc]]).

### Class effects seen in the IBD trials

| Effect | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Herpes zoster** | Tofacitinib 10 mg BID **5.1% vs 0.5%** placebo (OCTAVE); upadacitinib **3 patients vs none** | [[acg-2025-uc]] |
| **Lipids** | Measurable changes on tofacitinib, but total cholesterol:HDL and low-density lipoprotein:HDL **ratios stay stable** | [[acg-2025-uc]] |
| **Infections (UC)** | Serious adverse event (AE) rates comparable to placebo; **infectious complications slightly more frequent** on tofacitinib | [[acg-2025-uc]] |
| **Upadacitinib in CD** (meta-analysis, 1,917 patients) | Serious AEs **6.0%**, no significant difference vs placebo; discontinuation for AEs **5.1%**; opportunistic infections **0.7%**; VTE **1.4%** | [[acg-2025-crohns]] |
| **Malignancy in IBD** | **No demonstrated increased risk in IBD.** 78-trial meta-analysis: no excess vs placebo or methotrexate, **but higher vs TNF inhibitors** | [[aga-2024-ibd-malignancy]], [[acg-2025-ibd-preventive-care]] |
| **Vaccine response** | Not definitively established. **Vaccinate anyway** — do not hold small molecules for it | [[acg-2025-ibd-preventive-care]] |

*A cancer on therapy does **not** change the JAK inhibitor, whatever the tumour type — drug-by-drug table on [[ibd-in-malignancy]] ([[aga-2024-ibd-malignancy]]). Vaccination and cancer-screening schedule: [[ibd-preventive-care]].*

## Pre-Treatment Screening and Monitoring

| When | What | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Before starting** | **Document lipid levels** per the label — **and repeat after starting** (inpatient: **fasting** panel before a JAK inhibitor or [[calcineurin-inhibitors\|cyclosporine]]) | [[aga-2025-crohns-pharm]], [[aga-2026-inpatient-ibd]] |
| Before starting | **Latent tuberculosis** (tuberculin skin test or interferon-γ release assay [IGRA] — **IGRA preferred if bacille Calmette-Guérin–vaccinated**) and **hepatitis B virus** testing — reactivation risk | [[aga-2025-crohns-pharm]], [[aga-2026-inpatient-ibd]] |
| Before starting | Confirm **active inflammation objectively** — C-reactive protein (CRP) or fecal calprotectin, or endoscopy / cross-sectional imaging / [[intestinal-ultrasound\|intestinal ultrasound]]; functional symptoms overlap in up to **one-third** of CD patients | [[aga-2025-crohns-pharm]] |
| Before starting | Vaccinations up to date (schedule: [[ibd-preventive-care]]) | [[aga-2025-crohns-pharm]] |
| **Herpes zoster vaccine** | **2-dose inactivated recombinant** — all adults with IBD **aged 50+** (Rec 4), **and all adults 19+ on or starting immune-modifying therapy** (Rec 5); both Conditional | [[acg-2025-ibd-preventive-care]] |
| **Annually, on therapy** | **NMSC screening** — **particularly over age 50** (Rec 10, Conditional). Monitor for **shingles and VTE**, especially on the higher maintenance dose | [[acg-2025-ibd-preventive-care]], [[aga-2024-uc-pharm]] |
| Response assessment | Assess at a **defined time point (6–12 wk)**, then ongoing clinical response + CRP or fecal calprotectin with periodic structural reassessment | [[acg-2025-uc]], [[aga-2025-crohns-pharm]] |

- **No drug-level or antidrug-antibody testing** — small molecules are not immunogenic; [[therapeutic-drug-monitoring-ibd|therapeutic drug monitoring]] is an [[anti-tnf-agents|anti-TNF]] tool.
- Complete blood count and liver tests are attached to **immunomodulators**, lipids to **JAK inhibitors** — don't transfer one to the other. No lipid threshold, statin rule or repeat interval is given, only "document before, repeat after."

## Cautions and Situations to Avoid

- **Pregnancy / childbearing plans.** Data sparse; **animal data suggest adverse pregnancy effects at doses far above clinical doses** → **generally avoid in women contemplating pregnancy in the near future** ([[aga-2024-uc-pharm]], [[aga-2025-crohns-pharm]]).
- **High MACE risk** — use cautiously; apply the four EMA criteria and the CV risk-factor list above.
- **High doses with corticosteroids, or immediately after infliximab, in ASUC** ([[acg-2025-uc]]).
- **Ileal-predominant CD** — no significant upadacitinib efficacy signal vs placebo ([[aga-2025-crohns-pharm]]).
- **Higher infection risk** — prefer [[vedolizumab]] or an [[il-23-and-il-12-23-inhibitors|IL-23]] strategy.
- No **absolute contraindication** is stated, no **washout interval** when switching to or from a JAK inhibitor, and no data on **combining** a small molecule with a biologic ([[aga-2024-ibd-malignancy]]). The class is not addressed in [[pouchitis]], postoperative CD prophylaxis or paediatric IBD.

## Guideline Differences and Contradictions

- **ACG's UC recommendation vs the FDA label.** [[acg-2025-uc]] Recs 30/31 impose **no prior-anti-TNF precondition** while the same guideline records the label restriction; resolved as "use clinical judgment" — regulatory, not evidentiary. No such gap in CD.
- **ACG vs AGA on upadacitinib in the naïve CD patient.** [[acg-2025-crohns]] grades it Strong / Moderate *in anti-TNF–experienced patients only*; [[aga-2025-crohns-pharm]] ranks it **lower** efficacy when naïve. Compatible, since ACG's recommendation is scoped — the naïve ranking is AGA's alone, driven by the ileal-vs-colonic split.
- **ORAL Surveillance entry criteria described two ways.** [[acg-2025-crohns]]: **age ≥50 plus ≥1 additional CV risk factor**; [[acg-2025-uc]]: patients "who had pre-existing cardiovascular disease." The CD version matches the risk-factor list in [[aga-2024-uc-pharm]] and is used here.
- **ASUC: ACG says "insufficient data," AGA lists a JAK inhibitor as one of three rescue options.** AGA calls role and dosing unresolved, so the practical position is unchanged: **not routine, off-label if used.**
