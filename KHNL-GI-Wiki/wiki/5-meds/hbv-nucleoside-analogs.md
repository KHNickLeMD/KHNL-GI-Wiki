## The Class

- Two drug classes are approved for [[chronic-hepatitis-b|chronic hepatitis B (CHB)]]: pegylated interferon (peg-IFN) and **nucleos(t)ide analogues (NAs)**. ([[aasld-idsa-2025-chb-treatment]])
- **Three preferred NAs:** entecavir (ETV), tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF), tenofovir alafenamide (TAF).
  - All three give high efficacy for hepatitis B virus (HBV) DNA suppression, **low rates of antiviral resistance**, are well tolerated, and are broadly applicable — including decompensated [[cirrhosis]] and immunocompromised persons.
- **Peg-IFN is rarely used** — limited applicability, safety and tolerability compared with NA therapy. Functional cure is infrequent with all current therapies, though higher with peg-IFN than with NAs.
- **ETV and TDF are generic**; cost/availability is an explicit selection factor and a factor in shared decision-making about starting therapy.
- NAs suppress replication but **do not eradicate** HBV — therapy is long-term and in most cases lifelong (see [[#Stopping Therapy]]).

---

## Choosing an Agent

Selection among the three preferred NAs is driven by: availability/cost · age · renal or bone disease · pregnancy · human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) co-infection · prior treatment history. ([[aasld-idsa-2025-chb-treatment]])

### Preferred Agents — Avoid Rules

| Agent | Avoid / do not use when | Prefer when |
|---|---|---|
| **Entecavir (ETV)** | Pregnancy (insufficient safety data) · prior **lamivudine** exposure (resistance risk) · HIV co-infection **unless** HIV RNA is suppressed on an antiretroviral regimen | Renal or bone disease makes TDF unattractive and TAF is unavailable or contraindicated; generic cost |
| **Tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF)** | Renal or bone disease | Pregnancy — **most extensive safety record** of the three; generic cost; prior lamivudine exposure |
| **Tenofovir alafenamide (TAF)** | Creatinine clearance (CrCl) **<15 mL/min and not yet on dialysis** | Renal or bone disease (used **instead of** TDF); pregnancy (co-recommended with TDF); prior lamivudine exposure |

- The lamivudine rule is about **cross-resistance** — prior lamivudine exposure disqualifies ETV, not the tenofovirs.
- In the **immune-tolerant phase**, resistance rates remain low with ETV/TDF/TAF but **may be increased** by the very high replication levels; HBV DNA may never become undetectable even on long-term therapy. In a 4-year study, TDF alone achieved HBV DNA <69 IU/mL in **55%** vs **76%** for TDF plus emtricitabine (odds ratio 2.60, 95% confidence interval 1.21–5.56), with **no** improvement in hepatitis B e antigen (HBeAg) or hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) loss.

### Renal Function and Dialysis

- **All three agents require consideration of renal function and/or dialysis.**
- **ETV and TDF need dose adjustment** for renal dysfunction or dialysis.
- For a person **on TDF with declining renal function, changing the NA is preferred** to dose-adjusting TDF.
- **TAF is not recommended at CrCl <15 mL/min when not yet on dialysis.**

### Non-Preferred and Legacy Agents

| Agent | Status |
|---|---|
| **Lamivudine**, **telbivudine** | Non-preferred — higher resistance; in pregnancy TDF/TAF are preferred over both ([[aasld-idsa-2025-chb-treatment]]). Telbivudine remained an American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2016 third-trimester option ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]]) |
| **Adefovir** | Non-preferred; switch to ETV, TDF or TAF if virologic response is suboptimal |
| **Lamivudine, adefovir after [[liver-transplantation\|liver transplantation]]** | **No longer first-line** for post-transplant prophylaxis ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-graft-complications]] Rec 58) |

## Dosing and Administration

- **Administration:** tenofovir (TDF, TAF) is taken **with food**; ETV is taken **on an empty stomach**. Confirming that the drug is being taken correctly is the first step in any suboptimal virologic response. ([[aasld-idsa-2025-chb-treatment]])
- **The ingested guidelines do not print adult milligram doses or renal dose-adjustment cut-points for entecavir, TDF or TAF** — they state only that ETV and TDF require adjustment for renal dysfunction or dialysis. The only NA dose any ingested source gives is **telbivudine 600 mg daily**, from the pregnancy trial ACG 2016 cites (gestational weeks 20–32, HBeAg-positive, HBV DNA >7 log₁₀ copies/mL). ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]])
- Pill burden and long-term adherence are explicit considerations: therapy is anticipated to last **many years**, and a switch is generally preferred over add-on therapy because of lower pill burden.

## Treatment Targets

- **Clinical goals:** prevent progression to cirrhosis, [[hepatocellular-carcinoma|hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)]] and liver-related death. These endpoints take years to decades, so surrogate endpoints are used. ([[aasld-idsa-2025-chb-treatment]])

| Endpoint type | Target |
|---|---|
| Virologic | HBV DNA undetectable by a polymerase chain reaction (PCR)-based assay |
| Serologic | HBeAg loss and seroconversion; HBsAg loss and seroconversion |
| Biochemical | Serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT) normal |
| Histologic | Necroinflammation improved by ≥2 points with no worsening of fibrosis |

- **Functional cure (HBsAg loss) is the desired endpoint** — associated with better clinical outcomes and long-term durability. Definitions of functional and partial cure: [[chronic-hepatitis-b]].
- Other goals: reduced vertical and horizontal transmission, better patient-reported outcomes and quality of life, less stigma.
- **When the indication is preventing onward transmission**, the target is HBV DNA **below the lower limit of quantification**. For healthcare workers performing Society for Healthcare Epidemiology of America (SHEA) category III exposure-prone procedures, SHEA guidance is to maintain **HBV DNA <1000 IU/mL**; thresholds differ between advisory bodies, and the American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD)/Infectious Diseases Society of America (IDSA) guideline endorses no single number.

## Suboptimal Virologic Response — Switch and Add-On

*Response patterns — plateau, virologic breakthrough, slow decline — are defined on [[chronic-hepatitis-b]].*

**Initial steps in every case** ([[aasld-idsa-2025-chb-treatment]]):

- Evaluate and reinforce **adherence**, including correct administration (food vs empty stomach, above).
- **More frequent HBV DNA testing** to establish the pattern.
- **Consider resistance testing**, particularly if criteria for virologic breakthrough are met. Resistance testing may not be possible at **HBV DNA <1000 IU/mL**, and **no current resistance test for tenofovir is available**.

**Antiviral options if response is suboptimal:**

| Current drug | Switch to | Add on |
|---|---|---|
| Entecavir | TDF or TAF | TDF or TAF |
| TDF | TAF or ETV | ETV |
| TAF | ETV | ETV |
| Lamivudine or telbivudine | TDF or TAF | — |
| Adefovir | ETV, TDF or TAF | — |

- **Switching is preferred to adding on** in most cases (lower pill burden).
- **Slow decline alone** → continue monitoring, no change in therapy.
- **After any change of antiviral:** HBV DNA **every 3 months until undetectable, then every 6 months**.

## Where These Drugs Are Used

| Setting | Agent-level rule |
|---|---|
| CHB by phase (immune-active, immune-tolerant, indeterminate, inactive) | Phase definitions, ALT/HBV DNA thresholds and the treat-vs-monitor decision: [[chronic-hepatitis-b]] |
| **Compensated cirrhosis**, HBsAg-positive, any HBV DNA level | Treat with ETV, TDF or TAF **until HBsAg loss** |
| **Decompensated cirrhosis** (variceal hemorrhage, [[ascites]], [[hepatic-encephalopathy\|hepatic encephalopathy]], hepatorenal syndrome) | Treat with ETV, TDF or TAF **indefinitely**; monitor for **lactic acidosis** (rare, but likely more common with NA therapy when decompensated); refer for [[liver-transplantation\|transplant]] consideration |
| **[[acute-liver-failure\|Acute liver failure]] from HBV reactivation** | Start **entecavir or tenofovir** immediately (ACG 2023 — strong recommendation) ([[acg-2023-alf]]) |
| **Prophylaxis before immunosuppression** | Use an NA with a **high barrier to resistance**; entecavir or tenofovir if HBsAg-positive ([[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]]). Risk tiers, prophylaxis-vs-monitoring and duration: [[chronic-hepatitis-b]] ([[aga-2025-hbv-reactivation]]) |
| **Liver transplantation** | Entecavir, tenofovir disoproxil or TAF, started **before transplant** in candidates with CHB and continued **indefinitely**; also indefinite prophylaxis for an HBsAg-negative recipient of an anti-hepatitis B core-positive graft. Hepatitis B immune globulin (HBIG) rules: [[liver-transplantation]] |
| **[[hepatitis-d\|Hepatitis D virus (HDV)]] co-infection** | NAs control HBV only — **no activity against HDV**; the HBV DNA threshold for adding an NA is on [[hepatitis-d]] |
| **Prevention of horizontal transmission** in viremic persons who do not meet disease-specific treatment indications | Shared decision-making; see [[#Treatment Targets]] for the suppression target and [[chronic-hepatitis-b]] for the high-risk scenarios |

## Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

*Thresholds and timing of antiviral prophylaxis for mother-to-child transmission are on [[chronic-hepatitis-b]]; broader obstetric context on [[liver-disease-in-pregnancy]].*

- **TDF or TAF** — the agents to use. **TDF has the more extensive safety record in pregnancy**; TAF is co-recommended, with growing supporting data. ([[aasld-idsa-2025-chb-treatment]], [[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]])
- **Already on TDF or TAF at conception → continue.** **On ETV or another antiviral → switch to TDF or TAF**; safety data are insufficient to recommend ETV during pregnancy.
- **TDF/TAF are preferred over lamivudine and telbivudine** — lower risk of antiviral resistance and established long-term safety data in infants of HBV- and/or HIV-infected mothers.
- **Duration when the sole indication is preventing transmission:** may be stopped **at delivery**; the American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2024 update allows continuation until childbirth or **up to 12 weeks postpartum**. ALT flare risk is the same whether TDF is stopped at delivery or at postpartum weeks 4–12.
- **Breastfeeding is safe on TDF or TAF.** Nucleos(t)ides are excreted in breast milk at low levels; ACG 2016 advises avoiding breastfeeding with cracked or bleeding nipples. ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]])

## Stopping Therapy

- **Default: do not withdraw NA therapy until HBsAg loss** (AASLD/IDSA 2025 Rec 5 — conditional, very low certainty). The full stop criteria, post-stop monitoring schedule and restart triggers live on [[chronic-hepatitis-b]].
- **Exception — prophylaxis indications are finite:** therapy given solely to prevent mother-to-child transmission may stop at delivery (above); therapy given solely to prevent horizontal transmission may be revisited once transmission risk is no longer high (e.g., a healthcare worker no longer performing category III procedures, or susceptible contacts now immunized or harm reduction in place).
- **Counsel every patient started on an NA about the risks of cessation**, including severe liver flares, before starting.

---

## Adverse Effects and Safety

- Adverse events with NA therapy are **overall low**, which is part of why long-term treatment is acceptable. ([[aasld-idsa-2025-chb-treatment]])
- **TDF** — renal and bone toxicity concerns drive the avoid rule in renal or bone disease, and a change of NA (rather than dose reduction) when renal function declines on TDF.
- **TAF** — not used at CrCl <15 mL/min when not yet on dialysis.
- **Decompensated cirrhosis** — monitor for **lactic acidosis**, a rare adverse effect that is likely more common with NA therapy in decompensation.
- **Stopping therapy** is itself the major safety event: ALT flares, hepatic decompensation and death have been reported after NA withdrawal.
- AASLD/IDSA 2025 does not quantify the degree of renal dysfunction that triggers dose adjustment, nor the renal and bone monitoring intervals on TDF.

## Monitoring on Therapy

| Situation | Interval |
|---|---|
| After a change of antiviral for suboptimal response | HBV DNA every **3 months until undetectable**, then every **6 months** |
| Suspected plateau or breakthrough | More frequent HBV DNA testing to define the pattern; resistance testing if breakthrough |
| On treatment, compensated or decompensated cirrhosis | HCC surveillance every 6 months — criteria on [[hcc-surveillance]] |
| After stopping therapy (any indication) | Schedules are on [[chronic-hepatitis-b]] |

AASLD/IDSA 2025 is a focused update: it gives the intervals above and for untreated persons, but no routine on-treatment laboratory interval for the stable, suppressed patient.
