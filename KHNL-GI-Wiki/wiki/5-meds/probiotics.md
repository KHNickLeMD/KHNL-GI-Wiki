Live microorganisms that, when administered in adequate amounts, confer a health benefit on the host (Food and Agriculture Organization [FAO]/World Health Organization [WHO] definition). Sold as supplements rather than drugs in the US and Europe, so the industry is largely unregulated and product viability and manufacturing detail are usually unavailable ([[aga-2020-probiotics]]).

> [!pearl] A probiotic recommendation is a strain recommendation
> Effects are **strain- and dose-specific** — different strains and combinations have widely different biologic activity, so a result from one formulation does not transfer to "probiotics" as a class, and pooling across heterogeneous strains and populations can mislead ([[aga-2020-probiotics]]). Match the named strain or combination, and its dose, to the indication studied.

## AGA 2020 Recommendations by Indication

The American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2020 guideline ([[aga-2020-probiotics]]) graded probiotics across eight indications using Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE). **No recommendation in it is Strong** — every actionable one is Conditional, and four indications got no recommendation at all. Prebiotics are outside its scope. Recommendation numbers below are the guideline's own (Table 3).

| # | Indication (population) | Strain / combination | AGA 2020 stance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | [[clostridioides-difficile\|*Clostridioides difficile*]] (*C difficile*) infection — **prevention**, adults and children on antibiotic treatment | *S boulardii*; **or** *L acidophilus* CL1285 + *L casei* LBC80R; **or** *L acidophilus* + *L delbrueckii* subsp *bulgaricus* + *B bifidum*; **or** those three + *S salivarius* subsp *thermophilus* | **Suggest**, over no or other probiotics — conditional, low quality. **Reversed by AGA 2026 — see below** |
| 5 | [[pouchitis\|Pouchitis]], adults and children | 8-strain: *L paracasei* subsp *paracasei*, *L plantarum*, *L acidophilus*, *L delbrueckii* subsp *bulgaricus*, *B longum* subsp *longum*, *B breve*, *B longum* subsp *infantis*, *S salivarius* subsp *thermophilus* | **Suggest**, over no or other probiotics — conditional, very low quality |
| 8 | Necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) **prevention**, preterm (<37 weeks gestational age), low-birth-weight infants | A *Lactobacillus* spp + *Bifidobacterium* spp combination — *L rhamnosus* ATCC 53103 + *B longum* subsp *infantis*; or *L casei* + *B breve*; or *L rhamnosus* + *L acidophilus* + *L casei* + *B longum* subsp *infantis* + *B bifidum* + *B longum* subsp *longum*; or *L acidophilus* + *B longum* subsp *infantis*; or *L acidophilus* + *B bifidum*; or *L rhamnosus* ATCC 53103 + *B longum* Reuter ATCC BAA-999; or *L acidophilus* + *B bifidum* + *B animalis* subsp *lactis* + *B longum* subsp *longum* — **or** *B animalis* subsp *lactis* (including DSM 15954); **or** *L reuteri* (DSM 17938 or ATCC 55730); **or** *L rhamnosus* (ATCC 53103, ATC A07FA or LCR 35) | **Suggest**, over no and other probiotics — conditional, moderate/high quality |
| 7 | [[acute-diarrhea\|Acute infectious gastroenteritis]], children | — (no strain named; applies to probiotics generally) | **Suggest against** — conditional, moderate quality |
| 1 | *C difficile* infection — **treatment** | — | **No recommendation** (knowledge gap): use only in the context of a clinical trial |
| 3 | [[crohns-disease\|Crohn's disease]], adults and children | — | **No recommendation** (knowledge gap): use only in the context of a clinical trial |
| 4 | [[ulcerative-colitis\|Ulcerative colitis]], adults and children | — | **No recommendation** (knowledge gap): use only in the context of a clinical trial |
| 6 | [[irritable-bowel-syndrome\|Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)]], symptomatic children and adults | — | **No recommendation** (knowledge gap): use only in the context of a clinical trial |

### Qualifiers the Guideline Attaches

- ***C difficile* infection (CDI) prevention, Rec 2.** Benefit is concentrated in patients at **high baseline risk (>15%)** of CDI, with no significant benefit at low baseline risk. Patients who place a high value on potential harms (particularly those with severe illness), or on avoiding the cost, and a low value on a small risk of *C difficile* (particularly outpatients), would reasonably select no probiotics.
- **Pouchitis, Rec 5.** Patients for whom the feasibility and cost of the 8-strain combination are problematic would reasonably select no probiotics.
- **Pediatric gastroenteritis, Rec 7.** Framed to the United States and Canada: trials from other regions were judged not generalizable to the North American population (host genetics, diet, sanitation, endemic enteropathogens).
  - Conflicts with earlier European Society for Paediatric Gastroenterology, Hepatology and Nutrition (ESPGHAN) and Infectious Diseases Society of America (IDSA) positions, which predate the two negative North American trials. The IDSA position, stated exactly: probiotics **may be offered** to reduce symptom severity and duration in **immunocompetent adults and children** with infectious or antimicrobial-associated diarrhea *(weak recommendation, moderate-quality evidence)* — Rec 49, [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]; it names no strain, dose or duration.
  - **AGA 2020 governs this page** — newer within the same tier, and its recommendation runs the other way in children. The two are not reconcilable by population: IDSA's weak "may be offered" covers the same immunocompetent children AGA 2020 suggests against.

## C difficile — Probiotics Not Advised (AGA 2026)

- **[[aga-2026-cdiff-adults|AGA 2026 Clinical Practice Update (CPU) on CDI in adults]], Best Practice Advice (BPA) 12:** probiotics are **not advised to prevent an initial or recurrent *C difficile* infection** (expert review, ungraded). This reverses the AGA 2020 conditional recommendation FOR named formulations in antibiotic-exposed patients — the 2020 strain list is the prior position, not current practice.
- **[[aga-2026-cdiff-ibd|AGA 2026 CPU on CDI in IBD]], BPA 11:** do not advise probiotics for primary or secondary CDI prevention in [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]]. Data in IBD are limited and contradictory; **bacteremia and fungemia** have been documented, especially in immunocompromised or severely ill patients; and the commonly used *Lactobacillus* and *Bifidobacterium* strains are **susceptible to vancomycin**, which undercuts the rationale for giving them during CDI treatment.
- **[[acg-2021-cdiff|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2021]]** recommends against probiotics for primary prevention (conditional, moderate evidence) and for secondary prevention (strong, very low evidence).
- **What AGA 2026 advises instead (BPA 12):** a healthy, varied diet with fruits and vegetables, rich in **both soluble and insoluble** fiber — a low-fiber diet after antibiotics prolongs susceptibility to CDI. Avoid ultra-processed foods; do not prescribe bland or clear-liquid diets during CDI. For established recurrent CDI the effective intervention is [[fmt|fecal microbiota–based therapy]], not probiotics.

## SIBO — Probiotics Not Recommended (ACG 2020)

Not covered by the AGA guideline; per [[acg-2020-sibo]] (key concepts, expert consensus — not GRADE-rated):

- **No consistent data support recommending specific probiotics** in the treatment of [[small-intestinal-bacterial-overgrowth|small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO)]]; there is likewise no basis for fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT).
- Meta-analysis: probiotics appeared to reduce hydrogen production (odds ratio [OR] 1.61, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.19–2.17), but the trials were mostly small and of poor quality.
- **Probiotics may themselves cause SIBO and D-lactic acidosis**, producing gas and [[abdominal-bloating-and-distention|bloating]]; in a controlled study, withdrawal of the probiotic plus a course of antibiotics resolved symptoms.

## Evidence Behind Each Indication

*Overall certainty of evidence (CoE) per question, from the companion [[aga-2020-probiotics-technical-review|technical review]]. These are **evidence ratings, not recommendations** — in most of the eight contexts the rating reflects **untested**, not **disproven**.*

| Indication | Overall CoE |
|---|---|
| NEC / sepsis / all-cause mortality in preterm, low-birth-weight newborns | **Moderate/High** |
| Acute infectious gastroenteritis in children (evidence of **no** benefit in North America) | **Moderate** |
| CDI treatment · *C difficile*–associated diarrhea (CDAD) prevention · IBS | **Low** |
| Pouchitis prevention / maintenance | **Very Low** |
| Crohn's disease · ulcerative colitis induction or maintenance | Knowledge gap |

> [!pitfall] "No recommendation" usually means untested, not disproven
> Across the 8 questions there was mostly "either insufficient evidence… or a significant knowledge gap." For IBS, 55 trials in 5301 patients tested **44 different strains or combinations**, so most rest on a **single trial** — and registered protocols with no publication raise publication-bias concern. The exception is pediatric acute gastroenteritis, where two large North American trials were genuinely **negative** ([[aga-2020-probiotics-technical-review]]).

- **Pouchitis — small base, and the product may not be obtainable.** The whole 8-strain evidence base is **193 subjects**. Maintenance of chronic pouchitis remission at 9–12 mo: relative risk (RR) 20.24 (95% CI 4.28–95.81), Low CoE. Prevention of a first acute episode: RR 1.29 (95% CI 1.03–1.61), Very Low CoE, one of the two trials open-label. **Commercial availability has been in question** (ongoing litigation between manufacturer and patent holder), and **no other formulation has been tested as rigorously** — so substituting another 8-strain product is not evidence-based. Benefit in pouches created for [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis|familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP)]] or other intestinal disorders, and in children, is unknown.
- **NEC — the effect sizes belong to specific strains.** Reduced **all-cause mortality** on moderate/high-quality evidence only with *Lactobacillus* spp + *Bifidobacterium* spp combinations, including the 4-strain *L acidophilus* + *B bifidum* + *B animalis* subsp *lactis* + *B longum* subsp *longum*: OR 0.56 (95% CI 0.39–0.80). Severe NEC (Bell stage ≥II): *B animalis* subsp *lactis* OR 0.31, *L reuteri* OR 0.55, *L rhamnosus* OR 0.44. Combinations with the **largest** point estimates sit on low/very low evidence — a research priority, not a basis for practice.

## Dose and Duration

> **[[aga-2020-probiotics]] names strains only — no colony-forming units (CFU), no product quantity, no treatment duration, for any of its eight indications.** The panel wrote that it "was not able to assess the viability of each formulation reported in the studies, as this information was not routinely available," and names "the lack of product manufacturing details" as a barrier to comparison. The recommendations therefore identify *which organisms*, not how much or for how long — a prescriber has to take the dose from the specific product's studied regimen.

The companion technical review does not fill the gap. It states CFU doses only for a handful of individual trials, and only for the two indications where probiotics are **not** recommended:

| Indication (not recommended) | Strain / combination | Dose |
|---|---|---|
| CDI treatment | *S boulardii* | 1 g = **3 × 10¹⁰ CFU/d** |
| CDI treatment | 4-strain *L acidophilus* ATCC 700396 + *L paracasei* subsp *paracasei* ATCC 335 + *B animalis* subsp *lactis* SD5220 + SD5219 | **1.70 × 10¹⁰ CFU/d** |
| CDI treatment | *L plantarum* 299v | **5 × 10¹⁰ CFU/d** |
| Pediatric acute gastroenteritis | *L rhamnosus* ATCC 53103 | **1 × 10¹⁰ CFU twice daily × 5 d** |
| Pediatric acute gastroenteritis | *L rhamnosus* R0011 + *L helveticus* R0052 (95:5) | total **4 × 10⁹ CFU twice daily × 5 d** |

- **For pouchitis (the 8-strain combination) and for NEC prevention — the two indications recommended *for* — no CFU dose, frequency or duration is given anywhere in either document.** The technical review names trials "that define optimal doses, timing, and frequency of administration" as a future-research need, and lists dosing and duration differences among its own limitations. A dose for these indications has to come from the individual trial reports.

## Safety and Scope Limits

- **Harms:** sepsis, especially in immunocompromised or severely ill patients — the mechanism of concern is **translocation of ingested microbes from intestine into bloodstream**. Product **contamination** is a separate and real risk: a fungal contaminant caused fatal gastrointestinal mucormycosis in a preterm infant ([[aga-2020-probiotics-technical-review]]).
- **The trial evidence excludes the patients most often asked about.** Trials conducted exclusively in people with major comorbidities — cancer, HIV/AIDS, cystic fibrosis, spinal cord injury, organ failure, transplantation — were excluded from the technical review, so none of these ratings transfer to immunocompromised patients.
