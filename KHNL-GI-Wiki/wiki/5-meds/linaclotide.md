## Class

Guanylate cyclase-C (GC-C) agonist; intestinal secretagogue — ↑cyclic GMP → luminal Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ secretion → ↑intestinal fluid and accelerated GI transit.

## Indications & Dosing

| Indication | Dose | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| [[chronic-idiopathic-constipation\|CIC]], after OTC agents fail | **72 µg or 145 µg PO daily** (both FDA-approved for CIC) | Strong rec, moderate certainty ([[aga-acg-2023-constipation]]) |
| [[irritable-bowel-syndrome\|IBS]]-C | **290 µg PO daily** | Strong/High ([[acg-2020-ibs]]); **Strong / high certainty** ([[aga-2022-ibs-c]] Rec 3) |

- **Linaclotide is the only agent AGA recommends *strongly* for IBS-C.** Of the 9 recommendations in [[aga-2022-ibs-c]], one is strong (linaclotide) and eight are conditional — [[plecanatide]], [[tenapanor]], [[lubiprostone]] and [[tegaserod]] are all conditional alternatives. The two societies agree here, unlike on [[lubiprostone]].
- The **290 µg** dose is the IBS-C approval; CIC and IBS-C overlap and are often indistinguishable in practice. CIC trials studied 145 µg and 290 µg daily; 72 µg has been studied less.
- **Administration:** take **without food, ≥30 minutes before the first meal of the day**.
- **Duration:** trials ran **12 weeks**; the drug label sets no limit. Can be used as a replacement for, or an adjunct to, OTC agents.
- Useful when bloating, abdominal discomfort, and pain coexist — linaclotide improves these abdominal symptoms in IBS-C trials.

### Refractory Constipation

- **Linaclotide and [[prucalopride]] are the two agents with demonstrated efficacy in chronic constipation unresponsive to OTC laxatives** — [[plecanatide]] has shown efficacy only in *"severe"* constipation ([[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]]).
- Caveat the source raises about all three: these trials **lacked rigorous evaluation of prior OTC therapy or colonic transit**.
- **BPA 7** — patients with refractory constipation should trial **all** standard OTC and FDA-approved constipation therapies, **as standalone therapy or in combination**, where accessible; off-label agents (BPA 8) come only after that. Full escalation ladder on [[chronic-idiopathic-constipation]].

## Adverse Effects

- **Diarrhea** — most common treatment-emergent AE: ~**20%** of linaclotide-treated patients vs 3% placebo in IBS-C trials; **severe in 2%**, and **5% withdrew** because of it ([[acg-2020-ibs]]).
- In CIC, diarrhea leading to discontinuation is ~3× more likely than placebo (RR 3.35, 95% CI 2.09–5.36; 83 more per 1,000), though in one study only **4.7%** discontinued for diarrhea. Over the first year, the commonest reasons for stopping were **loss of efficacy and insurance/refill barriers**, not adverse events.
- **Contraindicated** in known or suspected **mechanical GI obstruction**.
- No clear outcome differences in patients >65 y in trials (sample too small for formal analysis).

> **Gap — pediatric contraindication not in the ingested corpus.** Neither [[acg-2020-ibs]] nor [[aga-acg-2023-constipation]] addresses use in children (the CIC guideline explicitly does not apply to pediatric populations). A previously stated contraindication in children <6 y was unsourced and has been removed; FDA labeling would be needed to state it.

## Place in Therapy

- **CIC:** stepwise after OTC (fiber, PEG) failure; one of the secretagogue options alongside [[plecanatide]] and [[lubiprostone]].
- **IBS-C:** the only strongly recommended prescription agent in [[aga-2022-ibs-c]]; [[plecanatide]] is the conditional GC-C alternative.
