Potassium-competitive acid blockers (P-CABs) are a drug **class** of gastric acid suppressants that reversibly block the H⁺/K⁺-ATPase (proton pump) at its **potassium-binding site**. Agents: revaprazan, **[[vonoprazan]]** (AGA refers to a single P-CAB currently available in the United States [US]), tegoprazan, fexuprazan, linaprazan, zastaprazan, keverprazan ([[aga-2024-pcab]] Table 1). Agent-specific detail: [[vonoprazan]].

## Mechanism vs PPIs

| Property | P-CAB | [[proton-pump-inhibitors\|Proton pump inhibitor (PPI)]] |
|---|---|---|
| Binding | Ionic, reversible; blocks K⁺ access to the potassium-binding site | Covalent, irreversible, to cysteines on **active** pumps |
| Prodrug | No — no conversion to an active form → more rapid onset | Yes (converted in acidic environment) |
| Acid stability | Acid-stable | Acid-labile (enteric coated) |
| Half-life | **6–9 h** | **1–2 h** |
| Days to maximal acid suppression | **1** | **3–5** |
| Meal timing | Independent of mealtimes | **30–60 min before meals** |
| CYP2C19 | Not metabolized by CYP2C19 → less genetic variability | Metabolism of some PPIs affected by CYP2C19 polymorphisms |

- Comparison from [[aga-2024-pcab]] Table 1 and text.
- Acid inhibition **as potent or more potent than PPIs**, varying by agent and dose; some trial doses (zastaprazan 10 mg, fexuprazan 40 mg, tegoprazan 50–100 mg, vonoprazan 20 mg) may only match standard PPI doses ([[aga-2024-pcab]]).

## When to Use — AGA 2024 Best Practice Advice

The American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2024 Best Practice Advice (BPA) statements are ungraded and **apply only where P-CABs are an available option** ([[aga-2024-pcab]]).

- **BPA 1:** based on nonclinical factors (cost, greater obstacles to obtaining the drug, fewer long-term safety data), clinicians should generally **not use P-CABs as initial therapy where clinical superiority has not been shown**.
- **BPA 2:** at current US costs, even modest superiority over **double-dose** PPIs may not make P-CABs cost-effective first-line (PPIs are cheaper, sold over the counter, and less often need prior authorization).

### GERD

| Scenario | Guidance | BPA |
|---|---|---|
| Uninvestigated heartburn / [[gerd\|nonerosive reflux disease (NERD)]] | Generally **not first-line**; **may use** in selected patients with documented acid-related reflux who fail **twice-daily PPIs** | 3 |
| On-demand therapy for heartburn (prior responders to antisecretory therapy) | Insufficient evidence for first-line use; rapid onset raises the possibility of utility | 4 |
| Milder erosive esophagitis (EE), [[reflux-testing\|Los Angeles (LA) grade]] **A/B** | Generally **not first-line**; **may use** in selected patients with documented acid-related reflux who fail twice-daily PPIs | 5 |
| Severe EE, **LA grade C/D** | **May use** for healing and maintenance of healing; with markedly higher US cost and no randomized comparison vs double-dose PPIs, unclear that benefit over standard-dose PPIs justifies routine first-line use | 6 |

> [!pitfall] PPI failure needs confirmed gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)
> AGA 2024 considers P-CABs in PPI treatment failures (e.g. refractory esophagitis) when confirmatory GERD evidence is present: **LA grade B or greater EE**, **biopsy-proven [[barretts-esophagus|Barrett's esophagus]]**, **peptic [[esophageal-stricture|stricture]]**, or **[[ambulatory-reflux-monitoring|ambulatory reflux monitoring]] with distal esophageal acid exposure time (AET) >6%** ([[aga-2024-pcab]]).

- **LA A/B:** healing similar to PPIs (US/Europe trial, 8 wk: vonoprazan 20 mg 94% vs lansoprazole 30 mg 91%).
- **LA C/D** — benefit shown only against lansoprazole (30 mg healing, 15 mg maintenance); PPIs vary in potency ([[aga-2024-pcab]] text, Table 2):

| LA C/D outcome | Trial | Vonoprazan | Lansoprazole |
|---|---|---|---|
| Healing, 2 wk | US/Europe | 20 mg: **70%** | 30 mg: **53%** |
| Healing, 8 wk | US/Europe | 20 mg: **92%** | 30 mg: **72%** |
| Maintenance of healing, 24 wk | US/Europe | 10–20 mg: **75–77%** | 15 mg: **62%** |
| Recurrence, 24 wk | Japan | 10–20 mg: **5–13%** | 15 mg: **39%** |

- PPIs used as initial therapy that **fail to maintain healing** → a P-CAB should be employed ([[aga-2024-pcab]]).

### Helicobacter pylori

- **BPA 7 — clinicians should use P-CABs in place of PPIs in eradication regimens for most patients** with [[helicobacter-pylori-infection|H. pylori]] infection ([[aga-2024-pcab]]).
  - First-line eradication: systematic review of 7 Asian randomized controlled trials (RCTs) — vonoprazan **92%** vs PPI **80%**.
  - US/Europe trial (1,046 treatment-naive adults, 14 days): vonoprazan triple **81%** and dual **77%** vs lansoprazole triple **69%**; **clarithromycin-resistant** strains **66–70% vs 32%**. All arms <90%.
  - 14 days generally advised; short course reduces the cost/safety concerns.
- **American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2024** ([[acg-2024-hp-treatment]]) — all regimens 14 days; its only *strong* first-line recommendation is optimized [[bismuth-quadruple-therapy|bismuth quadruple therapy (BQT)]] (Recommendation [Rec] 1):

| ACG 2024 | Population | Statement | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rec 3 | Treatment-naive | Dual therapy with a P-CAB + amoxicillin suggested first-line (vonoprazan 20 mg twice daily + amoxicillin 1,000 mg three times daily, Table 5) | Conditional; moderate quality evidence |
| Rec 4 | Treatment-naive, clarithromycin susceptibility unknown | P-CAB–clarithromycin triple suggested **over** PPI–clarithromycin triple | Conditional; moderate quality evidence |
| Rec 11 | Treatment-experienced | Insufficient North American evidence to recommend high-dose PPI or P-CAB dual therapy | No recommendation; evidence gap |
| Table 6 | Treatment-experienced | Vonoprazan triple pack not evaluated as salvage; not with prior macrolide exposure or without demonstrated clarithromycin-sensitive strain | No recommendation (evidence gap) |
| Key concept 5 | Treatment-experienced, confirmed clarithromycin-sensitive | PPI- or P-CAB–clarithromycin triple suggested | Key concept (ungraded) |

- ACG Key concept 3: avoid clarithromycin-containing regimens without demonstrated macrolide susceptibility.
- **US societies differ in emphasis:** AGA BPA 7 favors P-CABs over PPIs in most regimens; ACG grades the vonoprazan regimens conditional and prefers optimized BQT.

### Peptic Ulcer Disease

| Scenario | Guidance | BPA |
|---|---|---|
| Treatment or prophylaxis of [[peptic-ulcer-disease\|peptic ulcer disease (PUD)]] | Generally **not first-line** | 8 |
| [[upper-gi-bleeding\|Bleeding gastroduodenal ulcers]] with high-risk stigmata | Insufficient evidence for first-line use; rapid, potent acid inhibition raises the possibility of utility | 9 |

| Use | P-CAB | Comparator | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gastric ulcer healing, 8 wk | Vonoprazan 20 mg | Lansoprazole 30 mg | Noninferior (94% vs 94%) |
| Duodenal ulcer healing, 6 wk | Vonoprazan 20 mg | Lansoprazole 30 mg | Noninferior (96% vs 98%) |
| Gastric ulcer healing, 8 wk | Tegoprazan 50–100 mg | Lansoprazole 30 mg | Noninferior (95% vs 96%) |
| Secondary prophylaxis, long-term low-dose aspirin, 24 wk | Vonoprazan 10–20 mg | Lansoprazole 15 mg | Noninferior; recurrence 0.5–1.5% vs 2.8% |
| Secondary prophylaxis, long-term nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), 24 wk | Vonoprazan 10–20 mg | Lansoprazole 15 mg | Noninferior; recurrence 3% vs 6% |
| After endoscopic hemostasis, high-risk stigmata | Vonoprazan 20 mg twice daily × 3 d, then 20 mg daily × 28 d | Pantoprazole intravenous (IV) 8 mg/h × 3 d, then omeprazole 20 mg twice daily × 28 d | Noninferior for rebleeding at 3, 7 and 30 d (7.1% vs 10.4%) |

- Each row is a single RCT (Japan, Korea or Thailand) cited by [[aga-2024-pcab]].

> [!pitfall] PPI-refractory ulcer: exclude non-acid causes first
> P-CABs may help PPI treatment failures of ulcers only if the ulcer is not from a process that ulcerates without acid — cancer, opportunistic infection, vasculitis, ischemia ([[aga-2024-pcab]]).

- Zollinger-Ellison syndrome: potential indication; evidence scant ([[aga-2024-pcab]]).

## Value / Safety Caveats

- **Cost and access:** the US P-CAB costs markedly more than standard- or double-dose PPIs; more likely to need prior authorization ([[aga-2024-pcab]]).
- **Safety** — long-term data less robust than for PPIs; acid-suppression concerns raised for PPIs are expected to be shared ([[aga-2024-pcab]]):
  - [[clostridioides-difficile|*C. difficile*]]: association with vonoprazan comparable with, not beyond, PPIs (Japanese retrospective studies); other enteric infections expected to be similar to PPIs.
  - Gastric cancer: Japanese population data suggest a possible increased risk vs histamine-2 receptor antagonists (adjusted hazard ratio <2); rates similar to PPIs.
  - Serum gastrin rises higher than with PPIs and stays elevated for the duration of use, falling toward baseline within weeks of stopping.
  - Whether more potent acid inhibition adds adverse effects beyond PPIs is unknown.
- **Before an H. pylori test of cure** (urea breath or fecal antigen test): PPIs are stopped **2 weeks** beforehand; ACG says the same will *probably* apply to P-CABs but this has not been adequately studied ([[acg-2024-hp-treatment]]).
