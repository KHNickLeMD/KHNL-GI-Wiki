Epinephrine reaches gastrointestinal (GI) practice in three distinct roles, each with its own dilution and its own rule: **submucosal injection for hemostasis** (temporizing, never alone), **an adjunct in the lifting solution** for endoscopic resection, and **self-injectable epinephrine** for anaphylaxis in food-allergic patients seen by the GI clinician. The device-by-device hemostasis algorithm lives on [[endoscopic-hemostasis]]; this page owns the drug's concentrations, volumes, and limits.

| Use | Concentration | Source |

## Injection for Hemostasis

- **Mechanism:** injected at or near the bleeding site, it achieves initial hemostasis by **inducing vasospasm** — a local tamponade-plus-vasoconstriction effect, not vessel obliteration.
- Therefore it is a **temporizing measure**, used to slow bleeding and clear the field so definitive thermal or mechanical therapy can be aimed accurately.

### Never as Monotherapy

- **[[acg-2021-ugib\|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2021]], Recommendation 10 — strong recommendation, very-low- to moderate-quality evidence:** epinephrine injection is **not** to be used alone for ulcer bleeding, but in combination with another hemostatic modality.
  - Epinephrine alone vs standard monotherapies: **relative risk (RR) of further bleeding 2.20 (95% confidence interval [CI] 1.04–4.64)**.
  - Epinephrine **plus** a second modality vs epinephrine alone: **RR 0.34 (0.23–0.50)**.
  - Through-the-scope clips vs epinephrine monotherapy (indirect comparison): **RR 0.20 (0.07–0.56)**.
- ⚠ **But "never alone" is not "always add it."** [[aga-2020-nvugib-endoscopic-therapies\|AGA 2020]] finds **dual therapy has no advantage over properly applied thermal or mechanical monotherapy**. Epinephrine earns its place by improving visibility and buying time — not by adding hemostatic durability to a correctly executed clip or coagulation.
- **Epinephrine monotherapy for a high-risk lesion is on the list of reasons a first hemostasis attempt failed**, alongside ulcer size/location/fibrosis, single use of hemostatic powder, inadequate coagulation, and insufficient tissue grasped by a clip ([[aga-2020-nvugib-endoscopic-therapies]]).

### Volumes — Where They Are Stated

- **Diverticular bleeding** ([[asge-2014-lgib]]): **1-mL aliquots, up to 2 mL total**, injected at the site of active bleeding or around a nonbleeding visible vessel — alone or combined with thermal contact therapy.
- The same guideline's **Recommendation 9 (⊕⊕⊕⊕ high quality)** makes **epinephrine injection combined with thermal coagulation or clip placement** the preferred management of diverticular bleeding (site-specific algorithm: [[acute-lower-gi-bleeding]]).
- For **upper** GI bleeding, no ingested guideline prints an injection volume, an aliquot size, a site count, or a maximum dose (see below). Contrast absolute ethanol, where the **0.1–0.2 mL aliquot and 1–2 mL cap** *is* the safety margin ([[acg-2021-ugib]]) — that cap is on [[endoscopic-hemostasis]].

## Prophylaxis and Rescue During Polypectomy

- **Prophylaxis before resecting a large pedunculated polyp** ([[aga-2024-tailored-polypectomy\|AGA 2024]]): for **heads ≥20 mm and/or stalks ≥5 mm**, use **prophylactic epinephrine injection *or* a detachable loop/clip**. (Hot snare is itself indicated once size is ≥10 mm and/or the stalk is ≥5 mm — the bleeding-risk thresholds; see [[polypectomy]].)
- **Intraprocedural bleeding during endoscopic resection** ([[asge-2020-endoscopic-removal|ASGE 2020]] statement S2c.11, **conditional, low quality**; same position in [[usmstf-2020-endoscopic-removal|USMSTF 2020]]): treat with endoscopic coagulation (coagulation forceps or snare-tip soft coagulation) or mechanical therapy (clip), **with or without** combined dilute epinephrine injection — the epinephrine is optional, the coagulation or clip is not.
- ⚠ **In cold [[endoscopic-mucosal-resection|endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR)]] the need for epinephrine in the injectate "remains uncertain"** ([[asge-2020-endoscopic-removal]]); snare-tip soft coagulation of the edges and clip closure have not been studied in cold EMR either.

## As a Lifting-Solution Adjunct

[[aga-2025-lifting-agents|AGA 2025]] — full agent-by-agent comparison on [[endoscopic-mucosal-resection#Submucosal Lifting Agents|endoscopic mucosal resection]]:

- **Dilute epinephrine is added to the injectate for cold EMR and for EMR with electrocautery**, to reduce bleeding and improve visualization.
- **Trade-off on lift duration:** for colonic EMR, lift was **shortest with normal saline plus epinephrine (<3 minutes)** and **longest with hyaluronic acid (22 minutes)** — a cleaner field bought at the cost of the shortest working window of any agent studied.
- ⚠ **Unresolved harm:** serial injections of dilute epinephrine into the submucosa **may cause post-procedure abdominal pain**, which in the first hour is hard to distinguish from perforation or post-polypectomy syndrome. The agent that cleans the field can therefore muddy the most important early decision after resection.

## Self-Injectable Epinephrine for Anaphylaxis

From [[aga-2023-alpha-gal-syndrome|AGA 2023]], for [[alpha-gal-syndrome|alpha-gal syndrome]] — the relevant setting for a GI clinician, who often makes the diagnosis:

- **Ensure access to self-injectable epinephrine**, especially where there has been respiratory compromise. On accidental exposure: inform a reliable person and take **1–2 diphenhydramine 25 mg tablets**.
- **Supervised self-challenge** (only after alpha-gal immunoglobulin E [IgE] has fallen on repeat testing at 6–12 months): a **quarter serving** of meat, with antihistamines **and an epinephrine autoinjector available** and another person present.
- **Refer to an allergist — and counsel on the autoinjector — when skin or systemic changes occur:** facial or throat swelling, voice change, difficulty breathing, hives, or fainting. Patients with any systemic symptom should work with an allergist before self-challenge. **No established desensitization protocol exists.**

## What the Ingested Guidelines Do Not State

- **No maximum total dose, aliquot size, or number of injection sites for upper GI bleeding** — only the concentration. The 1-mL/2-mL figures above are the lower-GI diverticular numbers and should not be silently carried upward.
- **No systemic adverse-effect threshold** (tachyarrhythmia, hypertension) for submucosal use at any site, and no cumulative-dose ceiling across multiple injection sites in one session.
