Epinephrine reaches gastrointestinal (GI) practice in three distinct roles, each with its own dilution and its own rule: **submucosal injection for hemostasis** (temporizing, never alone), **an adjunct in the lifting solution** for endoscopic resection, and **self-injectable epinephrine** for anaphylaxis in food-allergic patients seen by the GI clinician. The device-by-device hemostasis algorithm lives on [[endoscopic-hemostasis]]; this page owns the drug's concentrations, volumes, and limits.

## Dilutions and Volumes

| Setting | Concentration | Volume | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ulcer bleeding, upper GI | **1:10,000 = 100 µg/mL** | **0.5–2.0 mL aliquots** — described as the common practice, not a graded recommendation | [[acg-2021-ugib\|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2021]], [[aga-2020-nvugib-endoscopic-therapies\|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2020]] |
| Diverticular bleeding | **1:10,000 or 1:20,000** | **1–2 mL aliquots**, at the site of active bleeding or around a nonbleeding visible vessel | [[asge-2014-lgib\|American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2014]] |
| Giant pedunculated polyp, to shrink the head before resection | **1:10,000** | **4–8 mL** into head *and* stalk — case series of 3 lesions ≥30 mm only | [[asge-2020-endoscopic-removal\|ASGE 2020]] |
| Lifting-solution adjunct, cold or electrocautery [[endoscopic-mucosal-resection\|endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR)]] | **1:200,000 to 1:500,000** usual; the agent table spans **1:50,000–1:500,000** | not specified | [[aga-2025-lifting-agents\|AGA 2025]] |

None of these documents sets a maximum total dose, a number of injection sites, or a systemic adverse-effect threshold (tachyarrhythmia, hypertension) for submucosal epinephrine.

## Injection for Hemostasis

- **Mechanism:** injected at or near the bleeding site, it achieves initial hemostasis by **inducing vasospasm** — a local tamponade-plus-vasoconstriction effect, not vessel obliteration ([[aga-2020-nvugib-endoscopic-therapies]]).
- Therefore it is a **temporizing measure** — slow the bleeding, clear the field, so definitive thermal or mechanical therapy can be aimed accurately. The same framing applies to bleeding from a colonic resection bed ([[asge-2026-lgib]]).

### Never as Monotherapy

- **[[acg-2021-ugib|ACG 2021]], Recommendation 10 — strong recommendation, very-low- to moderate-quality evidence:** epinephrine injection is **not** to be used alone for ulcer bleeding, but in combination with another hemostatic modality.

| Comparison (further bleeding) | Risk ratio (RR), 95% confidence interval (CI) | Trials |
|---|---|---|
| Epinephrine alone vs standard monotherapies (bipolar electrocoagulation, clips) | **2.20 (1.04–4.64)** — significant heterogeneity, I² = 56% | 4 |
| Epinephrine **+ second modality** vs epinephrine alone | **0.34 (0.23–0.50)** | 7 |
| Clips vs epinephrine monotherapy | **0.20 (0.07–0.56)** | 2 |

- **Epinephrine monotherapy for a high-risk lesion is on the checklist of reasons a first hemostasis attempt failed**, alongside ulcer size/location/fibrosis, single use of hemostatic powder, inadequate coagulation, and insufficient tissue grasped by a clip ([[aga-2020-nvugib-endoscopic-therapies]]).

> [!pitfall] "Never alone" is not "always add it"
> Dual therapy has **no advantage over properly applied thermal or mechanical monotherapy** ([[aga-2020-nvugib-endoscopic-therapies]]). Epinephrine earns its place by improving visibility and buying time — not by adding durability to a correctly executed clip or coagulation.

### Combining with Thermal or Mechanical Therapy

[[acg-2021-ugib|ACG 2021]] asked whether thermal devices or clips should *always* be preceded by epinephrine, and declined to say so:

- **Epinephrine before bipolar electrocoagulation vs bipolar alone:** further bleeding RR 0.35 (0.18–0.71), mortality RR 0.49 (0.09–2.60), 2 small trials — but further-bleeding rates in the bipolar-alone arms were unusually high (25%, 34%), so the panel would not endorse routine pre-injection.
- **Clips + epinephrine vs clips alone:** no difference in further bleeding, RR 1.10 (0.42–2.88), or mortality, RR 0.63 (0.10–3.87). In both trials the epinephrine was injected **after** the clip.
- **Timing:** epinephrine is commonly given *before* the second modality to reduce bleeding and improve visibility, or to prevent rebleeding while the second modality is applied. Some endoscopists inject *after* clip placement instead — large-volume injection causes local swelling that can make clip application harder — or to treat residual bleeding once clips are in.

### Diverticular Bleeding

- **Current preference is mechanical:** band ligation or through-the-scope clipping chosen by colonic side — band ligation where feasible for transverse and more proximal lesions, clipping for distal lesions ([[asge-2026-lgib|ASGE 2026]], conditional, very-low quality). Epinephrine is not part of that regimen; it remains an adjunctive temporizing measure. Site-specific algorithm: [[acute-lower-gi-bleeding]].
- **This replaces the older preference.** [[asge-2014-lgib|ASGE 2014]] Recommendation 9 (⊕⊕⊕⊕, high quality) made **epinephrine injection combined with thermal coagulation or clip placement** the preferred management of diverticular bleeding.
- **If epinephrine is used:** 1:10,000 or 1:20,000, 1–2 mL aliquots at the site of active bleeding or around a nonbleeding visible vessel, alone or with thermal contact therapy ([[asge-2014-lgib]]).

## Prophylaxis and Rescue During Polypectomy

### Pedunculated Polyp Prophylaxis

Both documents use the same lesion threshold and disagree only on whether epinephrine qualifies as prophylaxis:

| Source | Threshold | Prophylaxis | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[aga-2024-tailored-polypectomy\|AGA 2024]] | head ≥20 mm **and/or** stalk ≥5 mm | epinephrine injection into the stalk **or** detachable nylon loop or standard clips | expert review, statements not formally rated |
| [[usmstf-2020-endoscopic-removal\|US Multi-Society Task Force on Colorectal Cancer (USMSTF) 2020]] and [[asge-2020-endoscopic-removal\|ASGE 2020]] | head ≥20 mm **or** stalk thickness ≥5 mm | **mechanical ligation only** — detachable loop or clips | strong recommendation, moderate-quality evidence |

- The 2020 documents prefer mechanical ligation precisely because it **may reduce immediate and delayed bleeding compared with epinephrine injection alone or no therapy**; AGA 2024 is the more recent statement and accepts either.
- Independent of prophylaxis, pedunculated lesions >10 mm are removed by hot snare — thresholds and technique on [[polypectomy]].

### Intraprocedural Bleeding

- **Treat with endoscopic coagulation** (coagulation forceps or snare-tip soft coagulation) **or mechanical therapy** (clip), **with or without** combined dilute epinephrine injection ([[asge-2020-endoscopic-removal]] and [[usmstf-2020-endoscopic-removal]], conditional, low-quality evidence). The epinephrine is optional; the coagulation or clip is not.
- **In cold EMR the need for epinephrine in the injectate remains uncertain** ([[asge-2020-endoscopic-removal]]); snare-tip soft coagulation of the edges and clip closure have likewise not been used in cold EMR.

## As a Lifting-Solution Adjunct

[[aga-2025-lifting-agents|AGA 2025]] — full agent-by-agent comparison on [[endoscopic-mucosal-resection#Submucosal Lifting Agents|endoscopic mucosal resection]]:

- **Dilute epinephrine is added to the injectate for cold EMR and for EMR with electrocautery**, giving a cleaner working field with less intraprocedural oozing.
- **Trade-off on lift duration:** for colonic EMR, lift was **shortest with normal saline plus epinephrine (<3 minutes)** and **longest with hyaluronic acid (22 minutes)** — a cleaner field bought at the cost of the shortest working window of any agent studied.

> [!pitfall] Epinephrine can mimic a perforation
> Serial injections of dilute epinephrine into the submucosa **may cause post-procedure abdominal pain**, which in the first hour is hard to distinguish from perforation or post-polypectomy syndrome ([[aga-2025-lifting-agents]]). The agent that cleans the field can muddy the most important early decision after resection.

## Self-Injectable Epinephrine for Anaphylaxis

From [[aga-2023-alpha-gal-syndrome|AGA 2023]], for [[alpha-gal-syndrome|alpha-gal syndrome]] — the relevant setting for a GI clinician, who often makes the diagnosis:

- **Ensure access to self-injectable epinephrine**, especially where there is concern for respiratory compromise. On accidental exposure: tell a reliable person and take **1–2 diphenhydramine 25 mg tablets**.
- **Self-challenge:** if tick bites are avoided, it is reasonable to repeat alpha-gal immunoglobulin E (IgE) **6–12 months after diagnosis**; as the level falls or becomes negative, patients may resume **first dairy, then mammalian meat**, in small portions. Self-challenge = **a quarter serving** of meat, with antihistamines **and an epinephrine autoinjector available** and another person present.
- Patients with **significant systemic symptoms (itching, hives)** should work with an allergist before self-challenge.
- **Refer to an allergist — and arrange formal autoinjector counseling — for skin or systemic changes with reactions:** facial or throat swelling, voice change, difficulty breathing, hives, or fainting. **No allergist-established desensitization protocol exists for alpha-gal syndrome.**
