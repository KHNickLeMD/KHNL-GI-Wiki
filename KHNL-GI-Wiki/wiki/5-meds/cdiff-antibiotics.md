## Scope

Antibacterial drug therapy of *Clostridioides difficile* infection (CDI) — the agents, doses, routes, durations, and which regimen the clinical scenario selects. Diagnosis, the full severity discussion, surgery, infection control, and microbiota restoration itself live on [[clostridioides-difficile]] and [[fmt|fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT)]].

---

## The Agents

| Agent | Standard regimen | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Fidaxomicin** | 200 mg by mouth (PO) twice daily (BID) × 10 days | **Preferred first-line for all nonfulminant CDI** — narrower spectrum, preserves anaerobic commensals, lower recurrence |
| **Vancomycin, oral** | 125 mg PO four times daily (QID) × 10 days | Acceptable first-line alternative; the agent for severe and fulminant disease, suppression, pregnancy, and lactation |
| **Vancomycin, rectal** | 500 mg every 6 hours (q6h), in 100 mL normal saline (NS) | Fulminant disease **with ileus** only — oral delivery to the colon is impaired |
| **Metronidazole** | 500 mg intravenous (IV) every 8 hours (q8h) | **Fulminant disease only**, as an IV adjunct — it is secreted into the colon, giving intracolonic exposure |

- Fidaxomicin is a narrow-spectrum agent that targets *C. difficile* and preserves gut microbiota diversity. A **generic fidaxomicin is United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved**, which may improve access. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]], [[aga-2026-cdiff-ibd]]
- **Oral metronidazole is no longer a CDI regimen.** The [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2026 adult clinical practice update (CPU)]] states metronidazole **should not be used outside of fulminant disease** (Best Practice Advice [BPA] 4).

> **Guidelines differ — oral metronidazole.** [[acg-2021-cdiff|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2021]] Rec 6 permits *"oral metronidazole 500 mg 3 times daily (TID) for 10 days … for the treatment of an initial nonsevere CDI in low-risk patients"* (strong recommendation, moderate quality) — low-risk meaning younger outpatients with minimal comorbidity in cost-sensitive settings. **Follow AGA 2026**, the newer same-tier source: metronidazole is inferior in older patients, in those hospitalized for CDI, and in those with multiple comorbidities, and carries a higher recurrence risk. The ACG low-risk carve-out is superseded.

### Choosing Between Fidaxomicin and Vancomycin

Both achieve similar initial cure; the separation is in **recurrence**. Decide by recurrence risk, ease of dosing, access, and cost, in shared decision-making.

| Endpoint | Fidaxomicin | Vancomycin | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recurrence reduction (narrative range) | **6%–11% fewer** recurrent infections | — | [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]] |
| Clinical cure, modified intention-to-treat (mITT) | 88.2% | 85.8% (noninferior) | [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]] |
| Clinical cure, per-protocol | 92.1% | 89.8% | [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]] |
| Cochrane — symptomatic cure *without* recurrence at 1 month | **71%** | 61% (relative risk [RR] 1.17, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.07–1.27) | [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]] |
| Pivotal double-blind randomized controlled trial (RCT), cure (mITT) | 82.1% | 88.6% (similar) | [[acg-2021-cdiff]] |
| Recurrence within 30 days | **13.0%** | 26.6% (P = 0.02) | [[acg-2021-cdiff]] |

- **Give fidaxomicin early rather than late:** recurrence after fidaxomicin was **23% in patients with 1 previous episode vs 29% with ≥2 prior episodes** — the benefit is larger the earlier in the disease course it is used. [[acg-2021-cdiff]]
- **Practical dosing:** QID oral vancomycin doses may be given during **waking hours** — patients do not need to wake overnight to take it exactly q6h. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]
- **Assess response at day 3–5:** expect **≥50% reduction in loose or liquid bowel movements per day within 3 days, and no later than 5 days**. If not achieved, look for another explanation rather than extending or escalating the antibiotic. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]

## Severity Drives the Regimen

Classify every hospitalized patient at diagnosis — severity sets the drug, the dose, and the route. (Full severity discussion, including which creatinine rule to use when: [[clostridioides-difficile]].)

| Stratum | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **Nonsevere** | Neither severe criterion met |
| **Severe** | White blood cell (WBC) count **≥15,000 cells/mm³** ([[acg-2021-cdiff]]; AGA 2026 words it **>15,000 cells/µL**) **OR** creatinine **>1.5 mg/dL** (absolute cutoff, [[acg-2021-cdiff]]) **or >1.5 × premorbid baseline** (ratio, accepted by [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]) |
| **Fulminant** | Severe criteria **PLUS** hypotension/shock **OR** ileus **OR** megacolon |

## First Episode (nonfulminant)

- **Fidaxomicin 200 mg PO BID × 10 days** — favored, any nonfulminant severity. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]] BPA 4
- **Vancomycin 125 mg PO QID × 10 days** — acceptable. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]] BPA 4; [[acg-2021-cdiff]] Rec 4 (strong, low quality)
- Fidaxomicin for an initial nonsevere episode is also [[acg-2021-cdiff]] Rec 5 (strong, moderate quality).
- Ten-day courses are what every trial of both agents has used, and are sufficient for most patients. [[acg-2021-cdiff]]

## Severe Disease

- **Vancomycin 125 mg PO QID × 10 days** — [[acg-2021-cdiff]] Rec 7 (strong, low quality)
- **Fidaxomicin 200 mg PO BID × 10 days** — [[acg-2021-cdiff]] Rec 8 (conditional, very low quality; patients with fulminant and life-threatening illness were excluded from the fidaxomicin trials). AGA 2026 makes no severity carve-out and favors fidaxomicin for all nonfulminant disease.
- **Metronidazole is not for severe disease.** Vancomycin reduced 30-day all-cause mortality **15.3% vs 19.8%** (P = 0.01) in a propensity-matched cohort of 3,130 patients; switching to vancomycin only after metronidazole failure (≥48 hours) produced longer hospital stays, more acute kidney injury (AKI), and lower cure. [[acg-2021-cdiff]]

**Do not escalate the vancomycin dose in severe (nonfulminant) disease** [[acg-2021-cdiff]]:

- 125 mg QID consistently achieves stool drug levels **≥1,000-fold above the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) needed against *C. difficile* (1.2 µg/L)**.
- Small RCT (n = 46 hospitalized patients): **no difference** in cure rate, time to response, or recurrence between **125 mg and 500 mg** of oral vancomycin given QID × 10 days.
- Single-centre series (n = 78): low-dose (≤500 mg/day) vs high-dose (>500 mg/day) gave cure **60% vs 64%** at day 10, with no difference in time to cure, complications, or mortality; a nonsignificant trend to less recurrence with high dose (12% vs 2%, P = 0.09).
- Fecal vancomycin concentrations correlate **inversely** with stool frequency. If a patient is not responding to standard dosing, **assess for alternative causes of diarrhea** rather than raising the dose.

**Refractory severe (nonfulminant) disease** — hospitalized, ongoing diarrhea and persistent leukocytosis after **5–7 days** of oral vancomycin 125 mg q6h — should be referred for [[fmt|FMT]]; this is the [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults|AGA 2026]] authors' working definition, as no standard definition exists.

## Fulminant Disease

### Triple Therapy

All three components from the start, not sequential escalation. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]] BPA 10

| Component | Dose | When |
|---|---|---|
| Vancomycin, oral/enteral | **500 mg q6h** | All fulminant disease — **at least the first 48 hours** ([[acg-2021-cdiff]]: first 48–72 hours) |
| Metronidazole, **IV** | **500 mg q8h** | All fulminant disease ([[acg-2021-cdiff]] Rec 9: conditional, very low quality) |
| Vancomycin, **rectal/enema** | **500 mg q6h** ([[acg-2021-cdiff]]: in 100 mL NS) | **Add if ileus is present** ([[acg-2021-cdiff]] Rec 10: conditional, very low quality) |

- [[acg-2021-cdiff]] Rec 9 is strong/very low quality for the 500 mg q6h vancomycin component plus volume resuscitation.
- **The high dose is about delivery, not demonstrated benefit.** Direct comparison of low-dose (<500 mg/day) and high-dose (>500 mg/day) vancomycin failed to show significant differences in cure, time to cure, mortality, or complications; the recommendation is **entirely expert opinion**, justified by the high mortality of fulminant disease. [[acg-2021-cdiff]], [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]
- **The rectal route is theoretical too:** a retrospective case–control study of fulminant disease in the intensive care unit found no advantage from adjunctive vancomycin enemas (mortality 45.8% vs 41.7%, P = 0.73; need for colectomy 16.7% in both). [[acg-2021-cdiff]]
- **IV metronidazole evidence is split:** one single-centre intensive-care retrospective study found lower mortality with vancomycin plus metronidazole than vancomycin alone (15.9% vs 36.4%, P = 0.03); a later multicentre retrospective study of 526 patients found **no benefit** for colectomy, death, or recurrence. It is most defensible when paralytic ileus impairs transit of oral vancomycin. [[acg-2021-cdiff]]
- **Avoid empiric non-CDI antibiotics** — they worsen dysbiosis. If a coinfection genuinely requires them, continue **low-dose suppressive vancomycin 125 mg daily or BID** alongside. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]

### De-escalation and Total Duration

- After clinical response — **typically 3–5 days** — narrow to **vancomycin 125 mg QID monotherapy**. ([[acg-2021-cdiff]]: step down to 125 mg q6h for an additional 10 days once improvement is seen at 48–72 hours.)
- **Total duration: minimum 10 days; the AGA 2026 authors favor 14 days.**
- **No response or worsening at 48 hours** ([[acg-2021-cdiff]]: 48–72 hours) → multidisciplinary reassessment for [[fmt|FMT]] or colectomy.
- **After recovery from severe or fulminant disease:** vancomycin **125 mg QID to a total of 14 days**, then **125 mg once or twice daily** as suppression until a final outpatient fecal microbiota–based therapy can be arranged. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]

### Antibiotic Dosing Around FMT

- Oral vancomycin **125 mg q6h** (or fidaxomicin 200 mg every 12 hours) is **continued between FMT administrations for as long as pseudomembranes persist**. [[acg-2021-cdiff]]
- Vancomycin may be **held on the day of FMT** but is otherwise continued after it. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]
- If the patient improves clinically before pseudomembranes have fully resolved, continue vancomycin or fidaxomicin for a **minimum of 5 days**, with a final FMT as an outpatient. [[acg-2021-cdiff]]

## Recurrence

**Definition:** clinically significant diarrhea with a positive stool test within **8 weeks** of completing anti-CDI therapy. About 90% of recurrences fall in that window; events up to **1 year** out are also treated as recurrence or reinfection. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]

### First Recurrence — Switch Class

Treat a first recurrence with an agent **different from the one used initially**. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]] BPA 7

| Initial treatment | First-recurrence options |
|---|---|
| **Fidaxomicin** | Vancomycin **taper/pulse**, *or* fidaxomicin **EXTEND** pulsed regimen |
| **Vancomycin** | Fidaxomicin — standard 10-day course (200 mg BID) *or* **EXTEND** pulsed regimen |

**Vancomycin taper/pulse — the two published example schedules (both explicitly examples):**

| Step | [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults\|AGA 2026]] | [[acg-2021-cdiff\|ACG 2021]] |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 125 mg QID × **14 days** | 125 mg QID × **10–14 days** |
| 2 | 125 mg BID × **14 days** | 125 mg BID × **7 days** |
| 3 | 125 mg once daily × **14 days** | 125 mg once daily × **7 days** |
| 4 | 125 mg **every third day** × 14 days | 125 mg **every 2–3 days** × 8 weeks |

- Taper dose and duration may differ by individual practice, but **tapers extending beyond 60 days have not been studied and are unlikely to add benefit**. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]
- ACG Rec 12 (taper/pulse after an initial course of fidaxomicin, vancomycin, or metronidazole) is strong/very low quality; ACG Rec 13 (fidaxomicin after an initial course of vancomycin or metronidazole) is strong/moderate quality.

**Fidaxomicin EXTEND pulsed regimen:** **200 mg PO BID on days 1–5, then 200 mg once daily on alternate days, days 7–25.** [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]], [[aga-2026-cdiff-ibd]]

- Fidaxomicin after vancomycin or metronidazole prevented a **second** recurrence better than vancomycin: 19.7% vs 35.5% within 28 days (P = 0.045). [[acg-2021-cdiff]]
- EXTEND vs a standard 10-day vancomycin course in patients aged ≥60 years: sustained clinical cure at 30 days **70% (124/177) vs 59% (106/179)**; difference 11% (95% CI 1.0–20.7), P = 0.030; odds ratio (OR) 1.62 (95% CI 1.04–2.54). Patients with multiple previous recurrences were excluded. [[acg-2021-cdiff]]
- **Metronidazole must not be used for recurrent disease** ([[acg-2021-cdiff]] strong, low quality) — vancomycin cleared *C. difficile* by end of therapy far more often (89% vs 59%, P = 0.001), and metronidazole should be **limited to one lifetime course** because of neurotoxicity with prolonged or repeated use.
- **Bridge for patients with ongoing recurrence risk** (continuing systemic antibiotics, cytotoxic chemotherapy): after the 10-day course, continue **suppressive vancomycin 125 mg daily**, then taper off or hand over to microbiota restoration once those treatments finish. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]

### Second or Further Recurrence

Antibiotics here are a **bridge to microbiota restoration**, not the definitive treatment (see [[fmt]] and [[clostridioides-difficile]] for eligibility and delivery).

- Treat the episode itself with **fidaxomicin 200 mg BID × 10 days or vancomycin 125 mg QID × 10 days**, immediately followed by microbiota restoration therapy. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]] Figure 2B
- **Bridge dose while awaiting approval/access:** suppressive oral vancomycin **at least 125 mg daily**.
- **Washout before restoration:** stop antibacterial therapy **1–4 days before** the microbiota product is given ([[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]; [[aga-2026-cdiff-ibd]] states 1–3 days).

## Suppressive and Prophylactic Vancomycin

**Long-term suppression (secondary prophylaxis in multiply recurrent disease)** — [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]] BPA 9, [[acg-2021-cdiff]] Rec 17 (conditional, very low quality):

- **Who:** multiple recurrent episodes in a patient who is **not a candidate** for fecal microbiota–based therapy (ongoing comorbidity, limited life expectancy, ongoing or frequent systemic antibiotics) **or** who has failed multiple courses of it.
- **Dose: oral vancomycin 125 mg once daily**, prolonged or indefinite in selected patients. Some patients continue to pass loose stools at this dose — **twice or three times daily dosing may be necessary** in those cases. [[acg-2021-cdiff]]
- In the ACG-cited series (n = 20, median age 80, median 4 prior episodes), suppression ran for a **minimum of 8 weeks**; one relapse occurred over 200 patient-months on therapy, and **31% relapsed within 6 weeks of stopping**.
- Further recurrence **without** intervention exceeds **40%**; recurrence exceeds **30% after stopping** prophylaxis — hence "ongoing" rather than time-limited in some patients. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]

**Prophylaxis during subsequent systemic antibiotics (oral vancomycin prophylaxis, OVP) — not advised:**

> **Guidelines differ.** [[acg-2021-cdiff]] Rec 18 (conditional, low quality) permits OVP: **low-dose oral vancomycin 125 mg once daily, typically continued until 5 days after completion of the systemic antibiotics**, in *"patients aged 65 years or older with significant immunocompromise who were hospitalized for severe CDI within the past 3 months."* The newer [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults|AGA 2026 CPU]] (BPA 12) advises **against** vancomycin during systemic antibiotic administration to prevent CDI. **Follow AGA 2026.**
>
> **Why:** the only RCT showed **no statistically significant difference** in recurrent CDI; the 13.5% absolute reduction observed was in a trial **not powered** to detect it. ACG's support came from pooled observational data (10 studies, 9,258 patients: future CDI **13.3% with OVP vs 21.9% without**, OR 0.34, 95% CI 0.20–0.59) that the RCT did not confirm.
>
> **Instead:** close observation for diarrhea during and after antibiotics, with prompt testing and treatment; a **standing order for *C. difficile* testing** expedites the workup. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]
>
> [[aga-2026-cdiff-ibd|AGA 2026's IBD-specific CPU]] (BPA 12) still says OVP **may be considered** as secondary prevention in patients with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) and a history of CDI who are receiving systemic antibiotics — narrower guidance for that population only.

**Transplant-specific prophylaxis data** cited by [[acg-2021-cdiff]], for context — note AGA 2026 advises against prophylactic vancomycin generally:

- Oral vancomycin **125 mg BID** in allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplant recipients: 0/90 (0%) developed CDI vs 11/55 (20%) without prophylaxis (P < 0.001), retrospective.
- Placebo-controlled RCT of **600 adults** undergoing hematopoietic stem cell transplantation on broad-spectrum antimicrobials: once-daily **fidaxomicin** prophylaxis gave confirmed CDI **4.3% vs 10.7%** through 60 days post-treatment.
- The theoretical cost of prophylaxis is promotion of drug-resistant organisms such as vancomycin-resistant enterococci (VRE) and further microbiome disruption. [[acg-2021-cdiff]]

## Special Populations

### Pregnancy and Lactation

| Agent | Pregnancy / peripartum | Breastfeeding |
|---|---|---|
| **Vancomycin, oral** | **First-line** ([[acg-2021-cdiff\|ACG 2021]] Key Concept 6) — decades of clinical experience, minimal systemic absorption | **Recommended** (Key Concept 7) — not absorbed, large molecular weight, not expected to enter breast milk, and any that did would not be absorbed by the infant's gut |
| **Fidaxomicin** | **Reserve for vancomycin treatment failures** — no adequate or well-controlled human studies; no fetal harm in pregnant rabbits and rats at IV doses ~66× and 200× human plasma exposure | **Use with caution** until better data — acts locally with minimal systemic absorption, so transfer into breast milk should be minimal |
| **Metronidazole** | Avoid — treatment change for adverse events or nonresolution occurred in **50%** of 31 pregnant patients treated | Avoid — secreted in breast milk; the infant can be exposed to **up to 24% of the maternal dose** |

- Treatment failure risk and the consequences of severe disease in pregnancy are why vancomycin is chosen despite the cost. [[acg-2021-cdiff]]

### Immunocompromise and Cancer

- **Vancomycin or fidaxomicin first-line; avoid metronidazole.** [[acg-2021-cdiff]] Key Concept 8
- **Cancer** (post hoc analysis of two large fidaxomicin-vs-vancomycin RCTs): patients with cancer (n = 183) had a lower cure rate than those without (79% vs 87%). Within the cancer group, fidaxomicin achieved **higher initial cure — 85% vs 74%** (P = 0.065) — and **lower likelihood of recurrence** (OR 0.37, P = 0.018). [[acg-2021-cdiff]]

### IBD

- **Prefer fidaxomicin** for an initial episode; use vancomycin if fidaxomicin is unavailable or cost-prohibitive. **Metronidazole should not be used** — high resistance and treatment failure. [[aga-2026-cdiff-ibd]] BPA 4
- **Duration and regimen options** ([[aga-2026-cdiff-ibd]] Table 1): typically **10 days**; consider the **fidaxomicin EXTEND regimen**; alternatives are **prolonged vancomycin 21–42 days** for an initial infection, or a **vancomycin taper-pulse** for complicated or recurrent cases.
- [[acg-2021-cdiff]] Rec 22 (strong, very low quality): **vancomycin 125 mg PO QID for a minimum of 14 days** in patients with IBD and CDI — longer than the standard 10-day course.
- Supporting data: prolonged vancomycin (**21–42 days**) vs shorter (<21 days) reduced recurrence from **11.7% to 1.8%** (OR 0.13, P = 0.043); a vancomycin-containing regimen vs metronidazole gave fewer 30-day readmissions (0% vs 31.0%, P = 0.04) and shorter stays (6.38 vs 13.62 days, P = 0.02). [[acg-2021-cdiff]]
- Fidaxomicin in IBD: small single-centre studies give cure rates of **81%–82%** ([[acg-2021-cdiff]]) / approximately **80%–90%** ([[aga-2026-cdiff-ibd]]). Phase IIIb/IV pharmacokinetic data show fidaxomicin and its primary metabolite **OP-1118** have **minimal systemic absorption with high stool concentrations** in patients with IBD.
- Patients with active CDI who are **on concomitant systemic antibiotics** may benefit from fidaxomicin rather than vancomycin. [[aga-2026-cdiff-ibd]]
- **Give 2–3 days of standard-of-care anti-CDI antibiotics before escalating IBD therapy**, and do not stop immunomodulators, biologics, or small molecules abruptly. In **fulminant** disease, consider a temporary hold of nonessential immunosuppressants until the CDI improves. [[aga-2026-cdiff-ibd]]
- Disease-side management in [[ulcerative-colitis]], [[crohns-disease]], and [[pouchitis]]: see [[clostridioides-difficile]].

### Renal Disease

- Chronic kidney disease stages 3–4 was associated with **lower initial cure and greater chance of recurrence**. Initial cure rates were similar between vancomycin and fidaxomicin, but **recurrence was higher after vancomycin independent of renal function**. [[acg-2021-cdiff]]
- Oral vancomycin needs no renal dose adjustment for CDI: serum levels were undetectable in 98% of patients **even with renal insufficiency**. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]

## Adverse Effects, Absorption, and Resistance

These guidelines address tolerability only where it changes drug choice; neither gives a full adverse-effect profile for oral vancomycin or fidaxomicin.

**Oral vancomycin**

- Systemic absorption is extremely limited — **no detectable serum levels in 98%** of patients in a prospective study, including those with renal insufficiency, implying **negligible risk of ototoxicity**. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]
- **No emergence of VRE** has been observed with long-term suppressive use ([[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]); the ACG-cited series recorded no adverse events and no VRE over 200 patient-months. Resistance promotion remains a theoretical concern for prophylactic use.

**Fidaxomicin**

- Acts locally in the gut with minimal systemic absorption; fidaxomicin and metabolite OP-1118 reach high stool concentrations. [[acg-2021-cdiff]], [[aga-2026-cdiff-ibd]]
- Cost and access are the practical limits; a generic formulation is FDA-approved. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]

**Metronidazole**

- **Neurotoxicity with prolonged or repeated use** → **limit to one lifetime course**. [[acg-2021-cdiff]]
- Inferior to vancomycin in older patients, in those hospitalized for CDI, in those with multiple comorbidities, and in hypoalbuminemia; higher recurrence risk; high resistance and treatment failure. Delaying the switch to vancomycin after metronidazole failure increases length of stay and AKI and lowers cure. [[acg-2021-cdiff]], [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]], [[aga-2026-cdiff-ibd]]

**[[rifaximin|Rifaximin]]** — **30%–50% resistance rates** were reported across nonrandomized trials, which is why routine use is not recommended. [[acg-2021-cdiff]]

## What Not to Use

| Agent / class | Why |
|---|---|
| **Oral metronidazole** (outside fulminant disease) | Higher recurrence; inferior in older, hospitalized, and multimorbid patients. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults\|AGA 2026]] BPA 4 |
| **Cholestyramine and other bile acid–binding agents** | Not as monotherapy and **not concurrently** with oral vancomycin or fidaxomicin — they **bind the antibiotic and reduce its effectiveness**, and have no activity against *C. difficile*. Psyllium fiber may be used to bulk stools **after** recovery. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults\|AGA 2026]] BPA 5 |
| **Antimotility agents ([[loperamide\|loperamide]])** | Contraindicated in **untreated CDI, fulminant CDI, and severe colonic IBD**; not suggested generally. If used, shortest duration and **only after anti-CDI therapy has started** — theoretical risk of toxin retention and [[toxic-megacolon\|toxic megacolon]] |
| **Rifaximin** as a chaser | 400 mg TID × 20 days after standard therapy reduced recurrence 31% → 15% (not statistically significant, small sample); 400 mg TID × 14 days then 200 mg TID × 14 days reduced recurrence 30% → 16% (P = 0.06). Resistance 30%–50%. Routine use **not recommended** |
| **Tigecycline** | Early open-label efficacy only; a phase 2 trial was discontinued for slow enrollment. A review of 10 reports and meta-analysis of 4 reported clinical cure in 79%. **Not recommended** |
| **Cadazolid, surotomycin, teicoplanin, [[nitazoxanide\|nitazoxanide]]** | A Cochrane review concluded the evidence does not currently support their use; ACG agrees |
| **[[probiotics\|Probiotics]]** | Not advised to prevent an initial or recurrent infection. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults\|AGA 2026]] BPA 12; [[aga-2026-cdiff-ibd\|AGA 2026 IBD]] BPA 11 |
| **Empiric non-CDI antibiotics in fulminant disease** | Worsen dysbiosis. Treat a documented coinfection only, with infectious disease input, continuing suppressive vancomycin alongside |
| **Bezlotoxumab** | **No longer commercially available**, which supersedes [[acg-2021-cdiff\|ACG 2021]] Rec 19. [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults\|AGA 2026]] |

**Do not stop a [[proton-pump-inhibitors|proton pump inhibitor (PPI)]] that has a legitimate indication**, and **do not dose-reduce it** — there is no evidence supporting dose reduction; it is a binary decision. [[acg-2021-cdiff]] Rec 20 (strong, very low quality), [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]] BPA 13.
