## Class / Mechanism
- **Gamma-aminobutyric acid type B (GABA-B) receptor agonist** ([[acg-2024-ald|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2024]], [[aasld-2020-ald|American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD) 2020]]).
- **Reflux:** inhibits **transient lower esophageal sphincter (LES) relaxations** — the events that permit reflux episodes. Reduces postprandial acid and nonacid reflux events, nocturnal reflux activity, and belching episodes ([[acg-2021-gerd|ACG 2021]]).
- **Not US Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved for alcohol use disorder (AUD)**; it is the non-FDA-approved AUD agent studied most in advanced liver disease ([[acg-2024-ald]]).

## Indications

| Indication | Position |
|---|---|
| AUD in **compensated** [[alcohol-associated-liver-disease\|alcohol-associated liver disease (ALD)]] | Recommend as an option — **strong, moderate evidence** (ACG 2024 Recommendation 9) |
| [[gerd\|Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)]] **without** objective evidence of GERD | **Do not recommend** — strong, moderate evidence (ACG 2021 Recommendation 13) |
| Objectively confirmed GERD, still symptomatic on optimal [[proton-pump-inhibitors\|proton pump inhibitor (PPI)]] therapy | A trial can be considered (ACG 2021) |
| Regurgitation- or belch-predominant GERD | Phenotype-matched adjunct ([[aga-2022-personalized-gerd\|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2022]] Best Practice Advice (BPA) 10) |
| Supragastric belching **alone** | **Not advocated** ([[aga-2023-bloating\|AGA 2023]]) |
| Gastric belching due to GERD | May be considered (AGA 2023) |
| Refractory [[rumination-syndrome\|rumination syndrome]] | Reasonable next step after diaphragmatic breathing ([[aga-2018-rumination-syndrome\|AGA 2018]] BPA 7) |
| Severe muscle cramps in [[cirrhosis\|cirrhosis]] | Can be considered ([[aasld-2021-ascites-sbp-hrs\|AASLD 2021]]) |
| Extraesophageal reflux / chronic cough | **Role uncertain** — no controlled studies ([[aga-2018-extraesophageal-gerd\|AGA 2018]]) |

### Alcohol Use Disorder in Liver Disease
- **ACG 2024 Recommendation 9 (strong recommendation, moderate evidence):** in patients with **compensated ALD**, recommend baclofen as an option for treatment of AUD. The other agents are only *suggested* (acamprosate/naltrexone, gabapentin/topiramate — all conditional, very low), and disulfiram should not be used anywhere on the ALD spectrum.
- **AASLD 2020 guidance statement:** *"Based on limited data, the use of acamprosate or baclofen can be considered for the treatment of AUD in patients with ALD."*
- **Why it leads:** the **only AUD pharmacotherapy tested in a randomized controlled trial (RCT) in alcohol-associated cirrhosis with AUD**, plus two small uncontrolled observational studies ([[aasld-2020-ald]]).
  - 12-week course in compensated *and* decompensated alcohol-associated cirrhosis → **improved total abstinence and less relapse vs control over 1 year**, acceptable safety profile ([[aasld-2020-ald]]).
  - Average duration of use **5.8 months** safely reduced alcohol use in [[alcohol-associated-hepatitis|alcohol-associated hepatitis (AH)]] and increased abstinence rates in ALD cirrhosis ([[acg-2024-ald]]).
- **Metabolism hepatic (limited); excretion renal** ([[aasld-2020-ald]]).
- **Alcohol withdrawal syndrome (AWS):** benzodiazepines are the drugs of choice, but their side effects in advanced liver disease and abuse potential with repeated use have led to baclofen, clonidine, gabapentin and topiramate being *proposed* for AWS in ALD including alcohol-associated cirrhosis. **Efficacy and safety in AH are unknown** — prospective studies are required ([[acg-2024-ald]]).

> [!pitfall] Avoid baclofen if hepatic encephalopathy is significant
> Baclofen **may impair mentation**, and that effect may be exacerbated in more advanced liver disease. Patients with [[hepatic-encephalopathy|hepatic encephalopathy (HE)]] were **excluded** from the cirrhosis RCT, so its benefit in that group is untested ([[aasld-2020-ald]]).

### Reflux and Belching
> [!pearl] Objective GERD first, baclofen second
> **ACG 2021 Recommendation 13 (strong, moderate):** *"We do not recommend baclofen in the absence of objective evidence of GERD."* The gate is objective documentation of continued symptomatic reflux despite optimal PPI therapy — not symptoms alone ([[acg-2021-gerd]]).

- Short-term RCTs show symptomatic improvement. An RCT of medical therapy (including baclofen) vs antireflux surgery for PPI-refractory heartburn found **no significant benefit vs placebo at 1 year**, though it was not powered to detect a small but potentially important effect ([[acg-2021-gerd]]).
- **AGA 2022 BPA 10** — personalize adjunctive pharmacotherapy to the GERD phenotype rather than using it empirically: baclofen is the agent for **regurgitation- or belch-predominant symptoms**. Effective for belch-predominant symptoms and mild regurgitation, but **often limited by central nervous system (CNS) and gastrointestinal side effects** ([[aga-2022-personalized-gerd]]).
- **Belching** ([[aga-2023-bloating]]): **not advocated for supragastric belching alone** — that is a behavioural disorder treated with brain–gut behavioural therapy and speech therapy. It may be considered to prevent LES relaxation in **gastric belching due to GERD**.
- **[[extraesophageal-reflux|Extraesophageal reflux]]:** role uncertain ([[aga-2018-extraesophageal-gerd]]). Two studies in chronic cough — a 3-patient case series with improvement or resolution in all 3, and an 8-week add-on to omeprazole that improved cough in **56%** of patients who showed a reduction in acid reflux episodes. **No controlled studies show benefit in extraesophageal reflux.**

### Rumination Syndrome
- **AGA 2018 BPA 7 (ungraded):** a reasonable **next step in refractory patients** — i.e. after diaphragmatic breathing ± biofeedback, which is first-line in all cases ([[aga-2018-rumination-syndrome]]).
- Evidence: randomized cross-over trial in **22 patients (16 with rumination)** — modest reduction in postprandial flow events, with **symptom improvement in 63% on baclofen vs 26% on placebo**. An open-label 1-week study reduced postprandial rumination events after a 1,000-kcal meal in 12 adults.

### Muscle Cramps in Cirrhosis
- Muscle cramps are common in cirrhosis, particularly on diuretics, and hurt quality of life. **Correct electrolyte abnormalities first** (hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia) ([[aasld-2021-ascites-sbp-hrs]]).
- **AASLD 2021 guidance statement:** human albumin solution (20–40 g/week) **or** baclofen can be considered for **severe** muscle cramps. Orphenadrine, methocarbamol and quinidine have also been proposed.

## Dosing

| Indication | Dose | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **AUD in compensated ALD** | Start **5 mg three times a day**; escalate at **3–5-day intervals** by tolerance; **maximum 15 mg three times a day** | [[acg-2024-ald\|ACG 2024]] |
| AUD in ALD — dose range as tabulated | **30–60 mg/day** | [[aasld-2020-ald\|AASLD 2020]] |
| Alcohol-associated cirrhosis RCT regimen | 10 mg three times daily × 12 weeks | [[aasld-2020-ald\|AASLD 2020]] |
| **Objectively confirmed GERD**, symptomatic on optimal PPI | **5–20 mg three times a day** (trial) | [[acg-2021-gerd\|ACG 2021]] |
| Chronic cough attributed to reflux (role uncertain) | 20 mg three times daily × 8 weeks, added to omeprazole | [[aga-2018-extraesophageal-gerd\|AGA 2018]] |
| **Refractory rumination syndrome** | **10 mg three times daily** | [[aga-2018-rumination-syndrome\|AGA 2018]] |
| **Severe muscle cramps in cirrhosis** | **10 mg/day**, increasing by **10 mg/day each week**, **up to 30 mg/day** | [[aasld-2021-ascites-sbp-hrs\|AASLD 2021]] |

- **Two liver guidelines, two ceilings.** ACG 2024 caps AUD dosing at 15 mg three times a day (45 mg/day); AASLD 2020 tabulates a range of 30–60 mg/day. Follow the newer ACG titration.
- **Titration is specified only for AUD (ACG 2024) and for muscle cramps (AASLD 2021).** The GERD, cough and rumination doses are given as fixed ranges with no escalation schedule.
- **Excretion is renal, but none of these guidelines gives a renal dose adjustment.**

## Safety and Cautions
- **GERD/reflux use is limited by side effects: dizziness, somnolence, constipation** ([[acg-2021-gerd]]); AGA 2022 adds that CNS and GI side effects often limit it ([[aga-2022-personalized-gerd]]).
- **May impair mentation** — the basis for avoiding it when HE is significant (see pitfall above) ([[aasld-2020-ald]]).
- Off-label in every indication on this page: it is not FDA-approved for AUD, GERD, belching, rumination or cirrhotic muscle cramps.
