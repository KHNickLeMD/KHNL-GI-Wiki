## Class / Mechanism
- **Partial agonist of the 5-hydroxytryptamine type 4 (5-HT4) receptor** — stimulates gastrointestinal (GI) motility and increases fluid in the GI tract ([[aga-2022-ibs-c|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2022]]).

## Indication & Population Restriction

The two societies gate tegaserod differently — and **the American College of Gastroenterology's (ACG's) gate is the stricter one**, so it is the operative criterion:

| Source | Eligible population | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| [[acg-2020-ibs\|ACG 2020]] (Rec 18) | **Women <65 y with ≤1 cardiovascular risk factor** who have **not adequately responded to secretagogues** ([[linaclotide]], [[plecanatide]], [[lubiprostone]]) | **Conditional, low quality of evidence** |
| [[aga-2022-ibs-c\|AGA 2022]] | Women <65 y **without a history of cardiovascular (CV) ischemic events** (myocardial infarction [MI], stroke, transient ischemic attack [TIA], or angina) — the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) limited-reapproval wording; **no risk-factor count** | Conditional, moderate certainty |

- **Two separate gates, both must clear under ACG:** no prior CV ischemic event **and** ≤1 CV risk factor. AGA states only the first; ACG adds the second and makes ≥2 risk factors an outright contraindication (see below).
- **Cardiovascular risk factors that define the count.** ACG's own list — *"risk factors to consider when evaluating a woman with IBS-C for possible tegaserod use"* ([[acg-2020-ibs]] Supplemental Table 2) — is what the ≤1 gate is counted against:

| ACG 2020 Supplemental Table 2 | AGA 2022 |
|---|---|
| History of cardiovascular disease | — (AGA handles prior ischemic events as a separate exclusion) |
| History or presence of hypertension | Hypertension |
| Active smoking | Tobacco use |
| History or presence of diabetes mellitus | Diabetes |
| History or presence of hyperlipidemia | Hypercholesterolemia |
| **Age >55 y at baseline** | **Age ≥55 y** |
| **[[obesity\|Obesity]] (BMI >30 kg/m² at baseline)** | Obesity |

  ⚠ **The two lists differ in two ways that change the count.** ACG counts **history of cardiovascular disease as one of the risk factors**, where AGA keeps prior CV ischemic events as a separate, absolute exclusion. And ACG's age criterion is **>55 y** where AGA's is **≥55 y** — a woman aged exactly 55 scores a risk factor under AGA but not under ACG. ACG also specifies **active** smoking and measures obesity **at baseline**.
- **Sequencing matters** — tegaserod is a post-secretagogue option under ACG, not a first-line irritable bowel syndrome with constipation (IBS-C) drug.
- Regulatory history: FDA-approved 2002 (short-term IBS-C in women) and 2004 ([[chronic-idiopathic-constipation|chronic idiopathic constipation (CIC)]] in men and women <65); **withdrawn 2007** after a retrospective analysis showed a small excess of CV ischemic events; **re-approved 2019** under the limited indication above.

## Contraindications
- **More than 1 cardiovascular risk factor — contraindicated** ([[acg-2020-ibs|ACG 2020]], stated explicitly: "It is contraindicated in patients with more than 1 CV risk factors").
- **History of CV ischemic disease** (MI, stroke, TIA, angina) — outside the FDA reapproval in both guidelines.
- **Age ≥65 y** and **men** — outside the approved population.
- *Empirical basis for the ≤1 cutoff:* in the first adjudication of the trial database, **all 14 patients with confirmed CV ischemic events had ≥1 CV risk factor, and 11 of them had ≥2** ([[acg-2020-ibs]]).

## Dosing
- **6 mg twice daily** — the currently approved dose ([[acg-2020-ibs|ACG 2020]]; [[aga-2022-ibs-c|AGA 2022]]).
- **2 mg twice daily was also studied and is not the recommended dose:** at 12 weeks only the 6 mg arm showed improvement in individual symptoms of pain/discomfort; 9 of the 11 trials in the Ford meta-analysis used 6 mg twice daily ([[acg-2020-ibs]]).
- **Duration:** the pivotal and pooled randomized controlled trials (RCTs) ran **12 weeks**; neither guideline sets a stopping rule.

## Efficacy
- Evidence: **4 phase-3, 12-week RCTs** (Rome I IBS-C). AGA analysis limited to **women on 6 mg twice daily (n=1450) vs placebo (n=1433)** ([[aga-2022-ibs-c|AGA 2022]]).

| Outcome | Tegaserod vs Placebo | Relative Risk (RR; 95% confidence interval [CI]) |
|---|---|---|
| FDA responder endpoint for IBS-C | 35.1% vs 23.4% | RR 0.87 (0.81–0.93) |
| Global relief (Subject's Global Assessment) | 52.7% vs 44.8% | RR 0.85 (0.74–0.97) |
| Abdominal pain/discomfort improvement | 22.4% vs 17.6% | RR 0.92 (0.87–0.97) |
| Bowel-movement frequency improvement | 65.6% vs 51.2% | RR 0.71 (0.65–0.77) |

- **No improvement in overall IBS quality of life (IBS-QOL)** (mean difference 1.21 points, 95% CI −0.76 to 3.18).
- Overall certainty in evidence: **moderate** (rated down for imprecision on outcomes other than bowel movement (BM) frequency).

## Safety / Cardiovascular Signal
- Most common reasons for discontinuation: **diarrhea (1.6%)** and **headache (1.0%)**.
- 2007 retrospective clinical-trial analysis: ischemic CV events **13/11,614 (0.11%)** with tegaserod vs **1/7031 (0.01%)** placebo. Events: MI (n=3), stroke (n=3), CV death (n=1), unstable angina (n=6), TIA (n=1).
- Large epidemiological studies **failed to find a difference** in CV events vs matched non-users; risk occurred **predominantly in patients with prior CV ischemic events or CV risk factors** → basis for restricting to low-CV-risk women <65.
- In women <65 without CV disease: discontinuation 6.2% (tegaserod) vs 4.5% (placebo) (RR 1.37, 95% CI 0.99–1.88).
