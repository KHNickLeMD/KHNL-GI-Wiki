## Class / Mechanism
- **Partial agonist of the 5-hydroxytryptamine type 4 (5-HT4) receptor** — stimulates gastrointestinal (GI) motility and increases fluid in the GI tract ([[aga-2022-ibs-c|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2022]]).

## Indication & Population Restriction
- For [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|irritable bowel syndrome with constipation (IBS-C)]]. The American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) places it **after secretagogues**, not first line.

| Source | Eligible population | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| [[acg-2020-ibs\|ACG 2020]] (Rec 18) | **Women <65 y with ≤1 cardiovascular (CV) risk factor** who have **not adequately responded to secretagogues** ([[linaclotide]], [[plecanatide]], [[lubiprostone]]) | **Conditional, low quality of evidence** (in-text box; ACG Table 2 prints "Strong/conditional") |
| [[aga-2022-ibs-c\|AGA 2022]] (Rec 4) | Patients with IBS-C; implementation remark: reapproved for **women <65 y without a history of CV ischemic events** (myocardial infarction [MI], stroke, transient ischemic attack [TIA], or angina) — **no risk-factor count** | Conditional, moderate certainty |

- **Meeting both guidelines:** woman <65 y · no prior CV ischemic event (AGA) · ≤1 CV risk factor (ACG) · inadequate response to a secretagogue (ACG).
- US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) history: approved 2002 (short-term IBS-C in women) and 2004 ([[chronic-idiopathic-constipation|chronic idiopathic constipation (CIC)]] in men and women <65 y); withdrawal requested 2007 after a retrospective analysis showed a small excess of CV ischemic events ([[aga-2022-ibs-c|AGA 2022]]); approved again for IBS-C in 2019 ([[acg-2020-ibs|ACG 2020]]).

### Cardiovascular Risk Factors
- ACG counts its ≤1 limit against Supplemental Table 2 — *"risk factors to consider when evaluating a woman with IBS-C for possible tegaserod use"* ([[acg-2020-ibs]]). AGA lists known CV risk factors as examples in its safety discussion, not as a count ([[aga-2022-ibs-c]]).

| ACG 2020 Supplemental Table 2 | AGA 2022 (examples) |
|---|---|
| History of cardiovascular disease | — (prior CV ischemic events are outside the reapproved population) |
| History or presence of hypertension | Hypertension |
| Active smoking | Tobacco use |
| History or presence of diabetes mellitus | Diabetes |
| History or presence of hyperlipidemia | Hypercholesterolemia |
| **Age >55 y at baseline** | **Age ≥55 y** |
| **[[obesity\|Obesity]] (body mass index [BMI] >30 kg/m² at baseline)** | Obesity |

> [!pitfall] The two lists are not interchangeable
> ACG counts history of cardiovascular disease as one risk factor; AGA instead places prior CV ischemic events outside the reapproved population. ACG's age factor is >55 y, AGA's ≥55 y ([[acg-2020-ibs]]; [[aga-2022-ibs-c]]).

## Contraindications
- **More than 1 CV risk factor — contraindicated** ([[acg-2020-ibs|ACG 2020]]: "It is contraindicated in patients with more than 1 CV risk factors").
- **History of CV ischemic events** (MI, stroke, TIA, angina) — outside the FDA reapproval ([[aga-2022-ibs-c|AGA 2022]]).
- **Men and women ≥65 y** — outside the approved population (adult women <65 y) ([[acg-2020-ibs|ACG 2020]]; [[aga-2022-ibs-c|AGA 2022]]).
- *Basis for the ≤1 cutoff:* in the first adjudication of the trial database, **all 14 patients with confirmed CV ischemic events had ≥1 CV risk factor, and 11 had ≥2** ([[acg-2020-ibs]]).

## Dosing
- **6 mg twice daily** — the currently approved dose ([[acg-2020-ibs|ACG 2020]]; [[aga-2022-ibs-c|AGA 2022]]).
- 2 mg twice daily was also studied: at 12 weeks only the 6 mg arm (not 2 mg) reported improvement in individual symptoms of pain/discomfort; 9 of the 11 randomized controlled trials (RCTs) in the Ford meta-analysis used 6 mg twice daily ([[acg-2020-ibs]]).
- **Duration:** trials ran **12 weeks**.
- **Retreatment:** recurrence of IBS-C symptoms after initial response to **4 weeks** of tegaserod → may benefit from a **second course** ([[aga-2022-ibs-c]]).

## Efficacy
- AGA evidence: **4 phase 3, 12-week RCTs** (Rome I IBS-C); analysis limited to **women on 6 mg twice daily (n=1450) vs placebo (n=1433)** ([[aga-2022-ibs-c|AGA 2022]]).
- Trials predate the 2012 FDA guidance → **modified FDA responder endpoint:** ≥30% improvement in abdominal pain/discomfort **and** increase of ≥1 bowel movement (BM)/week from baseline, for ≥6 of 12 weeks.

| Outcome | Tegaserod vs Placebo | Relative Risk (RR; 95% confidence interval [CI]) |
|---|---|---|
| Modified FDA responder endpoint | 35.1% vs 23.4% | RR 0.87 (0.81–0.93) |
| Global relief (Subject's Global Assessment) | 52.7% vs 44.8% | RR 0.85 (0.74–0.97) |
| Abdominal pain/discomfort improvement | 22.4% vs 17.6% | RR 0.92 (0.87–0.97) |
| BM frequency improvement | 65.6% vs 51.2% | RR 0.71 (0.65–0.77) |

- RR = risk of not responding (AGA's evidence table: "failure of symptom relief"); <1 favors tegaserod.
- **No improvement in overall irritable bowel syndrome quality of life (IBS-QOL)** (mean difference +1.21 points, 95% CI −0.76 to 3.18).
- Overall certainty: **moderate** (rated down for imprecision on every outcome except BM frequency).
- ACG: Ford meta-analysis of 11 RCTs (9,242 patients) — less likely to have persistent IBS-C symptoms than placebo (RR 0.85; 95% CI 0.80–0.90) ([[acg-2020-ibs]]).

## Safety / Cardiovascular Signal
- Most common adverse event (AE): **diarrhea, 6% vs 2%** with placebo ([[acg-2020-ibs]]).
- Most common reasons for discontinuation: **diarrhea (1.6%)** and **headache (1.0%)** ([[aga-2022-ibs-c]]).
- Women <65 y without CV disease: discontinuation 6.2% vs 4.5% (RR 1.37, 95% CI 0.99–1.88) ([[aga-2022-ibs-c]]).

| CV analysis of the trial database | Tegaserod | Placebo |
|---|---|---|
| First adjudication (ACG; same counts as AGA's 2007 retrospective analysis): CV ischemic events | 13/11,614 (0.11%) | 1/7,031 (0.01%) |
| Second adjudication: confirmed CV ischemic events | 7 (0.06%) | 0 (P = 0.3) |
| Second adjudication: major adverse CV events (MACE) | 4 (0.03%) | 0 (P = 0.3) |
| Women <65 y, no history of CV ischemic disease, ≤1 CV risk factor | 1 (0.01%) CV ischemic event; no MACE | 0 |

- 2007 analysis events (AGA): MI (n=3), stroke (n=3), CV death (n=1), unstable angina (n=6), TIA (n=1).
- No evidence of increased proarrhythmic risk or platelet aggregation in these analyses ([[acg-2020-ibs]]).
- Large epidemiological studies **failed to find a difference** in CV events vs matched non-users; risk occurred **predominantly in patients with prior CV ischemic events or known CV risk factors** ([[aga-2022-ibs-c]]).
