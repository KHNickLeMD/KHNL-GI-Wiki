Antifolate **immunomodulator** used in [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]]. **The indication is disease-specific and asymmetric:** suggested for **maintenance in [[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease (CD)]]** after corticosteroid induction, and recommended **against** in [[ulcerative-colitis|ulcerative colitis (UC)]] — against monotherapy for induction, and against maintenance. Third role: **adjunctive**, to suppress immunogenicity on [[anti-tnf-agents|anti-tumor necrosis factor (TNF)]] therapy. **Contraindicated in pregnancy.**

## Positioning — by disease and by induction vs maintenance

| Setting | Recommendation | Strength / quality of evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Induction, moderately–severely active [[crohns-disease\|CD]] | Not a primary induction agent — no numbered American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2025 recommendation either way. Unrated Key Concept 43: methotrexate **may be used in treatment of active CD** ([[acg-2025-crohns]]) | Key Concepts carry no Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) label |
| **Maintenance in CD** after corticosteroid induction | **Suggest** methotrexate, up to **25 mg once weekly intramuscularly (IM) or subcutaneously (SC)** | **Conditional, moderate** ([[acg-2025-crohns]] Rec 11) |
| Induction, moderately–severely active [[ulcerative-colitis\|UC]] | **Against** monotherapy with [[thiopurines]] **or methotrexate** | **Strong, low** ([[acg-2025-uc]] Rec 23) |
| **Maintenance in UC** | **Suggest against** methotrexate | **Conditional, low** ([[acg-2025-uc]] Rec 37) |
| Adjunctive in CD — reducing anti-TNF immunogenicity | **May be used** ([[acg-2025-crohns]] Key Concept 43) | Unrated |

- Methotrexate is a **corticosteroid-sparing maintenance agent** in CD ([[acg-2025-crohns]]) — the decision point is after steroids have induced remission, not at the time of a flare. Compare thiopurines (same position, Rec 9) and [[corticosteroids-ibd]] (which the maintenance plan is replacing).
- **Combination with an anti-TNF agent in UC:** the only combination data are for infliximab + thiopurines ([[acg-2025-uc]] Key Concept 27), and data on methotrexate with an anti-TNF agent in UC are limited. Extrapolating from CD, methotrexate may offer the same reduction in immunogenicity and improvement in drug concentrations, and **may be the preferred immunomodulator for combination therapy where thiopurine adverse effects are most feared — young men, or patients with multiple skin cancers** ([[acg-2025-uc]]).
- **Not an add-on to non–anti-TNF advanced therapy.** Adding a concomitant immunomodulator such as methotrexate to [[vedolizumab]] **does not yield significant benefit** in clinical, endoscopic, or pharmacokinetic outcomes ([[acg-2025-crohns]]). In UC, adding or continuing methotrexate alongside vedolizumab or the [[il-23-and-il-12-23-inhibitors|interleukin (IL)-12/23 and IL-23 inhibitors]] has not been formally assessed; post hoc subset analyses show none of the benefit seen with infliximab plus azathioprine ([[acg-2025-uc]]).

### Recommendation statements, as written

> **11.** We suggest methotrexate (up to 25 mg once weekly intramuscular or subcutaneous) for maintenance of remission in patients with moderately to severely active CD who had induction of remission with corticosteroids (conditional recommendation, moderate level of evidence). — [[acg-2025-crohns]]

> **23.** In patients with moderately to severely active UC, we recommend against monotherapy with thiopurines or methotrexate for induction of remission (Strong recommendation, low quality of evidence). — [[acg-2025-uc]]

> **37.** In patients with prior moderately to severely active UC now in remission, we suggest against using methotrexate for maintenance of remission (Conditional recommendation, low quality of evidence). — [[acg-2025-uc]]

### Why methotrexate is not used in UC

- **Induction:** prior studies of **oral** methotrexate showed no benefit; **2 meta-analyses of methotrexate 25 mg intramuscularly are negative**. In the European multicentre induction study, more patients on **parenteral methotrexate 25 mg/wk** reached steroid-free remission at **week 16**, but the difference was **not statistically significant** ([[acg-2025-uc]]).
- **Maintenance:** in a prospective randomized controlled trial (RCT) methotrexate was **not superior to placebo**. The US **MERIT-UC** trial (parenteral methotrexate **25 mg/wk** after steroid induction) found no superiority — relapse in **29/44 (66%)** on methotrexate vs **25/40 (63%)** on placebo ([[acg-2025-uc]]).

## Dosing, Route, and Duration

| Indication | Dose | Route | Interval | Step-down / notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CD maintenance after steroid induction | **25 mg** | **SC or IM** (parenteral) | **Once weekly** | If steroid-free remission is maintained at 25 mg/wk for **4 months**, the dose **may be lowered to 15 mg/week** |
| CD maintenance — oral alternative | **15–25 mg** | **Oral (PO)** | Once weekly | Only in patients with **normal small bowel absorption**; may be started on oral or switched from parenteral. **Controlled data for oral methotrexate as primary CD treatment are lacking** |
| Adjunctive, to reduce anti-TNF immunogenicity | **12.5–15 mg** | **PO** | Once weekly | Immunogenicity suppression, **not** a disease-controlling dose; ⚠ off-label. Paired with [[anti-tnf-agents\|anti-TNF]] therapy |

> [!pitfall] Oral absorption is unreliable in small bowel disease
> In **extensive small bowel disease or risk factors for malabsorption**, the bioavailability of oral methotrexate **at higher dosages may be variable** — **parenteral methotrexate may be the preferred route** ([[acg-2025-crohns]]).

- No total treatment duration is specified; methotrexate is a maintenance agent, continued while remission holds.
- The ACG 2025 Crohn's and ulcerative colitis guidelines state no folic acid supplementation dose or schedule alongside methotrexate. Folate is one of the micronutrients worth checking in patients on methotrexate — see [[nutrition-in-ibd]].

## Adverse Effects

Reported adverse effects of methotrexate ([[acg-2025-crohns]]):

- Nausea and vomiting
- **Hepatotoxicity**
- **Pulmonary toxicity**
- **Bone marrow suppression**
- Skin cancer
- **Lymphoma — "likely,"** but an escalated risk of lymphoma **has not been conclusively demonstrated** in patients with CD

## Monitoring

- **White blood cell counts and liver chemistries should be routinely monitored** during methotrexate use ([[acg-2025-crohns]]). The guideline does not state how often.
- **Hepatotoxicity.** [[acg-2021-dili]] classes oral methotrexate as a **long-latency** injury with a **fatty liver and fibrosis** pattern, and notes that guidelines for considering liver biopsy in patients on chronic methotrexate have been published separately — it does not print them. Cumulative-dose, elastography, and biopsy thresholds: [[drug-induced-liver-injury|drug-induced liver injury]].
- **Histologic grading:** [[acg-2021-dili]] names the **Roenigk Classification System** as the recognized system for grading methotrexate-induced fibrosis and fatty change, but does not print the grade criteria.
- **Skin cancer:** annual **nonmelanoma skin cancer (NMSC) screening** is *suggested* while on methotrexate (also thiopurines, [[jak-inhibitors|Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors]], sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P) receptor modulators), **particularly if older than 50** — conditional, very low ([[acg-2025-ibd-preventive-care]] Rec 10). Melanoma screening and the rest of the schedule: [[ibd-preventive-care]].
- **Vaccination:** responses to inactivated vaccines are lowest with an [[anti-tnf-agents|anti-TNF]] agent **combined** with methotrexate or a thiopurine ([[acg-2025-ibd-preventive-care]]); detail on [[ibd-preventive-care]].

## Pregnancy and Contraception

Methotrexate is marked contraindicated across every phase — preconception, all three trimesters, and lactation ([[global-consensus-2025-pregnancy-ibd]] Table 9).

| Phase | Methotrexate | Operative detail |
|---|---|---|
| Preconception | **Stop** | **Cease 1–3 months before conception** — long enough to clear methotrexate conjugates and to let the mother transition to a pregnancy-compatible therapy. Statement 15: *recommend discontinuing maintenance methotrexate prior to conception* (**strong**, very low). The preconception checklist gives **at least 1 month** |
| First / second / third trimester | **Avoid** | Known teratogen when given in the first trimester |
| Lactation | **Avoid** | Active metabolite 7-hydroxymethotrexate is detectable in breastmilk; relative infant dose 0.11% |

- **Breastfeeding exception:** if **low-dose methotrexate ≤25 mg/wk** is used, **no alternative drug compatible with breastfeeding is available**, and the mother strongly desires breastfeeding, it **may be considered with close monitoring of the infant**. Infant exposure falls **40% by 24 hours** after the maternal dose, so breastfeeding is not initiated within the first 24 hours of intake ([[global-consensus-2025-pregnancy-ibd]]).
- **In utero exposure does not necessitate termination** — hold a detailed discussion with the mother about options. Continue or start **folic acid**: folate consumption in the presence of dihydrofolate reductase inhibitors, methotrexate included, reduced the relative risk of cardiac defects ([[global-consensus-2025-pregnancy-ibd]]).
- **The guidelines give different washouts.** [[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]] (Best Practice Advice 7) states methotrexate **must be stopped at least 6 months before conception**, citing its long half-life; the newer [[global-consensus-2025-pregnancy-ibd]] gives **1–3 months**.
- Full reproductive management: [[ibd-in-pregnancy]].

> [!pitfall] Confirm contraception before the first dose
> Methotrexate is a known teratogen. When prescribed to women with child-bearing capability it **should be administered only if highly effective contraception is in place** ([[acg-2025-crohns]]).
