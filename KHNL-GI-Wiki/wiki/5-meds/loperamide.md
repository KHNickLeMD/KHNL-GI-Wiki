## Class / Mechanism

- Synthetic **peripheral μ (mu)-opioid receptor agonist**; antimotility agent. Minimal penetration of the blood–brain barrier ([[aga-2017-opioids-in-gastroenterology]]).
- Two actions ([[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2016]]): segmental contraction of the gut slows intraluminal movement of fluid and allows greater absorption; inhibition of calmodulin reduces mucosal secretion.
- **First-line antimotility drug** for symptomatic control of [[acute-diarrhea|acute diarrhea]] — preferred over diphenoxylate, which has more central opiate effect and is combined with atropine (no antidiarrheal effect, objectionable side effects) ([[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]). Also preferred over the opiates (diphenoxylate-atropine, codeine, tincture of opium) in [[short-bowel-syndrome|short bowel syndrome (SBS)]] — not addictive or sedative ([[aga-2022-short-bowel-syndrome]], Best Practice Advice (BPA) 6).
- Delays intestinal transit, decreases stool frequency, increases water and ion absorption, improves stool consistency, and may improve **anal sphincter tone** ([[aga-2017-opioids-in-gastroenterology]]).

## Indications

| Setting | Recommendation | Strength / evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Traveler's diarrhea — **adjunct to antibiotics** | Administer: shortens diarrhea, increases chance of cure | Strong / moderate (Rec 8) | [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea\|ACG 2016]] |
| Acute watery diarrhea, immunocompetent **adults** | May be given | Weak / moderate (Rec 47) | [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea\|Infectious Diseases Society of America (IDSA) 2017]] |
| [[fecal-incontinence\|Fecal incontinence (FI)]] — **only when FI is accompanied by diarrhea** (⚠ **off-label**: approved for diarrhea, not for FI) | Antidiarrheals recommended — loperamide, diphenoxylate-atropine, bile-salt binders, anticholinergics, clonidine. The diarrhea qualifier *is* the indication; FI without diarrhea is not this recommendation | Strong / low (Rec 1) | [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders\|ACG 2021]] |
| [[irritable-bowel-syndrome\|Diarrhea-predominant irritable bowel syndrome (IBS-D)]] | Suggested for diarrhea control — improves abdominal pain and stool consistency, but **no benefit on global symptoms or urgency** | Conditional / **very low** (Rec 4) | [[aga-2022-ibs-d\|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2022]] |
| [[functional-diarrhea\|Functional diarrhea]] | Step 2, after medication review and before an empiric bile-acid sequestrant | Narrative review; the chapter grades no recommendations | [[rome-v-2026-bowel-disorders\|Rome V 2026]] |
| [[short-bowel-syndrome\|SBS]] / high-output stoma | Antidiarrheal of choice; guide use by **objective measurement of stool output**. Loperamide + codeine may be synergistic | Expert review (BPA 6) | [[aga-2022-short-bowel-syndrome\|AGA 2022]] |
| [[clostridioides-difficile\|*C. difficile* infection (CDI)]] in [[inflammatory-bowel-disease\|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]] | May consider **only** once inflammation and infection are improving but diarrhea persists | Expert review (BPA 9) | [[aga-2026-cdiff-ibd\|AGA 2026 (IBD)]] |
| CDI, general adults | **Not suggested.** If used at all: only **after anti-CDI therapy has started**, and for the **shortest duration** needed for symptom control | Expert review — narrative, not a numbered Best Practice Advice | [[aga-2026-cdiff-adults\|AGA 2026 (adults)]] |

*On IBS pain, the two AGA documents differ:* the 2017 update states loperamide does not significantly affect pain perception in IBS, though pain accompanying an attack of diarrhea may be reduced; the 2022 guideline — newer, and the finding stated in the table — found improvement in abdominal pain. AGA 2022 also leaves open **whether loperamide should be used in IBS-mixed type** during diarrheal episodes.

## Dosing

There is no single ceiling — the cap and the duration belong to the setting. Where loperamide fits within the acute-diarrhea pathway (by severity, fever, and travel association) is on [[acute-diarrhea]].

| Setting | Regimen | Ceiling / duration | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acute diarrhea, adult self-treatment | 4 mg first dose, then 2 mg after each loose stool | **≤8 mg/day**, and **not for more than 48 h** | [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea\|ACG 2016]] |
| Traveler's diarrhea, **combined with an antibiotic** | 4 mg first dose, then 2 mg after each loose stool | **≤16 mg per 24 h** (Table 4 footnote) | [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea\|ACG 2016]] |
| IBS-D | Optimal dose and method (as needed, daily, or after a set number of stools) **not known** — titrate to the symptom pattern | — | [[aga-2022-ibs-d\|AGA 2022]] |
| IBS-D — doses in typical use | 2 mg after each loose bowel movement; **2–4 mg preprandially** when diarrhea is prominent after feeding | usually <8 mg/day | [[aga-2017-opioids-in-gastroenterology\|AGA 2017]] |
| Functional diarrhea | 2–4 mg **up to 4 times a day**; may be used routinely **without laboratory monitoring** | — | [[rome-v-2026-bowel-disorders\|Rome V 2026]] |
| FI with diarrhea | 2 mg tablets — start **1 tablet 30 min before breakfast**, titrate as necessary | **up to 16 mg/day** | [[aga-2017-surgical-device-fecal-incontinence\|AGA 2017]] |
| SBS, particularly **without an ileum** | About **30 min before meals and at bedtime**; loperamide enters the enterohepatic circulation, which is disrupted here, so high doses are frequently needed | **up to 16 tablets (32 mg)/day** | [[aga-2022-short-bowel-syndrome\|AGA 2022]] |

## Adverse Effects

- **Post-treatment constipation** is the common complaint — **use the lowest dose that controls the diarrhea** ([[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]).
- Toxic dilatation of the colon or prolonged illness when used in **bacterial inflammatory** diarrhea — rare, and seen with *otherwise untreated* diarrhea from highly inflammatory pathogens; potentiation is very unlikely when the inflammatory colitis is itself being treated with an antimicrobial ([[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]).
- In SBS where **bowel dilatation has already occurred**, antimotility agents may *worsen* diarrhea by encouraging bacterial overgrowth ([[aga-2022-short-bowel-syndrome]]).

## Contraindications / Cautions

- **Children <18 y with acute diarrhea — do NOT give** (Strong/moderate, [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]). Deaths have been reported in 0.54% of children given loperamide, all in children <3 y.
- **Avoid at any age** in suspected or proven cases where **[[toxic-megacolon|toxic megacolon]] may result** — inflammatory diarrhea, or diarrhea with fever (Strong/low, [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]).
- **Antimotility agents are contraindicated in three named settings** ([[aga-2026-cdiff-adults]]): **untreated CDI**, **fulminant CDI**, and **severe colonic IBD** — the concern is toxin retention and toxic megacolon. "Untreated" is the operative qualifier: once anti-CDI antibiotics are running, brief use is permissible though still not suggested. Avoid while the workup is still under way ([[aga-2026-cdiff-ibd]]).

> [!pitfall] Bloody diarrhea — do not reach for loperamide
> Clinical conditions have worsened after antimotility agents in [[shigellosis]] and [[shiga-toxin-producing-e-coli|Shiga toxin-producing *E. coli* (STEC)]] infection; antidiarrheal and antimicrobial agents given for STEC diarrhea may increase the risk of hemolytic uremic syndrome ([[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]).
