- **Mechanism:** exogenous heme rapidly down-regulates hepatic ALAS1 expression, reducing overproduction of ALA and PBG.
- **Acute attack dose:** IV hemin **3–4 mg/kg body weight, once daily, typically for 4 days**, for attacks of [[acute-hepatic-porphyria]] severe enough to require hospital admission (AGA 2023 Best Practice Advice 4 — Expert Review, **no formal evidence rating**). Collect a random urine ALA/PBG/creatinine **before** starting hemin. Give into a **high-flow central vein** (PICC or central port) due to thrombophlebitis; heme bound to human serum albumin is preferred. Symptom relief typically requires 48–72 h.
- **Prophylaxis:** **off-label** prophylactic IV hemin for recurrent attacks (**≥4 per year**) — [[givosiran]] is the alternative (BPA 7). Effectiveness at *preventing* recurrence is less established than at stopping an attack, and chronic use brings indwelling-catheter needs, infections, and [[iron-overload-and-iron-metabolism|iron overload]].
- **Adverse effects / monitoring:** chronic hemin (**9% iron by weight**) can cause secondary iron overload with liver fibrosis.
  - Monitor **liver enzymes at least annually** in any AHP patient on active treatment.
  - On **≥monthly** prophylactic hemin, monitor **ferritin and iron every 3–6 months**.
  - **Ferritin >1000 ng/mL** → consider **therapeutic phlebotomy**.
  - Thrombophlebitis risk → give into a high-flow central vein.
