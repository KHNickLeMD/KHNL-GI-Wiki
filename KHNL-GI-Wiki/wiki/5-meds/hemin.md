- **Class / mechanism:** exogenous heme — rapidly down-regulates hepatic ALAS1 expression, cutting the continued overproduction and accumulation of δ-aminolevulinic acid (ALA) and porphobilinogen (PBG).
- **Status:** intravenous (IV) hemin is the **currently approved treatment for acute attacks** of [[acute-hepatic-porphyria|acute hepatic porphyria (AHP)]]. Prophylactic IV hemin to prevent recurrent attacks is **off-label**.

## Indications

| | |
|---|---|
| **Acute attack** | Attacks of AHP **severe enough to require hospital admission** — American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2023 Best Practice Advice (BPA) 4 |
| **Prophylaxis (off-label)** | **Recurrent attacks — 4 or more per year** — given in the **outpatient** setting (BPA 7); [[givosiran]] is the alternative at the same indication |

AGA 2023 is an Expert Review: its Best Practice Advice statements carry **no formal rating** of evidence quality or strength ([[aga-2023-acute-hepatic-porphyria]]).

## Dosing and Administration

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dose (acute attack)** | **3–4 mg/kg body weight IV, once daily** |
| **Duration** | **Typically 4 days** (the update's own conclusion restates the regimen as 3–5 days) |
| **Route** | **High-flow central vein** — peripherally inserted central catheter (PICC) or central port — because of potential thrombophlebitis |
| **Preparation** | **Heme bound to human serum albumin is preferred** — heme stabilization, less irritation to veins |
| **Expected response** | Symptom relief depends on elimination of excess ALA and PBG and typically takes **48–72 h**; recovery from neurologic symptoms varies significantly |
| **Prophylactic dose and interval** | Not given by AGA 2023; its monitoring advice addresses patients receiving prophylactic hemin **at least monthly** |

> [!pitfall] Draw the urine before the first dose
> Hemin normalizes ALA and PBG, so a sample drawn after treatment starts can no longer establish the diagnosis. Collect a **random urine ALA, PBG and creatinine before** starting hemin ([[aga-2023-acute-hepatic-porphyria]]).

> [!pearl] Treat early, empirically if need be
> Timely initiation normalizes ALA and PBG, improves symptoms, and **decreases the risk of long-term neurologic complications**. Rapid ALA/PBG assays are generally unavailable, so in a patient with **confirmed** AHP hemin may be started on empirical grounds ([[aga-2023-acute-hepatic-porphyria]]).

## Adverse Effects and Cautions

- **Secondary iron overload with liver fibrosis** — hemin is **9% iron by weight**; the risk is from chronic administration ([[iron-overload-and-iron-metabolism|iron overload]]).
- **Thrombophlebitis** — dictates the central route and the albumin-bound preparation above.
- **Chronic prophylactic use** also brings the need for **indwelling central venous catheters** and **infections**. Effectiveness at *preventing* recurrent attacks is less established than effectiveness at stopping an acute attack.
- AGA 2023 gives no contraindications, no renal or hepatic dose adjustment, and no pregnancy guidance for hemin.

## Monitoring

From BPA 9 and Table 2 of [[aga-2023-acute-hepatic-porphyria]].

| What | When |
|---|---|
| Liver enzymes | **At least annually** in any AHP patient actively undergoing treatment |
| Iron and ferritin | **Every 3–6 months** in patients on prophylactic hemin **at least monthly** |
| Ferritin **>1000 ng/mL** | Consider **therapeutic phlebotomy** to reduce iron overload |

- Patients on chronic hemin require close follow-up for adverse effects **and** treatment response.
- Disease-level surveillance runs independently of the drug — annual liver monitoring, [[hcc-surveillance|hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) surveillance]] from age 50 y, and chronic kidney disease (CKD) surveillance: see [[acute-hepatic-porphyria]].
