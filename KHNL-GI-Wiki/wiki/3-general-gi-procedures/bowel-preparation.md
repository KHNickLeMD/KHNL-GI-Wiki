## Scope and Terminology

Everything below applies to **ambulatory patients at low risk for inadequate preparation** unless stated otherwise; higher-risk patients are handled separately. [[usmstf-2025-bowel-prep]]

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Adequate** quality | A standard screening or surveillance interval **can** be assigned from the findings of the [[colonoscopy]] |
| **Inadequate** quality | A standard screening or surveillance interval **cannot** be assigned |
| **Purgative** / **adjunct** | The primary medication used to clear stool / any other medication or supplement given with it |
| **Day prior** regimen | Entire purgative the day before |
| **Split-dose** regimen | Part (typically half) the day before, remainder the day of |
| **Same-day** regimen | Entire purgative on the day of colonoscopy |
| **High / low / ultra-low volume** | **≥4 L** / **≥2 to <4 L** / **<2 L** of purgative |

- The US Multi-Society Task Force on Colorectal Cancer (USMSTF) calls its 2025 statements **"Recommendations," not "Guidelines,"** and **numbers none of them**; strength is strong ("We recommend") or weak ("We suggest"), evidence high / moderate / low / very low.

## Patient Education and Navigation

- **Verbal *and* written instructions covering every component** of the preparation — not just the purgative (strong, high).
- **Some form of patient navigation** — telephonic or virtual, including automated electronic messaging (weak, moderate).
- Write instructions at a **sixth-grade or lower reading level, in the patient's own language** — delivery in the preferred language improves both completion and preparation quality. [[acg-2014-bowel-cleansing]], [[asge-2015-bowel-preparation]]

---

## Diet

- **Limit dietary modification to the day before** colonoscopy (strong, high) — multi-day restriction is reserved for high-risk patients.
- **Low-residue / low-fiber foods or full liquids for the early and midday meals** on the day before, when using a split-dose regimen (strong, high).
- Low-residue is **noninferior to clear liquids** for adequacy and better tolerated.
- A low-residue diet limits cereals, beans, peas, nuts, seeds, raw or dried fruits and vegetables. **There is no standardized definition**; sample meals are listed in [[usmstf-2025-bowel-prep]].
- **Avoid red liquids** — mistaken for blood and they obscure mucosal detail. [[asge-2015-bowel-preparation]]

## Purgative Selection

- **No single purgative is superior** to others for adequacy in low-risk ambulatory patients (strong, high).
- **Select the regimen from the individual's medical history, medications, and — when available — the adequacy reported from prior colonoscopies** (strong, moderate).
- **Against hyperosmotic regimens in anyone at risk for volume overload or electrolyte disturbance** (strong, high).
- Also weigh patient preference, comorbidities, out-of-pocket cost, and ease of obtaining and drinking it (ungraded).

### Commonly Used Regimens

*All carry nausea, bloating/abdominal cramps and anal irritation; all carry bowel obstruction, ileus and allergy/hypersensitivity as contraindications. Only the additional items are listed.* [[usmstf-2025-bowel-prep]]

| Regimen (brand) | US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved | Tonicity | Volume to consume | Standard split-dose approach | Added side effects | Added contraindications |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Polyethylene glycol–electrolyte lavage solution (PEG-ELS) (GoLYTELY, CoLyte) | Yes | Isotonic | **4 L** | 2 L night before + 2 L day of exam | — | — |
| Sulfate-free PEG-ELS (NuLytely, TriLyte) | Yes | Isotonic | **4 L** | 2 L night before + 2 L day of exam | — | — |
| 2 L PEG-ELS + ascorbate (MoviPrep) | Yes | Isotonic | **2 L** | 16 oz clear liquids per 500 cc night before + same day of exam | Hemolysis in glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency | — |
| 1 L PEG + ascorbate (PLENVU) | Yes | Isotonic | **1 L** | 16 oz clear liquids per 500 cc night before + same day of exam | — | — |
| Oral sodium sulfate (SUTAB tablets, SUPREP liquid) | Yes | Hypertonic | **12 oz + 2.5 L H₂O**, or **24 tablets + 2 L H₂O** | 6 oz or 12 tablets night before + same dose day of exam | Vomiting | — |
| Sodium picosulfate + magnesium oxide + anhydrous citric acid (CLENPIQ) | Yes | Hypertonic | **10 oz + 2 L H₂O** | 5 oz + 1 L night before + same day of exam | Vomiting | Chronic or acute kidney disease |
| Sodium phosphate (NaP) tablets (OsmoPrep) | Yes | Hypertonic | **32 tablets + 2 L H₂O** | 16 tablets + 1 L night before + same day of exam | Vomiting | Chronic or acute kidney disease |
| PEG-3350 / sports drink (MiraLAX / Gatorade) | **No** | Hypotonic without sports drink | **238 g PEG-3350 in 2 L** sports drink | 1 L night before + same day of exam | Hypocalcemia, hyponatremia, hypokalemia | Chronic or acute kidney disease, seizures |
| Magnesium citrate (generic) | **No** | Hypertonic | **2 bottles (12 oz each) + 2 L H₂O** | 1 bottle night before + 1 bottle day of exam, each with 1 L H₂O | Hypermagnesemia | Chronic or acute kidney disease |
| Bisacodyl (generic) | **No** | — | **4 tablets (20 mg) + 2–3 L H₂O** | — | [[colon-ischemia\|Ischemic colitis]] | — |

- The authors of [[asge-2015-bowel-preparation]] suggest an **additional 1–2 L of clear fluid** beyond what the prescribing information specifies.

### Volume — High, Low, and Ultra-Low

- **2 L regimens are suggested instead of 4 L** (weak, moderate) — on **tolerability, not cleansing superiority**. Adequate cleanliness (overall and right colon) and adenoma detection are equivalent; adherence, tolerability (72.5% vs 49.6%) and willingness to repeat (89.5% vs 61.9%) all favour 2 L.
- **Ultra-low-volume (<2 L) regimens are not ready for general use** — pooled adequacy only **75%–83%**, with extreme heterogeneity.

### Agent-Specific Safety

- **NaP is not recommended** in any of the following ([[acg-2014-bowel-cleansing]]):

| Contraindication | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Renal insufficiency | **Creatinine clearance <60 mL/min/1.73 m²** |
| Pre-existing electrolyte disturbance | — |
| Congestive heart failure | **New York Heart Association (NYHA) class III or IV**, or **ejection fraction <50%** |
| [[cirrhosis\|Cirrhosis]], or [[ascites\|ascites]] | — |

  - **Caution rather than a bar:** elderly, hypertensive, or taking angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), or diuretics. Also avoid in children **<12 y** and in known or suspected [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]].
  - **NaP-induced acute phosphate nephropathy — risk factors:** female sex, pre-existing renal insufficiency, inadequate hydration during the preparation, **<12 h between the 2 NaP doses**, hypertension, older age, and diuretics / NSAIDs / renin–angiotensin inhibitors.
- **Magnesium-based preparations — avoid in chronic kidney disease** (all formulations); prefer a PEG-ELS–based regimen.
- **PEG-ELS is iso-osmotic** → preferred where fluid shifts are poorly tolerated (renal insufficiency, congestive heart failure, advanced liver disease). It **does not alter colonic mucosal histology**, so it can be used in suspected IBD without obscuring diagnosis or tissue analysis. [[asge-2015-bowel-preparation]]
- **2 L PEG-ELS + ascorbate** — also contraindicated in phenylketonuria; avoid with creatinine clearance **<30 mL/min** or congestive heart failure.
- **Sodium picosulfate + magnesium citrate** — also contraindicated in congestive heart failure and hypermagnesemia; small absolute excess of hospitalization with hyponatremia.
- **Senna** — high dose **24 tablets of 12 mg** (as effective as 4 L PEG-ELS but markedly more cramping); low dose **3–12 tablets** with 2 L PEG-ELS. [[acg-2014-bowel-cleansing]]

> [!pitfall] Prep lesions can be mistaken for new IBD
> NaP and sodium picosulfate produce aphthoid-like mucosal lesions far more often than PEG, and the endoscopic changes can resemble [[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease]]. The histology is distinctive — biopsy before letting a prep-day finding become an IBD diagnosis ([[usmstf-2025-bowel-prep]]).

## Dosing and Timing

| Parameter | Rule | Strength / evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Dosing strategy | **Split-dose for all patients**, regardless of high- or low-volume | Strong / high |
| Split-dose second portion | **Begin 4–6 hr before** the colonoscopy; **complete at least 2 hr before** the procedure start | Strong / moderate |
| Same-day regimen, **afternoon** colonoscopy | **Acceptable alternative** to split dosing | Strong / high |
| Same-day regimen, **morning** colonoscopy | **Inferior alternative** to split dosing | Weak / low |
| Same-day regimen, timing | Begin drinking **4–6 hr before**, complete **≥2 hr before** the procedure start | Ungraded key concept |

- **Why split-dose works:** the first dose clears solid stool; the second clears the chyme that enters the large bowel overnight.
- **The interval from last dose to scope insertion is inversely related to quality.** The split-dose advantage holds within **3 hr** of finishing the purgative, declines after **4–5 hr**, and is **gone after 5 hr**. Split dosing also raises adenoma and advanced-adenoma detection.
- A purgative finished no earlier than **2 hr** before endoscopy does not increase gastric volume, but not every anesthesiologist counts a purgative as a clear liquid, so some units require longer. Fasting intervals and the conditions raising aspiration risk: [[endoscopy-sedation]].
- **[[glp-1-receptor-agonists|Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists]]** delay gastric emptying and so may affect when to stop drinking; cessation policy is on [[endoscopy-sedation]]. Their use is **not** a listed risk factor for inadequate preparation and carries no prep modification of its own.
- **Nasogastric administration** of PEG-ELS — for the patient who cannot drink or has a significant swallowing disorder, and the most effective method in infants and children. Risk of severe aspiration; prokinetics/antiemetics and avoiding over-rapid instillation improve tolerance. [[asge-2015-bowel-preparation]] Rapid preparation for urgent colonoscopy in bleeding: [[acute-lower-gi-bleeding]].

## Adjuncts

- **Oral simethicone is suggested** (weak, moderate) — an upgrade from the 2014 position against routine adjuncts. **If it is used, give at least 320 mg** (ungraded). It improves preparation quality, reduces bubbles and cuts intraprocedural simethicone use, but has not been shown to improve adenoma detection. **The optimal timing relative to the purgative is not established.**
- **Against the routine use of non-simethicone adjuncts** (weak, low) — chewing gum, orange juice, diet cola, menthol candy drops, citrus peel tablets, lubiprostone and mosapride citrate all lack sufficient evidence. Bisacodyl improved quality only in regimens using ≤1 L, and increased discomfort.
- **Against metoclopramide** as an adjunct — it reduced nausea and bloating in one study but did not improve cleansing. [[asge-2015-bowel-preparation]]
- Dilution and channel precautions for **simethicone given through the scope** for intraprocedural bubbles: [[endoscope-reprocessing]].

## Assessing Preparation Quality

- **Grade only after all washing and suctioning are complete**, using reliably understood descriptors that communicate adequacy (strong, moderate). Grading before cleansing measures the purgative, not the examination.
- **When the patient reports incomplete adherence or suggests the preparation may be inadequate (e.g. dark bowel effluent), insert the colonoscope to the sigmoid colon to confirm inadequacy before aborting** (weak, low).
- **Irrigation pumps are suggested** for routine use to assist preparation during colonoscopy (weak, very low).
- **If the colonoscopy is aborted for inadequate preparation, photograph the segment(s) that caused the abortion** (ungraded) — cancellation rates vary widely between endoscopists.

> [!pearl] Never cancel on the patient's word alone
> Of patients who reported brown liquid or solid stool before the procedure, **42%** nonetheless had an adequate preparation once washing and suctioning were done — yet most units permit cancellation on self-report. Put the scope in ([[usmstf-2025-bowel-prep]]).

### Preparation Quality Scales

*Criteria from [[asge-2015-bowel-preparation]] and [[acg-2014-bowel-cleansing]]; [[usmstf-2025-bowel-prep]] names five scales but reproduces the criteria of none.*

**Use the Boston Bowel Preparation Scale (BBPS)** — best validation data of any scale, and the only one that does **not** downgrade for retained fluid. Score **each of the 3 segments** (right, transverse, left) 0–3 **after all cleansing maneuvers**; total **0** (very poor) – **9** (excellent):

- **3** — entire mucosa of the segment well seen after cleaning
- **2** — minor residual material after cleaning, mucosa generally well seen
- **1** — portion of mucosa seen after cleaning, other areas obscured by retained material
- **0** — unprepared segment, stool cannot be cleared
- **Examination aborted for inadequate preparation → score the unseen proximal segments 0.**

- The **Ottawa** (0–14) and **Aronchick** (1–5) scales are **not recommended for clinical practice** — Ottawa scores the preparation before washing and suctioning, Aronchick is a global rating that penalises retained fluid and reproduces poorly in the distal colon. The Harefield and Chicago scales are also in use; none of the documents above reproduces their criteria.

### Adequacy Cut-Points and Wording

- **The operative rule: a BBPS segment score of 2 or 3 in *every* segment = adequate** — validated against the ability to detect **adenomas >5 mm**.
- **Consider a total BBPS <6 caused by any segment scoring <2 to be inadequate**, requiring repeat colonoscopy as soon as feasible **within 12 months**.
- **Adequacy is indication-specific, and the report must say so:**
  - Score **all segments** on a screening or surveillance examination. For a small region that cannot be cleared, the endoscopist may exercise judgement based on the **overall likelihood of missing a clinically meaningful lesion**.
  - On a **non-screening/surveillance** colonoscopy the preparation may be **adequate for that indication** (e.g. [[chronic-diarrhea|diarrhea]], hematochezia) while still **inadequate for screening or surveillance** — the report must state the distinction so the right interval is assigned.
  - **The word "fair" is not an adequacy statement.** Wherever it is used it must be accompanied by an explicit statement of whether standard intervals can be assigned.
- **Descending colon, sigmoid and rectum well visualized** on an otherwise inadequate average-risk screening examination → reasonable to revisit screening options, treating it as a flexible sigmoidoscopy ([[colorectal-cancer-screening]]).
- Neither document sets a numeric threshold for how much residual stool makes a segment inadequate, beyond the ">5 mm lesion" visibility test.

> [!pitfall] The 2014 and 2025 cut-points are not the same rule
> The scale's developers set **total BBPS <5 = inadequate** *a priori*, and [[acg-2014-bowel-cleansing|USMSTF 2014]] operated at **≥5** — so a total of 5 built from segment scores 3/2/0 is adequate by that threshold and **inadequate** by the 2025 segment rule. **Use the segment rule.**

## Salvage of an Inadequate Preparation

**Use same-day salvage maneuvers when feasible** (weak, moderate). No single salvage strategy has enough evidence to be preferred over the others. [[acg-2014-bowel-cleansing]]

**Sequence:** confirm inadequacy on insertion → concerted washing and suctioning ± irrigation pump → right-colon or through-the-scope enema, or wake the patient and give further oral purgative → if a same-day repeat is not feasible, reschedule and photograph the segment that caused the abortion.

- **Washing and suctioning alone** converted **75%** of preparations rated poor or fair at insertion to good or excellent.
- **Right-colon enema** — instil with the patient in right lateral decubitus, withdraw, let them recover from sedation and evacuate, then rescope the same day. **PEG 500–1,000 mL or bisacodyl 10 mg**; success **53%–100%**.
- **Further oral purgative after waking the patient** — **2 L PEG beat a 1 L PEG enema, 82% vs 53%**, for achieving an adequate preparation. Same-day repeat requires 2 hr from the last dose; next-day is often the more feasible option.
- **Through-the-scope enema** — pass the colonoscope as proximally as possible, then instil through the accessory channel either a **phosphate enema (133 mL / 19 g)** followed by a **bisacodyl enema (37 mL / 10 mg)**, or **2 bisacodyl enemas**. A **500 mL PEG-ELS rescue enema** instilled at the **hepatic flexure** through the biopsy channel cleansed **96%**. Recover the patient from sedation and let them evacuate before completing the examination.
- **Ask about effluent before sedation** — brown liquid or solid effluent on arrival carries a **54%** chance of a fair or poor preparation, and identifying it early is what makes salvage possible.

## Repeat Colonoscopy After an Inadequate Preparation

| Indication for the original colonoscopy | Repeat within |
|---|---|
| Screening or surveillance | **12 months** |
| Abnormal noncolonoscopic colorectal cancer (CRC) screening test (e.g. fecal immunochemical test) | **As soon as possible — generally within 3 months** |
| Alarm symptoms (e.g. gastrointestinal blood loss) | **As soon as possible**; time it from symptom onset or the date the screening test was positive |
| Advanced neoplasia found *and* the preparation was inadequate | **Sooner than 1 year**, generally with a more aggressive regimen ([[acg-2014-bowel-cleansing]]) |

- **Why the interval matters:** inadequate preparation lowers adenoma and advanced-adenoma detection, and adenoma miss rates run **15%–40%**.
- **Repeat preparations fail often** — a second colonoscopy done specifically for inadequate preparation fails again for the same reason in **23%**; **next-day** timing carried the lowest risk of repeat failure. Always pair the repeat with a modified regimen (below).
- **Finding adenomas at the index examination removes average-risk status** — a stool test is then not a substitute, and repeat colonoscopy is recommended to exclude synchronous lesions. Interval grids: [[colonoscopy-surveillance]].

## High Risk for Inadequate Preparation

### Risk Factors

Odds ratios (ORs) for an inadequate preparation:

- **Strongest:** [[cirrhosis|cirrhosis]] 3.4 · Parkinson disease 3.2 · dementia 3.0 · tricyclic antidepressants 2.0
- **Moderate:** diabetes 1.8 · opioids 1.7 · [[gastroparesis|gastroparesis]] 1.6 · previous colorectal surgery 1.6 · lower level of education 1.5 · body mass index >30 kg/m² 1.5 · inpatient status 1.5 · hypertension 1.3 · tobacco use 1.3 · [[chronic-idiopathic-constipation|constipation]] 1.3 · Medicaid vs private insurance 1.3
- **Weak:** Medicare vs private insurance 1.2 · male sex 1.2 · age >65 1.1

- **Risk factors are additive** — risk rises **linearly with the number of factors, plateauing at a 98% likelihood once 7 are present**.
- **A previous inadequate preparation is the single most important predictor.** [[asge-2015-bowel-preparation]]
- **Additional independent predictors** ([[acg-2014-bowel-cleansing]]):
  - **≥8 prescription medications — OR 6.52 (5.12–8.56)**, the strongest single factor
  - **Prior colorectal surgery** — inadequate in **59.7%** after colonic and **64%** after gastric resection despite 4 L PEG-ELS on the morning of the examination
  - **American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status class III–V** — less likely to achieve a high-quality preparation than class I
  - **Spinal cord injury** — neurogenic bowel dysfunction; most preparations rated unacceptable

> [!pitfall] Do not rely on a risk score
> Predictive models yield positive predictive values of only **29%–41%**, have never been shown to improve preparation quality, and once split dosing is used the best model discriminates only modestly. Apply the modifications below instead ([[usmstf-2025-bowel-prep]]).

### Modifications and the High-Risk Regimen

- **Manage anyone at high risk exactly like someone with a prior inadequate preparation** (strong, moderate).
- **After a previous inadequate preparation, modify the instructions to include one or more of the following** (strong, moderate):
  - Increased attention to communicating the instructions
  - Increased use of patient navigation
  - **Restrict vegetables and legumes for 2 to 3 days** before colonoscopy
  - **Clear liquids only on the day before** colonoscopy
  - Addition of promotility agents
  - Treatment of underlying [[chronic-constipation|constipation]]
  - Temporary cessation of anticholinergic, opioid or other constipating medications
  - Use of a **high-volume** regimen
- **The specific high-risk regimen** (weak, low): **split-dose 4 L PEG-ELS + 15 mg bisacodyl the afternoon before the colonoscopy, with a low-residue diet 3 and 2 days before, changing to clear liquids the day before.**
- **This is the one setting in which 4 L is still preferred over 2 L.** After a previous inadequate preparation (BBPS ≤5), **4 L split-dose PEG achieved adequacy more often than 2 L PEG + ascorbate — 81% vs 67% (OR 2.07; 95% CI 1.16–3.69)**, all patients having had a 3-day low-residue diet and 10 mg bisacodyl the day before.
- **Intensive re-preparation before a repeat colonoscopy** ([[acg-2014-bowel-cleansing]]): low-fiber diet **72 h**, liquid diet the day before, **bisacodyl 10 mg + 1.5 L PEG-ELS the evening before**, then a **second 1.5 L PEG-ELS on the day** → **90%** adequate (BBPS ≥2 in every segment).

## Special Populations

- **After [[bariatric-surgery|bariatric surgery]]:** low-volume preparations, or **extended-time delivery** of a high-volume preparation.
- **Pregnancy:** colonoscopy is rarely indicated — defer to the **second trimester** where possible; **tap water enemas** to prepare for sigmoidoscopy. The American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) recommends avoiding NaP; the American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) advises caution with it.
- **Children and adolescents:** no national standards and insufficient evidence for a specific regimen; **NaP must not be used under 12 y**. Clear liquids for 24 h plus a normal saline enema **10 mL/kg** usually suffices for infants with normal or frequent bowel movements; alternatively **PEG-3350 1.5 g/kg/d for 4 days**, with clear liquids on day 4. Maintain hydration throughout.
- **Elderly** and **spinal cord injury:** insufficient evidence to specify a regimen; consider additional purgatives — and **no NaP** in the elderly.
- **Inpatients:** inpatient status is itself a risk factor, and a clear liquid diet before the purgative is the only dietary modification shown to improve quality in hospitalized patients; none of the documents above specifies an inpatient regimen.
- **Suspected IBD:** use PEG-ELS, which does not alter mucosal histology; avoid NaP and sodium picosulfate.

## Tracking Adequacy

- **Track the rate of adequate preparations at the level of the individual endoscopist *and* the endoscopy unit** (strong, moderate) — the unit rate reflects instructions, purgative prescribed, nursing and navigator support and pump availability; the endoscopist rate reflects cleaning effort and the threshold used to call a preparation adequate.
- **Target rate, denominators, and the other colonoscopy quality metrics:** [[colonoscopy-quality-indicators]].
- Adequacy remains a subjective judgement, and endoscopists vary widely in how often they call a preparation poor or fair; objective artificial-intelligence assessment may become feasible ([[artificial-intelligence-endoscopy]]).
