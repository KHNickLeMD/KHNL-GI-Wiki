# Argon Plasma Coagulation (APC)

Argon plasma coagulation (APC) is non-contact thermal ablation: monopolar current delivered to tissue through a jet of argon gas ionized by [[electrosurgery|electrocautery]], which limits depth of injury ([[usmstf-2020-endoscopic-removal]], [[asge-2020-endoscopic-removal]]). Used for hemostasis of superficial vascular lesions and to destroy residual superficial neoplastic tissue.

**The two dials** ([[aga-2026-electrosurgery]], Best Practice Advice (BPA) 11):

- **Monopolar circuit** — plasma carries current without contact, along the path of least resistance to the nearest tissue, exiting through the dispersive pad.
- **Power** sets the current conducted into tissue — **higher power and longer duration → deeper ablation**.
- **Flow rate** sets the *volume* of plasma available to carry that current. High enough to reach the target, but ⚠ **too high disperses the current → low current density at the target**. **0.8–1.0 L/min is typically sufficient for most clinical settings.**
- **Mode** — **pulsed modes cauterize more superficially than continuous modes** ([[asge-2026-lgib]]).
- Settings vary by manufacturer; the principles do not ([[aga-2026-electrosurgery]]).

General electrosurgical principles (current density, waveforms, dispersive pad, cardiac devices) are on **[[electrosurgery]]**; the other hemostatic modalities APC is weighed against — injection, contact thermal, clips, [[endoscopic-band-ligation|band ligation]], TC-325 — are on **[[endoscopic-hemostasis]]**.

## Why APC

- **Non-contact** → treats tangentially oriented lesions the endoscope cannot face head-on ([[asge-2019-radiation-proctopathy]], [[asge-2010-anorectal-disorders]]).
- Covers **large surface areas**, **predictable depth of penetration**, easy application ([[asge-2014-lgib]]).
- Widely available and portable; cheaper than neodymium-doped yttrium aluminum garnet (Nd:YAG) laser, which delivers a deeper tissue effect ([[asge-2010-anorectal-disorders]]).

## Indications by Condition

| Condition | What the guideline says | Strength / quality |
|---|---|---|
| Acute [[acute-lower-gi-bleeding\|lower gastrointestinal bleeding (LGIB)]] from vascular lesions — [[angioectasia\|angioectasias]] or [[radiation-proctopathy\|radiation proctopathy]] | **APC suggested** ([[asge-2026-lgib]], Recommendation 3). Noncontact thermal therapy is the most common modality for bleeding vascular lesions; no head-to-head trials compare modalities | Ungraded (no strength or quality assigned) |
| Colonic angioectasias | Endoscopic therapy indicated when there is **evidence of acute or chronic blood loss**; APC an "effective initial treatment modality" ([[acg-2023-lgib]]). Earlier guidance made APC the **preferred management** of bleeding angioectasias ([[asge-2014-lgib]]) | Recommendation, high quality (American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy [ASGE] 2014) |
| Chronic radiation proctopathy, bleeding | APC, bipolar electrocoagulation, heater probe, and [[radiofrequency-ablation\|radiofrequency ablation (RFA)]] all suggested; **insufficient evidence to prefer any one modality**. Suggest **against** 4% formalin compared with APC (higher adverse-event rate). **No mandate to treat "cosmetic" bleeding** — minor rectal bleeding without anemia ([[asge-2019-radiation-proctopathy]]) | Conditional, low |
| [[small-bowel-bleeding\|Small-bowel]] angioectasias | APC the **treatment of choice since 2001**, delivered by [[device-assisted-enteroscopy\|deep enteroscopy]] ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]); ablative thermal therapy such as APC, or clips ([[aga-2024-ida-management]], BPA 14). Lesions within standard-scope reach: APC among appropriate therapies, with electrocautery, injection, clips/bands, or combination ([[asge-2017-small-bowel-bleeding]]) | — |
| [[gastric-antral-vascular-ectasia\|Gastric antral vascular ectasia (GAVE)]] **requiring serial transfusion** | ⚠ **APC is now second choice.** **Endoscopic variceal ligation (EVL) recommended over APC** — higher eradication rates, fewer sessions, less recurrent bleeding, hospitalisation and transfusion ([[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension]] 5.43). For GAVE with [[iron-deficiency-anemia\|iron-deficiency anemia (IDA)]] and inadequate response to iron, band ligation **or** thermal methods such as APC remain offered ([[aga-2024-ida-management]], BPA 12) | Level of evidence (LoE) 1, strong (Baveno VIII) |
| [[portal-hypertension\|Portal hypertensive]] gastropathy (PHG) bleeding | ⚠ **Downgraded.** Management focuses **primarily on lowering portal pressure with [[nonselective-beta-blockers\|nonselective beta-blockers (NSBB)]]**; **[[tips\|transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS)]] is the most effective rescue** for severe/refractory PHG bleeding. Because endoscopic therapies do not improve portal hypertension they **provide limited benefit and are reserved for focal bleeding lesions or salvage** ([[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension]] 5.44) | LoE 4, strong (Baveno VIII) |
| Bleeding [[peptic-ulcer-disease\|peptic ulcer]] ([[upper-gi-bleeding\|upper gastrointestinal bleeding (UGIB)]]) | **Suggest** APC (with clips or soft monopolar electrocoagulation) — second tier behind bipolar electrocoagulation, heater probe, or absolute ethanol injection, which are *recommended* ([[acg-2021-ugib]], rec 9). **Which ulcers:** endoscopic therapy for **active spurting/oozing bleeding or a nonbleeding visible vessel** (strong, moderate); **adherent clot — no recommendation** (conflicting randomized controlled trials [RCTs]; irrigation plus high-dose [[proton-pump-inhibitors\|proton pump inhibitor (PPI)]] may suffice); **clean base — no endoscopic therapy** | Conditional, very-low to low |
| Post-resection bleeding ([[polypectomy\|postpolypectomy]], [[endoscopic-mucosal-resection\|endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR)]], submucosal dissection) | Through-the-scope clipping **or thermal coagulation** suggested as initial modalities, hemostatic powder adjunctive or for refractory bleeding; apply thermal therapy **cautiously to a resection bed, especially in the right colon** ([[asge-2026-lgib]], Recommendation 2). APC listed among treatment options ([[acg-2023-lgib]]) | Ungraded (no strength or quality assigned) |
| Post-EMR margin, **no** endoscopically visible adenoma despite meticulous inspection | Suggest adjuvant thermal ablation of the margin; **insufficient evidence to pick APC vs snare-tip soft coagulation (STSC)** ([[usmstf-2020-endoscopic-removal]], [[asge-2020-endoscopic-removal]]). Consider margin ablation after piecemeal EMR ([[aga-2026-electrosurgery]], BPA 7) | Conditional, moderate |
| Endoscopically **visible residual** polyp tissue | **Recommend against** ablative techniques (APC, STSC) — associated with increased recurrence ([[usmstf-2020-endoscopic-removal]], [[asge-2020-endoscopic-removal]]) | Strong, moderate |
| [[ampullary-adenoma\|Ampullary]] and [[nonampullary-duodenal-adenoma\|duodenal adenomas]] | Not primary therapy; ablative therapies (APC, laser, photodynamic therapy [PDT], mono-/bipolar electrocoagulation) destroy **residual or recurrent superficial adenomatous tissue** left after snare resection — APC the most frequently used ([[asge-2015-ampullary-duodenal-adenomas]]). After piecemeal papillectomy (>3 cm) or positive/indeterminate margins: adjunct APC to the residual margin or flat remnant, with follow-up **every 2–3 mo until histologic eradication** ([[aga-2026-ampullary-neoplasms]]) | — |
| Bleeding from endoscopic papillectomy | APC among standard hemostasis techniques — with epinephrine injection, electrocoagulation, hemoclips and hemostatic sprays — all to be employed carefully ([[aga-2026-ampullary-neoplasms]], BPA 11) | Ungraded (no strength or quality assigned) |
| [[barretts-esophagus\|Barrett's esophagus]] after EMR of visible lesions | [[endoscopic-eradication-therapy\|Ablation]] of the remaining segment suggested over no ablation, but the supporting trial used PDT/APC and the guideline notes **APC is not the current standard** ([[asge-2018-barretts-eet]]); hybrid-APC is one of several options, while **RFA has the highest-quality and most extensive RCT evidence** ([[aga-2024-barretts-eet]]) | Conditional, low |

> [!pitfall] Never ablate tissue you can still see
> APC is for the *margin of a defect with nothing visible left*, never for residual tissue in view — the same guideline recommends against it for visible residual tissue (strong) and suggests it for a clean margin (conditional) ([[usmstf-2020-endoscopic-removal]]). Visible residual neoplasia is resected, by cold snare or another adjunct technique ([[aga-2026-electrosurgery]]).

⚠ **The 2020 "insufficient evidence to pick APC vs STSC" tie has since been broken** — equally effective in a randomized trial, but **STSC may be more cost-effective in most practice environments** ([[aga-2026-electrosurgery]]). Recurrence figures on [[endoscopic-mucosal-resection]].

## Technique

### Setup

> [!pitfall] Full oral prep or no APC in the colon
> Poor preparation leaves higher **hydrogen and methane** concentrations which, with oxygen and a heat source, risk the rare but fatal complication of colonic explosion. **No consensus threshold for prep adequacy exists**, but a thorough **complete oral (not local colonic) preparation is highly advised** whenever APC is anticipated ([[aga-2026-electrosurgery]], [[asge-2019-radiation-proctopathy]]). A suspected distal source does not exempt it: established radiation proctopathy is treated with APC, so full preparation is still required ([[asge-2026-lgib]]).

- [[bowel-preparation|Prep regimens and adequacy scales]].
- **Duodenum and ampulla:** a larger-channel therapeutic duodenoscope eases aspiration of insufflated air and argon gas with instruments in the channel ([[asge-2015-ampullary-duodenal-adenomas]]).
- **Around the pancreatic duct orifice:** place a pancreatic duct stent **before** ablating ([[asge-2015-ampullary-duodenal-adenomas]]).
- ⚠ **Biopsy any suspicious area before ablating** — ablated tissue is not available for pathology ([[asge-2015-ampullary-duodenal-adenomas]]).

### Settings by Site

| Site / indication | Power | Argon flow | Probe–mucosa distance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rectum — chronic radiation proctopathy | 25–80 W (median 50 W) | 0.6–2.5 L/min (median 1.5 L/min) | not stated | [[asge-2019-radiation-proctopathy]] |
| Colon — angioectasias | 20–40 W typically | 0.8–1.0 L/min typically | not stated | [[acg-2023-lgib]] |
| Right colon (thin-walled) — older figures | 30–45 W | 1 L/min | **1–3 mm**, 1–2 s pulses | [[asge-2014-lgib]] |
| **Duodenal and gastric ulcer bleeding** | **40–70 W** | **1–2 L/min** | **2–10 mm** | [[acg-2021-ugib]] |
| Ampulla / duodenum — residual adenoma | 50–60 W | not stated | not stated | [[asge-2015-ampullary-duodenal-adenomas]] |

- The ulcer row describes **the settings used in the RCTs supporting the conditional recommendation**, not a graded recommendation about settings ([[acg-2021-ugib]]).
- Flow rate did **not** change proctopathy outcomes: clinical success 87% at 1 L/min vs 86% at >1 L/min ([[asge-2019-radiation-proctopathy]]).
- **Generator generation matters for the evidence base** — most published proctopathy series used the first-generation ICC/APC 300; only 3 of 33 used the second-generation VIO APC/APC 2 ([[asge-2019-radiation-proctopathy]]). Newer electrosurgical generators carry built-in APC settings by location of bleeding ([[acg-2023-lgib]]).

> [!pearl] Read the distance together with the power and the site
> **1–3 mm** applies to colonic angioectasias — a thin superficial vessel in the thin-walled right colon, where power is deliberately kept low ([[asge-2014-lgib]]). **2–10 mm** is what the ulcer-bleeding trials used at **40–70 W** in the thicker-walled stomach and duodenum ([[acg-2021-ugib|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2021]]). Higher power plus a thicker wall tolerates — and needs — a longer arc. Never carry one site's figure to another.

### Delivery and Endpoints

- **Short non-contact applications of 1–2 seconds** to the target tissue ([[asge-2019-radiation-proctopathy]]).
- **Target individual vessels — do not "paint."** Painting produces large confluent ulcers ([[asge-2019-radiation-proctopathy]]).
- **Suction frequently** to clear smoke and reduce distension during ulcer-bleeding APC ([[acg-2021-ugib]]), and **suction insufflated argon periodically** to relieve bowel distension and post-procedural pain ([[asge-2019-radiation-proctopathy]]).
- **Large right-colon angioectasias:** consider **submucosal injection of fluid before APC** to reduce [[endoscopic-management-of-perforation|perforation]] risk ([[acg-2023-lgib]], [[asge-2026-lgib]]). Clips combined with APC have also been reported ([[asge-2014-lgib]]).
- **Diverticular bleeding:** if a thermal modality is used, apply it at the **rim of the diverticulum, not the base**, with lower power and shorter duration of coagulation ([[asge-2026-lgib]]).
- **Sessions — proctopathy:** mean/median 1–3.7 sessions to control bleeding; interval 2–8 weeks, most studies **3–4 weeks** to let injured mucosa heal ([[asge-2019-radiation-proctopathy]]).

## Adverse Events and Safety

- **Pooled serious adverse event rate 4% (95% confidence interval [CI] 3–6%)** in chronic radiation proctopathy, where "serious" was pre-defined as colonic fistula, perforation, explosion, or stricture ([[asge-2019-radiation-proctopathy]]).
- **Most common adverse event:** abdominal, rectal, or anal pain — from treatment-induced ulceration or from bowel distension by rapidly instilled argon gas ([[asge-2019-radiation-proctopathy]]).
- **Colonic explosion** reported in 2 poorly prepped patients given an enema-only preparation ([[asge-2019-radiation-proctopathy]]).
- **Argon gas is not absorbed** — track the total volume delivered; it causes luminal distension and risk of bowel perforation ([[aga-2026-electrosurgery]]).
- **Perforation risk in the thin-walled right colon** drives the lower power/flow settings and the option of pre-injection ([[asge-2014-lgib]], [[acg-2023-lgib]]). Overall the safety profile of APC for angioectasias is excellent ([[acg-2023-lgib]]).
- Ablation of visible residual polyp tissue causes submucosal fibrosis and **increases recurrence** — an efficacy harm, not just a safety one ([[usmstf-2020-endoscopic-removal]]).
- **Antithrombotics ([[anticoagulation-gi-bleeding\|periprocedural management]]):** APC is a **low-risk** procedure for bleeding, alongside Barrett's ablation, [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]] without fine-needle aspiration (FNA), and [[capsule-endoscopy|capsule endoscopy]]; therapeutic balloon-assisted enteroscopy is high risk *other than* when the therapy is APC. Tumor ablation of the esophagus, stomach, colon, or rectum is **high risk** ([[asge-2016-antithrombotic]]).

## Comparative Efficacy

| Setting | Finding |
|---|---|
| Chronic radiation proctopathy | APC pooled clinical success **87%** (95% CI 85–90%; 33 studies, 957 patients) vs bipolar electrocoagulation **88%** (95% CI 68–96%; 4 studies, 96 patients) — the basis for "no preferred modality" ([[asge-2019-radiation-proctopathy]]) |
| Same — APC vs 4% formalin | Randomized (n = 30): bleeding cessation 94% vs 100%, no significant difference, median 2 sessions each. Cohort (n = 25): clinical success 79% vs 27% (P = .017) with fewer adverse events (36% vs 82%, P = .001) — the basis for suggesting against formalin ([[asge-2019-radiation-proctopathy]]) |
| Colonic + small-bowel angiodysplasia | Pooled recurrence **34%** (meta-analysis of uncontrolled studies) ([[acg-2023-lgib]]); recurrent bleeding **7.4%** after APC in a large angioectasia series ([[asge-2026-lgib]]) |
| Small-bowel angioectasia | 129/133 (97%) treated successfully by double-balloon enteroscopy (DBE); rebleeding 45/98 (46%) at 36 months ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]) |
| GAVE | Thermal therapy (APC/RFA) and band ligation both achieve high endoscopic success, but **endoscopic band ligation (EBL) is favored on every quantitative outcome** — head-to-head numbers on [[gastric-antral-vascular-ectasia]] ([[aga-2024-ida-management]]) |
| Bleeding peptic ulcer | Less further bleeding than water injection (4/58 [6.9%] vs 12/58 [20.7%]), comparable mortality; no significant difference vs other modalities across 3 RCTs — the basis for the conditional recommendation ([[acg-2021-ugib]]) |
| Barrett's after EMR | Ablation of the residual segment (PDT/APC) vs no ablation: metachronous neoplasia **16.5% vs 29.9%** (P = .0014) ([[asge-2018-barretts-eet]]) |

Unresolved: no trial of APC vs sham, and none treating only actively bleeding angioectasias vs all visible lesions, so rebleeding stands in as the effectiveness surrogate; outcomes in small-bowel disease have been "disappointing" ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]).

## Guideline Version Changes

- **Radiation proctopathy — APC lost first-line status.** [[asge-2010-anorectal-disorders]] suggested APC first-line (citing 25–60 W, 0.5–2.5 L/min, 1–3 sessions, bleeding reduced in 80–90%); [[asge-2019-radiation-proctopathy]] found insufficient evidence to prefer any modality. The 2019 position is what this page asserts, and [[asge-2026-lgib]] again suggests APC for these lesions in acute LGIB.
- **Colon settings drifted downward.** [[asge-2014-lgib]] cited 30–45 W at 1 L/min for the right colon; [[acg-2023-lgib]] states 20–40 W at 0.8–1.0 L/min is typical. Follow the newer figures and check the generator's site-specific presets.
- **Barrett's:** APC was the ablative modality in the trials behind "ablate the residual segment", but [[asge-2018-barretts-eet]] and [[aga-2024-barretts-eet]] both state RFA is the current standard.
- **GAVE and PHG — APC demoted at Baveno VIII (2026).** [[baveno-vii-2022-portal-hypertension]] 6.25/7.14 treated APC, RFA and band ligation as interchangeable for GAVE and said APC or hemospray "may be used" for PHG. [[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension]] separates them: GAVE requiring serial transfusion → EVL *over* APC (5.43); PHG → NSBB first, TIPS as rescue, endoscopic therapy of limited benefit (5.44). Same tier, newer publication date — Baveno VIII is what this page asserts. Lesion-side detail on [[gastric-antral-vascular-ectasia]] and [[portal-hypertensive-gastropathy]]; acute variceal bleeding context on [[variceal-upper-gi-bleeding]].
