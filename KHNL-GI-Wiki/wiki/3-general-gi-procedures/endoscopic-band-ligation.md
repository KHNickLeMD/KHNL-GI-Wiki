## Scope

Mechanical hemostasis by deploying an elastic band over a vessel or lesion drawn into a cap on the endoscope tip. Two names for the same device in different settings: **endoscopic variceal ligation (EVL)** for varices, **endoscopic band ligation (EBL)** for non-variceal targets.

The decision algorithms that *lead* to banding live on the syndrome pages; this page holds the procedure itself.

## Indications by Site and Setting

| Target | Role of banding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Esophageal varices and gastroesophageal varices type 1 (GOV1) | **Standard endoscopic therapy** for acute bleeding; with NSBB for secondary prophylaxis; primary prophylaxis when NSBB cannot be given | [[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]] guidance statements (GS) 19, 31, 38 |
| Small high-risk esophageal varices | **Not technically feasible** — NSBB only | [[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]] |
| Lesser-curve gastric varices | Often the **best definitive therapy** | [[aga-2021-bleeding-gastric-varices]] |
| Cardiofundal varices — gastroesophageal varices type 2 (GOV2) / isolated gastric varices type 1 (IGV1) | **Temporizing only**, and only under strict conditions; first-line options are cyanoacrylate injection, TIPS or retrograde transvenous obliteration | [[aga-2021-bleeding-gastric-varices]]; [[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]] GS 45 |
| [[gastric-antral-vascular-ectasia\|Gastric antral vascular ectasia (GAVE)]] requiring serial transfusion | **Recommended over [[argon-plasma-coagulation\|argon plasma coagulation (APC)]]** | [[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension]] 5.43 |
| [[portal-hypertensive-gastropathy\|Portal hypertensive gastropathy (PHG)]] | Not a banding target — lower portal pressure | [[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]] GS 50–51; [[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension]] 5.44 |
| Colonic diverticulum with stigmata of recent hemorrhage (SRH) | One of three co-equal options (with clips, coagulation); **lower rebleeding than clips** | [[acg-2023-lgib]] Recommendation 10 |

### Esophageal Varices and GOV1

| Setting | Position | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Acute bleeding**, esophageal variceal source confirmed at endoscopy | **EVL should be performed**; the acute-bleeding algorithm sends bleeding esophageal or GOV1 varices to band ligation | [[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]] GS 31, Figure 4 |
| Acute bleeding — alternatives | **Not first-line:** hemoclips, over-the-scope clips, topical hemostatic agents, fibrin | [[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension]] 5.28 |
| **Primary prophylaxis**, compensated advanced chronic liver disease (cACLD) with clinically significant portal hypertension (CSPH) and **high-risk varices** that cannot receive NSBB | **EVL** | [[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]] GS 19 |
| Decompensated cirrhosis, never bled, not on NSBB | Annual screening endoscopy; if high-risk varices → **NSBB or EVL, NSBB preferred** (benefits beyond preventing variceal hemorrhage) | [[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]] GS 22–23 |
| NSBB dose-reduced or stopped for persistent systolic blood pressure <90 mm Hg or severe adverse effects | Discontinuation **should prompt [[upper-endoscopy\|esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD)]]** for high-risk varices requiring band ligation | [[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]] GS 24 |
| **Secondary prophylaxis** — variceal bleeding, preemptive-TIPS criteria not met and/or no TIPS during admission | **NSBB + EVL**; start immediately after control of the index bleed, within 7 days of admission. Versus EVL alone, the combination reduced rebleeding in all patient categories and improved survival in Child-Turcotte-Pugh (CTP) class B and C | [[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]] GS 38 |
| After an esophageal variceal bleed as the single decompensating event, ineligible for preemptive/rescue TIPS, cannot take **either** EVL or carvedilol/conventional NSBB | Start/maintain whichever is tolerated as monotherapy | [[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension]] 4.9 |
| Occlusive bland or malignant [[portal-vein-thrombosis\|portal vein thrombosis (PVT)]] with varices | **NSBB or EVL, NSBB preferred** | [[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]] GS 54 |
| Pregnancy, medium or large varices | Primary prophylaxis with NSBB or EBL; **EBL preferred with cherry red spots or red wale signs** | [[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]] (section on portal hypertension in pregnancy) |

- **High-risk varices** = moderate/large varices, or varices of any size with red wale marks or in a patient with CTP class C ([[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]]).

> [!pitfall] Banding is not the safer prophylaxis
> With large high-risk varices in decompensated cirrhosis, a network meta-analysis found EVL carried more complications and higher mortality than NSBB — NSBB is preferred ([[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]]).

### Gastric Varices — Where Banding Stops

*Lesion types and the two-phase cardiofundal pathway: [[variceal-upper-gi-bleeding]].*

- **Cardiofundal varices (GOV2 / IGV1):** band ligation achieves initial hemostasis in **45%–93%** but is **inferior to cyanoacrylate for long-term control** → a reasonable **temporizing** measure only, and must be followed by more definitive therapy ([[aga-2021-bleeding-gastric-varices]]; [[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]]).
  - Rebleeding is higher with EVL than with glue → band a gastric varix **only if all** apply ([[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]]):
    - no other option is readily available
    - the rupture site (high-risk red signs or platelet plug) is visualized
    - the varix can be completely suctioned into the banding cap
- **Lesser-curve gastric varices:** banding **is often the best definitive therapy**, because these varices behave like esophageal varices ([[aga-2021-bleeding-gastric-varices]]).
  - No pre-endoscopy vascular imaging → still reasonable to band a bleeding lesser-curve varix in the acute setting.
  - Lesser-curve bleeding refractory to band ligation, or recurrent → **TIPS favored**.
- Classify the esophageal varices as well as the gastric ones at the index exam — the definitive gastric-varix therapies differ in their effect on later esophageal variceal bleeding ([[aga-2021-bleeding-gastric-varices]]).

### GAVE

- **GAVE requiring serial transfusion → EVL is recommended over APC** — higher eradication rates, fewer treatment sessions, less recurrent bleeding, fewer hospitalizations and transfusions ([[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension]] 5.43; level of evidence [LoE] 1, strong).
- The head-to-head transfusion, hemoglobin and session-count figures are on [[gastric-antral-vascular-ectasia]].
- **Changed from Baveno VII:** [[baveno-vii-2022-portal-hypertension]] 6.25 listed APC, radiofrequency ablation or band ligation as options that "may be used"; Baveno VIII makes ligation the recommendation over APC.
- **PHG is treated by lowering portal pressure:** NSBB to prevent rebleeding (GS 50); TIPS if bleeding becomes transfusion-dependent despite NSBB (GS 51) ([[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]]). Endoscopic therapies do not improve portal hypertension — limited benefit, reserved for focal bleeding lesions or salvage ([[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension]] 5.44).

### Colonic Diverticular Hemorrhage

- **Recommendation 10 ([[acg-2023-lgib]], strong recommendation, moderate-quality evidence):** treat diverticular SRH with through-the-scope clips, endoscopic band ligation, **or** coagulation — co-equal in the recommendation itself.
- **EBL's edge is rebleeding** ([[acg-2023-lgib]]):
  - Meta-analysis (16 studies): EBL vs clipping — early rebleeding **8% vs 19%**, late rebleeding **9% vs 29%**; EBL may be preferable for long-term rebleeding outcomes.
  - Initial hemostasis **99%–100%** with bipolar coagulation, clipping and EBL alike; EBL was more effective than clipping at avoiding transarterial embolization or surgery.
  - Even after endoscopic treatment of definitive diverticular hemorrhage, early rebleeding is **17.4%** and late rebleeding **32.0%**.
- **Perforation:** 2 patients (**0.31%**) after EBL vs none after clipping, in a 1,679-patient multicenter Japanese cohort ([[acg-2023-lgib]]).
- Most published EBL experience is from Japanese cohorts, where right-sided diverticulosis is more prevalent and presentation differs from Western populations ([[acg-2023-lgib]]).
- [[acg-2023-lgib]] discusses EBL only for diverticular hemorrhage: [[angioectasia|colonic angioectasias]] → APC as initial treatment; postpolypectomy bleeding → typically through-the-scope clips.

## Technique

### Varices

- **Bleeding gastric varices:** a promotility agent before the procedure and a therapeutic endoscope or advanced suction device are advised — intragastric blood often obscures the cardia and fundus ([[aga-2021-bleeding-gastric-varices]]).
- Large size and a mass-like configuration of cardiofundal varices can make band ligation difficult ([[aga-2021-bleeding-gastric-varices]]).

### Colonic Diverticula

> [!howto] Band a bleeding diverticulum
> 1. Mark the SRH with a clip; withdraw the endoscope.
> 2. Attach the banding device; reinsert to the marked diverticulum.
> 3. Suction the diverticulum into the device and deploy the band.
> ([[acg-2023-lgib]])

- Typically feasible when the diverticulum is **smaller than the diameter of the banding device** ([[acg-2023-lgib]]).
- An **adjacent tattoo** before EBL has also been reported — may help if endoscopic therapy fails and surgery is needed ([[acg-2023-lgib]]).
- **Device and reach:** a conventional esophageal variceal ligation device on a gastroscope may limit therapy to the left colon; a newer EBL device specific to colonic diverticular bleeding has a wider field of vision, seems equally effective, and may shorten procedure time ([[acg-2023-lgib]]).

## Session Schedule and Post-Eradication Surveillance

| Phase | Interval | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Repeat esophageal EVL sessions until variceal obliteration | **Every 2–4 weeks** (4 weeks favored to allow banding ulcers to heal) | [[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]] GS 20 |
| First EGD after obliteration | **6 months** | [[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]] GS 20 |
| Thereafter | **Every 12 months** | [[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]] GS 20 |

- **Gastric varices** ([[aga-2021-bleeding-gastric-varices]]):
  - Lesser-curve varices: band ligation repeated as for esophageal variceal eradication — endoscopy every 2–4 weeks until obliteration.
  - After confirmed eradication: endoscopy within 3–6 months, then yearly.
  - Lesser-curve varices persisting after TIPS → band ligation until eradication.

## Adverse Events

- **Post-EVL ulcer bleeding** ([[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension]] 5.47): **rare but serious.** Management is individualized and may require vasoactive therapy, endoscopic hemostasis, temporary stenting or tamponade, and **rescue TIPS with embolization** when needed — particularly in **high-risk patients (Model for End-Stage Liver Disease (MELD) score >18, or an emergency EVL)**.
- **Evidence is insufficient to recommend routine [[proton-pump-inhibitors|proton pump inhibitor (PPI)]] use to prevent post-EVL ulcers (or bleeding from them)** ([[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension]] 5.48; weak). Separately, PPIs are **discontinued** once acute variceal hemorrhage is confirmed as the bleeding source, absent other specific indications ([[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]] GS 37).
- **Variceal bands do not contraindicate a feeding tube** if indicated — enteral feeding starts once the hemorrhage episode is controlled ([[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]] GS 36).
