## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Anthropometric diagnosis** — body mass index (BMI). No confirmatory laboratory or imaging test.
- Affects ~**40% of US adults**; only ~**1.1%** of eligible patients receive primary [[bariatric-surgery|bariatric surgery]], and **<5%** of patients seeking weight loss are aware endoscopic options exist ([[aga-2021-intragastric-balloons]]).
- Drives gastrointestinal (GI)/metabolic comorbidity — [[nafld-masld|metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD)]], type 2 diabetes (T2DM), hypertension, dyslipidemia; each shows improved remission with effective weight loss ([[aga-2021-intragastric-balloons]]).

### Severity Assessment

- **BMI is the severity measure, and the band is what opens each treatment.** It approximates adiposity only — it does not account for sex, age, ethnicity or fat distribution, so access should never be denied on the traditional thresholds alone ([[asmbs-ifso-2022-bariatric-indications]]).
- Drug thresholds are the American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2022 treatment population; surgical thresholds come from the joint American Society for Metabolic and Bariatric Surgery (ASMBS) and International Federation for the Surgery of Obesity and Metabolic Disorders (IFSO) 2022 statement, which **replaced the 1991 National Institutes of Health criteria** (BMI ≥40, or ≥35 with comorbidity) — the older, higher thresholds are still widely applied.

| BMI (kg/m²) | What it opens | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **≥27 with a weight-related complication** | Pharmacotherapy added to lifestyle, after inadequate response to lifestyle alone | AGA 2022 |
| **≥30** (class I obesity = 30–34.9) | Pharmacotherapy, same lifestyle-failure clause | AGA 2022 |
| **30–40** | [[intragastric-balloon\|Intragastric balloon]] with lifestyle modification, after a failed trial of conventional weight-loss strategies | AGA 2021 |
| **30–34.9 with metabolic disease**, or class I obesity not achieving durable weight loss/comorbidity improvement by nonsurgical means | Bariatric surgery considered | ASMBS–IFSO 2022 |
| **≥35** | Bariatric surgery recommended, regardless of the presence of comorbidity | ASMBS–IFSO 2022 |
| **Asian populations: ≥25 = clinical obesity; ≥27.5** | Offer bariatric surgery — the thresholds above are shifted down, not applied as printed | ASMBS–IFSO 2022 |

## Differential Diagnosis

*No diagnostic schema applies — obesity is an anthropometric diagnosis, not an undifferentiated syndrome. The guidelines below address management; none gives a workup for secondary causes of weight gain.*

---

## Diagnostics

- **Track the metabolic response, not the weight alone:** effective weight loss improves hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c), fasting glucose, low-density lipoprotein (LDL) cholesterol and transaminases, and increases remission of diabetes, hypertension and dyslipidemia ([[aga-2021-intragastric-balloons]]).
- **Efficacy metric for every therapy below is percent total body weight loss (%TBWL)**, measured against lifestyle intervention alone.

---

## Therapeutics

### Lifestyle Modification
- Foundation of all therapy — every pharmacologic and endoscopic option below is **added to** lifestyle modification, never substituted for it.
- What the drug trials paired with the medication: **hypocaloric diet (500–600 kcal/day deficit) plus 150 minutes/week of physical activity** ([[aga-2022-obesity-pharm]]). Lifestyle intervention has no standardized definition, and trial programs are more structured than routine practice.
- During intragastric balloon therapy, AGA **strongly** recommends **moderate- to high-intensity concomitant lifestyle modification** ([[aga-2021-intragastric-balloons]]); continuing a moderate- to high-intensity diet **for 6 months after therapy** gave ongoing weight loss, progressive even after the balloon was out.

### Pharmacotherapy

**Who gets a drug (Rec 1 — Strong, moderate certainty):** adults meeting the BMI thresholds above **who have had an inadequate response to lifestyle interventions** → add a pharmacologic agent to lifestyle intervention, rather than continue lifestyle alone ([[aga-2022-obesity-pharm]]). The inadequate-response clause is part of the indication, and the drug is added to — never instead of — lifestyle modification.

**Before starting any anti-obesity medication (AOM)** ([[aga-2022-obesity-pharm]]):

- AOMs **generally need to be used chronically**; the choice rests on the patient's clinical profile — comorbidities, preferences, cost, access.
- **Do not use in pregnancy.** Do not use in **active bulimia nervosa**; monitor binge-eating disorder closely for decompensation.
- **On insulin or an insulin secretagogue** (e.g., sulfonylurea) → risk of hypoglycemia rises as intake and weight fall; adjust doses.
- **On antihypertensives** → blood pressure can drop with weight loss; caution when starting.
- Benefit threshold the panel used: a mean difference of **≥3% TBWL** over lifestyle alone (≈5% TBWL from baseline) is the minimal clinically important difference.

> [!pitfall] Hold these before a sedated procedure
> **Phentermine-containing drugs** — suggested to stop **at least 4 days before a procedure requiring anesthesia**: sympathomimetic effects risk perioperative hypertension, and norepinephrine-reuptake inhibition has caused refractory hypotension, bradycardia and hyperthermia ([[aga-2022-obesity-pharm]]).
> **Naltrexone-bupropion ER** — hold before any procedure using opiates (e.g., fentanyl for endoscopy), or the analgesic fails ([[aga-2022-obesity-pharm]]).

- GLP-1 RA delayed emptying carries its own [[endoscopy-sedation|pre-endoscopy]] rule — agent-by-agent hold intervals live on [[glp-1-receptor-agonists]].
- **No hierarchy beyond semaglutide.** The guideline offers no ranked algorithm for choosing among the rest; comorbidities, contraindications, historical response, cost, preference and drug–drug interactions decide, largely by trial and error.
- **Tirzepatide was not FDA-approved for obesity** when this guideline published and was not among the agents it evaluated.
- Combining two AOMs, and using AOMs after suboptimal results from bariatric surgery or alongside an intragastric balloon, are common practices with scarce or high-risk-of-bias evidence.

### Endoscopic Bariatric Therapy — Intragastric Balloons

*Eligibility, device choice, periprocedural orders, safety rates, and post-removal strategy: [[intragastric-balloon]]. Other endoscopic devices: [[endoscopic-bariatric-therapies]].*

The positioning decision at this level ([[aga-2021-intragastric-balloons]]):

- **Who:** **suggested** — with lifestyle modification, over lifestyle modification alone — in patients who have **failed a trial of conventional weight-loss strategies** (conditional, moderate certainty). The **only selection criterion the guideline gives is BMI 30–40 kg/m²** (the range US trials enrolled).
- **What it buys:** greatest %TBWL at **6–8 months**, incrementally smaller benefit thereafter; **no efficacy data beyond 1 year**. An intragastric balloon (IGB) augments lifestyle modification — it is not a standalone cure, and the guideline prescribes **no implantation duration**.
- **What it costs:** serious adverse events are **higher than with lifestyle alone**, and intolerance takes a minority of balloons out early — rates, relative risk and periprocedural orders are on the balloon page linked above.
- **Required alongside it:** the concomitant lifestyle modification above (**strong**), [[proton-pump-inhibitors|proton pump inhibitor (PPI)]] prophylaxis (**strong**), and a planned maintenance intervention for after removal — diet, pharmacotherapy, repeat IGB, or bariatric surgery, by shared decision-making.

### Bariatric Surgery
- Definitive option for eligible patients; BMI thresholds are in [[#Severity Assessment]] above.
- Procedure choice, preoperative assessment, surgery in cirrhosis, and complications: [[bariatric-surgery]]. Also a post-IGB maintenance pathway.
