A form of **dysautonomia** — impaired autonomic responses producing clinical symptoms of orthostatic intolerance. The autonomic nervous system innervates every organ and integrates heart rate, blood pressure and body temperature; dysfunction may occur at the peripheral nerves, autonomic ganglia, spinal cord and/or brain, giving cardiac, respiratory, alimentary, urinary, reproductive, ocular and sudomotor defects. Gastrointestinal (GI) symptoms are common and often bring these patients to gastroenterology first. Frequently overlaps [[hypermobile-ehlers-danlos-syndrome|hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (hEDS)]] / hypermobility spectrum disorder (HSD) and [[mast-cell-activation-syndrome|mast cell activation syndrome (MCAS)]]. ## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

**Adult definition — all four required.** [[aga-2025-heds-gi]]

| # | Element | Operative detail |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Heart rate rise | **≥30 beats/min within 10 minutes of upright posture** (**≥40 beats/min** in adolescents **12–19 years**) |
| 2 | Blood pressure | **Absence of orthostatic hypotension** — the tachycardia is not a response to a falling blood pressure |
| 3 | Duration | Symptoms of orthostatic intolerance present **≥6 months** |
| 4 | Exclusions | **Not explained by** dehydration, medications, diet, primary anxiety disorder, eating disorder, or another medical condition |

- Non-GI symptoms: palpitations, tremulousness, lightheadedness, fatigue, blurred vision, generalized weakness, cognitive and bladder dysfunction.
- **Prior infection is a risk factor.** Up to **40%** self-report a viral upper respiratory or GI infection as the precipitating event; both acute and long coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) are associated with POTS. A history of infectious insult should prompt consideration of POTS/MCAS.
- Mechanisms under study: vascular laxity and/or peripheral neuropathy; autoimmunity (increased autoimmune disease prevalence in patients and family members, and greater-than-expected frequency of nonspecific autoantibodies — cardiovascular G protein-coupled membrane receptors, ganglionic acetylcholine receptors, antinuclear antibody, Sjogren's antibodies, and antibodies to norepinephrine, acetylcholine and angiotensin II); COVID-19 neuronal tropism with angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 depletion; gut microbiome differences between patients and controls.

### Classification / Typing

Three phenotypes, representing different pathophysiologic perturbations leading to chronic orthostatic intolerance — **hypovolemic**, **neuropathic**, and **primary hyperadrenergic**. [[aga-2025-heds-gi]]

### GI Manifestations

- Nausea, abdominal pain, vomiting, [[chronic-diarrhea|diarrhea]], [[abdominal-bloating-and-distention|bloating]] and severe [[chronic-idiopathic-constipation|constipation]] are all common.
- **Nausea, pain and early satiety are the most predictive of abnormal GI motility** — the triad that should trigger motility testing.
- Motility findings reported in POTS: two-thirds of 163 patients with POTS and GI symptoms had **abnormal gastric emptying**; increased prevalence of **delayed small bowel transit and hypocontractility** on wireless motility capsule testing; impaired gastric myoelectrical activity and central sensitization proposed as a mechanism for [[disorders-of-gut-brain-interaction|disorder of gut-brain interaction (DGBI)]] symptoms.
- **One-third of patients with POTS carry a diagnosis of [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)]].** Among patients with hEDS/HSD, those with concomitant POTS were more likely to have fibromyalgia, IBS, [[gerd|gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)]] or dysmotility — the overlap prevalence figures are on [[hypermobile-ehlers-danlos-syndrome]].
- POTS/autonomic imbalance is a recognized comorbidity of [[cyclic-vomiting-syndrome|cyclic vomiting syndrome (CVS)]]; **treating the POTS improves CVS outcomes**. [[aga-2024-cvs]]

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[disorders-of-gut-brain-interaction]] for the GI-symptom arm; the orthostatic arm is a cardiology/neurology evaluation.*

- Orthostatic hypotension (excluded by definition — blood pressure falls rather than heart rate rising in isolation).
- Dehydration, medication effect (stimulants and other centrally acting agents), dietary cause, primary anxiety disorder, eating disorder — all explicit exclusions.
- [[gastroparesis]] and other GI dysmotility, which may coexist rather than substitute.
- [[hypermobile-ehlers-danlos-syndrome]] / HSD and [[mast-cell-activation-syndrome]] — overlapping, not mutually exclusive. The prevalence figures for the three-way overlap, and the caveats on them, are on [[hypermobile-ehlers-danlos-syndrome]].
- [[irritable-bowel-syndrome]] and [[dyspepsia|functional dyspepsia]] (DGBI overlap).

## Diagnostics

- **Bedside testing (Best Practice Advice [BPA] 4):** postural vital signs — a symptomatic heart rate rise of ≥30 beats/min within 10 minutes of standing during an **active stand**, or a **head-up tilt table test**, in the **absence of orthostasis**.
- **Referral for autonomic testing** (cardiology or neurology) should be considered in patients with **refractory GI symptoms who also report orthostatic intolerance**, after excluding medication side effects and after appropriate lifestyle or behavioral modification (adequate hydration, physical exercise) has been tried. **It is not required** for patients who report GI symptoms alone.
- **Once POTS is confirmed:** autonomic function testing (**tilt table or sudomotor**) plus **expanded laboratory testing with autoantibodies**.
- **Gastric motor testing (BPA 11):** in coexisting hEDS/HSD and POTS with chronic upper-GI symptoms, **timely** measurement of gastric emptying and/or accommodation should be considered **after appropriate exclusion of anatomical and structural disease** — abnormal gastric emptying may be more common than in the general population.
- No ingested source gives a POTS-specific motility-testing threshold or protocol beyond this; GI motility testing in these patients is otherwise guided by symptoms and response to first-line treatment, as in the general population.

## Therapeutics

**BPA 13.** Treatment is **conservative first**; pharmacologic treatment is for those who do not respond to conservative lifestyle measures, and is delivered with **integrated care from multiple specialties** (cardiology, neurology). [[aga-2025-heds-gi]]

### Conservative Measures First

| Measure | Target |
|---|---|
| Fluid intake | **≈2 L/day** |
| Salt intake | **≈10 g/day** |
| Exercise | Exercise training |
| Compression | Compression garments |

- Salt tablets and acute/chronic intravenous hydration (rare) are also listed as lifestyle-tier options.

### Pharmacologic Options

Reserved for more severely affected individuals / those failing conservative measures. Three therapeutic aims: **volume expansion, heart rate control, and vasoconstriction**. [[aga-2025-heds-gi]] (Table 2)

| Aim / class | Agents |
|---|---|
| **Blood volume expanders** | Fludrocortisone, desmopressin, erythropoietin |
| **Heart rate lowering** | Propranolol, ivabradine |
| **Central nervous system sympatholytics** | Clonidine, methyldopa |
| **Other** | Midodrine, pyridostigmine, droxidopa, modafinil |

- No ingested source gives doses for any of these agents.

### GI Symptom Management

- Manage the GI symptoms as in the general population — a positive symptom-based diagnostic strategy, limited noninvasive testing, and standard [[disorders-of-gut-brain-interaction|DGBI]] / motility treatment, **plus** the orthostatic treatment above (BPA 12, 16).
- The symptom-by-symptom agent grid (nausea/vomiting, abdominal pain, constipation, diarrhea) lives on [[hypermobile-ehlers-danlos-syndrome]] — see its GI Symptom Management table.
- **Opioids:** avoid for abdominal pain, and facilitate cessation where already prescribed, through a careful multidisciplinary approach.
- Patients whose symptoms worsen with stress or who use maladaptive coping benefit from **early** referral for brain-gut hypnotherapy targeting the emotional, cognitive, behavioral and **autonomic** aspects of gut-brain axis dysregulation.
- Nutrition: screen for weight loss and disordered eating and involve a dietitian; upper-GI-predominant symptoms → [[gastroparesis|gastroparesis (small particle)]] diet; lower-GI-predominant → low fermentable oligosaccharide, disaccharide, monosaccharide and polyol (FODMAP), wheat- or gluten-free, or dairy-free. Efficacy in this population is unstudied.
