Postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome (POTS) is a form of **dysautonomia** — impaired autonomic responses producing clinical symptoms of orthostatic intolerance. Autonomic dysfunction can arise at the peripheral nerves, autonomic ganglia, spinal cord and/or brain, giving cardiac, respiratory, alimentary, urinary, reproductive, ocular and sudomotor defects. Gastroenterologists increasingly see patients with chronic gastrointestinal (GI) symptoms and comorbid [[hypermobile-ehlers-danlos-syndrome|hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (hEDS)]] / hypermobility spectrum disorder (HSD), POTS and/or [[mast-cell-activation-syndrome|mast cell activation syndrome (MCAS)]]. [[aga-2025-heds-gi]]

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

**Adult definition — all four elements.** [[aga-2025-heds-gi]]

| # | Element | Operative detail |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Heart rate rise | **≥30 beats/min within 10 minutes of upright posture** (**≥40 beats/min** in adolescents **12–19 years**) |
| 2 | Blood pressure | **Absence of orthostatic hypotension** |
| 3 | Duration | Symptoms of orthostatic intolerance present **≥6 months** |
| 4 | Exclusions | **Not explained by** dehydration, medications, diet, primary anxiety disorder, eating disorders, or other medical conditions |

- Non-GI symptoms: palpitations, tremulousness, lightheadedness, fatigue, blurred vision, generalized weakness; cognitive and bladder dysfunction not uncommon.
- **Prior infection is a risk factor.** Up to **40%** self-report a viral upper respiratory or GI infection as the precipitating event; both acute and long coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) are associated with POTS. A history of infectious insult may prompt further consideration of POTS/MCAS.
- Possible immunologic cause: more autoimmune disease in patients and their family members, and more nonspecific autoantibodies than expected (cardiovascular G protein–coupled membrane receptors, ganglionic acetylcholine receptors, antinuclear antibody, Sjogren's antibodies; antibodies to norepinephrine, acetylcholine, angiotensin II).

### Classification / Typing

Three phenotypes, representing different pathophysiologic perturbations leading to chronic orthostatic intolerance — **hypovolemic**, **neuropathic**, and **primary hyperadrenergic**. [[aga-2025-heds-gi]]

### GI Manifestations

- Nausea, abdominal pain, vomiting, [[chronic-diarrhea|diarrhea]], [[abdominal-bloating-and-distention|bloating]] and severe [[chronic-idiopathic-constipation|constipation]] may be common.

> [!pearl] Nausea, pain, early satiety predict dysmotility
> Of the GI symptoms in POTS, nausea, abdominal pain and early satiety are the most predictive of abnormal GI motility ([[aga-2025-heds-gi]]).

- Motility findings: two-thirds of 163 patients with POTS and GI symptoms had **abnormal gastric emptying**; increased prevalence of **delayed small bowel transit and hypocontractility** on wireless motility capsule testing; impaired gastric myoelectrical activity and central sensitization proposed as mechanisms for [[disorders-of-gut-brain-interaction|disorder of gut-brain interaction (DGBI)]] symptoms. [[aga-2025-heds-gi]]
- In a small study, **one-third of patients with POTS reported an [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)]] diagnosis**. Overlap prevalence with hEDS/HSD and MCAS (and its caveats) is on [[hypermobile-ehlers-danlos-syndrome]].
- Autonomic imbalances including POTS are observed in a substantial subgroup of patients with [[cyclic-vomiting-syndrome|cyclic vomiting syndrome (CVS)]]; **treating POTS may improve overall functional status**. [[aga-2024-cvs]]

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup of the GI symptoms: see [[nausea-and-vomiting]].*

- Orthostatic hypotension — its absence is part of the definition.
- Dehydration, medications, diet, primary anxiety disorder, eating disorders, other medical conditions — explicit exclusions.
- [[gastroparesis]] and other GI dysmotility — abnormal gastric emptying is common in POTS.
- [[celiac-disease]] — risk elevated in hEDS/HSD and/or POTS.
- [[hypermobile-ehlers-danlos-syndrome]] / HSD and [[mast-cell-activation-syndrome]] — overlapping conditions.
- [[irritable-bowel-syndrome]] and [[functional-dyspepsia]] — DGBI overlap; both may also follow prior infection.

## Diagnostics

*Best Practice Advice (BPA) from [[aga-2025-heds-gi]]; ungraded expert guidance.*

| Question | Population | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Test for POTS at all? (BPA 2)** | Patients with clinical manifestations of POTS | Targeted testing only; universal testing of all hEDS/HSD patients is not supported |
| **Bedside test + referral (BPA 4)** | hEDS/HSD with **refractory GI symptoms who also report orthostatic intolerance**, after excluding medication side effects and trying lifestyle/behavioral changes (e.g., adequate hydration, physical exercise) | Consider postural vital signs (e.g., symptomatic heart rate rise ≥30 beats/min within 10 minutes of standing — active stand or head-up tilt table test — without orthostasis) and referral to cardiology or neurology for autonomic testing. **Not required** for all hEDS/HSD patients with GI symptoms alone |
| **POTS suspected** (text) | Any patient with the appropriate clinical history | Postural vital signs for exaggerated orthostatic tachycardia; **consider** referral for autonomic function testing (**tilt table or sudomotor**); **if POTS is confirmed** → expanded laboratory testing with autoantibodies |
| **GI symptom workup (BPA 8)** | hEDS/HSD with comorbid POTS and/or MCAS and DGBI-type symptoms | As for DGBI in the general population — positive symptom-based diagnostic strategy, limited noninvasive testing |
| **Gastric motor testing (BPA 11)** | hEDS/HSD with comorbid POTS and **chronic upper-GI symptoms** | Consider **timely** gastric emptying and/or accommodation testing **after excluding anatomical and structural disease** — abnormal gastric emptying may be more common than in the general population |

## Therapeutics

**BPA 13.** Conservative measures first; special pharmacologic treatment for volume expansion, heart rate control and vasoconstriction, with **integrated care from multiple specialties** (e.g., cardiology, neurology), should be considered in patients who do not respond to conservative lifestyle measures. [[aga-2025-heds-gi]]

### Conservative Measures First

| Measure | Target |
|---|---|
| Fluid intake | **2 L/day** |
| Salt intake | **10 g/day** (salt tablets also listed) |
| Exercise | Exercise training |
| Compression | Compression garments |
| Intravenous hydration | Acute/chronic — rare |

### Pharmacologic Options

More severely affected individuals may require pharmacologic treatment. [[aga-2025-heds-gi]] (Table 2)

| Class | Agents |
|---|---|
| **Blood volume expanders** | Fludrocortisone, desmopressin, erythropoietin |
| **Heart rate lowering agents** | Propranolol, ivabradine |
| **Central nervous system sympatholytics** | Clonidine, methyldopa |
| **Other** | Midodrine, pyridostigmine, droxidopa, modafinil |

- American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2025 gives no doses for these agents.

### GI Symptom Management

- **BPA 12:** treat the most prominent GI symptoms and abnormal GI function test results with general DGBI / GI motility treatment, **plus** treatment of any symptoms attributable to POTS.
- Agents by symptom (nausea/vomiting, abdominal pain, constipation, diarrhea), opioid avoidance, and diets: [[hypermobile-ehlers-danlos-syndrome]] (GI Symptom Management; Nutrition and Diet).
- Symptoms that worsen with stress **and** maladaptive coping behaviors → early referral for brain-gut hypnotherapy targeting emotional, cognitive, behavioral or **autonomic** aspects of gut-brain axis dysregulation (see [[brain-gut-behavioral-therapy]]).
