Post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder (PTLD): spectrum of lymphoid proliferations arising under immunosuppression after solid-organ transplant; an **uncommon but serious** de novo malignancy after [[liver-transplantation|liver transplant]] (LT). Incidence **~2%** of adult LT recipients ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]]); the earlier guideline gave **0.9%–2.9%** ([[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]]). Often, but not always, Epstein–Barr virus (EBV)–driven.

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Requires a high index of suspicion** — consider in LT recipients (especially high-risk individuals) presenting with **unexplained fever, lymphadenopathy, or cytopenias** ([[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]] Rec 58, *grade 1, level A*).
- **Presentation is often nonspecific or absent altogether**; when symptoms do appear they are vague — **fevers, night sweats, weight loss**. Imaging may show **bulky lymphadenopathy**, with the confirmatory diagnosis subsequently made by biopsy ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]]).
- **Organs involved** — beyond lymphadenopathy and cytopenias, disturbances of the **gastrointestinal (GI) tract, lungs, spleen, and central nervous system** ([[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]]).
- **Histopathological diagnosis is required** — EBV detection by nucleic acid testing supports suspicion but is **not diagnostic** of EBV-associated PTLD; biopsy of involved tissue is required ([[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]] Rec 59, *grade 1, level A*).

> [!pearl] Late presentation does not argue against PTLD
> Most cases occur **within the first 3 years** after LT, but patients **may present many years later** ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]]). Months 3–6 post-LT is separately a high-risk period for opportunistic herpesvirus infection, including EBV ([[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]]).

- **Risk factors** — primary EBV infection, cytomegalovirus (CMV) donor–recipient mismatch or CMV disease, and augmented immunosuppression, especially with **anti-lymphocyte antibodies** ([[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]]). **Patients who are EBV-negative at the time of LT are at the highest risk** ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]]). It is uncertain whether the etiology of the underlying liver disease influences PTLD development.

### Severity Assessment

- **Survival >50% at 10 years.** The two stated **negative prognostic factors are older age and monomorphic type** ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]]) — monomorphic type is also what escalates therapy ([[#Therapeutics]]).

### Classification / Typing

- **EBV-associated vs EBV-negative** — the association with EBV is variable in adult LT recipients; **later-onset PTLD is less likely to be EBV-associated** ([[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]]).
- **Monomorphic vs non-monomorphic** is the only histologic split either American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD) guideline uses, and it carries both the prognosis and the treatment decision.
- Neither guideline defines the World Health Organization PTLD categories (early lesions / polymorphic / monomorphic / classic Hodgkin-like) or gives a staging system; "monomorphic" is the only histologic term they use.

## Differential Diagnosis

*The presenting syndrome (fever, lymphadenopathy, cytopenias) overlaps with infection and rejection — tissue histopathology is what distinguishes PTLD ([[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]] Rec 59).*

- Other **de novo post-transplant malignancy**, including lymphoma — overall post-LT cancer risk is **2.45-fold** that of the general population, with the largest relative risk for non-Hodgkin lymphoma and the greatest absolute risk increase for non-melanoma skin cancer ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]]); full post-LT surveillance schedule on [[liver-transplantation]].
- **Opportunistic infection** driving fever or cytopenias — CMV disease, EBV infection, fungal infection.
- **[[antibody-mediated-rejection-liver-transplant|Allograft rejection]]** and other causes of unexplained fever or cytopenias in the LT recipient.

## Diagnostics

- **Tissue histopathology** — diagnostic gold standard; biopsy of an involved site is required ([[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]] Rec 59).
- **Radiographic studies** (cross-sectional imaging) — identify sites of involvement, especially **pulmonary or intra-abdominal** disease ([[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]]).
- **EBV nucleic acid testing (viral load)** — a diagnostic adjunct in a symptomatic patient; **not diagnostic** of EBV-associated PTLD and **not a surveillance test**.

> [!pitfall] EBV viral load neither screens for nor diagnoses PTLD
> Screening for PTLD with **serial EBV polymerase chain reaction (PCR) is not supported** in adult LT recipients ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]], Statement 52, *weak, level 3*) — a markedly elevated viral load does identify patients at increased risk, but the role of EBV screening for PTLD is not well established. Many centers still monitor.

## Therapeutics

| Situation | Action | Source |
|---|---|---|
| PTLD confirmed on histopathology — all subtypes | **Reduce immunosuppression** (initial treatment) | [[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]] |
| No clinical response within **2–4 weeks** of reduction | Add **anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody (rituximab)**, surgical therapy, radiation therapy, and/or cytotoxic chemotherapy | [[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]] |
| **Monomorphic** PTLD | Chemotherapy, as for other lymphomas | [[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]] |

- Neither guideline specifies which immunosuppressant to reduce, or by how much.
- **Antiviral therapy** — addition has **not** been proven to affect outcomes ([[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]]).
