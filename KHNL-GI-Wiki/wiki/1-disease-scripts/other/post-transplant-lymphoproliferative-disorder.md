Spectrum of lymphoid proliferations arising under immunosuppression after solid-organ transplant; an **uncommon but serious** de novo malignancy after [[liver-transplantation|liver transplant]] (LT), incidence **0.9–2.9%** in adults. Often, but not always, Epstein-Barr virus (EBV)–driven. [[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]]

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Requires a high index of suspicion** — consider in LT recipients (especially high-risk individuals) presenting with **undiagnosed fever, unexplained lymphadenopathy, or cytopenias**.
- **Manifestations** — lymphadenopathy, cytopenias, unexplained fever, and disturbances of the **GI tract, lungs, spleen, and CNS**.
- **Histopathological diagnosis is required** — biopsy of involved tissue is mandatory; the clinical syndrome alone does not establish PTLD.
- **EBV viremia is NOT diagnostic** — detection of EBV by nucleic acid testing supports suspicion but cannot establish the diagnosis.
- **Timing** — risk is greatest during the period of maximal immunosuppression (months 3–6 post-LT), when opportunistic pathogens (CMV, EBV, fungi) predominate.
- **Risk factors** — primary EBV infection, CMV donor–recipient mismatch or CMV disease, and augmented immunosuppression (especially anti-lymphocyte antibodies). It is uncertain whether the etiology of the underlying liver disease influences PTLD development.

### Classification / Typing

- **EBV-associated vs EBV-negative** — the association with EBV is variable in adult LT recipients; **later-onset PTLD is less likely to be EBV-associated**.
- ⚠ **Decision gap — no histologic subclassification or stage is on this page.** Neither ingested source ([[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]], [[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]]) gives the WHO PTLD histologic categories (early lesions / polymorphic / monomorphic / classic Hodgkin-like) or a staging system, and treatment intensity in practice keys off that subtype. A hematology/oncology PTLD source would be needed; do not infer the categories from this page.

## Differential Diagnosis

*No dedicated diagnostic schema — because the presenting syndrome (fever, lymphadenopathy, cytopenias) overlaps with infection and rejection, tissue histopathology is what distinguishes PTLD.*

- Other **de novo post-transplant malignancy** / lymphoma (lymphoma relative risk ~10–30× post-LT).
- **Opportunistic infection** driving fever/cytopenias — CMV disease, EBV infection, fungal infection.
- **[[antibody-mediated-rejection-liver-transplant|Allograft rejection]]** and other causes of unexplained fever or cytopenias in the LT recipient.

## Diagnostics

- **Tissue histopathology** — diagnostic gold standard; biopsy of an involved site is required.
- **Radiographic studies** (CT/cross-sectional imaging) — identify sites of involvement, especially **pulmonary or intra-abdominal** disease.
- **EBV nucleic acid testing (viral load)** — may support suspicion but is **not diagnostic** of EBV-associated PTLD.
- **Do NOT screen with serial EBV PCR.** Routine serial EBV PCR monitoring for PTLD is **not recommended** in adult LT recipients ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]], Rec 52, *weak, level 3*) — many centers still do it, and the recommendation acknowledges the low evidence base. EBV testing stays a diagnostic adjunct in a symptomatic patient, not a surveillance test.

**Where PTLD sits in post-LT cancer risk** ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]], Figure 3): overall post-LT cancer risk is **2.45-fold** elevated; **non-Hodgkin lymphoma carries the highest standardized incidence ratio (~10× the general population)**, while non-melanoma skin cancer carries the highest *absolute* risk (hence the Rec 45 annual dermatology exam). Age-appropriate USPSTF screening still applies (Rec 44) — see [[liver-transplantation]] for the full surveillance schedule.

## Therapeutics

- **First-line: reduction of immunosuppression.**
- **If no clinical response within 2–4 weeks**, add:
  - **Anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody (rituximab)**,
  - **surgical therapy**,
  - **radiation therapy**, and/or
  - **cytotoxic chemotherapy**.
- **Antiviral therapy** — addition has **not** been proven to affect outcomes.

## Guideline Recommendations

**AASLD/AST 2012:**

> **58.** PTLD should be considered in LT recipients (especially high-risk individuals) who present with unexplained fever, lymphadenopathy, or cytopenias (grade 1, level A).
>
> **59.** Although EBV may be associated with the development of PTLD, the detection of EBV viremia is not diagnostic for PTLD; a histopathological diagnosis is required (grade 1, level A).

**AASLD/AST 2025:**

> **52.** Serial EBV PCR monitoring for PTLD is not routinely recommended (weak, level 3).
