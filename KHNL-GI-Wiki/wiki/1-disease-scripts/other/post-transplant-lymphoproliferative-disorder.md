Spectrum of lymphoid proliferations arising under immunosuppression after solid-organ transplant; an **uncommon but serious** de novo malignancy after [[liver-transplantation|liver transplant]] (LT), incidence **0.9–2.9%** ([[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]]) / **~2%** ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]]) in adults. Often, but not always, Epstein-Barr virus (EBV)–driven.

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Requires a high index of suspicion** — consider in LT recipients (especially high-risk individuals) presenting with **undiagnosed fever, unexplained lymphadenopathy, or cytopenias** ([[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]] Rec 58, *grade 1, level A*). The presentation is frequently **nonspecific or absent altogether**; when symptoms do appear they are vague — **fevers, night sweats, weight loss** ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]]).
- **Organs involved** — beyond nodes and marrow, disturbances of the **GI tract, lungs, spleen, and CNS**. Imaging may show **bulky lymphadenopathy**.
- **Histopathological diagnosis is required, and EBV viremia is NOT diagnostic** — detection of EBV by nucleic acid testing supports suspicion but cannot establish the diagnosis; biopsy of involved tissue is mandatory ([[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]] Rec 59, *grade 1, level A*).
- **Timing — the two ingested sources frame it differently, and both windows matter:**
  - **Months 3–6 post-LT** is the period of maximal immunosuppression, when opportunistic pathogens (CMV, EBV, fungi) predominate ([[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]]).
  - **Most cases occur in the first 3 years after LT, but patients may present many years later** ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]] — newer, so this is the operative statement). *A late presentation does not argue against PTLD.*
- **Risk factors** — primary EBV infection, CMV donor–recipient mismatch or CMV disease, and augmented immunosuppression (especially anti-lymphocyte antibodies). **Patients who are EBV-negative at the time of LT are at the highest risk** ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]]). It is uncertain whether the etiology of the underlying liver disease influences PTLD development.

### Severity Assessment

- **Survival is >50% at 10 years.** The two stated **negative prognostic factors are older age and monomorphic type** ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]]) — which is also what escalates therapy (see [[#Therapeutics]]).

### Classification / Typing

- **EBV-associated vs EBV-negative** — the association with EBV is variable in adult LT recipients; **later-onset PTLD is less likely to be EBV-associated**.
- **Monomorphic vs non-monomorphic** is the only histologic split the ingested sources use, and it carries both the prognosis and the treatment decision (chemotherapy for monomorphic disease).
- ⚠ **Decision gap — the full histologic subclassification and staging are not on this page.** Neither ingested source ([[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]], [[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]]) gives the criteria that define the WHO PTLD categories (early lesions / polymorphic / monomorphic / classic Hodgkin-like) or a staging system — only the *name* "monomorphic". A hematology/oncology PTLD source would be needed; do not infer the categories from this page.

## Differential Diagnosis

*No dedicated diagnostic schema — because the presenting syndrome (fever, lymphadenopathy, cytopenias) overlaps with infection and rejection, tissue histopathology is what distinguishes PTLD.*

- Other **de novo post-transplant malignancy** / lymphoma (lymphoma relative risk ~10–30× post-LT).
- **Opportunistic infection** driving fever/cytopenias — CMV disease, EBV infection, fungal infection.
- **[[antibody-mediated-rejection-liver-transplant|Allograft rejection]]** and other causes of unexplained fever or cytopenias in the LT recipient.

## Diagnostics

- **Tissue histopathology** — diagnostic gold standard; biopsy of an involved site is required.
- **Radiographic studies** (CT/cross-sectional imaging) — identify sites of involvement, especially **pulmonary or intra-abdominal** disease.
- **EBV nucleic acid testing (viral load)** — may support suspicion but is **not diagnostic** of EBV-associated PTLD.
- **Do NOT screen with serial EBV PCR.** Routine serial EBV PCR monitoring for PTLD is **not recommended** in adult LT recipients ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]], Rec 52, *weak, level 3*) — many centers still do it, and the recommendation acknowledges the low evidence base. EBV testing stays a diagnostic adjunct in a symptomatic patient, not a surveillance test.
  - *The reasoning, stated by the guideline:* a **markedly elevated EBV viral load can identify patients at increased risk**, but the **role of EBV screening for PTLD is not well established** — so a high load raises suspicion without licensing a screening program.

**Where PTLD sits in post-LT cancer risk** ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]], Figure 3): overall post-LT cancer risk is **2.45-fold** elevated; **non-Hodgkin lymphoma carries the highest standardized incidence ratio (~10× the general population)**, while non-melanoma skin cancer carries the highest *absolute* risk (hence the Rec 45 annual dermatology exam). Age-appropriate USPSTF screening still applies (Rec 44) — see [[liver-transplantation]] for the full surveillance schedule.

## Therapeutics

- **First-line, all subtypes: reduction of [[calcineurin-inhibitors|immunosuppression]]** ([[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]], [[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]]).
- **Monomorphic PTLD: treat with chemotherapy, as for other lymphomas** ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]]) — the histologic subtype is what escalates therapy beyond immunosuppression reduction.
- **If no clinical response within 2–4 weeks**, add:
  - **Anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody (rituximab)**,
  - **surgical therapy**,
  - **radiation therapy**, and/or
  - **cytotoxic chemotherapy**.
- **Antiviral therapy** — addition has **not** been proven to affect outcomes.
