# Nonampullary Duodenal Adenoma

**Nonampullary duodenal adenomas (DAs)** are premalignant epithelial polyps of the duodenum that do **not** involve the major papilla (contrast [[ampullary-adenoma|ampullary adenoma]]). Nonampullary duodenal polyps are found in up to **5% of upper endoscopies** — mostly incidental in asymptomatic patients — and **adenomas make up 10%–20%** of them. DAs occur **sporadically** (up to 0.5% of patients) or with a polyposis syndrome ([[familial-adenomatous-polyposis|familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP)]], [[mutyh-associated-polyposis|*MUTYH*-associated polyposis]]). Most contain only low-grade dysplasia, but **up to 20% may progress to high-grade dysplasia (HGD) and ~5% to cancer**, so endoscopic resection is generally recommended.

*Governing source: [[aga-2025-nonampullary-duodenal|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2025 Clinical Practice Update (CPU) on Nonampullary Duodenal Lesions]] — the numbered **best practice advice (BPA)** statements throughout this page are its own and, as a CPU, carry **no Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) strength or evidence quality**. [[asge-2015-ampullary-duodenal-adenomas|American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2015]] (GRADE-rated) fills topics the CPU does not cover; where the two differ, AGA 2025 is newer and governs, and the difference is noted.*

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Nonampullary DA** = duodenal adenoma not involving the major papilla; must be distinguished from **non-neoplastic** and **subepithelial** mimics before resection.
- **Non-neoplastic lesions** (metaplastic foveolar epithelium, gastric heterotopia, inflammatory/pseudopolyps) may mimic adenoma — most frequent in the **duodenal bulb**. Careful optical evaluation + pathologic correlation may be needed to exclude dysplasia. **Nondysplastic lesions do not require resection unless symptomatic or bleeding.**
- **Biopsy before resection — the two guidelines differ.** AGA 2025: if there is concern about the underlying pathology, mucosal biopsies can be considered, but **caution if resection is likely** — biopsy can cause underlying fibrosis that makes later resection harder and riskier. ASGE 2015 (older): biopsies of **suspicious** lesions should be obtained and interpreted before attempted resection, since adenomas can look like nonadenomatous (e.g. inflammatory) polyps. AGA 2025 governs.
- In FAP, tissue confirmation is important, but **repeated or aggressive forceps biopsy and submucosal tattooing are discouraged** (interfere with future mucosectomy).
- **Duodenal tattoo injection is not required** (numerous anatomic landmarks; high risk of tattoo diffusion).

### Lesion Description & Risk Stratification

Describe every duodenal polyp by the following, to plan therapy and surveillance **(BPA 3):**

| Descriptor | Detail |
|---|---|
| Size | mm |
| [[polypectomy\|Paris]] morphology | e.g. 0-IIa, Is. *Full Paris criteria, with schematics and endoscopic examples, live on that page.* |
| Suspected histologic layer of origin | mucosal vs subepithelial lesion |
| Location | D1–D4 |
| Orientation | anterior, posterior, medial, or lateral wall |
| Papilla relationship | proximity/relationship to the **major papilla** (rule out ampullary involvement) |

- **Ideal inspection (BPA 2):** identify the **major and minor papilla** with **photodocumentation** to ensure no involvement by the lesion; scope choice → *Diagnostics* table.
- **Malignant transformation risk (BPA 7):** all sporadic DAs should be **considered for resection**, but vs colonic adenomas the time course to malignancy **may be more prolonged** and resection morbidity **much greater** → carefully factor in **comorbidities and anticipated longevity**.
  - Several studies: lesions initially **≥20 mm**, and those **proximal to the ampulla**, were associated with histologic progression.
  - One study of incidental adenomas (initially mostly low-grade): **20.9%** (9 of 43) progressed to HGD at 6 mo, including **4.7%** (n = 2) to carcinoma in situ.

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[subepithelial-lesion]].*

- **Non-neoplastic epithelial lesions:** metaplastic foveolar epithelium, gastric heterotopia, inflammatory/pseudopolyps (bulb-predominant; handling under *Establishing the Diagnosis*).
- **Brunner gland hamartoma:** uncommon, benign, often solitary pedunculated, up to 2–5 cm, from lobular proliferation of Brunner glands. Resection generally unnecessary unless symptomatic or large (>2 cm → obstruction, pain, bleeding); large lesions may cause gastrointestinal (GI) hemorrhage with [[iron-deficiency-anemia|iron deficiency]]; rare dysplasia.
- **Duodenal hamartoma:** associated with [[peutz-jeghers-syndrome|Peutz-Jeghers]], [[cowden-syndrome|Cowden]], [[juvenile-polyposis-syndrome|juvenile polyposis]]; solitary Peutz-Jeghers (PJ)-type polyps can occur **without** PJ syndrome — endoscopic resection generally recommended.
- **Subepithelial lesions:** [[gastroenteropancreatic-neuroendocrine-tumors|neuroendocrine tumor]], [[gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor|gastrointestinal stromal tumor (GIST)]], lipoma, leiomyoma.
- **Ampullary adenoma** — exclude papillary involvement.

## Diagnostics

| Modality | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Forward-viewing [[upper-endoscopy\|gastroscope]] + clear distal cap | Primary inspection/resection | Cap improves views of the papilla and medial wall (BPA 2) |
| Side-viewing duodenoscope | Papillae not seen with the gastroscope; most lesions on the **medial wall within 5 cm of the ampulla** (BPA 2) | Confirms no papillary involvement |
| Pediatric colonoscope | Posterior/lateral wall + distal duodenum | Added insertion length, favorable 5–6 o'clock working-channel orientation |
| [[endoscopic-ultrasound\|Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]] | Duodenal polyps with **features concerning for malignancy**, before endoscopic or surgical resection | ASGE 2015 Rec 2: *recommend* (moderate quality, ⊕⊕⊕○). Utility in small adenomas likely small; may change management of polyps **>2 cm** |
| [[colonoscopy]] | **Concomitant colonic neoplasia** | **Perform if no high-quality exam in the past 3 y (BPA 4)** — colonic neoplasia risk 2–7× the general population; in one study colonic polyps 61% vs 37% and advanced adenomas 18% vs 5% of controls. ASGE 2015 Rec 6 agrees: *suggest* offering screening colonoscopy for sporadic DAs (low quality, ⊕⊕○○) |
| [[capsule-endoscopy]] | Small-bowel survey | **Not advised** routinely for sporadic or nonsporadic DAs (BPA 5) — small-bowel polyp risk negligible. Periodic capsule may benefit **Peutz-Jeghers syndrome** |

## Therapeutics

**Endoscopic resection is the preferred treatment (BPA 6)** — less morbid, resource-intensive, and expensive than surgery. In a modeling study of 102 large DAs (mean 40 mm), endoscopic vs hypothetical surgical resection: morbidity 18% vs 31%, median hospital stay 1 vs 4.75 days, mean cost $11,093 vs $19,358. Technique is **individualized** to reduce bleeding risk, by lesion size, morphology, comorbidities, and endoscopist comfort (BPA 8).

> [!pearl] Finish the resection at the first session
> The initial attempt has the highest chance of success and the lowest complication risk; postresection fibrosis and scarring often force ablative or other higher-risk maneuvers and incomplete removal ([[aga-2025-nonampullary-duodenal]]).

### Resection Technique by Lesion

| Lesion | Technique (AGA 2025) |
|---|---|
| **Flat, nonbulky, <20 mm** | **Piecemeal cold-snare (CS) resection** — mitigates postprocedural bleeding, effective, minimal recurrence risk; **can be considered** with comorbidities (BPA 8). Most attractive for lesions **<15 mm**, particularly with significant comorbidities |
| **>20 mm, or large Paris subtype Is components** | **Conventional hot-snare [[endoscopic-mucosal-resection\|endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR)]]** (BPA 9); **thermal ablation of the post-EMR margin** to bring recurrence to **<2%–5%** is safe and effective and **should be considered** |
| **≤20 mm** | **Underwater EMR** (water immersion, no submucosal injection) — efficacy shown in two multicenter studies |

**Outcome data behind the choice** ([[aga-2025-nonampullary-duodenal]]):

| Technique | Population | Recurrence | Bleeding | Perforation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conventional hot-snare EMR | DAs of all sizes | 10%–15% at 6 mo, size-dependent | 25% postprocedural for lesions ≥30 mm | ~1% |
| EMR with thermal margin ablation (EMR-T) vs conventional EMR | 54 vs 125 DAs (median 30 mm) | **2.3%** (1/49) vs **17.6%** (19/108) | No difference in adverse events (not broken down) | — |
| Cold-snare EMR (CS-EMR) vs EMR-T | 50 large DAs (median 30 mm) | **24.4%** vs **2.3%** at 6 mo | Intraprocedural **2.0% vs 37%**; postpolypectomy **4.0% vs 16.7%** | 2 immediate (4.0%) with CS-EMR, clipped without sequelae |
| Underwater EMR | Lesions ≤20 mm | 2%–3% | Delayed <2% | <1% |

- CS resection almost completely eliminates bleeding risk, but **bulky DAs are not suitable** for it; its recurrence risk is related to primary-lesion size.
- **Clip closure** of the post-EMR defect is generally only effective in **defects ≤15 mm** (where bleeding risk is minimal). The thin, fixed retroperitoneal duodenal wall tears easily when the muscular layer is clipped → close **mucosa to mucosa**, avoid clip injury to the deep muscle.
- **General approach** (ASGE 2015): like colon polyps, particularly the right colon, because the duodenal wall is thin; a submucosal injection cushion may help remove flat polyps; EMR techniques have been described.
- **Giant (>3 cm) hemicircumferential laterally spreading tumors:** significantly higher adverse event rate — primarily bleeding — than lesions <3 cm (**26.3% vs 3.2%**) ([[asge-2015-ampullary-duodenal-adenomas]]).
- **Surgery** (ASGE 2015): larger lesions are harder to remove; lesions with **>33% circumferential involvement of the lumen should be considered for surgical resection**.
- **Minor papilla adenomas and [[pancreas-divisum|pancreas divisum]]:** dorsal pancreatic duct stenting during resection has been reported ([[asge-2015-ampullary-duodenal-adenomas]]).
- **Residual or recurrent superficial tissue:** adjuvant [[argon-plasma-coagulation|argon plasma coagulation (APC)]] or electrocoagulation ([[asge-2015-ampullary-duodenal-adenomas]]).

> [!pitfall] No lift on injection — suspect cancer or prior manipulation
> Lack of lifting suggests underlying malignancy or previous manipulation (biopsies, attempted removal or ablation) ([[asge-2015-ampullary-duodenal-adenomas]]).

> [!pitfall] Endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) is not for duodenal adenomas
> AGA 2025: [[endoscopic-submucosal-dissection|ESD]] offers no clear advantage over piecemeal EMR (covert adenocarcinoma in a DA is rare, ≤1%) and the thin muscularis propria brings high perforation rates. ASGE 2015: duodenal ESD, **especially beyond the bulb, is not recommended** — perforation as high as 23% and 35% in two high-volume Japanese and Korean series; the same Japanese group reported no perforations with EMR.

### Complications & Periprocedural Care

- **Postprocedural bleeding (BPA 10):** **higher risk than elsewhere in the GI tract**, usually in the **first 48 h**, risk **proportional to lesion size** — **>25% for lesions >3 cm**, may be life-threatening with hemodynamic compromise. After resuscitation, [[endoscopic-hemostasis|endoscopic hemostasis]] is generally effective.
- **Perforation (BPA 11):** evaluate the **postpolypectomy/EMR defect** — an unrecognized, untreated perforation may be life-threatening and often **mandates surgery**. Duodenal perforations are less easily recognized at resection than gastric or colonic ones, and the delay raises the risk of surgery and morbidity ([[asge-2015-ampullary-duodenal-adenomas]]).
- **High-risk patients — admission for observation should be strongly considered** (25% postprocedural bleeding risk): multiple comorbidities · need to promptly restart antiplatelet/anticoagulation · large bulky lesions **≥30 mm** · significant bleeding during resection · possible perforation during resection treated with endoscopic closure.
- **After resection** ([[aga-2025-nonampullary-duodenal]]):
  - Diet: generally **clear liquids overnight**, then advanced as tolerated
  - **Twice-daily [[proton-pump-inhibitors|proton pump inhibitor (PPI)]] for 6–8 weeks**
  - [[anticoagulation-gi-bleeding|Anticoagulation/antiplatelet therapy]]: timing of resumption individualized to the indication, with a risk–benefit discussion; **if acceptable, cessation for at least 48 h after resection should be considered**
  - Insufficient data to support routine prophylactic hemostatic spray or other topical agents
- *ASGE 2015 (older) describes one expert group's protocol instead — observation 4 h before discharge, clear liquids that day and normal diet the next, antiplatelet agents held 7 days, twice-daily PPI for 2 weeks. AGA 2025 governs.*

### Surveillance

- **Initial surveillance at 6 months (BPA 12)** for a completely resected DA. Recurrence is usually diminutive but **often scarred and not amenable to conventional snare resection** — may require **avulsion techniques** to achieve cure. The short interval reflects how hard recurrence can be to manage, particularly with a stricture from previous extensive resection; timing data are scarce (expert opinion).
- After a **negative 6-month exam:** the authors' general practice is to repeat at **1 year** and, if clear, **annually for 2–3 years**.
- *ASGE 2015 (older) recommended a surveillance program after endoscopic removal (Rec 5, moderate quality, ⊕⊕⊕○) but, on limited data, gave **no interval** — individualized to adequacy of resection, degree of dysplasia and comorbidities.*

### FAP-Associated Duodenal Adenomas

- Upwards of **90% of patients with FAP** eventually have one or many DAs. They tend to **spare the duodenal bulb** and mostly involve the **descending duodenum**, with **decreasing severity distally** (distal duodenum, jejunum, ileum). Most are **flat and may be very subtle**.
- **Consider resection (BPA 13)** based on **size (≥1 cm)**, morphologic characteristics, **advanced histology (HGD)**, and/or **Spiegelman criteria**.
  - The mucosal field defect makes permanent, complete polyp elimination unrealistic → aim to **control the disease** and remove all larger or morphologically advanced lesions.
  - Most endoscopists removed only lesions >1 cm; cold-snare polypectomy has led many to also remove **5–10 mm** lesions (to stop them coalescing) and multiple lesions in one session.
  - Polyp removal may **down-stage** the Spiegelman score.
- **Duodenal cancer** develops in ~4% of FAP patients overall. **Risk factors: Spiegelman stage IV, HGD, or lesion size ≥10 mm.** Too many lesions, significant dysplasia or a large lesion should raise attention and may warrant surveillance **every 6–12 months plus surgical referral** for definitive management.
- **Surveillance** may begin at **age 25**, interval by polyp characteristics (Spiegelman stage). [[device-assisted-enteroscopy|Deep enteroscopy]] or capsule endoscopy is usually reserved for **advanced duodenal polyposis** or polyps obviously extending to the **ligament of Treitz**. **Chromoendoscopy** increases detection and upstages severity, but its clinical value is not established.

**Spiegelman scoring** — number, size, histology and dysplasia, each scored 1–3 points, summed to stage **0 = 0 · I = 1–4 · II = 5–6 · III = 7–8 · IV = 9–12**. The points table lives on [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis#Duodenal Surveillance and Spigelman Staging|familial adenomatous polyposis]]. AGA 2025 Table 1 scores dysplasia mild / moderate / severe = 1 / 2 / 3.

**Adenocarcinoma risk and surveillance by stage — AGA 2025 Table 1:**

| Stage | Spiegelman score | Surveillance (AGA 2025) | Adenocarcinoma risk, % |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | 4 y | — |
| I | 1–4 | 2–3 y | — |
| II | 5–6 | 2–3 y | 2.3 |
| III | 7–8 | 6–12 mo | 2.4 |
| IV | 9–12 | Surgical consult, 6–12 mo | 36 |

> [!pitfall] Stage intervals differ between guidelines — check the FAP page
> The newer stage-by-stage intervals on [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis#Duodenal Surveillance and Spigelman Staging|familial adenomatous polyposis]] differ from AGA 2025 Table 1 and govern. The older [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2015]] Rec 10 set stage II at every 1–3 y and stage IV at surgical evaluation, starting upper endoscopy at age 25–30.
