Inherited cause of recurrent [[acute-pancreatitis|acute]] and [[chronic-pancreatitis|chronic pancreatitis]]. Classic form is **autosomal-dominant PRSS1** (cationic trypsinogen) disease; sits in the **G (Genetic)** arm of the TIGAR-O (toxic-metabolic, idiopathic, genetic, autoimmune, recurrent and severe acute pancreatitis, obstructive) v2.0 checklist ([[chronic-pancreatitis|see chronic pancreatitis]]). Carries the **highest relative risk of [[pancreatic-cancer|pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma]] of any hereditary pancreatic-cancer condition except [[peutz-jeghers-syndrome|Peutz-Jeghers]]** ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]]).

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- Suspect in **[[recurrent-acute-pancreatitis|recurrent acute pancreatitis (RAP)]] or chronic pancreatitis (CP) with unclear etiology, young age at onset, or a family history**.
- **Genetic testing is recommended in patients with clinical evidence of a pancreatitis-associated disorder or possible CP in which the etiology is unclear, especially in younger patients** (Rec 4, *strong recommendation, low quality of evidence*) ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]]).
- **Any one of these indicates testing** ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]]):
  - Etiology unclear.
  - Family history of pancreatic disease.
  - Disease persists after therapeutic intervention (e.g., RAP after the biliary system has been cleared).
  - Young patient — the text anchors "younger" at **<35 years of age**.
- Sits at **level B** of the American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) mechanistic diagnostic algorithm — family history, genetics, sweat chloride; level A is typical clinical, imaging and serum findings, level C is IgG4 and response to therapy ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]], Figure 2).
- In a patient **with pancreatic cancer**, genetic counseling for hereditary pancreatitis testing is warranted (expert opinion) for a personal history of **≥2 attacks of acute pancreatitis of unknown etiology, a family history of pancreatitis, or early-age-onset CP** ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]]).
- In acute pancreatitis, **genetic testing may be useful when more than 1 family member has pancreatic disease**; how to use genetic defects in most patients with idiopathic pancreatitis is not clear, and true recurrent idiopathic acute pancreatitis should be evaluated at a center of excellence offering advanced endoscopy, genetic testing and multidisciplinary care ([[acg-2024-acute-pancreatitis]]).

> [!pearl] In end-stage disease, test for the family's sake
> Genetic testing is of limited value once CP is end-stage — the inflammatory process has already irreversibly destroyed the pancreas. Exceptions: identifying a familial gene so relatives can make clinical decisions, explaining an etiology beyond alcohol and smoking, or research participation ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]]).

### Severity Assessment

- Grade established disease with **M-ANNHEIM** scoring; morphology by **Cambridge** grade — both live on [[chronic-pancreatitis]].
- **Progression rate:** genetic/idiopathic etiology progresses from acute pancreatitis (AP) → CP at **roughly half the rate of alcoholic CP** (alcoholic CP progresses ~2× faster than genetic/idiopathic and ~5× faster than biliary) ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]]).

### Classification / Typing

**Where hereditary pancreatitis sits in TIGAR-O v2.0** — the Genetic (G) arm is subdivided, and only one sub-arm is hereditary pancreatitis ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]], Table 5):

| Genetic sub-arm | Defining finding |
|---|---|
| Suspected | No or limited genotyping available |
| **Autosomal dominant** (Mendelian — single-gene syndrome) | **PRSS1 mutations = hereditary pancreatitis** |
| Autosomal recessive (Mendelian — single-gene syndrome) | CFTR, 2 severe variants in trans (cystic fibrosis) · CFTR, <2 severe variants in trans (CFTR-related disorder) · SPINK1, 2 pathogenic variants in trans (SPINK1-associated familial pancreatitis) |
| Complex genetics | Non-Mendelian — complex genotypes plus environment |
| Modifier genes | PRSS1-PRSS1 locus · CLDN2 locus · others |
| Hypertriglyceridemia | Pathogenic genetic variants listed |

Pathogenic genes implicated in CP, grouped as the guideline groups them ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]], Table 7):

| Gene | Inheritance pattern | Molecular / functional consequence | Clinical manifestation |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Acinar cell dysfunction* | | | |
| **PRSS1** (cationic trypsinogen) | Complex, low penetrance | Misfolding, endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress | RAP and CP |
| CPA1 (carboxypeptidase A1) | Complex, low penetrance | Misfolding, ER stress | High relative risk of nonalcoholic CP |
| CEL (carboxyl ester lipase) | Autosomal dominant complex | Misfolding / aggregation, ER stress | Maturity-onset diabetes of the young type 8 (MODY-8) pancreatitis |
| SPINK1 (serine protein inhibitor Kazal type 1) | Autosomal recessive | Loss of protein function — active site of trypsin blocked | 2% of the population; homozygotes with more severe disease |
| CTRC (chymotrypsin C) | Autosomal dominant | Activated trypsin resistant to degradation | Early-onset pancreatitis |
| *Ductal cell dysfunction* | | | |
| CFTR | Autosomal recessive | Loss of protein function — ductal bicarbonate secretion fails | 2,000 gene variants; widely divergent clinical presentation |
| *Disease-modifying genes* | | | |
| CASR (calcium-sensing receptor) | Autosomal recessive | Loss of ability to regulate intracellular calcium | Gain of function affects acinar cells; loss of function affects ductal cells |
| CTRC | Complex | Low-expression effect potentiated by smoking | Pancreatitis when present with a trypsin-activating variant |
| CLDN2 locus (claudin-2) | Unknown | High risk with alcoholism | Accelerates transition from RAP to CP |

- The two tables label PRSS1 differently: TIGAR-O places PRSS1 hereditary pancreatitis in the **autosomal-dominant** arm, while the gene table gives its inheritance pattern as **complex, low penetrance**. [[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening|American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2022]] names the screened population "autosomal-dominant hereditary pancreatitis."
- **Minimum testing panel:** at minimum, idiopathic CP is evaluated for **PRSS1, SPINK1, CFTR and CTRC**; extended panels covering over a dozen susceptibility and modifier genes, hypertriglyceridemia genes and pharmacogenetics are available ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]]).

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[recurrent-acute-pancreatitis]].*

- [[chronic-pancreatitis|Chronic pancreatitis]] of other TIGAR-O etiology — toxic-metabolic (alcohol, smoking, hypercalcemia, hypertriglyceridemia), autoimmune ([[autoimmune-pancreatitis|autoimmune pancreatitis (AIP)]] type 1/2), obstructive ([[pancreas-divisum|pancreas divisum]], ampullary stenosis).
- **Idiopathic early-onset CP (<35 yr)** — the principal alternative in a young patient; distinguished only by genetic testing.
- Recurrent acute pancreatitis from biliary, post-[[ercp|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]], or traumatic causes.
- [[pancreatic-cancer|Pancreatic adenocarcinoma]] — must be considered with any new stricture/mass, new diabetes, or weight loss.
- [[pancreatic-cysts|Intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm (IPMN)]] — ductal dilation can mimic CP.

## Diagnostics

- **Genetic testing** — minimum panel PRSS1, SPINK1, CFTR, CTRC (above).
- **Cancer risk quantification** — hereditary pancreatitis (HP):
  - **53-fold relative risk (RR)** of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, attributed to PRSS1 — the highest of the hereditary syndromes except [[peutz-jeghers-syndrome|Peutz-Jeghers syndrome (PJS)]] at up to 132-fold ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]], Table 12).
  - **Lifetime risk of pancreatic adenocarcinoma 5%–10%** in patients with well-characterized genetic polymorphisms putting them at risk for CP; data limited to case series and retrospective cohort studies ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]]).
  - **Surveillance threshold:** expert opinion holds that an RR **>5-fold** versus the general population (whose estimated US lifetime risk was 1.4% in 2013) warrants consideration for pancreatic cancer surveillance ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]]).
  - *The full cross-syndrome relative-risk table — PJS, HP, [[fammm-syndrome|familial atypical multiple mole melanoma (FAMMM)]], [[lynch-syndrome|Lynch]], [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis|familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP)]], [[brca-pathogenic-variants|BRCA2]], ATM — lives on [[pancreatic-cancer-screening]].*
- Imaging, endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) (Rosemont criteria), and pancreatic function testing follow the general [[chronic-pancreatitis]] pathway — not HP-specific; the Rosemont parenchymal/ductal feature list lives there.

## Therapeutics

### Pancreatic Cancer Surveillance

**Start at age 40** for autosomal-dominant hereditary pancreatitis — **annual**, by **[[endoscopic-ultrasound|EUS]], EUS alternating with [[mri-mrcp|magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)]], or MRI**, based on patient preference and available expertise ([[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening]], Recs 2/4/6i; Recs 2 and 4 conditional, very low quality — Rec 6 carries no grade).

> [!pitfall] No "10 years earlier" shortcut in hereditary pancreatitis
> Autosomal-dominant hereditary pancreatitis is the only condition in the ASGE 2022 start-age list given a flat age — **40** — with no "or 10 years earlier than the youngest relative with pancreatic cancer" modifier that the other eight conditions carry ([[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening]], Rec 6i).

- **Who qualifies without an affected relative:** HP, PJS and FAMMM carriers are offered surveillance as known mutation carriers. For BRCA1, BRCA2, PALB2, ATM and Lynch — lower RR — ACG 2015 limits surveillance to carriers with a **first- or second-degree relative affected with pancreatic cancer** (Rec 22, conditional / very low quality).
- **Positive surveillance finding:** a pancreatic cystic lesion found on surveillance of a hereditary pancreatic-cancer-prone family member requires evaluation by centers experienced with these high-risk individuals; when surgery is required is best individualized after multidisciplinary assessment ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]], Rec 24, conditional / low quality).
- Counsel on **harms before enrolling**: across screened cohorts, low-yield pancreatic surgery occurred in 2.8% of all screened but **46.6% of those operated**, with a **19.9% perioperative adverse-event rate** among operated patients. No adverse events from screening EUS or MRI. See [[pancreatic-cancer-screening]] for the full framework.

**Sources differ on the start age and on whether to screen at all:**

| Source | Position |
|---|---|
| [[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening\|ASGE 2022]] (guideline, newest) | Screen autosomal-dominant hereditary pancreatitis annually from **age 40** |
| [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer\|ACG 2015]] (guideline, older) | EUS and/or MRI of the pancreas **annually starting at age 50**, or 10 years younger than the earliest age of pancreatic cancer in the family (PJS starts at 35); surveillance ideally at **experienced centers, multidisciplinary, under research conditions** (Recs 22–23, conditional / very low quality) |
| [[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis\|ACG 2020]] (guideline) | "There is a lack of evidence to suggest that performing screening examinations on patients with CP to detect pancreatic malignancy is beneficial" (Key Concept 5, ungraded) — the supporting text applies this to patients at high risk from genetic risk factors as well |

Per source priority (guidelines tier; newer publication date wins), **ASGE 2022's age 40 governs this page**. ACG 2020 found no definitive benefit to screening patients with CP for pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, citing the invasive and costly nature of testing, the difficulty of screening a structurally abnormal gland, and the frequent inability to alter the natural history even when malignancy is detected early.

### Disease Management

- **Low-fat diet** — recommended in hereditary pancreatitis specifically, in addition to smoking cessation ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]]).
- **Smoking cessation** — strong recommendation, very low quality of evidence, in CP ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]]); all high-risk individuals in pancreatic-cancer-prone families should be counseled against smoking ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]]). Smoking also potentiates the disease-modifying CTRC effect.
- **Alcohol cessation** — strong recommendation, very low quality of evidence, in CP ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]]).
- Nothing else is hereditary-pancreatitis-specific: pain, [[exocrine-pancreatic-insufficiency|exocrine insufficiency]] and pancreatic enzyme replacement therapy, type 3c diabetes monitoring, [[celiac-plexus-block|celiac plexus block]] and total pancreatectomy with islet autotransplantation follow [[chronic-pancreatitis]].

### Family Counseling

- **Refer to a genetic counselor** in most instances; genetic referral is not necessary in centers where experienced nongeneticist clinicians are comfortable ordering and evaluating the results ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]]).
- Identifying the genetic etiology resolves the cause of poorly defined symptoms, connects patients to disease-specific support networks, informs prognosis and comorbidities, and **facilitates family planning** ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]]).
