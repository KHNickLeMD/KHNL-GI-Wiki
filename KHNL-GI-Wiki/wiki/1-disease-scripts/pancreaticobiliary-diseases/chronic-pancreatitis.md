## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- Chronic pancreatitis (CP) = **pathologic fibroinflammatory syndrome of the pancreas** in a patient with risk factors who develops persistent pathologic responses to parenchymal injury or stress (**mechanistic** definition) — allows dx **before end-stage morphology**, unlike the older **clinicopathologic** definition, which required **irreversible morphologic change**.
- **Cardinal features:** atrophy, fibrosis, ductal distortion/strictures, calcifications · pain (most common symptom) · [[exocrine-pancreatic-insufficiency|exocrine pancreatic insufficiency (EPI)]] · endocrine dysfunction (type 3c diabetes mellitus [DM]) · dysplasia (↑ ductal adenocarcinoma risk).

**Mechanistic algorithm (Fig 2) — 3 escalating levels of investigation:**

![[chronic-pancreatitis-2020-mechanistic-algorithm-12.png|700x369]]
*Figure 2 — Conceptual diagnostic algorithm based on the mechanistic definition. Rows a–c = escalating levels of investigation; TIGAR-O (toxic-metabolic, idiopathic, genetic, autoimmune, recurrent and severe acute pancreatitis, obstructive) risk factors guide each level. ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]])*

- **A — clinical:** pain, maldigestion, weight loss, glucose intolerance + TIGAR-O risk factors → CT, [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]] ± fine-needle aspiration (FNA), amylase/lipase, triglycerides, immunoglobulin G4 (IgG4), glucose, fat-soluble vitamins, tumor markers
- **B — risk/phenotype:** family history, early onset, cystic fibrosis (CF) organ involvement, syndromic features → genetic testing, sweat chloride, secretin-enhanced MRCP (sMRCP), exocrine function testing
- **C — response/histology:** [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]]/IgG4 disease, response to analgesics or pancreatic enzyme replacement therapy (PERT), steroid trial for autoimmune pancreatitis (AIP) type 2 → histology

### Severity Assessment (M-ANNHEIM)

| Domain | Points |
|---|---|
| Pain (none–continuous) | 0–4 |
| Pain control (none–potent opioids/endoscopic intervention) | 0–2 |
| Surgical intervention (any) | 4 |
| EPI (absent / mild / proven steatorrhea) | 0–2 |
| Endocrine insufficiency (DM present) | 0 or 4 |
| Imaging morphology (Cambridge: normal–marked) | 0–4 |
| Severe organ complications (none / reversible / irreversible) | 0–2–4 |

**Severity bands:** A Minor 0–5 · B Increased 6–10 · C Advanced 11–15 · D Marked 16–20 · E Exacerbated >20.

#### Imaging morphology grade (Cambridge)

**Pancreas Morphology Imaging Grade (I–IV)** — what the M-ANNHEIM imaging row and the evidence tiers below are scored from (American Pancreatic Association [APA] 2014, Table 6; [[apa-2014-chronic-pancreatitis]]).

| Modality | Equivocal (I) | Mild (II) | Moderate (III) | Marked (IV) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CT / magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)** — transabdominal ultrasound (US) graded the same as CT | **1** of: main duct enlarged **2–4 mm**; slight gland enlargement; heterogeneous parenchyma; small cavities **<10 mm**; irregular ducts; focal pancreatitis; increased echogenicity of main-duct wall; irregular head/body contour | **≥2** of the same list | Cannot be distinguished from mild | Moderate changes **plus ≥1** of: large cavities **>10 mm**, gland enlargement, intraductal filling defects/calculi, duct obstruction, stricture, or gross irregularity |
| **EUS** (conventional 0–9 criteria) | 0–2 features | 3–4 features | ≥5 features | *not separately defined in Table 6* |
| **MRCP / [[ercp\|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]]** | **<3** abnormal side-branch changes | **≥3** abnormal side-branch changes | Abnormal main duct **+ >3** abnormal side branches | Moderate changes **plus 1** of: obstruction, filling defects, severe irregularity/dilation of the main duct |

**Abnormal duct on pancreatography** ([[apa-2014-chronic-pancreatitis]], Cambridge criteria):

- **Normal** main-duct diameter averages **3.6 / 2.7 / 1.6 mm** (head / body / tail); **upper limit of normal reported as high as 6.5 / 5 / 3 mm**.
- Dilation is **general** (>⅔ of the main duct) or **local** (<⅔). **Severe dilation = >1 cm.**
- Main duct: dilation, narrowing, strictures, filling defects, leaks/cavities. Side branches: decreased number, shortened length, dilated or narrowed caliber — APA 2014 does not define the normal/abnormal boundary for side branches.

> [!pitfall] Never diagnose CP off the pancreatogram alone
> **81%** of a post-mortem series **without** CP had "abnormal" pancreatograms; age-related change confounds. Pair ductal findings with parenchymal change, calcification, or abnormal physiology. ([[apa-2014-chronic-pancreatitis]])

#### Diagnostic evidence tiers

Apply only when history/exam is suspicious for CP — pain, prior [[acute-pancreatitis|acute pancreatitis (AP)]], PERT-responsive steatorrhea, heavy alcohol/smoking, genetic or family risk.

| Tier | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **Definitive** | **Any one of:** moderate/marked imaging morphology (ductal **and** parenchymal abnormalities) · pancreatic calcifications · histologic confirmation |
| **Probable** | (**Mild** imaging morphology **or** recurrent pseudocyst/pancreatitis) **plus** abnormal pancreatic physiology (secretin test, diabetes, steatorrhea) |
| **Insufficient** | **Equivocal** imaging morphology, **or** abdominal pain with any of: no history of pancreatitis (lipase <3× upper limit of normal [ULN]), normal imaging, family history of pancreatitis, prior ERCP with pancreatic duct (PD) stenting, presence of TIGAR-O risk factors (smoking, alcohol) |

### Classification — TIGAR-O v2.0

**T**oxic-metabolic · **I**diopathic · **G**enetic · **A**utoimmune · **R**ecurrent AP · **O**bstructive

- **Toxic-metabolic:** alcohol (3–4/d; ≥5/d), smoking (pack-years), hypercalcemia (ionized calcium [iCa] **>12 mg/dL**), hypertriglyceridemia (fasting **>300** / nonfasting **>500**), medications
- **Idiopathic:** early-onset (**<35 yr**) vs late-onset (**>35 yr**)
- **Genetic:** PRSS1 (autosomal dominant [AD], [[hereditary-pancreatitis|hereditary pancreatitis]]), SPINK1 (autosomal recessive [AR], severe), CFTR (cystic fibrosis or CFTR-related disorder [CFTR-RD]), CTRC, CASR, CLDN2 (alcohol accelerator), CPA1, CEL (maturity-onset diabetes of the young type 8 [MODY-8])
- **Autoimmune:** AIP type 1 (IgG4-related) / type 2 (IBD-associated)
- **Recurrent (RAP) and severe (SAP) acute pancreatitis:** biliary, post-ERCP, traumatic, undetermined
- **Obstructive:** [[pancreas-divisum|pancreas divisum]], ampullary stenosis, main-duct stones, calcifications, strictures, mass

*Full gene table (inheritance, mechanism, phenotype): [[hereditary-pancreatitis]].*

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup of the recurrent acute episodes that precede CP: see [[recurrent-acute-pancreatitis]].*

- [[acute-pancreatitis]] — acute flares; RAP precedes 30% (≈60% of CP evolves from AP/RAP)
- **[[pancreatic-cancer|Pancreatic adenocarcinoma]]** — new stricture/mass; consider in any new-onset CP, especially older with new diabetes + weight loss
- **[[autoimmune-pancreatitis|Autoimmune pancreatitis]]** — type 1 (IgG4-related; sausage pancreas, IgG4↑, steroid-responsive) vs type 2 (IBD-associated, younger)
- [[biliary-stricture]] — CP = most common benign cause of extrahepatic stricture
- **[[pancreas-divisum|Pancreas divisum]] / ampullary stenosis** — consider in idiopathic CP
- **Intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm (IPMN) / [[pancreatic-cysts|pancreatic cysts]]** — duct dilation mimics CP
- Long-standing diabetes · renal disease · cyclosporine · age-related atrophy/fibrosis

## Diagnostics

### Imaging

| Modality | Sensitivity (95% confidence interval [CI]) | Specificity (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|
| [[endoscopic-ultrasound\|EUS]] | **81%** (70–89) | 90% (82–95) |
| MRI | 78% (69–85) | **96%** (90–98) |
| CT | 75% (66–83) | 91% (81–96) |
| ERCP | not reported | 94% (87–98) |
| Transabdominal US | not reported | **98%** (89–100) |

*Meta-analysis, suspected CP. **Sensitivities did not differ significantly** — choose on specificity, invasiveness and availability ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]]).*

- **CT — first-line:** calcifications (pathognomonic but late), ductal dilation (**>3 mm**), atrophy, pseudocysts.
- **MRI / [[mri-mrcp|MRCP]]:** ductal anatomy and side branches; **sMRCP** for subtle ductal change — next step when cross-sectional imaging is non-diagnostic.
- **EUS:** most sensitive for early/minimal-change CP, least specific — only when CT/MRCP are non-diagnostic and suspicion persists; **never the sole basis for diagnosis**. Graded by the conventional criteria and the **Rosemont classification** below.
- **Histology** = gold standard; limited by sampling and subjectivity.

#### Rosemont classification (EUS)

Features scored **Major A**, **Major B** or **minor**; combinations give four categories. *Feature definitions, size cut-offs, and the conventional 9-feature list with its duct diameters: [[endoscopic-ultrasound]].*

| Category | Qualifying combinations |
|---|---|
| **Consistent with CP** | **2 Major A** · **or** 1 Major A **+** Major B · **or** 1 Major A **+ ≥3 minor** |
| **Suggestive of CP** † | 1 Major A **+ <3 minor** · **or** Major B **+ ≥3 minor** · **or** **≥5 minor (any)** |
| **Indeterminate for CP** † | **3–4 minor, no major** · **or** **Major B alone** or with **<3 minor** |
| **Normal** | **≤2 minor ‡, no major features** |

- † **Not diagnoses — both need confirmation** by another study (ERCP, CT, MRI, pancreatic function testing); pair with fecal elastase or sMRCP. "Consistent with CP" counts only in a compatible clinical picture.
- ‡ ⚠ **The "normal" count excludes four minor features** — **cysts, dilated main pancreatic duct (MPD), hyperechoic non-shadowing foci, dilated side branches.** They do not count toward the ≤2, so a patient with three of them and nothing else still reads **normal**.
- Unvalidated expert opinion, low specificity (aging, anatomic variants, asymptomatic fibrosis all generate features). APA posture: **err toward stricter criteria**, accepting missed dx, to avoid over-diagnosis and risky therapy ([[apa-2014-chronic-pancreatitis]], [[catalano-2009-rosemont-classification]]).

### Pancreatic function tests

| Test | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cholecystokinin (CCK) stimulation | Hormonal (acinar) | Detects subtle EPI; cumbersome (Dreiling tube) |
| Secretin stimulation (endoscopic pancreatic function test, ePFT) | Hormonal (ductal) | Measures bicarbonate; not widely available |
| Fecal elastase-1 | Nonhormonal | Easiest, universally available. **Cutoff drives accuracy: <200 µg/g = high false-positive rate; <100 µg/g ↑ specificity, ↓ sensitivity** ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]]). Watery stool and small-bowel disease further limit specificity ([[apa-2014-chronic-pancreatitis]]) |
| ¹³C-mixed triglyceride breath test | Nonhormonal | Sensitivity ~90%; 4–6 hr; limited availability |
| Serum trypsinogen/trypsin | Nonhormonal | Easy; nonspecific — rises with pancreatic pain |

### Genetic testing

Indicated when etiology is unclear — especially **early onset (<35 yr)**, family history, or pediatric/young adult ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]]). Minimum panel: **PRSS1, SPINK1, CFTR, CTRC**; extended panels available.

### Other labs

- Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c), fasting glucose annually — type 3c DM
- Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K), B12, zinc, magnesium
- IgG4 — exclude AIP type 1

## Therapeutics

### Lifestyle

- **Alcohol cessation** (Strong / Very Low) — reduces AP recurrence ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]])
- **Smoking cessation** (Strong / Very Low) — slows calcification progression

### Pain management

| Step | Intervention | Grade / detail |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Lifestyle (alcohol + smoking cessation) + **non-opioid analgesics** | — |
| **2** | **Antioxidants** | Cond/Moderate. Selenium 600 µg · ascorbic acid 0.54 g · β-carotene 9000 IU · α-tocopherol 270 IU · methionine 2 g **daily**. Modest, mainly early disease |
| **3** | Adjuncts — **pregabalin/gabapentin, tricyclic antidepressant (TCA)** | Extrapolated from chronic-pain literature |
| **4** | **[[celiac-plexus-block\|Celiac plexus block]]** | Cond/Very Low — see below |
| **5** | **Endoscopic decompression** — ERCP ± extracorporeal shock wave lithotripsy (ESWL), stricture dilation, PD stent | First-line for *obstructive* CP |
| **6** | **Surgical drainage** — Puestow / Frey / Beger / Whipple | Strong/Moderate. **Superior to endoscopy** for long-term pain in obstructive CP; fallback if endoscopy fails |
| **7** | **Opiates** | Only when all else is exhausted (Key Concept 7) |
| **8** | **TPIAT** (total pancreatectomy + islet autotransplant) | Highly selected, refractory pain (Key Concept 8) |
| — | *Research only:* spinal cord stimulation, transmagnetic stimulation, radiation | Key Concept 9 |

- **Step 4 — who the block is for:** **nonobstructive (small-duct)** CP unresponsive to medical therapy with **no endoscopic or surgical decompression option**, or opioid side effects / wish to avoid opioids. **Not routine**; an obstructed main duct goes to step 5/6 instead. Candidacy, consent expectations, efficacy, technique: [[celiac-plexus-block]]. ([[asge-2024-chronic-pancreatitis-endoscopy|American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2024]] Rec 2, conditional/low)
- ⚠ **Active alcohol use = relative contraindication to elective interventional procedures** (Key Concept 6); urgent procedures for complications still warranted.

> [!pitfall] Enzymes do not treat pancreatic pain
> PERT is for the complications of EPI only — it does **not** improve pain (Rec 9, conditional/low). ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]])

### Endoscopic vs surgical therapy

*Governing source: [[asge-2024-chronic-pancreatitis-endoscopy|ASGE 2024]] (all six recommendations **conditional**). [[aga-2022-recurrent-pancreatitis-endoscopy|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2022]] agrees on direction but **is superseded on stent number**.*

> [!pearl] Ask the surgeon before the first ERCP
> **No contraindication to surgery → surgical evaluation *before* initiating endoscopic management** (more durable pain relief). Contraindication to surgery, or patient prefers a less-invasive modality → **endoscopic management first** (Rec 1). ([[asge-2024-chronic-pancreatitis-endoscopy]])

- **Multidisciplinary approach strongly recommended** after failed medical pain management — confirm access to an expert endoscopist, a pancreatic surgeon and lithotripsy before choosing a route.
- Surgery is one-time and gives **more durable** pain relief; endotherapy needs serial ERCPs over up to 12 mo ([[aga-2022-recurrent-pancreatitis-endoscopy]], best practice advice [BPA] 4).
- Decompression is **almost never indicated in asymptomatic patients** — rare exception: young patient with unifocal obstruction downstream of viable parenchyma, to slow islet-cell loss ([[aga-2022-recurrent-pancreatitis-endoscopy]]).

**Which intervention for which lesion** (ASGE 2024 Recs 3–6). *Cut-offs, stent diameters/intervals, technique: [[ercp]].*

| Lesion | Chosen intervention |
|---|---|
| **MPD stones** (~60% of CP; treat only for symptomatic obstruction) | ERCP ± pancreatoscopy **or** ESWL — chosen by stone **size, location and radiopacity** plus local expertise |
| **MPD stricture** | **Single** plastic stent, not multiple, upsized at serial ERCPs; **against** routine fully covered self-expanding metal stents (FCSEMS) |
| **CP-related benign [[biliary-stricture\|biliary stricture]]** (~15% of advanced CP) | ERCP + stent, **FCSEMS over multiple plastic stents**; treat when **symptomatic or unresolved at 4 weeks** → prevents secondary biliary [[cirrhosis\|cirrhosis]]. ⚠ Plastic if **indeterminate**, or if FCSEMS would occlude the cystic duct with gallbladder in situ ([[acute-cholecystitis\|cholecystitis]] risk) |
| **Pseudocyst** | **Endoscopic over surgical** drainage; percutaneous not as sole therapy (fistula risk) — [[pancreatic-fluid-collection-drainage]] |

> ⚠ **Contradiction on the record — PD stent number and FCSEMS.** AGA 2022 BPA 6 advised 6–12 mo of plastic stenting with **upsizing or multiple parallel stents**, FCSEMS "emerging" (fewer ERCPs, equivalent pain relief, **adverse events 39% vs 14%**). ASGE 2024 favors a **single** plastic stent and recommends **against** routine FCSEMS — **ASGE 2024 governs the page.**

### Exocrine pancreatic insufficiency (EPI)

- **PERT is the treatment, required once EPI is diagnosed** (Rec 11, Cond/Low) — **≥40,000–50,000 United States Pharmacopeia (USP) units of lipase with each meal, taken *during* the meal** ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]]). Titration, snack/weight-based and maximum dosing, product choice, acid suppression, diet: [[exocrine-pancreatic-insufficiency]].
- **[[small-intestinal-bacterial-overgrowth|Small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO)]]:** pancreatic insufficiency is a recognized risk factor — evaluate for SIBO with persistent diarrhea/[[abdominal-bloating-and-distention|bloating]] despite adequate PERT ([[acg-2020-sibo]]).

### Endocrine dysfunction (type 3c DM)

- Insulin often required; metformin may be cancer-protective; avoid hypoglycemia-inducing agents if brittle
- Distinct from type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) — insulin- **and** glucagon-deficient → hypoglycemia-prone

### Surveillance and counseling

- **No routine [[pancreatic-cancer-screening|pancreatic cancer screening]]** in CP (Key Concept 5) — insufficient evidence; possible exception, hereditary pancreatitis at expert centers
- Periodic EPI/nutrition assessment (annual or symptom-driven): weight, fat-soluble vitamins
- Dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA) at baseline, then periodically — osteoporosis risk (Key Concept 10)
- Genetic counselor referral preferred (nongeneticists can order the panel); family-planning implications for AD inheritance (PRSS1)
