## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

Chronic pancreatitis (CP) = **pathologic fibroinflammatory syndrome of the pancreas** in a patient with genetic, environmental, and/or other risk factors who develops persistent pathologic responses to parenchymal injury or stress (mechanistic definition, 2016/2018). The older clinicopathologic definition required **irreversible morphologic change**; the mechanistic definition allows diagnosis **before end-stage morphology**.

**Cardinal features:** atrophy, fibrosis, ductal distortion/strictures, calcifications · pain (most common symptom) · [[exocrine-pancreatic-insufficiency|exocrine pancreatic insufficiency (EPI)]] · endocrine dysfunction (type 3c diabetes mellitus [DM]) · dysplasia (↑ ductal adenocarcinoma risk).

**Diagnostic algorithm (American College of Gastroenterology [ACG] 2020, Fig 1 — clinicopathologic model):**

![[chronic-pancreatitis-2020-diagnostic-algorithm-11.png|700x416]]
*Figure 1 — Diagnostic algorithm for chronic pancreatitis based on the clinicopathologic disease model. CT, computed tomography; MRCP, magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography. ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]])*

Genetic testing recommended if etiology unclear, especially **<35 yr** ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]]).

**Mechanistic algorithm (Fig 2) — 3 escalating levels of investigation:**

![[chronic-pancreatitis-2020-mechanistic-algorithm-12.png|700x369]]
*Figure 2 — Conceptual diagnostic algorithm based on the mechanistic definition of chronic pancreatitis. Rows a–c represent escalating levels of clinical investigation. TIGAR-O (toxic-metabolic, idiopathic, genetic, autoimmune, recurrent and severe acute pancreatitis, obstructive) risk factors guide workup at each level. ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]])*

- **A — clinical:** pain, maldigestion, weight loss, glucose intolerance + TIGAR-O risk factors + CT, [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]] ± fine-needle aspiration (FNA), amylase/lipase, triglycerides, immunoglobulin G4 (IgG4), glucose, fat-soluble vitamins, tumor markers
- **B — risk/phenotype:** family history, early onset, cystic fibrosis (CF) organ involvement, syndromic features → genetic testing, sweat chloride, secretin-enhanced MRCP (sMRCP), exocrine function testing
- **C — response/histology:** [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]]/IgG4 disease, response to analgesics, pancreatic enzyme replacement therapy (PERT), or a steroid trial for autoimmune pancreatitis (AIP) type 2 → histology

### Severity Assessment — M-ANNHEIM scoring

| Domain | Points |
|---|---|
| Pain (none–continuous) | 0–4 |
| Pain control (none–potent opioids/endoscopic intervention) | 0–2 |
| Surgical intervention (any) | 4 |
| EPI (absent / mild / proven steatorrhea) | 0–2 |
| Endocrine insufficiency (DM present) | 0 or 4 |
| Imaging morphology (Cambridge: normal–marked) | 0–4 |
| Severe organ complications (none / reversible / irreversible) | 0–2–4 |

**Severity bands:** A Minor 0–5 · B Increased 6–10 · C Advanced 11–15 · D Marked 16–20 · E Exacerbated >20.

#### Imaging morphology grade — the criteria behind the "Cambridge" row

American Pancreatic Association (APA) 2014 **Table 6, Pancreas Morphology Imaging Grade (I–IV)** — what the M-ANNHEIM imaging row and the evidence tiers below are scored from ([[apa-2014-chronic-pancreatitis]]).

| Modality | Equivocal (I) | Mild (II) | Moderate (III) | Marked (IV) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CT / magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)** — transabdominal ultrasound (US) graded the same as CT | **1** of: main duct enlarged **2–4 mm**; slight gland enlargement; heterogeneous parenchyma; small cavities **<10 mm**; irregular ducts; focal pancreatitis; increased echogenicity of main-duct wall; irregular head/body contour | **≥2** of the same list | Cannot be distinguished from mild | Moderate changes **plus ≥1** of: large cavities **>10 mm**, gland enlargement, intraductal filling defects/calculi, duct obstruction, stricture, or gross irregularity |
| **EUS** (conventional 0–9 criteria) | 0–2 features | 3–4 features | ≥5 features | *not separately defined in Table 6* |
| **MRCP / [[ercp\|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]]** | **<3** abnormal side-branch changes | **≥3** abnormal side-branch changes | Abnormal main duct **+ >3** abnormal side branches | Moderate changes **plus 1** of: obstruction, filling defects, severe irregularity/dilation of the main duct |

**What counts as an abnormal duct on pancreatography** ([[apa-2014-chronic-pancreatitis]], Cambridge criteria):

- **Normal** main-duct diameter averages **3.6 / 2.7 / 1.6 mm** (head / body / tail); **upper limit of normal reported as high as 6.5 / 5 / 3 mm**.
- Dilation is **general** (>⅔ of the main duct) or **local** (<⅔). **Severe dilation = >1 cm.**
- Main duct: dilation, narrowing, strictures, filling defects, leaks/cavities. Side branches: decreased number, shortened length, dilated or narrowed caliber — the normal/abnormal boundary for side branches is not defined in the source.
- ⚠ Ductal abnormalities **alone** misdiagnose CP — **81%** of a post-mortem series without CP had "abnormal" pancreatograms; age-related change confounds.

#### Diagnostic evidence tiers (APA 2014, Table 5)

Interpret only in a patient whose history/exam is suspicious for CP — pain, prior [[acute-pancreatitis|acute pancreatitis (AP)]], PERT-responsive steatorrhea, heavy alcohol/smoking, genetic or family risk.

| Tier | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **Definitive** | **Any one of:** moderate/marked imaging morphology (ductal **and** parenchymal abnormalities) · pancreatic calcifications · histologic confirmation |
| **Probable** | (**Mild** imaging morphology **or** recurrent pseudocyst/pancreatitis) **plus** abnormal pancreatic physiology (secretin test, diabetes, steatorrhea) |
| **Insufficient** | **Equivocal** imaging morphology, **or** abdominal pain with any of: no history of pancreatitis (lipase <3× upper limit of normal [ULN]), normal imaging, family history of pancreatitis, prior ERCP with pancreatic duct (PD) stenting, presence of TIGAR-O risk factors (smoking, alcohol) |

### Classification — TIGAR-O v2.0 risk/etiology checklist

**T**oxic-metabolic · **I**diopathic · **G**enetic · **A**utoimmune · **R**ecurrent AP · **O**bstructive

- **Toxic-metabolic:** alcohol (3–4/d; ≥5/d), smoking (pack-years), hypercalcemia (ionized calcium [iCa] **>12 mg/dL**), hypertriglyceridemia (fasting **>300** / nonfasting **>500**), medications
- **Idiopathic:** early-onset (**<35 yr**) vs late-onset (**>35 yr**)
- **Genetic:** PRSS1 (autosomal dominant [AD], [[hereditary-pancreatitis|hereditary pancreatitis]]), SPINK1 (autosomal recessive [AR], severe), CFTR (cystic fibrosis or CFTR-related disorder [CFTR-RD]), CTRC, CASR, CLDN2 (alcohol accelerator), CPA1, CEL (maturity-onset diabetes of the young type 8 [MODY-8])
- **Autoimmune:** AIP type 1 (IgG4-related) / type 2 (IBD-associated)
- **Recurrent (RAP) and severe (SAP) acute pancreatitis:** biliary, post-ERCP, traumatic, undetermined
- **Obstructive:** [[pancreas-divisum|pancreas divisum]], ampullary stenosis, main-duct stones, calcifications, strictures, mass

*Full gene table (inheritance, mechanism, phenotype) lives on [[hereditary-pancreatitis]].*

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup of the recurrent acute episodes that precede CP: see [[recurrent-acute-pancreatitis]].*

- [[acute-pancreatitis]] — acute flares; RAP is precursor in 30% (overall ~60% of CP evolves from AP/RAP)
- **[[pancreatic-cancer|Pancreatic adenocarcinoma]]** — new stricture/mass; consider in any new-onset CP, especially older, new diabetes + weight loss
- **[[autoimmune-pancreatitis|Autoimmune pancreatitis]]** — type 1 (IgG4-related; sausage pancreas, IgG4↑, steroid-responsive) vs type 2 (IBD-associated, granulocytic epithelial lesions, younger)
- [[biliary-stricture]] — CP = most common benign cause of extrahepatic [[biliary-stricture|biliary stricture]]
- **[[pancreas-divisum|Pancreas divisum]] / ampullary stenosis** — consider in idiopathic CP
- **Intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm (IPMN) / [[pancreatic-cysts|pancreatic cysts]]** — duct dilation can mimic CP
- Long-standing diabetes; renal disease; cyclosporine; age-related atrophy/fibrosis

## Diagnostics

### Imaging

| Modality | Sensitivity (95% confidence interval [CI]) | Specificity (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|
| [[endoscopic-ultrasound\|EUS]] | **81%** (70–89) | 90% (82–95) |
| MRI | 78% (69–85) | **96%** (90–98) |
| CT | 75% (66–83) | 91% (81–96) |
| ERCP | not reported | 94% (87–98) |
| Transabdominal US | not reported | **98%** (89–100) |

*Systematic review/meta-analysis, 43 studies, 3,460 patients with suspected CP. **Sensitivities did not differ significantly from one another**, so modality choice turns on specificity, invasiveness and availability rather than pick-up rate; not every included study used a histologic gold standard. ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]])*

- **CT — first-line:** calcifications, ductal dilation (**>3 mm**), parenchymal atrophy, pseudocysts; widespread calcifications pathognomonic but late.
- **MRI / [[mri-mrcp|MRCP]]:** ductal anatomy and side branches; **sMRCP** for subtle ductal change (dilated side branches, ectatic main duct) — next step when cross-sectional imaging is non-diagnostic.
- **EUS:** most sensitive for early/minimal-change CP, least specific — only when CT/MRCP are non-diagnostic and suspicion persists, and **never the sole basis for diagnosis**. Graded by the conventional criteria and the **Rosemont classification** below.
- **Histology** = gold standard, but limited by sampling and subjectivity.

#### Rosemont classification (EUS)

Weighted consensus system — features scored **Major A**, **Major B** or **minor**; combinations give four categories. *Feature definitions and size cut-offs, where in the gland each may be scored, the conventional 9-feature list with its duct diameters, and the validity caveats all live on [[endoscopic-ultrasound]].*

| Category | Qualifying combinations |
|---|---|
| **Consistent with CP** | **2 Major A** · **or** 1 Major A **+** Major B · **or** 1 Major A **+ ≥3 minor** |
| **Suggestive of CP** † | 1 Major A **+ <3 minor** · **or** Major B **+ ≥3 minor** · **or** **≥5 minor (any)** |
| **Indeterminate for CP** † | **3–4 minor, no major** · **or** **Major B alone** or with **<3 minor** |
| **Normal** | **≤2 minor ‡, no major features** |

- † **"Suggestive" and "indeterminate" are not diagnoses — both require confirmation** by another study (ERCP, CT, MRI, or pancreatic function testing); pair them with fecal elastase or sMRCP rather than treating the score as definitive. "Consistent with CP" is meaningful only in a compatible clinical picture.
- ‡ ⚠ **The "normal" count excludes four minor features** — **cysts, dilated main pancreatic duct (MPD), hyperechoic non-shadowing foci, and dilated side branches.** They do not count toward the ≤2, so a patient carrying three of them and nothing else still reads **normal**.
- Unvalidated expert opinion with low specificity (aging, anatomic variants and asymptomatic fibrosis all produce features). APA's posture: **err toward more stringent criteria**, accepting missed diagnoses, to avoid over-diagnosis and risky therapy ([[apa-2014-chronic-pancreatitis]], [[catalano-2009-rosemont-classification]]).

### Pancreatic function tests

| Test | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cholecystokinin (CCK) stimulation | Hormonal (acinar) | Direct, detects subtle EPI; cumbersome, Dreiling tube |
| Secretin stimulation (endoscopic pancreatic function test, ePFT) | Hormonal (ductal) | Endoscopic, measures bicarbonate; not widely available |
| Fecal elastase-1 | Nonhormonal | Universally available, easiest test for EPI. **Cutoff drives accuracy: <200 µg/g has a high false-positive rate; <100 µg/g improves specificity but lowers sensitivity** ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]]). Specificity further limited by watery stool and small-bowel disease ([[apa-2014-chronic-pancreatitis]]) |
| ¹³C-mixed triglyceride breath test | Nonhormonal | Sensitivity ~90%; not universally available; 4–6 hr |
| Serum trypsinogen/trypsin | Nonhormonal | Easy; nonspecific; elevated with pancreatic pain |

### Genetic testing

Indicated when etiology unclear, family history, early onset, or pediatric/young adult. Minimum panel: **PRSS1, SPINK1, CFTR, CTRC**. Extended panels available.

### Other labs

- Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c), fasting glucose (annually) — screen for type 3c DM
- Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K), B12, zinc, magnesium
- Dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA) — osteoporosis risk (Key Concept 10)
- IgG4 — exclude AIP type 1

## Therapeutics

### Lifestyle

- **Alcohol cessation** (Strong / Very Low) — single randomized controlled trial (RCT) shows reduced AP recurrence; cornerstone despite weak data [[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]]
- **Smoking cessation** (Strong / Very Low) — case series show reduced calcification progression

### Pain management — stepwise

| Step | Intervention | Grade / detail |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Lifestyle (alcohol + smoking cessation) and **non-opioid analgesics** | — |
| **2** | **Antioxidants** | Cond/Moderate. Selenium 600 µg · ascorbic acid 0.54 g · β-carotene 9000 IU · α-tocopherol 270 IU · methionine 2 g **daily**. Modest effect, mainly early disease |
| **3** | Adjuncts — **pregabalin/gabapentin, tricyclic antidepressant (TCA)** | Extrapolated from the chronic-pain literature |
| **4** | **[[celiac-plexus-block\|Celiac plexus block]]** | Cond/Very Low — see below |
| **5** | **Endoscopic decompression** — ERCP ± extracorporeal shock wave lithotripsy (ESWL) for stones, stricture dilation, PD stent | First-line for *obstructive* CP |
| **6** | **Surgical drainage** — Puestow / Frey / Beger / Whipple | Strong/Moderate. **Superior to endoscopy** for long-term pain in obstructive CP, and the fallback if endoscopy fails |
| **7** | **Opiates** | Only when all other options are exhausted (Key Concept 7) |
| **8** | **TPIAT** (total pancreatectomy + islet autotransplant) | Highly selected; refractory pain after medical therapy exhausted (Key Concept 8) |
| — | *Experimental, research only:* spinal cord stimulation, transmagnetic stimulation, radiation | Key Concept 9 |

- **Step 4 — who the block is for:** CP **nonresponsive to medical therapy with no endoscopic or surgical decompression option** — i.e. **nonobstructive (small-duct)** disease — plus patients with opioid side effects or wishing to avoid opioids. **Not routine.** Set the expectation before consenting: **fewer than 60% get pain relief and it is not sustained (<6 months)**; almost all still need analgesics. An obstructed main duct belongs in step 5/6 instead. Route, injectate, technique, efficacy and adverse-event rates: [[celiac-plexus-block]]. ([[asge-2024-chronic-pancreatitis-endoscopy|American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2024]] Rec 2, conditional/low)
- ⚠ **Active alcohol consumption is a relative contraindication to elective interventional procedures** (Key Concept 6) — urgent/emergent procedures for complications are still warranted.

> [!pitfall] Enzymes do not treat pancreatic pain
> PERT is for the complications of EPI only — it does **not** improve pain (Rec 9, conditional/low). ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]])

### Endoscopic vs surgical therapy for painful obstructive CP

*Governing source: [[asge-2024-chronic-pancreatitis-endoscopy|ASGE 2024]] (newest tier-1 guideline; all six recommendations **conditional**). [[aga-2022-recurrent-pancreatitis-endoscopy|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2022]] agrees on the surgery-vs-endotherapy direction and supplies supporting data, but **is superseded on stent number**.*

> [!pearl] Ask the surgeon before the first ERCP
> **No contraindication to surgery → surgical evaluation *before* initiating endoscopic management** (more durable pain relief). Contraindication to surgery, or patient prefers a less-invasive modality → **endoscopic management first** (Rec 1). ([[asge-2024-chronic-pancreatitis-endoscopy]])

- **Multidisciplinary approach strongly recommended** after failed medical pain management — check availability of an expert endoscopist, a pancreatic surgeon and lithotripsy before committing to a route.
- Supporting data (AGA 2022 best practice advice [BPA] 4): 3 RCTs incl. **ESCAPE** (n=88, dilated MPD) — pain relief **58% surgery vs 39% endotherapy** at 18 mo. Surgery is one-time; endotherapy needs serial ERCPs over up to 12 mo.
- Decompression is **almost never indicated in asymptomatic patients** — rare exception: young patient with unifocal obstruction downstream of viable parenchyma, to slow islet-cell loss ([[aga-2022-recurrent-pancreatitis-endoscopy]]).

**Procedure selection — full criteria on [[ercp]]:**

- **MPD stones (Rec 3)** — strategy set by **size, location *and* radiopacity**; **consider ESWL for radiopaque stones >10 mm**, otherwise pancreatoscopy vs ESWL turns on local expertise (pancreatoscopy is difficult with stones upstream of a stricture). Stones occur in ~60% of CP; treat only for symptomatic obstruction ([[aga-2022-recurrent-pancreatitis-endoscopy]]).
- **MPD stricture (Rec 4)** — a **single** plastic stent, not multiple, at the largest safely deployable diameter, upsized gradually, reassessed at **3-monthly** ERCPs; **against routine fully covered self-expanding metal stents (FCSEMS)** for refractory strictures.
- **CP-related benign [[biliary-stricture|biliary stricture]] (Rec 5; ~15% of advanced CP)** — ERCP + stent, **FCSEMS over multiple plastic stents** (fewer exchanges); treat when **symptomatic or unresolved at 4 weeks**, to prevent secondary biliary [[cirrhosis|cirrhosis]]. ⚠ Revert to multiple plastic stents for an **indeterminate** stricture, or when FCSEMS would risk **cystic duct obstruction with the gallbladder in situ** ([[acute-cholecystitis|cholecystitis]] risk).
- **Pseudocyst (Rec 6)** — **endoscopic over surgical drainage**; avoid percutaneous as sole therapy (pancreaticocutaneous fistula). Technique: [[pancreatic-fluid-collection-drainage]].

> ⚠ **Contradiction on the record — PD stent number and FCSEMS.** AGA 2022 BPA 6 advised 6–12 months of plastic-stent therapy with dilation and sequential **upsizing or multiple parallel stents**, and treated FCSEMS as "emerging" (vs multiple plastic stents: pain improvement 88% vs 89%, stricture recurrence 8% vs 11%, fewer ERCPs, but **adverse events 39% vs 14%**). ASGE 2024 favors a **single** plastic stent initially and recommends **against** routine FCSEMS. **ASGE 2024 governs the page.**

### Exocrine pancreatic insufficiency (EPI)

- **PERT (Rec 11, Cond/Low):** **at least 40,000–50,000 United States Pharmacopeia (USP) units of lipase with each meal**, taken **during** the meal ([[acg-2020-chronic-pancreatitis]]); titrate to symptoms (steatorrhea, weight, vitamin levels).
- Snack and weight-based dosing, maximum dose, product choice, acid suppression for non-enteric-coated formulations, and diet: [[exocrine-pancreatic-insufficiency]].
- **[[small-intestinal-bacterial-overgrowth|Small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO)]]:** pancreatic insufficiency is a recognized risk factor (altered bile acid composition + reduced digestive enzyme activity) — evaluate for SIBO with persistent diarrhea/[[abdominal-bloating-and-distention|bloating]] despite adequate PERT ([[acg-2020-sibo]]).

### Endocrine dysfunction (type 3c DM)

- Annual HbA1c screening
- Insulin often required; metformin may have a cancer-protective effect; avoid agents causing hypoglycemia if brittle
- Distinct from type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) — insulin-deficient **and** glucagon-deficient → hypoglycemia-prone

### Genetic and family counseling

- Genetic counselor referral preferred; nongeneticist clinicians can order the minimum panel
- Family planning implications for AD inheritance (PRSS1)

### Surveillance

- **No routine [[pancreatic-cancer-screening|pancreatic cancer screening]]** in CP (Key Concept 5) — insufficient evidence, except possibly hereditary pancreatitis at expert centers
- Periodic EPI/nutrition assessment (annual or symptom-driven): weight, fat-soluble vitamins
- DEXA at baseline and periodically (Key Concept 10)

### Pregnancy / fertility

- Not addressed by the guideline; manage individually
