## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Definition:** failure of fusion of the **dorsal and ventral pancreatic ductal systems** during embryogenesis → dominant pancreatic duct drainage through the **minor papilla** ([[aga-2022-recurrent-pancreatitis-endoscopy]]).
- **Most common congenital pancreatic anomaly** — ~6%–10% of individuals.
- **The large majority are asymptomatic** → considerable controversy about the clinical significance of pancreas divisum (PD) in pancreatic disease.
- **Putative mechanism:** a subset have impaired drainage of pancreatic secretions through the dorsal-dominant system → increased intraductal pressure → may precipitate [[recurrent-acute-pancreatitis|recurrent acute pancreatitis (RAP)]]. [[rome-v-2026-gallbladder-sphincter-of-oddi|Rome V]] describes the same mechanism as an **imbalance between the volume of flow through the dominant dorsal system and the small size of the minor papilla**.
- There is no severity score and no typing scheme for PD in any cited source; the only distinction that drives management is whether there are **objective radiologic signs of ductal outflow obstruction** (see [[#Patient Selection]]).

> [!pearl] Pancreas divisum is not a sphincter disorder
> The minor papilla does not comprise a true anatomic sphincter, so PD sits outside the [[sphincter-of-oddi-dysfunction|sphincter of Oddi (SO) disorders]] — Rome V lists PD among the etiologies that must be **excluded** before pancreatic SO disorder can be diagnosed ([[rome-v-2026-gallbladder-sphincter-of-oddi]]).

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[recurrent-acute-pancreatitis]].*

Attributing RAP to PD requires the other known causes of pancreatitis to be ruled out (Rome V exclusion list):

- Gallstones / occult biliary lithiasis — the most common occult finding on endoscopic evaluation of unexplained pancreatitis ([[choledocholithiasis|choledocholithiasis / microlithiasis]])
- Alcohol, hypertriglyceridemia, hypercalcemia, medications
- [[autoimmune-pancreatitis|Autoimmune pancreatitis]]
- Pancreatic mass ([[pancreatic-cancer|pancreatic cancer]]) or ampullary lesion ([[ampullary-adenoma|ampullary adenoma]]) — occult ampullary or pancreatobiliary malignancy in up to **5%** after one unexplained episode and **12%** with RAP
- Anomalous pancreaticobiliary junction
- [[hereditary-pancreatitis|Genetic / hereditary pancreatitis]]
- Acute exacerbation of underlying [[chronic-pancreatitis|chronic pancreatitis]]
- Pancreatic sphincter of Oddi disorder — same putative mechanism, but diagnosable only once PD and the causes above are excluded

## Diagnostics

- **[[mri-mrcp|Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) with magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP)]] makes the anatomic diagnosis.** [[endoscopic-ultrasound|Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]] is the preferred *initial* test in unexplained [[acute-pancreatitis|acute pancreatitis (AP)]]/RAP and is more likely than MRI to establish a probable cause (odds ratio 3.79, driven by sensitivity for occult biliary stones), but **MRI/MRCP better identifies ductal variants** such as PD and anomalous pancreaticobiliary union. Full workup sequence and timing: [[recurrent-acute-pancreatitis]].
- **Secretin-enhanced MRCP** improves diagnostic yield over standard MRI/MRCP, but clinical utility is limited by availability, the logistics of secretin administration, and variability in dynamic acquisition and interpretation.
- **[[ercp|Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]] with minor papilla cannulation** demonstrates the dorsal-dominant drainage, but ERCP here is a *therapeutic* procedure: PD / minor papilla intervention is **not** among the accepted pancreatic indications for ERCP and appears instead on the controversial-indications list ([[acg-asge-2026-ercp-quality-indicators]]). Performing and documenting ERCP for an accepted indication is **quality indicator 1, performance target >98%** ([[ercp-quality-indicators]]).

## Therapeutics

Endotherapy aims to relieve outflow obstruction by **enlarging the minor papilla** — minor papilla sphincterotomy (papillotomy), balloon dilation, stent placement, or a combination, at ERCP. Stated intent: improve drainage, reduce intraductal hypertension, avert future attacks.

### Patient Selection

- **RAP only.** No role for ERCP to treat **pain alone** in PD — AGA 2022 states there are no data supporting it.
- **Objective radiologic signs of ductal outflow obstruction** may be more likely to benefit:
  - **Dilated dorsal pancreatic duct**
  - **Santorinicele** — AGA 2022 names it as such a finding but does not define it, and gives no size cutoff for either feature.
- **Evidence-based predictors of response are yet to be defined** — there is no validated threshold, score or stratum that selects responders.
- Other causes of RAP excluded first (see [[#Differential Diagnosis]]).

> **Best Practice Advice 2 (American Gastroenterological Association [AGA] 2022):** The role of ERCP for reducing the frequency of AP episodes in patients with PD is controversial, but minor papilla endotherapy may be considered, particularly for those with objective signs of outflow obstruction, such as a dilated dorsal pancreatic duct and/or santorinicele. There is no role for ERCP to treat pain alone in patients with PD.

**Where the evidence now stands:** AGA 2022 wrote Best Practice Advice 2 while the sham-controlled trial was still underway. That trial — **SHARP** (Sphincterotomy for Acute Recurrent Pancreatitis) — has since reported **no benefit**, and both 2026 documents carry the negative result as challenging the longstanding practice of empiric minor papilla sphincterotomy. The newer sources govern: empiric minor papilla sphincterotomy for RAP with PD is not supported.

### Evidence for Minor Papilla Endotherapy

| Design | Intervention | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retrospective uncontrolled studies, in aggregate | minor papilla endotherapy | RAP episodes reduced or eliminated in up to **60%–80%** | [[aga-2022-recurrent-pancreatitis-endoscopy\|AGA 2022]] |
| Systematic reviews of observational data | minor papilla interventions | **>70% benefit**, limited by heterogeneity in study subjects and endoscopic interventions | [[acg-asge-2026-ercp-quality-indicators\|ACG/ASGE 2026]] |
| Single randomized trial, **n = 19** | minor papilla stenting vs no therapy | future AP episodes **10% vs 67%, *P* < 0.05**; stenting alone is not contemporary practice and is unlikely to produce durable reshaping of the dorsal drainage system | [[aga-2022-recurrent-pancreatitis-endoscopy\|AGA 2022]] |
| **SHARP** — international, multicentre, randomized, **sham-controlled** | minor papilla sphincterotomy vs sham | **no significant reduction in recurrence of AP**; reported in abstract form at Digestive Disease Week 2025 | [[acg-asge-2026-ercp-quality-indicators\|ACG/ASGE 2026]], [[rome-v-2026-gallbladder-sphincter-of-oddi\|Rome V]] |

### Adverse Events

| Adverse event | Rate / consequence |
|---|---|
| Post-ERCP pancreatitis | **10%–15%** |
| Post-papillotomy stenosis (minor papilla) | **up to 1 in 5 (19%)**; may leave RAP frequency *higher* than at the original presentation |
| Prolonged stent therapy | no rate given — need for frequent exchanges, stent migration/occlusion, potential stent-related ductal injury |

> [!pitfall] Papillotomy can leave the patient worse than before
> Post-papillotomy stenosis occurs in up to **1 of 5** patients and may result in RAP **more frequent than at presentation** ([[aga-2022-recurrent-pancreatitis-endoscopy]]) — weigh it against a negative sham-controlled trial.
