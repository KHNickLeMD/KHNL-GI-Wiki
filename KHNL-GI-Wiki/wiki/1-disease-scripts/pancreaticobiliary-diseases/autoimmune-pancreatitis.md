A **chronic, steroid-responsive fibroinflammatory disease of the pancreas** whose whole clinical problem is that it **mimics [[pancreatic-cancer|pancreatic cancer]]** — found in **2%–3% of resections done for presumed pancreatic cancer**. Two diseases sit under the name, in the source's nomenclature: **autoimmune pancreatitis (AIP) = type 1** (lymphoplasmacytic sclerosing pancreatitis [LPSP], an immunoglobulin G4 [IgG4]-related disorder); **IDCP = type 2** (idiopathic duct centric pancreatitis).

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **A *painless* obstructive picture.** AIP: **obstructive jaundice**, **no or mild pain despite intense inflammation**. IDCP: **[[acute-pancreatitis|acute pancreatitis]] in 50%**.
- Jaundice mechanism (distal compression by head swelling vs proximal stricture): [[igg4-related-cholangitis|IgG4-sclerosing cholangitis (IgG4-SC)]].
- **Never a single test** — serology + cross-sectional imaging (computed tomography [CT] / magnetic resonance imaging [MRI]) + ductal imaging + other organ involvement (OOI) + histology + steroid response.

**International Consensus Diagnostic Criteria (ICDC)** — most sensitive published set. **Five cardinal features:** pancreatic imaging (parenchyma **or** duct) · serology (serum IgG4) · OOI · pancreatic histology · steroid response. Output: **type 1 AIP**, **type 2 (IDCP)** or **AIP not otherwise specified**.

- Each feature scores **level 1** (higher reliability) or **level 2**; with *typical* vs *indeterminate* parenchymal imaging that yields **definitive** or **probable** disease. Optional sixth criterion **Rt (steroid response)** applies only in indeterminate-imaging rows.
- Fitting none of the combinations below — incl. clinically suspected but histologically unconfirmed type 2 — is **"probable AIP."** **Burnt-out AIP is outside these criteria** ([[shimosegawa-2011-icdc-autoimmune-pancreatitis]]).

#### ICDC Level 1 vs Level 2 — Type 1 AIP

| | Criterion | **Level 1** | **Level 2** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P** | Parenchymal imaging | **Typical:** diffuse enlargement with delayed enhancement (± rim-like enhancement) | **Indeterminate** (incl. atypical): segmental/focal enlargement with delayed enhancement |
| **D** | Ductal imaging (endoscopic retrograde pancreatography, ERP) | **Long (>1/3 the main pancreatic duct) or multiple strictures**, without marked upstream dilatation | Segmental/focal narrowing without marked upstream dilatation (**duct <5 mm**) |
| **S** | Serology | **IgG4 >2× upper limit of normal (ULN)** — see [[#Serum IgG4]] | IgG4 **1–2× ULN** |
| **OOI** | Other organ involvement | ***a* or *b*.** **a** Extrapancreatic histology, **any 3 of:** marked lymphoplasmacytic infiltration with fibrosis and no granulocytic infiltration · storiform fibrosis · obliterative phlebitis · **>10 IgG4-positive cells/high-power field (HPF)**. **b** Typical radiology, **≥1 of:** segmental/multiple proximal (hilar/intrahepatic), or proximal **and** distal, bile duct stricture · retroperitoneal fibrosis | ***a* or *b*.** **a** Extrapancreatic histology **including endoscopic bile-duct biopsy**, **both of:** marked lymphoplasmacytic infiltration without granulocytic infiltration · >10 IgG4-positive cells/HPF. **b** Physical/radiological, **≥1 of:** symmetrically enlarged salivary/lachrymal glands · renal involvement of the type described with AIP |
| **H** | Pancreatic histology | **LPSP (core biopsy or resection), ≥3 of:** periductal lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate without granulocytic infiltration · obliterative phlebitis · storiform fibrosis · >10 IgG4-positive cells/HPF | **LPSP (core biopsy), any 2** of the same four |
| **Rt** | Response to steroid | **Rapid (≤2 weeks) radiologically demonstrable resolution or marked improvement** of pancreatic/extrapancreatic manifestations | — |

#### How the Levels Combine — Type 1

| Diagnosis | Basis | Imaging | Collateral evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Definitive type 1** | Histology | Typical / indeterminate | **Histologically confirmed LPSP (level 1 H)** |
| **Definitive type 1** | Imaging | **Typical** | **Any non-D level 1 or level 2** |
| **Definitive type 1** | Imaging | **Indeterminate** | **≥2 from level 1** (+ level 2 D, which counts as level 1 here) |
| **Definitive type 1** | Steroid response | Indeterminate | **Level 1 S/OOI + Rt**, or **level 1 D + level 2 S/OOI/H + Rt** |
| **Probable type 1** | — | Indeterminate | **Level 2 S/OOI/H + Rt** |

#### Type 2 (IDCP) and AIP–NOS

| | Criterion | **Level 1** | **Level 2** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **OOI** | Other organ involvement | — | **Clinically diagnosed [[inflammatory-bowel-disease\|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]]** |
| **H** | Pancreatic histology (core biopsy or resection) | **IDCP — both of:** granulocytic infiltration of the duct wall (granulocytic epithelial lesion) ± granulocytic acinar inflammation; **absent or scant (0–10 cells/HPF) IgG4-positive cells** | **Both of:** granulocytic **and** lymphoplasmacytic acinar infiltrate; absent/scant (0–10 cells/HPF) IgG4-positive cells |

*P, D and Rt are defined identically to type 1.*

| Diagnosis | Imaging | Collateral evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Definitive type 2** | Typical / indeterminate | **Histologically confirmed IDCP (level 1 H)**, OR **clinical IBD + level 2 H + Rt** |
| **Probable type 2** | Typical / indeterminate | **Level 2 H / clinical IBD + Rt** |
| **AIP–not otherwise specified** | Typical / indeterminate | **D1/2 + Rt** — ductal evidence only |

> [!pearl] Type 2 is the one that needs tissue
> Definitive **type 1** can be reached on surrogate criteria with no histology; **definite IDCP requires histological confirmation**. Imaging and steroid response do not separate the types — only **serology, OOI and histology** do, and all three are type-1 features, so a seronegative patient without OOI is **not thereby type 2** ([[shimosegawa-2011-icdc-autoimmune-pancreatitis]]).

#### The Diagnostic Steroid Trial

- **Prednisone 0.6–1 mg/kg**; reassess **imaging and CA 19-9 at 2 weeks**. Use **sparingly**, **only after a negative cancer workup including [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]]-guided sampling**, and only by pancreatologists ([[shimosegawa-2011-icdc-autoimmune-pancreatitis]]).
- **Endpoint is radiological, not symptomatic** — definite improvement of the imaging abnormalities (biliary stricture, pancreatic enlargement). Symptomatic improvement and a sense of well-being do not count.
- **A rising CA 19-9 means the diagnosis is wrong.**
- **A falling IgG4 is not an endpoint** — falsely elevated IgG4 in pancreatic cancer and other non-AIP states falls on steroids too.
- **Cancer-induced pancreatitis can improve radiologically on its own** and be mistaken for steroid response.
- Full resolution may take **weeks to months**, sometimes with gland atrophy.

> [!pitfall] "If steroids work, it must be AIP" is not a diagnosis
> The trial is no substitute for a thorough search for an etiology, and the strategy is *"fraught with problems"* without collateral evidence ([[shimosegawa-2011-icdc-autoimmune-pancreatitis]]).

#### AIP vs Pancreatic Cancer on CT/MRI

| CT/MRI pattern | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Diffusely enlarged pancreas (especially capsule-like rim), no ductal dilatation/cutoff, no mass**, with obstructive jaundice | **Highly likely AIP** — less collateral evidence required |
| **Low-density mass, ductal dilatation/cutoff, ± distal atrophy** | **Manage as [[pancreatic-cancer\|pancreatic cancer]]** unless the cancer workup is negative *and* collateral evidence for AIP is strong |
| Neither pattern | **Investigate for cancer first**; consider AIP only if that workup is negative and collateral evidence is strong |

- **Points to cancer, not AIP:** marked cachexia, inability to eat, narcotic-requiring pain. Typical idiopathic pancreatitis and painful [[chronic-pancreatitis|chronic pancreatitis]] are likewise uncommon in histologically confirmed AIP.
- A biopsy with **some but not all** features of LPSP or IDCP is **supportive, not diagnostic**.

### IgG4-Related Disease and Other Organ Involvement

**~50% have OOI, ~50% isolated pancreatic involvement.** OOI counts twice — ICDC cardinal feature, and marker of relapse risk ([[#Relapse and Maintenance]]). Established by **histology**, by **imaging** (proximal bile duct stricture, retroperitoneal fibrosis) or by **physical examination** (salivary gland enlargement).

- Bile duct stricture (IgG4-SC) · interstitial nephritis · orbital pseudotumor · IgG4-related plasmacytic exocrinopathy of the salivary gland · pulmonary interstitial fibrosis or nodules · mediastinal or retroperitoneal fibrosis
- **Does not count as OOI:** rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthropathy, true seropositive Sjögren — not typically associated with AIP ([[shimosegawa-2011-icdc-autoimmune-pancreatitis]]).
- **IDCP has no IgG4-related disease association.** Its systemic association is **IBD**, especially **[[ulcerative-colitis|ulcerative colitis]]** — a majority of IDCP patients are diagnosed with IBD before or concurrently with IDCP.

### Severity Assessment

- **No severity score or stage is defined for AIP or IDCP.** Burden is carried by **relapse risk** ([[#Relapse and Maintenance]]) and by the sequelae of persistent inflammation — **[[exocrine-pancreatic-insufficiency|exocrine insufficiency]] and diabetes**, as in other causes of chronic pancreatitis (surveillance intervals on [[chronic-pancreatitis]]; no AIP-specific interval or treatment is given).
- **Future pancreatic cancer risk is *not* elevated** — similar to other causes of chronic pancreatitis. This does not relax the need to exclude a *prevalent* cancer at diagnosis.

### Classification / Typing

Type determines the serology, the histology to ask the pathologist for, the systemic associations and — above all — the **relapse risk**, and therefore whether maintenance therapy is on the table.

| Feature | **AIP** (type 1, lymphoplasmacytic sclerosing) | **IDCP** (type 2, idiopathic duct centric) |
|---|---|---|
| Age at presentation | **7th decade** | **5th decade** |
| Male sex | 75% | 50% |
| OOI / IgG4-related disease | Present in **~50%** | **None** |
| Association with IBD | Low | **High (10%–20%)** |
| Serum IgG4 elevation | Present in **~66%** | Present in ~25% |
| *Histology —* lymphoplasmacytic infiltration | Present | Present |
| *Histology —* storiform fibrosis | Present | Present |
| *Histology —* obliterative phlebitis | **Characteristic** | Rarely noted |
| *Histology —* **granulocyte epithelial lesion** | **Absent** | **Characteristic** |
| *Histology —* periductal inflammation | Present | Present |
| *Histology —* IgG4 staining | **≥10 cells/HPF** | **<10 cells/HPF** |
| Response to steroids | **~100%** | **~100%** |
| **Relapse risk** | **Up to 60%** | **<10%** |

*Table 1 recreated ([[aga-2019-autoimmune-pancreatitis]]).*

- **Only two histologic findings discriminate:** the **granulocyte epithelial lesion** (IDCP) and **obliterative phlebitis** (AIP). The other four occur in both.
- **No reliable serologic marker exists for IDCP** — type 2 is a tissue diagnosis; the minority with a raised IgG4 gain no diagnostic weight from it.
- Apart from the IgG4 cells/HPF cutoff, the sources give no counts or definitions for the histologic features.

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[jaundice]] and [[recurrent-acute-pancreatitis]].*

- **[[pancreatic-cancer|Pancreatic adenocarcinoma]]** — the differential that defines this disease ([[#AIP vs Pancreatic Cancer on CT/MRI]]). **Assume the mimic until excluded**, and keep monitoring for occult malignancy afterwards.
- **Chronic pancreatitis of other cause** — AIP is **5%–6%** of all chronic pancreatitis and shares its long-term sequelae.
- **AIP vs IDCP** — each other's differential; separated by age, sex, serology, OOI, IBD association and histology ([[#Classification / Typing]]).
- **Other causes of a [[biliary-stricture|biliary stricture]]** — AIP produces both a distal stricture (head swelling) and a proximal one, so it enters that differential from both ends.

> [!pitfall] A mildly raised IgG4 does not exclude cancer
> Pancreatic cancer can elevate serum IgG4; **only 1% exceed 2× ULN (280 mg/dL)**, which is what gives the ≥2× cutoff its 99% specificity. Focal mass-like lesions are also **more common in IDCP (~35%)** — the type with no serologic marker ([[shimosegawa-2011-icdc-autoimmune-pancreatitis]], [[aga-2019-autoimmune-pancreatitis]]).

## Diagnostics

### Serum IgG4

| | Value |
|---|---|
| ICDC **level 1** cutoff (AIP) | **IgG4 >2× ULN** ([[shimosegawa-2011-icdc-autoimmune-pancreatitis]]); reported as **≥2× ULN = 280 mg/dL** by [[aga-2019-autoimmune-pancreatitis]] |
| ICDC **level 2** | **IgG4 1–2× ULN** |
| Sensitivity / specificity | **53% / 99%** |
| Elevated in AIP overall | **~66%** |
| False positives in pancreatic cancer | Elevated IgG4 can occur; **only 1% exceed 280 mg/dL** |
| Utility in **IDCP** | **None** — no reliable serologic marker for type 2 |
| Utility **after** treatment | **None** for predicting relapse or diagnosing recurrence ([[aga-2019-autoimmune-pancreatitis]]) — but see [[#Relapse and Maintenance]] |

- **Score the fold elevation, not the absolute value** — the ULN varies between laboratories; 280 mg/dL is the equivalent printed by [[aga-2019-autoimmune-pancreatitis]], not an ICDC cut-point.
- **Only serum IgG4 counts.** IgG, IgG4 and antinuclear antibody titres are all commonly raised in AIP and each is falsely raised in pancreatic cancer and other disorders — measured as a panel, false positivity reaches **up to 40%**.
- **A normal IgG4 does not exclude AIP** (sensitivity 53%): about half of patients never reach level 1 on serology, hence the four other cardinal features. A raised marker alone never diagnoses AIP without a typical imaging finding.
- Biliary IgG4 cut-points (for excluding IgG4-SC during a [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis|primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)]] workup) answer a different question → [[igg4-related-cholangitis]]. **Do not merge the thresholds.**

### Cross-Sectional Imaging

| Finding | Description | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **Diffuse parenchymal enlargement** | the **"sausage shape"** pancreas | Characteristic |
| **Low-attenuating capsule** | the **capsule-like "rim enhancement"** | Characteristic |
| **Focal mass-like lesion** | the dangerous one — overlaps pancreatic cancer, triggers the tissue pathway | **Less common** overall; **~35% in IDCP** |
| **Diffuse pancreatic atrophy** | | Less common |

### Ductal Imaging

- **[[mri-mrcp|Magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP)]] or [[ercp|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]]** — **diffuse or multifocal narrowing of the pancreatic duct *without upstream dilatation*.** **Absent upstream dilatation is the discriminator** from an obstructing tumour.
- **The D criterion rests on ERP**, which Western endoscopists generally avoid in suspected AIP, so a level 1 D finding is often unobtainable in US practice. Its place is as **collateral evidence** when parenchymal imaging is not typical, or in a seronegative patient without OOI.

### Histology and Tissue Acquisition

- **Core biopsy, not aspiration.** EUS-guided fine-needle **aspiration** (cytology) has **poor sensitivity even with larger-diameter needles** — the diagnosis is architectural (storiform fibrosis, obliterative phlebitis, periductal inflammation, granulocyte epithelial lesions). **EUS-guided fine-needle *biopsy* preserves gland architecture** and smaller cohort studies showed it safe and effective in AIP. Needle choice and yield: [[endoscopic-ultrasound]].
- **Ask for a core when:** **focal mass and/or obstructive jaundice, cancer excluded, diagnosis unclear** ([[aga-2019-autoimmune-pancreatitis]], citing the ICDC).
- **IgG4 immunostaining separates the types:** **≥10 cells/HPF (AIP)** vs **<10 cells/HPF (IDCP)**.
- **Do not re-biopsy after treatment** ([[#Monitoring After Treatment]]).

## Therapeutics

Both AIP and IDCP are managed **primarily medically**, with **close follow-up and monitoring for occult malignancy**.

### Who Is Treated

*Bracketed letters are [[okazaki-2017-aip-treatment-consensus]]'s own grades — it could not apply GRADE and used the United States Preventive Services Task Force scale: **A** strongly recommendable · **B** ordinarily recommendable · **C** unrecommendable · **D** strongly unrecommendable · **I** conflicting benefits and harms.*

| Patient | Treat? |
|---|---|
| **Symptomatic** — pancreatic: obstructive jaundice, [[acute-pancreatitis\|acute pancreatitis]], abdominal pain, back pain; **or** OOI, e.g. jaundice from a bile-duct stricture | **Indication for treatment** [B] |
| **Asymptomatic** | **Only** for a **persistent pancreatic mass on imaging**, **or** persistent liver function test abnormalities in the setting of **IgG4-SC / other organ involvement** [B] |
| **Asymptomatic, neither of those** | **Watchful waiting** — about **10%–25% improve spontaneously** without any intervention |

- **Urgent indications:** proximal biliary stricture with superimposed infectious cholangitis; irreversible hepatic fibrosis or cirrhosis; pancreatic involvement heading for irreversible exocrine and endocrine failure.

### Corticosteroids

- **Steroid is first-line in every patient with active untreated AIP unless contraindicated** [A]. Before starting, **control blood glucose and settle biliary drainage** where indicated.
- **Known or high risk of severe steroid intolerance → rituximab induction instead** (immunomodulators only if rituximab is unavailable). Rituximab can induce remission as a single agent [B]; **thiopurines and other steroid-sparing agents cannot** [C]. In a jaundiced patient its onset is delayed enough that **4–6 weeks of steroid cover may be needed first**.
- **Failure to respond is a diagnostic event, not a dosing one** — the only branch for non-response is to reconsider the diagnosis, in a disease whose chief mimic is pancreatic cancer.
- **Reassess imaging and serology within 1–2 weeks** of starting; taper against symptoms, liver enzymes, IgG/IgG4 and repeat imaging.
- Screen for hepatitis B before rituximab, an immunomodulator or steroids — reactivation risk, testing and monitoring: [[chronic-hepatitis-b]].

**Induction and taper ([[okazaki-2017-aip-treatment-consensus]]):**

| Step | Regimen |
|---|---|
| **Starting dose** | **Prednisone 0.6–1.0 mg/kg/day** [A], **minimum 20 mg/day** — below that the remission rate suffers [B]. Held **2–4 weeks** before tapering |
| **Taper** | **5–10 mg every 1–2 weeks down to 20 mg/day, then 5 mg every 2 weeks** [B] |
| **Alternative taper** | **40 mg/day × 4 weeks, then 5 mg/week until off** [B] — the regimen [[aga-2019-autoimmune-pancreatitis]] prints |
| **Total induction** | **Generally 12 weeks** [A] |
| **Not acceptable** | Induction **shorter than 4 weeks** at a high dose (**≥20 mg**) [C] |
| **Refractory induction** | **Steroid mini-pulse — methylprednisolone 500 mg × 3 days, two courses 4 days apart** |

### Biliary Drainage and Surgery

> [!pearl] The jaundice is treated with steroids, not a stent
> **[[ercp|ERCP]] and biliary stenting are *rarely* indicated** for the obstructive jaundice of AIP: in **mild jaundice without signs of infection, steroid treatment alone can be performed safely without biliary stenting** [B]. **Both qualifiers carry the decision** — *mild* jaundice and *no signs of infection*; neither source gives a bilirubin threshold for "mild" or says what to do above it ([[aga-2019-autoimmune-pancreatitis]], [[okazaki-2017-aip-treatment-consensus]]).

- The case *for* drainage: it **prevents biliary infection**, and **brushings separate IgG4-SC from biliary malignancy** [B] ([[brush-cytology|brush cytology]]).
- **71% of type 1 and 77% of type 2** jaundiced patients reached remission without a stent, at the same starting prednisolone dose with or without drainage (0.60 mg/kg/day).
- **Surgery is indicated for suspected malignancy** ([[aga-2019-autoimmune-pancreatitis]]); it **may also help some refractory cases** [B] — realistically, poor medical response that would otherwise mean long-term biliary stenting for continuing obstructive jaundice.

### Monitoring After Treatment

- **Do not follow serum IgG4 as a relapse test** — see [[#Relapse and Maintenance]] for the disagreement between the sources.
- **Do not re-biopsy** — histologic re-evaluation is not recommended after treatment.
- **Expect imperfect restitution.** Despite the efficacy of corticosteroids, **normal pancreatic morphology and function may not be restored** — as the inflammation resolves the **fibro-atrophic component becomes more prominent**. Persistent abnormal morphology is therefore not, by itself, treatment failure.
- Close follow-up for underlying malignancy is required, but **no modality, interval or duration is specified**.

### Relapse and Maintenance

- **Up to 60% of AIP relapse after initial response — vs <10% of IDCP.** At least one relapse in **31% of type 1 vs 9% of type 2**; **type 1 relapses hit the biliary system or the pancreas, type 2 only the pancreas.**
- **Timing: during the steroid taper or after discontinuation** — **67% after discontinuation**, 15% during taper, 18% on maintenance; **56% within 1 year, 76% within 2, 92% within 3** ([[okazaki-2017-aip-treatment-consensus]]).

**Relapse predictors** (the consensus prefaces its own list with *"risk factors for relapsing remain poorly understood"*):

| Predictor | Source |
|---|---|
| **Remarkably high pre-treatment serum IgG4 — e.g. >4× ULN** | [[okazaki-2017-aip-treatment-consensus]] [B] |
| **Persistently high serum IgG4 after steroid treatment** | [[okazaki-2017-aip-treatment-consensus]] [B] |
| **Diffuse enlargement of the pancreas** | [[okazaki-2017-aip-treatment-consensus]] [B] |
| **Proximal type of IgG4-SC** (56.1% relapse vs 25.7% without) | both |
| **Extensive multi-organ involvement (≥2 OOI)** | [[okazaki-2017-aip-treatment-consensus]] [B] |
| **Elevated pre-treatment immunoglobulin E (IgE)** | [[aga-2019-autoimmune-pancreatitis]] |

- **The sources disagree about serum IgG4 after treatment.** [[aga-2019-autoimmune-pancreatitis]] — the newer source, and the one this page follows — finds **no value in following serum IgG4** to predict relapse or diagnose recurrence. [[okazaki-2017-aip-treatment-consensus]] makes persistently high post-treatment IgG4 both a level-B predictor and a maintenance indication (below), and notes that **69% of relapsing patients re-elevate serum IgG4 before the relapse**. **Do not make the relapse decision on IgG4 alone** — the pre-treatment IgE, the pancreatic morphology and the OOI/stricture phenotype carry it.
- **What high risk buys:** long-term maintenance with steroids or an immunomodulator — **one of the four regimens in the [[#Management Algorithm]]**, which [[aga-2019-autoimmune-pancreatitis]] presents as alternatives with **no immunomodulator or rituximab dose and no criterion for choosing among them**.
- **Relapsed IDCP is handled differently** — repeat induction (**prednisone 40 mg/day × 4 weeks, taper 5 mg/week**), not one of the four.

**Who needs maintenance, and who does not ([[okazaki-2017-aip-treatment-consensus]]):**

| | |
|---|---|
| **No maintenance** | **Type 1 with low disease activity before treatment, and all type 2** [C]. Low activity = pancreas-only segmental/focal lesion, **no OOI**, complete radiological remission with normalized IgG/IgG4, rapid steroid response → **taper off within 3 months** |
| **Maintenance** | Type 1 with **diffuse pancreatic enlargement** · **delayed radiological remission** · **persistently high serum IgG4 (>2× ULN) after treatment** · **≥2 OOI** · **proximal IgG4-SC before treatment** [B] |
| **Maintenance regimen** | **Low-dose glucocorticoid 2.5–7.5 mg/day, up to 3 years**, stopping once radiological and serological improvement is established — or a steroid-sparing agent / rituximab |
| **Relapse treatment** | No gold standard [B]. **Re-administer or increase steroid — usually the same dose as the initial course, tapered more slowly.** Immunomodulators (thiopurines, mycophenolate mofetil, methotrexate) **require 6–8 weeks of overlap with steroids** and are **not effective as single agents**; azathioprine added for relapse induced remission in **85%** |
