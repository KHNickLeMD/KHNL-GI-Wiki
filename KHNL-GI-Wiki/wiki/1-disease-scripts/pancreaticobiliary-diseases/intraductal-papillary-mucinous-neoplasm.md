A mucin-producing intraductal neoplasm of the pancreas that communicates with the ductal system — the commonest pancreatic cyst and the commonest incidental one (**~80% of incidentally discovered pancreatic cysts are branch-duct IPMN**). It is a **precursor lesion**: it can progress from low-grade dysplasia through high-grade dysplasia to invasive carcinoma, and it independently marks the whole gland as at risk for a **separate, concomitant pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma**. ([[iap-2024-kyoto-ipmn]])

Cyst typing against the other cystic lesions — mucinous cystic neoplasm, serous cystadenoma, pseudocyst, solid pseudopapillary neoplasm — and the cyst-fluid carcinoembryonic antigen and amylase cut-offs live on [[pancreatic-cysts]].

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

Diagnosis is made **on imaging first**, and on pathology only for lesions that are resected. ([[iap-2024-kyoto-ipmn]])

| Type | Imaging definition |
|---|---|
| **Branch-duct IPMN (BD-IPMN)** | Pancreatic cyst **>5 mm** communicating with the main pancreatic duct (MPD). Rule out a pseudocyst where there is a history of [[acute-pancreatitis\|acute pancreatitis]] or trauma |
| **Main-duct IPMN (MD-IPMN)** | **Segmental or diffuse MPD dilation >5 mm** without another cause of duct obstruction |
| **Mixed IPMN** | Meets the criteria for both |

- Most patients with a small BD-IPMN are **asymptomatic**. IPMN-related symptoms when present: abdominal distention or pain, back pain (from pancreatitis), jaundice.
- **Blood work:** blood counts, transaminases, bilirubin, biliary and pancreatic enzymes, plus **carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA19-9) and haemoglobin A1c (HbA1c)**. Raised tumor markers and unexpected new or worsening diabetes suggest invasive carcinoma.
- A **patulous, "fish-mouth" ampullary orifice** is seen in ~50% of main-duct IPMN ([[acg-2018-pancreatic-cysts]]).
- **Asymptomatic cysts ≤5 mm:** management remains controversial; surveillance is still recommended for the time being.

### Severity Assessment

Risk of **high-grade dysplasia (HGD) or invasive carcinoma (IC)** is stratified by **high-risk stigmata (HRS)** and **worrisome features (WF)**. The guideline keeps these terms rather than "absolute" and "relative" indication for surgery, because the operative decision also turns on the patient's general condition, comorbidity, life expectancy and preference. ([[iap-2024-kyoto-ipmn]])

> [!pitfall] Thresholds unchanged since 2017 — two proposed revisions were rejected
> The panel considered raising the enhancing-mural-nodule threshold to ≥10 mm and making **any** MPD dilation ≥5 mm a worrisome feature, and **rejected both** for want of supporting evidence. Evidence it cites itself favours 10 mm (odds ratio 7.90 for HGD/IC, vs 2.93 for a 5–10 mm nodule), but **5 mm remains the operative threshold** ([[iap-2024-kyoto-ipmn]]).

#### High-Risk Stigmata

1. **Obstructive jaundice** in a patient with a cystic lesion of the **head** of the pancreas
2. **Enhancing mural nodule ≥5 mm**, **or a solid component**
3. **Main pancreatic duct ≥10 mm**
4. **Suspicious or positive cytology** (if cytology was performed — an option, not a requirement)

- **Mural nodule vs solid component cannot be reliably separated preoperatively; both count.** A mural nodule protrudes from the cyst wall and usually indicates a non-invasive lesion; a solid component is a mass in the parenchyma and raises the possibility of IC or a concomitant [[pancreatic-cancer|pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC)]].
- **Measure a mural nodule by *height* on [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]]** — not width, not maximal diameter. On computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) it is measured as maximal diameter.
- Obstructive jaundice is rare in IPMN but strongly predictive: sensitivity 75–83%, specificity 61–65%. Even a benign IPMN causing jaundice usually needs an operation for symptom relief.
- Cytology graded **"suspicious"** carries an absolute HGD/IC risk of **91–100%**, **"positive"** of **100%** — which is why cytology joined the stigmata.
- **Each stigma alone is weaker than its status suggests:** nodule presence alone gives an odds ratio of only 1.19–3.16 for HGD/IC, and MPD ≥10 mm alone 1.06–1.76.

#### Worrisome Features

| Clinical | Imaging |
|---|---|
| Acute pancreatitis | Cyst **≥30 mm** |
| Increased serum CA19-9 | Enhancing mural nodule **<5 mm** |
| **New onset or acute exacerbation of diabetes within the past year** | Thickened/enhancing cyst walls |
| | MPD **≥5 mm and <10 mm** |
| | Abrupt change in duct caliber **with distal pancreatic atrophy** |
| | Lymphadenopathy |
| | **Cystic growth rate ≥2.5 mm/year** |

*Worrisome features for high-grade dysplasia or invasive carcinoma in IPMN*
*Source: redrawn from the IAP 2024 Kyoto guideline, Fig. 3 worrisome-feature box*
*[[iap-2024-kyoto-ipmn|IAP 2024 Kyoto Guidelines for IPMN]]*
*CA19-9 = carbohydrate antigen 19-9 · MPD = main pancreatic duct*

- **The two new entries in 2024** are new-onset/exacerbated diabetes and the growth-rate criterion, which replaced the previous ≥5 mm per 2 years. New-onset diabetes occurs in **25%** of IPMN patients and raises the risk of HGD (risk ratio 1.27) and IC (risk ratio 1.61). The guideline does **not** define "new onset" or "acute exacerbation".
- **CA19-9 >37 U/L** for IPMN with IC: sensitivity 41–74%, specificity 85–96%.
- **Growth rate:** reported predictive rates span 0.96–3.5 mm/year; **2.5 mm/year is the most frequently reported and is the one adopted**.
- **"Thickened/enhancing cyst wall" has no established measurement method or cut-off** — the guideline calls it an ambiguous factor. One report gives an odds ratio of 3.51 for HGD/IC when septal thickness is ≥2.5 mm on EUS, comparable to the 3.36 for a ≥5 mm nodule.
- **Acute pancreatitis is a worrisome feature but not a malignancy marker:** larger series find the same HGD/IC incidence in IPMN patients with pancreatitis as in those with low-grade dysplasia. Its mechanism is MPD obstruction by viscous mucin or tumour stenosis. **Repeated episodes are an operative indication on quality-of-life grounds**, independent of dysplasia grade.
- **Rising MPD diameter was considered and *not* adopted** as a worrisome feature — the evidence on this dynamic measure is lacking.

#### Multiple Features Are Additive

**Risk of HGD/IC by number of worrisome features: 1 → 22% · 2 → 34% · 3 → 59% · ≥4 → 100%.** Where several HRS/WF are positive, the guideline weakly recommends using a **nomogram** to weigh the operative indication (reported area under the curve 0.739–0.955). ([[iap-2024-kyoto-ipmn]])

- Nomogram limitations the guideline names: several do not distinguish BD- from MD-IPMN, resected-IPMN numbers are small in some regions, and external validation is lacking. Pick the one best fitted to the local population.

### Classification / Typing

#### By Duct Involvement

Risk of HGD/IC **in resected specimens** ([[iap-2024-kyoto-ipmn]]):

| Type | HGD or IC | Invasive carcinoma |
|---|---|---|
| **BD-IPMN** | **31%** (15–48%) | **19%** (6–38%) |
| **MD-IPMN** | **62%** (36–100%) | **43%** (11–81%) |

- **These are resection-series figures.** Because the great majority of BD-IPMNs are never resected, the true BD-IPMN rate is much lower — the real message is that **MD-IPMN carries a markedly higher risk**. Mixed IPMN behaves like main-duct disease ([[acg-2018-pancreatic-cysts]]).
- **Mixed IPMN categories were left unchanged in 2024** despite acknowledged discordance in both directions: many imaging-"mixed" lesions are really BD-IPMNs whose MPD is dilated by mucin hypersecretion, while many imaging-"branch-duct" lesions have pathological MPD involvement without duct dilation.

#### By Morphological Subtype

| Subtype | Behaviour | Associated invasive carcinoma |
|---|---|---|
| **Gastric** | Most frequent; mostly low-grade BD-IPMN; **best prognosis** | Tubular ductal adenocarcinoma |
| **Intestinal** | Intermediate | **Colloid carcinoma** — prognostically more favourable than ordinary PDAC |
| **Pancreatobiliary** | **Highest risk of neoplastic progression** | Tubular ductal adenocarcinoma |

- Subtype adds prognostic information **beyond** grade of dysplasia, and predicts HGD/IC risk in the remnant pancreas. Where several subtypes coexist in one lesion, assign the subtype of the **highest grade of dysplasia**.
- ⚠ **Low-grade gastric IPMN is not a no-risk lesion** — molecular work shows it is the ancestor of co-occurring pancreatobiliary and intestinal high-grade lesions.
- **Intraductal oncocytic papillary neoplasm (IOPN)** — previously the "oncocytic subtype" — is **a distinct entity, separated from IPMN** on the basis of recurrent *PRKACA* and *PRKACB* fusion genes.
- **Terminology:** "carcinoma in situ" is an acceptable synonym for HGD. **"Malignant IPMN" is discouraged** for lack of clarity. HGD and IC components are reported **separately**, and the **size of the invasive component is measured and reported separately** from the IPMN.

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[pancreatic-cysts]].*

- [[pancreatic-cysts|Mucinous cystic neoplasm (MCN)]] — no duct communication, ovarian-type stroma, **GNAS wild-type**
- [[pancreatic-cysts|Serous cystic neoplasm]] — *VHL* mutation without *KRAS* or *GNAS*
- [[pancreatic-fluid-collection-drainage|Pancreatic pseudocyst]] — history of pancreatitis or trauma; non-mucinous, high amylase
- **Retention cyst** — flat columnar lining epithelium with upstream obstruction
- **Intraductal tubulopapillary neoplasm** — no mucin secretion, no MUC5AC expression
- **Large-duct-type [[pancreatic-cancer|PDAC]]** — invasive dilated ducts without ductal contour
- [[chronic-pancreatitis|Chronic pancreatitis]] with duct dilation — the main alternative cause of a dilated MPD

## Diagnostics

### Imaging

- **Primary modalities: contrast-enhanced MRI with magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP), and contrast-enhanced multi-detector CT.**
- **CT, MRI and EUS are equivalent for diagnosing IPMN with HGD/IC.** Diagnostic accuracy for a pancreatic cystic lesion is **47–78%**; for predicting HGD/IC, **73–97%**.
- MRI and EUS avoid radiation; MRI is limited by cost and availability in some regions, and EUS is **highly operator-dependent**.
- **FDG-PET/CT:** little evidence — sensitivity 62–82%, specificity 71–100% at maximum standardized uptake values of 1.3–3.0.

### EUS and EUS-FNA

**EUS, including contrast-enhanced EUS and EUS-guided fine-needle aspiration (FNA), is recommended where HGD/IC is suspected and the setting allows.** It is **not** recommended in every case. ([[iap-2024-kyoto-ipmn]])

> [!pitfall] Do not aspirate when the answer will not change the plan
> **Skip EUS-FNA when high-risk stigmata are already obvious on CT or MRI and surgery will follow regardless.** Cyst-fluid cytology sensitivity is only **28.7% (4.8–61.5%)**, needle-tract seeding is reported at **0.3%**, and peritoneal seeding after preoperative FNA in surgical IPMN series was **2.3% vs 4.4%** without FNA (p = 0.403) — every seeded case proving HGD/IC on pathology ([[iap-2024-kyoto-ipmn]]).

- A **positive** cytology result, though insensitive, strongly influences management — it is one of the four high-risk stigmata.
- Contrast enhancement improves EUS's ability to confirm a mural nodule and measure it.

### Cyst Fluid Markers

| Purpose | Marker | Sensitivity | Specificity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Is it mucinous?** | *KRAS* and/or *GNAS* mutation | 79% | 98% |
| | CEA **>192 ng/mL** | 58% | 87% |
| | Glucose **<50 ng/dL** | 93% | 89% |
| **Is it HGD/IC?** | *TP53*, *SMAD4*, *CDKN2A*, *PIK3CA* | 9–39% | 92–98% |

*Cyst-fluid markers for distinguishing IPMN and for detecting advanced neoplasia*
*Source: data from the systematic review underpinning IAP 2024 Kyoto clinical questions 5-1 and 5-2*
*[[iap-2024-kyoto-ipmn|IAP 2024 Kyoto Guidelines for IPMN]]*
*CEA = carcinoembryonic antigen · HGD = high-grade dysplasia · IC = invasive carcinoma*

- ***GNAS* mutation at codon 201 is exclusive to IPMN** and is what separates it from MCN. ***VHL* mutation with neither *KRAS* nor *GNAS* gives >99% sensitivity for a serous cystic neoplasm.**
- ⚠ **Cyst-fluid CEA is of limited use for IPMN:** up to **30% of IPMNs have low levels**, it does **not** separate IPMN from MCN, and its concentration does **not** correlate with grade of dysplasia.
- **Same gate as EUS-FNA itself:** sample for molecular markers only **when the cyst diagnosis is unclear and the result will alter surveillance**.
- **Tissue mutations do not help prognostically.** *KRAS* (60–70%), *GNAS* (50–70%) and *RNF43* (15%) are the frequent mutations; *KRAS* is a founder and *TP53*, *CDKN2A* and *SMAD4* are progressor mutations. Neither *KRAS* nor *GNAS* is reliably associated with the prognosis of a resected IPMN. Genetic studies show multifocality and polyclonality — **IPMN behaves as a disease of the entire pancreas**.

### What Not to Do

- **Routine [[ercp|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]] for pancreatic-juice cytology is not recommended** — sensitivity for HGD/IC only 10–50%, plus the risk of post-ERCP pancreatitis. ERCP's role in IPMN is **biliary drainage in the jaundiced patient**.
- **Pancreatoscopy is not a routine examination.** It can help define the resection line in MD- and mixed IPMN, but it overdiagnoses (a low-papillary MPD lesion may be low-grade dysplasia) and risks peritoneal dissemination. If needed, do it **preoperatively** rather than intraoperatively.
- **No additional screening for extra-pancreatic neoplasms is necessary** — they occur in 20–30% of IPMN patients, but at population-based rates.

## Therapeutics

### Management Algorithm

![[ipmn-2024-kyoto-management-algorithm-07.png|780x978]]
*Management of suspected branch-duct IPMN: high-risk stigmata, then worrisome features, then surveillance interval by largest cyst size*
*Source: figure 3 of IAP 2024 Kyoto guidelines, open access (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0)*
*[[iap-2024-kyoto-ipmn|IAP 2024 Kyoto Guidelines for IPMN]]*
*HGD = high-grade dysplasia · IC = invasive carcinoma · MRI = magnetic resonance imaging · MRCP = magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography · MDCT = multi-detector computed tomography · EUS = endoscopic ultrasound · CA19-9 = carbohydrate antigen 19-9*

**The three factors that push a worrisome-feature lesion toward surgery rather than surveillance**, per the algorithm's third box:
1. **Repeated acute pancreatitis** degrading quality of life
2. **Multiple worrisome features** augmenting the likelihood of HGD/IC (consider a nomogram)
3. **Young, fit for surgery**

- None of the three present, and the only worrisome feature is a **cyst ≥30 mm alone** → surveillance.
- None of the three present, with worrisome features other than size alone → **surveillance at 1–6-month intervals according to estimated risk**.

### Surveillance of Non-Resected IPMN

**Progression risk depends on the size at diagnosis** — BD-IPMN **<10 mm** progresses to a worrisome feature in **4.8%** at a median 54 months; **10–20 mm** in **10%** at 55 months; **20–30 mm** in **48.8%** at a median 23 months. The interval follows that gradient. ([[iap-2024-kyoto-ipmn]])

| Largest cyst | Protocol (after an initial 6-month check) |
|---|---|
| **<20 mm** | 6 months **once**, then every **18 months** if stable |
| **≥20 and <30 mm** | 6 months **twice**, then every **12 months** if stable |
| **≥30 mm** | **Every 6 months** |

*Surveillance interval for non-resected branch-duct IPMN by largest cyst size*
*Source: redrawn from IAP 2024 Kyoto clinical question 2-2 (evidence level 2+, grade B)*
*[[iap-2024-kyoto-ipmn|IAP 2024 Kyoto Guidelines for IPMN]]*

- **Modality:** MRI, plus physical examination, tumour marker and assessment for new-onset diabetes. Add CT and EUS **when the MRI changes**.
- **Progression at any point → back to the top of the algorithm.**
- **Cumulative transformation of an indolent BD-IPMN to HGD/IC:** 0.94–3.3% at 5 years, 2.3–6.6% at 10 years, 7.6–15.0% at 15 years.

**When surveillance may stop** — the 2024 guideline's headline change:

- **Stable cyst <20 mm, no morphological change and no worrisome features over 5 years** → **two acceptable options: stop, or continue for possible concomitant PDAC.** The guideline presents these as equally acceptable and does not pick one.
- **Stop** for a patient **unfit for surgery**, or with a **life expectancy <10 years**.
- ⚠ **Discontinuation may not apply to younger patients, or to those with familial or genetic risk** — in them pancreatic-cancer risk is cumulative over time.

### Resection

Operative principles follow the 2017 guidelines ([[iap-2024-kyoto-ipmn]]):

| Situation | Operation |
|---|---|
| **IC suspected** | **Radical pancreatectomy with lymph node dissection** |
| **Low suspicion of IC** | **Organ-preserving pancreatectomy without lymphadenectomy** — middle pancreatectomy, spleen-preserving distal pancreatectomy. Laparoscopic and robotic approaches available |
| **MD- or mixed IPMN** | Same choice by suspicion of IC, but **extension along the MPD is the concern and the goal is a negative margin** |
| **Prophylaxis** | **Total pancreatectomy is not recommended prophylactically** — though the possibility must be disclosed to every patient |

**Margins and frozen section** — intraoperative frozen section is recommended even after macroscopically complete resection, to rule out unexpected MPD involvement:

- **Low-grade dysplasia at the margin does not justify completion pancreatectomy.** Evidence on its effect on recurrence is mixed, and the margin lesion is often an independent neoplasm.
- **HGD or IC at the margin warrants clinical consideration** of additional resection — but **leaving HGD may be appropriate where invasive cancer is already present** in the specimen, since prognosis is set by the invasive component. Relevant in the older or frail patient facing total pancreatectomy.
- ⚠ **A denuded duct is not a negative margin.** If possible, resect further.
- **Colloid-type HGD/IC at the margin progresses much more slowly and is far more curable than tubular-type PDAC at the margin.**
- **HGD and IC are often grossly indistinguishable from LGD**, so a representative frozen section cannot exclude them.

> [!pearl] Four things every IPMN patient is told before surgery
> (1) The surgeon will usually be choosing in the operating room without a final diagnosis. (2) Resection may extend to **total pancreatectomy** if the margins show HGD or IC. (3) Low-grade dysplasia at the cut margin, or a small indolent branch-duct lesion, may be **deliberately left behind**. (4) **Long-term surveillance is needed even after partial pancreatectomy for low-grade dysplasia with a negative margin** — because of multifocality and skip progression ([[iap-2024-kyoto-ipmn]]).

**Ablation and systemic therapy:**
- **Cyst ablation (ethanol ± gemcitabine or paclitaxel) should only be done within a clinical trial.** Reported efficacy 9–72%, adverse events 9–23%, commonest being pancreatitis.
- No established role for chemotherapy or radiotherapy in unresectable IPMN with IC, nor for adjuvant or neoadjuvant therapy in resectable disease; in practice ordinary [[pancreatic-cancer|PDAC]] regimens are used. Node-positive, MUC2-negative tubular carcinoma may benefit from adjuvant chemotherapy.

### Surveillance After Resection

**Resection does not end the risk.** After margin-negative partial pancreatectomy for *non-invasive* IPMN, the **median 5-year risk of a clinically significant remnant lesion is 10% (0–21%)**, and the risk keeps rising after 5 years. ([[iap-2024-kyoto-ipmn]])

| Measure | Median incidence after partial pancreatectomy for non-invasive IPMN |
|---|---|
| All remnant pancreatic lesions | **19%** (8–28%) |
| **Clinically significant** (would be an indication for resection) | **10%** (0–21%) |
| **Invasive** remnant lesions | **4%** (1–10%) |

| Risk profile | Imaging interval |
|---|---|
| **No additional risk factor** | **Yearly** |
| **HGD on the initial pathology, or family history of pancreatic cancer** | **Every 6 months** |
| **Total pancreatectomy for a non-invasive lesion** | IPMN-specific surveillance **may stop after 5 uneventful years** |

- **Continue surveillance as long as the patient remains fit for further therapeutic intervention** — the duration rule, not a fixed number of years.
- **The two risk factors for a clinically significant remnant lesion are HGD on the initial pathology and a family history of pancreatic cancer.** HGD marks an aggressive phenotype that spreads through the gland by intraductal **skip progression**.
- **Imaging predictors of HGD/IC in the remnant:** solid component, MPD dilatation, growth of a cystic lesion. This is the guideline's **weakest-graded statement (grade D)**.
- ⚠ **A dilated MPD after pancreatoduodenectomy or middle pancreatectomy may be pancreato-enteric anastomotic stenosis, not neoplastic progression.** Examine which it is before acting.
- **Three mechanisms** of a remnant lesion: new or progressing multifocal IPMN · true recurrence of the resected lesion (margin-positive local recurrence or margin-negative skip progression) · concomitant PDAC. **Only the second is real recurrence.**
- After resection of **IPMN with invasive carcinoma**, recurrence patterns (liver, peritoneum, node, lung) and stage-for-stage survival match ordinary PDAC — follow [[pancreatic-cancer]].

### Concomitant Pancreatic Cancer

**"Dual carcinogenesis" is the central idea:** an IPMN can progress along the adenoma–carcinoma sequence *and* a PDAC can arise **elsewhere in the same pancreas**. ([[iap-2024-kyoto-ipmn]])

- **Yearly incidence of concomitant PDAC 0.4–1.0%; risk about 3–5× the age-matched population.** IPMN is therefore a risk factor for pancreatic cancer in its own right.
- ⚠ **The IPMNs in patients who develop concomitant PDAC are typically small branch-duct lesions, and the risk continues past 5 years of surveillance** — which is exactly why "continue surveillance" remains an option for the stable small cyst.
- **Geographic caveat the panel states plainly:** most of these data come from Japan; in other countries the concomitant-PDAC risk in IPMN patients appears **similar to the general population**. The difference may reflect Japanese pathology practice (whole resected pancreas cut in 5 mm slices) and heightened awareness.
- **Family history of pancreatic cancer** = pancreatic cancer in **one or more first-degree relatives**. Keep it distinct from **[[familial-pancreatic-cancer|familial pancreatic cancer]]**, defined by germline mutations such as *ATM*, *BRCA1/2*, *MLH1*, *CDKN2A*. **Family history raises the risk of concomitant PDAC but not of LGD→HGD/IC progression within the IPMN.**

### Multifocal Disease

- **Multifocal BD-IPMN occurs in 20–40%**, the lesions mostly arising independently under a "field defect".
- **Multifocality does not increase the risk of HGD/IC.**
- **Manage and survey by the highest-risk lesion.** Where surgery is indicated, resect **only that lesion** and leave the others — avoiding prophylactic total pancreatectomy.
