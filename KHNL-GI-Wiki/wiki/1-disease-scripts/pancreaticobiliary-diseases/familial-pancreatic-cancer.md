## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Familial pancreatic cancer (FPC) kindred** = a family with **≥2 first-degree relatives (FDRs)** with [[pancreatic-cancer|pancreatic cancer]] who do **not** meet criteria for a known pancreatic-cancer–associated hereditary syndrome ([[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening|American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2022]]; [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2015]] states the same consensus definition).
- **Diagnosis of exclusion** — a recognized syndrome that accounts for the cluster means it is not FPC, and a positive germline result reclassifies the kindred out of FPC (see [[#Differential Diagnosis]]).
- **Inheritance:** segregation analysis of multi-case families shows an **autosomal-dominant** pattern; modeling studies suggest a rare allele as the likely etiology. **No causative gene has been identified** — that is what separates FPC from the named syndromes (ACG 2015).
- Known susceptibility-gene variants explain only **~10%–20%** of the familial clustering of pancreatic cancer (ACG 2015: ~20%; [[caps-2020-pancreatic-surveillance|International Cancer of the Pancreas Screening (CAPS) Consortium 2020]]: ~10%–20%).
- Up to **10%** of patients with pancreatic cancer have a first- or second-degree relative with pancreatic cancer (ACG 2015).

> [!pearl] A negative gene panel does not end eligibility
> Most familial clustering has no identified gene, so a kindred that meets the family-history criteria stays eligible for surveillance after a normal panel ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]], [[caps-2020-pancreatic-surveillance]]).

**Broader "at risk for familial pancreatic adenocarcinoma"** — an ungraded ACG 2015 summary statement that triggers evaluation rather than defining the kindred; any one of (syndromes and genes: [[#Differential Diagnosis]]):

1. a known genetic syndrome associated with pancreatic cancer; **OR**
2. **two relatives** with pancreatic adenocarcinoma, **one of them an FDR**; **OR**
3. **≥3 relatives** with pancreatic cancer; **OR**
4. a history of hereditary pancreatitis.

Criteria 2 and 3 take in families that the ≥2-FDR definition of an FPC kindred excludes.

### Severity Assessment

Risk inside an FPC kindred tracks the **number of pancreatic-cancer–affected FDRs**:

| Affected FDRs in the kindred | Relative risk of pancreatic cancer |
|---|---|
| 1 or 2 | **4- to 7-fold** |
| ≥3 | **17- to 32-fold** |

*Relative risks as published in [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer|ACG 2015]] Table 12.*

- **Lifetime risk:** segregation analysis gives **32% by age 85** in FPC families (ACG 2015); two affected FDRs ≈ **8%** (CAPS 2020). Average United States lifetime risk is ~1 in 64 — too low for population-based screening (CAPS 2020).
- **PancPRO** risk-prediction model refines the individual estimate using ages at diagnosis, family size, and the relationships between affected members (ACG 2015).
- **Family history that does not meet FPC criteria** — ~**2-fold** risk; degree of relatedness and age at onset in the index case do not appear to change it, and screening is **generally not recommended** (ASGE 2022).

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup, modality comparison, yield and harms across all the high-risk conditions: see [[pancreatic-cancer-screening|high-risk pancreatic cancer surveillance]].*

Hereditary syndromes that must be excluded before a kindred is called FPC — each has its own gene, risk magnitude, and surveillance start age:

| Syndrome (gene) | Relative risk of pancreatic cancer |
|---|---|
| [[peutz-jeghers-syndrome\|Peutz-Jeghers syndrome (PJS)]] (STK11/LKB1) | up to 132-fold |
| [[hereditary-pancreatitis\|Hereditary pancreatitis]] (PRSS1) | 53-fold |
| [[fammm-syndrome\|Familial atypical multiple mole melanoma (FAMMM)]] (CDKN2A) | 13- to 39-fold |
| [[lynch-syndrome\|Lynch syndrome]] (MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2) | 9- to 11-fold |
| [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis\|Familial adenomatous polyposis]] (APC) | 5-fold |
| Hereditary breast and ovarian cancer ([[brca-pathogenic-variants\|BRCA2]]) | 3- to 9-fold |
| Hereditary breast and ovarian cancer ([[brca-pathogenic-variants\|BRCA1]]) | ~2-fold |
| Ataxia-telangiectasia (ATM) | ~3-fold |
| Fanconi anemia / breast cancer (PALB2) | no risk estimate available |
| Li-Fraumeni syndrome (p53) | unknown |

*Relative risks as published in [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer|ACG 2015]] Table 12.*

## Diagnostics

- **Pedigree, not a gene test, makes the diagnosis** — FPC is defined by family structure plus the absence of a syndromic explanation.
- **Offer germline testing to anyone eligible for pancreatic surveillance** (CAPS 2020).
- **Panel for suspected FPC:** **BRCA1/2, CDKN2A, PALB2, and ATM**; add PJS-, Lynch- and hereditary-pancreatitis–associated genes when the personal or family history meets those criteria (ACG 2015).
- **Yield of germline testing in FPC kindreds without criteria for a known syndrome** (ACG 2015 Table 13):

| Gene | Yield of testing |
|---|---|
| BRCA1 | 0–6% |
| BRCA2 | 0–6% |
| CDKN2A | 0–20% |
| PALB2 | 0–5% |
| ATM | 2.4% |
| STK11 | 0% |
| PALLD | 0% |

*Yield is unknown for Lynch syndrome, familial adenomatous polyposis and Li-Fraumeni syndrome ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer|ACG 2015]] Table 13).*

- **Surveillance imaging:** [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]], [[mri-mrcp|magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)]], or EUS alternating with MRI — chosen by patient preference and available expertise (ASGE 2022, conditional recommendation, very low quality of evidence). Comparative yield and technique: [[pancreatic-cancer-screening]].

## Therapeutics

### Who Qualifies for Surveillance

| Source | Who in an FPC kindred is offered surveillance |
|---|---|
| [[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening\|ASGE 2022]] (Rec 6d) | **All first-degree relatives** of affected family members |
| [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer\|ACG 2015]] (Rec 22, conditional/very low) | Kindred members **with a pancreatic-cancer–affected FDR**; ideally at an experienced centre, multidisciplinary, under research conditions |
| [[caps-2020-pancreatic-surveillance\|CAPS 2020]] (Table 1) | Any one of the three family-history patterns below — **no germline mutation required** |

CAPS 2020 family-history patterns, regardless of gene mutation status ("relative" means blood relative):

| Family-history pattern | Agreement | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| **≥3** affected relatives **on the same side of the family**, ≥1 of them an FDR of the individual considered for surveillance | 97% | 2 |
| **≥2** affected relatives who are **FDRs of each other**, ≥1 of them an FDR of the individual considered | 93% | 2 |
| **≥2** affected relatives **on the same side of the family**, ≥1 of them an FDR of the individual considered | 88% | 2 |

*CAPS action grades: 1 = definitely do it · 2 = probably do it · 3 = no recommendation · 4 = probably don't do it · 5 = definitely don't do it; consensus = ≥75% agreement.*

> [!pitfall] Affected relatives alone do not make someone eligible
> Every CAPS pattern also requires that **at least one affected relative is a first-degree relative of the person being considered** — two affected cousins on the same side does not qualify them ([[caps-2020-pancreatic-surveillance]]).

### Start Age, Interval, and Findings

- **Start:** age **50**, or **10 years earlier than the youngest relative with pancreatic cancer** — whichever comes first (ASGE 2022 Rec 6d; ACG 2015 Rec 23, conditional/very low). CAPS 2020 statement 1 agrees (grade 2); **age 55** was an accepted alternative (grade 2) and **age 45** was voted down (grade 4 — probably don't).
- **Interval:** **annual** (ASGE 2022 Rec 4, conditional/very low; ACG 2015 Rec 23). CAPS 2020 sets **12 months** when surveillance finds no pancreatic abnormality (statement 9, 90.4%, grade 2).
- **A pancreatic cystic lesion found on surveillance** in a hereditary-pancreatic-cancer–prone family member carries a higher cancer risk than a sporadic cyst and requires evaluation at a centre experienced with these patients; the decision to operate is difficult and is best individualized after multidisciplinary assessment (ACG 2015 Rec 24, conditional/low). Lesion strata, intervals after an abnormal finding, and the operative thresholds are on [[pancreatic-cancer-screening]].
- **Stopping age:** CAPS 2020 reached no consensus on when to stop surveillance; see [[pancreatic-cancer-screening]].
