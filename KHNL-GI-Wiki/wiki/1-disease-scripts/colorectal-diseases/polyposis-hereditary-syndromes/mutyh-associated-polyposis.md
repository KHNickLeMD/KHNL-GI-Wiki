*MUTYH-associated polyposis (MAP) is an autosomal recessive attenuated polyposis syndrome, surveilled and operated on like attenuated familial adenomatous polyposis (AFAP). The shared Spigelman staging, gastric-polyposis and colectomy tables live on [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis|familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP)]].*

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Genetics:** biallelic ***MUTYH*** germline pathogenic variants (GPVs); autosomal **recessive** — unique among the polyposis syndromes ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]]). ***MUTYH*** is a **base-excision repair** gene that excises mispaired adenine residues from oxidised DNA; loss produces a signature **G:C → T:A transversion** burden ([[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]])
- **Prevalence:** population **1:20,000 to 1:60,000**; single-*MUTYH*-GPV carrier frequency **1%–2%** in US, European and Australian populations
- **Y179C and G396D** (formerly Y165C / G382D) are the two most prevalent variants — present in **>80%** of individuals of European ancestry with MAP, and ~90% of "western" MAP patients carry at least one. **Full gene sequencing** is offered, since other variants cause MAP in non-European populations ([[asge-2020-fap]])
- **Colorectal phenotype:** usually **10–100 adenomas**, typically fewer than 100, although **100–1,000 has been reported** ([[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]] Table 5). [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer|ACG 2015]] described **20–99 adenomas** most commonly, rarely >500
- Adenoma predominates, but **hyperplastic polyps and sessile serrated lesions** occur — 47% of a 17-patient series had at least one, and ~18% met [[serrated-polyposis-syndrome|serrated polyposis syndrome (SPS)]] criteria
- **Take a complete family history including consanguinity** — recessive inheritance is easy to miss ([[asge-2020-fap]])

> [!pearl] A normal-looking colon does not exclude MAP
> In 8 population-based studies, **35% (28/79)** of MAP patients with [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer (CRC)]] had **no concurrent polyps** and 22% had **<10 adenomas** ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]]). CRC can occur in the absence of adenomas, and polyp number may not predict CRC risk ([[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]]).

|---|---|

| 20–99 | **4.5%–20%** |

**Who to test**

- **Send *MUTYH* testing** ([[asge-2020-fap]]) for any one of: **≥20 colorectal adenomas over multiple colonoscopies**; a **known family history of MAP**; **≥10 adenomas on a single colonoscopy**; or **SPS criteria met with at least some adenomas** present.
- [[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis|ACG 2026]] instead triggers **multigene panel testing (MGPT)** — which includes *MUTYH* — on cumulative lifetime adenoma count: **≥20 (strong), 10–19 (conditional)**. See [[colorectal-polyposis]].
- Testing for suspected adenomatous polyposis includes **both *APC* and *MUTYH*** ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]]) — a negative *APC* test alone does not exclude polyposis.
- **Test at-risk relatives at 18–20 years** *([[asge-2020-fap|ASGE 2020]] Rec 2)* — deliberately deferred out of childhood so the individual can make their own informed decision, since MAP's onset and its screening both begin in adulthood (contrast **FAP, tested at 10–12**). Once a proband is confirmed, screen their relatives for *MUTYH* variants.

### Severity Assessment

- **CRC is already present at MAP diagnosis in ~60%** of cases ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]]); [[asge-2020-fap|ASGE 2020]] puts the CRC risk at roughly **28× the general population**
- Average age at CRC onset **48 years**; **earliest reported CRC at age 21** — the reason screening starts at 18–20
- Older same-tier estimates of cumulative CRC risk: **19% by age 50 and 43% by age 60** ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]]); **63% by age 60** ([[asge-2020-fap]])
- 5-year survival after CRC in MAP **78% vs 63%** in matched general-population CRC controls (147 cases, 272 controls; hazard ratio (HR) 0.48, advantage persisting after adjustment) ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]])
- **Extracolonic lesions in 17%** of a 276-patient cohort, with an estimated **38% lifetime risk of extracolonic malignancy** — about double the general population ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]])
- **Desmoids, congenital hypertrophy of the retinal pigment epithelium (CHRPE), osteomas and hepatoblastoma are rare in MAP** — a review of 77 articles covering 2,109 MAP patients found desmoids in only 3

- In a prospective Colon Cancer Family Registry cohort of 41 MAP patients, **only bladder (HR 19; 95% CI 3.7–97) and ovarian (HR 17; 2.4–115) cancers were significantly elevated**
- Gastric findings in the same 150-patient study: **adenomas 2.7%, fundic gland polyps (FGPs) 6%**. [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer|ACG 2015]] reported gastric lesions in up to **11%** with no established increase in [[gastric-adenocarcinoma|gastric cancer]] risk — the newer SIR of 4.2 is consistent with that, its confidence interval crossing 1

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[colorectal-polyposis]]. MAP is managed like AFAP; see [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis]] for the shared staging, gastric and surgical tables.*

- **Classic FAP / AFAP** — *APC* germline variant; autosomal **dominant** (vs MAP's recessive inheritance); overlapping 10–100 adenoma phenotype in AFAP
- **[[lynch-syndrome]]** — mismatch repair (MMR) gene variants; typically fewer polyps; microsatellite instability-high (MSI-high) tumors
- **[[serrated-polyposis-syndrome|SPS]]** — predominantly serrated/hyperplastic polyps; biallelic *MUTYH* variants reported in a subset
- **[[colonic-polyposis-of-unknown-etiology|Colonic polyposis of unknown etiology (CPUE)]]** — ≥10 cumulative adenomas with negative MGPT; where the MAP-negative polyposis patient lands ([[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]] key concept (KC) 6)

## Diagnostics

### Surveillance

| Site | Start age | Interval | Procedure and notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Colon** | **18–20 yr** | **every 1–3 yr**, by polyp number, size and histology *([[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]] KC 8)* | [[colonoscopy\|Colonoscopy]] — flexible sigmoidoscopy is inadequate, since the rectum is often spared *([[asge-2020-fap]])* |
| **Upper gastrointestinal (GI)** | **30–35 yr**, or **earlier** if the family's upper GI phenotype warrants *(KC 19)*; [[asge-2020-fap\|ASGE 2020]] adds **"or before colectomy"** | baseline, then by **duodenal Spigelman stage** (table on [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis]]) | [[upper-endoscopy\|Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD)]] **with visualization of the duodenal papilla** *(ACG 2026 Rec 8, conditional)* |
| **Thyroid** | — | **Not recommended in MAP** | Thyroid ultrasound is specified for FAP and AFAP only *(KC 26–27; Figure 6 lists it inside the shared AFAP/MAP box as "in AFAP")* |
| **Abdomen and pelvis** | — | **Only for a palpable abdominal mass**; may be considered preoperatively with a family history of desmoid | Cross-sectional imaging, to assess for desmoid disease. **No role for screening imaging** for desmoids or adrenal adenomas *(KC 25)* |

> **What changed in 2026.** The colonoscopy interval widened from **1–2 yr** ([[asge-2020-fap|ASGE 2020]] Rec 8; [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer|ACG 2015]] advised at least yearly colonoscopy) to **1–3 yr**, chosen by polyp number, size and histology. **Thyroid ultrasound is dropped** for MAP — ACG 2015 recommended it annually for FAP, MAP and attenuated polyposis alike. The colonoscopy start age (18–20 yr) is unchanged. ACG 2015 put upper endoscopy at **around age 30** for MAP; ACG 2026 and ASGE 2020 both set it at **30–35 yr**.

### Upper GI Phenotype

- The upper GI phenotype is **the same in kind as FAP but less frequent and less severe** ([[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]]): **duodenal adenomas in 14%–34%** (vs 50%–90% in FAP), diagnosed at a later median age of **48–51 yr**, with a lower Spigelman stage
- Among **92 MAP patients**, 34% had duodenal polyposis at a median age of 50 yr — **84% Spigelman stage I–II, 16% stage III**
- **Progression is usually slow:** of 34 MAP patients with duodenal polyposis, 18 were followed a median **8.2 yr** — the Spigelman stage advanced in **6 (33%)** and none developed high-grade dysplasia (HGD) or cancer
- **But a low Spigelman stage is less reassuring than in FAP** — unlike FAP, duodenal cancer in MAP **often arises in non-advanced disease, including in patients with no duodenal adenomas**. Of 394 patients, 18 duodenal adenomas with HGD were detected and **half were <10 mm**; among the 92 patients above, one duodenal and one [[ampullary-adenoma|ampullary]] cancer were found at baseline **in the absence of duodenal polyposis** (ages 83 and 63)
- **Biopsy the papilla at baseline EGD even if it appears normal** — it carries prognostic information if it harbours adenomatous change — and again on follow-up if its size or morphology changes *(KC 17)*. [[asge-2020-fap|ASGE 2020]] had found no reported ampullary involvement in MAP and left ampullary screening undecided; ACG 2026 settles it by naming MAP in Rec 8 and KC 17

### Monoallelic Carriers

- **Monoallelic *MUTYH* carriers** make up **1%–2%** of the general population. Neither older guideline reached consensus on intensified screening; both offer the same option — manage as someone with a **first-degree relative (FDR) with CRC**:
  - [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer|ACG 2015]] — [[colonoscopy]] **every 5 yr, beginning 10 yr before the earliest CRC diagnosis in the family**; it put the carrier CRC risk at **1.5–2×** the general population
  - [[asge-2020-fap|ASGE 2020]] Table 3 — start at **age 40, or 10 yr before the FDR's age at CRC diagnosis**, then **every 5 yr** *(very low quality)*
  - With **no** family history of CRC, ASGE lists the examination, start age and interval all as **unknown**

> [!pitfall] A single *MUTYH* variant is not a reason to intensify surveillance
> [[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]] is more definite than the older documents: a **monoallelic *MUTYH* GPV is not convincingly associated with adenomatous polyposis, nor with a CRC risk warranting increased surveillance**. Among **4,636 individuals of European descent with CRC** and **51,372 cancer-free controls**, the prevalence of a single *MUTYH* variant was **2.1% vs 1.8%** (odds ratio (OR) 1.2; 95% CI 0.99–1.5, *P* = 0.064). **Personal and family history of polyps or CRC — not the carrier state — guides screening.**

## Therapeutics

**When to operate:** the absolute and relative colectomy criteria are the same for FAP, AFAP and MAP — the table ([[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]] Table 8) lives on [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis]] and is not repeated here.

> [!pitfall] Partial colectomy leaves too much colon behind in MAP
> In 134 MAP patients, synchronous and metachronous CRCs occurred in **7% and 12%**; in 254 patients the **1- and 5-year risks of primary and metachronous CRC** were **1% and 9%** in those presenting with polyps and **2% and 11%** in those presenting with CRC. In both studies, **partial colectomy carried a higher risk of CRC, unmanageable polyposis burden and repeat surgery** than extended colectomy — MAP progresses rapidly to CRC regardless of frank polyposis ([[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]]).

- **Colectomy with ileorectal anastomosis (IRA)** when polyps become endoscopically uncontrollable; **restorative proctocolectomy with ileal pouch-anal anastomosis (IPAA)** if the rectum is substantially involved. IRA may be the better option for MAP patients with relative rectal sparing ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]])
- Once the colonic polyp burden is unmanageable or CRC is diagnosed, **subtotal colectomy rather than hemicolectomy**, given the risk of metachronous cancers ([[asge-2020-fap]])
- **Postsurgical surveillance** *([[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]], ungraded)*: **annual lower endoscopy** of the rectum or ileal pouch after IRA or IPAA, shorter if adenomas are large, numerous or advanced *(KC 13)*; surveillance **ileoscopy is not established** and **may be considered every 3 yr with annual visual inspection of the stoma** *(KC 15)*. [[asge-2020-fap|ASGE 2020]] instead puts pouch or [[ostomy-management|ileostomy]] endoscopy at **1–2 yr** and sigmoidoscopy after IRA at **6–12 mo indefinitely**
- **[[nonampullary-duodenal-adenoma|Duodenal]] disease** is staged and managed by Spigelman stage, and **[[gastric-polyps|gastric polyposis]]** follows the same resection rules as FAP — resect antral polyps, gastric adenomas, polyps ≥10 mm, proximal polypoid mounds and white mucosal patches *([[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]] Rec 12)*. Both tables live on [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis]]; MAP follows the same intervals
- **Chemoprevention** (sulindac or celecoxib as an adjunct to endoscopic surveillance, never a replacement for an indicated colectomy) is written for adenomatous polyposis as a whole — see [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis]]
