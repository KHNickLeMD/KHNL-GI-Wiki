## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- Biallelic **MUTYH** mutations; autosomal **recessive** — unique among the polyposis syndromes [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]]. ***MUTYH*** is a **base-excision repair** gene that excises mispaired adenine residues from oxidised DNA; loss produces a signature **G:C → T:A transversion** burden [[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]]
- Estimated population prevalence **1:20,000 to 1:60,000**; single-*MUTYH*-GPV carrier frequency **1%–2%** in US, European and Australian populations [[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]]
- Y179C and G396D (formerly Y165C / G382D) are the two most prevalent mutations, present in **>80%** of individuals of European ancestry with MAP; ~90% of "western" MAP patients carry ≥1 of the two
- Phenotype: **20–99 colorectal adenomas** most commonly; rarely >500. Among patients **in whom a disease-causing APC mutation is not found**, biallelic MUTYH is present in **7.5–12.5%** of those with >100 adenomas and in **16–40%** of those with **15–99** adenomas but not FAP — the higher-yield group to test
- Polyps may be absent: in 8 population-based studies, **35% (28/79)** of MAP cases with colorectal cancer (CRC) had **no concurrent polyps** and 22% had <10 adenomas — a normal-looking colon does not exclude MAP
- Adenoma is the predominant polyp type, but hyperplastic and/or sessile serrated polyps occur (47% of a 17-patient series had ≥1; ~18% met [[serrated-polyposis-syndrome|serrated polyposis]] criteria)

### Severity Assessment

- [[colorectal-cancer|CRC]] present at MAP diagnosis in **~60%** of cases
- **Cumulative CRC risk: 19% by age 50** ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]]); **by age 60, 63%** ([[asge-2020-fap]], the newer estimate) — [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer|ACG 2015]] put the age-60 risk at **43%**. Average age of onset **48 years**
- Extracolonic: **17%** of a 276-patient cohort had extracolonic lesions; estimated **38% lifetime risk of extracolonic malignancy** (~2× general population). **Duodenal cancer lifetime risk ~4%** (similar to FAP/AFAP). Gastric lesions in up to 11%, but no established increase in [[gastric-adenocarcinoma|gastric cancer]] risk
- 5-year survival after CRC in MAP **78% vs. 63%** in matched general-population CRC controls (hazard ratio (HR) 0.48; survival advantage persisted after adjustment)

**[[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]] figures** (newest source; where they differ from the above, they govern):

- **CRC risk 43%–63%** at mean age **40–60 yr**; earliest reported CRC at **age 21**
- **Biallelic *MUTYH* yield rises with adenoma count:** 1%–4% at 10–19 cumulative adenomas, 4.5%–20% at 20–99, 7%–15% at ≥100
- Upper GI phenotype is **the same in kind as FAP but less frequent and less severe** — duodenal adenomas in **14%–34%** (vs 50%–90% in FAP), usually diagnosed later (median 48–51 yr) and with a lower Spigelman stage
- **Duodenal cancer risk 0.35%–1.8%** at mean age 61–64 yr; **standardized incidence ratio (SIR) 4.2 (95% CI 0.9–12.3)**. Unlike FAP, **duodenal cancer in MAP often arises in non-advanced disease, including without duodenal adenomas** — so a low Spigelman stage is less reassuring here
- Gastric adenomas in **2.7%** and fundic gland polyps in **6%** of a 150-patient multicentre endoscopy study; **gastric cancer ≤1%** (mean age 38 yr), SIR 4.2 (0.9–12.3)
- Other cancers by age 75: **skin (melanoma, basal cell, squamous cell) 17%** (SIR 2.8), **ovary 10%** (SIR 5.7), **bladder 6%** (SIR 7.2). Bladder (HR 19) and ovarian (HR 17) cancers were the only sites significantly elevated in a prospective Colon Cancer Family Registry cohort
- **Desmoids, CHRPE, osteomas and hepatoblastoma are rare in MAP** — in a review of 27 articles covering 2,109 MAP patients, desmoids occurred in only 3

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[colorectal-polyposis]]. MAP is managed like attenuated [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis|FAP]]; see that page for full ADDT content.*

- **Classic FAP / AFAP** — APC germline mutation; autosomal **dominant** (vs MAP's autosomal recessive inheritance); overlapping 20–99 adenoma phenotype in AFAP
- **[[lynch-syndrome]]** — mismatch repair (MMR) gene mutations; typically fewer polyps; microsatellite instability-high (MSI-high) tumors
- **[[serrated-polyposis-syndrome]]** — predominantly serrated/hyperplastic polyps; MUTYH biallelic mutations reported in a subset

## Diagnostics

Surveillance mirrors AFAP (start age / interval):

| Site | Start age | Interval | Procedure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Colon | **18–20** | **every 1–3 y**, by polyp number, size and histology ([[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]] KC 8) | [[colonoscopy\|Colonoscopy]] — flexible sigmoidoscopy is inadequate (rectum may be spared) |
| Upper gastrointestinal (GI) | **30–35** — concordant across [[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis\|ACG 2026]] (KC 19), [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer\|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2015]] and [[asge-2020-fap\|American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2020]], or **earlier** if the family's upper GI phenotype warrants | baseline, then per duodenal Spigelman stage (as for FAP/AFAP) | [[upper-endoscopy\|esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD)]] **with visualization of the duodenal papilla** — ACG 2026 Rec 8 makes papillary visualization explicit for MAP as well as FAP and AFAP, settling the older ambiguity |
| Thyroid | — | **Not recommended** | [[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis\|ACG 2026]] Figure 6 lists thyroid ultrasound for FAP and AFAP only, **not MAP** — superseding ACG 2015's annual-ultrasound statement, which covered MAP |

> **What changed in 2026.** Colonoscopy interval widened from **1–2 y** to **1–3 y**, chosen by polyp number/size/histology rather than fixed. Thyroid surveillance is **dropped** for MAP. The start ages (colon 18–20, upper GI 30–35) are unchanged. Older colon start ages of 25–30 ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer|ACG 2015]]) are superseded.

- Genetic testing for suspected adenomatous polyposis should include **both APC and MUTYH** analysis ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]]) — a negative APC test alone does not exclude polyposis.
- Take a **complete family history including consanguinity** — recessive inheritance is easy to miss ([[asge-2020-fap]]). Full gene sequencing is offered, since mutations other than Y179C/G396D cause MAP in non-European populations.
- **Age to test an at-risk relative: 18–20 years** *(ASGE 2020 Rec 2)* — deliberately deferred out of childhood so the individual can make their own informed decision, since MAP's onset and its screening both begin in adulthood (contrast **FAP, tested at 10–12**).
- Once a proband is confirmed to have MAP, **screen his or her relatives for MUTYH mutations** [[asge-2020-fap]].
- **When to send MUTYH specifically** [[asge-2020-fap]] — any one of: ≥20 colorectal adenomas over multiple colonoscopies; known family history of MAP; ≥10 adenomas on a single colonoscopy; or [[serrated-polyposis-syndrome|serrated polyposis syndrome (SPS)]] criteria met with at least some adenomas present.
- **Monoallelic carriers** (**1–2%** of the general population; most estimates put CRC risk at **1.5–2×** the general population): no consensus on intensified screening in either guideline. Both nonetheless offer the same option — manage as someone with a **first-degree relative (FDR) with CRC**: [[colonoscopy]] **every 5 y**, beginning **10 y before the earliest CRC diagnosis in the family** ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]]); [[asge-2020-fap|ASGE 2020]] adds an **age-40** floor for a carrier with an affected FDR *(very low quality)*. With **no** family history, ASGE lists the examination, start age and interval all as unknown.

> [!pitfall] A single *MUTYH* variant is not a reason to intensify surveillance
> [[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]] is more definite than the older documents: a **monoallelic *MUTYH* GPV is not convincingly associated with adenomatous polyposis, nor with a CRC risk warranting increased surveillance**. In 4,636 individuals of European descent with polyposis or CRC vs 51,372 cancer-free controls the prevalence was **1.8% vs 1.2% (OR 1.2; 95% CI 0.99–1.5, *P* = 0.064)**. **Personal and family history of polyps or CRC — not the carrier state — guides screening.**

## Therapeutics

**When to operate:** the absolute/relative colectomy criteria are the same for FAP, AFAP and MAP — the table ([[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]] Table 8) lives on [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis]] and is not repeated here.

> [!pitfall] Polyp count does not predict CRC risk in MAP
> CRC can occur **in the absence of adenomas**, and the number of polyps does not predict risk ([[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]]). Two studies of patients presenting with polyps vs with CRC found **1- and 5-year risks of synchronous and metachronous CRC of 7% and 12%** vs **1% and 9%** respectively — and in both, **partial colectomy carried a higher risk of repeat CRC and repeat surgery** than extended colectomy. **MAP can progress rapidly to CRC regardless of frank polyposis.**

- **Colectomy + ileorectal anastomosis (IRA)** when polyps become endoscopically uncontrollable; **restorative proctocolectomy + ileal pouch-anal anastomosis (IPAA)** if the rectum is substantially involved. IRA may be the better option for MAP patients with relative rectal sparing.
- Once colonic polyp burden is unmanageable or CRC is diagnosed, **subtotal colectomy rather than hemicolectomy**, given the risk of metachronous cancers ([[asge-2020-fap]]).
- **Postsurgical surveillance** *(ACG Rec 13; Strong, low)*: yearly endoscopy of the rectum or ileal pouch; examine an [[ostomy-management|ileostomy]] every 2 years. [[asge-2020-fap|ASGE 2020]] instead puts pouch or ileostomy endoscopy at **1–2 y** and sigmoidoscopy after IRA at **6–12 mo indefinitely**.
- **[[nonampullary-duodenal-adenoma|Duodenal]] disease is staged and managed by Spigelman stage** — the staging table and its surveillance intervals live on [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis]]; MAP follows the same intervals.
