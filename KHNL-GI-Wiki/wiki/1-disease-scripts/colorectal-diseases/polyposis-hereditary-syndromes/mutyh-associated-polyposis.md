## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- Biallelic **MUTYH** mutations; autosomal **recessive** — unique among the polyposis syndromes [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]]
- Y179C and G396D (formerly Y165C / G382D) are the two most prevalent mutations, present in **>80%** of individuals of European ancestry with MAP; ~90% of "western" MAP patients carry ≥1 of the two
- Phenotype: **20–99 colorectal adenomas** most commonly; rarely >500. Biallelic MUTYH is found in **7.5–12.5%** of patients with >100 adenomas
- Polyps may be absent: in 8 population-based studies, **35% (28/79)** of MAP cases with CRC had **no concurrent polyps** and 22% had <10 adenomas — a normal-looking colon does not exclude MAP
- Adenoma is the predominant polyp type, but hyperplastic and/or sessile serrated polyps occur (47% of a 17-patient series had ≥1; ~18% met [[serrated-polyposis-syndrome|serrated polyposis]] criteria)

### Severity Assessment

- [[colorectal-cancer|CRC]] present at MAP diagnosis in **~60%** of cases
- **Cumulative CRC risk: 19% by age 50, 43% by age 60**; average age of onset **48 years**
- Extracolonic: **17%** of a 276-patient cohort had extracolonic lesions; estimated **38% lifetime risk of extracolonic malignancy** (~2× general population). **Duodenal cancer lifetime risk ~4%** (similar to FAP/AFAP). Gastric lesions in up to 11%, but no established increase in gastric cancer risk
- 5-year survival after CRC in MAP **78% vs. 63%** in matched general-population CRC controls (HR 0.48; survival advantage persisted after adjustment)

## Differential Diagnosis

*MAP is managed like attenuated [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis|FAP]]; see that page for full ADDT content.*

- **Classic FAP / attenuated FAP (AFAP)** — APC germline mutation; autosomal **dominant** (vs MAP's autosomal recessive inheritance); overlapping 20–99 adenoma phenotype in AFAP
- **[[lynch-syndrome]]** — MMR gene mutations; typically fewer polyps; MSI-high tumors
- **[[serrated-polyposis-syndrome]]** — predominantly serrated/hyperplastic polyps; MUTYH biallelic mutations reported in a subset

## Diagnostics

Surveillance mirrors AFAP — [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]] Table 10 (start age / interval):

| Site | Start age | Interval | Procedure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Colon | **25–30** | **every 1–2 y** (shorten with rising polyp burden) | [[colonoscopy\|Colonoscopy]] |
| Upper GI | **30–35** | baseline, then per duodenal findings (as for FAP/AFAP) | [[upper-endoscopy\|EGD]] **with a side-viewing instrument** |
| Thyroid | — | **annual** | Thyroid ultrasound *(Rec 11; conditional, low)* — applies to FAP, MAP, and attenuated polyposis |

- Genetic testing for suspected adenomatous polyposis should include **both APC and MUTYH** analysis (a negative APC test alone does not exclude polyposis).

## Therapeutics

**When to operate — [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]] Rec 9** *(Strong, low)*; identical criteria for FAP, AFAP, and MAP:

| | Indication |
|---|---|
| **Absolute** (immediate colectomy) | Documented or suspected cancer · significant symptoms |
| **Relative** | Multiple adenomas **>6 mm** · a significant **increase in adenoma number** · inability to adequately survey the colon because of multiple diminutive polyps |

- **Colectomy + IRA** when polyps become endoscopically uncontrollable; **restorative proctocolectomy + IPAA** if the rectum is substantially involved. IRA may be the better option for MAP patients with relative rectal sparing.
- **Postsurgical surveillance** *(Strong, low)*: yearly endoscopy of the rectum or ileal pouch; examine an ileostomy every 2 years.
- **Duodenal disease is staged and managed by Spigelman stage** — the staging table and its surveillance intervals live on [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis]]; MAP follows the same intervals.
