## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- Rare **autosomal dominant** attenuated colonic polyposis, originally described in a large **Ashkenazi Jewish** kindred with multiple colorectal polyps and cancer [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]] [[aga-2022-hamartomatous-polyposis]]
- **No formal diagnostic criteria exist** in either ingested source — HMPS is recognised by its **polyp mix** in a dominantly inherited kindred, and confirmed genetically
- In the original Whitelaw kindred: **13 members developed [[colorectal-cancer|CRC]]** and **23 developed multiple polyps of several histologic types**
- **Mean age of polyp occurrence: 28 years** in one family; **onset of polyposis in the late 20s** in the largest reported series (4 families, 16 affected members)

**Defining polyp spectrum — the mix is the diagnosis:**

| Polyp type found in HMPS |
|---|
| **Mixed juvenile–adenomatous** polyps (the characteristic admixed lesion) |
| Adenomatous |
| Hyperplastic |
| Serrated adenomas |
| Mixed hyperplastic–adenomatous |
| Adenocarcinoma |

**Genetics** [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]] [[aga-2022-hamartomatous-polyposis]]:

- Linked to a locus on chromosome **15q13.3–q14** (which contains the **CRAC1** gene) in a number of families
- Associated with **large duplications of the promoter region or the entire *GREM1* gene**. The duplication **increases GREM1 expression**, which then **inhibits the bone morphogenetic protein (BMP) pathway** → predisposition to multiple colorectal polyps
- The *GREM1* promoter duplication was found in **0.7% of Ashkenazi Jews in Israel who met clinical criteria for [[lynch-syndrome|Lynch syndrome]]**
- **The genetic basis of the majority of HMPS families remains unknown**

### Severity Assessment

> **Decision gap — not answerable from the ingested sources.** Neither [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer|ACG 2015]] nor [[aga-2022-hamartomatous-polyposis|USMSTF 2022]] gives a lifetime CRC risk figure, a penetrance estimate, or an extraintestinal cancer risk for HMPS: *"There are not enough data to know the optimal surveillance intervals or whether extraintestinal neoplasia is a risk."* A dedicated HMPS/GREM1 series would be needed — do not infer a number.

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[colorectal-polyposis]].*

- **[[juvenile-polyposis-syndrome]]** — HMPS is explicitly noted to be **misdiagnosed as JPS and vice versa**; JPS polyps are uniformly juvenile, SMAD4/BMPR1A
- **[[serrated-polyposis-syndrome]]** — same two-way misdiagnosis risk; SPS is defined by serrated polyp count/distribution ([[serrated-polyposis-syndrome|WHO criteria]]) without the admixed juvenile–adenomatous lesion
- **[[familial-adenomatous-polyposis]] / AFAP** — purely adenomatous; APC
- **[[mutyh-associated-polyposis]]** — recessive; adenomas ± serrated polyps; MUTYH
- **[[lynch-syndrome]]** — few polyps, MMR deficiency; relevant because the *GREM1* duplication is found in a small fraction of Ashkenazi kindreds **meeting Lynch clinical criteria** with no MMR variant

## Diagnostics

- **[[colonoscopy]] with [[polypectomy]] and histology** of multiple polyps — the diagnosis is suggested when **more than one polyp histology** occurs in the same patient/kindred
- **Genetic testing for *GREM1* mutation and expression** *may be considered* in families with **adenomatous and hamartomatous polyposis in which an etiology cannot be determined** [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]]
- No tumour molecular test distinguishes HMPS; no extraintestinal screening protocol is defined

---

## Therapeutics

- **Begin colonoscopic surveillance in the late 20s** — the age at which polyposis begins in the largest series [[aga-2022-hamartomatous-polyposis]]
- **Optimal surveillance interval is unknown** (explicitly stated by the source) — do not quote one
- Management "from what is now known should probably be **similar to that for [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis|FAP]]**, depending on polyp number, size, and histology" [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]] — i.e. endoscopic clearance while feasible, colectomy when the polyp burden is no longer endoscopically controllable or cancer/high-grade dysplasia appears
