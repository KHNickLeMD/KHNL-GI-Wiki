## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- Cowden syndrome (CS) is one phenotype of the **PTEN hamartoma tumor syndrome (PHTS)**, caused by inactivating germline pathogenic variants in the **PTEN** tumor suppressor gene
- PHTS also covers **Bannayan-Riley-Ruvalcaba syndrome (BRRS)** and some cases of Proteus syndrome; patients with CS are additionally at risk for dysplastic cerebellar gangliocytoma (**Lhermitte-Duclos disease**) [[aga-2022-hamartomatous-polyposis]]
- Gain-of-function germline variants in **WWP1** inhibit PTEN activity and cause a CS-like syndrome (oligopolyposis, cancer-prone phenotype) [[aga-2022-hamartomatous-polyposis]]
- **Genetic diagnosis = a germline pathogenic PTEN variant.** Neither ingested guideline reproduces the clinical diagnostic criteria for CS: [[aga-2022-hamartomatous-polyposis|US Multi-Society Task Force on Colorectal Cancer (USMSTF) 2022]] states they are "complex, and can be found at the National Comprehensive Cancer Network website," and [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2015]] prints only testing indications. The list below is a **referral threshold for PTEN testing, not a diagnosis**

**Triggers for genetic evaluation** [[aga-2022-hamartomatous-polyposis]]:

| Trigger | Statement | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **Multiple gastrointestinal (GI) hamartomas or ganglioneuromas** → evaluate for Cowden's syndrome and related conditions | R12 | Strong recommendation, low quality of evidence |
| Any hamartomatous polyposis syndrome: **≥2 lifetime hamartomatous polyps**, a family history of hamartomatous polyps, or a hamartomatous-syndrome–associated cancer in a **first- or second-degree relative** → test with a **multigene panel** | R1 | Strong recommendation, low quality of evidence |
| Organs to survey — **breast, thyroid, kidney, uterus, colon, skin**, via a **multidisciplinary** approach | R13 | Strong recommendation, low quality of evidence |

*R-numbers are the wiki's reading-order labels; USMSTF 2022 does not number its 14 recommendations.*

**Indications for PTEN genetic evaluation** — ACG 2015 Table 11, adapted from the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]]:

- Individual from a family with a known PTEN mutation
- Individual meeting clinical diagnostic criteria for CS
- Personal history of: BRRS · adult Lhermitte-Duclos disease · autism spectrum disorder **with** macrocephaly · **≥2** biopsy-proven trichilemmomas · **≥2 major criteria** (one must be macrocephaly) · **3 major criteria** without macrocephaly · **1 major + ≥3 minor criteria** · **≥4 minor criteria**
- At-risk individual with **one major or two minor criteria** *and* a relative with a clinical diagnosis of CS or BRRS for whom testing has not been performed

| | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **Major** | Breast cancer · endometrial cancer · follicular thyroid cancer · **multiple** GI hamartomas or ganglioneuromas · macrocephaly (megalocephaly, **≥97th percentile**) · macular pigmentation of glans penis · mucocutaneous lesions **alone only if** one biopsy-proven trichilemmoma, **or** multiple palmoplantar keratoses, **or** multifocal/extensive oral mucosal papillomatosis, **or** multiple cutaneous facial papules (often verrucous) |
| **Minor** | Autism spectrum disorder · [[colorectal-cancer\|colon cancer]] · esophageal glycogenic acanthosis (**≥3** lesions) · lipomas · mental retardation (intelligence quotient (IQ) **≤75**) · papillary or follicular variant of papillary thyroid cancer · thyroid structural lesions (adenoma, nodule(s), goiter) · renal cell carcinoma · **single** GI hamartoma or ganglioneuroma · testicular lipomatosis · vascular anomalies (including multiple intracranial developmental venous anomalies) |

**Counting rule (Table 11, footnote b):** with **≥2 major criteria but no macrocephaly**, one of those major criteria **may be counted as one of the three minor criteria** — so the "1 major + ≥3 minor" pathway can be met using a surplus major criterion.

### Severity Assessment — Cancer Risk Profile

| Site | General population | CS risk | Mean age at diagnosis (y) | Standardized incidence ratio (SIR)* | Cumulative lifetime risk* |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Breast | 12.9% | **25–85%** | 38–46 | 25.4 (19.8–32.0) | **85.2%** (71.4–99.1) |
| Thyroid | 1.3% | **3–38%** | 31–38 | 51.1 (38.1–67.1) | **35.2%** (19.7–50.7) |
| Endometrium | 3.1% | **5–28%** | 25 | 42.9 (28.1–62.8) | **28.2%** (17.1–39.3) |
| Kidney (renal cell) | 1.7% | **15–34%** | 40 | 30.6 (17.8–49.4) | **33.6%** (10.4–56.9) |
| Colorectum | 4.3% | **9–18%** | 44 (range 35–49) | 10.3 (5.6–17.4) | **9.0%** (3.8–14.1) |
| Melanoma | 2.3% | **6%** | 3 (youngest onset) | 8.5 (4.1–15.6) | **6%** (1.6–9.4) |

\* From the **International Cowden Consortium**, 368 germline PTEN carriers, as reported by [[aga-2022-hamartomatous-polyposis|USMSTF 2022]]; parentheses are 95% confidence limits.

- General-population figures are Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) 1975–2017 lifetime risk of diagnosis by site. [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer|ACG 2015]] uses slightly different denominators (colorectum 4.8%, breast 12.4%, thyroid 1.1%, endometrium 2.7%, kidney 1.6%) and gives colorectal risk as **9–16%, average age 44–48**
- USMSTF 2022 prints **35 y** as the mean age of colorectal cancer in its risk table and **44 y (range 35–49)** in its text
- ACG 2015 qualifies three of the ages: thyroid 31–38 is a **median**, endometrium 25 and kidney 40 are **onset of risk**, melanoma 3 is the **youngest age of onset**
- Median age at first cancer **36 y**; cumulative risk of **any** cancer by age 70 is **85%** (French series, 154 carriers) to **89%** (Mayo series, 211 patients, in which lifetime colorectal cancer (CRC) risk was **16%**)
- Cumulative risk of **colonic polyps 70% by age 60**; benign GI polyps found in **31%** of one cohort at mean age 38
- In a "relaxed" International Cowden Consortium series, **13% of PTEN carriers who underwent colonoscopy developed CRC — all before age 50** (youngest 35), SIR **224.1** (95% confidence interval 109.3–411.3). An earlier 9-country report put cumulative CRC risk at **18% by age 60**, occurring between ages 53 and 62

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[colorectal-polyposis]].*

- **[[juvenile-polyposis-syndrome]]** — *SMAD4*/*BMPR1A*; no breast/thyroid/endometrial high-risk; distinct histology
- **[[peutz-jeghers-syndrome]]** — *STK11*; mucocutaneous pigmentation; arborizing hamartomas
- **BRRS** — allelic to CS (*PTEN*); a **paediatric** condition with macrocephaly, developmental delay, GI hamartomatous polyps, and **pigmented macules on the toes and glans penis** (also called Bannayan-Zonana or Ruvalcaba-Riley-Smith). Members of the *same* family may show either the BRRS or the CS phenotype, and some BRRS cases have no detectable PTEN variant [[aga-2022-hamartomatous-polyposis]]
- **[[juvenile-polyposis-syndrome|Juvenile polyposis of infancy]]** — a **10q22.3–q24.1 deletion removing both PTEN and BMPR1A** looks like BRRS *and* like juvenile polyposis syndrome (JPS); consider it when the two phenotypes overlap in an infant
- **[[familial-adenomatous-polyposis]]** — adenomatous polyps; *APC* gene
- **[[hereditary-mixed-polyposis-syndrome|Hereditary mixed polyposis syndrome]]** — *GREM1* duplication; mixed polyp types

## Diagnostics

### Endoscopic Findings

- GI polyps in **35–93%** of CS patients; colonic polyps in up to **95%** of those undergoing [[colonoscopy]] — few to numerous (even hundreds), distributed throughout the colon and the rest of the gut
- Prospective endoscopic subset of PTEN carriers: **64/69 (93%)** had GI polyps, half of them hyperplastic; number ranged from 1 to "innumerable"
- Histology: **hamartomatous polyps are the most common type** (up to 29% in one study); also juvenile polyps, ganglioneuromas, adenomas, inflammatory and hyperplastic polyps, and less commonly leiomyomas, lipomas, lymphoid polyps. **Multiple synchronous histologic types at colonoscopy are common**
- Multiple hamartomatous polyps also occur in the stomach, duodenum, and small bowel
- **Diffuse esophageal glycogenic acanthosis** — frequent; one or a few lesions are nonspecific, but **diffuse lesions (sometimes hundreds) are seen in ≥80% of PHTS patients**. It is **benign**: no reported increased [[esophageal-cancer|esophageal cancer]] risk

> [!pearl] Glycogenic acanthosis plus polyposis is pathognomonic
> Diffuse esophageal glycogenic acanthosis **combined with colonic polyposis** should be considered pathognomonic for Cowden syndrome ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]]) — look for it on the [[upper-endoscopy|upper endoscopy]] done at diagnosis.

### Assessment at Diagnosis

Assessment at the time of CS/PHTS diagnosis should include a complete clinical examination (**especially dermatologic and neurologic**) plus **mammography + breast magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), thyroid ultrasound, transvaginal ultrasound, [[upper-endoscopy|upper gastrointestinal endoscopy]], colonoscopy, and renal ultrasound** [[aga-2022-hamartomatous-polyposis]].

### Colonoscopy Surveillance

**Begin at age 35, repeat at intervals no greater than 5 years, depending on polyp burden** *(weak recommendation, low quality of evidence — [[aga-2022-hamartomatous-polyposis|USMSTF 2022]] R14)*:

- **Early start — the guideline states the trigger two ways:**
  - **R14 (the graded statement):** begin at 35 **"or 10 years younger than age of any relative with colorectal cancer"** — no age limit on the relative
  - **The narrative text:** begin at 35 unless symptomatic, **or if a close relative has had colon cancer *before age 40*,** then start 10 years before the earliest known family colon cancer
  - Use the earlier of the two
- **Start earlier if symptomatic**; shorten the interval inside 5 years if the patient is symptomatic or polyps are found
- Where **very-early-onset cancers** occur in the family, the task force allows starting surveillance **10 years earlier than the youngest cancer diagnosis** in that family

> [!pitfall] Start at 35, not 15 — follow the 2022 schedule
> [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer|ACG 2015]] advised colonoscopy **from age 15, every 2 years**. USMSTF 2022 sets **age 35, interval ≤5 years** and names the disagreement: "This recommendation differs from a recent American College of Gastroenterology Guideline, which recommends initiating colonoscopy at age 15 years. Recent evidence suggesting later onset of significant colon cancer risk informed our recommendations."
> The newer schedule is the *less* intensive one — a later start **and** a longer maximum interval.

The [[aga-2020-young-adult-onset-crc|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2020 Clinical Practice Update on young adult–onset colorectal cancer]] lists colonoscopy **every 1–3 years** for PHTS with no start age; USMSTF 2022 is the newer guideline and governs here.

### Surveillance of Other Organs

Ages in years; `—` = the source gives no value.

| Examination | [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer\|ACG 2015]] | NCCN 2020 † | [[aga-2022-hamartomatous-polyposis\|USMSTF 2022]] — *use this* |
|---|---|---|---|
| Colonoscopy | **15**, q2 y | **35**, q5 y | **35**, q≤5 y — *graded (weak, low)* |
| [[upper-endoscopy\|Upper endoscopy]] | **15**, q2–3 y; repeat sooner if duodenal polyposis | — | At diagnosis; **no surveillance interval given** |
| Thyroid exam + ultrasound | **Adolescence**, annual | **7**, annual | **Annual from the time of PHTS diagnosis, including in childhood** ‡ |
| Breast | Self-examination **monthly from 25**; mammography + MRI **30–35**, annual | **30–35**, annual | Clinical breast examination **q6–12 mo from 25**; mammography + MRI annual from **30–35** (or **5–10 y before the earliest known family breast cancer**) ‡ |
| Endometrium | Endometrial sampling **or** vaginal ultrasound, **30–35**, annual | (no start age given), q1–2 y | From **30–35**: symptom education (abnormal uterine or postmenopausal bleeding) + **endometrial biopsy q1–2 y can be considered** ‡ |
| Renal | Urinalysis with cytology ± renal ultrasound, **18**, annual | **40**, q1–2 y | Renal ultrasound and/or MRI: **annual from 40 if family history of renal cancer, otherwise every 2 years** ‡ |
| Skin examination | **By 18**, annual | **At diagnosis**, annual | **Annual clinical skin examination from age 18** ‡ |

**†** The NCCN 2020 values are transcribed from Table 4 of [[aga-2022-hamartomatous-polyposis|USMSTF 2022]], which prints them as a comparison column — read them as *"USMSTF reports that NCCN 2020 advises…"*

**‡** Only the colonoscopy row is one of the guideline's 14 graded recommendations. USMSTF 2022 reserved its own recommendations to GI and GI-malignancy questions and **defers to other expert groups** for the other cancers; those rows are its narrative report of those groups' schedules.

ACG 2015 notes that its CS surveillance schedule is **entirely expert opinion** — derived from screening guidelines for the relevant cancers in other settings and adjusted for the risks seen in PHTS, not from prospective data.

## Therapeutics

### Colorectal Surgery

- **Colectomy is rarely required** in CS; reserve it for **CRC**, or when surveillance and clearing of premalignant lesions is **not endoscopically feasible** [[aga-2022-hamartomatous-polyposis]]

### Risk-Reducing Surgery

- There are **no data** on risk-reduction surgery in women with CS; **risk-reducing mastectomy and hysterectomy should be discussed case by case** [[aga-2022-hamartomatous-polyposis]]
- Refer to a **breast cancer specialist** for breast surveillance; a multidisciplinary approach **including a breast surgeon** is recommended whenever prophylactic mastectomy is considered [[aga-2022-hamartomatous-polyposis]]
- [[aga-2020-young-adult-onset-crc|AGA 2020]] lists, for PHTS: consider **bilateral mastectomy**; **total thyroidectomy for benign lesions and cancer**; consider **prophylactic hysterectomy once childbearing is complete**

### Genetic Counseling

- Once a PTEN mutation is identified in the proband, **all at-risk relatives should undergo mutation-specific testing** so that surveillance can be directed [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]]
- BRRS and CS are **allelic** — the same PTEN variants can present as either phenotype in different members of one family [[aga-2022-hamartomatous-polyposis]]
- Genetic testing also separates CS from [[juvenile-polyposis-syndrome|JPS]], in which juvenile polyps also form [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]]
