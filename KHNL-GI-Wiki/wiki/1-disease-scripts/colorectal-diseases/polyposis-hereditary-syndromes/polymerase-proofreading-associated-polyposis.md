*Polymerase proofreading-associated polyposis (PPAP) is one of the rare adenomatous polyposis syndromes outside familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP) and MUTYH-associated polyposis (MAP). Colonoscopy technique, polyp-resection thresholds and Spigelman staging are shared with FAP; this page carries what is specific to PPAP.*

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Autosomal dominant**, caused by germline pathogenic variants (GPVs) in the **exonuclease (proofreading) domains** of ***POLD1*** or ***POLE***, which encode the catalytic subunits of DNA polymerases delta and epsilon ([[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]])
- Loss of proofreading → tumours with a **high mutational burden and a distinct mutational spectrum**
- ACG 2026 localises the causal variants to the exonuclease domain but does not name specific variants or codons
- **Dominantly inherited, and penetrance appears high** ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]])
- **Diagnosis is made on germline multigene panel testing (MGPT)** — *POLE* and *POLD1* sit on the minimum 17-gene panel ([[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]] key concept (KC) 1; full gene list on [[colorectal-polyposis]]). Trigger is the **cumulative lifetime adenomatous colorectal polyp count**:
  - **≥20** → MGPT (strong recommendation; low certainty)
  - **10–19** → MGPT suggested (conditional recommendation; low certainty)
- **Rare:** GPVs detected in **0.3%–0.7%** of 2,813 unrelated probands tested for colorectal cancer (CRC) and polyposis
- Colorectal phenotype in [[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]] Table 5 is **30–100 adenomas**, with duodenal adenomas in the small intestine

### Severity Assessment

**Intestinal phenotype** — from a review of 132 cases, 105 *POLE* and 27 *POLD1* ([[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]]):

| | ***POLE*** | ***POLD1*** |
|---|---|---|
| **CRC** | **70.5%**, median age **44.5 yr** | **44%**, median age **41 yr** |
| **Colorectal adenomas** | **74.3%** had **1–100** adenomas (median 12), median age 36 yr | **66.6%** had **2–70** adenomas (median 13), median age 40 yr |
| **Duodenal adenomas** | **15.2%**, median age 51 yr | **7.4%**, median age 55 yr |
| **Duodenal cancer** | **9.5%** | Not reported |

> [!pitfall] The reported duodenal rates understate the risk
> Duodenal adenomas were found in **more than 50%** of patients who actually underwent upper gastrointestinal (GI) endoscopy — the 15.2% / 7.4% figures reflect how few were scoped ([[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]]).

**Extraintestinal cancers** — from further studies cited in [[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]]:

- **Endometrial cancer** — **30%–53%** with a *POLD1* GPV, **12%** with *POLE*
- **Ovarian cancer** — **9%–12%**, reported with *POLE*
- **Brain / central nervous system (CNS)** tumours (astrocytoma, glioblastoma) and **breast** cancer occur with either gene
- **Pancreatic** and **skin** cancers are also reported in PPAP; the guideline gives no breakdown by gene for these

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[colorectal-polyposis]].*

- **[[familial-adenomatous-polyposis|FAP]] and attenuated FAP (AFAP)** — *APC*; the commonest explanation for an oligo-adenomatous phenotype
- **[[mutyh-associated-polyposis|MAP]]** — biallelic *MUTYH*, autosomal recessive
- **[[lynch-syndrome]]** — the endometrial-plus-colorectal pattern overlaps; distinguished by the mismatch repair genes on panel testing and by tumour mismatch-repair immunohistochemistry
- **[[colonic-polyposis-of-unknown-etiology]]** — the label for ≥10 cumulative adenomas when the whole panel, including *POLE* and *POLD1*, is negative
- Other rare adenomatous syndromes — ***NTHL1*** tumor syndrome, ***AXIN2***-, ***MLH3***- and ***MSH3***-associated polyposis, ***MBD4***-associated neoplasia syndrome

## Diagnostics

> [!pearl] Refer to an expert centre
> PPAP, *NTHL1* tumor syndrome, *AXIN2*-, *MSH3*- and *MLH3*-associated polyposis and *MBD4*-associated neoplasia syndrome should all be **referred to an expert centre** — the syndromes are rare, the cancer risks are still evolving, and management is multispecialty ([[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]] KC 5).

Surveillance proposed by [[acg-2026-adenomatous-polyposis]] (Figure 6) for *POLE*, *POLD1*, *NTHL1*, *AXIN2*, *MLH3*, *MSH3* and *MBD4* as one group — explicitly **"standing on the side of safety"**, since publication and ascertainment bias and the absence of systematic endoscopic evaluation preclude knowing the full spectrum of neoplasia:

| Test | When to start | Interval |
|---|---|---|
| **[[colonoscopy]]** | **Age 25–30 yr**, or **5 yr before the youngest age at diagnosis of adenomatous polyposis** and **10 yr before the youngest CRC** in an affected relative — whichever is earlier | **Every 1–3 yr** |
| **[[upper-endoscopy\|Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD)]]** — ***POLE* and *POLD1* only** | **Age 25–30 yr**, performed in conjunction with a colonoscopy | By **Spigelman stage** of duodenal polyposis (staging table and intervals on [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis\|FAP]]) |
| **Post-colectomy lower endoscopy** | After surgery | **Every year** |

- Figure 6 sets out **no thyroid ultrasound and no surveillance ileoscopy** for this group, unlike FAP, AFAP and MAP
- **Women with a *POLD1* GPV:** pelvic ultrasound and selective endometrial biopsy may be considered, given the endometrial and possible brain tumour association ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]])

## Therapeutics

- Colorectal polyps are managed by the guideline's general adenomatous-polyposis statements: **endoscopically resect polyps ≥10 mm** that have no features of submucosal invasion, and **excise polyps ≥4 mm where feasible** (KC 10)
- Polyp burden not amenable to endoscopic management, with or without chemoprevention → consider **secondary surgery** (KC 14)
- Duodenal disease is staged and managed by **Spigelman stage**, as in FAP
- [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer]] made **no treatment or surveillance recommendations** for PPAP — the frequency of polyps and cancer and the extracolonic phenotype had yet to be determined — while noting that close endoscopic surveillance and colectomy seemed reasonable options. ACG 2026 supplies the surveillance scheme above and states that larger cohorts are needed before management recommendations for these disorders evolve
