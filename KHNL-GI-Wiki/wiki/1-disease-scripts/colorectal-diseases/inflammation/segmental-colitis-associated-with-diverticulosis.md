Segmental colitis associated with diverticulosis (SCAD) is a **rare** chronic inflammatory condition of colonic segments that bear diverticula, **typically sparing the rectum**. Inflammation is usually centered on and around the diverticula.

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- Chronic segmental colitis in a diverticula-bearing segment — **typically the sigmoid colon**, with the **rectum spared**.
- **Endoscopic and histologic features mimic [[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease]] or [[ulcerative-colitis]]** — the overlap is the diagnostic challenge, and rectal sparing is the discriminator.
- **When to suspect it:** segmental **left-sided** colitis in a patient with diverticulosis — consider SCAD alongside Crohn's disease and [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]]–unclassified, particularly in older adults, where SCAD is one of several conditions more likely than IBD ([[aga-2021-ibd-elderly]]; see [[ibd-in-older-adults]]).
- **A separate diagnosis from smoldering / chronic [[diverticulitis]]**, which is defined by persistent inflammation on imaging rather than by a mucosal colitis pattern.
- Whether SCAD is a **distinct entity or a manifestation of IBD** remains unresolved: [[acg-2026-diverticulitis|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2026]] leaves it unclear, while the [[aga-2021-colonic-diverticulitis|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2021 update]] calls it likely on the IBD spectrum.

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[chronic-diarrhea]].*

| Mimic | What separates it from SCAD |
|---|---|
| [[ulcerative-colitis]], Crohn's disease | SCAD is confined to the diverticula-bearing segment and spares the rectum; endoscopic and histologic appearances overlap, so rectal biopsies decide |
| Smoldering / chronic diverticulitis | Also weeks to months of persistent inflammation in a diverticular segment, but improves partially or completely on antibiotics and relapses shortly after they are stopped; confirmed on cross-sectional imaging rather than by mucosal histology |
| Acute diverticulitis | Acute inflammation of the diverticula (colonic wall thickening and/or pericolic fat stranding on computed tomography [CT]), not chronic mucosal colitis |
| Symptomatic uncomplicated diverticular disease (SUDD) | Bloating, chronic or recurrent abdominal pain, or altered bowel habits in a patient with diverticulosis, but **no** systemic inflammation and **no** overt colitis |

## Diagnostics

- **[[colonoscopy|Colonoscopy]] with segmental biopsies** — inflammation localized to the diverticular segment, rectum endoscopically and histologically spared.

> [!pitfall] Biopsy the rectum or SCAD reads as IBD
> SCAD should spare the rectum, so rectal sampling is what separates it from IBD — take rectal biopsies in every case ([[asge-2025-ibd-endoscopy-consensus|American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2025]]).

## Therapeutics

- Neither ACG 2026 nor the AGA 2021 update makes a treatment recommendation for SCAD, and the ASGE consensus covers only its endoscopic diagnosis — no treatment algorithm or prognosis is given.
- ACG 2026's **strong recommendation against [[mesalamine-5-asa|mesalamine]]** is scoped to preventing recurrence in patients with a history of acute diverticulitis; it does not address SCAD.
