Microscopic colitis is a common cause of **chronic watery, non-bloody diarrhea**, especially in older women. Two subtypes — **collagenous colitis** (thickened subepithelial collagen band >10 µm) and **lymphocytic colitis** (intraepithelial lymphocytes >20 per 100 epithelial cells) — are distinguished only by histology; the colonoscopic appearance is typically **normal**, so random biopsies of the right and left colon are required to make the diagnosis. Distribution is **patchy**, so left-sided-only sampling (e.g. flexible sigmoidoscopy alone) can miss the diagnosis ([[asge-2010-diarrhea]]). Associated with smoking and with drugs (NSAIDs, PPIs, SSRIs, statins). First-line therapy is **budesonide** 9 mg daily; withdraw offending drugs and counsel smoking cessation. See [[chronic-diarrhea]] for the diagnostic context.
