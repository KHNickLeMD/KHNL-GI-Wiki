Pouchitis is inflammation of the ileal pouch — the most common complication after restorative proctocolectomy with **ileal pouch–anal anastomosis (IPAA, "J-pouch")** for [[ulcerative-colitis|ulcerative colitis (UC)]]. The [[aga-2024-pouchitis|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2024 guideline]] frames management by clinical pattern (intermittent → chronic antibiotic-dependent → chronic antibiotic-refractory) plus two related inflammatory pouch disorders: Crohn's-like disease of the pouch and cuffitis.

- Pouchitis affects **48%** of IPAA patients within 2 years; up to **80%** have symptoms at some point; **~17%** develop chronic relapsing–remitting symptoms; **10%** develop Crohn's-like disease of the pouch ([[aga-2024-pouchitis]]).

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis
- **Normal pouch function** after the postoperative adjustment period: average **4–8 bowel movements/day** and **1–2 at night**. [[aga-2024-pouchitis]]
- **Typical symptoms:** increased stool frequency, urgency, lower abdominal pain or cramping, and/or pelvic discomfort.
- **Symptoms do not necessarily correlate with endoscopy or histology** → when to confirm with pouchoscopy: [[#Pouchoscopy]].
- **Atypical symptoms** (incomplete evacuation, straining, pelvic discomfort) → think mechanical or evacuation causes ([[#Differential Diagnosis]]).

### Severity Assessment
- **Pouchitis Disease Activity Index (PDAI)** and its modified form (mPDAI) can be used to assess pouchitis, Crohn's disease (CD) of the pouch and cuffitis (Statement 18); used for diagnosis, monitoring and trial response, but variably used in practice ([[asge-2025-ibd-endoscopy-consensus]]).
- Cutoffs in the AGA 2024 evidence base ([[aga-2024-pouchitis]]):
  - **mPDAI remission = mPDAI ≤4 *and* a ≥2-point reduction from baseline** — EARNEST trial primary endpoint (week 14).
  - **Acute pouchitis = PDAI >7 lasting <4 weeks** — the definition used in the one sulfasalazine case series.
  - AGA 2024 does not print the PDAI item point values.
- Endoscopic scoring varies greatly between observers, but **documenting ulceration and ulcerated surface area in the pouch body is consistent** ([[asge-2025-ibd-endoscopy-consensus]]).
- **Treatment goal = resolution of symptoms.** Endoscopic/histologic resolution was not a critical goal; asymptomatic patients with endoscopic pouch inflammation may not routinely warrant treatment (AGA 2024 Table 4).

### Subtype Classification
Pragmatic, treatment-anchored definitions (AGA 2024 Table 5; cuffitis is not defined there):

| Subtype | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Intermittent pouchitis** | Isolated, infrequent episodes of typical symptoms that resolve with therapy (usually antibiotics) or spontaneously, then extended normal function (typically months–years) |
| **Chronic antibiotic-dependent** | Recurrent pouchitis that responds to antibiotics but **relapses shortly after stopping** (typically days–weeks); often needs recurrent/continuous antibiotics or other advanced therapies. Deliberately not defined by a number of episodes per 12 months |
| **Chronic antibiotic-refractory** | Relapsing–remitting or continuous symptoms with **inadequate response** to typical antibiotics, often needing escalation |
| **Crohn's-like disease of the pouch** | AGA Table 5 criteria: perianal or other **fistula developing ≥12 mo after the final IPAA stage** · **stricture** of the pouch body or prepouch ileum · **prepouch ileitis**. Pouchitis may coexist |
| **Cuffitis** | Inflammation of the retained rectal cuff — remnant rectal mucosa, so treated like UC |

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[chronic-diarrhea]].*

- **[[crohns-disease|Crohn's]]-like disease of the pouch** and **cuffitis** — criteria in [[#Subtype Classification]].
- **Mechanical obstruction:** stricture at the ileoanal anastomosis, the pouch inlet, or the stoma takedown site (~20–40 cm proximal to the pouch inlet) (AGA 2024 Table 4).
- **Anastomotic leak / chronic sinus** — classic pouch site is the tip of the J ([[aga-2026-therapeutic-endoscopy-ibd]]).
- **[[defecation-disorders|Evacuation disorder]]:** nonrelaxing pelvic floor dysfunction — incomplete evacuation, straining, pelvic discomfort ([[aga-2024-pouchitis]]).
- **[[disorders-of-gut-brain-interaction|Functional]]:** irritable pouch syndrome — pouchoscopy separates it from pouchitis more reliably than symptoms ([[asge-2025-ibd-endoscopy-consensus]]).
- **Infection and other mucosal causes:** *[[clostridioides-difficile|C. difficile]]*, cytomegalovirus (CMV), ischemia, prolapse; afferent-limb ulcers also from [[nsaid-enteropathy|nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) enteritis]] ([[asge-2015-ibd]], [[asge-2025-ibd-endoscopy-consensus]]).
- Out of AGA 2024 scope: pouches created for **Crohn's** or **[[familial-adenomatous-polyposis|familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP)]]**, and pouch dysplasia surveillance.

> [!pitfall] Anastomosis-only inflammation is not necessarily pouchitis
> Pouchitis findings (erythema, edema, granularity, friability, bleeding, erosions, ulcers) also occur in CD of the pouch, and abnormalities isolated to the anastomosis are not necessarily pouchitis ([[asge-2015-ibd]]).

## Diagnostics

### Pouchoscopy
- **When (AGA 2024 Table 4):** frequent recurrent episodes (suspected antibiotic-dependent), inadequate response to antibiotics (suspected antibiotic-refractory), atypical symptoms, or suspected Crohn's-like disease of the pouch. **Not routinely required** for typical symptoms before starting antibiotics, or for infrequent episodes that respond to treatment ([[aga-2024-pouchitis]]).
  - Before chronic antibiotics, advanced therapy or corticosteroids: confirm inflammation and rule out alternative causes (implementation considerations, Recs 6–9, 11, 12).
  - Persistent symptoms → pouchoscopy to assess treatment response (Statement 13, [[asge-2025-ibd-endoscopy-consensus]]).
- **Endoscopic + histologic assessment of the pouch and afferent limb in symptomatic patients** — recommended, high quality ([[asge-2015-ibd]]).
  - Afferent-limb inflammation over a **long segment (≥10 cm)**, especially with discrete ulcers, suggests CD (after excluding NSAIDs and infection).
- **Report the five landmarks** — prepouch ileum, inlet, pouch body, anastomosis, cuff — and their diseased state; location helps separate structural, inflammatory and neoplastic disorders (Statement 17, [[asge-2025-ibd-endoscopy-consensus]]).
  - **Biopsy prepouch ileum, pouch body and rectal cuff** — grades severity and shows superimposed CMV, ischemia, CD of the pouch and prolapse.

### Stool Testing and Biomarkers
- ***C. difficile* — consider and treat it in any IPAA or [[ostomy-management|end ileostomy]] patient with worsening diarrhea**; Clostridioides difficile infection (CDI) is found in **10%–18%** of IPAA patients ([[aga-2026-cdiff-ibd]]).
  - **Multistep toxin-based assay**: nucleic acid amplification test (NAAT) alone may reflect carriage, since colonization is more common in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD).
  - Initial episode: **[[cdiff-antibiotics|fidaxomicin]]** preferred, vancomycin if unavailable or cost-prohibitive; no metronidazole.
  - **Retest if diarrhea recurs** after successful treatment.
  - Refractory pouchitis: evaluating for *C. difficile* and other secondary causes may be particularly beneficial ([[aga-2024-pouchitis]]).
- **Fecal calprotectin / lactoferrin** correlate with PDAI and endoscopic inflammation, but **cannot tell apart** acute vs chronic pouchitis, CD of the pouch or cuffitis ([[asge-2025-ibd-endoscopy-consensus]]); AGA 2024 did not systematically examine them and notes they are not routinely used.

### Pouch Neoplasia Surveillance
*Outside AGA 2024 scope.* [[asge-2025-ibd-endoscopy-consensus|ASGE 2025]] (newest; modified-Delphi consensus, no evidence grades), Statements 25–27:

| Risk | Who | Pouchoscopy interval |
|---|---|---|
| **High** | **Precolectomy colitis-associated neoplasia** — the only *established* risk factor | **Yearly** |
| **Moderate** | [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis\|Primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)]] · chronic pouchitis · CD of the pouch · chronic cuffitis · family history of [[colorectal-cancer\|colorectal cancer (CRC)]] | **1–3 y** |
| **Average** | Everyone else | **3 y** |

- **Biopsy the cuff and anal transition zone** — IPAA-associated neoplasia mostly arises there.
- None of the moderate-risk factors is statistically confirmed (sample-size limits).
- ⚠ **Change from older US documents:** [[aga-2021-ibd-colorectal-dysplasia|AGA 2021]] puts **PSC beside prior colorectal dysplasia/cancer at annual** pouchoscopy ([[asge-2015-ibd|ASGE 2015]]: prior dysplasia/cancer "should be considered" for annual surveillance; PSC, refractory pouchitis and type C mucosa "may be considered"); AGA 2021 also surveys persistent moderate-to-severe pouchitis and/or prepouch ileitis at least annually. ASGE 2025 is newest and **governs the interval here**. Colon surveillance in PSC-IBD stays annual ([[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis]]).
- Risk data ([[aga-2021-ibd-colorectal-dysplasia]]): prior colorectal **dysplasia → 4.4×** and prior **CRC → 15.0×** pouch-cancer risk; **without risk factors, cumulative pouch and cuff cancer incidence is 3.4% at 25 years**.
- IBD dysplasia surveillance of the colon: [[ulcerative-colitis]], [[colonoscopy]].

## Therapeutics

Recommendation numbers and certainty for every AGA 2024 statement: [[#Recommendation Summary]].

- **[[fmt|Fecal microbiota transplantation]] is *not* a pouchitis therapy** — [[aga-2024-fmt|AGA 2024 FMT guideline]] suggests **against conventional fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) for pouchitis except within a clinical trial** *(Rec 6, conditional / very low)*. Separate from recurrent or fulminant *C. difficile* infection **in** a pouch patient, where that guideline's Recs 1–3 still apply.

### Prevention
- **Primary prevention:** [[probiotics]] — no recommendation (Rec 1); antibiotics — suggested against (Rec 2; in the one tinidazole trial, some patients developed pouchitis 4–10 months after stopping) ([[aga-2024-pouchitis]]).
  - Contrast: [[aga-2020-probiotics|AGA 2020]] suggests the 8-strain combination "in adults and children with pouchitis" (conditional, very low); its evidence included 2 trials preventing an initial episode, rated Very Low certainty ([[aga-2020-probiotics-technical-review]]). AGA 2024 is newer and governs.
- **Secondary prevention (recurrent, antibiotic-responsive) — probiotics (Rec 5):** the **De Simone multistrain formulation** is the one used in the trials ([[aga-2024-pouchitis]]).
  - Trials gave it at **6 g/day after antibiotics had induced remission** (eg, ciprofloxacin + rifaximin for 1 month, or metronidazole + ciprofloxacin for 4 weeks).
  - 3 randomized controlled trials (RCTs): **87% lower relapse risk over 12 months** (relapse 6/45 on probiotic vs 36/41 on control).
  - Patients with infrequent recurrences, or for whom long-term probiotics are a burden, may reasonably choose no preventive treatment.
  - AGA 2020 suggests the 8-strain combination **over no or other probiotics**: no other formulation has been tested as rigorously, commercial availability has been in question (manufacturer–patent holder litigation), and benefit in [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis|FAP]] pouches or children is unknown ([[aga-2020-probiotics]], [[aga-2020-probiotics-technical-review]]).

### Treatment by Subtype

#### Intermittent Pouchitis
- **Antibiotics (Rec 3): ciprofloxacin and/or metronidazole preferred**; typical course **2–4 weeks** ([[aga-2024-pouchitis]]).
  - Panel's standard practice, **2 weeks** of either **ciprofloxacin 500 mg orally twice daily (BID)** or **metronidazole 500 mg orally 2–3 times daily**; ciprofloxacin generally better tolerated.
  - No response to one antibiotic → **combination** may work better.
  - No response, allergy or intolerance → alternative regimen such as **oral vancomycin** (125 mg BID effective in 1 cohort). [[rifaximin|Rifaximin]]: 1 pilot trial, numerically (not significantly) higher remission than placebo.
  - Pooled response **65%** (160/239; 95% confidence interval (CI) 52–75).

#### Chronic Antibiotic-Dependent Pouchitis
- Pouchoscopy first: confirm inflammation, rule out alternative causes.
- **Chronic antibiotics (Rec 6)** — to decrease antimicrobial resistance, consider:
  - **lowest effective dose** (eg, **ciprofloxacin 500 mg daily or 250 mg BID**) with **intermittent gap periods (~1 week off per month)**, or
  - **cyclical antibiotics** — rotate ciprofloxacin, metronidazole and vancomycin **every 1–2 weeks**.
  - Long-term risks: antimicrobial resistance, drug-resistant colonization, neuropathy (chronic metronidazole), tendinopathy (chronic ciprofloxacin). Chronic antibiotics may not significantly increase CDI risk after IPAA.
  - Episodes every 2–4 months: some patients prefer intermittent antibiotic courses (6–12 weeks/year) to chronic therapy.
- **Advanced immunosuppressive therapy (Rec 7)** — may be used **in lieu of chronic continuous antibiotics**, particularly if antibiotic-intolerant or if patient/provider is concerned about long-term antibiotic risks. Agents and data: next section. Therapies used **before colectomy** may be reconsidered.

#### Chronic Antibiotic-Refractory Pouchitis
- Pouchoscopy first: confirm inflammation, rule out alternative causes.
- **Advanced immunosuppressive therapy (Rec 8)** — any approved for UC or CD: [[anti-tnf-agents|tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) antagonists]] (infliximab, adalimumab, golimumab, certolizumab pegol), [[vedolizumab]], [[il-23-and-il-12-23-inhibitors|ustekinumab, risankizumab]], ozanimod, [[jak-inhibitors|tofacitinib, upadacitinib]].
  - **Vedolizumab: the only advanced therapy studied in a rigorous RCT (2 RCTs of biologics — vedolizumab and adalimumab), and the only advanced therapy with European Medicines Agency (EMA) approval for this indication.**
  - **EARNEST RCT:** mPDAI remission at week 14 in **18/51 (vedolizumab) vs 5/51 (placebo)**; all patients also received ciprofloxacin 500 mg BID through week 4.
  - Therapies used before colectomy may be reconsidered; some patients keep deriving partial benefit from continued antibiotics alongside advanced therapy.
  - Observational pooled response by class — **no significant difference between therapies (P = .24)**; small-cohort estimates have uninformative CIs, so they are not a ranking:

| Class | Cohorts / n | Pooled response (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|
| TNF-α antagonists | 14 cohorts, n = 245 | **54% (42–66)** |
| Vedolizumab | 9 cohorts, n = 194 | **52% (39–65)** |
| Ustekinumab | 2 cohorts, n = 31 | 72% (**4–99**) |
| Tofacitinib | 2 cohorts, n = 13 | 31% (**2–92**) |

- **[[corticosteroids-ibd|Corticosteroids]] (Rec 9)** — oral budesonide or beclomethasone: pooled response **77%** (95% CI 58–88; 2 case series, n = 30).
  - **Controlled ileal-release budesonide preferred.**
  - **Short duration (<8–12 weeks)**; plan steroid-sparing therapy for long-term use.
  - Steroid use, or response, is not required before switching to advanced therapy.
- **[[mesalamine-5-asa|Mesalamine]] (Rec 10):** no recommendation. Sulfasalazine may work in infrequent episodes (1 case series, 11 patients); unknown in chronic antibiotic-refractory pouchitis.

#### Crohn's-Like Disease of the Pouch
- Pouchoscopy to confirm the diagnosis (criteria: [[#Subtype Classification]]).
- **Corticosteroids (Rec 11):** controlled ileal-release budesonide preferred; **<8 weeks** (shorter than the <8–12 weeks for refractory pouchitis); plan steroid-sparing therapy for long-term use.
- **Advanced immunosuppressive therapy (Rec 12):** same UC/CD-approved agents as Rec 8; pooled response **74%** (95% CI 68–79; 10 cohorts, 288 patients). Pre-colectomy agents may be reconsidered; some patients still need chronic antibiotics for associated pouchitis.
- Strictures and leaks: [[#Endoscopic Therapy of Structural Pouch Complications]].

#### Cuffitis
- **First-line topical therapy (Rec 13):** mesalamine suppositories, corticosteroid suppositories, or corticosteroid ointment applied directly to the cuff.
- **Refractory →** immunosuppressive therapies approved for UC (TNF-α antagonists, vedolizumab, ustekinumab, risankizumab, ozanimod, tofacitinib, upadacitinib).
- Almost no direct data — **extrapolated from the AGA UC guidelines**, because the cuff is remnant rectal mucosa. Cuffitis often coexists with pouchitis → direct therapy at the predominant source, often both.

### Endoscopic Therapy of Structural Pouch Complications

*Beyond the AGA 2024 pouchitis guideline — [[aga-2026-therapeutic-endoscopy-ibd|AGA 2026 clinical practice update (CPU) on therapeutic endoscopy in IBD]].*

- **Strictures:** **endoscopic balloon dilation (EBD)** can be performed anywhere in the gastrointestinal tract, including anastomotic sites, and safely at ostomy-site strictures, including a continent (Kock) pouch. **Avoid EBD with deep ulcers or an associated fistula.** Selection criteria, target diameter and surgical risk factors: [[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease]].
- **Anastomotic leaks (ALs)** — classified **acute (within 30 days)** vs **chronic**.
  - Small ALs: conservative therapy or diverting ileostomy.
  - **Large (>3 cm) acute AL-associated abscess:** drain — externally by interventional radiology, or **internally** by endoscopic drainage endosponge or drainage stent; attempt closure with through-the-scope or over-the-scope clips **after** drainage.
  - **Chronic AL sinuses/fistulas:** endoscopic sinusotomy or fistulotomy; the text says over-the-scope clip closure at the tip of the J may be attempted, but the CPU's own Table 1 says to **avoid clipping** in a disease-associated fistula or chronic leak-associated sinus — the document does not reconcile the two.

### Recommendation Summary
All 13 AGA 2024 recommendations (10 conditional + 3 "no recommendation" knowledge gaps; the abstract says 9 conditional); evidence is mostly retrospective/observational. [[aga-2024-pouchitis]]

| # | Population / setting | Statement | Certainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | IPAA, primary prevention | Probiotics — **no recommendation** | Knowledge gap |
| 2 | IPAA, primary prevention | **Against** antibiotics | Very low |
| 3 | Infrequent symptoms | **Use antibiotics** (treat) | Very low |
| 4 | Infrequent episodes | Probiotics (treat) — **no recommendation** | Knowledge gap |
| 5 | Recurrent, antibiotic-responsive | **Use probiotics** (prevent recurrence) | Low (recommendation text, Table 9); very low in the Table 3 summary |
| 6 | Chronic antibiotic-dependent | **Use chronic antibiotics** | Very low |
| 7 | Chronic antibiotic-dependent | **Use advanced immunosuppressive therapy** | Low (vedolizumab) / very low (others) |
| 8 | Chronic antibiotic-refractory | **Use advanced immunosuppressive therapy** | Low (vedolizumab) / very low (others) |
| 9 | Chronic antibiotic-refractory | **Use corticosteroids** | Very low |
| 10 | Chronic antibiotic-refractory | Mesalamine — **no recommendation** | Knowledge gap |
| 11 | Crohn's-like disease of pouch | **Use corticosteroids** | Very low |
| 12 | Crohn's-like disease of pouch | **Use advanced immunosuppressive therapy** | Very low |
| 13 | Cuffitis | **Use UC-approved therapy** (start topical mesalamine / topical steroids) | Very low |
