## Assessment

- **Rectal prolapse = intussusception of the rectal wall through the anal canal**, from loss of normal rectal attachments. [[aga-2026-hemorrhoids]]
- It is a **structural** disorder, not a disorder of gut–brain interaction — [[rome-v-2026-dgbi|Rome V]] removed the "functional defecation disorders" category in part *because* that label swept in structural abnormalities such as rectal prolapse and perineal descent. [[rome-v-2026-dgbi]]
- **Pathogenesis is straining over time:** chronic excessive straining weakens the pelvic floor, producing excessive perineal descent, rectal intussusception, solitary rectal ulcer syndrome, and pudendal neuropathy. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Diagnosis is made by seeing the prolapse.** Examine in the left lateral position first.
- **If prolapse is suspected but not demonstrated in the left lateral position, repeat the examination with the patient squatting or sitting on a toilet/commode.** [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]
  - Decision point: a negative left-lateral exam does **not** exclude prolapse — the positional re-exam is what makes or breaks the diagnosis at the bedside.
- **Distinguish it from prolapsing [[hemorrhoids]] at inspection** — the single most useful bedside discriminator: [[aga-2026-hemorrhoids]]

| Finding | Rectal prolapse | Prolapsed hemorrhoids |
|---|---|---|
| **Mucosal folds** | **Circular** folds of pink rectal mucosa | **Radial** folds |
| **Vessels** | Not a feature | **Engorged blood vessels** |
| **Mechanism** | Intussusception of rectal wall through anal canal; loss of rectal attachments | Distal displacement of the anal cushions |

### Classification / Typing

**Full-thickness (overt) prolapse must be separated from the two more benign entities — it is the distinction that decides whether to operate.** [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]

| Type | Course | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Full-thickness (overt) rectal prolapse** | Progressive; leaving it untreated leads to **very significant morbidity** | Consider surgical correction — **with or without defecatory symptoms**, and with or without progressive [[fecal-incontinence\|FI]] |
| **Mucosal prolapse** | **More benign**; may be found in asymptomatic patients | Not an automatic surgical indication |
| **Internal intussusception** | **More benign**; may be found in asymptomatic patients | Not an automatic surgical indication; relevant when it is the lead point of a solitary rectal ulcer |

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup of the defecatory-symptom presentation: see [[chronic-constipation]]; the prolapse itself is diagnosed by positional examination — see [[#Diagnostics]] below.*

- [[hemorrhoids|Prolapsing hemorrhoids]] — radial folds and engorged vessels vs. circular pink mucosal folds (table above). [[aga-2026-hemorrhoids]]
- **Mucosal prolapse** and **internal intussusception** — the benign ends of the same spectrum. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]
- **Rectocele** — also identified on defecography; commonly present in **asymptomatic** women and usually does **not** require surgery. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]
- **Enterocele / sigmoidocele**, bladder and uterovaginal prolapse — co-identified on defecography once the vagina and small intestine are opacified; the utility of repairing enteroceles/sigmoidoceles is unclear. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]], [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]]
- [[defecation-disorders|Dyssynergic defecation]] — the functional mimic; may coexist. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]
- **Solitary rectal ulcer syndrome** — when found at [[colonoscopy|endoscopy]], it **suggests underlying rectal prolapse** and should prompt evaluation for it. [[asge-2014-constipation]]

## Diagnostics

| Test | Role |
|---|---|
| **Digital/visual rectal examination** — left lateral, then **squatting or seated on a commode** if negative | First and often definitive; the positional repeat is required before calling prolapse absent [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]] |
| **Defecography** (barium or MR) | Identifies **internal intussusception, solitary rectal ulcers, rectoceles, and rectal prolapse**; also enteroceles, bladder and uterovaginal prolapse when vagina/small bowel are opacified. Consider when clinical features suggest a structural abnormality, or when [[anorectal-manometry\|ARM]] and balloon expulsion are inconclusive [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]], [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]] |
| **[[anorectal-manometry]] + balloon expulsion** | Assesses coexisting dyssynergia — relevant because [[biofeedback-therapy\|biofeedback]], not surgery, treats the functional component [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]] |
| **[[colonoscopy]]** | Not diagnostic of prolapse, but a **solitary rectal ulcer** found incidentally should prompt evaluation for prolapse [[asge-2014-constipation]] |

- Caveat on defecography: **structural abnormalities of the pelvic floor occur commonly in asymptomatic subjects and usually do not require surgical correction**, and surgical restoration of structure often does not restore function. The named **exceptions** are **overt rectal prolapse** and a **sizeable, non-emptying rectocele** with typical vaginal-bulge/prolapse symptoms. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]

## Therapeutics

> **ACG 2021 Recommendation (defecation disorders, Table 2, #2):** "We suggest that **full-thickness rectal prolapse often requires surgical treatment with abdominal rectopexy or in selected cases a perineal procedure**." *(Conditional recommendation; moderate risk of harm; quality of evidence: very low. Consensus score: 30.)* [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]

- **Indication:** overt full-thickness prolapse, **with or without defecatory symptoms and/or progressive [[fecal-incontinence|fecal incontinence]]** — surgery relieves symptoms and improves continence, and the untreated alternative carries very significant morbidity (low-level evidence). [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]
- **Note the contrast with dyssynergia:** in a [[defecation-disorders|defecation disorder]] *without* overt prolapse, surgery requires **both** failure of [[biofeedback-therapy|biofeedback]] **and** a structural abnormality. Overt full-thickness prolapse is itself the structural indication and does not require a failed biofeedback trial first. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]

### Choosing the Operation

| Approach | Procedures | Outcome data |
|---|---|---|
| **Rectal suspension (preferred for most)** | **Laparoscopic rectopexy — posterior or ventral**; open posterior/ventral rectopexy | **Substantially lower recurrence than perineal procedures**; **73%–91% "some benefit"** globally; ventral mesh rectopexy gives **66%–86% improvement in preoperative constipation** [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]] |
| **Perineal** | **Altemeier**, **Delorme** | **27% recurrence** (Altemeier); a reasonable option for the **elderly or frail** [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]] |

- **Laparoscopic rectopexy (posterior or ventral) is the most appropriate operation for most patients with full-thickness prolapse**, on the basis of recurrence. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]
- **Perioperative complications of rectal suspension procedures: 5%–15%**, occasionally severe. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]

### Who Does Worse After Surgery

Added caution — worse outcomes reported in: psychiatric disorders; chronic pain or [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|IBS]]; morbid [[obesity]]; joint hypermobility; connective tissue disorders; women planning pregnancy; and those at high risk for pelvic surgery from previous surgery, infection, or radiotherapy. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]

### Solitary Rectal Ulcer Syndrome

- **[[biofeedback-therapy|Biofeedback]] and behavioral intervention first.** [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]
- **Surgery (e.g. laparoscopic ventral mesh rectopexy) may be considered when biofeedback and behavioral interventions are not effective** — especially when the ulcer sits at **the lead point of an internal rectal intussusception**. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]
- Across 2 studies (75 patients), **the ulcer healed in 78% of cases**. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]

> **Corpus gap:** no ingested source grades prolapse severity (e.g. by length of prolapsed segment) or covers non-operative/conservative management. A dedicated colorectal-surgery or pelvic-floor guideline would be needed to add those.
