## Assessment

- **Rectal prolapse = intussusception of the rectal wall through the anal canal**, from loss of normal rectal attachments. [[aga-2026-hemorrhoids]]
- It is a **structural** disorder, not a [[disorders-of-gut-brain-interaction|disorder of gut–brain interaction]] — [[rome-v-2026-dgbi|Rome V]] removed the "functional defecation disorders" category in part *because* that label swept in structural abnormalities such as rectal prolapse and perineal descent. [[rome-v-2026-dgbi]]
- **Pathogenesis is straining over time:** chronic excessive straining weakens the pelvic floor, producing excessive perineal descent, rectal intussusception, solitary rectal ulcer syndrome, and pudendal neuropathy. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]

**What comes with it** ([[ascrs-2017-rectal-prolapse]]) — these are the symptoms that drive the operative choice, so ask about all of them:

| Association | Frequency |
|---|---|
| [[fecal-incontinence\|Fecal incontinence (FI)]] | **50%–75%** |
| [[chronic-constipation\|Constipation]] | **25%–50%** |
| Pudendal neuropathy | **up to 50%** — denervation atrophy of the external sphincter |
| Urinary incontinence | **20%–35%** |
| Significant vaginal vault prolapse | **≈15%–30%** |
| Anterior solitary rectal ulcer on proctoscopy | **10%–15%** |

- **Multivisceral prolapse needs a multidisciplinary approach** — anterior-compartment symptoms are asked about on purpose, so both compartments can be repaired in one operation.

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Diagnosis is made by seeing the prolapse.** Examine in the left lateral position first.
- **If prolapse is suspected but not demonstrated in the left lateral position, repeat the examination with the patient squatting or sitting on a toilet/commode.** [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]
  - Decision point: a negative left-lateral exam does **not** exclude prolapse — the positional re-exam is what makes or breaks the diagnosis at the bedside.
- **If it still will not come out** ([[ascrs-2017-rectal-prolapse]], Evaluation Rec 1, Strong/low 1C): have the patient **strain on a toilet, with or without an enema or a rectal balloon**, and inspect sitting or squatting; failing that, ask the patient to **photograph it at home**. Expect a **patulous anus with diminished sphincter tone**.
- **Distinguish it from prolapsing [[hemorrhoids]] at inspection** — the single most useful bedside discriminator: [[aga-2026-hemorrhoids]]

| Finding | Rectal prolapse | Prolapsed hemorrhoids |
|---|---|---|
| **Mucosal folds** | **Circular** folds of pink rectal mucosa | **Radial** folds |
| **Vessels** | Not a feature | **Engorged blood vessels** |
| **Mechanism** | Intussusception of rectal wall through anal canal; loss of rectal attachments | Distal displacement of the anal cushions |

### Classification / Typing

**Full-thickness (overt) prolapse must be separated from the two more benign entities — it is the distinction that decides whether to operate.** [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]

| Type | Course | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Full-thickness (overt) rectal prolapse** | Progressive; leaving it untreated leads to **very significant morbidity** | Consider surgical correction — **with or without defecatory symptoms**, and with or without progressive [[fecal-incontinence\|fecal incontinence (FI)]] |
| **Mucosal prolapse** | **More benign**; may be found in asymptomatic patients | Not an automatic surgical indication |
| **Internal intussusception** | **More benign**; may be found in asymptomatic patients | Not an automatic surgical indication; relevant when it is the lead point of a solitary rectal ulcer |

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup of the defecatory-symptom presentation: see [[chronic-constipation]]; the prolapse itself is diagnosed by positional examination — see [[#Diagnostics]] below.*

- [[hemorrhoids|Prolapsing hemorrhoids]] — radial folds and engorged vessels vs. circular pink mucosal folds (table above). [[aga-2026-hemorrhoids]]
- **Mucosal prolapse** and **internal intussusception** — the benign ends of the same spectrum. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]
- **Rectocele** — also identified on defecography; commonly present in **asymptomatic** women and usually does **not** require surgery. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]
- **Enterocele / sigmoidocele**, bladder and uterovaginal prolapse — co-identified on defecography once the vagina and small intestine are opacified; the utility of repairing enteroceles/sigmoidoceles is unclear. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]], [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]]
- [[defecation-disorders|Dyssynergic defecation]] — the functional mimic; may coexist. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]
- **Solitary rectal ulcer syndrome** — when found at [[colonoscopy|endoscopy]], it **suggests underlying rectal prolapse** and should prompt evaluation for it. [[asge-2014-constipation]]

## Diagnostics

| Test | Role |
|---|---|
| **Digital/visual rectal examination** — left lateral, then **squatting or seated on a commode** if negative | First and often definitive; the positional repeat is required before calling prolapse absent [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]] |
| **Defecography** (barium or magnetic resonance (MR)) | Identifies **internal intussusception, solitary rectal ulcers, rectoceles, and rectal prolapse**; also enteroceles, bladder and uterovaginal prolapse when vagina/small bowel are opacified. Consider when clinical features suggest a structural abnormality, or when [[anorectal-manometry\|anorectal manometry (ARM)]] and balloon expulsion are inconclusive [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]], [[aga-2026-refractory-constipation]] |
| **[[anorectal-manometry]] + balloon expulsion** | Assesses coexisting dyssynergia — relevant because [[biofeedback-therapy\|biofeedback]], not surgery, treats the functional component [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]] |
| **[[colonoscopy]]** | Not diagnostic of prolapse, but a **solitary rectal ulcer** found incidentally should prompt evaluation for prolapse [[asge-2014-constipation]]. **Should typically be done before prolapse surgery** — rarely a neoplasm is the lead point, the population is older, and a finding **may change the operative plan** [[ascrs-2017-rectal-prolapse]] (Strong/moderate, 1B) |
| **Urodynamics / urogynecologic examination** | Selectively, when there are anterior-compartment or urinary-incontinence symptoms — lets both compartments be repaired in one operation [[ascrs-2017-rectal-prolapse]] |

- Caveat on defecography: **structural abnormalities of the pelvic floor occur commonly in asymptomatic subjects and usually do not require surgical correction**, and surgical restoration of structure often does not restore function. The named **exceptions** are **overt rectal prolapse** and a **sizeable, non-emptying rectocele** with typical vaginal-bulge/prolapse symptoms. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]

## Therapeutics

> **American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2021 Recommendation (defecation disorders, Table 2, #2):** "We suggest that **full-thickness rectal prolapse often requires surgical treatment with abdominal rectopexy or in selected cases a perineal procedure**." *(Conditional recommendation; moderate risk of harm; quality of evidence: very low. Consensus score: 30.)* [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]

- **Indication:** overt full-thickness prolapse, **with or without defecatory symptoms and/or progressive [[fecal-incontinence|fecal incontinence]]** — surgery relieves symptoms and improves continence, and the untreated alternative carries very significant morbidity (low-level evidence). [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]
- **Note the contrast with dyssynergia:** in a [[defecation-disorders|defecation disorder]] *without* overt prolapse, surgery requires **both** failure of [[biofeedback-therapy|biofeedback]] **and** a structural abnormality. Overt full-thickness prolapse is itself the structural indication and does not require a failed biofeedback trial first. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]

### Nonoperative Management

**Rectal prolapse cannot be corrected nonoperatively** — there are **no reports of it resolving on medical therapy alone**. What medicine treats is the symptoms ([[ascrs-2017-rectal-prolapse]], Weak/low 2C):

- **Constipation** → fiber and stool softeners.
- **Incarcerated prolapse** → **table sugar applied topically** reduces edema and assists reduction.
- **Skin care** to avoid maceration.
- **Treat the incontinence *before* the operation** — pre-treatment gave better postoperative continence (retrospective, 139 women).

> [!pitfall] Watchful waiting has a price, and it applies to the elderly too
> Patients managed medically only **develop irreversible fecal incontinence**, and **prolapse left untreated beyond 4 years** carries **higher recurrence after eventual repair**, presumably from a secondarily weakened pelvic floor. Reasonable surgical candidates — **including elderly ones** — should be advised to avoid unnecessary delay, and incarceration is the other reason not to wait ([[ascrs-2017-rectal-prolapse]]).

### Choosing the Operation

| Approach | Procedures | Outcome data |
|---|---|---|
| **Rectal suspension (preferred for most)** | **Laparoscopic rectopexy — posterior or ventral**; open posterior/ventral rectopexy | **Substantially lower recurrence than perineal procedures**; **73%–91% "some benefit"** globally; ventral mesh rectopexy gives **66%–86% improvement in preoperative constipation** [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]] |
| **Perineal** | **Altemeier**, **Delorme** | **27% recurrence** (Altemeier); a reasonable option for the **elderly or frail** [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]] |

- **Laparoscopic rectopexy (posterior or ventral) is the most appropriate operation for most patients with full-thickness prolapse**, on the basis of recurrence. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]
- **Perioperative complications of rectal suspension procedures: 5%–15%**, occasionally severe. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]

**Two selection rules decide which operation, not just which approach** ([[ascrs-2017-rectal-prolapse]]):

> [!pearl] Prolapse plus incontinence — fix the prolapse first
> Many patients' continence improves once the prolapse is gone, so **correct the prolapse as the first step** rather than working up the incontinence. In that same patient, the constipating manoeuvre is *desirable*: **dividing the lateral stalks lowers recurrence at the cost of constipation** (Weak/moderate, 2B) — "especially attractive in the patients presenting with fecal incontinence without constipation."

- **Coexisting constipation or dyssynergia disqualifies the manoeuvres that worsen it** — posterior rectal mobilization, lateral-ligament transection during suture rectopexy, and levatorplasty during perineal proctectomy. **Severe preoperative constipation → more than a suture rectopexy** (resection rectopexy, ventral rectopexy, or a perineal repair).
- ⚠ **Suture rectopexy alone creates constipation:** **≥50%** of already-constipated patients worsen and **≈15%** become constipated de novo.
- ⚠ **The one hard technical rule:** **posterior mobilization without a rectopexy is not recommended** (Strong/moderate, 1B) — an RCT found an **8-fold rise in recurrence** with mobilization alone. **Rectopexy is a key component of the abdominal approach** (Strong/high, **1A**).

**Operations and their numbers** ([[ascrs-2017-rectal-prolapse]]) — ASCRS explicitly **declines to rank posterior against anterior repair** (Strong/low, 1C: insufficient evidence either way; both <10% recurrence at 10 years). Choice is by surgical expertise, surgical history, comorbidity and patient preference.

| Operation | Recurrence | Decision notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Suture rectopexy** (posterior) | 3%–9% at 2 y; **29% at 10 y** | Worsens constipation (above) |
| **+ sigmoid resection** (resection rectopexy) | 2%–5% | **Add it for preoperative constipation** (Strong/moderate, 1B). ⚠ **Avoid after a failed perineal rectosigmoidectomy — risk of ischemic bowel.** ⚠ **FI resolves less often** when resection is added; avoid with markedly reduced anal pressures or severe baseline incontinence. Not usually combined with mesh |
| **Posterior mesh rectopexy** (modified Ripstein) | 2%–5% | Morbidity **20%**, mostly minor; **improves FI in 20%–60%**. The original anterior-wall Ripstein had excessive complications (mesh erosion, obstruction, ureteral injury, rectovaginal fistula) |
| **Ventral mesh rectopexy** (D'Hoore) | 3.4%; **8.2% at 10 y** (919 patients) | **Limited anterior mobilization only, no posterior dissection** → spares rectal innervation: **84% improvement in constipation, no de novo constipation**; mesh complications **4.6%**. ⚠ Preferred by many in Europe but **"has yet to gain full acceptance in the United States"** |
| **Delorme** (perineal) | 10%–15% | **For a short (<5 cm) full-thickness prolapse** — mucosal sleeve resection + muscularis imbrication. Early complications **4%–12%**; urgency and tenesmus occur |
| **Altemeier / perineal rectosigmoidectomy** | **16%–30% at 2 y** (27% per [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]) | **Can be done without general anesthesia**; shorter stay, fewer complications than abdominal. **Adding levatorplasty cut recurrence 21% → 7%** |

- **Minimally invasive beats open when feasible** (Strong/moderate, 1B): equivalent recurrence (4%–8%) and morbidity (10%–33%), with better pain control, shorter stay and faster return of bowel function. **Robotic appears comparable to laparoscopic**, with longer operating time and higher cost.

### Who Does Worse After Surgery

Added caution — worse outcomes reported in: psychiatric disorders; chronic pain or [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)]]; morbid [[obesity]]; joint hypermobility; connective tissue disorders; women planning pregnancy; and those at high risk for pelvic surgery from previous surgery, infection, or radiotherapy. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]

### Solitary Rectal Ulcer Syndrome

- **[[biofeedback-therapy|Biofeedback]] and behavioral intervention first.** [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]
- **Surgery (e.g. laparoscopic ventral mesh rectopexy) may be considered when biofeedback and behavioral interventions are not effective** — especially when the ulcer sits at **the lead point of an internal rectal intussusception**. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]
- Across 2 studies (75 patients), **the ulcer healed in 78% of cases**. [[acg-2021-anorectal-disorders]]

> **No ingested source, including the ASCRS guideline, gives a severity grading or staging scale for rectal prolapse.** The only length-based threshold in the corpus is the **<5 cm** cut-off that selects the Delorme procedure.
