*Entamoeba histolytica* is a protozoan cause of **bloody (dysenteric) diarrhea** and of persistent diarrhea, acquired mainly through travel to resource-challenged countries and through sexual contact. It is **microscopically indistinguishable from the non-pathogenic *Entamoeba dispar***, so the diagnosis rests on a species-specific stool test, and treatment follows a microbiologic diagnosis rather than a syndrome ([[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]], [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]).

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Visible blood in stool** puts *E. histolytica* on the differential — it is one of the few parasitic causes of grossly bloody stools (differential below). [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- **Higher body temperature** — fever is not highly discriminatory, but higher temperatures suggest either a bacterial etiology or *E. histolytica*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- **Persistent or [[chronic-diarrhea|chronic diarrhea]]** — *E. histolytica* heads this differential together with *Cryptosporidium*, *Giardia*, *Cyclospora* and *Cystoisospora*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- Confirmation is **microbiologic, not clinical**, and ordinary stool microscopy cannot make it (see [[#Diagnostics]]).

**When to send stool studies**

- Dysentery, moderate-to-severe disease, or symptoms lasting **>7 days** *(strong recommendation, very low level of evidence)*. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Rec 2)
- Travelers with diarrhea **≥14 days** should be evaluated for intestinal parasites *(strong, moderate)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 13)
- Moderate-to-severe primary or secondary immune deficiency warrants a **broad** differential evaluated by culture, viral studies and parasite examination; people with acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS) and persistent diarrhea need added testing for the AIDS-associated organisms *(strong, moderate)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 12) See [[hiv-aids-related-diarrhea]].

**Exposures that raise suspicion** ([[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]): travel to resource-challenged countries; anal-genital, oral-anal or digital-anal contact. *E. histolytica* is spread by **low inocula**, so secondary transmission to close contacts occurs.

### Severity Assessment

- Severity is graded clinically, and it is what decides whether stool studies are sent (Rec 2 above) — **mild** = no change in activities; **moderate** = able to function but with forced change in activities due to illness; **severe** = total disability due to diarrhea. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Figure 1)
- **Intestinal perforation** is the recognized extraintestinal/postinfectious manifestation of *E. histolytica*; patients should be evaluated for postinfectious and extraintestinal manifestations of enteric infection *(strong, moderate)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 7, Table 4)

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[acute-diarrhea]].*

- Bacterial dysentery — [[shigellosis]], [[campylobacter-infection]], [[salmonella-infection]], and **[[shiga-toxin-producing-e-coli|Shiga toxin–producing / enterohemorrhagic *E. coli* (STEC/EHEC)]]** — the last of which must **not** be given antimicrobials; it is a different organism from [[enterotoxigenic-e-coli|enterotoxigenic *E. coli* (ETEC)]]
- [[clostridioides-difficile|*C. difficile*]] colitis — though bloody stools are not an expected manifestation of it
- Other protozoa — [[giardiasis]] (watery, non-bloody), [[cryptosporidiosis]]
- [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]] flare ([[ulcerative-colitis]]) — a key mimic before immunosuppression is started
- Travel-associated febrile illness — see [[fever-returning-traveler]]

## Diagnostics

Stool is the optimal specimen for every option below. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]

| Test | Role |
|---|---|
| ***E. histolytica* species-specific immunoassay, or nucleic acid amplification test (NAAT)** | The species-level test — the only listed way to establish *E. histolytica* rather than a morphologically identical non-pathogen. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Table 5) |
| Multiplex gastrointestinal (GI) polymerase chain reaction (PCR) panel | Singleplex and multiplex PCR for enteric pathogens are **more sensitive than culture, microscopy or antigen detection**; culture-independent methods are recommended at least as an adjunct to traditional methods *(strong, low)*. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Rec 3) |
| Ova-and-parasite examination, including permanent stained smear | Detects *Entamoeba* but does not speciate it — see pitfall. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Table 5) |
| Serology | Not recommended to establish the etiology of infectious diarrhea *(strong, low)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 21) |
| [[colonoscopy\|Endoscopy]] / proctoscopy | Consider in persistent unexplained diarrhea with AIDS, in acute diarrhea with clinical colitis or proctitis, and in persistent diarrhea in people who engage in anal intercourse *(strong, low)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 24) |

> [!pitfall] A plain *Entamoeba* result is not an indication to treat
> Microscopy, and even enzyme-linked immunoassay and direct immunofluorescence staining, **may not distinguish** the pathogen *E. histolytica* from the non-pathogenic, microscopically indistinguishable *E. dispar* ([[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]). Ask for the species-specific immunoassay or NAAT before treating ([[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]).

- Interpret a positive NAAT with the clinical picture: these assays detect DNA, and not necessarily viable organisms *(strong, low)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 14)

## Therapeutics

- **Agents: metronidazole *or* tinidazole.** [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] names these two for *E. histolytica* and rates the evidence for antimicrobial treatment of specific parasitic causes of acute diarrhea as **strong**.
  - Neither guideline states a dose, route, interval or duration for amebiasis, and neither names a follow-on luminal agent.
- **Treat only a speciated infection.** For dysenteric diarrhea the sequence is microbiologic assessment, then an antimicrobial agent directed to the cause — for every pathogen except STEC. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Figure 1) Antimicrobial treatment should be modified or discontinued once a clinically plausible organism is identified *(strong, high)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 38)
- **Empiric therapy while awaiting results is not recommended** for bloody diarrhea in immunocompetent children and adults *(strong, low)*. Exceptions *(weak, low)*: infants <3 months with suspicion of a bacterial etiology; an ill immunocompetent person with fever documented in a medical setting plus abdominal pain, bloody diarrhea and bacillary dysentery (frequent scant bloody stools, fever, cramps, tenesmus) presumptively from *Shigella*; recent international travel with temperature **≥38.5°C** and/or signs of sepsis. In immunocompromised people with severe illness and bloody diarrhea, empiric antibacterial treatment **should be considered** *(strong, low)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Recs 30, 32) Agent choice when empiric therapy is given: see [[acute-diarrhea]].
- **Asymptomatic detection:** asymptomatic people who practice hand hygiene and live or work in low-risk settings need no treatment *(weak, low)*; those who provide healthcare, child care or elderly-adult care, or are food-service employees, should be treated per local public health guidance *(strong, high)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 51)
- **[[loperamide]] — avoid.** Antimotility drugs are to be avoided at any age in inflammatory diarrhea or diarrhea with fever, and where toxic megacolon may result *(strong, low)*; never under 18 years of age *(strong, moderate)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 47)
- **Rehydration** with reduced-osmolarity oral rehydration solution (ORS) is first-line for mild-to-moderate dehydration *(strong, moderate)* — details on [[acute-diarrhea]]. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 39)

### Prevention and public health

- Hand hygiene is of limited value against most traveler's diarrhea pathogens but **may be useful where low-dose pathogens are responsible** *(conditional, low)*. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Rec 15)
- Symptomatic people should **avoid swimming, water-related activities and sexual contact** while symptomatic, with meticulous hand hygiene *(strong, low)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 56)
