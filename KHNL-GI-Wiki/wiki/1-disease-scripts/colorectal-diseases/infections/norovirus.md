Most common cause of epidemic gastroenteritis in the US. Very low infectious inoculum → explosive foodborne and person-to-person spread; classic agent of cruise-ship, institutional, and endemic outbreaks. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- Abrupt onset of **vomiting** (often prominent) + watery, **non-bloody** diarrhea, cramps, low-grade fever; self-limited over **24–72 h**.
- Usually a **clinical/epidemiologic diagnosis** in an outbreak; confirm with **multiplex GI PCR** when etiology is needed. Fresh stool is the preferred specimen for viral agents. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- Serology, fecal leukocytes, and lactoferrin should **not** be used to establish etiology. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]

### Severity Assessment

- **Mild–moderate** — self-limited in immunocompetent hosts.
- **Severe** — dehydration risk in the elderly and infants; **prolonged/chronic** viral shedding and diarrhea in immunocompromised hosts. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]

---

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[acute-diarrhea]].*

- Other viral gastroenteritis — [[rotavirus]], adenovirus (prominent vomiting favors viral over bacterial).
- Inflammatory bacterial enteritides — [[campylobacter-infection]], [[salmonella-infection]], [[shigellosis]] (bloody/febrile favors these).
- [[clostridioides-difficile|*C. difficile*]] colitis (antibiotic/healthcare exposure).
- Preformed-toxin food poisoning (*S. aureus*, *B. cereus*) — even shorter incubation.

---

## Diagnostics

- **Multiplex GI PCR** detects norovirus; fresh stool preferred for viral targets. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- No role for stool culture (bacterial only) or special stains.

---

## Therapeutics

- **Supportive only** — oral rehydration / ORS; IV fluids if unable to maintain intake.
- **No antibiotics** — community-acquired diarrhea is predominantly viral and is not shortened by antibiotics (Rec 10). [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]
- **[[rifaximin|Rifaximin]]: no role** — viral pathogen.
- **Antimotility ([[loperamide]]):** may be given to immunocompetent adults with watery diarrhea; **not** in children <18 y and avoid with fever/inflammatory features. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- **Infection control:** hand hygiene is of limited value against most traveler's-diarrhea pathogens but **useful for norovirus** in cruise-ship/institutional/endemic settings (Rec 15); exclude symptomatic food handlers. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]
