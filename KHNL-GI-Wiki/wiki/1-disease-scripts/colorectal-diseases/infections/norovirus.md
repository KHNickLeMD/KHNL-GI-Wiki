Most common cause of diarrheal-disease outbreaks in the United States, and the agent accounting for most deaths and healthcare visits associated with acute gastroenteritis outbreaks (2009–2013). [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] Very low infectious inoculum plus environmental stability → explosive foodborne and person-to-person spread; classic agent of cruise-ship, institutional, and endemic outbreaks. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- Abrupt onset of **[[nausea-and-vomiting|vomiting]]** (often prominent) + watery, **non-bloody** diarrhea and cramps, lasting **2–3 days or less** and self-limited; low-grade fever is present during the **first 24 h in 40%** of infections. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- Usually a **clinical and epidemiologic diagnosis in an outbreak** — recognized norovirus infection has largely been limited to outbreak investigations, and endemic rates remain under study. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- Norovirus was implicated in **84% of the 2,430 reported person-to-person acute gastroenteritis outbreaks** with a suspected or confirmed etiology (United States, 2009–2013); where a setting was reported, **70%** of these person-to-person outbreaks occurred in long-term care facilities. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- **Very low infectious inoculum** — noroviruses and *Shigella* are the most contagious enteric pathogens, from low inoculum requirements plus stability in the environment. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]

**Exposure clues that raise suspicion** [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]

| Exposure | Note |
|---|---|
| Hotels, cruise ships, resorts, restaurants, catered events | Classic foodborne-outbreak setting |
| Raw shellfish | Also *Vibrio* species, hepatitis A, *Plesiomonas* |
| Fruits, unpasteurized juices, vegetables, leafy greens, sprouts | Shared with [[shiga-toxin-producing-e-coli\|Shiga toxin–producing *E. coli* (STEC)]], *Salmonella*, *Cyclospora* |
| Healthcare, long-term care, prison exposure or employment | Shared with [[clostridioides-difficile\|*C. difficile*]], [[shigellosis\|*Shigella*]], [[rotavirus]] |

### Severity Assessment

- Illness severity is graded **functionally** (mild = no change in activities · moderate = forced change in activities · severe = total disability) — the grade is what decides whether stool testing is indicated and whether antimotility therapy is offered; criteria table on [[acute-diarrhea#Severity Classification (ACG 2016)|the American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2016 severity classification]]. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]
- What makes the illness dangerous is **dehydration**, not the organism — assess dehydration grade, oral intake, and host (infant, elderly, immunocompromised). [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- **Immunocompromised host:** chronic and severe norovirus infection is reported with immunosuppression after organ transplantation. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- **Hospital-acquired infection:** patients who acquire norovirus while hospitalized — especially those with immunocompromising conditions and those of advanced age — may be more likely to die. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- **Residents >90 y of long-term care facilities:** 20%–30% increased risk of death and of hospitalization during norovirus outbreaks. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[acute-diarrhea]].*

- Other viral gastroenteritis — [[rotavirus]], adenovirus (prominent vomiting favors viral over bacterial).
- Inflammatory bacterial enteritides — [[campylobacter-infection]], [[salmonella-infection]], [[shigellosis]] (bloody/febrile favors these).
- [[clostridioides-difficile|*C. difficile*]] colitis (antibiotic/healthcare exposure).
- Preformed-toxin food poisoning — *S. aureus* enterotoxin or *Bacillus cereus* short-incubation emetic syndrome gives nausea and vomiting lasting **≤24 h**; *Clostridium perfringens* or *B. cereus* long-incubation emetic syndrome gives diarrhea and cramping lasting **1–2 days**. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]

## Diagnostics

| Test | Role in norovirus |
|---|---|
| Multiplex gastrointestinal (GI) panel — nucleic acid amplification test (NAAT) / polymerase chain reaction (PCR), stool | **The test.** The only diagnostic procedure the guideline lists for caliciviruses (norovirus, sapovirus); multiplex NAAT brought norovirus within reach of the clinical laboratory [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] |
| Specimen | **Fresh stool** preferred for viral agents *(Rec 19, weak/low)*; a rectal swab substitutes only for **bacterial** targets when timely stool cannot be collected [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] |
| Stool culture | **No role** — routine culture covers *Salmonella*, *Shigella*, *Campylobacter*, and *E. coli* O157/STEC only [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] |
| Serology · fecal leukocytes · stool lactoferrin | **Do not use** to establish etiology *(Recs 20–22)* [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] |

- **Molecular caveat:** a NAAT/multiplex result is nucleic acid, not necessarily a viable organism, and detection of more than one pathogen is common — interpret in clinical context *(Rec 14, strong/low)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- **No test of cure.** Follow-up testing is not recommended after diarrhea resolves *(Rec 26, strong/moderate)*; the serial negative-stool rules some jurisdictions require before return to child care or work are **culture-based and apply to bacterial pathogens**. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]

### When to Test

- Patient is at **high risk of spreading disease to others**, or a **known or suspected outbreak** → stool culture and culture-independent methods *(ACG Rec 1, strong/low)*. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]
- **Possible outbreak:** test a broader bacterial, viral, and parasitic set regardless of fever or bloody/mucoid stools, choosing agents by host and epidemiologic risk and coordinating with public health *(Rec 11, strong/moderate)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- **Immunocompromised host:** broaden to culture, viral studies, and parasite examination *(Rec 12, strong/moderate)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- Otherwise most uncomplicated watery diarrhea needs **no etiologic testing**; the general indications (dysentery, moderate-to-severe illness, symptoms >7 days) are on [[acute-diarrhea#When to Test (ACG 2016 Indications)]]. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]

## Therapeutics

### Antibiotics — no role

- Antibiotics for community-acquired diarrhea **should be discouraged** — most is viral (norovirus, [[rotavirus|rotavirus]], adenovirus) and is **not shortened** by antibiotics *(Rec 10, strong/very low)*. This includes [[rifaximin|rifaximin]], whose role in the guideline is confined to noninvasive bacterial traveler's diarrhea. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]
- Empiric antimicrobial therapy is **not recommended** for acute watery diarrhea without recent international travel *(Rec 36, strong/low)* — exception: the immunocompromised or an ill-appearing young infant. Avoid empiric treatment once watery diarrhea has persisted **≥14 days**. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]

### Supportive care — the treatment

- **Rehydration by dehydration grade:** reduced-osmolarity oral rehydration solution (ORS) for mild–moderate; nasogastric ORS if moderate dehydration with intolerance of oral intake; isotonic intravenous (IV) crystalloid (lactated Ringer's, normal saline) for severe dehydration, shock, altered mental status, or ORS failure *(Recs 39–43)*. Age- and weight-banded volumes are on [[acute-diarrhea]]. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- **Ancillary agents only once the patient is adequately hydrated** — never a substitute for fluid and electrolyte therapy *(Rec 46, weak/low)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- **Antimotility ([[loperamide]]):** may be given to immunocompetent **adults** with acute watery diarrhea *(Rec 47, weak/moderate)*; **never <18 y** *(strong/moderate)*; avoid at any age with fever, inflammatory diarrhea, or where [[toxic-megacolon|toxic megacolon]] may result *(strong/low)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] For mild illness, hydration alone ± loperamide to control stooling. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]

### Infection control and exclusion

- A patient with diarrhea is placed on **contact precautions in addition to standard precautions**: gloves, gowns, and hand hygiene, with the hand-hygiene product chosen for the known or suspected pathogen and the setting *(Rec 53, strong/high; product choice strong/low)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]

> [!pitfall] Alcohol gel does not reliably remove norovirus
> For norovirus — as for [[cryptosporidiosis|*Cryptosporidium*]] and spore-formers such as [[clostridioides-difficile|*C. difficile*]] — hand hygiene with **soap and water may be more effective than an alcohol-based sanitizer**; alcohol-based hygiene is the default only when these are *not* the suspected agent and there is no visible soiling ([[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]). [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] cites the same finding: handwashing with soap and water removed norovirus from hands while alcohol-based sanitizers did not.

- Hand washing and alcohol-based sanitizers are of **limited value against most traveler's-diarrhea pathogens** (high inoculum) but **may be useful where a low-dose pathogen is responsible** — a cruise-ship norovirus outbreak, an institutional outbreak, or endemic diarrhea prevention *(Rec 15, conditional/low)*. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]
- **While symptomatic:** avoid swimming and water-related activities and sexual contact, with meticulous hand hygiene *(Rec 56, strong/low)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- **Occupational exposure:** people with diarrhea who attend or work in child care, long-term care, patient care, food service, or recreational water venues follow **jurisdictional outbreak-reporting and infection-control rules** *(Rec 2, strong/high)*. Public health may place symptomatic food handlers, recreational-water staff, healthcare providers, and child-care providers **on furlough until the risk of transmission is eliminated or reduced**. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- Neither guideline states an incubation period, a duration of viral shedding, or a fixed exclusion interval for norovirus; [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] points instead to separate published guidance on preventing and controlling norovirus gastroenteritis outbreaks in healthcare settings.
