Enterotoxigenic *E. coli* (ETEC) is the most common cause of traveler's diarrhea (TD) worldwide and a leading cause of diarrheal illness in developing countries. Produces heat-labile (LT) and/or heat-stable (ST) enterotoxins → watery, non-bloody diarrhea. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- Watery, non-bloody diarrhea with cramps and nausea, typically **no fever**, after travel to an endemic region.
- **Diarrheagenic *E. coli* are the most common bacterial pathogens in the Western Hemisphere.** [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]
- Routine stool testing not required for uncomplicated TD; diarrheagenic *E. coli* pathotypes require specialized/molecular assays if pursued.

### Severity Assessment

- **Mild** — tolerable, non-limiting → oral rehydration ± [[loperamide]] alone.
- **Moderate-to-severe** — distressing or function-limiting diarrhea → antibiotics + loperamide.
- Grade illness with the ACG mild / moderate / severe activity definitions — see [[acute-diarrhea]].

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[acute-diarrhea]].*

- Other traveler's-diarrhea pathogens — [[campylobacter-infection]], [[salmonella-infection]], [[shigellosis]]
- **Shiga toxin–producing / enterohemorrhagic *E. coli* (STEC/EHEC)** — bloody diarrhea + hemolytic uremic syndrome (HUS); a **different pathotype from ETEC** and the reason to identify which *E. coli* you have. **Do NOT give antimicrobials** for STEC O157 or any STEC producing Shiga toxin 2 — *or when the toxin genotype is unknown* *(strong, moderate)*; for non–toxin-2 STEC the evidence is insufficient either way *(strong, low)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 35). Antibacterials appear to **increase HUS risk** in Shiga-toxin disease, and [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] directs pathogen-targeted therapy in febrile, prolonged diarrhea **"for all but STEC infection."**
- Viral gastroenteritis — [[norovirus]], [[rotavirus]], adenovirus; watery and non-bloody, and the reason antibiotics are discouraged for community-acquired (non-travel) diarrhea *(strong, very low)*. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Rec 10)
- Noninfectious causes of watery diarrhea

---

## Diagnostics

- Clinical diagnosis in the TD setting; multiplex GI PCR can identify diarrheagenic *E. coli* if testing pursued.
- **High inoculum requirement** — this is why hand hygiene helps less against ETEC than against the low-inoculum pathogens *[[shigellosis|Shigella]]* and norovirus. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]

---

## Therapeutics

- **Antibiotics:** fluoroquinolone or azithromycin for moderate-to-severe TD — single dose or 3-day course; agent-by-agent doses are in the ACG Table 4 TD regimens on [[acute-diarrhea]]. If a single dose has not resolved symptoms by **24 h**, complete a 3-day course. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]
  - **Azithromycin is the empiric first line if *resistant ETEC* is suspected** (as it is in Southeast Asia and India for [[campylobacter-infection|Campylobacter]]). [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Table 4, footnote c)
- **[[rifaximin|Rifaximin]] 200 mg PO three times daily × 3 days** — effective against non-invasive diarrheagenic *E. coli* including ETEC. Appropriate for ETEC TD, but **do NOT use if there is clinical suspicion for [[campylobacter-infection|Campylobacter]], [[salmonella-infection|Salmonella]], [[shigellosis|Shigella]], or other causes of invasive diarrhea.** [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Table 4, footnote e)
- **[[loperamide|Loperamide]] adjunct is recommended with antibiotics** for TD — decreases duration and increases chance of cure *(strong recommendation, moderate level of evidence)*. Dosing when combined with an antibiotic regimen: **4 mg first dose, then 2 mg after each loose stool, not to exceed 16 mg in 24 h** — the 16 mg ceiling applies **only when combined with an antibiotic**; unaccompanied self-treatment is capped at **8 mg/day and ≤48 h**. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Rec 8; Table 4 footnote b)
- Oral rehydration is the foundation of supportive care.

### Postinfectious sequelae

- **[[postinfectious-ibs|Postinfectious IBS]]** is a recognized sequela of infectious gastroenteritis, diagnosed on Rome criteria; there is **no commercially available serologic test** for it. Symptoms persisting past the acute illness are evaluated as persistent diarrhea, where **serologic/clinical lab testing (Rec 11)** and **endoscopy (Rec 12)** are *not* recommended between 14 and 30 days with a negative stool workup. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]
