Enterotoxigenic *Escherichia coli* (ETEC) is one of the diarrheagenic *E. coli* pathotypes, alongside enteroaggregative, enteroinvasive, and enteropathogenic *E. coli*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] Diarrheagenic *E. coli* are the **most common causes of traveler's diarrhea (TD)** and the **most common bacterial pathogens in the Western Hemisphere**. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- Clinical diagnosis in the traveler: **watery, non-bloody diarrhea**. ETEC is not among the pathogens that cause visible blood in the stool. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Table 3)
- Exposures that put ETEC in the differential: **travel to a resource-challenged country**, and **foodborne outbreaks in hotels, cruise ships, resorts, restaurants, and catered events**. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Table 2)
- **Testing is not needed in most uncomplicated TD** unless treatment is indicated. A traveler with diarrhea ≥14 days is evaluated for intestinal parasites, and tested for [[clostridioides-difficile|*Clostridioides difficile*]] if antimicrobials were given in the preceding 8–12 weeks. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 13)
- Stool studies are indicated for dysentery, moderate-to-severe disease, or symptoms lasting >7 days — American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) indications in full on [[acute-diarrhea]]. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Rec 2)

### Severity Assessment

ACG grading decides whether antibiotics are given; the full severity table is on [[acute-diarrhea]].

- **Mild** (no change in activities) → hydration only; [[loperamide|loperamide]] 4 mg initially may be used to control stooling. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Figure 1)
- **Moderate** (forced change in activities) **to severe** (total disability) and travel-associated → empiric antibiotic plus loperamide. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Rec 9, strong recommendation, high level of evidence; Figure 1)

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[acute-diarrhea]].*

- Other pathogens of travel to resource-challenged countries — [[campylobacter-infection]], [[salmonella-infection]] (typhoidal and nontyphoidal), [[shigellosis]], *Vibrio cholerae*, [[entamoeba-histolytica-infection|*Entamoeba histolytica*]], [[giardiasis|*Giardia*]], [[cryptosporidiosis|*Cryptosporidium*]], *Cyclospora*, *Cystoisospora*, *Blastocystis*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Table 2)
- The other diarrheagenic *E. coli* pathotypes (enteroaggregative, enteroinvasive, enteropathogenic) — found by the same specialized assays, not by routine culture.
- **[[shiga-toxin-producing-e-coli|Shiga toxin–producing / enterohemorrhagic *E. coli* (STEC/EHEC)]]** — bloody diarrhea and hemolytic uremic syndrome (HUS); a **different pathotype from ETEC**, with the opposite treatment rule.
- Viral gastroenteritis — [[norovirus]], [[rotavirus]], adenovirus; watery and non-bloody, and the reason antibiotics are discouraged for community-acquired (non-travel) diarrhea *(strong recommendation, very low level of evidence)*. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Rec 10)
- Noninfectious causes of watery diarrhea.

> [!pitfall] Settle the pathotype before giving antibiotics
> Antimicrobials must be **avoided** in STEC O157 and other Shiga toxin 2 producers because of HUS risk, so bloody diarrhea is not treated the way ETEC is ([[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]], Rec 35).

## Diagnostics

- Routine stool culture is built for [[salmonella-infection|*Salmonella*]], [[shigellosis|*Shigella*]], [[campylobacter-infection|*Campylobacter*]], and *E. coli* O157 / STEC — **it does not cover ETEC**. Detection requires **specialized stool culture, molecular assays, or nucleic acid amplification testing (NAAT)** on stool, so **notify the laboratory** when ETEC is suspected. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Table 5)
- A positive multiplex panel reflects nucleic acid, not necessarily a viable organism — interpret with the clinical picture. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 14) Panels and their targets: [[acute-diarrhea]].
- Antibiotic sensitivity testing is not recommended for management of the individual patient. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Rec 4)

## Therapeutics

### Antibiotics

- **Indicated** for moderate-to-severe travel-associated diarrhea; empiric antimicrobials are not supported for routine acute diarrheal infection outside TD. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Rec 9)
- **Not indicated** without travel: most community-acquired diarrhea is viral (Rec 10), and the Infectious Diseases Society of America (IDSA) guideline likewise advises against empiric antimicrobials in acute watery diarrhea without recent international travel *(strong, low)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 36) ETEC also appears in domestic foodborne outbreaks, where that rule applies.

| Agent | Dose | Duration | Role in ETEC |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[rifaximin\|Rifaximin]] | 200 mg by mouth three times daily | 3 days | Effective against noninvasive diarrheagenic *E. coli*; **do not use** if *Campylobacter*, *Salmonella*, *Shigella*, or another invasive cause is suspected |
| Azithromycin | 1,000 mg by mouth once, or 500 mg by mouth daily | Single dose, or 3 days | Empiric first line when **resistant ETEC** is suspected, and in Southeast Asia and India (fluoroquinolone-resistant *Campylobacter*); preferred regimen for dysentery or febrile diarrhea |
| Fluoroquinolone | Levofloxacin 500 mg, or ofloxacin 400 mg, or ciprofloxacin 750 mg (single dose) / 500 mg (3-day course) — all by mouth | Single dose, or 3 days | Acceptable regimen when the azithromycin conditions above do not apply |

*ETEC-relevant rows and footnotes of the ACG 2016 traveler's diarrhea regimen table (Table 4); the full table is on [[acute-diarrhea]].*

- Single-dose therapy is as effective as 3 days for noninvasive TD pathogens; **if symptoms have not resolved after 24 h, complete a 3-day course**. A 3-day course is recommended for fever or dysentery. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Table 4 footnotes b–d)

### Adjunctive and Supportive Care

- **Loperamide alongside the antibiotic** decreases duration of diarrhea and increases chance of cure *(strong recommendation, moderate level of evidence)*. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Rec 8)
  - **4 mg first dose, then 2 mg after each loose stool.** Ceiling **16 mg in 24 h only when combined with an antibiotic**; unaccompanied self-treatment is capped at **8 mg/day for no more than 48 h**. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Table 4 footnote a)
  - IDSA restricts it further: never under 18 years of age, and avoided at any age with fever, inflammatory diarrhea, or risk of toxic megacolon — so for febrile illness the two guidelines diverge. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 47)
- **Oral rehydration** is the foundation of supportive care; a balanced solution is preferred for the elderly with severe diarrhea and for any traveler with cholera-like watery diarrhea *(strong recommendation, moderate level of evidence)*. Formulations on [[acute-diarrhea]]. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Rec 5)
- **Bismuth subsalicylate** controls the rate of stool passage and may help travelers function better during mild-to-moderate illness *(strong recommendation, high level of evidence)*; dosing on [[acute-diarrhea]]. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Rec 7)

### Prevention

- Pretravel counseling on avoiding high-risk food and beverages *(conditional, very low)*; bismuth subsalicylate or antibiotic chemoprophylaxis may be considered for high-risk travelers on short trips. Regimens and efficacy on [[acute-diarrhea]]. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Rec 14, 16, 18)

> [!pearl] Hand sanitizer does not prevent ETEC
> Diarrheagenic *E. coli* need a high infective dose — on the order of a million organisms or more, nearly always from improperly handled food — so hand washing and alcohol-based sanitizers are of limited value against TD, unlike for the low-inoculum pathogens [[norovirus|norovirus]] and *Shigella* ([[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]], Rec 15).

### Postinfectious Sequelae

- **[[postinfectious-ibs|Postinfectious irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)]]** is a recognized sequela of infectious gastroenteritis, diagnosed on Rome criteria; there is **no commercially available serologic test** for it. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]
- Symptoms outlasting the acute illness are handled as persistent diarrhea (pathway on [[acute-diarrhea]]); at ≥14 days consider noninfectious causes, including [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]] and IBS. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 28)
