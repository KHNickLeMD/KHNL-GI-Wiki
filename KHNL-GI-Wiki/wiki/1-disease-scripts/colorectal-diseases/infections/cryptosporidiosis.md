*Cryptosporidium* is a protozoan cause of watery diarrhea. Treatment is **pathogen-directed** — the trigger is a microbiologic diagnosis, not a syndrome ([[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]). People with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV)/acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS) and persistent diarrhea require testing for it specifically ([[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]).

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Watery diarrhea** puts *Cryptosporidium* on the differential; confirmation is microbiologic, not clinical.
- **Persistent (14–29 day) or chronic (≥30 day) diarrhea** — *Cryptosporidium* is one of the protozoa that head this differential, and infectious persistent diarrhea is **best managed with pathogen-specific therapy rather than empiric antimicrobials before the organism is known**; empiric treatment should be avoided in persistent watery diarrhea ≥14 days (strong recommendation, low quality). [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- In **[[hiv-aids-related-diarrhea|AIDS with persistent diarrhea]]**, test specifically for *Cryptosporidium* (alongside *Cyclospora*, *Cystoisospora*, microsporidia, *Mycobacterium avium* complex (MAC), cytomegalovirus (CMV)). [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]

**Exposures that raise suspicion** ([[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]):

| Route | Exposure |
|---|---|
| Water | Swimming in or drinking untreated fresh water; swimming in a treated recreational water facility when disinfectant concentrations are inadequately maintained |
| Food | Unpasteurized milk or dairy products; fruits, unpasteurized fruit juices, vegetables, leafy greens, sprouts; foodborne outbreaks in hotels, cruise ships, resorts, restaurants, catered events |
| Animal / travel | Visiting a farm or petting zoo; travel to resource-challenged countries |
| Setting | Child care center attendance or employment; healthcare, long-term care, or prison exposure or employment |
| Sexual | Anal-genital, oral-anal, or digital-anal contact |
| Host | Any underlying immunocompromising condition; AIDS or immunosuppressive therapy |

### Severity Assessment

- **Immunocompromised hosts** — moderate-to-severe primary or secondary immune deficiency warrants a **broad** differential, evaluated by culture, viral studies, and parasite examination (strong recommendation, moderate quality). [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- Protozoal diarrhea is **more likely to be severe, chronic, or relapsing** in immunocompromised people, **particularly those with impaired cell-mediated immunity, including advanced HIV infection** — in [[hiv-aids-related-diarrhea|AIDS]], [[chronic-diarrhea|chronic diarrhea]] is the expected course. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- Neither guideline sets a CD4 threshold for severe or chronic disease; the **<100 cells/mm³** cutoff on [[hiv-aids-related-diarrhea]] governs endoscopic biopsy yield, not severity.

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[acute-diarrhea]].*

- Other parasitic watery diarrhea — [[giardiasis|*Giardia*]], *Cyclospora*, *Cystoisospora*, microsporidia.
- Viral gastroenteritis — [[norovirus]], [[rotavirus]].
- [[entamoeba-histolytica-infection|*Entamoeba histolytica*]] (dysenteric, not watery).
- Enterotoxigenic bacteria — [[enterotoxigenic-e-coli|enterotoxigenic *E. coli* (ETEC)]].

## Diagnostics

Stool is the optimal specimen for every option below. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]

| Test | Role |
|---|---|
| Multiplex gastrointestinal (GI) nucleic acid amplification test (NAAT) / polymerase chain reaction (PCR) panel | The test to send. Culture-independent molecular methods surpass culture, microscopy (with and without special stains and immunofluorescence), and antigen testing for yield and speed, and are recommended at least as an **adjunct to traditional methods** (strong recommendation, low level of evidence); in one study PCR detected **22-fold** more *Cryptosporidium* and *Giardia* than conventional microscopy. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] |
| Direct fluorescent immunoassay, or enzyme immunoassay (EIA) | Organism-specific alternatives where a panel is unavailable. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] |
| Ova-and-parasite microscopy | Unreliable for this organism — see pitfall below. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] |

> [!pitfall] Ask for *Cryptosporidium* by name
> Microscopic stool examination for ova and parasites is **unlikely to include** *Cryptosporidium* or *Cyclospora* — request each specifically. *Cryptosporidium* and *Giardia* testing is commonly offered together as the primary parasitology examination, with further studies only if the epidemiologic setting or clinical picture suggests other parasitic disease ([[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]).

## Therapeutics

- **[[nitazoxanide]]** is the named agent for cryptosporidiosis. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]], [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
  - **HIV-uninfected:** nitazoxanide is first choice.
  - **HIV-infected:** nitazoxanide **only in combination with effective combination antiretroviral therapy (cART)** — it is not given alone in this setting. The listed alternative is **effective cART by itself**, since immune reconstitution may produce the microbiologic and clinical response.
  - Neither guideline states a dose, interval, or duration — see [[nitazoxanide]].
- **Immunocompromised:** restore immunity — effective cART is the key intervention; nitazoxanide has limited efficacy as monotherapy in advanced immunosuppression. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- **Supportive:** rehydration — see [[acute-diarrhea]].

### Prevention and public health

- Cryptosporidiosis is a **nationally notifiable condition**; all notifiable diarrheal diseases are reported to the state, territorial, or local health department (strong recommendation, high quality). People with diarrhea who attend or work in recreational water venues (e.g., pools and lakes), child care, long-term care, patient care, or food service should follow jurisdictional outbreak-reporting and infection-control recommendations (strong recommendation, high quality). [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- Symptomatic people should **avoid swimming, water-related activities, and sexual contact** while symptomatic, with meticulous hand hygiene (strong recommendation, low quality). [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]

> [!pitfall] Alcohol sanitizer is the wrong choice here
> *Cryptosporidium* is **extremely chlorine-tolerant**, and when it is the infecting agent **hand hygiene with soap and water may be more effective than an alcohol-based sanitizer**. Specific infection-control guidelines are recommended for its control in institutional settings ([[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]).

### Postinfectious sequelae

- *Cryptosporidium* is a named trigger of [[postinfectious-ibs|postinfection irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)]], and **parasitic enteritis carries a markedly higher risk than bacterial** — rates by preceding pathogen class are tabulated on [[postinfectious-ibs]]. [[acg-2020-ibs]]
