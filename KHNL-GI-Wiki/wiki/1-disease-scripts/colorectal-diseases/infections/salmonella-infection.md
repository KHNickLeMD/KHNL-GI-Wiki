Non-typhoidal *Salmonella* species are a major cause of foodborne diarrheal illness in the US and a common cause of traveler's diarrhea. Typically febrile, sometimes bloody diarrhea. Invasive potential (bacteremia, endovascular seeding) distinguishes it from noninvasive enteric pathogens. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- Acute febrile, sometimes bloody, diarrhea after foodborne exposure or travel.
- **Stool testing** (culture or multiplex gastrointestinal (GI) polymerase chain reaction (PCR) panel) is indicated when diarrhea comes with **fever, bloody or mucoid stools, severe abdominal cramping or tenderness, or signs of sepsis**. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 8)
- Non-typhoidal *Salmonella* is a **notifiable** pathogen — report it and **submit the isolate**; a molecular-panel–positive stool is cultured in the clinical or public health laboratory so the strain can be subtyped and susceptibility-tested *(Rec 15, strong/low)*.
- **Exposures raising pre-test probability** (Infectious Diseases Society of America (IDSA) Table 2): poultry and undercooked eggs; unpasteurized milk/dairy; fruits, unpasteurized juices, vegetables, leafy greens, sprouts; untreated fresh water; contact with young poultry or reptiles; travel to resource-challenged countries; recent antimicrobial therapy (multidrug-resistant strains); anal-genital, oral-anal or digital-anal contact.
- **Suspect enteric (typhoid) fever** instead — fever with or without diarrhea plus travel to an endemic area, food prepared by someone with recent endemic exposure, or laboratory exposure to *S.* Typhi/Paratyphi *(Rec 4, strong/moderate)*.

### Severity Assessment

- **Uncomplicated** — immunocompetent host, self-limited gastroenteritis.
- **Severe / invasive** — high fever, bacteremia, sepsis, or immunocompromise; risk of endovascular seeding (aortitis, mycotic aneurysm) in older/atherosclerotic hosts.
- Grade illness activity with the American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) mild / moderate / severe definitions — see [[acute-diarrhea]].

**Host features that define "increased risk for invasive infection"** — the list that decides whether to give antibiotics at all (see Therapeutics) [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Table 6):

- Neonates / infants **up to 3 months** of age
- Persons **>50 years** with **suspected atherosclerosis**
- **Immunosuppression**
- **Cardiac disease** — valvular or endovascular
- **Significant joint disease**

Separately named as risk factors for **invasive** non-typhoidal infection: young and advanced age, impaired immunity from human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection or cytotoxic chemotherapy, malnutrition, hemoglobinopathies, recent malaria, and [[cirrhosis]]. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[acute-diarrhea]].*

- Other inflammatory bacterial enteritides — [[campylobacter-infection]], [[shigellosis]], *Yersinia enterocolitica*
- **[[shiga-toxin-producing-e-coli|Shiga toxin–producing / enterohemorrhagic *E. coli* (STEC/EHEC)]]** — bloody diarrhea ± hemolytic uremic syndrome (HUS). **The one bloody-diarrhea pathogen you must NOT treat** — the opposite of the rule for *Salmonella*. *(Distinct again from [[enterotoxigenic-e-coli|enterotoxigenic *E. coli* (ETEC)]], which is watery and noninvasive.)*
- Amebic dysentery — [[entamoeba-histolytica-infection|*Entamoeba histolytica*]]; needs species-level enzyme immunoassay (EIA)/PCR (microscopy cannot separate it from non-pathogenic *E. dispar*). [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]
- [[clostridioides-difficile|*C. difficile*]] colitis (antibiotic/healthcare exposure)
- [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|Inflammatory bowel disease]] flare
- Typhoid/enteric fever (*S.* Typhi/Paratyphi) — see [[fever-returning-traveler]]

## Diagnostics

- **Stool:** culture or multiplex GI PCR. A molecular panel detects nucleic acid, not necessarily viable organisms — interpret with the clinical picture *(Rec 14, strong/low)*.
- **Blood cultures** — infants <3 months, any age with signs of septicemia or suspected enteric fever, systemic manifestations of infection, immunocompromise, high-risk conditions such as hemolytic anemia, and febrile illness of unknown etiology in travelers to (or contacts of travelers from) enteric-fever–endemic areas *(strong, moderate)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 9)
- **Bone marrow culture** is likely more sensitive than blood culture for invasive non-typhoidal infection, but routine aerobic blood culture is the practical first test for suspected invasive salmonellosis or enteric fever.
- **Enteric fever:** blood + stool + urine ± bone-marrow, duodenal-fluid culture; **serology must not be used** to diagnose it *(Rec 17)* — full workup on [[fever-returning-traveler]].
- **Imaging (ultrasound, computed tomography (CT), or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI))** to look for aortitis, mycotic aneurysm, peritonitis, free air, [[toxic-megacolon|toxic megacolon]], or extravascular foci — in **older** people with invasive *Salmonella enterica*, when any of: **sustained fever or bacteremia despite adequate therapy**, **underlying atherosclerosis**, or **recent-onset chest / back / abdominal pain** *(weak, low)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 25)

## Therapeutics

### Whom to treat

**Antibiotics are usually NOT indicated for uncomplicated infection** in immunocompetent adults — meta-analysis shows they do not shorten the illness and they **prolong the period of intestinal carriage / shedding** (ACG notes short-term carriage is of limited clinical significance to the person affected). [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]], [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]

| Situation | Treat? | Agent |
|---|---|---|
| Uncomplicated gastroenteritis, immunocompetent | **No** | — |
| Any host at **increased risk for invasive infection** (list under *Severity Assessment*) | **Yes**, if susceptible | **Ceftriaxone**, **ciprofloxacin**, **trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX)**, or **amoxicillin** |
| **Invasive disease suspected or confirmed** | **Yes** | **Ceftriaxone preferred over ciprofloxacin** — increasing ciprofloxacin resistance |
| **Enteric (typhoid/paratyphoid) fever** — *S.* Typhi / Paratyphi | **Yes** | First: **ceftriaxone or ciprofloxacin**; alternatives: **ampicillin, TMP-SMX, or azithromycin** |
| Sepsis with suspected enteric fever | **Yes — empiric** | Broad-spectrum, started **after** blood + stool + urine cultures; **narrow** when susceptibilities return *(Rec 34)* |
| Any pathogen-directed course | — | **Modify or stop** antimicrobials once a clinically plausible organism is identified *(Rec 38, strong/high)* |

*Agent selection from [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] Table 6; check current US susceptibility patterns (National Antimicrobial Resistance Monitoring System (NARMS)) when choosing.*

**Neither guideline gives a dose, route, or duration for these agents in salmonellosis** — IDSA Table 6 names agents only, and the ACG dose table covers only the traveler's-diarrhea regimens (azithromycin, fluoroquinolones, [[rifaximin]]). Dose from a dedicated enteric-fever / non-typhoidal *Salmonella* treatment reference.

> [!pitfall] Two drugs that look useful here and are not
> **Rifaximin — do NOT use:** non-absorbable, and explicitly not for invasive pathogens ([[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]).
> **Antimotility agents ([[loperamide]]):** avoid at any age with fever or inflammatory diarrhea, and in anyone <18 years ([[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]], Rec 47).

### Asymptomatic carriage

- **Non-typhoidal:** adults commonly keep shedding sporadically **for weeks** after acute illness. Carriers who practise hand hygiene and live/work in **low-risk** settings need **no treatment**; the only randomized decolonization trial (Thailand) showed **no benefit over placebo**. Treatment **may be considered** where state/local law mandates negative stool cultures before returning to work. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 51)
- ***S.* Typhi:** asymptomatic shedding is common and can **persist beyond a year**; these carriers **may be treated empirically to reduce transmission** *(weak, low)* — the decolonization trials behind this used fluoroquinolones.
- **High-risk occupations** (healthcare, child or elderly care, food service): treat per local public-health guidance *(strong, high)*.

**Follow-up cultures before returning to child care, work, or group activities** *(Rec 26, strong/moderate)* — rules vary by jurisdiction, so confirm with local public health; IDSA's worked example:

| Scenario | What is typically required |
|---|---|
| Non-typhoidal *Salmonella* | Negative stool cultures are typically **not** required for return to child care |
| *S.* Typhi | **3 negative stool cultures**, ≥24 h apart, ≥48 h after stopping antimicrobials, and **not earlier than 1 month** after symptom onset |
| *S.* Typhi, any culture still positive | Monthly stool cultures for the next **12 months**, until ≥3 consecutive cultures show no growth |

Repeat testing uses **traditional culture** — molecular panels do not show that living organisms are present and are not validated as proof of cure.

### Prevention & public health

- **Notifiable** — report and **submit the isolate** *(Rec 60, strong/high)*.
- Cook and hold meats and eggs at proper temperatures; avoid cross-contamination of foods, surfaces and utensils *(Rec 54)*.
- **Typhoid vaccination** — for travelers to moderate-to-high-risk areas, people with intimate exposure to a documented *S.* Typhi chronic carrier, and laboratory personnel routinely exposed to *S.* Typhi cultures; **boost while risk continues** *(Rec 58, strong/high)*. Not routine in the US. Each vaccine gives **50%–80%** protection, and **neither protects against *S.* Paratyphi A, B, or C**. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]

| Vaccine | Licensed for | Booster |
|---|---|---|
| **Ty21a** — live attenuated, oral (enteric capsules) | Immunocompetent people, including children **≥6 years** | every **5 years** |
| **Vi polysaccharide** — parenteral | Children **≥2 years** and adults | every **2 years** |

### Postinfectious sequelae

- **Reactive arthritis**, erythema nodosum, [[postinfectious-ibs|post-infectious irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)]], and intestinal perforation.
- **Meningitis** — infants ≤3 months are at highest risk.
- **Aortitis, osteomyelitis, and other extravascular deep-tissue foci** — the invasive complications that drive the imaging rule above. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Table 4)
