Rotavirus — with [[norovirus]] and adenovirus — is one of the viral agents behind **most community-acquired [[acute-diarrhea|acute diarrhea]]**, which antibiotics do not shorten. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] Before US vaccine programs began in 2006 it was the leading cause of acute gastroenteritis producing medical visits and hospitalization in children **<5 years**; a routine infant vaccine is now recommended for all. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Non-inflammatory, watery diarrhea.** Without high fever, significant abdominal pain, or duration >3 days — the features that suggest an inflammatory bacterial infection — viral infection becomes significantly more likely, and antimicrobial treatment is ineffective and potentially harmful. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- No clinical feature identifies rotavirus; confirmation is a stool test, and **most cases need no etiologic testing**. Stool studies are reserved for dysentery, moderate-to-severe disease, or symptoms lasting >7 days *(strong, very low)* (Rec 2), and for the patient at high risk of spreading disease to others or a known/suspected outbreak *(strong, low)* (Rec 1). [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]
- **Possible outbreak** (several people who shared a common meal, a sudden rise in cases) → consider a broader bacterial, viral, and parasitic set regardless of fever or bloody/mucoid stools, choosing agents by host and epidemiologic risk and coordinating with public health *(strong, moderate)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 11)
- **Immunocompromised host** → broad differential evaluated by culture, viral studies, and parasite examination *(strong, moderate)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 12)

**Exposures and host factors that raise the pretest probability** [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Table 2)

| Factor | Detail |
|---|---|
| Age | **6–18 months** |
| Setting exposure | Child care center attendance or employment; healthcare, long-term care, or prison exposure or employment |
| Inoculum | Low-inoculum pathogen (like [[norovirus]], *Giardia*, [[shigellosis\|*Shigella*]], [[shiga-toxin-producing-e-coli\|Shiga toxin–producing *E. coli* (STEC)]]) → efficient person-to-person spread |

> [!pearl] Persistent rotavirus diarrhea — look for primary immunodeficiency
> Persistent rotavirus diarrhea, both non-vaccine strain and vaccine strain, has been reported in young children with primary immunodeficiency ([[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]).

### Severity Assessment

- **Evaluate every patient with acute diarrhea for dehydration** — it raises the risk of life-threatening illness and death, especially in the young and in older adults *(strong, high)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 5) Severity is driven by volume depletion, not by the organism.
- A variety of scales grade dehydration severity in young children, but **no single standard, validated method exists**; signs of dehydration may be **masked when a child is hypernatremic**. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- The grade sets the rehydration route — see [[#Supportive Care]].

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[acute-diarrhea]].*

- Other viral gastroenteritis — [[norovirus]], adenovirus
- Non-inflammatory bacterial — [[enterotoxigenic-e-coli|enterotoxigenic *E. coli* (ETEC)]]
- Protozoal (watery, more often persistent) — [[giardiasis]], [[cryptosporidiosis]]
- [[clostridioides-difficile|*C. difficile*]] colitis (antibiotic / healthcare exposure)
- Non-infectious at symptoms ≥14 days — [[postinfectious-ibs|post-infectious irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)]], [[irritable-bowel-syndrome]], [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]] *(strong, moderate)* [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 28)

## Diagnostics

| Test | Role |
|---|---|
| Stool nucleic acid amplification test (NAAT) — multiplex gastrointestinal (GI) panel | Detects rotavirus (Table 5); culture-independent methods are recommended at least as an adjunct to traditional methods (culture, microscopy, antigen testing), which miss most etiologies *(strong, low)* [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Rec 3) |
| **Stool enzyme immunoassay (EIA; antigen)** | The traditional rotavirus-specific assay; also detects enteric adenovirus (Table 5) [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] |
| Specimen | **Fresh stool** is preferred for viral agents *(weak, low)*; a rectal swab substitutes only for **bacterial** targets when a timely diarrheal stool cannot be collected [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 19) |
| Serology | **Do not use** to establish etiology *(strong, low)* — exception: may be considered in post-diarrheal hemolytic uremic syndrome (HUS) when stool culture yielded no Shiga toxin–producing organism *(weak, low)* [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 21) |
| Peripheral white blood cell (WBC) count + differential | **Do not use** to establish etiology *(strong, low)*; may still be clinically useful *(weak, low)* [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 22) |
| Fecal leukocytes / stool lactoferrin | **Do not use** to establish etiology *(strong, moderate)*; insufficient data on fecal calprotectin [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 20) |
| Antibiotic sensitivity testing | Not recommended for management of the individual with acute diarrheal infection *(strong, very low)* [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Rec 4) |

- **Molecular caveat:** multiple-pathogen NAAT panels detect nucleic acid, not necessarily a viable organism — interpret positives with clinical consideration *(strong, low)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 14)

## Therapeutics

### Antimicrobial Therapy

- Antibiotics for community-acquired diarrhea **should be discouraged**: most is viral (norovirus, rotavirus, adenovirus) and is not shortened by antibiotics *(strong, very low)*. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Rec 10)
- Empiric antimicrobial therapy is **not recommended** for acute watery diarrhea without recent international travel *(strong, low)* — **exception:** may be made in the **immunocompromised** or an **ill-appearing young infant**. Avoid empiric treatment entirely once watery diarrhea has lasted **≥14 days** *(strong, low)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 36)

### Supportive Care

**Rehydration is the treatment** — route follows the dehydration grade. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]

| Dehydration | Route and fluid | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Mild-to-moderate, any age | Reduced-osmolarity oral rehydration solution (ORS), first-line for acute diarrhea of any cause — including mild-to-moderate dehydration with [[nausea-and-vomiting\|vomiting]] or severe diarrhea (Rec 39) | strong, moderate |
| Moderate, cannot tolerate oral intake — or a child with normal mental status too weak or refusing to drink adequately | Nasogastric ORS (Rec 40) | weak, low |
| Severe dehydration, shock, altered mental status, failure of ORS | Isotonic intravenous (IV) crystalloid — lactated Ringer's, normal saline (Rec 41) | strong, high |
| Ileus | Isotonic IV crystalloid (Rec 41) | strong, moderate |

- **Endpoints:** in severe dehydration continue IV fluid until pulse, perfusion, and mental status normalize and the patient awakens, has no aspiration risk and no ileus *(strong, low)*, then replace the remaining deficit with ORS *(weak, low)*; in mild-to-moderate dehydration give ORS until clinical dehydration is corrected *(strong, low)* (Rec 42). Once rehydrated, give maintenance fluids and replace ongoing stool losses with ORS until diarrhea and vomiting resolve *(strong, low)* (Rec 43). Age- and weight-banded volumes are on [[acute-diarrhea]]. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- Balanced electrolyte rehydration is preferred in the **elderly with severe diarrhea** and in any traveler with cholera-like watery diarrhea; most others keep up with water, juices, sports drinks, soups, and saltine crackers *(strong, moderate)*. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] (Rec 5)
- **Feeding:** continue human-milk feeding throughout the episode *(strong, low)* (Rec 44); resume an age-appropriate usual diet during or immediately after rehydration *(strong, low)* (Rec 45). [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- **Ancillary agents only once the patient is adequately hydrated** — antimotility, antinausea, and antiemetic agents are never a substitute for fluid and electrolyte therapy *(weak, low)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 46)
- **Children:** ondansetron may be given to facilitate tolerance of oral rehydration in children **>4 years** and adolescents with vomiting *(weak, moderate)* (Rec 48); oral **zinc** shortens diarrhea in children **6 months–5 years** who reside in countries with a high prevalence of zinc deficiency or who have signs of malnutrition *(strong, moderate)* (Rec 50). [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- [[probiotics]] — **may** be offered to immunocompetent adults and children with infectious diarrhea to reduce symptom severity and duration *(weak, moderate)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 49)
  > **Guidelines differ.** The older [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2016]] recommends **against** probiotics or prebiotics for treatment of acute diarrhea in adults **except** in post-antibiotic-associated illness *(strong, moderate)* (Rec 6), and against probiotics, prebiotics, and synbiotics for traveler's diarrhea (TD) prophylaxis *(conditional, low)* (Rec 17). The newer [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea|Infectious Diseases Society of America (IDSA) 2017]] position above is the one to follow.

> [!pitfall] No loperamide under 18 years
> [[loperamide]] and other antimotility drugs must **not** be given to children <18 y with acute diarrhea *(strong, moderate)*. They may be given to immunocompetent **adults** with acute watery diarrhea *(weak, moderate)*, but are avoided at any age with fever, inflammatory diarrhea, or where [[toxic-megacolon|toxic megacolon]] may result *(strong, low)* ([[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]], Rec 47).

### Prevention

- **Rotavirus vaccine should be administered to all infants without a known contraindication** *(strong, high)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 57)

| US-licensed vaccine | Type | Schedule |
|---|---|---|
| Pentavalent (RotaTeq, Merck) | Live, attenuated, oral | **3 doses** |
| Monovalent (Rotarix, GSK) | Live, attenuated, oral | **2 doses** |

- Large phase 3 trials showed efficacy of **74%–87% against any rotavirus infection** and **85%–98% against severe gastroenteritis** — the basis of the Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP) universal infant recommendation (2006 and 2008). Surveillance since shows significant reductions in outpatient visits and hospitalizations in vaccinated infants (direct protection) and in unvaccinated, age-ineligible children and adults (community protection). [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] does not enumerate the contraindications or the dose ages and upper age limit for either vaccine; those are in the ACIP rotavirus vaccine statement.
- Pre-vaccine US burden in children <5 y: an estimated ~3 million episodes of acute gastroenteritis, >500,000 outpatient visits, 27,000 hospitalizations, and about 25 deaths annually; [[norovirus]] has held the lead since vaccine introduction. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- Symptomatic people should avoid swimming, water-related activities, and sexual contact, with meticulous hand hygiene *(strong, low)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] (Rec 56)
