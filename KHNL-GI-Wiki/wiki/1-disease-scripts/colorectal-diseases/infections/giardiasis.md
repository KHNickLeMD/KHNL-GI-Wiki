*Giardia* is a common cause of watery diarrhea throughout the United States and the leading parasitic cause of traveler's diarrhea. Diagnosis is by **stool antigen immunoassay or polymerase chain reaction (PCR)**, not ova-and-parasite microscopy ([[aga-2019-lab-evaluation-functional-diarrhea-ibs-d]]), and treatment is pathogen-directed ([[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]).

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- *Giardia* (*G. duodenalis* / *G. lamblia*) — the most common parasitic enteric pathogen in the United States; approximately **20,000 reported cases per year**, with rates decreasing since 2012 (**5.8 per 100,000 population**). [[acg-2020-ibs]]
- About **10% of traveler's diarrhea is parasitic, giardiasis the most common**; parasitic [[fever-returning-traveler|traveler's diarrhea]] can persist for **weeks to months**. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- One of the protozoa heading the differential for **persistent or [[chronic-diarrhea|chronic diarrhea]]**. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- Presentation is not specific: parasitic infection ranges from **asymptomatic to self-limited to chronic [[abdominal-bloating-and-distention|bloating]], diarrhea, and abdominal pain — similar to [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)]]**. [[acg-2020-ibs]]

**Who to test — Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) risk factors for *Giardia* infection** (Table 5). These are the criteria that convert "do not test routinely" into "test". [[acg-2020-ibs]]

| Risk factor |
|---|
| Children in childcare settings, in particular diaper-aged children |
| Close contacts of people with giardiasis (e.g. living in the same household) or people who care for them |
| People who drink water or use ice from places where *Giardia* may live (untreated or improperly treated water from lakes, streams, or wells) |
| Backpackers, hikers, and campers who drink unsafe water or do not practice good hygiene (e.g. proper handwashing) |
| People who swallow water while swimming and playing in recreational water |
| People exposed to human feces through sexual contact |
| International travelers where *Giardia* may live, especially lakes, rivers, springs, ponds, and streams |

- The American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) **recommends against routine stool testing for enteric pathogens in all patients with IBS** *(conditional recommendation; low quality of evidence)* — **except those with high pretest probability and definite risk factors for *Giardia* exposure**, in whom testing is indicated. Testing is also warranted in the appropriate clinical setting: travel to endemic areas, poor water quality, camping, daycare exposure. [[acg-2020-ibs]]
- CDC recommends **screening patients with acute diarrhea lasting >3 days**. [[acg-2020-ibs]]

### Severity Assessment

- Severity is judged by **duration and host**, not by a score — no ingested guideline grades giardiasis severity.
- **Persistent symptoms (14–30 days)** trigger the ACG persistent-diarrhea workup: serologic and clinical laboratory testing is recommended *(strong recommendation, very low level of evidence)*. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]]
- Protozoal diarrhea is **more likely to be severe, chronic, or relapsing** in impaired cell-mediated immunity, including advanced [[hiv-aids-related-diarrhea|human immunodeficiency virus (HIV)]] infection. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[acute-diarrhea]].*

- Other persistent-diarrhea parasites: [[cryptosporidiosis]], [[entamoeba-histolytica-infection]]
- Bacterial enteritis: [[campylobacter-infection]], [[salmonella-infection]], [[shigellosis]]
- Post-infectious / functional: [[postinfectious-ibs]], [[irritable-bowel-syndrome]]
- Malabsorptive: [[celiac-disease]]

## Diagnostics

| Test | Role and performance | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Stool *Giardia* antigen enzyme immunoassay (EIA) or nucleic acid amplification test (NAAT) / PCR** | The tests to send. Sensitivity and specificity **>95%** in many studies *(strong recommendation, high-quality evidence — the only high-quality rating in that document)*; PCR targets the *Giardia* small subunit ribosomal RNA | [[aga-2019-lab-evaluation-functional-diarrhea-ibs-d]], [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] |
| **Fecal immunoassay or PCR** (same tests, ACG figures) | Sensitivity **82%–100%**, specificity **91.5%–100%** | [[acg-2020-ibs]] |
| **Multiplex gastrointestinal (GI) molecular panel** | *Giardia* is a routine target; real-time PCR for *G. intestinalis* detects as little as **10² spores/mL** of stool vs microscopy, which required **>10⁶ spores/mL** | [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] |
| **Ova-and-parasite microscopy** | Suggested **against** for parasites *other than Giardia* when there is no travel history to, or recent immigration from, a high-risk area *(conditional recommendation, low-quality evidence)* | [[aga-2019-lab-evaluation-functional-diarrhea-ibs-d]] |
| **Duodenal fluid / aspirate** | **May be considered in select people** for suspected *Giardia*, *Strongyloides*, *Cystoisospora*, or microsporidia *(weak, low)* — shown useful when stool evaluation did not yield an etiology in recurring diarrhea | [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]] |

> [!pearl] *Giardia* is the one stool parasite still worth sending
> The American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) **recommends testing for *Giardia* in all patients with chronic diarrhea** *(strong, high-quality)* while suggesting **against** routine ova and parasites otherwise — the assays perform well and "because treatments are straightforward, there is little risk in utilizing these tests" ([[aga-2019-lab-evaluation-functional-diarrhea-ibs-d]]). ACG is narrower in IBS, testing only those with risk factors ([[acg-2020-ibs]]).

- **Before endoscopy:** ACG 2016 recommends **against endoscopic evaluation in individuals with persisting symptoms (14–30 days) and a negative stool work-up** *(strong recommendation, very low level of evidence)* — the later Infectious Diseases Society of America (IDSA) guidance still allows duodenal aspirate in selected patients, as above. [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]], [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]

## Therapeutics

**Agent selection** (Table 6) [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]:

| Line | Agent | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **First choice** | **Tinidazole** | Approved in the United States for children aged **≥3 years**; available as tablets that can be crushed |
| **First choice** | **[[nitazoxanide\|Nitazoxanide]]** | Both first choices are based on data from HIV-uninfected children |
| **Alternative** | **Metronidazole** | High frequency of GI side effects; no commercial pediatric suspension (can be compounded from tablets) |

- [[acg-2016-acute-diarrhea]] independently names **metronidazole, tinidazole, or nitazoxanide** for *Giardia* infections, stating the evidence is **strong** for antimicrobial treatment of specific parasitic causes of acute diarrheal infection.
- Neither guideline states a dose, interval, or duration for any of the three agents.

> [!pitfall] Metronidazole is not approved for this indication
> Metronidazole is **not US Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved for the treatment of giardiasis** and is the alternative, not a first choice ([[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]).

### Prevention and public health

- **Giardiasis is nationally notifiable** — report to the state, territorial, or local health department *(strong, high)*. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]
- Symptomatic people should **avoid swimming, water-related activities, and sexual contact**, with meticulous hand hygiene *(strong, low)* — relevant to the recreational-water and sexual-contact routes above. [[idsa-2017-infectious-diarrhea]]

### Postinfectious sequelae

- *Giardia* is a named trigger of [[postinfectious-ibs|postinfection IBS]]. Relative risk (RR) of IBS by Rome III criteria after giardiasis **3.4 (95% confidence interval (CI) 2.9–3.9)**; 1-year IBS incidence **37.7 per 1,000 person-years vs 4.4 per 1,000 person-years** without previous *Giardia* infection. [[acg-2020-ibs]]
- Exposure to *Giardia* is also associated with the development of **food intolerances up to 3 years after infection**. [[acg-2020-ibs]]
