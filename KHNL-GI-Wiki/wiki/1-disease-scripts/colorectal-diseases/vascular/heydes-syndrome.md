## Assessment

- **Heyde's syndrome** = aortic stenosis + gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding from [[angioectasia]]. [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- Clinical association, not a test: known/newly found aortic stenosis **plus** bleeding angioectasia.
- Angioectasia is identified wherever it is looked for — at [[colonoscopy]], or on the [[small-bowel-bleeding|small bowel bleeding]] workup ([[capsule-endoscopy|video capsule endoscopy (VCE)]] → [[device-assisted-enteroscopy|double-balloon enteroscopy (DBE)]]).
- **No laboratory or echocardiographic cutoff defines the syndrome.** Neither guideline gives a von Willebrand factor (vWF) or multimer assay threshold, nor an aortic-stenosis severity grade; the diagnosis rests on the clinical association. [[asge-2014-lgib|American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2014]] states the hypothesis as **severe** aortic stenosis causing type 2 von Willebrand disease that precipitates bleeding from underlying angioectasia, without defining "severe".

### Severity Assessment

- Neither guideline grades the syndrome. Severity is judged by the bleeding itself: **ongoing bleeding** is the qualifier that moves the patient to valve replacement (below).
- **Cardiac valvular disease is itself a risk factor for recurrent bleeding** from small bowel angioectasia (full risk-factor list: [[angioectasia]]). [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]

### Mechanism

- [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2015]] calls the aortic-stenosis/[[angioectasia]] link a **controversial association**, thought to be secondary to an **acquired type 2 von Willebrand deficiency**. Two settings share that defect:

| Setting | Acquired defect | What the guideline reports |
|---|---|---|
| **Aortic stenosis** (Heyde's syndrome) | Acquired type 2 von Willebrand deficiency | Some patients have resolution of GI bleeding after **aortic valve replacement (AVR)** — the stated support for the association |
| **Left ventricular assist device (LVAD)** | Same acquired von Willebrand deficiency syndrome | At risk for angioectasia and recurrent bleeding; in pilot studies **decreased vWF levels predict recurrent bleeding** from small bowel angioectasia |

- Neither guideline describes the mechanism beyond the acquired von Willebrand defect.

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[small-bowel-bleeding]].*

- **Angioectasia without aortic stenosis** — same lesion, same endoscopic therapy, no valve to replace.
- **LVAD-associated angioectasia bleeding** — same acquired vWF mechanism, device rather than stenotic valve.
- Other small bowel sources of bleeding — see the schema above.

## Diagnostics

- Bleeding source localized by the standard small bowel bleeding algorithm; see [[small-bowel-bleeding]] for the sequence and diagnostic yields.
- **What the lesion looks like and where to look:** red flat lesions, **2 mm to several centimetres**, with ectatic vessels radiating from a central feeding vessel; **predominantly cecum and ascending colon**. Colonoscopy sensitivity for angioectasia is **80%**. ([[asge-2014-lgib]])

> [!pitfall] Narcotic sedation can hide the lesion
> Narcotics reduce mucosal blood flow and impair detection of angioectasia at colonoscopy — weigh that in [[endoscopy-sedation|sedation]] choice when the exam is a hunt for a vascular lesion ([[asge-2014-lgib]]).

## Therapeutics

- **AVR** for Heyde's syndrome with **ongoing bleeding**. *(Conditional recommendation, moderate level of evidence.)* [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]
  - Meta-analysis of two studies: reduced bleeding risk after AVR, pooled rebleeding event rate **0.16 (95% confidence interval [CI] 0.05–0.38)**.
  - "Ongoing bleeding" is the only qualifier the recommendation carries — no hemoglobin, transfusion or bleeding-episode threshold is given.
- Bleeding lesions themselves are treated as for [[angioectasia]] — [[argon-plasma-coagulation|argon plasma coagulation (APC)]] preferred, with **high rebleeding rates despite endoscopic treatment**, which is why definitive management may require AVR. ([[asge-2014-lgib]])
