## Assessment

- **Heyde's syndrome** = aortic stenosis + gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding from [[angioectasia]]. [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- Clinical association, not a test: known/newly found aortic stenosis **plus** bleeding angioectasia.
- Angioectasia is typically identified on the [[small-bowel-bleeding|small bowel bleeding]] workup ([[capsule-endoscopy|video capsule endoscopy (VCE)]] → [[device-assisted-enteroscopy|double-balloon enteroscopy (DBE)]]).

### Mechanism

- [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2015]] calls the aortic-stenosis/[[angioectasia]] link a **controversial association**, thought to be secondary to an **acquired type 2 von Willebrand deficiency**.
- Support for the relationship: some patients with aortic stenosis have resolution of GI bleeding after **aortic valve replacement**.
- **Left ventricular assist device (LVAD) patients are likewise at risk** for angioectasia and recurrent bleeding, again secondary to an acquired von Willebrand deficiency syndrome.
- Pilot studies: **decreased von Willebrand factor (vWF) levels predict recurrent bleeding** from small bowel angioectasia **in LVAD patients**.
- Aortic stenosis is itself listed as a risk factor for angioectasia, and cardiac valvular disease as a risk factor for recurrent bleeding from small bowel angioectasia.
- The upstream shear-stress/multimer mechanism is not described further in the guideline.

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[small-bowel-bleeding]].*

- **Angioectasia without aortic stenosis** — same lesion and same endoscopic therapy, but no valve to replace.
- **LVAD-associated angioectasia bleeding** — same acquired vWF mechanism in a patient with a device rather than valvular stenosis.
- Other small bowel sources of bleeding — see the schema above.

## Diagnostics

- Bleeding source localized by the standard small bowel bleeding algorithm; see [[small-bowel-bleeding]] for the sequence and diagnostic yields.
- **What the lesion looks like and where to look:** red flat lesions, **2 mm to several centimetres**, with ectatic vessels radiating from a central feeding vessel; **predominantly cecum and ascending colon**. [[colonoscopy]] sensitivity for angioectasia is **80%**. ([[asge-2014-lgib]])
- ⚠ **Narcotic sedation can hide the lesion** — it reduces mucosal blood flow, so angioectasias may not be seen. Factor this into [[endoscopy-sedation|sedation]] choice when the indication is a hunt for a vascular lesion. ([[asge-2014-lgib]])

Neither guideline specifies a von Willebrand factor or multimer assay threshold, or an echocardiographic aortic-stenosis severity cutoff, for making the diagnosis — it rests on the clinical association.

## Therapeutics

- **Aortic valve replacement (AVR)** for Heyde's syndrome with **ongoing bleeding**. *(Conditional recommendation, moderate level of evidence.)* [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]
  - Meta-analysis of two studies: reduced bleeding risk after AVR, pooled rebleeding event rate **0.16 (95% confidence interval [CI] 0.05–0.38)**.
- Bleeding lesions themselves are treated as for [[angioectasia]] — endoscopic therapy, with **high rebleeding rates despite endoscopic treatment**, which is why definitive management may require AVR. ([[asge-2014-lgib]]) AVR is the step specific to this syndrome.
