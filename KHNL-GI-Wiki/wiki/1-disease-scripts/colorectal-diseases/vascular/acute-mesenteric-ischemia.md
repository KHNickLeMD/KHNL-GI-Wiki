## Assessment

- Acute mesenteric ischemia (AMI) = ischemic insult to the **small intestine**; can be catastrophic (loss of most of the small bowel, or death) [[acg-2015-colon-ischemia]]
- Contrast with [[colon-ischemia|colon ischemia]] (CI): CI is usually nonocclusive, and vascular imaging is not indicated in most suspected CI; in AMI, conventional mesenteric angiography or computed tomography angiography (CTA) **plays an essential role** [[acg-2015-colon-ischemia]]

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Mesenteric venous thrombosis (MVT):** abdominal pain **disproportionate to physical findings** should raise suspicion (Key concept 9) [[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]]
  - Pain = most common presentation of acute MVT, with or without cirrhosis; also nausea, vomiting, fever, anorexia, jaundice
  - Chronic MVT may present with complications of portal hypertension: esophageal and/or gastric varices, variceal hemorrhage
- **CI that may herald AMI:** isolated right colon ischemia (IRCI), severe pain without bleeding, atrial fibrillation (Imaging Rec 5) [[acg-2015-colon-ischemia]]
- Confirm by imaging — [[#Imaging]]

### Severity Assessment

The decision point is **intestinal ischemia → infarction or gangrene** (drives resection; [[#Mesenteric Venous Thrombosis]]).

- In portal and/or MVT, suspect intestinal ischemia with (Key concept 10) [[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]]:
  - fever, ascites, rebound abdominal tenderness
  - leukocytosis, elevated serum lactate
  - text adds absence of bowel sounds and **increasing** lactate as signs of compromised intestinal circulation
- Computed tomography (CT) features raising suspicion for intestinal infarction or gangrene: thickened bowel wall and mesentery, indistinct bowel wall margins, ascites [[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]]

### Classification / Typing

| Type | What the sources say |
|---|---|
| **Arterial occlusion** (mesenteric arterial embolism, mesenteric arterial thrombosis) | Mostly cardiovascular and embolic causes; American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2020 does not discuss it [[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]] |
| **MVT** | About **10–20%** of all mesenteric vascular ischemic disorders; more often from extension of [[portal-vein-thrombosis\|portal vein thrombosis]] (PVT) into the mesenteric veins than independent of it [[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]] |

- MVT risk factors = those of PVT (ACG 2020 Table 3: thrombophilia, local injury to the portal or mesenteric veins, sluggish flow) — full list on [[portal-vein-thrombosis]]

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup of the hematochezia presentation: see [[acute-lower-gi-bleeding]].*

- [[colon-ischemia|Colon ischemia]] — the principal mimic; **IRCI** is the overlap case: the colonic episode may be the "heralding" sign of superior mesenteric artery (SMA) disease and impending AMI → multiphasic CTA in any suspected IRCI (Imaging Rec 3) [[acg-2015-colon-ischemia]]
- [[portal-vein-thrombosis|Portal vein thrombosis]] — MVT often results from its extension
- [[mesenteric-artery-aneurysm|Mesenteric artery aneurysm]]
- [[acute-colonic-pseudo-obstruction|Acute colonic pseudo-obstruction]], [[colonic-volvulus|colonic volvulus]]

## Diagnostics

### Imaging

| Test | When / role | Rec (grade) |
|---|---|---|
| **Multiphasic CTA** — intravenous contrast, no oral contrast, arterial + portal venous phases | Standard for AMI diagnosis. **Perform in any patient in whom AMI cannot be excluded**, and in suspected IRCI. Oral contrast adds minimal benefit to the two vascular phases [[acg-2015-colon-ischemia]] | Imaging Rec 3 (strong, moderate) |
| **Traditional splanchnic angiography** | CI that may herald AMI **and** multiphasic CT negative for vascular occlusive disease → consider [[acg-2015-colon-ischemia]] | Imaging Rec 5 (conditional, low) |
| **Contrast-enhanced CT** (venous) | About **90% accurate** for acute or chronic MVT; better with multidetector technique and thin slices. More accurate than ultrasound (US) for PVT extension into the mesenteric veins [[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]] | Rec 4 (strong, very low) |
| **Doppler US** | Initial noninvasive test for **PVT**; splanchnic vasculature not as well seen → contrast-enhanced CT or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) to assess **extension into the mesenteric veins** (and, in cirrhosis with new thrombus, to exclude tumor thrombus) [[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]] | Rec 4 (strong, very low) |
| **MRI / magnetic resonance angiography (MRA)** | Alternative to CT for the mesenteric venous system: less radiation. MRA limited by motion/flow artifacts, longer time, cost, overestimation of mesenteric vein stenosis or thrombosis, metallic devices or clips; 3-D gadolinium-enhanced MRA overcomes many [[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]] | — |

### CT Accuracy

| Study (as cited) | Target | Sensitivity | Specificity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meta-analysis, 3 prospective + 3 retrospective tertiary-centre studies | Primary vascular AMI (arterial embolism, arterial thrombosis, MVT) | 93.3% | 95.9% |
| Retrospective triple-phase CT: 39 pathologically confirmed AMI vs 41 suspected AMI with another diagnosis | SMA thrombus or occlusion | 26% | approached 100% |
| Same study | Celiac and inferior mesenteric artery (IMA) thrombus | 5% | 98% (estimated) |

*CT accuracy for vascular occlusion in AMI*
*Source: data from the AMI studies cited in ACG 2015 (no data exist on CT for vascular occlusion in CI)*
*[[acg-2015-colon-ischemia|ACG 2015 Colon Ischemia Guideline]]*
*AMI = acute mesenteric ischemia · MVT = mesenteric venous thrombosis · SMA = superior mesenteric artery · IMA = inferior mesenteric artery · CT = computed tomography · CI = colon ischemia*

> [!pitfall] A negative triple-phase CT does not exclude occlusion
> SMA sensitivity was only 26% in the one controlled study — when CI may herald AMI, consider splanchnic angiography (Imaging Rec 5) ([[acg-2015-colon-ischemia]]).

### Thrombophilia Testing

- **No cirrhosis:** test for thrombophilia in portal and/or MVT unless there is an obvious etiology such as an acute intraabdominal process (Key concept 7) [[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]]
- **Cirrhosis:** test only with (i) prior thrombosis, (ii) thrombosis at unusual sites such as hepatic veins, or (iii) family history of thrombosis (Key concept 7)
- **JAK2 mutation** testing for myeloproliferative neoplasm (MPN) — the most common thrombophilic cause in the absence of cirrhosis (Key concept 8)

## Therapeutics

### Mesenteric Venous Thrombosis

| Setting | Recommendation | Rec (grade) |
|---|---|---|
| **No cirrhosis**, acute symptomatic | **Anticoagulate all** absent contraindication — anticoagulation, not thrombolysis, first | Rec 5 (strong, low) |
| No cirrhosis — initial agent | Unfractionated heparin **or** low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH); weigh pros and cons | Rec 9 (conditional, very low) |
| No cirrhosis — maintenance | LMWH **or** warfarin. Limited experience with direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs); absorption may fall with intestinal edema → monitor: normal thrombin time and activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT) for dabigatran, normal prothrombin time or anti-Xa for apixaban/rivaroxaban rule out substantial drug effect | Rec 10 (conditional, very low) |
| No cirrhosis, no demonstrable thrombophilia, **reversible** etiology — duration | **At least 6 months** | Rec 7 (conditional, very low) |
| **Thrombophilia** — duration | **Indefinite** | Rec 7 (conditional, very low) |
| Chronic PVT, no cirrhosis | Anticoagulate if inherited or acquired thrombophilia, thrombus progression **into the mesenteric veins**, or current or previous **bowel ischemia** | Rec 6 (conditional, very low) |
| **Cirrhosis** with MVT or PVT extending into the mesenteric veins | **Anticoagulate**; weigh bleeding risk (e.g., platelets <50,000/µL, hepatic encephalopathy at risk of falls) | Rec 11 (strong, low) |
| Cirrhosis — initial agent | Unfractionated heparin or LMWH; unfractionated heparin preferred with renal insufficiency, LMWH with thrombocytopenia | Rec 15 (conditional, very low) |
| Cirrhosis, acute portal or MVT, no inherited thrombophilia — duration | **6 months**; continue beyond while on the liver transplant waiting list | Rec 13 (conditional, very low) |

- **Duration conflict within ACG 2020:** the text and the Figure 3 footnote give a finite **3–6 months** for a reversible etiology (acute intraabdominal process, trauma) or discrete precipitant; graded Rec 7 suggests **at least 6 months** [[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]]
- Benefits of anticoagulation in acute MVT: prevention of bowel ischemia, reduced hospitalization, improved survival; superior mesenteric vein patency rose from 42% to 73% in a prospective noncirrhotic acute PVT cohort [[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]]
- **Progressive thrombosis despite anticoagulation + risk of intestinal ischemia** → thrombolytic therapy may be considered (case series of 20: streptokinase, mostly transhepatic; 19 recanalized; bleeding in 6, none needing major intervention) [[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]]
- **Suspected or confirmed intestinal infarction or gangrene** → surgical resection; bowel viability judged at surgery to limit resection and prevent [[short-bowel-syndrome|short bowel syndrome]] [[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]]
- DOACs in cirrhosis: successful use established, but generally avoided in Child-Pugh class B and C [[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]]
- High-risk varices + portal and/or MVT needing anticoagulation → [[nonselective-beta-blockers|nonselective beta-blockers]]; endoscopic variceal ligation if contraindicated or not tolerated (Recs 8, 14; strong, low) — detail on [[portal-vein-thrombosis]] [[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]]

### Arterial Occlusion

- Neither ACG guideline makes a recommendation on revascularization, its timing, or bowel resection for arterial AMI
- ACG 2015 observation (no supporting data): in IRCI, a growing number of patients had occluding SMA thrombi at angiography, and stenting and/or thrombectomy gave good outcomes; some with SMA thrombi did well without either [[acg-2015-colon-ischemia]]
