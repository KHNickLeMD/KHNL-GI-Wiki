## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- Angioectasia (also called angiodysplasia) = acquired vascular ectasias of the GI mucosa and submucosa; the **most common cause of small bowel bleeding (SBB)** overall. [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]
- **Prevalence:** small-bowel **vascular lesions** were found in **24%** of patients with suspected SBB on both [[capsule-endoscopy|VCE]] (n=371) and [[device-assisted-enteroscopy|DBE]] (n=364) — 2008 meta-analysis, 10 studies. *(The guideline's denominator is "vascular lesions", a slightly broader category than angioectasia.)*
- **Age stratifies the differential:** angioectasia, other vascular lesions, and NSAID ulcers are the likely causes of SBB in patients **>40 years**. Angioectasia are more common in Western than Asian countries.

**Risk factors for angioectasia** (guideline summary statement 3):

- Advancing age
- Aortic stenosis ([[heydes-syndrome|Heyde's syndrome]] — acquired type 2 von Willebrand deficiency)
- Left ventricular assist devices (LVADs — same acquired von Willebrand mechanism)
- Chronic renal failure
- **Other hereditary disorders** — the guideline's own wording. [[hereditary-hemorrhagic-telangiectasia|HHT (Osler-Weber-Rendu)]] appears in ACG 2015 as a **cause of SBB** and as a physical-exam syndrome, **not** as a named angioectasia risk factor; do not cite it as one.

### Severity Assessment

**Risk factors for recurrent bleeding from small bowel angioectasia:**

- Number of vascular lesions
- Age >65 years
- Lesions in the jejunum
- Cardiac valvular disease
- Chronic renal disease
- [[anticoagulation-gi-bleeding|Anticoagulant use]]
- Need for transfusion

> **Read that list with the guideline's own caveat:** ACG notes there are **no prospective data that withdrawal of anticoagulation is beneficial** and **no data that cessation of antiplatelet therapy reduces recurrent bleeding**; in two cohorts anticoagulant use was *not* a predictor of rebleeding.

**Thresholds the guideline does and does not give:**

| Quantity | Threshold in ACG 2015 |
|---|---|
| "Significant ongoing anemia or active bleeding" (the trigger for endoscopic therapy) | **Not defined** — no Hb or transfusion cutoff is attached to the recommendation |
| **Refractory** bleeding (the somatostatin-analog population) | **>5 U of blood within 3 months** after conventional treatment |
| Predictor of positive angiography | Hemodynamic instability, particularly transfusion of **≥5 U** |
| Recurrent bleeding risk in aortic-stenosis / LVAD patients | Predicted by **decreased von Willebrand factor levels** — the acquired-vWF mechanism and its valve-replacement consequence live on [[heydes-syndrome]] |

## Diagnostics

- Identified at capsule endoscopy and deep (double-balloon) enteroscopy during the SBB workup; deep enteroscopy also enables concurrent treatment (below).
- **Yield of VCE depends on when it is done and on how the patient is bleeding** (overt vs occult, Hb threshold, days from the bleed) — those thresholds are the schema's, and live on [[small-bowel-bleeding]] rather than being repeated here.

## Therapeutics

### Whom to treat — the threshold decision

| Situation | Action | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Source found on [[capsule-endoscopy\|VCE]] and/or [[device-assisted-enteroscopy\|deep enteroscopy]] **with significant ongoing anemia or active bleeding** | Endoscopic therapy | Strong, low |
| **No source found** after appropriate small-bowel investigation | Conservative: oral or IV iron per severity/persistence of the [[iron-deficiency-anemia\|iron-deficiency anemia]]. **A small vascular lesion found on capsule endoscopy does not always need treatment.** | Strong, very low |
| Bleeding **persists with worsening anemia** | Repeat upper + lower endoscopy, VCE, deep enteroscopy, CT or MR enterography as appropriate | Strong, low |
| Bleeding **persists/recurs, or lesion cannot be localized** | Consider medical therapy — iron, somatostatin analogs, or antiangiogenic therapy | Strong, **moderate** |
| Any small-bowel hemorrhage | **Discontinue [[anticoagulation-gi-bleeding\|anticoagulation and/or antiplatelet therapy]] if possible** | Conditional, very low |
| [[heydes-syndrome\|Heyde's syndrome]] (aortic stenosis + angioectasia) with **ongoing bleeding** | **Aortic valve replacement** | Conditional, moderate |
| **Recurrence** of small bowel bleeding after prior therapy | **Endoscopic management can be considered**, depending on the clinical course and the response to prior therapy | Conditional, moderate |

### Endoscopic therapy

- **[[argon-plasma-coagulation|Argon plasma coagulation (APC)]]** has been the treatment of choice since 2001, delivered via deep enteroscopy.
- **Rebleeding is the rule, not the exception** — recurrence after treating vascular lesions ranges **20% to nearly 50%**. Meta-analysis pooled rebleeding **34% (95% CI 27–42%)** at a mean of **22 ± 13 months**, rising to **45%** when only the 341 small-bowel angioectasia patients are analyzed.
- Rebleeding rates **have not declined significantly** since deep enteroscopy was introduced in 2004. No published trial compares endoscopic therapy against sham, or selective vs. treat-all-lesions.
- **What "success" looks like in cohort data:** French DBE cohort — **129/133 (97%)** treated successfully with APC, yet **45/98 (46%)** rebled by 36 months. Two-center DBE series (n=274): at **12 months** 43% had no further overt bleeding, 23% recurrent overt bleeding, 35% ongoing iron/transfusion requirement; at **30 months** 59% / 24% / 18%.

### Medical therapy

| Agent | Regimen as studied | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **[[somatostatin-analogs\|Octreotide]]** | **100 µg SC three times daily × 1 month**; from week 2, **depot 20 mg monthly × 6 months** | Retrospective series (n=98, mean 78-mo follow-up): **40% complete responders, 32% partial, 26% non-responders**. Predictors of **poor** outcome: age >65, male sex, antiplatelet therapy, COPD, chronic renal failure |
| **Octreotide LAR / lanreotide** — for *refractory* bleeding as defined in the threshold table above | Depot **octreotide LAR IM monthly** or **lanreotide 90 mg monthly**, mean 12 months (range 6–36) | Transfusions fell **10 → 2 units** (*P*<0.001); bleeding episodes **73% → 20%** |
| **Thalidomide** (antiangiogenic) | **25 mg four times daily × 4 months** vs. iron 100 mg daily, ≥12-mo follow-up; entry required ≥6 bleeding episodes | ≥50% reduction in bleeding episodes in **20/28 (71%) vs. 1/27 (4%)** on iron (*P*<0.001). **Adverse events (fatigue, constipation, somnolence) in 73% vs. 34%** |
| **Thalidomide** — after **endoscopic** therapy fails | **200 mg daily × 4 months** | **9/12 (75%)** benefited; Hb **6.5 → 12.1 g/dL**; **3 withdrawals** for adverse effects |
| **Hormonal therapy** | — | **Not effective** — RCT vs. placebo showed a nonsignificant 7% difference after a mean 412 days; meta-analysis gives it no support |

- **[[heydes-syndrome|Heyde's syndrome]]:** aortic valve replacement is the disease-modifying step; the mechanism and the post-AVR rebleeding data live on that page.

### Surgery

- **Who:** reserve for **recurrent bleeds requiring multiple transfusions or hospitalizations after a comprehensive negative evaluation**, for massive bleeding, or when adhesiolysis is needed to complete deep enteroscopy.
- **Tattoo the lesion pre-surgically** to localize it *(strong, low)*, and have **intraoperative enteroscopy (IOE) available** at the time of the procedure *(conditional, low)*. Methylene blue injected through an angiographic catheter is an alternative intraoperative localization technique.
- **IOE is not a benign fallback:** diagnostic yield **58–88%**, rebleeding **up to 60%**, **mortality 17%**.
- Diffuse multiple angioectasias are still treated endoscopically at operation, so rebleeding rates are expected to be similar.
