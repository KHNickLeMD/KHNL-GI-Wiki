## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- Angioectasia (also called angiodysplasia) is the **most common cause of small bowel bleeding (SBB)** overall. [[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]
- **Prevalence:** small-bowel **vascular lesions** (a broader category than angioectasia) in **24%** on both [[capsule-endoscopy|video capsule endoscopy (VCE)]] (n=371) and [[device-assisted-enteroscopy|double-balloon enteroscopy (DBE)]] (n=364) — 2008 meta-analysis, 10 studies.
- **Age stratifies the differential:** angioectasia, other vascular lesions, and [[nsaid-enteropathy|nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) ulcers]] are the likely causes of SBB in patients **>40 years**. Angioectasia are more common in Western than Asian countries.

**Risk factors for angioectasia** (American College of Gastroenterology [ACG] 2015 summary statement 3):

- Advancing age
- Aortic stenosis ([[heydes-syndrome|Heyde's syndrome]] — acquired type 2 von Willebrand deficiency)
- Left ventricular assist devices (LVADs — acquired von Willebrand deficiency syndrome)
- Chronic renal failure
- Other hereditary disorders (not named in the statement). [[hereditary-hemorrhagic-telangiectasia|Hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia (HHT; Osler-Weber-Rendu)]] is listed separately as a rare cause of SBB that a skin exam can suggest.

### Severity Assessment

**Risk factors for recurrent bleeding from small bowel angioectasia:**

- Number of vascular lesions
- Age >65 years
- Lesions in the jejunum
- Cardiac valvular disease
- Chronic renal disease
- [[anticoagulation-gi-bleeding|Anticoagulant use]]
- Need for transfusion

> [!pitfall] Anticoagulant use is a weak rebleeding predictor
> No prospective data show that withdrawing anticoagulation helps, and no data show that stopping antiplatelet therapy reduces rebleeding; in two cohorts (2009, 2010) anticoagulant use did *not* predict rebleeding ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]).

**Thresholds the guideline does and does not give:**

| Quantity | Threshold in ACG 2015 |
|---|---|
| "Significant ongoing anemia or active bleeding" (the trigger for endoscopic therapy) | **Not defined** — no hemoglobin (Hb) or transfusion cutoff is attached to the recommendation |
| **Refractory** bleeding (definition used in the somatostatin-analog series) | **>5 U of blood within 3 months** after conventional treatment |
| Recurrent bleeding risk in **LVAD** patients | Predicted by **decreased von Willebrand factor (vWF) levels** (pilot studies). Aortic stenosis and valve replacement: [[heydes-syndrome]] |

## Diagnostics

- Identified at VCE and deep enteroscopy during the SBB workup; deep enteroscopy also enables concurrent treatment (below).
- **Angioectasias in the stomach and colon may be markers for small bowel angioectasia.**
- VCE yield depends on timing and bleeding pattern (overt vs occult, Hb, days from the bleed) — thresholds on [[small-bowel-bleeding]].

## Therapeutics

### Whom to treat — the threshold decision

| Situation | Action | Recommendation · grade |
|---|---|---|
| Source found on VCE and/or deep enteroscopy **with significant ongoing anemia or active bleeding** | Endoscopic therapy | 23 · strong, low |
| **No source found** after appropriate small-bowel investigation | Conservative: oral or intravenous (IV) iron per severity/persistence of the [[iron-deficiency-anemia\|iron-deficiency anemia]]. **A small vascular lesion found on capsule endoscopy does not always need treatment.** | 24 · strong, very low |
| Bleeding **persists with worsening anemia** (either situation above) | Repeat [[upper-endoscopy\|upper]] + [[colonoscopy\|lower]] endoscopy, VCE, deep enteroscopy, computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) enterography as appropriate | 25 · strong, low |
| Bleeding **persists/recurs, or lesion cannot be localized** | Consider medical therapy — iron, somatostatin analogs, or antiangiogenic therapy | 26 · strong, **moderate** |
| Any small-bowel hemorrhage | **Discontinue [[anticoagulation-gi-bleeding\|anticoagulation and/or antiplatelet therapy]] if possible** | 27 · conditional, very low |
| [[heydes-syndrome\|Heyde's syndrome]] (aortic stenosis + angioectasia) with **ongoing bleeding** | **Aortic valve replacement** | 30 · conditional, moderate |
| **Recurrence** of small bowel bleeding | **Endoscopic management can be considered**, depending on the clinical course and the response to prior therapy | 31 · conditional, moderate |

*Recommendation numbers follow ACG 2015 Table 1 (numbered 1–9 within the guideline's treatment section).*

- Pre-capsule data (stomach and colon angioectasias): non-bleeding lesions were not treated; actively bleeding ones were treated endoscopically.

### Endoscopic therapy

- **[[argon-plasma-coagulation|Argon plasma coagulation (APC)]]** has been primarily used as the treatment of choice since 2001 (replacing monopolar/bipolar electrocoagulation probes and laser).
- Recurrence after treating vascular lesions ranges **20% to nearly 50%**. Meta-analysis (14 studies, 623 subjects): pooled rebleeding **34% (95% confidence interval [CI] 27–42%)** at a mean of **22 ± 13 months**, rising to **45%** in the 341 small-bowel angioectasia patients.
- Rebleeding rates **have not declined significantly** since deep enteroscopy was introduced after 2004. No published trial compares endoscopic therapy against sham, or treating only actively bleeding or certain-size lesions vs all visualized lesions.

| Cohort · time point | No further bleeding | Recurrent overt bleeding | Ongoing iron/transfusion need |
|---|---|---|---|
| French tertiary center, APC via DBE (129/133 [97%] treated successfully) · 36 months | — | **45/98 (46%)** | — |
| Two-center DBE series (n=274) · 12 months (n=101) | 43% | 23% | 35% |
| Same series · mean 30 months (n=85) | 59% | 24% | 18% |

*Rebleeding after endoscopic therapy of small-bowel vascular lesions*
*Source: data from the two cohorts as reported in ACG 2015*

### Medical therapy

| Agent | Regimen as studied | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Iron** (oral or IV) ± packed red blood cell transfusion | Supportive care | Mainstay for **mild** bleeding — maintains Hb and, in more severe cases, reduces transfusion frequency. Transfusion is essential in more severe bleeding, particularly when mechanistic and medical methods fail |
| **[[somatostatin-analogs\|Octreotide]]** | **100 µg subcutaneous (SC) three times daily × 1 month**; from week 2, **depot 20 mg monthly × 6 months** | Retrospective series (n=98, mean 78-mo follow-up): **40% complete responders, 32% partial, 26% non-responders**. Predictors of **poor** outcome: age >65, male sex, antiplatelet therapy, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), chronic renal failure |
| **Octreotide long-acting release (LAR) / lanreotide** — for *refractory* bleeding as defined in the threshold table above | Depot **octreotide LAR intramuscular (IM) monthly** or **lanreotide 90 mg monthly**, mean 12 months (range 6–36) | Transfusion requirement **10 (6–24) → 2 (0–14)** (*P*<0.001); patients with a bleeding episode **73% → 20%** (*p*=0.001) |
| **Thalidomide** (antiangiogenic) | Open-label randomized controlled trial (RCT): **25 mg four times daily × 4 months** vs. iron 100 mg daily, ≥12-mo follow-up; entry required ≥6 bleeding episodes (positive immunoassay fecal occult blood test) | ≥50% reduction in bleeding episodes in **20/28 (71%) vs. 1/27 (4%)** on iron (*p*<0.001). **Adverse events (fatigue, constipation, somnolence) in 73% vs. 34%** |
| **Thalidomide** — after **endoscopic** therapy fails | **200 mg daily × 4 months** | **9/12 (75%)** benefited; mean Hb **6.5 → 12.1 g/dL**; **3 withdrawals** for adverse effects |
| **Hormonal therapy** | — | **Not effective** — RCT vs. placebo showed a nonsignificant 7% difference in rebleeding after a mean 412 days; meta-analysis gives it no support |

- Heyde's syndrome: aortic valve replacement (Recommendation 30, table above); mechanism and post-replacement rebleeding data on [[heydes-syndrome]].

### Surgery

- **Who:** generally a **last resort**, or for patients needing **lysis of adhesions** to complete deep enteroscopy. In **massive** small bowel bleeding, surgery may be useful (Recommendation 28).
- **Tattoo the lesion before surgery** to localize it (Recommendation 28 · strong, low), and have **intraoperative enteroscopy (IOE) available** at the procedure to localize and treat (Recommendation 29 · conditional, low).
- Angiographic option: catheter placed at angiography is left in place; at laparotomy, methylene blue injected through it highlights the segment to resect.
- Multiple (diffuse) angioectasias are usually treated endoscopically at operation — rebleeding expected to be similar to deep/push enteroscopy; no long-term follow-up data.
- IOE as a diagnostic test (yield, mortality, who qualifies): [[small-bowel-bleeding#Intraoperative Enteroscopy (IOE)]].
