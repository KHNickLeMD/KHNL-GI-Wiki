## Assessment

- Aneurysm or pseudoaneurysm of the splanchnic arterial circulation — the celiac, superior mesenteric, and inferior mesenteric arteries, all arising from the abdominal aorta, and their branches. [[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]]
- Seen in **10% of autopsies** but **infrequently cause problems**. Usually present **after the sixth decade**; **multiple in ~1/3** of patients.

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Most are asymptomatic**, detected incidentally on imaging. Too small to palpate, though **a bruit may be heard**.
- Typical symptoms when present: **abdominal pain**, **intra-abdominal and gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding**.
- **Computed tomography angiography (CTA)** characterizes the aneurysm and informs every management decision below.

### Severity Assessment

- **Rupture is the event that matters — mortality after rupture as high as 70%.**
- **Factors associated with rupture are uncertain**, and little is known about the natural history of these aneurysms; the guideline therefore asks that the uncertainty behind the management decision be discussed thoroughly with the patient.
- **Pseudoaneurysms have a higher risk of rupture than true aneurysms.**
- [[acute-pancreatitis|Acute pancreatitis]] → increased risk of **pseudoaneurysm** from local enzymatic vascular injury; **mortality 30–50%** when these rupture.

> [!pearl] Treat splenic artery aneurysms in women of childbearing age
> Risk of rupture in pregnancy may be low, but when rupture does occur maternal and fetal mortality is high — the stated reason for treating these irrespective of diameter ([[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]]).

### Classification / Typing

**Wall integrity** — the distinction that drives rupture risk and post-treatment imaging:

| | True aneurysm | Pseudoaneurysm ("false aneurysm") |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | Permanent, localized dilatation **>1.5× the expected diameter** of the artery | Localized **disruption of the intimal and medial layers**, caused by trauma |
| Wall | Involves **all 3 layers** of the vessel wall | Lined by **adventitia or perivascular tissue** |

**Shape:** *fusiform* = entire circumference; *saccular* = only a portion of the vessel wall.

**Site** — distribution among mesenteric artery aneurysms:

| Artery | Share | Sex distribution |
|---|---|---|
| **Splenic** | **~60%** — most common | More common in multiparous women |
| **Hepatic** | Next most common | Male preponderance |
| **Gastroduodenal** | Not given | Male preponderance |
| **Celiac** | Not given | Both sexes equally |
| **Superior mesenteric artery (SMA)** | Not given | Both sexes equally |

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: the incidental aneurysm is imaging-led — CTA, as above. Presenting as bleeding → see [[upper-gi-bleeding]] or [[acute-lower-gi-bleeding]]; the guideline says only "GI bleeding" and does not specify the tract.*

- Mesenteric vein thrombosis and [[portal-vein-thrombosis|portal vein thrombosis]] — the other mesenteric vascular disorders covered by the same guideline; venous rather than arterial.
- [[acute-mesenteric-ischemia|Acute mesenteric ischemia]] — overlapping presentation when an aneurysm thromboses or ruptures.
- [[hereditary-hemorrhagic-telangiectasia|Hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia]] — hepatic/visceral vascular malformations.

## Diagnostics

- **CTA** — site, true vs. pseudoaneurysm, diameter.
- **Surveillance of the observed (untreated) aneurysm:** schedule is in the treat-vs-observe table under [[#Therapeutics]].
- **After endovascular repair:** follow-up CTA considered at **3-yearly** intervals.
- **After embolization of a pseudoaneurysm:** **no follow-up imaging**.

## Therapeutics

**Treat vs. observe — location and patient factors first, then size.** [[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]]

| Scenario | Action |
|---|---|
| **Symptomatic** (abdominal pain in the absence of other causes) | **Treat** — irrespective of diameter or location *(Conditional, low)* |
| **Pseudoaneurysm associated with [[acute-pancreatitis\|acute pancreatitis]]** | **Treat** — irrespective of diameter or location |
| **True, asymptomatic, any diameter**, if in the **pancreaticoduodenal or gastroduodenal arcade**, an **intraparenchymal hepatic artery branch**, a **woman of childbearing age**, or a **[[liver-transplantation\|liver transplant]] recipient** | **Treat** — size does not matter *(Conditional, low)* |
| **Asymptomatic >2 cm** | **Treat all**, even when asymptomatic *(Strong, low)* |
| **Asymptomatic <2 cm and none of the above** | **Observe** — imaging at **6 mo**, **1 y**, then **every 1–2 y** *(Conditional, low)* |

- **Threshold wording:** the graded recommendation sets intervention at **>2 cm**; the guideline's supporting text says **"greater than 2–2.5 cm"** for intervention and **"smaller than 2–2.5 cm"** for the surveillance group.

**Repair modality** — a **minimally invasive approach is preferred in general**:

| Aneurysm | Repair |
|---|---|
| **Proper hepatic artery** | Open repair with arterial reconstruction has been recommended, but **endovascular stents** give excellent results with lower morbidity |
| **Not involving the proper hepatic artery** | **Coil embolization** may be considered |
