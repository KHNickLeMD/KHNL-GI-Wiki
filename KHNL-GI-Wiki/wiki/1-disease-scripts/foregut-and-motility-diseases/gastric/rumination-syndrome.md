# Rumination Syndrome

---

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

**Definition:** Rumination syndrome is a behavioral [[disorders-of-gut-brain-interaction|disorder of gut–brain interaction (DGBI)]] characterized by the **effortless, repetitive regurgitation of recently ingested food** back into the mouth, where it is then re-chewed and either re-swallowed or expelled. In Rome V it is classified among the **gastroduodenal disorders (B4)**. The behavior is an unconscious, learned, habitual contraction of the abdominal wall musculature — it is **not** a structural or primary motility disorder, and it is **not** an eating disorder (though it overlaps with and is also classified in psychiatric nosology).

**Cardinal features:**

- **Effortless regurgitation** of undigested, recognizable food — typically within **minutes (usually <15 min) of eating**, and recurring repeatedly through the postprandial period
- Regurgitated material is **bland or tastes like the food eaten** (not sour/bitter/acidic), because it has not yet mixed with gastric acid or undergone digestion
- **No retching, no nausea** preceding the event (key discriminator from vomiting)
- Re-chewing and re-swallowing (or spitting out) of the regurgitated bolus
- Often **stops once the bolus becomes acidic** (unpalatable), which is why it is predominantly an early-postprandial phenomenon

**Rome IV / V clinical criteria** (must include **both**, fulfilled for the last 3 months with symptom onset ≥6 months before diagnosis):

1. Persistent or recurrent regurgitation of recently ingested food into the mouth with subsequent spitting or remastication and swallowing
2. Regurgitation is **not preceded by retching**

**Supportive criteria:**

- Effortless regurgitation events are usually not preceded by nausea
- Regurgitant contains recognizable food with a pleasant taste
- The process tends to cease when the regurgitated material becomes acidic

> Diagnosis is fundamentally **clinical** (Rome criteria + compatible history) after an appropriate medical work-up. Objective testing (postprandial HRM-impedance) is confirmatory and reserved for atypical or refractory cases.

### Pathophysiology

The core mechanism is an **unintentional, learned voluntary increase in intra-abdominal/intragastric pressure** that overcomes a transiently relaxed lower esophageal sphincter (LES), reversing the normal gastro-esophageal pressure gradient and propelling gastric contents cranially:

- Subconscious, habitual contraction of the **intercostal and anterior abdominal wall muscles** → costal expansion + rise in intra-abdominal pressure
- Postprandial **intragastric pressure typically exceeds 25–30 mmHg** (vs. normally low intragastric pressure with high LES pressure)
- During an event there is a rapid gastric pressure spike **>30 mmHg (the manometric "R wave")** with concurrent LES relaxation and retrograde flow of gastric content on impedance
- The increase in abdominal pressure and onset of gastric strain **precedes** (≈58%) or is simultaneous with (≈37%) the onset of esophageal retrograde liquid flow
- Frequently associated with antecedent psychological stress, prior GI illness, or as a maladaptive habit; common in patients with anxiety, and historically described in children and the developmentally disabled, but **well recognized across all ages and cognitive levels**

## Differential Diagnosis

| Condition | Distinguishing features |
|---|---|
| **GERD / refractory reflux** | Regurgitant is **acidic/sour**, often delayed and nocturnal; heartburn predominates. See [[gerd]]. Rumination is effortless, early-postprandial, bland, never nocturnal during sleep. |
| **Gastroparesis** | Vomiting of **partially digested** food **hours** after meals, **preceded by nausea**, with delayed gastric emptying. See [[gastroparesis]]. |
| **Vomiting (incl. cyclic vomiting, [[disorders-of-gut-brain-interaction|CVS]])** | True emesis is **forceful**, **preceded by nausea/retching**, with autonomic features. Rumination has **no retching/nausea**. |
| **Supragastric belching / belching–regurgitation** | Manometric pattern can be **identical** to rumination; **impedance distinguishes gas (belch) vs. liquid/food (rumination)**. Belching air is swallowed then expelled before reaching the stomach. |
| **Achalasia / esophageal outflow obstruction** | Regurgitation of **retained undigested food**, dysphagia, weight loss; abnormal LES relaxation on manometry. See [[achalasia]]. |
| **Bulimia nervosa / eating disorders** | **Self-induced**, purposeful vomiting tied to body image and compensatory behavior; rumination is effortless and habitual, not purgative. Screen for comorbidity. |
| **Zenker's / esophageal diverticulum** | Regurgitation of older retained food, halitosis, gurgling, dysphagia. |

## Diagnostics

Rumination syndrome is a **clinical diagnosis**; testing is used to confirm in uncertain/refractory cases and to exclude mimics.

### Postprandial High-Resolution Esophageal Manometry with Impedance (HRIM) — Confirmatory Gold Standard

- **Protocol:** baseline manometry followed by a **test meal** (often the patient's typical symptomatic meal) with **continued recording for ~30–60 minutes postprandially** ([[acg-2020-esophageal-physiologic-testing]])
- **Diagnostic finding:** episodes of **abrupt rise in intragastric pressure ≥25–30 mmHg (the "R wave")** preceding or coinciding with retrograde esophageal flow of liquid/food on impedance, in the absence of preceding retching
- **Impedance is essential** to distinguish rumination (retrograde **liquid/food**) from supragastric belching (retrograde **gas**), since the pressure tracings alone overlap
- **Subtypes** described on HRIM: primary rumination (gastric pressure event initiates flow), secondary rumination (triggered by reflux), and rumination associated with supragastric belching
- **Limitations:** requires expertise; lack of fully standardized protocols; provocation of the behavior in the lab can be inconsistent

### Adjunctive / Exclusionary Testing (atypical presentations)

- **Upper endoscopy** ([[upper-endoscopy]]) — exclude mechanical/mucosal disease, retained food, stricture, malignancy
- **Gastric emptying scintigraphy** — only if gastroparesis is suspected (atypical, delayed, or nausea-predominant symptoms)
- **Ambulatory reflux monitoring** ([[ambulatory-reflux-monitoring]] / [[reflux-testing]]) — if GERD is the leading alternative; postprandial impedance can also capture rumination events
- These tests are **not required** for diagnosis when the history is classic

## Therapeutics

### 1. Behavioral Therapy — First-Line for All Patients

**Diaphragmatic (abdominal) breathing** is the cornerstone and first-line treatment in **all cases**:

- Teaches the patient to **relax the abdominal wall** and engage slow diaphragmatic breathing, which is **physically incompatible** with the abdominal-wall contraction that drives regurgitation
- Practiced especially **during and after meals**, when events occur
- Can be delivered with or without **biofeedback** (EMG-guided control of abdominothoracic muscle activity), which improves learning and outcomes
- **Highly effective**, durable, and free of pharmacologic risk — should be offered to every patient
- Best delivered by a **GI psychologist, behavioral therapist, or speech-language pathologist** familiar with the technique

**Other behavioral options** (effective alternatives/adjuncts):

- **Cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT)** — comparable efficacy to diaphragmatic breathing in trials
- **Biofeedback-guided** abdominothoracic muscle control
- **Hypnotherapy / gut-directed hypnosis**
- Address comorbid **anxiety, stress, and any eating-disorder pathology**

### 2. Pharmacologic Therapy — For Refractory Cases

**Baclofen (first-line drug):**

- **Dose:** 10 mg three times daily (GABA-B agonist)
- **Mechanism:** increases **basal LES pressure** and reduces transient LES relaxations, raising the pressure barrier against retrograde flow; also reduces rumination event frequency
- **Evidence:** in a **randomized, placebo-controlled crossover study**, baclofen 10 mg TID significantly reduced regurgitation events vs. placebo
- **Side effects:** drowsiness, dizziness, fatigue, confusion — limit tolerability; taper to discontinue
- Reserved for patients who **fail or cannot access** behavioral therapy, or as an adjunct

**Other agents (limited/anecdotal evidence):**

- Treatment of any contributing **GERD** (PPI) may help when reflux triggers secondary rumination
- Neuromodulators / addressing psychiatric comorbidity as adjuncts
- **Levosulpiride** and other prokinetics have weak supporting data

### 3. Patient Education

- Explaining the **benign, behavioral nature** of the condition and the mechanism is itself therapeutic and improves adherence to breathing exercises
- Reassurance reduces the unnecessary diagnostic odyssey, repeated testing, and exposure to acid-suppression/antiemetics that these patients frequently accumulate before correct diagnosis

## Clinical Pearls

- **"Effortless, bland, early, no nausea/retching"** — the four words that separate rumination from vomiting and from GERD. Regurgitant tastes like the meal because it hasn't met acid yet.
- **Rumination stops when the food turns sour** — the postprandial, self-limiting time course (cessation once contents acidify) is a strong historical clue.
- **Diagnosis is clinical** — a classic history meets Rome criteria; you do not need manometry to diagnose, but **postprandial HRM-impedance** confirms atypical cases and distinguishes supragastric belching (impedance shows gas vs. liquid).
- **Diaphragmatic breathing is first-line for everyone** and is often curative; **baclofen 10 mg TID** is the go-to drug for refractory disease.
- **Frequently misdiagnosed** as refractory GERD or gastroparesis for years — recognizing it spares patients escalating PPIs, antiemetics, and even fundoplication.
- **Screen for eating disorders and anxiety** — overlap is common, and untreated psychiatric comorbidity undermines behavioral therapy.
