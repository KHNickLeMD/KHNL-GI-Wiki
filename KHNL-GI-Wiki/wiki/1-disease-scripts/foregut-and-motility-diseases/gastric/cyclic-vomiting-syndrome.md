Cyclic vomiting syndrome (CVS) is a chronic [[disorders-of-gut-brain-interaction|disorder of gut–brain interaction (DGBI)]] of **stereotyped, acute episodes of vomiting and retching separated by episode-free periods**; strong migraine association. Common (~2% US prevalence, more common in women) but markedly **under-diagnosed and under-treated** — most patients endure years of diagnostic delay and futile testing; about half visit the emergency department (ED) at least annually and 1 in 3 become disabled ([[aga-2024-cvs]]). [[cannabinoid-hyperemesis-syndrome|Cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome (CHS)]] is closely related — see [[#Differential Diagnosis]].

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

**Rome V criteria (B2b)** ([[rome-v-2026-gastroduodenal-disorders]]) — must include **all**:

> 1. **Stereotypical episodes of repetitive vomiting** regarding onset (acute) and duration (**up to 10 days**)
> 2. **At least 3 discrete episodes in the prior year and 2 episodes in the past 6 months**, occurring **at least 1 week apart**
> 3. **Milder symptoms including nausea and isolated vomiting can be present between cycles**
>
> *Supportive remark:* history or family history of **migraine** headaches can be present.

**What changed from Rome IV** — the version both 2024 American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) Clinical Practice Updates (CPUs) reproduce ([[aga-2024-cvs]], [[aga-2024-chs]]):

| Element | Rome IV (as printed in AGA 2024 CVS CPU, Box 1) | Rome V 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Episode duration | **<7 days** | **Up to 10 days** |
| Between episodes | **Absence of vomiting**; milder symptoms (nausea, occasional vomiting, dyspepsia) may be present | Milder symptoms **including isolated vomiting** can be present |
| Frequency | ≥3 discrete episodes in a year, 2 in the prior 6 months, separated by ≥1 week of baseline health | ≥3 in the prior year, 2 in the past 6 months, ≥1 week apart |

- Rome V is the Rome Foundation's current revision of its own criteria. Nearly **15%** of patients have episodes lasting longer than 7 days — beyond the Rome IV cutoff ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).
- Diagnosis is **clinical** — consider CVS in any adult presenting with episodic bouts of repetitive vomiting ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).
- **Abdominal pain is present in most episodes and should not preclude a CVS diagnosis** ([[aga-2024-cvs]]); nearly two-thirds report abdominal pain and diarrhea during the emetic phase ([[rome-v-2026-gastroduodenal-disorders]]).

### Severity Assessment

Severity (American Neurogastroenterology and Motility Society–Cyclic Vomiting Syndrome Association [ANMS-CVSA] criteria, AGA Box 1) determines whether prophylaxis is offered — **this is the key management decision** ([[aga-2024-cvs]]):

| Stratum | Criteria | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Mild** | <4 episodes/yr, each lasting <2 days, **without** ED visits or hospitalizations | **Abortive therapy only** |
| **Moderate–severe** | ≥4 episodes/yr, each lasting >2 days, **and** requiring ≥1 ED visit or hospitalization | **Prophylactic + abortive therapy** |
| **Coalescent CVS** | Severe CVS with a worsening course over years — longer, more frequent episodes, progressively few or no asymptomatic days; may culminate in daily nausea and vomiting and meet Rome IV chronic nausea vomiting syndrome criteria. **Distinguished by years of prior episodic nausea and vomiting on careful history** | Prophylaxis akin to moderate–severe |

- AGA's prophylaxis section words the threshold as **>4** episodes/yr; Box 1 and the severity text say **≥4** ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).

### Phases

Four phases, each with its own treatment goal ([[aga-2024-cvs]]):

| Phase | Features | Goal | Management |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Well (inter-episodic)** | Weeks to months. Absent or minimal symptoms — **30%** may have [[dyspepsia]] or nausea ([[aga-2024-chs]]); Rome V: **up to 63%** have milder nausea, dyspepsia and intermittent vomiting ([[rome-v-2026-gastroduodenal-disorders]]) | Prevent future episodes | Identify and avoid triggers · good sleep routine · stress reduction · prophylactic medication (moderate–severe) |
| **Prodrome** | ~**65%** of patients, median **1 h** ([[aga-2024-cvs]]); nausea, abdominal pain, fatigue, weakness, stereotypical in timing; sense of impending doom, panic | Halt transition to emetic phase | Abortive medication — intranasal sumatriptan, ondansetron, antihistamines |
| **Emetic** | Uncontrollable retching and vomiting for **hours to days** ([[aga-2024-cvs]]); Rome V: starts within minutes to hours, lasts **3–6 days**, vomiting **>8 times hourly** on average ([[rome-v-2026-gastroduodenal-disorders]]) | Terminate episode | Continue abortive regimen · may need ED for intravenous (IV) therapy · hydration and medications |
| **Recovery** | ~**1–2 days** ([[aga-2024-cvs]]); Rome V: **up to 1 week**, with persisting nausea, lightheadedness and meal intolerance in some ([[rome-v-2026-gastroduodenal-disorders]]) | Return to well phase | Rehydration/nutrition · rest |

> [!pearl] Abort in the prodrome
> The prodrome is the ideal time for abortive therapy — earlier treatment means a higher chance of aborting the episode. Rehearse with the patient the steps they will take ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).

### Clinical Clues and Comorbidities

- Episodes can occur at any time of day, but **most start in the early morning hours** ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).
- Each patient has a stereotyped cluster of prodromal/emetic symptoms — e.g. fatigue, feeling hot or cold, mental fog, restlessness, anxiety, headache, bowel urgency, diarrhea or constipation, diaphoresis, flushing, tremulousness ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).
- **Triggers** (migraine-like): stress in **70%–80%** (positive *and* negative events), sleep deprivation, menstrual cycle, travel, motion sickness, acute infection or surgery; less often prolonged fasting or intense exercise ([[aga-2024-cvs]]). Rome V adds food triggers (cheese, cured meats, chocolate, monosodium glutamate) and flares that begin before menstruation (catamenial CVS) ([[rome-v-2026-gastroduodenal-disorders]]).
- **Hot-water bathing/showering** gives relief in ~**48%** of CVS patients who do not use cannabis → **not pathognomonic of CHS**; also seen in some chronic nausea vomiting syndrome. Prolonged exposure can cause burns ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).
- Drinking large volumes of water or self-induced vomiting is a self-soothing pattern — not malingering ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).
- **Comorbidities** (support the diagnosis, guide management): mood disorders — anxiety, depression, panic — in **50%–60%**, migraine **20%–30%**, seizure disorder ~**3%**, [[postural-orthostatic-tachycardia-syndrome|postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome (POTS)]]/autonomic imbalance. Treating anxiety may reduce episode frequency and inter-episodic nausea; treating POTS may improve overall functional status ([[aga-2024-cvs]]). Rome V: **78%** screen positive for depression and **84%** for anxiety ([[rome-v-2026-gastroduodenal-disorders]]).
- Acute complications: fluid loss, electrolyte abnormalities, hematemesis; **dental enamel damage** with repeated attacks ([[rome-v-2026-gastroduodenal-disorders]]).

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[nausea-and-vomiting]].*

- [[cannabinoid-hyperemesis-syndrome]] — a **separate adult entity in Rome V (B2c)** ([[rome-v-2026-dgbi]]), framed by the 2024 CPUs as a CVS subtype (see that page for how the sources differ). Proposed when **prolonged (>1 yr) and heavy (>4×/wk, often daily)** cannabis use **precedes** symptom onset; in CVS, cannabis use is more occasional and often **postdates** the onset of episodic vomiting. Retrospective CHS diagnosis requires cannabis cessation for **6 months or ≥3 typical cycle lengths**; vomiting that continues after this cessation period rules CHS out ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).
- **Outpatient, once structural disease is excluded** ([[aga-2024-chs]]): chronic nausea vomiting syndrome · [[gastroparesis]] · [[rumination-syndrome]] · [[nausea-and-vomiting-of-pregnancy|pregnancy]] · migraine
- [[abdominal-migraine]] — the Rome V D2 entity the two overlap with. **The discriminator: in abdominal migraine the pain, not the vomiting, is dominant, and the vomiting is not repetitive.** Pain is not a CVS criterion, but severe abdominal pain is common during vomiting episodes; where episodic pain and severe vomiting coexist the two overlap, and **the predominant symptom directs the primary diagnosis and the treatment approach** ([[rome-v-2026-centrally-mediated-gi-pain]]).
- **Metabolic/endocrine mimics:** [[acute-hepatic-porphyria]], Addison's disease, hypothyroidism ([[aga-2024-cvs]])
- **Life-threatening mimics (ED):** acute abdomen, bowel obstruction, [[acute-mesenteric-ischemia|mesenteric ischemia]], [[acute-pancreatitis|pancreatitis]], myocardial infarction ([[aga-2024-chs]])

> [!pearl] Treat whether or not cannabis is involved
> Offer abortive and/or prophylactic therapy to every patient, including those with ongoing cannabis use and an unsettled CVS-vs-CHS diagnosis — these treatments still work for many ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).

## Diagnostics

- **Clinical diagnosis** (Rome V criteria above); no confirmatory test. Testing serves only to exclude other causes, with negligible yield ([[rome-v-2026-gastroduodenal-disorders]]).
- **Basic workup for uninvestigated episodic vomiting:** complete blood count (CBC), serum electrolytes and glucose, liver function tests, lipase, urinalysis ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).
- **One-time [[upper-endoscopy|esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD)]] or upper gastrointestinal (GI) imaging** to exclude obstructive lesions; **do not repeat** EGD or upper GI imaging ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).
- **Do not routinely order gastric emptying scans** — few CVS patients have delayed emptying, results obtained during an episode are uninterpretable, and cannabis or opiate use complicates interpretation ([[aga-2024-cvs]]). Rome V: rapid gastric emptying in **40%–80%** between episodes; delayed emptying described in **27%** ([[rome-v-2026-gastroduodenal-disorders]]).
- Further testing only as history dictates — Addison's disease, hypothyroidism, [[acute-hepatic-porphyria]]. **Brain imaging + neurology referral** for any localizing neurologic symptoms ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).
- **In the ED,** exclude the life-threatening mimics above first ([[aga-2024-chs]]).

> [!pitfall] Post-retching findings on EGD are not the cause
> EGD soon after an episode may show mild gastritis, erythematous streaking, a Mallory-Weiss tear or esophagitis — epiphenomena of recent retching, not causal ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).

## Therapeutics

> Evidence base for all CVS pharmacotherapy is **case series / expert opinion** — no randomized, placebo-controlled trials ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).

### Lifestyle (all patients)

- Regular sleep, avoid prolonged fasting, stress management; identify and mitigate triggers during the inter-episodic phase ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).
- Involve referral specialists (neurology, psychiatry, sleep) and allied services (psychology/counseling, substance use) for comorbid anxiety, depression, migraine, sleep and substance-use disorders ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).
- [[brain-gut-behavioral-therapy|Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT)]] or mindfulness meditation can substantially improve CVS symptoms and quality of life ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).

### Prophylactic Therapy (moderate–severe)

Goal: lengthen the inter-episodic phase and/or reduce the length and severity of the emetic phase. Choice is individualized ([[aga-2024-cvs]], Table 1).

| Line | Agent | Dosing | Key considerations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First-line** (strongly recommended) | **Tricyclic antidepressant (TCA)** — amitriptyline, nortriptyline, doxepin | Start **25 mg at bedtime** → goal **75–150 mg or 1–1.5 mg/kg at bedtime**; titrate by 10–25 mg every 2 wk | Amitriptyline more anticholinergic/antihistaminergic than nortriptyline; adverse effects include prolonged corrected QT interval (QTc) on electrocardiogram (ECG) |
| Second-line | Topiramate | Start 25 mg daily → 100–150 mg daily in divided doses; titrate by 25 mg weekly | Kidney stones — do not use with a stone history; **must not be used during pregnancy**; monitor electrolytes and renal function twice annually; some weight loss |
| Second-line | Zonisamide | Start 100 mg daily → 200–400 mg daily; titrate by 100 mg every 2 wk | Kidney stones; monitor electrolytes and renal function twice annually; weight loss |
| Second-line | Levetiracetam | Start 500 mg twice daily → 1000–2000 mg daily in divided doses; titrate by 500 mg every 2 wk | No therapeutic drug monitoring needed; monitor CBC |
| Second-line | Aprepitant (neurokinin-1 [NK-1] receptor antagonist) | 125 mg 2–3×/wk (adults >60 kg); 80 mg 2–3×/wk (40–60 kg) | **Safer in pregnancy**; may interfere with oral contraceptive pills; neutropenia; off-label in CVS — expensive, insurance coverage hard to obtain |
| Nutritional supplements (no line assigned) | Coenzyme Q10 · riboflavin | 300–400 mg daily · 200 mg twice daily | Monitor liver enzymes |

- **Response rates reported by Rome V** ([[rome-v-2026-gastroduodenal-disorders]]): TCA improvement in **69%** — poorer with migraine, psychological illness, or cannabis or opioid use; topiramate reduced attacks and health care use in **65%**; TCA-refractory patients had **50% fewer attacks** on zonisamide or levetiracetam; mitochondrial supplements (coenzyme Q10, L-carnitine, riboflavin) show some prophylactic efficacy.

### Abortive Therapy (all severities)

- Highest abort probability when taken **as early in the prodrome as possible**. Some respond to monotherapy, but **nearly all need combinations of ≥2 agents** — most often sumatriptan + an antiemetic such as ondansetron ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).
- A short prodrome may make abortive treatment infeasible; once emesis starts, **nonoral** treatments are most likely to help ([[rome-v-2026-gastroduodenal-disorders]]).

| Class | Agent | Dose | Cautions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Triptan | Sumatriptan | **20 mg intranasal** (head-forward position — usable even while retching) or **6 mg subcutaneous**; repeat once after 2 h if needed; **max 2 doses/24 h** | Contraindicated in ischemic heart disease, stroke, peripheral vascular disease, uncontrolled hypertension; not in pregnancy |
| Antiemetic | Ondansetron | **8 mg sublingual (SL) every 4–6 h** during episode | Baseline ECG advised — prolonged QTc; constipation with frequent doses |
| Antiemetic | Promethazine | **12.5–25 mg by mouth or per rectum (PR) every 4–6 h** during episode | Peripheral IV administration can cause tissue injury (gangrene, thrombophlebitis); its sedation can help abort |
| Antiemetic | Prochlorperazine | **5–10 mg every 6–8 h**, or **25 mg suppository every 12 h** | Caution with leukopenia/neutropenia, dementia, glaucoma, seizure disorder; can cause leukopenia/neutropenia; rare neuroleptic malignant syndrome |
| Sedative | Alprazolam or lorazepam | **0.5–2 mg every 4–6 h** (alprazolam also SL or PR) | Caution in pregnancy and with a history of substance abuse |
| Sedative | Diphenhydramine | **12.5–25 mg every 4–6 h** during episode | Caution in older adults, glaucoma, benign prostatic hypertrophy, ischemic heart disease, hypertension |

- Inducing sedation is often an effective abortive strategy — the "abortive cocktail" ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).
- Rome V: NK-1 antagonists (aprepitant, fosaprepitant) reduce emesis frequency and hospitalizations in refractory patients; intranasal or injectable triptans may reduce vomiting and abdominal pain, especially with a family history of migraine; ondansetron is widely used without CVS-specific trials ([[rome-v-2026-gastroduodenal-disorders]]).

### ED / Recovery Management

- Episode not aborted at home → ED for IV fluids and IV abortive therapy is reasonable; patients with shorter attacks (<24 h) tend to stay home ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).
- Treat every patient with uncontrolled retching and vomiting **regardless of suspicion for CHS** ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).
- **All** patients: **IV dextrose-containing fluids**; most also benefit from **IV antiemetics** ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).
- **IV ketorolac = first-line non-narcotic analgesic**; narcotics only in the most severe refractory episodes ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).
- Sedation is a treatment goal in itself — quiet, darker room + IV benzodiazepines. Sedating antipsychotics (droperidol, haloperidol) also work, typically reserved for the ED ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).
- **Recovery phase:** electrolyte-rich fluids (sports drinks) or nutrient drinks; patients may still feel nauseated or dyspeptic but generally tolerate moderate volumes of liquid ([[aga-2024-cvs]]).
