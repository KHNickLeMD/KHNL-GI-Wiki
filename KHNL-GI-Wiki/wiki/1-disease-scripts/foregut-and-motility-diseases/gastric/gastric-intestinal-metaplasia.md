## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

**Gastric intestinal metaplasia (GIM)** = replacement of normal gastric epithelium by intestinal-type epithelium (goblet cells, absorptive cells, Paneth cells) at any location in the stomach. GIM is a premalignant lesion in the [[gastric-premalignant-conditions|Correa cascade]]: [[helicobacter-pylori-infection|H. pylori]] gastritis → [[atrophic-gastritis|atrophic gastritis (AG)]] → GIM → dysplasia → [[gastric-adenocarcinoma|gastric adenocarcinoma]].

- **Diagnosis is histologic** — goblet-cell metaplasia on gastric biopsies taken by the updated Sydney protocol (see [[#Biopsy Protocol]]). Endoscopic appearance alone may be inaccurate and should be confirmed with a standardized biopsy protocol ([[acg-2025-gastric-premalignant]]).
- **High-quality endoscopy is the prerequisite** (American College of Gastroenterology [ACG] 2025, Strong/Low) — the five components of an adequate gastric examination are listed on [[gastric-premalignant-conditions#Endoscopic Evaluation (Required)|gastric premalignant conditions]]; a 2–3 minute upper endoscopy falls short of the standard of care in these patients.
- **Usually incidental** on [[upper-endoscopy|esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD)]] done for another indication. Pooled prevalence among 897,371 people undergoing gastric biopsy was **4.8% (95% confidence interval [CI] 4.8%–4.9%)** — a yield among biopsied patients, not a population estimate ([[aga-2020-gastric-intestinal-metaplasia]]).

**Baseline risk of progression** — the numbers both guidelines stratify around ([[aga-2020-gastric-intestinal-metaplasia]]):

| Outcome in patients with GIM | Pooled estimate |
|---|---|
| Incident gastric cancer (GC) at 3 y | 0.4% (95% CI 0.1%–0.8%), 4 studies |
| Incident GC at 5 y | 1.1% (95% CI 1.0%–1.2%), 7 studies |
| Incident GC at 10 y | 1.6% (95% CI 1.5%–1.7%), 4 studies |
| Annual rate of progression | 0.16% per year |
| GC within 1 y of GIM diagnosis (proxy for missed prevalent cancer) | 0.5% (95% CI 0.4%–0.6%), 4 cohort studies |

The American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2020 panel pre-specified that it would support surveillance at an annual progression rate **above 0.5%–1%**; the observed 0.16% per year sits below that bar, which is why it suggests against routine surveillance. ACG 2025 cites the same 10-year figure of 1.6% and stratifies instead by risk factors.

### Severity Assessment

**Every GIM diagnosis is described on four axes, reported per compartment** (antrum, incisura, corpus) — ACG 2025 Recommendations 7 (subtype, Conditional/Low) and 8 (extent and severity, Conditional/Very low):

| Axis | Categories |
|---|---|
| **Histologic severity** — proportion of glands involved in an individual biopsy | **mild** = up to 1/3 · **moderate** = up to 2/3 · **severe** = >2/3 |
| **Anatomic extent** | **limited** = antrum and/or incisura only · **extensive** = corpus involvement in addition |
| **Focality** | **unifocal** = 1 biopsy specimen containing GIM · **multifocal** = ≥2 biopsies containing GIM |
| **Histologic subtype** | **complete** = small-intestinal phenotype · **incomplete** = at least partial colonic phenotype · **mixed** = both present |

- Increased severity predicts progression **independently of anatomic extent** ([[acg-2025-gastric-premalignant]]).
- Complete vs incomplete vs mixed is readable on routine hematoxylin and eosin sections; the older Felipe subtyping (type I complete; types II and III incomplete) needs mucin histochemistry and is reserved for research.
- Subtype is rarely reported in the United States (US), and in one US population-based study **66%** of GIM had the anatomic location recorded as "not otherwise specified".

> [!pitfall] Antrum-limited is not automatically low risk
> Moderate-to-severe AG/GIM, even when anatomically limited to the antrum, is still considered high-risk and such individuals should be considered for surveillance ([[acg-2025-gastric-premalignant]]).

**Risk magnitudes within a GIM population** ([[aga-2020-gastric-intestinal-metaplasia]]):

| Factor | Relative risk (RR) for incident GC | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Incomplete vs complete subtype | **3.33 (95% CI 1.96–5.64)** | 7 studies, low quality; none from the US |
| First-degree relative with GC | **4.53 (95% CI 1.33–15.46)** | 3 studies, very low |
| Extensive vs limited GIM | **2.07 (95% CI 0.97–4.42)** | 2 studies, very low |

ACG 2025 frames the same picture as a **2.0- to 20-fold** higher risk of progression for GIM with additional risk factors — corpus-extended AG/GIM, incomplete-type GIM, moderate-to-severe AG/GIM (i.e. OLGA/OLGIM III–IV), and family history of GC in a first-degree relative.

**Operative Link on Gastritis Assessment (OLGA)/Operative Link on Gastric Intestinal Metaplasia Assessment (OLGIM) staging** — stages 0–IV from the combination of severity (0–3) in antrum/incisura and corpus; not routinely used in the US. Staging criteria, risk bands and the autoimmune gastritis (AIG) caveat live on [[atrophic-gastritis#Severity Assessment|atrophic gastritis]]; surveillance intervals by stage are tabled on [[gastric-premalignant-conditions#Surveillance Strategy|gastric premalignant conditions]].

### Classification / Typing

| Feature | Lower risk | Higher risk |
|---------|-----------|------------|
| Histologic severity | Mild (**up to 1/3** of glands) | Moderate (**up to 2/3**) or severe (**>2/3**) — qualifies **independently of extent** |
| Anatomic extent | Limited to antrum/incisura | Corpus extension (extensive) |
| Focality | Unifocal (**1** biopsy with GIM) | Multifocal (**≥2** biopsies with GIM) |
| Histologic subtype | Complete only | Incomplete or mixed |
| OLGA/OLGIM stage | 0–I | III–IV; **II is intermediate**, individual risk assessment |
| Family history | None | GC in a first-degree relative |
| Demography | None of the adjacent | Foreign-born from a high-incidence nation; East Asian, Latino/a, Black, American Indian and Alaska Native (AIAN) — **but see the demography disagreement under [[#2. Surveillance Endoscopy]]** |

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[dyspepsia]].*

| Condition | Key Features |
|-----------|-------------|
| [[atrophic-gastritis\|Atrophic gastritis]] (without GIM) | Gastric gland atrophy on biopsy; precedes GIM in the Correa cascade; test for H. pylori |
| Reactive (chemical) gastropathy | Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID)/bile reflux injury; foveolar hyperplasia, smooth muscle proliferation; no goblet cells |
| [[atrophic-gastritis\|Autoimmune gastritis]] | Corpus-predominant atrophy with antral sparing unless concomitant H. pylori atrophic gastritis; anti-parietal cell antibodies |
| [[gastric-premalignant-conditions#Dysplasia Management\|Gastric dysplasia]] | Architectural and cytologic atypia; endoscopic resection when a visible lesion is present; not just metaplasia |
| [[gastric-adenocarcinoma\|Gastric adenocarcinoma]] | Early GC may look benign; ulcerated lesions often mean invasive disease — any nodularity, ulceration or irregularity → targeted biopsies |

## Diagnostics

### Endoscopic Appearance

On high-definition white-light endoscopy (HDWLE), GIM is **irregular, patchy, white mucosa with a tubulovillous pattern**, better appreciated with narrow-band imaging (NBI). AGA 2021 describes the same areas as **mildly nodular** on HDWLE.

| Image-enhanced endoscopy (IEE) sign | Sensitivity | Specificity |
|---|---|---|
| Tubulovillous pattern (irregular patchy white mucosa) | 89% | 90% |
| Light blue crest — fine light blue-white lines on the crests of the epithelial surface | 48%–89% | 93%–96% |
| Marginal turbid band | 100% | 66% |
| White opaque substance — nodular patches of white raised mucosa (lipid droplets) | *not given* | *not given* |

- Interobserver reliability of these signs is only moderate; agreement improves with experience ([[acg-2025-gastric-premalignant]]).
- ACG 2025 **suggests HDWLE plus IEE** (NBI, blue laser imaging) for gastric examination (Conditional/Low). NBI has inadequate illumination for a wide-field view in the stomach, so it is **less useful for detecting** gastric lesions than for **characterizing** them; near focus and optical zoom help but are not mandatory.
- Light blue crest and white opaque field are best seen on magnifying NBI ([[aga-2021-atrophic-gastritis]]).

*See Figure 3 in [[acg-2025-gastric-premalignant]] and Figure 2 in [[aga-2021-atrophic-gastritis]] for endoscopic example images.*

### Biopsy Protocol

- **Updated Sydney protocol** — systematic biopsies of **5 sites**: greater and lesser curvature of the **antrum**, greater and lesser curvature of the **corpus**, and the **incisura angularis**; typically **1–2 biopsies per site**.
- **Containers (ACG 2025 Rec 6, Conditional/Low):** at minimum 2 — antrum/incisura in one, corpus in the other; targeted biopsies of any other mucosal abnormality in additional separate containers.
- Biopsies may be *directed* within a Sydney zone when the appearance suggests disease; *targeted* biopsies are those taken from a visible lesion or abnormality.
- The **incisura**, a transition zone, is often the first site to show AG or GIM in H. pylori gastritis — sampling it raises detection.
- **Pathology report should state** (ACG 2025 key concept, ungraded): stomach location, severity of AG/GIM per biopsy, anatomic extent, GIM subtype, grade of dysplasia if present, and presence/absence of H. pylori organisms.
- AGA 2020 names US practice as a barrier: biopsies submitted without the total number specified and without jars labelled by location defeat extent-based risk stratification.

### H. pylori Testing

- **Test every patient with GIM and eradicate if positive** — AGA 2020 Recommendation 1 (Strong/Moderate) and ACG 2025 Recommendation 16 (Strong/Moderate).
- Use a **nonserologic** test, and confirm eradication after treatment; test choice, timing and regimens live on [[helicobacter-pylori-infection]] (AGA 2020 puts the testing method outside its scope).
- Ideally confirm eradication **at least 1–2 months before** a surveillance endoscopy — active infection alters both the endoscopic and the histologic appearance ([[acg-2025-gastric-premalignant]]).

### Serum Markers

- **Serum pepsinogens (PG)** — **PG I <70 µg/L *and* PG I:II ratio <3.0** have high sensitivity/specificity for **severe corpus atrophy** (data mostly from high-incidence East Asian regions); **not available for routine clinical use in the US** ([[aga-2021-atrophic-gastritis]]).
- ACG 2025 **suggests against** noninvasive biomarkers (serum pepsinogens, anti-H. pylori immunoglobulin G [IgG]) for gastric premalignant condition (GPMC) or GC screening or surveillance in the US (Conditional/Very low).
- Anti-parietal cell antibodies (most sensitive for AIG) and intrinsic factor antibodies (low sensitivity, high specificity) are for suspected autoimmune gastritis — see [[atrophic-gastritis]].

## Therapeutics

### 1. H. pylori Eradication (All GIM Patients)

**Strong recommendation, Moderate evidence** — AGA 2020 Rec 1, ACG 2025 Rec 16.

- Eradication in individuals **with or without GIM** → incident GC **RR 0.68 (95% CI 0.48–0.96)**, a 32% reduction; GC mortality **RR 0.67 (95% CI 0.38–1.17)**.
- Restricted to **confirmed GIM**, the effect is directionally similar but not significant — **RR 0.76 (95% CI 0.36–1.61)**; mortality data in confirmed GIM were insufficient. This is why the evidence is graded moderate rather than high.
- Confirm eradication (failure rates with current therapies are high); regimens: [[helicobacter-pylori-infection]], usually optimized [[bismuth-quadruple-therapy|bismuth quadruple therapy]].
- **Does established GIM regress?** Unsettled. Non-US cohorts show improvement or regression particularly after eradication in patients with **less severe baseline histology**; other data show persistence or progression. AGA 2020 reports the conflicting evidence and takes no position.

> [!pearl] Eradication is an adjunct, not a substitute for surveillance
> In high-risk GPMC, H. pylori eradication alone is not sufficient to prevent progression — it does not remove the indication for surveillance ([[acg-2025-gastric-premalignant]]).

### 2. Surveillance Endoscopy

| Guideline | Position |
|-----------|---------------|
| **ACG 2025 GPMC** | Surveillance **every 3 years for high-risk GIM** (Rec 9) and for **severe** GIM or AG in antrum or corpus (Rec 10); **against** surveillance for low-risk GIM or mild AG (Rec 11). All three Conditional/Very low |
| **AGA 2020 GIM** | **Against routine surveillance** (Conditional/Very low); shared decision-making for higher-risk patients, and if surveillance is elected, repeat EGD in **3–5 years** with careful mucosal visualization and biopsies of antrum and body plus any concerning lesion. Also **against routine short-interval (<1 y) repeat endoscopy** for risk stratification (Conditional/Very low) — reasonable by shared decision-making where there are high-risk stigmata (e.g. nodularity), concern about completeness of the baseline exam, or overall increased GC risk. Separates **GIM-specific** risk (incomplete type, extensive, family history) from **overall** GC risk (racial/ethnic minorities, immigrants from high-incidence regions); does not use OLGA/OLGIM |
| **AGA 2021 Atrophic Gastritis** | Interval not well defined — individual risk plus shared decision-making; **every 3 years should be considered for advanced AG**, defined by anatomic extent and histologic grade (risk highest at OLGA/OLGIM III/IV). AIG: individualized |
| **European Society of Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ESGE) 2019** *(as summarized in [[aga-2020-gastric-intestinal-metaplasia]])* | **Every 3 years** for severe gastric atrophy or GIM in **both antrum and body** and/or OLGA/OLGIM III–IV (strong, low quality). GIM at a **single location plus** family history of GC, incomplete GIM, or persistent H. pylori gastritis: 3 years may be considered (weak, low). Advanced AG **plus** family history: **1–2 years** may benefit (weak, low). GIM at a single location alone does **not** justify surveillance in most cases (strong, moderate) |
| **AGA 2026 [[gastric-polyps\|Gastric Polyps]]** | In patients with gastric polyps: targeted biopsies **every 3 years** are considered for persistent H. pylori infection, advanced AG when **incomplete** GIM is detected, or **mild/focal GIM *with* family history of GC**. Surveillance frequency should **not** be altered for ethnicity or family history alone |

> [!pitfall] Demography alone — the guidelines disagree
> [[acg-2025-gastric-premalignant|ACG 2025]] makes family history of GC, foreign birth in a high-GC country, or high-risk race/ethnicity **each independently sufficient** to make otherwise-limited complete-type GIM high-risk (every 3 years). [[aga-2026-gastric-polyps|AGA 2026]] says surveillance frequency should **not** be altered on ethnicity or family history alone, as there is little evidence the association is mediated by anything other than gastric mucosal status.
> They converge once demography is **paired with a mucosal finding** — AGA 2026 itself endorses 3-yearly biopsies for mild/focal GIM **plus** family history. For the isolated-demography case the wiki follows the newer AGA 2026: limited, complete-type, mild GIM with no mucosal risk factor is not upgraded by ethnicity or family history by itself. Population-level stratification for *screening* is a separate decision — [[gastric-cancer-screening]].

**ACG 2025 risk definitions:**

- **High-risk GIM → surveillance every 3 years (Rec 9):** GIM **plus at least one** of — incomplete subtype (vs complete) · corpus extension (corpus involvement together with antrum or incisura) · **any** GIM histology with family history of GC in a first-degree relative, foreign birth with emigration from a high-incidence nation, or high-risk race/ethnicity (East Asian, Latino/a, Black, AIAN).
- **Severe GIM or AG** in antrum or corpus biopsies → surveillance every 3 years (Rec 10). The guideline's commentary extends this to **moderate-to-severe** disease, antrum-limited included.
- **Low-risk GIM or atrophy → against surveillance (Rec 11):** complete-type GIM without any incomplete component · focal extent confined to the antrum · none of the Rec 9 criteria · AG mild in severity.
- **Multiple risk factors** → shorter than 3 years; the guideline's worked example is extensive GIM **plus** family history of GC → a **1–2-year** interval.
- **Against routine repeat endoscopy within 12 months** for nondysplastic GPMC done for risk stratification, **unless** there are concerns about endoscopy quality or adherence to the Sydney protocol (key concept, ungraded). Up to **30%** of patients called low-risk on an index exam without systematic biopsies are upstaged on a repeat exam at ~1–2 years in non-US studies; there are no US data, so the approach is individualized.

**Practical bands:**

- **All patients:** [[test-and-treat|test and treat]] H. pylori and confirm cure.
- **Low-risk GIM** — complete-type, focal, antrum-confined, mild, no other risk factor: no routine surveillance.
- **High-risk by mucosal findings** — incomplete or mixed subtype, corpus extension, moderate-to-severe severity even if antrum-limited, OLGA/OLGIM III–IV, persistent H. pylori: surveillance EGD every 3 years with Sydney protocol biopsies, by shared decision-making.
- **Mucosal finding *plus* demography** — e.g. mild/focal GIM with family history of GC: every 3 years, shortening toward **1–2 years** when several risk factors stack. This is the band where ACG 2025 and AGA 2026 agree.

The ACG 2025 management algorithm for nondysplastic GPMC is redrawn on [[gastric-premalignant-conditions#Surveillance Strategy|gastric premalignant conditions]], together with the stage-by-stage OLGA/OLGIM intervals.

### 3. If Dysplasia Is Found

Dysplasia is the next step of the Correa cascade and is managed off this page. The ACG 2025 dysplasia pathway — resection when margins are visible, the repeat-EGD intervals when they are not (indefinite for dysplasia [IND]/low-grade dysplasia [LGD] 12 months, high-grade dysplasia [HGD] 3 months), referral to a high-volume centre, and post-resection surveillance — lives with its algorithm on [[gastric-premalignant-conditions#Dysplasia Management|gastric premalignant conditions]].

### 4. Chemoprevention

- ACG 2025 **does not suggest** aspirin, NSAIDs, cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) inhibitors, or antioxidants for GC chemoprevention in patients with GPMC (Conditional/Very low).
- Apart from H. pylori eradication, no chemopreventive agent is recommended: prospective trials with GC incidence and mortality as primary endpoints are lacking.
