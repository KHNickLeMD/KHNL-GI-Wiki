## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Gastric antral vascular ectasia (GAVE) is an endoscopic diagnosis** made in the gastric antrum. It is an **uncommon but often difficult-to-manage cause of chronic gastrointestinal (GI) blood loss** ([[aga-2024-ida-management]]).
- **Associated chronic conditions — not only liver disease:**
  - [[cirrhosis]]
  - **Chronic kidney disease**
  - **Systemic sclerosis**
- ⚠ **GAVE must be distinguished from [[portal-hypertensive-gastropathy|portal hypertensive gastropathy (PHG)]] — the treatments differ** (Baveno VI/VII, retained as still valid in Baveno VIII). PHG is diffuse, portal-pressure-driven, and managed by lowering portal pressure; GAVE is antral and managed endoscopically.
- **GAVE scores within the PHG severity system.** In the Baveno III classification its presence scores 2 and its absence 1 — the full table is on [[portal-hypertensive-gastropathy]].
- ⚠ **Concomitant GAVE in a patient with PHG confers a higher risk of hemorrhage** ([[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]]).

### Severity Assessment

**No ingested source grades GAVE endoscopically.** Severity is instead operationalized by the transfusion and iron response, and the two guideline triggers for escalating to endoscopic therapy are not the same:

| Document | Trigger for endoscopic therapy |
|---|---|
| Baveno VIII 5.43 | **Requiring serial transfusion** |
| [[aga-2024-ida-management]] best practice advice (BPA) 12 | **Inadequate response to iron replacement** |

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[upper-gi-bleeding]].*

- [[portal-hypertensive-gastropathy|PHG]] — the key distinction, because the treatment diverges (see above).
- [[angioectasia|Angioectasia]] elsewhere in the GI tract — same lesion type, different site and different management route.
- [[portal-hypertension|Portal hypertension]]–related gastric polyps — antral, hyperplastic, biopsy discouraged; detail on [[portal-hypertensive-gastropathy]].
- [[variceal-upper-gi-bleeding|Gastric varices]].
- *[[helicobacter-pylori-infection|Helicobacter pylori]]* gastritis.

## Diagnostics

- **[[upper-endoscopy|Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD)]] is the diagnostic test** — GAVE is recognized by its antral appearance at endoscopy; no ingested source offers a non-endoscopic surrogate.
- **[[iron-deficiency-anemia|Iron-deficiency anemia (IDA)]] is usually how it presents** — chronic occult blood loss rather than overt hemorrhage. Ferritin and transferrin-saturation cutoffs are on that page.
- No ingested source specifies a surveillance-endoscopy interval after GAVE is treated.

## Therapeutics

### Iron Repletion — Every Patient

- **All patients with IDA and GAVE should receive iron repletion**, oral or intravenous (IV) **depending on the severity of iron depletion and tolerance** ([[aga-2024-ida-management]]).
- Formulation choice, dosing interval, and the response targets that define "inadequate response": [[iron-deficiency-anemia]].
- ⚠ The 15 BPA statements in that update are **unrated** — no systematic review was performed.

### When to Add Endoscopic Therapy

- **IDA secondary to GAVE with an inadequate response to iron replacement → consider endoscopic therapy** with **endoscopic band ligation (EBL)** or a thermal method such as **[[argon-plasma-coagulation|argon plasma coagulation (APC)]]** (BPA 12).
- **GAVE requiring serial transfusion → endoscopic therapy, and specifically ligation** (Baveno VIII 5.43, below).
- Endoscopic therapy with EBL or a thermal method (APC or [[radiofrequency-ablation|radiofrequency ablation (RFA)]]) **decreases blood loss and reduces red-blood-cell transfusion requirements**.

### Band Ligation over Argon Plasma Coagulation

> **Baveno VIII 5.43** — In patients with GAVE **requiring serial transfusion, endoscopic variceal ligation (EVL) is recommended over argon plasma coagulation**, as it is associated with higher eradication rates and fewer treatment sessions, with reductions in recurrent bleeding, hospitalisations, and transfusion requirements. *(LoE 1; SoR: strong; modified; Agreement: 90%)*

**Why ligation wins — the head-to-head numbers** ([[aga-2024-ida-management]]; observational studies and randomized controlled trials [RCTs] of thermal therapy vs EBL):

| Outcome | Result | Favors |
|---|---|---|
| Endoscopic success | High for **both** modalities | — |
| Transfusion requirement | Difference in mean transfusions **−2.30 units** | EBL |
| Hemoglobin improvement | **+0.59 g/dL** (95% confidence interval 0.17–1.00) | EBL |
| Sessions to obliteration | **2.63 vs 3.83** | EBL |
| Safety | Favorable for both | — |

- ⚠ **This is a change in direction, not just emphasis.** **Baveno VII 6.25** had listed APC, RFA and band ligation as interchangeable local options for PHG and GAVE alike *(C.2)*. **Baveno VIII 5.43 makes ligation the recommendation and APC the comparator it beats.** Baveno VIII governs — same tier, newer publication date.

### What Is Not Established

- **There are no proven pharmacologic therapies for GAVE** ([[aga-2024-ida-management]]). Unlike PHG, portal-pressure reduction is not the answer — [[nonselective-beta-blockers|NSBB]] are not recommended for GAVE in any ingested source, and this is precisely why the PHG/GAVE distinction matters at endoscopy.
- A small case series documented some response of GAVE- and PHG-related **acute** bleeding to **hemostatic spray** ([[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]]).
- No ingested source gives a number of bands, a power setting, or a session interval for GAVE treatment; technique detail that does exist sits on [[argon-plasma-coagulation]] and [[endoscopic-hemostasis]].
