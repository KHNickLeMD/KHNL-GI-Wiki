## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Gastric antral vascular ectasia (GAVE) is an endoscopic diagnosis** made in the gastric antrum — an **uncommon but often difficult-to-manage cause of chronic gastrointestinal (GI) blood loss** ([[aga-2024-ida-management]]).
- **Associated chronic conditions — not only liver disease:** [[cirrhosis]], chronic kidney disease, systemic sclerosis.
- The antral appearance is taken as recognized at endoscopy: neither [[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension|Baveno VIII]] nor the [[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension|AASLD 2023 guidance]] describes the pattern, prints example images, or defines diagnostic criteria beyond the lesion's antral location.
- **GAVE scores within the severity system for [[portal-hypertensive-gastropathy|portal hypertensive gastropathy (PHG)]].** In the Baveno III classification its presence scores 2 and its absence 1 — the full table is on that page.
- ⚠ **Concomitant GAVE in a patient with PHG confers a higher risk of hemorrhage** ([[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]]).

### Severity Assessment

Neither document grades GAVE endoscopically. Severity is operationalized by transfusion need and by the response to iron — and the two guideline triggers for escalating to endoscopic therapy are not the same:

| Document | Trigger for endoscopic therapy | What the document leaves open |
|---|---|---|
| [[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension\|Baveno VIII]] 5.43 | **Requiring serial transfusion** | How many transfusions, over what interval, count as "serial" |
| [[aga-2024-ida-management\|AGA 2024]] best practice advice (BPA) 12 | **Inadequate response to iron replacement** | What counts as inadequate in GAVE specifically |

- AGA 2024's stated expectation on **adherent oral iron** is a **hemoglobin (Hgb) rise of 1 g/dL within 2 weeks** and a **rise in ferritin within a month**. Failing those is its trigger to switch oral → **intravenous (IV)** iron; it is not in itself an indication for endoscopic therapy.

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[upper-gi-bleeding]].*

> [!pitfall] GAVE is not portal hypertensive gastropathy
> Lowering portal pressure treats PHG, not GAVE: PHG is managed with [[nonselective-beta-blockers|nonselective beta-blockers (NSBB)]] and, when refractory, [[tips|transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS)]], while GAVE has no proven pharmacologic therapy and is managed endoscopically ([[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension]] 5.43 and 5.44; [[aga-2024-ida-management]]).
> The two must be distinguished at the index endoscopy "because treatments are different" — [[baveno-vii-2022-portal-hypertension|Baveno VII]] 7.12 *(B.1)*, carried forward among the prior statements that remain valid in Baveno VIII.

### Distinguishing From Portal Hypertensive Gastropathy

| Feature | GAVE | PHG |
|---|---|---|
| Location and appearance | Gastric **antrum** | Diffuse mucosal lesions — **"snakeskin" mosaic pattern** with variable intraepithelial hemorrhage |
| Relation to portal pressure | Also occurs with chronic kidney disease and systemic sclerosis | [[portal-hypertension\|Portal hypertension]] is necessary but not sufficient; development usually requires clinically significant portal hypertension |
| First move for chronic blood loss | Iron repletion, then endoscopic therapy if the trigger above is met | **Lower portal pressure — NSBB** |
| Endoscopic therapy | **The definitive treatment** — [[endoscopic-band-ligation\|endoscopic variceal ligation (EVL)]] preferred over [[argon-plasma-coagulation\|argon plasma coagulation (APC)]] | Limited benefit — it does not lower portal pressure; reserved for focal bleeding lesions or salvage |
| Rescue when transfusion-dependent | Not addressed | **TIPS — the most effective rescue therapy** |
| Iron route | Oral or IV by severity of iron depletion and tolerance | **Oral first** (no malabsorptive defect); IV if ongoing bleeding without response to oral |

*Rows from [[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension]] 5.43–5.44, [[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]], and [[aga-2024-ida-management]] BPA 10 and 12. PHG grading, prevalence and acute-bleeding management are on [[portal-hypertensive-gastropathy]].*

Also consider:

- [[angioectasia|Angioectasias]] elsewhere in the GI tract — small-bowel lesions are reached by deep enteroscopy with a distal attachment and treated with APC or hemostatic clips ([[aga-2024-ida-management]] BPA 14).
- [[gastric-polyps|Portal hypertension–related gastric polyps]] — antral, predominantly hyperplastic, routine biopsy discouraged; detail on [[portal-hypertensive-gastropathy]].
- [[variceal-upper-gi-bleeding|Gastric varices]].

## Diagnostics

- **[[upper-endoscopy|Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD)]] is the diagnostic test** — GAVE is recognized by its antral appearance at endoscopy.
- **[[iron-deficiency-anemia|Iron-deficiency anemia (IDA)]] is usually how it presents** — chronic occult blood loss rather than overt hemorrhage. Ferritin and transferrin-saturation cutoffs are on that page.
- Baveno VIII and the AGA update give no interval for repeat endoscopy once GAVE has been treated.

## Therapeutics

### Iron Repletion

- **All patients with IDA and GAVE should receive iron repletion**, oral or IV **depending on the severity of iron depletion and tolerance** ([[aga-2024-ida-management]]).
- Formulation choice, dosing interval, and the response targets above: [[iron-deficiency-anemia]].
- ⚠ The 15 BPA statements in that update are **unrated** — no systematic review was performed.

### Endoscopic Therapy

- **Both modalities work:** endoscopic band ligation (EBL) or a thermal method — APC or [[radiofrequency-ablation|radiofrequency ablation (RFA)]] — **decreases blood loss and reduces red-blood-cell transfusion requirements** ([[aga-2024-ida-management]]); BPA 12 offers EBL **or** a thermal method such as APC.
- **Baveno VIII picks a winner:**

> **5.43** — In patients with GAVE **requiring serial transfusion, EVL is recommended over argon plasma coagulation**, as it is associated with higher eradication rates and fewer treatment sessions, with reductions in recurrent bleeding, hospitalisations, and transfusion requirements.

*(Level of evidence [LoE] 1; strength of recommendation: strong; modified from Baveno VII; 90% faculty agreement.)*

**Why ligation wins — the head-to-head numbers** (observational studies and randomized controlled trials [RCTs] of endoscopic thermal therapy vs EBL, [[aga-2024-ida-management]]):

| Outcome | Result | Favors |
|---|---|---|
| Endoscopic success | High for **both** modalities | — |
| Transfusion requirement | Difference in mean transfusions **−2.30 units** | EBL |
| Hemoglobin improvement | **+0.59 g/dL** (95% confidence interval 0.17–1.00) | EBL |
| Sessions to obliteration | **2.63 vs 3.83** (means) | EBL |
| Safety | Favorable for both | — |

- ⚠ **This is a change in direction, not just emphasis.** **Baveno VII 6.25** had listed APC, RFA and band ligation as interchangeable local options for PHG and GAVE alike *(C.2)*. **Baveno VIII 5.43 makes ligation the recommendation and APC the comparator it beats** — same tier, newer publication date, so Baveno VIII governs.
- Neither document gives a number of bands, a power or flow setting, or an interval between sessions for GAVE; general device settings sit on [[argon-plasma-coagulation]], [[endoscopic-band-ligation]] and [[endoscopic-hemostasis]].

### Therapies Without Established Benefit

- **There are no proven pharmacologic therapies for GAVE** ([[aga-2024-ida-management]]).
- A small case series documented some response of GAVE- and PHG-related **acute** bleeding to **hemostatic spray** ([[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]]).
