## Assessment

- **Gastric mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) lymphoma = extranodal marginal zone B-cell lymphoma** — a low-grade B-cell lymphoma arising in MALT of the stomach; also occurs in lung, small bowel, and other organs. [[asge-2015-gastric-premalignant]]
- **Closely linked to chronic [[helicobacter-pylori-infection|H. pylori]] gastritis** — nearly all patients with gastric MALT lymphoma have H. pylori infection; chronic H. pylori inflammation may trigger the B-cell clonal expansion. [[acg-2024-hp-treatment]], [[asge-2015-gastric-premalignant]]

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Presenting symptoms:** [[dyspepsia]], weight loss, or [[upper-gi-bleeding|gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding]]. [[asge-2015-gastric-premalignant]]
- **Endoscopic findings range from subtle erosions to nodular masses.** [[asge-2015-gastric-premalignant]]
- **Diagnosis is confirmed with mucosal sampling.** [[asge-2015-gastric-premalignant]]

### Severity Assessment

- **[[endoscopic-ultrasound|Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]] may be used for prognostic information** — accurate assessment of **the degree of lymphoma infiltration in the gastric wall** and **regional lymph node involvement**. [[asge-2015-gastric-premalignant]]
- Neither the American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2024 nor the American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2015 document gives a staging system for gastric MALT lymphoma or the criteria that define "early stage."

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[dyspepsia]].*

- **[[gastric-adenocarcinoma]]** — may present with the same symptoms (dyspepsia, weight loss, GI bleeding); **pathologically distinct**. [[asge-2015-gastric-premalignant]]
- **[[peptic-ulcer-disease]]** — a principal sequela of H. pylori infection (MALT lymphoma is listed as an additional association). [[acg-2024-hp-treatment]]

## Diagnostics

| Test | Role |
|---|---|
| **[[upper-endoscopy\|Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD)]] with mucosal sampling** | **Confirms the diagnosis** [[asge-2015-gastric-premalignant]] |
| **[[helicobacter-pylori-infection\|H. pylori]] testing** | Marginal zone B-cell lymphoma, MALT type, is a listed group to [[test-and-treat\|test and treat]] (Table 4) [[acg-2024-hp-treatment]] |
| **[[endoscopic-ultrasound\|EUS]]** | **Prognostic** — see [[#Severity Assessment]] [[asge-2015-gastric-premalignant]] |
| **Post-treatment H. pylori test of cure** | All treated patients should have one (Key concept 4). When upper endoscopy is needed shortly after treatment (e.g., gastric MALT lymphoma), **histology and/or a biopsy urease test** can also be used [[acg-2024-hp-treatment]] |

- Test-of-cure timing, drug holds, the bar on serology, and the caveat that a negative biopsy urease test alone does not prove eradication apply unchanged — see [[helicobacter-pylori-infection#Post-Treatment Confirmation|H. pylori infection]].

## Therapeutics

- **[[helicobacter-pylori-infection|H. pylori]] eradication = primary treatment for early-stage gastric MALT lymphoma.** Clinical practice guidelines recommend it **despite the lack of high-quality randomized controlled trials (RCTs)** — strong potential for benefit and negligible risk, especially compared with other treatments for MALT lymphoma. [[acg-2024-hp-treatment]]
- **ASGE 2015: treatment of choice for low-grade MALT lymphoma.** [[asge-2015-gastric-premalignant]]

| Outcome of eradication | Result |
|---|---|
| **Complete remission** after successful eradication — early-stage disease with H. pylori infection (meta-analysis of 61 uncontrolled, single-arm, observational reports [46 prospective, 15 retrospective]; 2,936 patients) | **75.2% (95% confidence interval [CI] 70.5–79.9)** [[acg-2024-hp-treatment]] |
| **Clinical remission**, low-grade disease | **Up to 80%** [[asge-2015-gastric-premalignant]] |

- Regimen choice and retreatment of persistent infection follow the general pathway — see [[helicobacter-pylori-infection]] and [[bismuth-quadruple-therapy]].
- **Without a test of cure, persistent infection leaves the patient at risk** of peptic ulcer, gastric adenocarcinoma, and gastric MALT lymphoma. [[acg-2024-hp-treatment]]
- Neither ACG 2024 nor ASGE 2015 gives management for H. pylori-negative, high-grade, or non-regressing gastric MALT lymphoma.

### Post-Eradication Surveillance

> [!pearl] Keep surveilling after successful eradication
> Extended follow-up with endoscopy and tissue sampling is recommended: complete regression may take a prolonged period, and recurrence can occur with or without H. pylori reinfection ([[asge-2015-gastric-premalignant]]).

| Interval after eradication | Surveillance |
|---|---|
| **First 2 years** | [[upper-endoscopy\|Endoscopy]] + mucosal sampling **every 3–6 months** |
| **Thereafter** | Extend to **every 6–12 months** |

- **The optimal interval has not been defined.** The schedule above is from one large international series reporting low rates of progression on it (median follow-up 42.2 months, range 2–144). [[asge-2015-gastric-premalignant]]
