## Assessment

- Nausea and vomiting of pregnancy (NVP) is common — [[gerd|heartburn]]/nausea/vomiting frequency **30%–90%** in pregnancy.
- Timing: begins **4–6 weeks**, peaks **8–12 weeks**, subsides by **week 20**.
- Associated with elevated human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) and estrogen; progesterone slows gastrointestinal (GI) and small-bowel motility → delayed gastric emptying. ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]])

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- Clinical diagnosis: nausea ± vomiting in early pregnancy without an alternative cause.
- **Hyperemesis gravidarum (HG)** = intractable NVP with **dehydration, >5% prepregnancy weight loss, and electrolyte imbalances**; affects 0.3%–2%; usually starts before week 22.
- Course of HG: resolves in >50% by week 16 and 80% by week 20; persists throughout pregnancy in ~10%. ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]])
- HG is accompanied by **[[abnormal-liver-chemistries|elevated liver enzymes]] in 40%–50%** — expected, not itself a reason to look elsewhere, but the pregnancy-specific liver diseases still have to be excluded (see [[liver-disease-in-pregnancy]]).

### Severity Assessment

| 1 | Not at all | None | None |
| 2 | ≤1 | 1–2 | 1–2 |
| 3 | 2–3 | 3–4 | 3–4 |
| 4 | 4–6 | 5–6 | 5–6 |
| 5 | >6 | ≥7 | ≥7 |

- AGA grades severity with the PUQE score but does not tie the bands to specific drug steps: **ondansetron, metoclopramide, promethazine, and intravenous (IV) glucocorticoids** are reserved for **moderate-to-severe** cases. ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]] best practice advice [BPA] 4)

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[nausea-and-vomiting]].*

- [[gastroparesis|Gastroparesis]] and other primary GI dysmotility
- [[cyclic-vomiting-syndrome|Cyclic vomiting syndrome]] / [[cannabinoid-hyperemesis-syndrome|cannabinoid hyperemesis]]
- Molar/multiple pregnancy, thyroid disease (hyperthyroidism) — associated with/mimic HG
- Hepatobiliary, vascular ([[portal-vein-thrombosis|portal vein thrombosis]]), or renal causes — ultrasound helps exclude gallstones, molar pregnancy, and assess fetal growth
- Pregnancy-specific liver disease — [[intrahepatic-cholestasis-of-pregnancy|intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP)]], [[acute-fatty-liver-of-pregnancy|acute fatty liver of pregnancy (AFLP)]], hemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, and low platelets (HELLP) syndrome (see [[liver-disease-in-pregnancy]])

## Diagnostics

- Focused evaluation: history (prior pregnancies — HG recurs; pre-existing conditions), exam for dehydration (orthostasis, decreased skin turgor, dry mucous membranes) and malnutrition (weight loss, muscle wasting), neurologic exam for neuropathy/vitamin deficiency.
- Labs: electrolytes, extent of dehydration, nutritional/vitamin deficiencies, liver enzymes.
- **Ultrasound** to detect multiple/molar pregnancy, assess fetal growth, and exclude hepatobiliary/vascular/renal causes. ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]])

## Therapeutics

> [!pearl] Treat early to head off hyperemesis
> Early intervention and treatment of NVP may prevent progression to HG ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]] BPA 4).

### Stepwise Treatment

| Step | Treatment | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Diet and lifestyle | Small, frequent, bland meals — bananas, rice, applesauce, and toast (BRAT) diet; high-protein/low-fat; avoid strong-odor triggers | Cutting spicy, fatty, acidic, and fried foods also helps coexisting heartburn |
| 2 | Ginger | 250-mg capsule 4 times daily | For persistent or severe symptoms |
| 3 | Vitamin B6 (pyridoxine) | 10–25 mg every 8 h | American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) option; also first-line for mild HG |
| 4 | Doxylamine + pyridoxine | 10 mg/10 mg or 20 mg/20 mg combinations | US Food and Drug Administration (FDA)–approved; ACOG-recommended for persistent NVP refractory to nonpharmacologic therapy; safe, well tolerated |
| 4 | Other histamine-1 (H1) antagonists — promethazine, dimenhydrinate | Not stated | Safe first-line antiemetics |
| 5 | Metoclopramide | Not stated | ACOG step-up for non-responders; efficacy similar to promethazine but less drowsiness, dizziness, dystonia, and discontinuation for adverse events; no increased congenital defects |
| 6 | Ondansetron | Not stated | Second-line — mainly severe NVP requiring hospitalization; case-by-case before 10 weeks of pregnancy (some reports of congenital heart defects with 1st-trimester use; no increase in stillbirth, spontaneous abortion, or major birth defects) |

- **Hydration and adequate nutrition run alongside every step** (BPA 4). AGA does not print doses for metoclopramide, ondansetron, promethazine, or dimenhydrinate.
- Coexisting heartburn: antacids and histamine-2 (H2) receptor blockers (cimetidine, famotidine) are routine; **[[proton-pump-inhibitors|proton pump inhibitors (PPIs)]]** (omeprazole, pantoprazole) if lifestyle measures and antacids fail.

### Hyperemesis Gravidarum

- Goals: prevent dehydration, correct electrolytes, support maternal (and thereby fetal) nutrition; treatment guided by symptom severity. Multidisciplinary — obstetrics, nutrition, psychology, GI, and mental health (anxiety, depression).
- **Vitamin B1 (thiamine) 100 mg/day for ≥7 days**, then 50 mg/day until adequate oral intake — prevents refeeding syndrome and Wernicke encephalopathy.
- Severe symptoms → hospitalization for IV hydration and replacement of electrolytes, vitamins, and nutrients; **[[nutrition-in-hospitalized-patients|enteral or parenteral nutrition]]** if weight loss/symptoms persist.
- **Methylprednisolone — last resort:** 16 mg IV every 8 hours for up to 3 days, then taper over 2 weeks to the lowest effective dose, maximum duration 6 weeks; reduces rehospitalization. **Avoid before 10 weeks of gestation** (slight increase in cleft palate reported — data conflicting; use with caution in the 1st trimester).
- No significant efficacy difference among metoclopramide, ondansetron, and promethazine in HG (Cochrane review of 25 studies).
- European Association for the Study of the Liver (EASL) guidance, as relayed by AGA 2024: doxylamine + pyridoxine and phenothiazines first-line for HG, metoclopramide and ondansetron second-line; **withdraw the drug if extrapyramidal adverse effects appear** (phenothiazines, metoclopramide). ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]])
