## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Definition:** a defect in the gastric or duodenal wall extending **through the muscularis mucosa** (the lowermost limit of the mucosa) **into the deeper layers of the wall — submucosa or muscularis propria** [[asge-2010-pud]]
- Site: stomach (gastric ulcer, **GU**) or duodenum (duodenal ulcer, **DU**)
- **Uncomplicated peptic ulcer disease (PUD)** = absence of gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding, obstruction, and perforation [[asge-2010-pud]]
- Presentations: [[dyspepsia|dyspepsia]], GI bleeding, [[iron-deficiency-anemia|anemia]], gastric outlet obstruction. Who with dyspepsia gets endoscopy is on [[dyspepsia]]
- **Etiology:** [[helicobacter-pylori-infection|H. pylori]] and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs)/aspirin are the two treatable causes the American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) directs management at; also idiopathic (H. pylori-negative, NSAID-negative), gastrin-hypersecretory states ([[gastroenteropancreatic-neuroendocrine-tumors|Zollinger-Ellison syndrome]]), and malignancy (GU) [[asge-2010-pud]]

### Severity Assessment

- Bleeding ulcers are stratified endoscopically by their **bleeding stigmata** (active spurting, active oozing, nonbleeding visible vessel, adherent clot, flat spot, clean base), which drives the decision to apply [[endoscopic-hemostasis|endoscopic hemostasis]] — see [[upper-gi-bleeding]] for the stigma-by-stigma therapy recommendations and the American College of Gastroenterology (ACG)-sourced further-bleeding data
  - The **Forrest classification** labels (Ia/Ib/IIa/IIb/IIc/III) are the familiar shorthand for those same stigmata, but [[acg-2021-ugib|ACG 2021]] does **not** use or define Forrest and prints **no** class-specific rebleeding percentages. Class definitions, endoscopic images, and post-hemostasis rebleeding rates per class are on [[upper-gi-bleeding#Step 5: Endoscopic Evaluation — Ulcer Stigmata|upper GI bleeding]] (from ACG/ASGE 2025 and American Gastroenterological Association [AGA] 2024)

**Giant ulcers — the size thresholds differ by site** [[asge-2010-pud]]:

| Site | Giant = | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Stomach** | **>3 cm** | 10%–24% of all GUs in older literature; now rarely encountered with widespread antisecretory therapy. Barium may miss them — large, shallow craters |
| **Duodenum** | **>2 cm** | Higher incidence of complications — bleeding, penetration, perforation. Barium may mistake them for the entire duodenal bulb, a pseudodiverticulum, or a true bulb diverticulum |

- Across both sites, patients with giant ulcers are **older**, may present atypically (anorexia, weight loss), and have more aggressive disease: **mortality 10% vs 3%** and **urgent surgery 65% vs 12%** compared with smaller ulcers [[asge-2010-pud]]
- [[upper-endoscopy|Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD)]] is preferred over barium here, and also excludes malignancy and the rare causes of giant ulcers — [[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease]], eosinophilic gastroenteritis, ischemia
- No data clearly show that surveillance endoscopy of giant peptic ulcers improves outcomes, though documenting healing can be justified by their higher adverse-event rates [[asge-2010-pud]]

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[dyspepsia]].*

- [[gastric-adenocarcinoma|Gastric adenocarcinoma]] (esp non-healing GU), [[gastric-malt-lymphoma|gastric lymphoma (mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue [MALT]/diffuse large B-cell lymphoma [DLBCL])]], erosive gastritis, [[functional-dyspepsia|functional dyspepsia]], [[gastroparesis]], biliary pain, [[atrophic-gastritis|atrophic gastritis]] / [[helicobacter-pylori-infection|H. pylori]] gastritis without ulcer

## Diagnostics

Biopsy, surveillance, and healing all split by site ([[asge-2010-pud]], Recommendations 2–7 — the document's own numbering):

| | Gastric ulcer | Duodenal ulcer |
|---|---|---|
| **Biopsy at index EGD** | **Most** — malignant GU can look endoscopically benign. Individualized: risk is very low in e.g. a young NSAID user with multiple benign-appearing shallow flat antral ulcers and erosions | **Not routine** — extremely unlikely to be malignant |
| **Biopsy when the appearance suggests malignancy** | mass lesion · elevated irregular ulcer borders · abnormal adjacent mucosal folds | — |
| **Technique** | multiple specimens from the **base and edges**. Cytologic **brushings add little and are not an alternative or adjunct** to biopsy | — |
| **Surveillance EGD** | Individualized. **Suggested** if still symptomatic despite an appropriate course of medical therapy; **also consider** if no clear etiology or if not biopsied at the index EGD. Low yield when benign on both endoscopy and biopsy with a defined etiology (NSAID or H. pylori) and asymptomatic after therapy | Consider if symptoms persist despite an appropriate course of therapy — to rule out refractory ulcer and nonpeptic etiologies. Low yield once symptoms resolve on acid suppression + eradication + stopping NSAIDs |
| **Healing** | **Any ulcer unhealed after 8–12 weeks** of medical therapy should be biopsied | **>90% heal with 4 weeks** of [[proton-pump-inhibitors\|proton pump inhibitor (PPI)]] therapy |
| **Ulcer seen on imaging only** | → EGD. Radiologic criteria for benignity (concurrent duodenal ulceration or deformity) are **not reliable**; if the ulcer looks benign, is responding, and there are no alarm features, treat medically for the 8–12 weeks expected to heal it, then biopsy if unhealed | Benign-appearing and uncomplicated → **no EGD needed** |

> [!pitfall] A benign biopsy does not clear a suspicious ulcer
> **False-negative biopsies occur in 2%–5%** of malignant gastric ulcers, so an endoscopically suspicious GU gets surveillance EGD and repeat biopsy **even when the index biopsy was benign** ([[asge-2010-pud]]).

- **Test for [[helicobacter-pylori-infection|H. pylori]] in all PUD** (Recommendation 1) — endoscopic options are biopsy for histology, culture, or rapid urease test [[asge-2010-pud]]. **[[acg-2024-hp-treatment|ACG 2024]] Table 4 makes the indication "peptic ulcer disease: prior history *or* active disease"** — a healed or historical ulcer still warrants testing, and treatment is offered to anyone positive by a **non-serological** test. PUD is also the worked example of a **high pre-test probability** in which serology remains acceptable

## Therapeutics

### Uncomplicated Disease

- **Acid suppression:** PPI. ASGE frames non-healing as an ulcer persisting after **8–12 weeks** of antisecretory therapy, and specifies no agent, dose, or healing duration beyond that mark; standard dosing is on [[proton-pump-inhibitors]] [[asge-2010-pud]]
- **Remove the cause:** stop NSAIDs if possible; eradicate [[helicobacter-pylori-infection|H. pylori]] if positive — regimens, and the test of cure every treated patient needs, are on [[helicobacter-pylori-infection]]
- **Maintain antisecretory therapy** when aspirin must be continued, or when the ulcer is deemed **idiopathic** [[asge-2010-pud]]. Gastroprotection for patients who must keep taking an NSAID or aspirin: [[proton-pump-inhibitors]]

### Bleeding Ulcer

- **Timing:** EGD early in the hospitalization (Recommendation 8) — ASGE defines *early* as **within 24 hours of admission** [[asge-2010-pud]]
- **Disposition:** hemodynamically stable patients whose endoscopy shows ulcers **without** high-risk stigmata may be discharged home after endoscopy [[asge-2010-pud]]
- **Hemostasis is directed by the bleeding stigma, not by a Forrest label.** [[acg-2021-ugib|ACG 2021]] recommends endoscopic therapy for **active spurting, active oozing, and nonbleeding visible vessels** (Rec 6, Strong) and **could not reach a recommendation** for or against therapy of an **adherent clot resistant to vigorous irrigation** (Rec 7, ungraded). ⚠ The older [[asge-2010-pud|ASGE 2010]] listed adherent clot among the high-risk stigmata that "should all undergo endoscopic therapy" — ACG 2021 is newer and governs. Modality choice is on [[upper-gi-bleeding]]
- **High-dose PPI after successful endoscopic hemostasis:** continuously **or intermittently for 3 days** (ACG 2021 Rec 13, Strong). *High dose* = **≥80 mg daily for at least 3 days**; the continuous regimen is an **80-mg bolus followed by 8 mg/hr infusion**, and the intermittent option is **40 mg 2–4 times daily, orally or intravenously (IV)**, with an initial 80-mg bolus potentially appropriate — higher total daily doses may be preferable in western populations. Meta-analysis of the supporting randomized controlled trials (RCTs) found no difference between continuous and intermittent [[acg-2021-ugib]]
- **Then step down, don't stop:** high-risk patients who received endoscopic hemostasis followed by short-term in-hospital high-dose PPI should **continue twice-daily PPI until 2 weeks after the index endoscopy** (ACG 2021 Rec 14, Conditional/low). *High risk* here means the entry criterion of the single supporting RCT — **Rockall score ≥6** with active bleeding, a nonbleeding visible vessel, or adherent clot; ACG 2021 neither endorses nor defines the Rockall score ([[upper-gi-bleeding#Rockall Score|see upper GI bleeding]]) [[acg-2021-ugib]]
- **Rebleeding** occurs in as many as **10%** despite endotherapy plus high-dose PPI → **repeat endoscopic therapy before** surgical or radiologic intervention (ASGE Rec 9; ACG 2021 Rec 15, Conditional). Escalation after failed repeat endotherapy is on [[upper-gi-bleeding]] [[asge-2010-pud]] [[acg-2021-ugib]]
- **[[potassium-competitive-acid-blockers|Potassium-competitive acid blockers (P-CABs)]]:** insufficient evidence for first-line use in bleeding ulcers with high-risk stigmata, though rapid potent acid inhibition may prove useful (AGA 2024 clinical practice update [CPU], best practice advice [BPA] 9) [[aga-2024-pcab]]
- Resuming antiplatelet or anticoagulant therapy after hemostasis: [[anticoagulation-gi-bleeding]]

### Gastric Outlet Obstruction

- **[[gastric-outlet-obstruction|Gastric outlet obstruction (GOO)]]** in PUD arises from inflammation and scarring of the pylorus and/or duodenum. **EGD is recommended** to confirm it and differentiate benign from malignant disease (Recommendation 11); **biopsies to exclude malignancy should be considered** [[asge-2010-pud]]
- **Medical management alongside:** PPI to heal any active ulcers, avoidance of NSAIDs, and H. pylori eradication — which minimizes subsequent ulcer-related complications [[asge-2010-pud]]
- **Endoscopic balloon dilation (EBD)** is suggested for *benign* GOO (Recommendation 12) [[asge-2010-pud]]:
  - **67%–83%** respond, with good to excellent **short-term** relief
  - Hydrostatic or pneumatic balloons, inflations **30 seconds to 3 minutes**, **incremental sequential** dilation typically to **at least 15 mm** and up to **20 mm**
  - **Perforation 4%–7%**; long-term results poor — **restenosis in up to 84%**, **51% ultimately need surgery**; **>2 dilations** predicts endoscopic failure and usually surgery

> [!pitfall] Stop short of 20 mm at the pylorus
> Pyloric dilation perforates in **4%–7%** of cases, and in one series **dilation to 20 mm perforated 2 of 3 patients** — dilate incrementally and stop at the smallest diameter that relieves the obstruction ([[asge-2010-pud]]).

- **PUD is the benign cause most likely to respond to EBD — but only once the drivers are removed:** stop NSAIDs and smoking and treat H. pylori. Strictures that are **>3 cm long**, **corrosive/caustic**, or from **[[chronic-pancreatitis|chronic pancreatitis]]** respond poorly, as do those needing **multiple dilations** [[asge-2021-gastroduodenal-obstruction]]. Modality selection is on [[gastric-outlet-obstruction]]

### Perforation and Penetration

- **Clinical evidence of acute perforation → no endoscopy** (Recommendation 10). Mechanical clips are **not** recommended for acute perforation in PUD — the perforated ulcer's **decreased tissue compliance** (vs an acute iatrogenic perforation) is expected to defeat them; **surgical closure is the usual approach**. In combined laparoscopic-endoscopic series, endoscopy served only to identify the perforation site and guide repair [[asge-2010-pud]]
- **Penetrating ulcer:** endoscopy has **no therapeutic role**; biopsies have occasionally diagnosed penetration into an adjacent organ [[asge-2010-pud]]

### Refractory Disease

- **Refractory = fails to heal despite 8–12 weeks of antisecretory therapy** [[asge-2010-pud]]
- **Surveillance endoscopy until the ulcer heals or the etiology is defined** (Recommendation 7) — look for **surreptitious NSAID use, high gastrin states, ischemia**. **Surgical consultation may be considered** for persistent non-healing PUD [[asge-2010-pud]]
- **P-CABs are generally not first-line** for PUD treatment or prophylaxis (AGA 2024 CPU, BPA 8) — noninferior rather than superior to PPIs, and higher cost. Reserve [[vonoprazan|a P-CAB such as vonoprazan]] for **PPI failures** (after excluding non-acid causes of the ulcer) and for **secondary prophylaxis after PPI failure** [[aga-2024-pcab]]
