Mucosal injury of the small bowel and right colon caused by nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs). Injury sits **distal to the duodenum** and is endoscopically indistinguishable from [[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease (CD)]] — the whole clinical problem is separating the two, in both directions. When the colon and terminal ileum are involved, [[asge-2014-lgib|ASGE 2014]] calls it **NSAID colopathy**.

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **No confirmatory test and no diagnostic criteria.** Diagnosis = drug exposure + compatible lesions + exclusion of mimics.
- **Lesions by site:**

| Site | Lesions |
|---|---|
| Small bowel, distal to the duodenum | Mucosal ulcerations, erosions and webs ([[acg-2025-crohns]]); erythema, erosions, ulcers or strictures with chronic use ([[asge-2025-ibd-endoscopy-consensus\|ASGE 2025]]) |
| Terminal ileum | The small-bowel site most often affected ([[asge-2025-ibd-endoscopy-consensus\|ASGE 2025]]); ulcers and diaphragm-like strictures predominate here ([[asge-2014-lgib]]) |
| Right colon | Colon ulcerations and diaphragm-like strictures ([[asge-2014-lgib]]) |

- **Histology does not settle it** ([[asge-2025-ibd-endoscopy-consensus|ASGE 2025]]): features of NSAID enteropathy and CD overlap; **lymphoplasmacytic inflammation is less frequent in NSAID enteropathy**.
- **Timing** ([[asge-2015-ibd]]): NSAID-induced small-bowel mucosal injury can be detected **within 1–2 weeks of ingestion** — recent exposure is enough to explain the findings.
- **Mechanism** ([[acg-2025-crohns]]): NSAID therapy increases mucosal permeability → more exposure to luminal toxins and antigens.
- **Typical presentations:**
  - **Bleeding** — ulcers from anti-inflammatory agents, together with [[angioectasia|angioectasias]] and other vascular lesions, are **more likely in patients over 40 years**; under 40, [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]] and [[meckels-diverticulum|Meckel's diverticulum]] are the likelier causes of small-bowel bleeding ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding]]).
  - **[[acute-lower-gi-bleeding|Lower gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding]]** — NSAID use raises the risk, including diverticular bleeding; NSAID-use prevalence **as high as 86%** in patients with lower GI bleeding ([[asge-2014-lgib]]).
  - **[[chronic-diarrhea|Chronic diarrhea]] with abdominal pain** — capsule endoscopy yield **13%–24%**, NSAID-induced enteropathy among the diagnoses found alongside CD, [[celiac-disease|celiac disease]] and submucosal masses ([[asge-2010-diarrhea]]).

> [!pitfall] Small-bowel mucosal breaks do not diagnose anything on their own
> Up to **14% of healthy individuals** have mucosal breaks and other nonspecific lesions on capsule endoscopy, and a break is **not necessarily diagnostic of CD** — infection, ischemia, radiation injury and drug-induced injury all produce them ([[asge-2015-ibd]]).

### Severity Assessment

- **No severity score is specific to NSAID enteropathy.** Severity is carried by the complication.
- **Complications** ([[asge-2014-lgib]]): NSAID colopathy may be associated with **lower GI bleeding and perforation**.
- The **Lewis score** grades small-bowel inflammatory burden on capsule endoscopy (components and bands on [[capsule-endoscopy]]), but it has **no discriminatory ability** between the causes of small-bowel inflammatory change — radiation enteritis, drug and NSAID effects, infection, ischemia and allergic disorders score alike. *Denuded mucosa*, the candidate NSAID-specific marker, was **eliminated** from the final three-variable index ([[gralnek-2008-lewis-score]]). Use it for burden, never for cause.

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[small-bowel-bleeding]].*

The differential runs in both directions — NSAID injury is mistaken for IBD, and IBD is attributed to NSAIDs.

| Mimic | Separating feature |
|---|---|
| [[crohns-disease\|Crohn's disease]] | NSAID colopathy **may be misdiagnosed as IBD** ([[asge-2014-lgib]]); NSAIDs cause terminal ileal changes that **mimic IBD** ([[asge-2010-diarrhea]]). Histology overlaps — only **less frequent lymphoplasmacytic inflammation** favors NSAID injury ([[asge-2025-ibd-endoscopy-consensus\|ASGE 2025]]). Drug history and response to withdrawal are the other discriminators |
| Infection, [[colon-ischemia\|ischemia]], radiation injury | Listed beside drug-induced injury as causes of small-bowel mucosal ulceration ([[asge-2015-ibd]]) |
| Carcinoid, tuberculosis, lymphoma, adenocarcinoma | Other causes of abnormal terminal ileal findings in diarrhea ([[asge-2010-diarrhea]]) |

**In the ileal pouch** ([[asge-2015-ibd]]): the differential for an abnormal afferent limb includes pre-pouch ileitis, **NSAID enteritis**, infection and ischemia; a long segment (**≥10 cm**) of involved afferent limb, especially with concomitant discrete ulcers, favors CD. **Afferent-limb ulcers are suggestive of CD only when NSAID use and infection are excluded** — biopsy any afferent small-bowel abnormality found at pouchoscopy. See [[pouchitis]].

## Diagnostics

- **Targeted drug history first** — a history that specifically includes NSAID use helps determine the source of bleeding and guide further management ([[asge-2014-lgib]]).
- **[[capsule-endoscopy|Capsule endoscopy]]** — demonstrates the lesions, and is the test most easily fooled by them.
- **[[colonoscopy|Ileocolonoscopy]]** with terminal ileal intubation — reaches the right-colon and ileal territory where NSAID colopathy lives.
- **[[upper-endoscopy|Upper endoscopy (EGD)]]** — consider when no source is found in the colon, **especially with upper GI symptoms, iron deficiency anemia, or NSAID use** ([[asge-2014-lgib]]); NSAID use is also one of the factors that raises the yield of a **repeat** EGD in occult bleeding ([[asge-2017-small-bowel-bleeding]]).
- **[[device-assisted-enteroscopy|Deep enteroscopy]]** — for tissue and therapy when a lesion must be reached.

### NSAID Hold Before the Examination

Stopping the drug before you look is what keeps NSAID injury from being read as disease.

| Before | Hold | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Capsule endoscopy | **1–2 months**, to minimize misdiagnosis | European Society of Gastrointestinal Endoscopy / European Crohn's and Colitis Organisation recommendation, cited in [[asge-2015-ibd]] |
| Diagnostic ileocolonoscopy for suspected IBD | Avoid NSAIDs beforehand — no interval given | Consensus statement 3 ([[asge-2025-ibd-endoscopy-consensus\|ASGE 2025]]); also discouraged by [[asge-2015-ibd]], which pairs it with sodium phosphate–based bowel cleansing as a cause of IBD-mimicking mucosal change |
| Ileocolonoscopy in known IBD | 4 weeks was proposed but **did not achieve panel consensus** | [[asge-2025-ibd-endoscopy-consensus\|ASGE 2025]] |

## Therapeutics

- **Withdrawal is the intervention.** Neither the ACG Crohn's guideline nor the ASGE endoscopy guidelines give a drug therapy, a prophylaxis regimen, a re-scope interval, or a rule for dilating NSAID diaphragm-like strictures.
- **After hospitalization for diverticular hemorrhage** ([[acg-2023-lgib]]): *discontinue nonaspirin NSAIDs* (strong recommendation, low-quality evidence). Aspirin is handled separately — see [[acute-lower-gi-bleeding]].
- **In CD** ([[acg-2025-crohns]], Key concept 35 — ungraded): *NSAIDs may exacerbate disease activity and routine use should be viewed with caution among patients with CD.* Supporting data:
  - **17%–28% relapse of quiescent IBD within 9 days** of therapy with the nonselective NSAIDs naproxen and nabumetone, in a comparison study against acetaminophen, nimesulide and aspirin.
  - Recent NSAID use associated with increased risk of emergency hospital admission in IBD; a systematic review and meta-analysis of low-risk-of-bias studies found NSAID use associated with **increased risk of CD exacerbation**.
  - ⚠ **Counter-evidence:** in a large Veterans Association study the NSAID–flare association was judged to reflect **residual bias rather than true causation** — disease-activity rates were similar before and after exposure.
- **COX-2 selective agents** ([[acg-2025-crohns]]): selective cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) inhibitors in **short-term** therapy have not been shown to exacerbate [[ulcerative-colitis|ulcerative colitis (UC)]] — **no equivalent study has been performed in CD**, so the UC result does not transfer.
