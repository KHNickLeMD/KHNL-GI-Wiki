Excess methane-producing organisms in the intestinal tract, distinguished from classical hydrogen-predominant [[small-intestinal-bacterial-overgrowth|SIBO]]. "IMO" is the term proposed by [[acg-2020-sibo]] because the responsible organisms are **archaea, not bacteria** — the "B" in SIBO is technically incorrect, and methane may arise **throughout** the intestinal tract rather than only the small bowel.

**Mechanism:** *Methanobrevibacter smithii* is the key methanogen; it consumes hydrogen to make methane (4H₂ → 1CH₄). Methane augments contractility and delays ileal peristaltic conduction velocity via cholinergic neurons, slowing transit.

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Positive methane breath test:** CH₄ **≥10 ppm at any point** during a glucose or lactulose breath test (North American Consensus threshold) **plus** symptoms — a positive test alone is not the diagnosis.
- Excess methane on breath testing does **not** indicate SIBO (Key Concept 7) — the two overgrowths are reported and treated separately.
- Individuals with positive methane breath tests also have increased methanogen levels in **stool**, not only the small bowel.

### Severity Assessment

- No graded severity system. Breath **methane level is proportional to constipation severity**, and constipation is associated with both elevated breath methane and stool *M. smithii* (Key Concept 9).
- IMO is strongly associated with [[chronic-idiopathic-constipation|constipation]] — **OR 3.51 (95% CI 2.00–6.16)** across multiple studies and 1 meta-analysis.

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[abdominal-bloating-and-distention]].*

- [[small-intestinal-bacterial-overgrowth|SIBO]] (hydrogen-predominant) — rise in H₂ **≥20 ppm above baseline by 90 minutes**; may coexist.
- Hydrogen sulfide (H₂S)–predominant overgrowth — recognized by the guideline but not measurable on standard 2-gas breath testing.
- [[chronic-idiopathic-constipation|Chronic idiopathic constipation]] and constipation-predominant [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|IBS]] without methanogen overgrowth.

## Diagnostics

- **ACG Recommendation 5:** suggest testing for methane using **glucose or lactulose breath tests** to diagnose IMO in symptomatic patients (conditional, very low quality).
- Substrate: glucose or lactulose; sampling window **90–120 minutes**.
- Threshold: **≥10 ppm at any point** (unlike hydrogen, which requires a *rise* from baseline).
- *Breath-test mechanics, preparation, and interpretation pitfalls: see [[small-intestinal-bacterial-overgrowth]].*

## Therapeutics

Two studies of antibiotic therapy, both defining positivity as methane **≥3 ppm** (see caveat). Retrospective chart review of 74 patients with IMO by lactulose breath test; all arms treated **10 days**; endpoint = methane reduced to undetectable (**<3 ppm**) on repeat breath test:

| Regimen | Dose | Duration | Methane normalized (<3 ppm) |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[rifaximin]] + neomycin | 400 mg t.i.d. + 500 mg b.i.d. | 10 days | **87%** |
| Neomycin alone | 500 mg b.i.d. | 10 days | 33% |
| [[rifaximin]] alone | 400 mg t.i.d. | 10 days | 28% |

- Separate placebo-controlled trial of 84 [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|IBS]] patients with IMO: neomycin 500 mg b.i.d. × 10 days reduced methane below 3 ppm in **20%** vs **1%** with placebo.
- **Caveat on the thresholds:** both neomycin studies used **≥3 ppm** for positivity, *not* the **≥10 ppm** now recommended and used for diagnosis above — efficacy figures are therefore not directly transferable to patients diagnosed by the current cutoff.
- The IMO rifaximin dose (**400 mg t.i.d.**) differs from the SIBO dose (550 mg t.i.d.) — see [[small-intestinal-bacterial-overgrowth]].
- Rationale for treating at all: **targeting methanogens may reduce methane production and improve constipation** (Key Concept 10).
