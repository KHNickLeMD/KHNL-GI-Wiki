Excess methane-producing organisms in the intestinal tract, distinguished from classical hydrogen-predominant [[small-intestinal-bacterial-overgrowth|small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO)]]. Intestinal methanogen overgrowth (IMO) is the term proposed by the American College of Gastroenterology ([[acg-2020-sibo|ACG]]) because the responsible organisms are **archaea, not bacteria** — the "B" in SIBO is technically incorrect — and because methane may arise **throughout** the intestinal tract rather than only the small bowel.

**Mechanism:** *Methanobrevibacter smithii* is the predominant methanogen in the human gut and the organism behind a positive methane breath test (Key Concept 8). It consumes hydrogen to make methane — 4 moles H₂ yield 1 mole CH₄ ([[aga-2020-sibo]]). Methane infusion into the small intestine slows transit in a canine model, and *in vitro* methane augments contractility and delays ileal peristaltic conduction velocity through effects on cholinergic neurons — **suggesting a direct causal relationship** between methanogen overgrowth and [[chronic-idiopathic-constipation|constipation]].

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Positive methane breath test plus symptoms.** Recommendation 5 frames testing for **symptomatic patients with constipation** — a positive gas result on its own is not the diagnosis.
- The two gases are read by **different rules**, and this is where the diagnosis is most often got wrong:

| Gas | Positive test | Timing | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|---|
| CH₄ (methane) | **≥10 ppm** — an absolute level | any point during the test | IMO |
| H₂ (hydrogen) | **rise ≥20 ppm above baseline** | within 90 min | [[small-intestinal-bacterial-overgrowth\|SIBO]] |

> [!pitfall] Methane is a level, not a rise
> Do not apply the hydrogen "rise above baseline" logic to methane — any methane value ≥10 ppm at any time point is positive ([[acg-2020-sibo]]).
> Unsettled: "some experts recommend a **rise** of 10 ppm in methane levels, and this requires confirmation" — the absolute ≥10 ppm North American Consensus threshold is what the guideline uses.

- Excess methane on breath testing does **not** indicate SIBO (Key Concept 7) — methanogens are archaea, not bacteria, so the two overgrowths are reported and treated separately.

### Severity Assessment

- No graded severity system.
- Breath **methane level is proportional to the degree of constipation**, and constipation is associated with both elevated breath methane and stool *M. smithii* (Key Concept 9).
- IMO is associated with [[chronic-idiopathic-constipation|constipation]] — **odds ratio (OR) 3.51 (confidence interval [CI] 2.00–6.16)** across multiple studies and 1 meta-analysis.

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[abdominal-bloating-and-distention]].*

- [[small-intestinal-bacterial-overgrowth|SIBO]] (hydrogen-predominant) — different threshold (see table above); may coexist, since one test reports both gases.
- Hydrogen sulfide (H₂S)–predominant overgrowth — H₂S testing exists but its cutoff is **not yet validated**, and conventional breath testing measures only H₂ and CH₄.
- [[chronic-idiopathic-constipation|Chronic idiopathic constipation]] and constipation-predominant [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)]] without methanogen overgrowth. CH₄-positive breath tests are specifically linked to constipation-predominant IBS, whereas SIBO is associated with IBS with diarrhea ([[aga-2020-sibo]]).

## Diagnostics

- **ACG 2020 Recommendation 5 (verbatim):** *"We suggest testing for methane using glucose or lactulose breath tests to diagnose the overgrowth of methane-producing organisms (IMO) in **symptomatic patients with constipation**"* — **conditional recommendation, very low level of evidence.** The constipation qualifier is part of the recommendation: this is not a test for undifferentiated symptoms. The guideline does not define constipation for this recommendation; the **<3 complete spontaneous bowel movements (CSBM)/week** figure below is its proposed *trial*-enrollment criterion.
- Substrate: **75 g glucose** or **10 g lactulose**, taken with or followed by ~250 mL water (Key Concept 5).
- Breath sampled for **both** H₂ and CH₄ — a single test reports both gases, so IMO and SIBO are diagnosed off the same study. Measuring both also matters because **the presence of CH₄ suppresses H₂** on the breath test ([[aga-2020-sibo]]), so a methane producer can read falsely low for hydrogen.
- Threshold: CH₄ ≥10 ppm at any point (see [[#Establishing the Diagnosis]]).
- **Preparation matters and is the same as for SIBO** — no antibiotics for **4 weeks**, no promotility agents or laxatives for **≥1 week**, avoid fermentable foods (e.g., complex carbohydrates) the day before, **fast 8–12 h**, and avoid smoking and minimize physical exertion during the test. Full mechanics and interpretation pitfalls: [[small-intestinal-bacterial-overgrowth]].
- **Stool, not just small bowel:** individuals with positive methane breath tests also have increased methanogen levels in **stool** — methanogens may occur throughout the intestinal tract, which is why the guideline dropped "small intestinal" from the name.

## Therapeutics

Rationale for treating at all: **targeting methanogens may reduce methane production and improve constipation** (Key Concept 10).

### Antibiotic Regimens

Two studies, both in patients whose IMO was defined by **lactulose** breath test. Larger of the two was a **retrospective chart review of 74 patients**; all arms treated **10 days**; endpoint = methane reduced to undetectable (**<3 ppm**) on repeat breath testing:

| Regimen | Dose | Duration | Methane normalized (<3 ppm) |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[rifaximin]] + neomycin | 400 mg three times daily + 500 mg twice daily | 10 days | **87%** |
| Neomycin alone | 500 mg twice daily | 10 days | 33% |
| [[rifaximin]] alone | 400 mg three times daily | 10 days | 28% |

- Separate **placebo-controlled trial of 84 [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|IBS]] patients** with IMO: neomycin 500 mg twice daily × 10 days reduced methane below 3 ppm in **20%** vs **1%** with placebo.
- The IMO [[rifaximin]] dose (**400 mg three times daily**) is **not** the dose the same guideline lists for SIBO (550 mg three times daily) — see [[small-intestinal-bacterial-overgrowth]]. This page is the home for the IMO regimens.
- [[aga-2020-sibo]] corroborates only the direction: *"One uncontrolled trial suggested that the combination of neomycin and rifaximin might be effective."* It gives no doses, no numbers and no graded statement on IMO, so the regimens above are [[acg-2020-sibo]]'s, resting on a retrospective chart review.

> [!pitfall] Efficacy data used a lower cutoff
> Both neomycin studies defined methane positivity as **≥3 ppm**, not the **≥10 ppm** now used for diagnosis — so these response rates do not transfer directly to patients diagnosed by the current threshold ([[acg-2020-sibo]]).

### Response and Monitoring

- **Routine retesting is not required when symptoms respond:** *"It is not necessary to repeat diagnostic tests for SIBO after antibiotic therapy should gastrointestinal symptoms respond"* ([[aga-2020-sibo]], the later 2020 publication).
- Where retesting is done, [[acg-2020-sibo]] holds that it **may** correlate with symptom improvement and **may be confirmed by normalization** of methane levels (Key Concept 15), and treats a post-treatment methane of **no greater than 10 ppm** as successful eradication.
- The guideline's proposed criteria for an IMO treatment trial set the response bar explicitly (Table 6 — trial-design guidance, not a clinical pathway):

| Element | ACG proposed criterion |
|---|---|
| Enrollment | **CH₄ ≥10 ppm at any point during the first 90 minutes** of the breath test **AND** constipation (**<3 CSBM/week**) |
| Primary outcome | Increase in **CSBM/week by >1** **AND** normalization of CH₄ (**no CH₄ ≥10 ppm within 90 min**) |
| Key secondary | Improvement in bloating by **>50%** (frequency or severity) |

- Note the **combination rule** — gas normalization alone is not response; the bowel-movement change must come with it.
