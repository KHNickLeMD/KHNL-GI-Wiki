A small-bowel diverticulum that bleeds from **diverticulum-associated ulceration** ([[asge-2017-small-bowel-bleeding|American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2017]]). **Ectopic (heterotopic) gastric mucosa** — present in **10–60%** of Meckel's diverticula — is the tissue the diagnostic scan detects ([[acg-2015-small-bowel-bleeding|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2015]]). A **rare** cause of [[small-bowel-bleeding]] overall, but disproportionately represented in patients **under 40 years**.

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- Presents as **overt or occult** small-bowel bleeding (definitions on [[small-bowel-bleeding]]).
- Confirmed with a **⁹⁹ᵐTc-pertechnetate (Meckel) scan** detecting the ectopic gastric mucosa — ordered once the standard small-bowel workup is unrevealing in a younger patient (see [[#Diagnostics]]).

### Severity Assessment

- **No Meckel-specific severity score appears in the guidelines.** Severity is carried entirely by the bleeding phenotype, which is what sets the pathway on [[small-bowel-bleeding]]: **occult vs overt**, and within overt, **ongoing/massive vs intermittent**.

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[small-bowel-bleeding]].*

Age skews the list: patients **under 40 years** are more likely to have [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]] or Meckel's diverticulum, patients over 40 [[angioectasia]] or [[nsaid-enteropathy|nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) ulcers]] (ACG 2015). Other small-bowel bleeding sources to consider:

- [[crohns-disease]] / other IBD
- NSAID ulcers
- Angioectasias / vascular lesions (Dieulafoy's lesion, [[hereditary-hemorrhagic-telangiectasia|Osler–Weber–Rendu]])
- Small-bowel neoplasms ([[gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor|gastrointestinal stromal tumor (GIST)]], lymphoma, [[gastroenteropancreatic-neuroendocrine-tumors|carcinoid]], adenocarcinoma, polypoid lesions)
- [[colorectal-polyposis|Polyposis syndromes]], radiation enteropathy, small-bowel varices, aortoenteric fistula

## Diagnostics

### Meckel Scan (⁹⁹ᵐTc-Pertechnetate)

**When to order** — the scan is reserved for the young patient whose standard workup is negative:

| Source | Trigger | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| ACG 2015, Rec 22 | Younger patient **and** ongoing overt bleeding **and** normal [[capsule-endoscopy\|video capsule endoscopy (VCE)]] **and** normal enterography — all four together | Conditional recommendation, very low quality of evidence |
| ASGE 2017 | Younger patient in whom bleeding **recurs** after negative upper and lower endoscopy, VCE and [[device-assisted-enteroscopy\|device-assisted enteroscopy]]; named a useful test in pediatric patients and young adults with overt bleeding | Ungraded body text (not one of its numbered recommendations) |

Neither document defines "younger"; the age band both use for the etiology split is **under 40 years**.

**Diagnostic performance:**

| Population | Sensitivity | Specificity |
|---|---|---|
| Not restricted by age (ACG 2015) | 50–90% | 9–95% |
| Pediatric patients and young adults (ASGE 2017) | 62–87.5% for ectopic gastric mucosa | Not reported |

> [!pearl] Yield is highest in children
> Diagnostic yield from these scans is highest when they are performed in children, and the result depends on the **quantity and functional quality** of the heterotopic gastric mucosa (ACG 2015).

**Misleading results:**

| Error | Causes (ACG 2015) |
|---|---|
| **False positive** | Uptake in ulcers, inflammatory lesions, arteriovenous malformations, obstruction, intussusception, and ectopic gastric mucosa in other lesions such as duplication cysts |
| **False negative** | Anatomic or physiologic causes; other inflammation such as ectopic **pancreatic** mucosa, which can be present in up to **74%** of diverticula |

### Other Small-Bowel Tests

- VCE and computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance (MR) enterography come **first** — a normal pair in a younger patient with ongoing overt bleeding is what triggers the Meckel scan.
- Full sequencing of VCE, enterography, deep enteroscopy and the nuclear studies: [[small-bowel-bleeding]].

## Therapeutics

- **Reachable lesion with significant ongoing anemia or active bleeding** → endoscopic therapy at [[device-assisted-enteroscopy|deep enteroscopy]] (ACG 2015, Rec 23 — strong recommendation, low quality of evidence).
- **Massive small-bowel bleeding** → surgery, greatly aided by presurgical localization with a **tattoo** marking the lesion (Rec 28 — strong, low). **Intraoperative enteroscopy should be available** at the time of surgery to help localize the source and deliver endoscopic therapy (Rec 29 — conditional, low).
- A **bleeding Meckel's diverticulum** is one of the rare scenarios that might prompt **emergent laparotomy as the initial modality of choice** in [[acute-lower-gi-bleeding|acute lower gastrointestinal bleeding]] ([[asge-2026-lgib|ASGE 2026]], ungraded).
- The resection itself — diverticulectomy vs segmental resection — is not specified in these guidelines.
