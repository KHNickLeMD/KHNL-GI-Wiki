## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

Functional chest pain = **recurring retrosternal chest pain of presumed esophageal origin, different from heartburn**, not explained by [[gerd|gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)]], other mucosal disorders, or motor processes. It is a subset within the broad umbrella of **noncardiac chest pain (NCCP)**, and an esophageal disorder of gut–brain interaction (E-DGBI) ([[disorders-of-gut-brain-interaction]]).

- **History and physical examination do not reliably separate esophageal from cardiac chest pain** → cardiac evaluation first in appropriate clinical settings.
- **A diagnosis of exclusion** — there is **no definitive clinical test** to confirm esophageal origin of chest pain.
- Pooled prevalence **1.4%** (95% confidence interval 1.3%–1.5%); women 1.5% vs men 1.3%; more prevalent under age 65 (1.4%–1.5%) than over 65 (1.0%).

**Rome V criteria (A1)** ([[rome-v-2026-functional-esophageal-disorders]]):

> Must include **all** of the following:
> 1. **Retrosternal chest pain or discomfort** — cardiac causes should be ruled out
> 2. **Absence of associated esophageal symptoms**, such as heartburn or [[dysphagia]]
> 3. Absence of evidence that **GERD** (abnormal endoscopy ≥Los Angeles [LA] grade B esophagitis, or reflux burden on reflux testing guided by Lyon Consensus 2.0) **or nonreflux esophagitis** is the cause of the symptoms
> 4. **Absence of disorders of esophagogastric junction (EGJ) outflow and/or disorders of esophageal peristalsis** — [[achalasia]] or conclusive [[esophagogastric-junction-outflow-obstruction|EGJ outflow obstruction (EGJOO)]]; absent contractility, hypercontractile esophagus, distal esophageal spasm — **except ineffective esophageal motility (IEM)**, guided by [[chicago-classification-v4|Chicago Classification version 4.0]]
>
> Criteria fulfilled for the past 3 months, symptom onset ≥6 months before diagnosis, frequency **at least once a week**, despite **optimized antisecretory therapy for at least 8 weeks**. Symptoms must be **bothersome**.
>
> *Consider mucosal biopsies if chest pain occurs with meals, as this could be a surrogate for dysphagia.*

> [!pitfall] Rome V changed who gets esophageal biopsies
> Rome IV required biopsies for **all** patients with chest pain. **Rome V requires them only if the chest pain is meal related** ([[rome-v-2026-functional-esophageal-disorders]]). Readers carrying the Rome IV rule will over-biopsy.

- **Inconclusive EGJOO may coexist with functional chest pain; conclusive EGJOO remains exclusionary.** IEM is not an exclusion.

### Severity Assessment

- **Rome V sets no severity grade for functional chest pain** — the criteria give only a floor: bothersome symptoms at least once a week.
- **Assess psychological comorbidity in every patient.** Anxiety, depression and somatization are common in NCCP, chest pain is a common feature of panic attacks, and these patients often show higher levels of neuroticism.
- These psychological factors track with **poorer quality of life, more frequent pain episodes and reduced treatment response**. Whether causal or consequential, **addressing them is essential to effective management**.

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[noncardiac-chest-pain]].* The share of NCCP attributable to each esophageal cause is tabled there.

- **Cardiac disease** — coronary artery disease, excluded **first**
- GERD — the most common cause of NCCP
- Esophageal motor disorders — achalasia, conclusive EGJOO, distal esophageal spasm, hypercontractile esophagus, absent contractility
- [[eosinophilic-esophagitis|Eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE)]] — especially chest pain **during eating**, which may be a surrogate for dysphagia
- [[reflux-hypersensitivity|Reflux hypersensitivity]] — chest pain triggered by physiologic reflux events
- **Abelchia** (retrograde cricopharyngeal dysfunction / inability to belch) — chest pain with **audible gurgling noises in the chest**; see [[abdominal-bloating-and-distention]]
- Panic disorder and other psychiatric causes
- Musculoskeletal chest wall pain

## Diagnostics

Testing sequence (cardiac clearance → antisecretory trial → endoscopy → reflux and motility testing) is on [[noncardiac-chest-pain]]. What each A1 requirement takes to satisfy:

| Requirement | How it is met | Operative detail |
|---|---|---|
| Cardiac causes ruled out | Cardiac evaluation **before** any esophageal workup | Confirmation — typically from the patient's cardiologist or primary care physician — that symptoms are unrelated to concurrent or new coronary artery disease |
| Symptoms persist despite acid suppression | **8 weeks of optimized [[proton-pump-inhibitors\|proton pump inhibitor (PPI)]] or [[potassium-competitive-acid-blockers\|potassium-competitive acid blocker (PCAB)]]** | Simple and cost-effective given the high prevalence of GERD within NCCP, and determines whether GERD is triggering the pain |
| GERD excluded | [[upper-endoscopy\|Upper endoscopy]] (unclear role, but exclusionary value) + [[ambulatory-reflux-monitoring\|ambulatory reflux monitoring]] read by Lyon Consensus 2.0 | **≥LA grade B esophagitis**, or an abnormal reflux burden, means GERD rather than functional chest pain — Lyon 2.0 cut-points on [[reflux-testing]] |
| Nonreflux esophagitis excluded | Mucosal biopsies — **considered only if the chest pain is meal-triggered** | EoE confirmed histologically at **<15 eosinophils/high power field** in the appropriate clinical context must be excluded before any E-DGBI diagnosis |
| Major motor disorders excluded | [[high-resolution-manometry\|High-resolution manometry (HRM)]] read by Chicago Classification version 4.0 | Achalasia, conclusive EGJOO, absent contractility, hypercontractile esophagus and distal esophageal spasm are exclusionary; **IEM and inconclusive EGJOO are not** |

**Mechanism — why no test confirms it:**

- Esophageal hypersensitivity from peripheral and/or central sensitization, altered visceral signal processing, and autonomic dysregulation; peripheral sensitization may follow tissue injury, inflammation or repetitive mechanical stimuli and persist beyond the original insult.
- Balloon distention produces pain at lower thresholds than in healthy controls — **up to 75% of NCCP patients are hypersensitive to balloon distention**.
- Cerebral evoked potential studies show distinct latency patterns in NCCP, but findings are variable → mechanistic heterogeneity; autonomic dysfunction may be a distinct subset.
- **Minor motility abnormalities are unlikely to play a causal role**; sustained longitudinal muscle contractions have been linked to chest pain, possibly through an ischemic mechanism — unproven.

## Therapeutics

Treatment primarily targets **neuromodulation of pain** ([[rome-v-2026-functional-esophageal-disorders]]).

- **Tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs), serotonin norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs) and trazodone — up to 50% effectiveness above placebo** in randomized trials; **selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) show inconsistent results**.
- **Side effects or stigma may limit use of neuromodulators.**
- Alternative agents explored but requiring **cautious use because of potential side effects**: gabapentin, pregabalin, dronabinol.

Randomized trials of neuromodulators bearing on chest pain (Table 2 of the source; response wording as printed there):

| Class | Drug | Dose | Trial population | Response |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TCA | Imipramine | 50 mg/d | NCCP | Symptoms −52% (n = 20); significant (n = 18, significant side effects) |
| TCA | Amitriptyline | 10 mg/d | Functional chest pain | Patients −70.6% (n = 20) |
| SSRI | Sertraline | 50–200 mg/d | NCCP | Symptoms −48% (n = 30); symptoms −50% (n = 115) |
| SSRI | Paroxetine | 50–75 mg/d | NCCP | Modest (n = 50) |
| SSRI | Paroxetine | 10–40 mg/d | NCCP | Symptoms −21.7% (n = 69) |
| Trazodone | Trazodone | 100–150 mg/d | Esophageal dysmotility | Symptoms −59% (n = 29) |
| SNRI | Venlafaxine | 75 mg/d | Functional chest pain | Symptoms −59% (n = 43) |
| Other | Theophylline | 200 mg twice daily | NCCP | Symptoms −58% (n = 24, significant side effects) |

- **Behavioral interventions are complementary and carry minimal adverse effects** ([[brain-gut-behavioral-therapy]]):
  - **Gastrointestinal cognitive behavioral therapy (GI-CBT)** — a Cochrane review showed **reduced symptom frequency (relative risk 0.70; 95% confidence interval 0.53–0.92)**; controlled trials confirm reductions in severity and in psychological comorbidities.
  - **Gut-directed hypnotherapy and biofeedback, including diaphragmatic breathing**, also offer symptomatic benefit.
