## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

Functional chest pain is **recurring retrosternal chest pain of presumed esophageal origin, different from heartburn**, not explained by [[gerd|gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)]], other mucosal disorders, or motor processes. It is a subset within the broad umbrella of **noncardiac chest pain (NCCP)**, and an esophageal disorder of gut–brain interaction (E-DGBI) ([[disorders-of-gut-brain-interaction]]).

**Rome V criteria (A1)** ([[rome-v-2026-functional-esophageal-disorders]]):

> Must include **all** of the following:
> 1. **Retrosternal chest pain or discomfort** — **cardiac causes should be ruled out**
> 2. **Absence of associated esophageal symptoms**, such as heartburn or [[dysphagia]]
> 3. Absence of evidence that **GERD** (abnormal endoscopy ≥Los Angeles [LA] grade B esophagitis, or reflux burden by Lyon Consensus 2.0) **or [[eosinophilic-esophagitis|eosinophilic esophagitis]]** is the cause of the symptoms
> 4. **Absence of disorders of esophagogastric junction (EGJ) outflow and/or disorders of esophageal peristalsis** — [[achalasia]], conclusive [[esophagogastric-junction-outflow-obstruction|EGJ outflow obstruction (EGJOO)]], absent contractility, hypercontractile esophagus, distal esophageal spasm — **except ineffective esophageal motility (IEM)**, per [[chicago-classification-v4|Chicago Classification version 4.0]]
>
> Criteria fulfilled for the past 3 months, symptom onset ≥6 months before diagnosis, frequency **at least once a week**, despite **optimized antisecretory therapy for at least 8 weeks**.
>
> *Consider mucosal biopsies if chest pain occurs with meals, as this could be a surrogate for dysphagia.*

> [!pitfall] Rome V changed who gets esophageal biopsies
> Rome IV required biopsies for **all** patients with chest pain. **Rome V requires them only if the chest pain is meal related** ([[rome-v-2026-functional-esophageal-disorders]]). Readers carrying the Rome IV rule will over-biopsy.

- **Inconclusive EGJOO may coexist with functional chest pain; conclusive EGJOO remains exclusionary.** IEM is not an exclusion.
- **Functional chest pain is a diagnosis of exclusion** — there is **no definitive clinical test** to confirm esophageal origin of chest pain.

### Where it sits within noncardiac chest pain

Among patients with NCCP:

| Cause | Share of NCCP |
|---|---|
| [[gerd\|GERD]] | **~50%–60%** |
| Esophageal motility disorder | **15%–18%** |
| **Functional chest pain** | **32%–35%** |

- **Pooled prevalence of functional chest pain in the population: 1.4%** (95% confidence interval 1.3%–1.5%), from a global survey of 54,127 individuals across 26 countries plus 18,949 household interviews in 9 countries.
- Slightly more common in **women (1.5%) than men (1.3%)**, and more prevalent in those **younger than 65 (1.4%–1.5%) than older than 65 (1.0%)**.
- **Psychiatric comorbidity is present in up to 75% of NCCP patients** — anxiety, depression, somatization. Chest pain is a common feature of **panic attacks**, and these patients exhibit higher levels of neuroticism. Whether causal or consequential, **addressing psychological comorbidity is essential for effective management.**

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[noncardiac-chest-pain]].*

- **Cardiac disease** — coronary artery disease must be excluded **first**, by a cardiologist or primary care physician
- [[gerd|GERD]] — the single most common cause of NCCP
- [[eosinophilic-esophagitis|Eosinophilic esophagitis]] — especially when chest pain occurs **during eating**, which may be a surrogate for dysphagia
- [[achalasia]], conclusive [[esophagogastric-junction-outflow-obstruction|EGJOO]], distal esophageal spasm, hypercontractile esophagus, absent contractility — exclusionary major motor disorders
- **Abelchia (inability to belch syndrome / retrograde cricopharyngeal dysfunction)** — an under-recognized cause of chest pain and **audible gurgling noises in the chest**; see [[abdominal-bloating-and-distention]]
- [[reflux-hypersensitivity|Reflux hypersensitivity]] — when chest pain is triggered by physiologic reflux events
- Panic disorder and other psychiatric causes
- Musculoskeletal chest wall pain

## Diagnostics

1. **Exclude cardiac disease first.** Esophageal workup should only proceed after confirmation — typically from the patient's cardiologist or primary care physician — that symptoms are unrelated to either concurrent or new coronary artery disease.
2. **8-week course of optimized antisecretory therapy** ([[proton-pump-inhibitors|proton pump inhibitors (PPIs)]] or [[potassium-competitive-acid-blockers|potassium-competitive acid blockers (PCABs)]]) — **simple and cost-effective** given the high prevalence of GERD within NCCP, and it determines whether GERD may be triggering the pain.
3. **[[upper-endoscopy|Upper endoscopy]]** — performed using indications similar to traditional GERD. Its role is **unclear but it has exclusionary value**. **Mucosal biopsies are considered to rule out eosinophilic esophagitis if the chest pain is meal-triggered.**
4. **[[ambulatory-reflux-monitoring|Ambulatory reflux testing]]** for PPI nonresponders when clinical suspicion for GERD remains high — **performed off acid suppression**.
   - **No reflux-monitoring method is superior to the others** in evaluating NCCP. pH monitoring, combined pH-impedance monitoring, or prolonged wireless pH monitoring beyond 24 hours can all be used, reflecting availability and the expertise of the interpreting physician.
5. **[[high-resolution-manometry|High-resolution manometry]]** — **most NCCP patients have normal motor function**, so HRM should be considered **once GERD has been comfortably ruled out**. Esophageal motor disorders represent the other important exclusion.

**Mechanism** — esophageal hypersensitivity from peripheral and/or central sensitization, altered visceral signal processing, and autonomic dysregulation. Balloon distention produces pain at lower thresholds; up to **75% of NCCP patients exhibit hypersensitivity to balloon distention**. Cerebral evoked potential studies show distinct latency patterns. **Minor motility abnormalities are unlikely to play a causal role**, although sustained longitudinal muscle contractions have been linked to chest pain, possibly through an ischemic mechanism — unproven.

## Therapeutics

Treatment primarily targets **neuromodulation of pain** ([[rome-v-2026-functional-esophageal-disorders]]).

| Drug class | Agents | Trial doses | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs) | imipramine, amitriptyline | imipramine **50 mg/d**; amitriptyline **10 mg/d** | up to **50% effectiveness above placebo**; amitriptyline 10 mg/d — 70.6% of patients responded |
| Serotonin norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs) | venlafaxine | **75 mg/d** | symptoms reduced 59% |
| Other | trazodone | **100–150 mg/d** | symptoms reduced 59% |
| Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) | sertraline, paroxetine | sertraline **50–200 mg/d**; paroxetine **10–75 mg/d** | **inconsistent results** |

- **TCAs, SNRIs and trazodone show up to 50% effectiveness above placebo; SSRIs show inconsistent results.** **Side effects or stigma may limit use of neuromodulators.**
- **Alternative agents** — gabapentin, pregabalin, dronabinol, theophylline (200 mg twice daily reduced symptoms 58% in NCCP) — have been explored but **require cautious use because of potential side effects**.
- **Behavioral interventions are complementary and carry minimal adverse effects** ([[brain-gut-behavioral-therapy]]):
  - **Gastrointestinal cognitive behavioral therapy (GI-CBT)** — a Cochrane review showed **reduced symptom frequency (relative risk 0.70; 95% confidence interval 0.53–0.92)**.
  - **Gut-directed hypnotherapy** and **biofeedback** have emerged as important complementary therapies.
  - Controlled trials confirm reductions in **severity and psychological comorbidities**.
  - **Diaphragmatic breathing** also offers symptomatic benefit.
