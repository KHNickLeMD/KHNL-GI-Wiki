A **manometric finding, not a diagnosis** — impaired esophagogastric junction (EGJ) relaxation with preserved (or only partially disordered) peristalsis, distinguishing it from [[achalasia]]. In isolation it should **not** justify any permanent intervention; it has many mimics and requires correlation with symptoms and confirmatory testing.

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

**Two separate bars must be cleared: a *manometric* diagnosis, then a *clinically relevant conclusive* diagnosis.** A manometric esophagogastric junction outflow obstruction (EGJOO) on its own is, in the words of the Chicago Classification v4.0 (CCv4.0), **always clinically inconclusive**.

**Bar 1 — manometric diagnosis of EGJOO** ([[chicago-classification-v4|CCv4.0]], Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE): Low / conditional). **All three** required on [[high-resolution-manometry|high-resolution manometry (HRM)]]:

| # | Criterion | Operative threshold |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Elevated **median integrated relaxation pressure (IRP)** in **both** the primary **and** secondary position (i.e. supine **and** upright) | Medtronic — supine **≥15 mmHg**, upright **≥12 mmHg**; Laborie/Diversatek — supine **≥22 mmHg**, upright **≥15 mmHg** |
| 2 | **≥20% of supine swallows with elevated intrabolus pressure** | **≥20 mmHg** by the isobaric contour tool, supine wet swallows, Medtronic (Moderate GRADE, **strong**) |
| 3 | **Evidence of peristalsis** (i.e. does not meet [[achalasia]] criteria) | Any appreciable peristalsis; 100% absent peristalsis (every swallow failed or premature) is achalasia, not EGJOO |

- **Why both positions:** appreciable peristalsis lowers the pre-test probability of true lower esophageal sphincter (LES) dysfunction, so EGJOO — unlike achalasia — needs an abnormal IRP in the primary **and** secondary position plus complementary testing to confirm.
- **Combination rule — any one abnormality alone is NOT EGJOO.** CCv4.0 calls each of these **inconclusive** for a manometric diagnosis (Low GRADE, **strong**): isolated elevated **supine** IRP; isolated elevated **upright** IRP; isolated elevated **supine intrabolus pressure**. It is the conjunction that makes the diagnosis.

> [!pitfall] A high IRP with no intrabolus pressurization is artifact
> An elevated IRP **in the absence** of intrabolus pressurization is likely an artifactual rise, not EGJOO ([[chicago-v4-2021-esophageal-dysmotility]]). Large hiatal/paraesophageal hernias and prior foregut surgery also put a study outside CCv4.0 altogether.

**Bar 2 — clinically relevant conclusive diagnosis of EGJOO** (Moderate GRADE, conditional). Requires **all three**:

1. A manometric diagnosis of EGJOO (Bar 1), **and**
2. **Clinically relevant symptoms** — defined by CCv4.0 as **dysphagia and/or non-cardiac chest pain**, **and**
3. **≥1 supportive investigation showing obstruction** — timed barium esophagram (TBE), **preferably with a tablet**, and/or [[flip-panometry|functional lumen imaging probe (FLIP)]] (thresholds under [[#Diagnostics]])

- Also useful but **not required**: outflow obstruction and esophageal pressurization during the rapid drink challenge (RDC), on a solid test meal (especially if temporally linked to symptoms), or abnormal EGJ function after pharmacologic provocation.

### Classification / Typing

Every EGJOO is reported **with its accompanying peristaltic pattern** (CCv4.0, Accepted Clinical Observation) — the pattern names the coexisting disorder and shapes therapy:

| Subtype | Accompanying peristalsis |
|---|---|
| EGJOO **with spastic features** | ≥20% premature (spastic) swallows — the features of type III [[achalasia]]; may represent an achalasia variant |
| EGJOO **with hypercontractile features** | Hypercontractile swallows alongside the elevated IRP and intrabolus pressurization ([[hypercontractile-esophagus]] pattern) |
| EGJOO **with [[ineffective-esophageal-motility\|ineffective motility]]** | Ineffective swallows |
| EGJOO **with no evidence of disordered peristalsis** | Normal contractile vigor |

### Severity Assessment

- **No graded severity scale exists** — CCv4.0 stratifies EGJOO by *diagnostic certainty* (inconclusive → manometric → clinically relevant conclusive), not by severity.
- The nearest severity surrogate is **objective obstruction on supportive testing**: retained barium on a tablet TBE and reduced EGJ distensibility on [[flip-panometry|FLIP]]. These, not the IRP value, are what justify escalating from observation to intervention.

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[dysphagia]].*

Nearly 10% of patients undergoing HRM show an EGJOO pattern and **more than one-third may be clinically irrelevant** — mechanical effects, opioid use, artifact. Exclude these before treating it as a primary motility disorder:

- **Mechanical / structural:** obstructive [[hiatal-hernia|hiatal hernia]], external compression, [[subepithelial-lesion|submucosal mass]], [[achalasia|early/evolving achalasia]]
- **Other:** [[obesity]], [[gerd|gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)]], opiate-induced esophageal dysfunction, manometric artifact
- **Related spastic disorders:** [[distal-esophageal-spasm|distal esophageal spasm]], [[hypercontractile-esophagus|hypercontractile esophagus]]

## Diagnostics

| Test | Role | Operative finding |
|---|---|---|
| **[[high-resolution-manometry\|HRM]]** | Makes (or excludes) the manometric diagnosis | The three Bar 1 criteria, supine **and** upright |
| **[[upper-endoscopy\|Upper endoscopy]]** | Excludes mechanical obstruction, mucosal disease, submucosal mass; retroflexed exam | Endoscopic or radiographic mechanical obstruction **precludes** CCv4.0 — do it before manometry |
| **Timed barium esophagram** | Supportive test for Bar 2; CCv4.0 prefers the **tablet** protocol, which elicits subtle narrowing a liquid-only study misses | Delayed emptying / EGJ narrowing. CCv4.0 sets no barium-column cut-off that defines an abnormal study |
| **[[flip-panometry\|FLIP]]** | Supportive test for Bar 2; also rules obstruction out | **Reduced EGJ opening (REO)** = EGJ distensibility index **<2.0 mm²/mmHg and** maximum EGJ diameter **<12 mm** → supports an EGJ outflow disorder. **Normal EGJ opening (NEO)** = **≥2.0 mm²/mmHg and ≥16 mm** → high negative predictive value against EGJOO ([[aga-2025-flip-esophageal\|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2025]] Best Practice Advice (BPA) 4) |
| **pH study** | Excludes GERD as the driver of the symptoms | — |

- Confirmatory testing (impedance planimetry, TBE, pH study) to exclude GERD and document symptomatic delayed emptying is **mandatory** before intervention ([[aga-2024-poem-advances|AGA 2024]] BPA 3).

## Therapeutics

- **First, exhaust less invasive options** — many cases are self-limited, artifactual, or explained by a mimic.
- **Medical therapy, [[botulinum-toxin|botulinum toxin]] injection, endoscopic dilation** — try before any myotomy (AGA 2024 BPA 3).

> [!pearl] A normal FLIP sends you away from the EGJ
> Of 17 patients with EGJOO on manometry alone **and** NEO on FLIP, none had a 5-cm barium column at 5 min on TBE, and 9 of 10 followed longitudinally did well on **conservative** treatment — once NEO is found, further testing adds little ([[aga-2025-flip-esophageal]]).

- **[[poem|per-oral endoscopic myotomy (POEM)]]:** evidence limited; consider **only case-by-case in very selected patients** after less invasive approaches are exhausted and symptomatic obstruction is confirmed. Long-term success for POEM in EGJOO ~**80–85%** (lower than for classic [[achalasia]] subtypes); studies limited by disease rarity ([[aga-2024-poem-advances|AGA 2024]] BPA 3).
- **LES obstruction meeting CCv4.0 EGJOO criteria must be addressed before [[antireflux-surgery|antireflux surgery (ARS)]]** (**86% agreement**, [[padova-2025-hrm-antireflux|Padova consensus]]) — evaluate with confirmatory testing first and act only if obstruction is definitively identified; the full pre-ARS motor-disorder framework is on [[hrm-antireflux-surgery]].
