## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

Functional dysphagia is **the sensation of food sticking or passing abnormally through the esophagus in the absence of structural, mucosal, or major motor abnormality** that could explain it. It is an esophageal disorder of gut–brain interaction (E-DGBI) ([[disorders-of-gut-brain-interaction]]) and **the least common of the five**.

**Rome V criteria (A5)** ([[rome-v-2026-functional-esophageal-disorders]]):

> Must include **all** of the following:
> 1. **Sense of solid and/or liquid foods sticking to, lodging in, or passing abnormally through the esophagus**
> 2. **Absence of evidence that esophageal mucosal or structural abnormality is the cause of the symptom** — a normal esophagram with a tablet/marshmallow, or a normal functional lumen imaging probe (FLIP) study, may be supportive
> 3. Absence of evidence that **[[gerd|gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)]]** (abnormal endoscopy ≥Los Angeles [LA] grade B esophagitis, or reflux burden by Lyon Consensus 2.0) **or nonreflux esophagitis** is the cause of the symptoms
> 4. **Absence of disorders of esophagogastric junction (EGJ) obstruction and/or disorders of esophageal peristalsis** — [[achalasia]], conclusive [[esophagogastric-junction-outflow-obstruction|EGJOO]], absent contractility, hypercontractile esophagus, distal esophageal spasm — **except ineffective esophageal motility (IEM)**, per [[chicago-classification-v4|Chicago Classification version 4.0]]. A **normal solid test meal during high-resolution manometry (HRM) may be supportive**.
>
> Criteria fulfilled for the past 3 months, symptom onset ≥6 months before diagnosis, frequency **at least once a week**.

> [!pearl] This is the one E-DGBI with no antisecretory-therapy requirement
> Functional chest pain, functional heartburn, reflux hypersensitivity and globus all require symptoms to persist despite **8 weeks of optimized antisecretory therapy**. **Functional dysphagia does not** ([[rome-v-2026-functional-esophageal-disorders]]).

### A shrinking diagnosis

- Early surveys estimated **7%–8%** of dysphagia was unexplained. A global Rome IV–based survey of nearly 55,000 individuals reported **3.2%** prevalence (3.5% female, 2.9% male) — but many of those cases likely included **oropharyngeal or structural causes not systematically excluded**.
- In a large tertiary referral series, **only 2.4% of 694 patients had persistent dysphagia despite negative testing** — and FLIP was not used in that cohort.
- Because FLIP identifies subtle EGJ or contractile abnormalities missed by standard testing, **the true prevalence is likely even lower**, and as FLIP is integrated earlier in the diagnostic pathway, the prevalence of **truly idiopathic functional dysphagia is anticipated to continue to decline**.

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[dysphagia]].*

- **Oropharyngeal dysphagia** — a thorough history must exclude it; videofluoroscopy is the test of choice when suspected
- [[globus|Globus]], xerostomia, odynophagia — symptom mimics
- [[eosinophilic-esophagitis|Eosinophilic esophagitis]] — a key exclusion; **biopsies may miss it** if taken on proton pump inhibitor (PPI) therapy or in fibro-stenotic phenotypes
- **Other mucosal disease without visible endoscopic findings** — lichen planus, pemphigus, pemphigoid, lymphocytic esophagitis
- [[gerd|GERD]] — may occasionally present solely with dysphagia
- Subtle strictures, rings/webs, **paraesophageal hernias** — may be missed on endoscopy; found on barium study
- [[achalasia]], conclusive [[esophagogastric-junction-outflow-obstruction|EGJOO]], absent contractility, hypercontractile esophagus, distal esophageal spasm — exclusionary major motor disorders
- **Opioid-induced esophageal dysfunction** — see below

> [!pitfall] Ask about opioids every time
> **Opioid use has emerged as a significant factor** contributing to esophageal dysmotility, esophageal outflow obstruction and heightened symptom reporting, and **should be routinely queried** during clinical evaluation ([[rome-v-2026-functional-esophageal-disorders]]).

## Diagnostics

A diagnosis of functional dysphagia can be made **only after comprehensive exclusion** of mucosal, structural and major motor abnormalities, using endoscopy with biopsy, barium studies, HRM, and potentially FLIP.

- **[[upper-endoscopy|Upper endoscopy]] with biopsy** — GERD and eosinophilic esophagitis are the key exclusions and are typically ruled out by a combination of antisecretory therapy and endoscopy with biopsy. **Mucosal biopsies are essential**, because lichen planus, pemphigus, pemphigoid and lymphocytic esophagitis can occur without visible endoscopic findings.
- **Barium studies with solid boluses** (tablet, cookie, marshmallow) — useful to detect **subtle strictures or obstructive lesions, including paraesophageal hernias, that may be missed on endoscopy**. Upright timed esophagograms and solid bolus testing help identify subtle strictures and EGJ obstruction.
- **Videofluoroscopy** — the test of choice when **oropharyngeal** dysphagia is suspected.
- **[[high-resolution-manometry|HRM]] per Chicago Classification version 4.0** — version 4.0 improved specificity by requiring adjunctive evidence for diagnosing EGJ outflow obstruction and peristaltic abnormalities. **Provocative maneuvers — multiple rapid swallows, test meals, rapid drink challenge — enhance detection of subtle motor dysfunction** but are complementary rather than required.
- **[[ambulatory-reflux-monitoring|Reflux monitoring off PPI therapy]]** may be needed to rule out GERD.

### FLIP panometry

[[flip-panometry|FLIP]] provides real-time assessment of EGJ opening, esophageal wall distensibility, secondary peristalsis, and contractile responses to distension — **detecting mechanical or functional outflow resistance not seen on manometry alone**.

- **17 of 34** symptomatic patients with normal HRM had abnormal FLIP responses in one study.
- **Up to 32% of 164** patients with normal HRM had abnormal FLIP patterns; in that cohort FLIP identified previously undetected EGJ obstruction or abnormal contractility in **nearly one-third**.
- A subset of patients previously classified as functional dysphagia under Rome IV actually had **EGJ abnormalities or borderline achalasia-like patterns** that would have been missed without FLIP.
- **FLIP is not mandatory for the diagnosis** — it is not universally available.
- **The Dallas Classification** recommends that in patients with **negative endoscopy and normal FLIP metrics**, a diagnosis of functional dysphagia is likely and **can be made earlier in the workup**.

**Mechanism** — heightened esophageal perception rather than structural abnormality. Mild bolus stasis and ineffective swallows occur in asymptomatic individuals, complicating the definition of physiologic vs pathologic transit. Major motor disorders correlate strongly with impaired bolus transit, whereas **minor disorders such as IEM, or even normal motility, may be seen in patients with significant dysphagia and normal objective findings** — upright high-resolution impedance manometry with 200 mL water showed dysphagia reported in **23%–25%** of those with normal motility or IEM despite no stasis or pressurization. Esophageal distensibility may be impaired despite normal bolus transit metrics, and nadir impedance values correlate with narrow luminal areas. **Hypervigilance, anxiety and fear of swallowing or choking** contribute to symptom severity regardless of whether organic abnormalities are present.

## Therapeutics

Functional dysphagia often follows a **benign, self-limited course**.

- **Mild cases — reassurance and simple strategies suffice:** upright eating, thorough chewing, liquid chasers.
- **A short course of acid suppression** may be helpful, particularly if reflux is suspected.
- **Neuromodulators** — tricyclic antidepressants and others may relieve symptoms in selected patients, **although not definitively proven**.
- **Empiric bougie dilation to 50 to 54 Fr has shown benefit in 68% to 85% of patients** with unexplained food dysphagia, likely by addressing subtle **proximal** esophageal abnormalities missed on routine testing.
  - **Unlike balloon dilation at the EGJ, which has shown limited utility** in this setting.
  - With FLIP panometry, dilation can now be **targeted more precisely** to areas of subtle obstruction or wall stiffness — such as reduced EGJ distensibility or abnormal esophageal compliance — improving both diagnostic accuracy and therapeutic outcomes. See [[esophageal-dilation]].
- **[[brain-gut-behavioral-therapy|Gastrointestinal cognitive behavioral therapy (GI-CBT)]]** — psychoeducation, diaphragmatic breathing, fear-exposure — may be effective in patients with **maladaptive beliefs, symptom-specific anxiety, or other psychological comorbidity**. Gut-directed hypnotherapy may offer additional benefit by reducing visceral hypersensitivity and hypervigilance.
