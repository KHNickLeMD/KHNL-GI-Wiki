## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

Functional dysphagia is **the sensation of food sticking or passing abnormally through the esophagus in the absence of structural, mucosal, or major motor abnormality** that could explain it. It is an esophageal disorder of gut–brain interaction (E-DGBI) ([[disorders-of-gut-brain-interaction]]) and **the least common of the five**.

**Rome V criteria (A5)** ([[rome-v-2026-functional-esophageal-disorders]]):

> Must include **all** of the following:
> 1. **Sense of solid and/or liquid foods sticking to, lodging in, or passing abnormally through the esophagus**
> 2. **Absence of evidence that esophageal mucosal or structural abnormality is the cause of the symptom** — a normal esophagram with a tablet/marshmallow, or a normal functional lumen imaging probe (FLIP) study, may be supportive
> 3. Absence of evidence that **[[gerd|gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)]]** (abnormal endoscopy ≥Los Angeles [LA] grade B esophagitis, or reflux burden by Lyon Consensus 2.0) **or nonreflux esophagitis** is the cause of the symptoms
> 4. **Absence of disorders of esophagogastric junction (EGJ) obstruction and/or disorders of esophageal peristalsis** — [[achalasia]], conclusive [[esophagogastric-junction-outflow-obstruction|EGJOO]], absent contractility, [[hypercontractile-esophagus|hypercontractile esophagus]], [[distal-esophageal-spasm|distal esophageal spasm]] — **except [[ineffective-esophageal-motility|ineffective esophageal motility (IEM)]]**, per [[chicago-classification-v4|Chicago Classification version 4.0]]. A **normal solid test meal during high-resolution manometry (HRM) may be supportive**.
>
> Criteria fulfilled for the past 3 months, symptom onset ≥6 months before diagnosis, frequency **at least once a week**.

**Where the exclusion thresholds live:** reflux-burden cut-offs behind criterion 3 (acid exposure time bands, reflux-episode counts, mean nocturnal baseline impedance, Lyon Consensus 2.0) are on [[ambulatory-reflux-monitoring]]; the manometric criteria for each exclusionary diagnosis in criterion 4 are on [[chicago-classification-v4]]. **IEM does not exclude the diagnosis** — it is not consistently associated with symptom generation or bolus stasis.

> [!pearl] This is the one E-DGBI with no antisecretory-therapy requirement
> Functional chest pain, functional heartburn, reflux hypersensitivity and globus all require symptoms to persist despite **8 weeks of optimized antisecretory therapy**. **Functional dysphagia does not** ([[rome-v-2026-functional-esophageal-disorders]]).

### Epidemiology

**A shrinking diagnosis** — the true prevalence remains uncertain, and falls as testing improves.

- Early surveys estimated **7%–8%** of dysphagia was unexplained. A global Rome IV–based survey of nearly 55,000 individuals reported **3.2%** prevalence (3.5% female, 2.9% male) — but many of those cases likely included **oropharyngeal or structural causes not systematically excluded**.
- In a large tertiary referral series, **only 2.4% of 694 patients had persistent dysphagia despite negative testing** — and FLIP was not used in that cohort.
- Because FLIP identifies subtle EGJ or contractile abnormalities missed by standard testing, **the true prevalence is likely even lower**, and as FLIP is integrated earlier in the diagnostic pathway, the prevalence of **truly idiopathic functional dysphagia is anticipated to continue to decline**.

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[dysphagia]].*

- **Oropharyngeal dysphagia** — a thorough history must exclude it; videofluoroscopy is the test of choice when suspected
- [[globus|Globus]], xerostomia, odynophagia — symptom mimics
- [[eosinophilic-esophagitis|Eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE)]] — a key exclusion; **biopsies may miss it** if taken on proton pump inhibitor (PPI) therapy or in fibro-stenotic phenotypes
- **Other mucosal disease without visible endoscopic findings** — lichen planus, pemphigus, pemphigoid, [[lymphocytic-esophagitis|lymphocytic esophagitis]]
- [[gerd|GERD]] — may occasionally present solely with dysphagia, or cause a subtle stricture
- Subtle [[esophageal-stricture|strictures]], rings/webs, **[[hiatal-hernia|paraesophageal hernias]]** — may be missed on endoscopy; found on barium study
- [[achalasia]], conclusive [[esophagogastric-junction-outflow-obstruction|EGJOO]], absent contractility, hypercontractile esophagus, distal esophageal spasm — exclusionary major motor disorders
- **Opioid-induced esophageal dysfunction** — see below

> [!pitfall] Ask about opioids every time
> **Opioid use has emerged as a significant factor** contributing to esophageal dysmotility, esophageal outflow obstruction and heightened symptom reporting, and **should be routinely queried** during clinical evaluation ([[rome-v-2026-functional-esophageal-disorders]]).

## Diagnostics

A diagnosis of functional dysphagia can be made **only after comprehensive exclusion** of mucosal, structural and major motor abnormalities.

| Test | What it contributes |
|---|---|
| [[upper-endoscopy\|Upper endoscopy]] **with mucosal biopsies** | GERD and EoE are the key exclusions — ruled out by antisecretory therapy plus endoscopy with biopsy. **Biopsies are essential:** lichen planus, pemphigus, pemphigoid and lymphocytic esophagitis occur without visible endoscopic findings |
| **Barium study with a solid bolus** (tablet, cookie, marshmallow); upright timed esophagram | Subtle strictures, obstructive lesions and paraesophageal hernias missed at endoscopy; EGJ obstruction |
| **Videofluoroscopy** | Test of choice when **oropharyngeal** dysphagia is suspected |
| [[high-resolution-manometry\|HRM]] per Chicago Classification version 4.0 | Excludes the major motor disorders; version 4.0 improved specificity by requiring adjunctive evidence for EGJ outflow obstruction and peristaltic abnormalities. **Provocative maneuvers** — multiple rapid swallows, test meals, rapid drink challenge — enhance detection of subtle motor dysfunction, but are **complementary rather than required** |
| [[ambulatory-reflux-monitoring\|Reflux monitoring off PPI therapy]] | May be needed to rule out GERD |
| [[flip-panometry\|FLIP]] | Mechanical or functional outflow resistance not seen on manometry — see below. **Not mandatory** (not available everywhere) |

*Tests used to exclude organic disease before diagnosing functional dysphagia*

### FLIP panometry

FLIP gives real-time assessment of EGJ opening, esophageal wall distensibility, secondary peristalsis and contractile response to distension.

- **Yield when HRM is normal:** abnormal FLIP responses in **17 of 34** symptomatic patients in one study; **up to 32% (nearly one-third) of 164** patients in another — previously undetected EGJ obstruction or abnormal contractility.
- A subset previously classified as functional dysphagia under Rome IV actually had **EGJ abnormalities or borderline achalasia-like patterns** that would have been missed without FLIP.
- **A normal FLIP** — adequate EGJ opening, distensibility and peristaltic response — **rules out mechanical causes and supports a perceptual origin** for the symptom.
- **The Dallas Classification** recommends that with **negative endoscopy and normal FLIP metrics** (metric cut-offs on [[flip-panometry]]), functional dysphagia is likely and **can be diagnosed earlier in the workup**.

**Mechanism** — heightened esophageal perception rather than structural abnormality:

- Mild bolus stasis and ineffective swallows occur in **asymptomatic** individuals, and dry solid boluses may need multiple swallows even in health — physiologic vs pathologic transit is hard to define.
- Major motor disorders correlate strongly with impaired bolus transit; **normal motility or IEM may be seen with significant dysphagia and normal objective findings** — on upright high-resolution impedance manometry with 200 mL water, dysphagia was reported by **23%–25%** of those with normal motility or IEM despite no stasis or pressurization.
- **Distensibility may be impaired despite normal bolus transit metrics**; nadir impedance during provocative swallows correlates with narrow luminal areas, i.e. less esophageal stretch underlying perceived bolus arrest.
- **Hypervigilance, anxiety and fear of swallowing or choking** contribute to symptom severity regardless of whether organic abnormalities are present; rates of depression and somatization are higher, and acute stress can transiently disrupt esophageal motility.

## Therapeutics

Functional dysphagia often follows a **benign, self-limited course**.

- **Mild cases — reassurance and simple strategies suffice:** upright eating, thorough chewing, liquid chasers.
- **A short course of acid suppression** may be helpful, particularly if reflux is suspected. Rome V does not specify a duration.
- **Neuromodulators** — tricyclic antidepressants and others may relieve symptoms in selected patients, **although not definitively proven**. None of the randomized neuromodulator trials Rome V tabulates enrolled functional dysphagia patients, so no dysphagia-specific agent or dose is given.
- **Empiric bougie dilation to 50 to 54 Fr has shown benefit in 68% to 85% of patients** with unexplained food dysphagia, likely by addressing subtle **proximal** esophageal abnormalities missed on routine testing.
  - **Unlike balloon dilation at the EGJ, which has shown limited utility** in this setting.
  - With FLIP panometry, dilation can now be **targeted more precisely** to areas of subtle obstruction or wall stiffness — such as reduced EGJ distensibility or abnormal esophageal compliance — improving both diagnostic accuracy and therapeutic outcomes. See [[esophageal-dilation]].
- **[[brain-gut-behavioral-therapy|Gastrointestinal cognitive behavioral therapy (GI-CBT)]]** — psychoeducation, diaphragmatic breathing, fear-exposure — may be effective in patients with **maladaptive beliefs, symptom-specific anxiety, or other psychological comorbidity**. Gut-directed hypnotherapy may offer additional benefit by reducing visceral hypersensitivity and hypervigilance.
