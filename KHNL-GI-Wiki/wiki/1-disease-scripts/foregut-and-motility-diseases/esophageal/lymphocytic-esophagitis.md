Lymphocytic esophagitis (LyE) is a histologically defined esophageal disorder: a **dense peripapillary lymphocytic infiltrate with peripapillary spongiosis of the esophageal epithelium, without significant eosinophilic or neutrophilic infiltrates**. Etiology is unknown and its status as a distinct clinical entity is unclear — lymphocytic recruitment seems to occur in response to a variety of scenarios of esophageal injury ([[aga-2024-esophageal-immunity-infection]]).

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Who to suspect:** a woman with dysphagia whose endoscopy mimics eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE). Rare — **0.1%** of adults undergoing [[upper-endoscopy|esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD)]] with esophageal biopsy, in a retrospective review of **129,252** adults.
- **Histologic diagnosis** on esophageal biopsy (peripapillary lymphocytosis + spongiosis, few/no eosinophils or neutrophils).
- **Diagnosis of exclusion** — first rule out **EoE-like disease** (biopsy protocol under [[#Diagnostics]]) and [[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease]], which can present similarly histopathologically.
- **Endoscopy does not discriminate.** LyE is a scarring disease that can produce rings and [[esophageal-stricture|strictures]] **in any part of the esophagus**, so biopsies are what separate it from EoE and the other endoscopic mimics ([[asge-2026-eoe-endoscopic-management-part-2]]).

**LyE vs EoE** — both from [[aga-2024-esophageal-immunity-infection]]:

| | LyE | [[eosinophilic-esophagitis\|EoE]] |
|---|---|---|
| Prevalence on esophageal biopsy | **0.1%** | **2.8%** |
| Typical patient | Mostly **women above age 60**; Table 1 clinical clue is **female >50 y with dysphagia** | Atopic dermatitis, asthma, or significant food allergy/atopy; children and adults |
| Dysphagia as primary symptom | ~**2/3** | Similar frequency |
| Endoscopy | Abnormal in ~**70–75%** (single-centre series, n=81): **rings, esophagitis, stricture**, in that order of frequency; can be normal; read as EoE in **1/3** of LyE cases | Edema, rings, exudates, linear furrows, strictures; can appear normal |
| Histology | Dense peripapillary lymphocytic infiltrate + spongiosis, no significant eosinophils/neutrophils | Excess esophageal eosinophils without another cause |

### Severity Assessment

- **No severity score or grading system for LyE is given** by either source.

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[dysphagia]].*

- [[eosinophilic-esophagitis]] — the chief mimic, and must be excluded before LyE is diagnosed
- [[crohns-disease|Esophageal Crohn's disease]]
- [[infectious-esophagitis]]
- [[gerd|Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)]] / medication-induced ulceration (e.g. doxycycline)
- [[esophageal-dysfunction-systemic-disease|Immune-mediated/dermatologic esophageal disease]] (lichen planus)

## Diagnostics

- **EGD with esophageal biopsies for routine hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining**, obtained **off [[proton-pump-inhibitors|proton pump inhibitor (PPI)]] therapy for at least 2–4 weeks** — otherwise EoE-like disease is masked and LyE cannot be diagnosed.
- Histopathology confirms the peripapillary lymphocytic pattern.

*See Figure 2, panel H in [[asge-2026-eoe-endoscopic-management-part-2]] for an endoscopic example.*

## Therapeutics

- **Consider treating the lymphocytic inflammation with PPI therapy or swallowed topical corticosteroids, and [[esophageal-dilation|esophageal dilation]] as needed** (Best Practice Advice [BPA] 5).
- Topical corticosteroids are **not well studied** in LyE. No dose or duration for either drug in LyE is given.
- Elimination diets, budesonide oral suspension, and dupilumab are EoE therapies, not LyE therapies — see [[eosinophilic-esophagitis]].
