Esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC) is a cancer of the distal esophagus/esophagogastric junction (EGJ) arising through the metaplasia–dysplasia–carcinoma sequence from [[barretts-esophagus|Barrett's esophagus (BE)]] (intestinal metaplasia → low-grade dysplasia [LGD] → high-grade dysplasia [HGD] → intramucosal carcinoma [IMC] → invasive EAC) — the only known precursor. Its incidence is among the fastest-rising in Western populations and overall 5-year survival is <20%, falling to <5% once there is distant disease — which **~40%** of patients already have at diagnosis ([[asge-2019-barretts-screening-surveillance]]). **Depth of invasion is the pivotal variable:** mucosal disease (HGD/T1a) carries very low lymph node (LN)-metastasis risk (~0%–2%) and is treated with [[endoscopic-eradication-therapy|endoscopic eradication therapy (EET)]], whereas deep submucosal (T1b sm2–3) invasion raises LN-metastasis risk to ≥20% and generally requires esophagectomy.

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- Detected either on **[[barretts-esophagus|Barrett's surveillance]]** (early lesion, often subtle or endoscopically invisible) or symptomatically — solid-food dysphagia, weight loss, odynophagia, [[iron-deficiency-anemia|iron-deficiency anemia]] (alarm features → prompt [[upper-endoscopy|esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD)]] with biopsy).
- Diagnosis is histologic. **[[endoscopic-mucosal-resection|Endoscopic mucosal resection]] (EMR) of any visible lesion is the local staging step** — it yields a deep specimen (to muscularis mucosa/submucosa), changing the pathologic diagnosis in **39%** (95% confidence interval [CI] 34%–45%) of patients, mostly **upstaging** ([[asge-2018-barretts-eet]]) — and changing **management in 30%–40%** ([[acg-2022-barretts|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2022]]; American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2018 notes the management-change outcome was *not* consistently reported in the studies it pooled). Resect all visible lesions (nodularity, ulceration, plaque, depression, discoloration) "no matter how subtle."
- **Suspected T1 EAC → refer for consideration of EET**. Conversely, **obstructive or ulcerated lesions are unlikely to represent T1 disease and can forego endoscopic resection** (ungraded implementation points, [[aga-2024-barretts-eet|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2024]]).

### Severity Assessment (Depth of Invasion → LN-metastasis risk)

The central treatment decision follows depth of invasion and adverse histology, because these determine lymph-node-metastasis risk. **The T category is what is being conditioned on, so its definition is given here** (American Joint Committee on Cancer [AJCC] 8th ed. via [[nccn-2026-esophageal-egj-cancer]]); the full T2–T4 / N / M table and the stage groups live on [[esophageal-cancer]].

| Depth (T category) | What the pathologist must see | LN-metastasis risk | Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tis** (HGD) | Malignant cells confined to the epithelium by the basement membrane | **0%** | [[endoscopic-eradication-therapy\|EET]] |
| **T1a** (IMC) | Invades **lamina propria or muscularis mucosae** | **~0%–2%** | EET **preferred over esophagectomy** (strong rec) |
| **T1b sm1** | **Superficial** submucosa — upper third, **<500 µm** | **6%** ([[aga-2019-esd-t1b-esophageal-cancer\|AGA 2019]]) | EET only if **all four** low-risk features hold (below) |
| **T1b sm2–3** | **Mid-to-deep** submucosa, **>500 µm** | **≥20%** (20%–30%) | Esophagectomy generally preferred |

- **Adverse histology overrides depth.** Independent of sm level, **poorly differentiated** cancer or **lymphovascular invasion** ("lymphatic or vascular infiltration" in the document's wording) carries the same ≥20% LN-metastasis risk and makes esophagectomy the treatment of choice ([[asge-2018-barretts-eet]]). The µm/sm depth thresholds themselves come from [[acg-2022-barretts|ACG 2022]], not from ASGE 2018.

**The sm1 exception — all four criteria must hold** for EET to be a viable alternative to esophagectomy in T1b EAC ([[acg-2022-barretts]]):

| Criterion | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Depth** | **sm1** — invasion into the upper third of the submucosa, **<500 µm** |
| **Deep (vertical) margin** | **Negative** |
| **Differentiation** | **Well or moderately** differentiated |
| **Lymphovascular invasion** | **Absent** |

- With all four, post-EET LN-metastasis risk **appears lower than the operative mortality of esophagectomy**; miss any one and the patient goes to surgery. ACG 2022 frames this as an **expanding** role for EET in superficial submucosal invasion supported by observational data.
- **AGA 2024's favorable-prognosis set is nearly the same but requires mucosal depth** — negative deep margin, **T1a depth**, moderate-or-well differentiation, no lymphovascular invasion — the profile that may be cured by endoscopic resection when EET is carried through to complete eradication with ongoing surveillance ([[aga-2024-barretts-eet]]). It is ACG 2022 that extends the option to sm1.
- **Size is an independent nodal-risk factor**, so the ideal candidate for endoscopic treatment alone is **well-differentiated, <2 cm, sm1, no lymphovascular invasion** ([[aga-2019-esd-t1b-esophageal-cancer|AGA 2019]]). Nodal risk by depth, size and histology: [[esophageal-cancer#T1b Substaging and Nodal Risk]].

> [!pitfall] Duplicated muscularis mucosae overstages Barrett's neoplasia
> The muscularis mucosae is often duplicated or trifurcated in [[barretts-esophagus|Barrett's]], so [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]] and EMR specimens read deeper than the tumor is.
> Tumor invading the space *between* the duplicated layers carries the nodal risk of mucosa-confined disease → **classified T1a, not T1b** (99 esophagectomies, 41 with duplication — [[aga-2019-esd-t1b-esophageal-cancer]]).

- **The alternative is not benign.** Esophagectomy operative mortality is **~2%** with high morbidity even at high-volume centers ([[asge-2018-barretts-eet]]); a high-volume series of T1a disease reported **4%** operative mortality and **34%** postoperative complications (anastomotic leak or stricture, cardiopulmonary complications, feeding-jejunostomy leak), and most survivors live with reflux through an absent lower esophageal sphincter ([[aga-2019-esd-t1b-esophageal-cancer]]).

### Classification / Typing

- **Histology:** adenocarcinoma (distal esophagus/EGJ; [[barretts-esophagus|Barrett's]]-associated). For the two-histology framework (adenocarcinoma vs squamous cell) and EGJ/Siewert considerations, see [[esophageal-cancer]].
- **Visible-lesion morphology** graded by the [[polypectomy|Paris classification]] — **0-I and 0-IIc** lesions were more likely to invade the submucosa, and **deep ulceration (0-III)** likely reflects deep submucosal invasion, making such lesions **less optimal for endoscopic resection**. These correlations are prognostic but **imperfect and inadequate to base management on in the absence of tissue sampling** ([[acg-2022-barretts]]). Subtype criteria on that page; BE-specific application on [[barretts-esophagus]].
- **Dysplasia grading** and precursor detail live on [[barretts-esophagus]].

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[dysphagia]].*

- [[esophageal-cancer|Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma]] — mid/upper esophagus; distinct risk factors (smoking, alcohol, [[achalasia]]).
- [[barretts-esophagus|Barrett's]] with HGD (vs invasive EAC — the depth distinction is made by EMR histology, not imaging).
- [[gerd|Peptic/benign stricture]], [[eosinophilic-esophagitis|eosinophilic esophagitis]].
- Esophageal [[gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor|gastrointestinal stromal tumor (GIST)]] / other [[subepithelial-lesion|subepithelial lesions]], and extrinsic compression.

## Diagnostics

- **EGD + biopsy** establishes histology; **EMR/[[endoscopic-submucosal-dissection|endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD)]] of the visible lesion** is the definitive local (T) staging tool — superior to imaging.
- **EUS:** [[asge-2019-barretts-screening-surveillance|ASGE 2019]] Rec 5 — in BE patients with **HGD/IMC or nodules**, **recommend against** routine EUS to separate mucosal from submucosal disease (**strong, moderate quality**). Pooled performance at the T1a-vs-T1b level (11 studies): **sensitivity 41%** (95% CI 35%–48%), **specificity 89%** (86%–91%), **accuracy 75%** — about **one third of EUS-positive results are false positives**, so resection histology is the staging step. [[aga-2024-barretts-eet|AGA 2024]] likewise makes **endoscopic resection the test of choice over EUS** both for separating EAC from HGD and for staging depth in early cancer. EUS retains a role in confirmed EAC for accurate staging of advanced (T1b+) disease and nodal assessment/fine-needle aspiration (FNA).
- **Computed tomography (CT) chest/abdomen + positron emission tomography (PET)/CT** for T1b and beyond, to detect distant disease ([[nccn-2026-esophageal-egj-cancer]]).
- **Biomarkers** (HER2/ERBB2, PD-L1 combined positive score [CPS], microsatellite instability-high [MSI-H]/mismatch repair deficient [dMMR]) on advanced/metastatic tumors to direct systemic therapy — see [[esophageal-cancer]].

## Therapeutics

Stage-directed; depth of invasion is the pivot.

- **Tis (HGD) / T1a (IMC), no high-risk features → [[endoscopic-eradication-therapy|EET]].** EMR of the visible lesion plus [[radiofrequency-ablation|ablation]] of the residual flat [[barretts-esophagus|Barrett's]] segment. EET over esophagectomy is a **strong** recommendation given LN-metastasis risk ~0%–2%, with fewer major adverse events than surgery; >95% of post-EET recurrences are successfully re-treated endoscopically ([[asge-2018-barretts-eet]]). Full technique, outcomes, and adverse-event data: [[endoscopic-eradication-therapy]] and [[barretts-esophagus]]. **Choosing EMR vs ESD** for the visible lesion is size-driven (ESD over EMR for well-differentiated nonulcerated EAC **>20 mm**; either technique at ≤20 mm) — table on [[esophageal-cancer]].
- **T1b sm1 (<500 µm) meeting all four low-risk criteria → EET is a viable alternative** to surgery ([[acg-2022-barretts]]); criteria in [[#Severity Assessment (Depth of Invasion → LN-metastasis risk)]].
- **T1b sm2–3 (>500 µm), poorly differentiated, or lymphovascular invasion → esophagectomy** — any one is sufficient (LN-metastasis risk ≥20%; operative mortality ~2%) ([[asge-2018-barretts-eet]]).
  - **Poor surgical candidate →** discuss at a **multidisciplinary conference** and consider alternatives such as **adjuvant chemoradiation** ([[acg-2022-barretts]]).
  - Same route when the resection specimen meets **none of the favorable-prognosis criteria**, or when EUS or cross-sectional imaging suggests **metastases**: multidisciplinary consultation for esophagectomy, chemotherapy and/or radiation **according to stage and functional status** ([[aga-2024-barretts-eet]]). Endoscopic resection followed by chemoradiation is the documented fallback for patients who decline or are unfit for surgery ([[aga-2019-esd-t1b-esophageal-cancer]]; series on [[esophageal-cancer]]).
- **Beyond T1 (locally advanced or metastatic) → stage- and biomarker-directed oncologic therapy** — perioperative chemotherapy or chemoradiation then esophagectomy, and HER2-, PD-L1- and MSI-H/dMMR-directed systemic therapy. Regimens, CPS thresholds and the treatment algorithm: [[esophageal-cancer]] ([[nccn-2026-esophageal-egj-cancer]]).
