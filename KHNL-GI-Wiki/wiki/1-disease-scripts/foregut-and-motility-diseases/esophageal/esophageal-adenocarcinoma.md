Esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC) is a cancer of the distal esophagus/EGJ arising through the metaplasia–dysplasia–carcinoma sequence from [[barretts-esophagus|Barrett's esophagus]] (intestinal metaplasia → LGD → HGD → intramucosal carcinoma → invasive EAC) — the only known precursor. Its incidence is among the fastest-rising in Western populations and overall 5-year survival is <20% (~40% present with distant disease). **Depth of invasion is the pivotal variable:** mucosal disease (HGD/T1a) carries very low lymph-node-metastasis risk (~0%–2%) and is treated with [[endoscopic-eradication-therapy|endoscopic eradication therapy]], whereas deep submucosal (T1b sm2–3) invasion raises LN-metastasis risk to ≥20% and generally requires esophagectomy.

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- Detected either on **[[barretts-esophagus|Barrett's surveillance]]** (early lesion, often subtle or endoscopically invisible) or symptomatically — solid-food dysphagia, weight loss, odynophagia, [[iron-deficiency-anemia|iron-deficiency anemia]] (alarm features → prompt [[upper-endoscopy|EGD]] with biopsy).
- Diagnosis is histologic. **[[endoscopic-mucosal-resection|Endoscopic mucosal resection]] (EMR) of any visible lesion is the local staging step** — it yields a deep specimen (to muscularis mucosa/submucosa), changing the pathologic diagnosis in **39%** (95% CI 34%–45%) of patients, mostly **upstaging** ([[asge-2018-barretts-eet]]) — and changing **management in 30%–40%** ([[acg-2022-barretts|ACG 2022]]; ASGE 2018 notes the management-change outcome was *not* consistently reported in the studies it pooled). Resect all visible lesions (nodularity, ulceration, plaque, depression, discoloration) "no matter how subtle."

### Severity Assessment (Depth of Invasion → LN-metastasis risk)

The central treatment decision follows depth of invasion and adverse histology, because these determine lymph-node-metastasis risk. **The T category is what is being conditioned on, so its definition is given here** (AJCC 8th ed. via [[nccn-2026-esophageal-egj-cancer]]); the full T2–T4 / N / M table and the stage groups live on [[esophageal-cancer]].

| Depth (T category) | What the pathologist must see | LN-metastasis risk | Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tis** (HGD) | Malignant cells confined to the epithelium by the basement membrane | **0%** | [[endoscopic-eradication-therapy\|EET]] |
| **T1a** (intramucosal carcinoma, IMC) | Invades **lamina propria or muscularis mucosae** | **~0%–2%** | EET **preferred over esophagectomy** (strong rec) |
| **T1b sm1** | **Superficial** submucosa — upper third, **<500 µm** | Low (not quantified in the ingested sources) | EET only if **all four** low-risk features hold (below) |
| **T1b sm2–3** | **Mid-to-deep** submucosa, **>500 µm** | **≥20%** (20%–30%) | Esophagectomy generally preferred |

- **Adverse histology overrides depth.** Independent of sm level, **poorly differentiated** cancer or **lymphovascular invasion** carries the same ≥20% LN-metastasis risk and makes esophagectomy the treatment of choice ([[asge-2018-barretts-eet]]). The µm/sm depth thresholds themselves come from [[acg-2022-barretts|ACG 2022]], not from ASGE 2018.
- **The sm1 exception — all four must hold** for EET to be a viable alternative to surgery: sm1 depth (**<500 µm**), **negative deep (vertical) margin**, **well or moderately differentiated**, and **no lymphovascular invasion** ([[acg-2022-barretts]]). With all four, post-EET LN-metastasis risk appears lower than the mortality of esophagectomy; miss any one and the patient goes to surgery. Criteria table: [[barretts-esophagus]].
- Esophagectomy operative mortality **~2%**, with high morbidity even at high-volume centers ([[asge-2018-barretts-eet]]).

### Classification / Typing

- **Histology:** adenocarcinoma (distal esophagus/EGJ; [[barretts-esophagus|Barrett's]]-associated). For the two-histology framework (adenocarcinoma vs squamous cell) and EGJ/Siewert considerations, see [[esophageal-cancer]].
- **Visible-lesion morphology** graded by the [[polypectomy|Paris classification]] (0-I and 0-IIc carry higher submucosal-invasion rates; 0-III suggests deep invasion) — subtype criteria on that page; BE-specific application on [[barretts-esophagus]].
- **Dysplasia grading** and precursor detail live on [[barretts-esophagus]].

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[dysphagia]].*

- [[esophageal-cancer|Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma]] — mid/upper esophagus; distinct risk factors (smoking, alcohol, [[achalasia]]).
- [[barretts-esophagus|Barrett's]] with HGD (vs invasive EAC — the depth distinction is made by EMR histology, not imaging).
- [[gerd|Peptic/benign stricture]], [[eosinophilic-esophagitis|eosinophilic esophagitis]].
- Esophageal [[gastrointestinal-stromal-tumor|GIST]] / other [[subepithelial-lesion|subepithelial lesions]], and extrinsic compression.

## Diagnostics

- **EGD + biopsy** establishes histology; **EMR/[[endoscopic-submucosal-dissection|ESD]] of the visible lesion** is the definitive local (T) staging tool — superior to imaging.
- **[[endoscopic-ultrasound|EUS]]:** [[asge-2019-barretts-screening-surveillance|ASGE 2019]] Rec 5 — in BE patients with **HGD/IMC or nodules**, **recommend against** routine EUS to separate mucosal from submucosal disease (**strong, moderate quality**). Pooled performance at the T1a-vs-T1b level (11 studies): **sensitivity 41%** (95% CI 35%–48%), **specificity 89%** (86%–91%), **accuracy 75%** — about **one third of EUS-positive results are false positives**, so resection histology is the staging step. EUS retains a role in confirmed EAC for accurate staging of advanced (T1b+) disease and nodal assessment/FNA.
- **CT chest/abdomen + PET/CT** for T1b and beyond, to detect distant disease ([[nccn-2026-esophageal-egj-cancer]]).
- **Biomarkers** (HER2/ERBB2, PD-L1 CPS, MSI-H/dMMR) on advanced/metastatic tumors to direct systemic therapy — see [[esophageal-cancer]].

## Therapeutics

Stage-directed; depth of invasion is the pivot.

- **Tis (HGD) / T1a (IMC), no high-risk features → [[endoscopic-eradication-therapy|EET]].** EMR of the visible lesion plus [[radiofrequency-ablation|ablation]] of the residual flat [[barretts-esophagus|Barrett's]] segment. EET over esophagectomy is a **strong** recommendation given LN-metastasis risk ~0%–2%, with fewer major adverse events than surgery; >95% of post-EET recurrences are successfully re-treated endoscopically ([[asge-2018-barretts-eet]]). Full technique, outcomes, and adverse-event data: [[endoscopic-eradication-therapy]] and [[barretts-esophagus]]. **Choosing EMR vs ESD** for the visible lesion is size-driven (ESD over EMR for well-differentiated nonulcerated EAC **>20 mm**; either technique at ≤20 mm) — table on [[esophageal-cancer]].
- **T1b sm1 (<500 µm) meeting all four low-risk criteria → EET is a viable alternative** to surgery ([[acg-2022-barretts]]); criteria in [[#Severity Assessment (Depth of Invasion → LN-metastasis risk)]].
- **T1b sm2–3 (>500 µm), poorly differentiated, or lymphovascular invasion → esophagectomy** — any one is sufficient (LN-metastasis risk ≥20%; operative mortality ~2%) ([[asge-2018-barretts-eet]]).
- **Locally advanced resectable → oncologic pathway** ([[esophageal-cancer]]; [[nccn-2026-esophageal-egj-cancer]]): perioperative **FLOT** (± durvalumab in selected tumors) followed by esophagectomy; adjuvant **nivolumab** if residual disease after neoadjuvant chemoradiation + R0 resection (CheckMate-577).
- **Metastatic → biomarker-directed systemic therapy:** HER2-overexpressing → first-line trastuzumab (later-line trastuzumab deruxtecan); PD-L1 CPS → add nivolumab or pembrolizumab; MSI-H/dMMR → immunotherapy. Detail on [[esophageal-cancer]].
