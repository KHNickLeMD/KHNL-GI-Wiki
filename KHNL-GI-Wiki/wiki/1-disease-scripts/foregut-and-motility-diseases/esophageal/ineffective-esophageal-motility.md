*A disorder of peristalsis under [[chicago-classification-v4|Chicago Classification v4.0 (CCv4.0)]]. CCv4.0 absorbed fragmented peristalsis into ineffective esophageal motility (IEM), made the IEM threshold more stringent, and no longer separates major from minor motility disorders.*

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

IEM is a manometric diagnosis on [[high-resolution-manometry|high-resolution manometry (HRM)]]. CCv4.0 requires **both**:

1. **Normal median integrated relaxation pressure (IRP)** — i.e. no esophagogastric junction (EGJ) outflow disorder. Abnormal is system- and position-specific: **Medtronic supine ≥15 / upright ≥12 mmHg; Laborie/Diversatek supine ≥22 / upright ≥15 mmHg** (full table on [[chicago-classification-v4]]).
2. **Enough ineffective swallows** — bands below.

**Which swallows count:** the diagnosis is made on the **10 wet swallows done in the primary position** (supine or upright, whichever the lab starts in). Swallows in the secondary position and on provocation are supportive only — concordance strengthens the diagnosis, discordance should prompt reconsideration and further supportive testing. By contrast, absent contractility and [[esophagogastric-junction-outflow-obstruction|EGJ outflow obstruction (EGJOO)]] require a qualifying IRP in **both** positions.

Grades below are as CCv4.0 prints them — Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) evidence level + recommendation strength.

| Findings in the primary position, with normal median IRP | CCv4.0 reading | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| **>70% ineffective swallows** *or* **≥50% failed peristalsis** | **Conclusive IEM** | Very Low, Strong |
| **50–70% ineffective swallows** (without ≥50% failed) | **Inconclusive** for IEM — supportive testing strengthens confidence | Very Low, Conditional |
| **<50% ineffective swallows** | Criteria not met | — |
| **100% failed peristalsis**, with normal IRP supine **and** upright | **Absent contractility** — a separate diagnosis, not "severe IEM" | Accepted Clinical Observation |

> [!pitfall] 50–70% ineffective swallows is not a diagnosis
> CCv4.0 calls that band inconclusive and asks for supportive testing before IEM is diagnosed. Because the threshold was raised, a study read as IEM under v3.0 may be inconclusive under v4.0 ([[chicago-v4-2021-esophageal-dysmotility]]).

**What counts as an "ineffective swallow"** — any one of three patterns; the third is the one that used to be its own disorder:

| Pattern | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Weak contraction** | distal contractile integral (DCI) **≥100 and <450** mmHg·s·cm |
| **Failed peristalsis** | DCI **<100** mmHg·s·cm |
| **Fragmented swallow** | Transition-zone defect of peristalsis **>5 cm** under the **20 mmHg** isobaric contour, in the setting of a **DCI ≥450** mmHg·s·cm |

For contrast, normal contraction = DCI 450–8,000 mmHg·s·cm. Note the two swallow thresholds differ: **>70%** for ineffective swallows, **≥50%** for failed peristalsis.

**Overlapping patterns:** when more than one peristaltic abnormality is present, classify hierarchically — [[distal-esophageal-spasm|distal esophageal spasm (DES)]] first, [[hypercontractile-esophagus|hypercontractile esophagus]] next, IEM last — and comment on the overlap. EGJ obstruction with ineffective peristalsis is reported as *EGJOO with ineffective motility*, not as IEM.

### Supportive Testing

Not required for the definition of IEM; each adds confidence, and is what CCv4.0 asks for in the inconclusive band:

- **Poor bolus transit** on impedance or barium esophagram (Very Low GRADE, Conditional Recommendation).
- **Lack of contraction reserve** on multiple rapid swallow (MRS) (Very Low GRADE, Conditional Recommendation).
  - Intact MRS response = absent esophageal body contractility (DCI <100 mmHg·s·cm) with complete deglutitive lower esophageal sphincter (LES) inhibition **during** the repetitive swallows, **plus post-MRS augmentation** — post-MRS DCI greater than the mean single-swallow DCI. The post-MRS contraction must be true peristalsis, not artifact or pressurization.
  - MRS protocol: **5 repetitive 2 mL water swallows <3 s apart** ([[acg-2020-esophageal-physiologic-testing]]).

### Severity Assessment

- **CCv4.0 gives no graded severity scale for IEM.** The diagnostic bands above are the only ordinal structure — report the **actual percentage of ineffective and of failed swallows**, not just the label.
- The severe end of the spectrum is **absent contractility** (criteria above), not "severe IEM".
- What carries clinical weight is functional rather than manometric: poor bolus transit, and **absent contraction reserve**, which is associated with a higher likelihood of post-fundoplication dysphagia after [[antireflux-surgery|antireflux surgery (ARS)]] and with **development or worsening of IEM over time** ([[acg-2020-esophageal-physiologic-testing]]).

> [!pearl] Report the numbers, not just the label
> With no severity grade to fall back on, the percentage of ineffective and of failed swallows, bolus transit, and contraction reserve are what the next decision rests on ([[chicago-v4-2021-esophageal-dysmotility]]).

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[dysphagia]].*

- **Absent contractility** — 100% failed peristalsis with normal IRP in both positions. A borderline supine median IRP (**10–15 mmHg** on Medtronic) should prompt consideration of type I [[achalasia]]; if dysphagia is the dominant symptom, add supportive testing with a timed barium esophagram (TBE, preferably with tablet) and/or [[flip-panometry|functional lumen imaging probe (FLIP) panometry]].
- **[[esophagogastric-junction-outflow-obstruction|EGJOO]] with ineffective motility** — an abnormal IRP moves the study out of IEM.
- **[[distal-esophageal-spasm|DES]]** and **[[hypercontractile-esophagus]]** — take precedence when features overlap.
- **[[gerd|Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)]]** — motor disorders and GERD symptoms overlap; HRM cannot diagnose GERD but does exclude motor mimics and assess peristaltic performance.
- **Systemic disease** — systemic sclerosis and other connective tissue disease: see [[esophageal-dysfunction-systemic-disease]].

## Diagnostics

| Test | Role in IEM |
|---|---|
| **HRM** | Makes the diagnosis (criteria above). A careful index endoscopy comes first — endoscopic or radiographic mechanical obstruction precludes the use of CCv4.0, which also does not apply after foregut surgery or with a large hiatal/paraesophageal hernia |
| **Impedance or barium esophagram** | Bolus transit — supportive, not required |
| **MRS during HRM** | Contraction reserve — supportive, and the pre-ARS question |
| **[[flip-panometry\|FLIP panometry]]** | Not a substitute for HRM here. With **normal EGJ opening** (EGJ distensibility index ≥2.0 mm²/mm Hg **and** maximum EGJ diameter ≥16 mm), an absent or diminished contractile response → test and/or treat for reflux or a disorder of gut–brain interaction, and **consider HRM if hypocontractility or spasm is suspected**. Absent contractile response occurs in **23%–43%** of patients with normal primary peristalsis on HRM, so with normal EGJ opening it does not by itself indicate a distinct motor disorder ([[aga-2025-flip-esophageal]]) |

## Therapeutics

- **No IEM-specific drug therapy in either guideline.** CCv4.0 is a diagnostic classification and ACG 2020 addresses physiologic testing; neither names a medication for IEM. Management is directed at the symptom syndrome and at the operative decision below.
- **Do HRM before ARS or invasive GERD management** — it rules out motor disorders that mimic GERD, where a standard fundoplication would worsen obstruction, and demonstrates the adequacy of esophageal peristalsis (Key concept 9, [[acg-2020-esophageal-physiologic-testing]]).
- **Add MRS to the study:** provocative maneuvers uniformly added clinically useful information over the 10-swallow protocol alone, and absent contraction reserve is the finding that flags post-ARS dysphagia risk. Full pre- and post-ARS manometric framework: [[hrm-antireflux-surgery]].
