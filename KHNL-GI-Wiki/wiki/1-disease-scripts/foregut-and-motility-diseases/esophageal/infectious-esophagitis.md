# Infectious Esophagitis

Esophageal infection causing dysphagia, odynophagia, and chest pain. Most common organisms: **Candida, herpes simplex virus (HSV), cytomegalovirus (CMV)**. More common in immunosuppressed hosts but can occur in the immunocompetent. Candida esophagitis is the **most commonly encountered** infectious esophagitis. ([[aga-2024-esophageal-immunity-infection]])

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- Presents with **dysphagia and/or (often severe) odynophagia**; consider in any patient with these symptoms, especially with risk factors.
- **[[upper-endoscopy|EGD]] with biopsies ± brushings** is the diagnostic test — endoscopic appearance suggests the organism; biopsy/cytology confirms.
- Identify **immunocompromise** — if an esophageal infection is found, assess whether accompanying signs/symptoms suggest a more systemic infection (consult infectious disease). (BPA 2)
- **If symptoms do not improve after therapy**, evaluate for refractory infection or an additional underlying esophageal/immunologic disorder. (BPA 3)

### Organism-specific features

| Organism | Endoscopic appearance | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| **Candida** | White nummular plaques/lesions; may carpet the entire esophagus in severe cases | Cytobrush or biopsy → fungal forms |
| **HSV** | Multiple small, shallow, well-circumscribed ulcers | Biopsy → intranuclear eosinophilic inclusions |
| **CMV** | Few large cratered ulcers; **deep** ulceration(s) on the source's history-based table | Biopsy → large eosinophilic **or** basophilic intranuclear inclusions |
| **HIV** | A few discrete esophageal ulcerations | Named by the source as another cause of discrete esophageal ulceration; no diagnostic criteria given |

### Risk Factors (candida esophagitis)

- Recent antibiotics; local or systemic steroids/immunosuppression; malignancy
- [[proton-pump-inhibitors|PPI]] use; older age; chronic alcohol use
- Chronic kidney disease; diabetes; motility disorders causing esophageal stasis

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[dysphagia]].*

- [[eosinophilic-esophagitis]] and [[lymphocytic-esophagitis]] (edema, rings, furrows, exudates can mimic candida)
- [[gerd|Reflux/pill esophagitis]] (medication-induced ulceration — e.g. doxycycline: **1–2 well-marked ulcerations**)
- [[esophageal-dysfunction-systemic-disease|Immune-mediated/systemic esophageal disease]] — lichen planus (pale, edematous mucosa with peeling/sloughing on contact, thick white exudates, narrowed-caliber esophagus), bullous dermatoses (pemphigus/pemphigoid: sloughing/blistering, erythema, red longitudinal lines), [[crohns-disease|esophageal Crohn's disease]]
- [[esophageal-cancer|Malignancy]]
- **Secondary [[achalasia]]** — infectious and inflammatory causes of achalasia should be considered at initial evaluation (query recent COVID infection, Chagas risk, eosinophilic disease). (BPA 10)

## Diagnostics

- **[[upper-endoscopy|EGD]] with biopsies and brushings** — take brushings **if nummular lesions are identified**; take esophageal biopsies for the viral causes. The source does not specify whether to sample the ulcer edge or base.
- **Consider infectious disease consultation** when an esophageal infection is identified in an immunosuppressed patient (BPA 2).

### History-driven evaluation

The source's Table 1 links the pertinent history to what to look for — the practical entry point when a patient presents with esophageal symptoms ([[aga-2024-esophageal-immunity-infection]]):

| Pertinent history | Diagnostic considerations | Tests to consider | Supportive endoscopic findings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recent antibiotics | Candidal esophagitis; medication-induced ulceration (e.g. doxycycline) | Upper endoscopy with biopsies, **and brushings if nummular lesions identified** | Whitish nummular lesions (Candida); 1–2 well-marked ulcerations for pill-induced esophagitis |
| Immunosuppression | Candidal esophagitis; HSV; CMV | Upper endoscopy with biopsies/brushings; **consider infectious disease consult if infection identified** | Whitish nummular lesions (Candida); shallow ulcers for HSV; **deep** ulceration(s) in CMV |
| Recent COVID infection | Post-COVID dysmotility / [[achalasia]] | Upper endoscopy to rule out other diagnoses; [[high-resolution-manometry\|high-resolution esophageal manometry]]; timed barium esophagram with pill | Most likely normal-appearing esophagus |
| Bullous dermatoses (pemphigoid, pemphigus) | Esophageal involvement of bullous dermatoses | Upper endoscopy with biopsies for routine H&E **plus additional biopsies for DIF** sent to a center with dermatopathology expertise | Sloughing/blistering of tissues, erythema, red longitudinal lines, erosions; sloughing with dislodgement of epithelium in pemphigus during biopsy (skin Nikolsky-like sign) |
| Lichen planus (oral, dermatologic, genital; middle–older-age female) | Esophageal involvement | Upper endoscopy with biopsies for routine H&E **plus DIF** biopsies | Narrowed caliber of the majority of the esophagus; pale, edematous mucosa peeling/sloughing on scope contact; thick white exudates; non-specific short or pan-esophageal stenoses |
| Atopic dermatitis, asthma, or significant food allergy/atopy | [[eosinophilic-esophagitis\|EoE]] (also consider HES/EGPA) | Upper endoscopy with a **minimum of 3–4 distal + 3–4 proximal** esophageal biopsies, **off [[proton-pump-inhibitors\|PPI]] ≥2–4 weeks** | Edema, rings, exudates, furrows, some with stenoses (part or all of esophagus) — can appear normal |
| Autoimmune disease ([[crohns-disease\|Crohn's]], scleroderma, lupus, MCTD, Sjögren's, myositis) | Esophageal involvement of extra-esophageal autoimmune disease (inflammatory or dysmotility) | Upper endoscopy with biopsies **plus** high-resolution esophageal manometry | Ulcerations (Crohn's); dilation with reflux changes (scleroderma); non-specific edema and crepe-paper tissue with non-specific stenoses (Sjögren's). SSc manometry: absent contractility with low-amplitude LES |
| Prior travel/exposure in Central or South America | Chagas disease with potential [[achalasia]] | Infectious disease consult if positive; upper endoscopy with biopsies to rule out other diagnoses; high-resolution esophageal manometry | Normal mucosal appearance vs chronic stasis changes |
| Female >50 years with dysphagia, often mimicking EoE endoscopically | [[lymphocytic-esophagitis\|LyE]] | Upper endoscopy with biopsies for routine H&E, **off PPI ≥2–4 weeks** | Overlapping features with EoE; can be normal |

## Therapeutics

| Organism | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Candida** | **Fluconazole 200–400 mg/day × 14–21 days** — the preferred treatment per the available guidelines cited by [[aga-2024-esophageal-immunity-infection]]. Nystatin for prophylaxis in high-risk patients or when colonization vs infection is unclear. In immunocompetent hosts candida may resolve when the inciting risk is removed; whether asymptomatic candidiasis warrants treatment is unclear (asymptomatic candidiasis rarely becomes symptomatic). |
| **HSV** | **Acyclovir, valacyclovir, or famciclovir** — up to **10 days (immunocompetent)** or **21 days (immunosuppressed)**. |
| **CMV** | **Ganciclovir (or valganciclovir)** — up to **6 weeks**. |

- Refractory symptoms after appropriate therapy → reassess for resistant/refractory infection or a coexisting immune-mediated esophageal disorder. (BPA 3)
