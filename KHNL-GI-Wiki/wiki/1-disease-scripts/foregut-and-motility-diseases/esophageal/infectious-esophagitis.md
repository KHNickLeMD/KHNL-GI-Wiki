Esophageal infection causing dysphagia, odynophagia, and chest pain. Most common organisms: **Candida, herpes simplex virus (HSV), cytomegalovirus (CMV)**. More common in immunosuppressed hosts but can occur in the immunocompetent. Candida esophagitis is the **most commonly encountered** infectious esophagitis. ([[aga-2024-esophageal-immunity-infection]])

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- Presents with **dysphagia or odynophagia** (Candida); viral esophagitis with **dysphagia or severe odynophagia** ([[aga-2024-esophageal-immunity-infection]]).
- Identify risks for an infectious cause of esophageal symptoms and investigate for it (Best Practice Advice [BPA] 1, [[aga-2024-esophageal-immunity-infection]]).
- **Endoscopy with biopsies ± brushings** (see [[#Diagnostics]]) — endoscopic appearance suggests the organism; biopsy/cytology confirms.
- **Candida — empiric trial before endoscopy:** a **diagnostic trial of antifungal therapy is appropriate before endoscopic examination** (Rec 131, [[idsa-2016-candidiasis]]).
  - Oropharyngeal candidiasis + dysphagia/odynophagia in an immunocompromised host is frequently predictive of esophageal candidiasis; a fluconazole trial is a cost-effective alternative to endoscopy.
  - Esophageal candidiasis can present as odynophagia **without** oropharyngeal candidiasis.

### Severity Assessment

- Neither source gives a severity score. Candida in severe cases may **carpet the entire esophagus** ([[aga-2024-esophageal-immunity-infection]]).
- **Once an esophageal infection is identified:** look for signs/symptoms suggesting immunocompromise leading to a **more systemic infection**; an infectious disease consult will aid in guiding treatment (BPA 2, [[aga-2024-esophageal-immunity-infection]]).

### Organism-specific features

| Organism | Endoscopic appearance | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| **Candida** | White nummular plaques/lesions; may carpet the entire esophagus in severe cases | Cytobrush or biopsy → fungal forms |
| **HSV** | Multiple small, shallow, well-circumscribed ulcers | Biopsy → intranuclear eosinophilic inclusions |
| **CMV** | Few large cratered ulcers; **deep** ulceration(s) | Biopsy → large eosinophilic **or** basophilic intranuclear inclusions |
| **Human immunodeficiency virus (HIV)** | A few discrete esophageal ulcerations | AGA 2024 gives no diagnostic criteria |

*Source: [[aga-2024-esophageal-immunity-infection]] (text and Table 1)*

### Risk Factors (candida esophagitis)

- Recent antibiotics; local or systemic steroids/immunosuppression; malignancy
- [[proton-pump-inhibitors|Proton pump inhibitor (PPI)]] use; older age; chronic alcohol use
- Chronic kidney disease; diabetes; motility disorders causing esophageal stasis
- Esophageal candidiasis typically occurs at **lower CD4 counts** than oropharyngeal disease; effective antiretroviral therapy has sharply reduced it in HIV ([[idsa-2016-candidiasis]])

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[dysphagia]].*

- **Pill (medication-induced) esophagitis** — e.g. doxycycline after recent antibiotics: **1–2 well-marked ulcerations**
- [[eosinophilic-esophagitis|Eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE)]] and [[lymphocytic-esophagitis|lymphocytic esophagitis (LyE)]] — edema, rings, furrows, exudates
- [[esophageal-dysfunction-systemic-disease|Esophageal involvement of systemic disease]] — lichen planus (thick white exudates, peeling/sloughing mucosa), bullous dermatoses (sloughing/blistering), [[crohns-disease|esophageal Crohn's disease]] (ulcerations)
- **Secondary [[achalasia]]** — consider infectious and inflammatory causes at initial evaluation; ask about recent coronavirus disease (COVID) infection, Chagas risk, eosinophilic disease (BPA 10)

## Diagnostics

- **[[upper-endoscopy|Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD)]] with biopsies, plus brushings if nummular lesions are identified** ([[aga-2024-esophageal-immunity-infection]]). AGA 2024 does not say whether to sample the ulcer edge or base.

### History-driven evaluation

Infection rows of AGA 2024 Table 1; the full table (systemic, dermatologic, allergic, autoimmune histories) lives on [[esophageal-dysfunction-systemic-disease#Evaluation by Pertinent History|esophageal dysfunction in systemic disease]].

| Pertinent history | Diagnostic considerations | Tests to consider | Supportive endoscopic findings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recent antibiotics | Candidal esophagitis; medication-induced ulceration (e.g. doxycycline) | Upper endoscopy with biopsies, **and brushings if nummular lesions identified** | Whitish nummular lesions (Candida); 1–2 well-marked ulcerations for pill-induced esophagitis |
| Immunosuppression | Candidal esophagitis; HSV; CMV | Upper endoscopy with biopsies/brushings; **consider infectious disease consult if infection identified** | Whitish nummular lesions (Candida); shallow ulcers for HSV; **deep** ulceration(s) in CMV |
| Prior travel/exposure in Central or South America | Chagas disease with potential [[achalasia]] | Infectious disease consult if positive diagnosis; upper endoscopy with biopsies to rule out other diagnoses; [[high-resolution-manometry\|high-resolution esophageal manometry]] | Normal mucosal appearance vs chronic stasis changes |

## Therapeutics

### Candida

- **Systemic antifungal therapy is always required** (Rec 131, [[idsa-2016-candidiasis]]).
- Fluconazole is the preferred treatment; AGA 2024 gives the same first-line dose and duration as Rec 132 below, and notes Candida treatment is not well studied ([[aga-2024-esophageal-immunity-infection]]).

| Situation | Regimen ([[idsa-2016-candidiasis]]) | Rec · strength; evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **First line** | Oral fluconazole **200–400 mg (3–6 mg/kg) daily × 14–21 days** | 132 · strong; high-quality |
| **Cannot tolerate oral therapy** | Intravenous (IV) fluconazole **400 mg (6 mg/kg) daily**, OR an echinocandin: micafungin **150 mg daily** / caspofungin **70 mg load → 50 mg daily** / anidulafungin **200 mg daily** | 133 · strong; high-quality |
| Cannot tolerate oral therapy, less preferred | Amphotericin B (AmB) deoxycholate **0.3–0.7 mg/kg daily** | 134 · strong; moderate-quality |
| Able to tolerate oral intake | **Consider de-escalating** to oral fluconazole 200–400 mg (3–6 mg/kg) daily | 135 · strong; moderate-quality |
| **Fluconazole-refractory** | Itraconazole solution **200 mg daily**, OR voriconazole **200 mg (3 mg/kg) twice daily** IV or oral, **× 14–21 days** | 136 · strong; high-quality |
| Fluconazole-refractory, alternatives | Echinocandin (doses as above) **× 14–21 days**, OR AmB deoxycholate 0.3–0.7 mg/kg daily **× 21 days** | 137 · strong; high-quality |
| Fluconazole-refractory, could be considered | Posaconazole suspension **400 mg twice daily**, or extended-release tablets **300 mg once daily** | 138 · weak; low-quality |
| **Recurrent esophagitis** | Chronic suppressive fluconazole **100–200 mg three times weekly** | 139 · strong; high-quality |
| **HIV infection** | Antiretroviral therapy strongly recommended to reduce recurrent infections | 140 · strong; high-quality |

- Echinocandins work as well as fluconazole but relapse more often → higher echinocandin doses for esophageal disease than for candidemia ([[idsa-2016-candidiasis]]).
- **Nystatin:** prophylaxis for patients at high risk of Candida esophagitis; or consider when it is unclear whether infection or colonization is present ([[aga-2024-esophageal-immunity-infection]]).
- **Asymptomatic or immunocompetent:** Candida may resolve on its own once the inciting risk is removed in immunocompetent hosts; whether asymptomatic candidiasis needs treatment is unclear (one study: it rarely becomes symptomatic) ([[aga-2024-esophageal-immunity-infection]]).

> [!pitfall] Thrush doses do not treat the esophagus
> Esophageal candidiasis takes **higher doses and longer courses** than moderate–severe oropharyngeal disease (fluconazole 200–400 mg × 14–21 days vs 100–200 mg × 7–14 days) ([[idsa-2016-candidiasis]]).

### HSV and CMV

| Organism | Drug | Duration ([[aga-2024-esophageal-immunity-infection]]) |
|---|---|---|
| **HSV** | Acyclovir, valacyclovir, or famciclovir | Up to **10 days** (immunocompetent) or **21 days** (immunosuppressed) |
| **CMV** | Ganciclovir (or valganciclovir) | Up to **6 weeks** may be needed |

- AGA 2024 gives no antiviral doses.

### No Improvement on Therapy

- Expect improvement: most esophageal candidiasis improves or resolves **within 7 days** of starting antifungal therapy ([[idsa-2016-candidiasis]]).
- **Symptoms not improving after therapy** → evaluate for **refractory infection** or an **additional underlying esophageal or immunologic disorder** (BPA 3, [[aga-2024-esophageal-immunity-infection]]).
