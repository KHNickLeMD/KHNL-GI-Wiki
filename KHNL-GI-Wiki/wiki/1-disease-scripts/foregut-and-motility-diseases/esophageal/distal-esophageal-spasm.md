Related: [[hypercontractile-esophagus]], [[ineffective-esophageal-motility]].*## Assessment### Establishing the DiagnosisDistal esophageal spasm (DES) is defined by **Chicago Classification v4.0** criteria on [[high-resolution-manometry]]:- ≥20% premature (spastic) contractions with DCI >450 mmHg·s·cm- IRP normal (unlike achalasia — see [[achalasia]])**Distinguishes DES from hypercontractile esophagus:** DES is premature/disordered contraction; hypercontractile esophagus is high-amplitude contraction — see [[hypercontractile-esophagus]].**Symptoms:** Dysphagia (to solids and liquids), non-cardiac chest pain (NCCP); symptoms episodic and may not correlate with manometric abnormality during study.---## Differential Diagnosis- [[achalasia]] — elevated IRP distinguishes; absent peristalsis- [[hypercontractile-esophagus]] (jackhammer esophagus) — DCI >8000 vs. premature contractions- Cardiac chest pain — must exclude before esophageal workup- GERD — see [[gerd]]; acid exposure can trigger spasm---## Diagnostics- **HRM (required):** Chicago v4.0 criteria — see [[high-resolution-manometry]]- **Barium esophagram (TBE):** May show "corkscrew" esophagus during symptomatic episode (low sensitivity)- **FLIP panometry:** See [[flip-panometry]]; may show disordered contractile response pattern---## Therapeutics*Stub — smooth muscle relaxants (CCBs, nitrates), PPI if GERD co-present, TCAs/SSRIs for visceral hypersensitivity, peppermint oil, POEM (selected cases with refractory dysphagia-predominant symptoms).*
