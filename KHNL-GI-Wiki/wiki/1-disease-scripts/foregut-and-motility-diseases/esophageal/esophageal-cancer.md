Two dominant histologies: **adenocarcinoma** (distal esophagus/GEJ; arises from [[barretts-esophagus]]; risk factors [[gerd|GERD]], [[obesity]], male sex) and **squamous cell carcinoma** (mid/upper esophagus; risk factors smoking, alcohol, [[achalasia]], caustic injury, tylosis). Presents with progressive solid-food [[dysphagia]], weight loss, and anemia — a classic alarm presentation. Diagnosis by [[upper-endoscopy]] with biopsy; staging with EUS ([[endoscopic-ultrasound]]), CT, and PET. Early/T1a lesions may be amenable to endoscopic resection ([[polypectomy-emr|EMR/ESD]]); locally advanced disease treated with neoadjuvant chemoradiation followed by esophagectomy.
