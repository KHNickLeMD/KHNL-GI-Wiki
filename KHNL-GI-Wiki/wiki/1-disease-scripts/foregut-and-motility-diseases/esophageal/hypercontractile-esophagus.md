## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

Hypercontractile esophagus (jackhammer esophagus) = excessive peristaltic vigor on [[high-resolution-manometry|high-resolution manometry (HRM)]], which may include an excessive lower esophageal sphincter (LES) after-contraction, **not** associated with mechanical obstruction (Very Low GRADE, conditional). CCv4.0 separates the **manometric pattern** — of unclear clinical relevance on its own — from a **clinically relevant** diagnosis ([[chicago-v4-2021-esophageal-dysmotility]]).

**Hypercontractile swallow** = distal contractile integral (DCI) **>8,000 mmHg·s·cm** — a threshold CCv4.0 states is suitable across HRM systems (unlike the pressure thresholds).

Grades below are as CCv4.0 prints them — Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) evidence level plus recommendation strength. Its statements are unnumbered.

| CCv4.0 band | Criteria | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| **Conclusive manometric hypercontractile esophagus** | Normal median integrated relaxation pressure (IRP) **and ≥20%** hypercontractile **supine** swallows | Very Low GRADE, conditional |
| **Clinically relevant hypercontractile esophagus** | Conclusive manometric diagnosis **plus** clinically relevant symptoms | Very Low GRADE, strong |
| *Clinically relevant symptoms* | Dysphagia **and** non-cardiac chest pain | Very Low GRADE, strong |
| **Exclusions — the label cannot be applied otherwise** | Criteria for [[achalasia]] **not** met, criteria for [[distal-esophageal-spasm\|distal esophageal spasm (DES)]] **not** met, **and** mechanical obstruction carefully ruled out | Very Low GRADE, strong |

Manufacturer- and position-specific IRP cutoffs are on [[chicago-classification-v4]].

> [!pitfall] Hypercontractility can be the obstruction's fault
> Obstruction at the esophagogastric junction (EGJ) or the distal esophagus can itself induce a hypercontractile response, so CCv4.0 makes careful exclusion of mechanical obstruction a condition of the diagnosis ([[chicago-v4-2021-esophageal-dysmotility]]).

**Three structural rules that gate the label:**

- **Position:** classify from the **primary position** in which the 10 wet swallows were performed (supine or upright) — but the conclusive statement is written for **supine** swallows. Secondary position and provocative swallows are supportive; concordance strengthens the classification, discordance should prompt reconsideration.
- **Order:** disorders of peristalsis are considered only once a disorder of EGJ outflow has been excluded. Where features overlap, classify **DES first**, then hypercontractile esophagus, then [[ineffective-esophageal-motility|ineffective esophageal motility (IEM)]], with a comment acknowledging the overlap.
- **Scope:** CCv4.0 applies only to normal foregut anatomy — not after prior surgical or invasive foregut intervention, and not with large hiatal or paraesophageal hernias.

### Severity Assessment

- **CCv4.0 defines no graded severity scale** — the framework is conclusive vs inconclusive vs clinically relevant, not mild/moderate/severe, and it sets no DCI bands above the 8,000 mmHg·s·cm cutoff.
- The nearest thing to a severity gradient is the **sub-group** (see [[#Classification / Typing]]), so name the sub-group rather than only the DCI.

### Classification / Typing

CCv4.0 describes three general sub-groups; the patterns are heterogeneous and differ in clinical significance.

| Sub-group | Manometric feature |
|---|---|
| Single-peaked | Single-peaked hypercontractile swallows |
| **Jackhammer** | Repetitive prolonged contractions, especially in the **post-peak** phase |
| LES after-contraction | Hypercontractile swallows with a vigorous LES after-contraction |

- The **jackhammer** sub-group is typically associated with **higher DCI values and worse symptom severity**; CCv4.0 draws no such distinction between the other two.

*See Figure 8 in [[chicago-v4-2021-esophageal-dysmotility]] for example manometry tracings of each sub-group.*

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[dysphagia]].*

- [[achalasia]] **type III** — abnormal median IRP, ≥20% premature swallows, no evidence of peristalsis; hypercontractile esophagus requires a **normal** median IRP and may be diagnosed only when achalasia criteria are not met
- [[distal-esophageal-spasm]] — normal median IRP with ≥20% premature/spastic contractions (distal latency <4.5 s with DCI ≥450 mmHg·s·cm); where both patterns appear, DES is classified first
- [[esophagogastric-junction-outflow-obstruction|EGJ outflow obstruction (EGJOO) with hypercontractile features]] — abnormal median IRP **supine and upright** plus ≥20% swallows with elevated intrabolus pressure (supine), with a hypercontractile peristaltic pattern
- Mechanical obstruction — [[esophageal-stricture|stricture]], mass, or other obstruction at the EGJ or distal esophagus; endoscopic or radiographic obstruction precludes CCv4.0 altogether
- [[gerd|Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)]] — hypercontractile esophagus **without** EGJOO can be associated with GERD ([[padova-2025-hrm-antireflux]])
- [[hiatal-hernia|Hiatus hernia]] and mucosal inflammation such as [[eosinophilic-esophagitis|eosinophilic esophagitis]] — a spastic contractile response on [[flip-panometry|functional lumen imaging probe (FLIP)]] panometry may be **secondary** to these ([[aga-2025-flip-esophageal]])
- Cardiac chest pain — CCv4.0's symptom criterion is explicitly **non-cardiac** chest pain

## Diagnostics

- **HRM — required.** Criteria above; standard protocol and metric thresholds on [[high-resolution-manometry]] and [[chicago-classification-v4]].
- **Index [[upper-endoscopy|endoscopy]] before manometry** — endoscopic or radiographic evidence of mechanical obstruction precludes the use of CCv4.0.
- **Supportive testing** — timed barium esophagram (TBE, preferably with a tablet) and/or FLIP where manometry is equivocal or obstruction is suspected without achalasia criteria being met.
- **FLIP** ([[aga-2025-flip-esophageal]]):
  - Reduced EGJ opening (EGJ distensibility index **<2.0 mm²/mm Hg** *and* maximum EGJ diameter **<12 mm**) with a spastic or disordered contractile response → **spastic achalasia is likely**, not hypercontractile esophagus.
  - A spastic contractile response — sustained LES contraction (sLESc, >5 s LES diameter reduction with a pressure rise) or sustained occluding contraction (SOC, >10 s nonpropagating lumen-occluding contraction with a pressure rise) — calls for **alternate testing with HRM and/or TBE before any irreversible LES disruption**.

## Therapeutics

> [!pearl] Treat the symptoms, not the contractile vigor
> Given how heterogeneous hypercontractile patterns are, CCv4.0 urges caution in treating contractile vigor as an endpoint and advocates **conservative medical therapy before endoscopic or surgical intervention** is considered ([[chicago-v4-2021-esophageal-dysmotility]]).

### Antireflux Surgery

Hypercontractile esophagus is **not by itself a contraindication** to fundoplication, but EGJ obstruction must be settled first ([[padova-2025-hrm-antireflux|Padova consensus]]):

| Finding before surgery | Padova recommendation | Median (1–9) · agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Hypercontractile esophagus by CCv4.0 criteria **with** objective evidence of GERD, typical reflux symptoms, and **at least partial** symptom improvement on acid-suppression medication | Can be **referred for [[antireflux-surgery\|antireflux surgery (ARS)]]** | 7 · 81% |
| LES obstruction meeting CCv4.0 EGJOO criteria | **Must be addressed** before undertaking ARS | 8 · 86% |

Full pre- and post-ARS manometric framework on [[hrm-antireflux-surgery]].

### Per-Oral Endoscopic Myotomy

- **Nonachalasia spastic disorders are case-by-case only.** Hypercontractile esophagus and DES on manometry require a comprehensive evaluation with **correlation of symptoms**; evidence for [[poem|per-oral endoscopic myotomy (POEM)]] in these manometric findings is limited, so it should be considered only case-by-case after less invasive approaches — medical therapy, [[botulinum-toxin|botulinum toxin]] injection, and [[esophageal-dilation|endoscopic dilation]] — have been exhausted (American Gastroenterological Association [AGA] 2024 clinical practice update, best practice advice 3; [[aga-2024-poem-advances]]).
- **Mandatory first:** confirmatory compliance testing (impedance planimetry, TBE, pH study) to exclude GERD and document symptomatic delayed esophageal emptying, with careful consideration of false positives ([[aga-2024-poem-advances]]).
- **Why myotomy length is raised in spastic disease:** for **type III achalasia** — not hypercontractile esophagus — the American College of Gastroenterology prefers POEM over laparoscopic Heller myotomy (success 93% vs 71%) because the myotomy can be tailored to the spastic segment ([[acg-2020-achalasia]]); AGA 2024 adds that POEM allows unlimited proximal extension, though long-term data on long myotomies are limited.

*The cited guidelines name medical therapy, botulinum toxin, and endoscopic dilation as the less invasive options but give no specific agents, doses, or durations for hypercontractile esophagus.*
