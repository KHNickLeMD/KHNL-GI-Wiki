*Hepatitis A virus (HAV) infection — acute, self-limited, never chronic. All content is from the Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP) 2020 recommendations ([[acip-2020-hepatitis-a]]). ACIP numbers none of its recommendations and prints no evidence grades; items it labels **Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) clinical guidance** keep that label.*

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis
- **Cannot be diagnosed on clinical or epidemiologic features alone** → immunoglobulin M (IgM) anti-HAV in serum, or HAV RNA in serum or stool (p. 5)
- Transmission: fecal-oral — person to person, contaminated food or water (p. 4)
- **Incubation:** average 28 days (range 15–50) (p. 5)
- Presentation: clinically indistinguishable from other acute viral hepatitis — abrupt fever, malaise, anorexia, nausea, abdominal discomfort, then within days to a week dark urine, pale stools, jaundice (p. 5)
- **Age decides symptoms:** children <6 years — 70% asymptomatic, usually no jaundice; older children and adults — usually symptomatic, jaundice in >70% (p. 5)
- Course: resolves within 2–3 months; complete recovery within 6 months; **no chronic infection** (p. 5)
- **Prolonged or relapsing hepatitis A:** 10%–15% of symptomatic persons, up to 6 months — **consider infectious**; recovery from relapse is universal (p. 5)

### Severity Assessment
- **Fulminant hepatic failure:** <1% of cases; a higher proportion in the outbreaks that began in 2016 (p. 5) → see [[acute-liver-failure]]
- Severity rises with **older age** (adults >40 years more often hospitalized), immunocompromise, chronic liver disease and other comorbidity (pp. 1, 9)
- **Chronic liver disease** does not raise the risk of acquiring HAV, but [[chronic-hepatitis-b|hepatitis B virus (HBV)]], [[hepatitis-c|hepatitis C virus (HCV)]], [[cirrhosis]], fatty liver disease, alcoholic liver disease and [[autoimmune-hepatitis|autoimmune hepatitis]] are associated with **increased risk of fulminant hepatitis**; 38.3% of US HAV hospitalizations in 2010–2011 had some liver disease (p. 8)
- **Human immunodeficiency virus (HIV):** infection is prolonged, may increase HIV replication and lengthen HAV transmission (p. 8)

ACIP Table 3 (p. 15) groups who is at higher risk — it drives the immune globulin (IG) decisions below:

| Increased risk for HAV infection | Close personal contacts of a person with HAV* | Household contacts · caretakers · sexual contacts · anticipated close contact with an international adoptee |

## Differential Diagnosis
*Workup: see [[abnormal-liver-chemistries]] and [[jaundice]].*
- Other acute viral hepatitis — [[chronic-hepatitis-b|hepatitis B]], [[hepatitis-c|hepatitis C]], [[hepatitis-e|hepatitis E]]: HAV is clinically indistinguishable; serology decides (p. 5)

## Diagnostics

### Serology

| Test | When to use | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| **IgM anti-HAV** | **Only in a symptomatic person suspected of HAV infection** | Detectable 5–10 days after symptom onset in most; peaks within 1 month; undetectable within 6 months. Positives >1 year after infection and false positives occur |
| Total or immunoglobulin G (IgG) anti-HAV | Immunity check in a person with a preexposure indication; prevalence studies | Lifelong after infection or vaccination. **Total positive + IgM negative = immune** (past infection or vaccine) |
| HAV RNA (serum or stool) | Alternative to IgM for diagnosis | — |

*Hepatitis A serology — which test, when, and what it means*
*Source: built from ACIP 2020 Diagnosis section (pp. 5, 12)*
*[[acip-2020-hepatitis-a|ACIP 2020 Hepatitis A Recommendations]]*
*IgM = immunoglobulin M · IgG = immunoglobulin G · HAV = hepatitis A virus · RNA = ribonucleic acid*

- Total anti-HAV positive in a symptomatic person with no IgG assay → test IgM to establish acute infection (p. 5)
- Aminotransferases rise 5–10 days before symptoms; bilirubin and aminotransferases usually normal by 2–3 months (p. 5)

> [!pitfall] A positive IgM after a recent vaccine
> IgM anti-HAV is detectable 2–3 weeks after one vaccine dose in 8%–20% of adults — test IgM only when the patient is symptomatic and HAV is suspected ([[acip-2020-hepatitis-a]], p. 12).

### Infectivity
- **Peak:** the 2 weeks before jaundice onset or enzyme elevation (highest stool viral load); most persons non-infectious **1 week after jaundice onset** (p. 5)
- Children may shed up to 10 weeks after illness onset; neonatally infected infants up to 6 months (p. 5)
- Prolonged viremia/fecal shedding: >1 month in immunocompetent and >1 year in immunocompromised persons reported, incl. after organ transplantation (p. 5)
- **Inactivation:** heat food >185°F (>85°C) for 1 minute, or disinfect surfaces with 1:100 household bleach in tap water (p. 4)

## Therapeutics
ACIP addresses prevention only — it gives no treatment for acute infection beyond the fulminant-failure risk above.

### Who to Vaccinate
**Routine (ACIP BOX, p. 17; pp. 18–20):**
- **Children:** all at **12–23 months**; catch-up for all unvaccinated **2–18 years** (new 2020)
- **Increased risk of infection:** international travelers to high/intermediate endemicity · men who have sex with men (MSM) · persons who use injection or noninjection drugs (do a thorough risk-factor assessment) · occupational (nonhuman primates, HAV research laboratory only) · anticipated close contact with an international adoptee from a high/intermediate-endemicity country in the first **60 days** after arrival (first dose as soon as adoption is planned, ideally **≥2 weeks** before arrival) · persons **≥1 year** experiencing homelessness
- **Increased risk of severe disease:**
  - **Chronic liver disease** — incl. HBV, HCV, cirrhosis, fatty liver disease, alcoholic liver disease, autoimmune hepatitis, ALT or AST persistently >2× upper limit of normal → **routine vaccination**
  - **HIV, age ≥1 year** → routine vaccination (new 2020)
- **Pregnant women** at risk of infection or a severe outcome → vaccinate during pregnancy if not previously vaccinated (new 2020)
- **Anyone who asks** — no risk factor need be disclosed ("may" receive)
- **Outbreaks:** **1 dose** for all unvaccinated persons **≥1 year** at risk of infection or severe disease; a single dose is adequate for outbreak control (p. 22)
- Settings serving adults with many risk factors (drug-use health care settings, group homes/day care for developmental disabilities): providers **may assume** unvaccinated adults **≥19 years** are at risk; consider standing orders (pp. 20–21)

**Not recommended routinely:** blood-product recipients for clotting disorders (no longer recommended, 2020), food handlers, health care personnel, sewage workers (pp. 9–10).

> [!pearl] Liver disease is a vaccine indication, not a reason to wait
> ACIP recommends routine HepA vaccination in chronic liver disease because HAV raises the risk of fulminant hepatitis there; seroprotection in chronic liver disease is similar to healthy adults ([[acip-2020-hepatitis-a]], pp. 8, 15).

### Vaccines and Immune Globulin

| Vaccine | Age (years) | Dose | Schedule | Booster |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Havrix | 1–18 | 0.5 mL (720 ELISA units) | 0, 6–12 months | None |
| Havrix | ≥19 | 1 mL (1,440 ELISA units) | 0, 6–12 months | None |
| Vaqta | 1–18 | 0.5 mL (25 units) | 0, 6–18 months | None |
| Vaqta | ≥19 | 1 mL (50 units) | 0, 6–18 months | None |
| Twinrix (HepA + HepB) | ≥18 | 1 mL (720 ELISA units HAV + 20 µg HBsAg) | 0, 1, 6 months | None |
| Twinrix, accelerated | ≥18 | Same | 0, 7, 21–30 days | 12 months |

*Hepatitis A vaccines — all intramuscular*
*Source: ACIP 2020 Table 1*
*[[acip-2020-hepatitis-a|ACIP 2020 Hepatitis A Recommendations]]*
*HepA = hepatitis A vaccine · HepB = hepatitis B vaccine · ELISA = enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay · HAV = hepatitis A virus · HBsAg = hepatitis B surface antigen*

- All inactivated; a dose given subcutaneously is still valid (p. 11). Efficacy: Havrix 94%, Vaqta 100% (lower bound 87%) in pediatric trials (p. 13)
- **Twinrix is not for postexposure prophylaxis (PEP)** — half the adult HAV antigen dose, no PEP data (pp. 12, 21)
- **Contraindication:** severe allergic reaction (e.g., anaphylaxis) to a previous dose or severe allergy to a component; moderate–severe acute illness → may defer (p. 16)
- **Duration:** >97% of adults seropositive at 20 years; modeling ≥95% at 30 and ≥90% at 40 years; a single dose protected for almost 11 years (pp. 13–14)
- **Reduced response:** HIV (seroconversion 50%–94%, p. 8; CD4 >200–300 cells/mm³ respond nearly as well as immunocompetent persons, p. 15), transplant, chemotherapy (pp. 14–15); adults ≥40 years less often seroconverted **at 15 days** after dose 1 than adults 20–30 years (79.7% vs 92.3%) (p. 13)
- **IG = GamaSTAN** (only US Food and Drug Administration [FDA]-approved product), intramuscular, weight-based with **no maximum dose** (Table 2, p. 12):
  - PEP: **0.1 mL/kg** within 2 weeks of exposure
  - Travel up to 1 month: 0.1 mL/kg · up to 2 months: 0.2 mL/kg · ≥2 months: 0.2 mL/kg **every 2 months**
  - Live vaccines (measles, mumps and rubella [MMR], varicella) **≥2 weeks before or ≥6 months after IG** (pp. 12–13, 23)

### Postexposure Prophylaxis
Give **as soon as possible and within 2 weeks** of exposure (sexual or household contact, known contaminated food) to anyone not previously vaccinated; efficacy beyond 2 weeks is not established (p. 21). Healthy persons who completed the series at any time need no PEP (Appendix B, p. 36).

| Age / group | HepA vaccine | IG |
|---|---|---|
| <12 months, healthy | No | 0.1 mL/kg |
| 12 months–40 years, healthy | 1 dose | None |
| >40 years, healthy | 1 dose | 0.1 mL/kg if the provider's risk assessment (Table 3) warrants |
| ≥12 months, **immunocompromised or chronic liver disease** | 1 dose | **0.1 mL/kg, same visit, different site** |
| ≥12 months, vaccine contraindicated | No | 0.1 mL/kg |

*Hepatitis A postexposure prophylaxis by age and risk*
*Source: ACIP 2020 Table 4 (postexposure rows)*
*[[acip-2020-hepatitis-a|ACIP 2020 Hepatitis A Recommendations]]*
*HepA = hepatitis A vaccine · IG = immune globulin*

- PEP dose = first dose → second dose at least 6 months later for long-term immunity, not needed for PEP (Table 4 footnote †)
- Only one product available → give it now; return for the other within the 2-week window (p. 23)
- Severely immunocompromised persons vaccinated in the past → assess individually for PEP (p. 21)
- *CDC clinical guidance:* consider IG in addition to vaccine for special risk factors (Tables 3–4) (p. 21)
- Infants <12 months, or contraindicated → no MMR (and no varicella) for 6 months after IG (p. 21)

**By setting (CDC guidance, Appendix B, pp. 36–37):**
- Close contact with **serologically confirmed** HAV (household, sexual, shared drug use, caregivers without personal protective equipment) → PEP **should be given**
- **Child care:** ≥1 case in children, or cases in ≥2 attendee households → PEP for all unvaccinated staff and attendees
  - centers with no diapered children → PEP can be considered **only** for center contacts of the index patient
  - case(s) among employees → consider PEP for other staff and attendees based on duties, hygiene and symptoms at work
- **Food handler case:** patron PEP typically not indicated; may be considered only if the handler **both** handled food without gloves **and** had diarrhea or poor hygiene while likely infectious
- **Schools/offices:** single case from an outside source → not routine; transmission within the setting → PEP for close contacts
- **Congregate settings** (correctional facility, homeless shelter, psychiatric facility, group home): confirmed case → consider PEP for all unvaccinated residents and employees
- **Hospitals:** staff not routinely given PEP; contact precautions for diapered or incontinent patients; case by case when exposure risk is high

### Travel
For susceptible travelers to high or intermediate endemicity (pp. 18, 35):

| Age / group | Before departure |
|---|---|
| <6 months | IG only: 0.1 mL/kg (≤1 month), 0.2 mL/kg (≤2 months), 0.2 mL/kg every 2 months (≥2 months) until vaccinated at ≥6 months |
| 6–11 months | 1 HepA dose — does **not** count toward the series (restart at 1 year). *CDC:* **no IG** — MMR takes priority; HepA (off-label) and MMR can be given together |
| 12 months–40 years, healthy | 1 dose as soon as travel is considered; complete the series later |
| >40 years, immunocompromised, or **chronic liver disease** | 1 dose as soon as travel is considered; leaving **<2 weeks** → IG may be given at the same visit, different site. *CDC:* IG should be considered |
| Vaccine contraindicated or declined | IG by trip length (as for <6 months) |

*Hepatitis A protection before international travel*
*Source: built from ACIP 2020 travel recommendations (p. 18), Table 4 and Appendix A*
*[[acip-2020-hepatitis-a|ACIP 2020 Hepatitis A Recommendations]]*
*IG = immune globulin · HepA = hepatitis A vaccine · MMR = measles, mumps and rubella vaccine · CDC = Centers for Disease Control and Prevention*

- One prior dose ≥6 months ago → give dose 2; prior dose within 6 months → no extra dose for travel (Appendix A, p. 35)
- Table 4 prints the immunocompromised/chronic-liver-disease and declined/contraindicated travel rows as ">6 months", while the text places these groups under ≥12 months (p. 18) — ACIP is internally inconsistent on the age floor

### Testing Before and After Vaccination
- **Prevaccination testing: not routinely recommended** — may be considered to save cost (most useful in adults born or long resident in high/intermediate-endemicity areas); never a barrier to vaccinating; **not indicated in children**; if done → total or IgG anti-HAV (p. 22)
- **Postvaccination testing:** not after routine vaccination; **recommended for HIV and other immunocompromised persons** (hematopoietic cell or solid organ transplant, chemotherapy) (p. 22)
- **HIV** (*CDC guidance*, p. 20): test **≥1 month** after the series (protective = IgG anti-HAV **≥10 mIU/mL**); don't delay vaccination for a CD4 threshold; nonresponders → consider revaccination (repeat series), retest ≥1 month later, still <10 mIU/mL → stop and counsel about IG after exposure
- **Revaccination:** not for healthy persons; may be considered after HIV- or chemotherapy-related nonresponse (pp. 22–23)
- Solid organ transplant candidates, particularly liver transplant candidates (Infectious Diseases Society of America [IDSA] guidance quoted by ACIP, p. 14): unvaccinated, undervaccinated or seronegative candidates aged 12–23 months and ≥2 years should receive a HepA series → see [[liver-transplantation]]

> [!pearl] In cirrhosis, test immunity and vaccinate
> American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2026 Rec 10 recommends HAV vaccination in cirrhosis (strong; low certainty); its text and summary table add testing for immunity first ([[acg-2026-preventive-care-cirrhosis]]). ACIP makes prevaccination testing optional and never a barrier (p. 22); the newer ACG rule governs in cirrhosis — details on [[cirrhosis]].

### Schedule Rules
- Interrupted series → **do not restart** (p. 23)
- Doses <6 months apart → dose 2 invalid; repeat 6 months after the invalid dose (p. 23)
- Doses ≤4 days early are valid, except the first 3 accelerated Twinrix doses (p. 23)
- Adults ≥19 years: never 2 pediatric doses in place of 1 adult dose (p. 23)
- Brands interchangeable if the same one is unavailable or unknown (p. 23)
- Missing records → don't postpone; treat as susceptible (p. 23)
