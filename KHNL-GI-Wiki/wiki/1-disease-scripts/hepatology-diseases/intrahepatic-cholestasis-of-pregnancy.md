## Assessment

Most common pregnancy-specific liver disease; frequency **0.3–0.5%**. Onset in the **second or third trimester** — 80% present after **30 weeks**, though first-trimester cases are reported. Recurs in **45%–70%** of subsequent pregnancies. Fetal stillbirth risk; fetal mortality **0.4%–1%**. Maternal complications are minimal. ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]]; [[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]])

**Risk factors** ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]): advanced maternal age · multiparity · multifetal pregnancy · metabolic syndrome · [[hepatitis-c|hepatitis C virus (HCV)]] infection · personal or family history of intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP) · *ABCB11*, *ABCB4*, *ATP8B1* variants. Highest rates reported in South America (Chile, Araucanian admixture) and in Sweden/the Baltic countries.

> ⚠ **Sources give different prevalence figures.** [[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2024]] states 0.3%–0.5% and is the newest, so it is the figure used above. [[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2016]] quotes 0.3%–5.6%; [[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver|American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD) 2021]] quotes 0.4%–10% overall and **up to 5.6% among Latina women in the United States**. The spread is geographic and ethnic, so expect a higher pre-test probability in those populations.

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Pruritus** (generalized, most severe on palms/soles, **no rash**) in the 2nd/3rd trimester **plus total serum bile acids >10 μmol/L**, with other causes of pruritus excluded = diagnostic. ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]] Best Practice Advice [BPA] 10; [[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]])
  - >10 μmol/L is used as the upper limit of normal because most women without ICP sit below it. No consensus on fasting vs postprandial sampling; levels run **higher postprandially**. ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]])
- **Aminotransferases are supportive, not required.** Aspartate aminotransferase (AST)/alanine aminotransferase (ALT) are frequently elevated but a normal value does not exclude ICP ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]). Reported ranges differ by source: **up to 10–20× upper limit of normal (ULN)** ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]]), **2–30× ULN** ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]), and values **>1,000 U/L** ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]]) — a transaminase in the hundreds does **not** rule ICP out.
- Total bilirubin **<6 mg/dL**; alkaline phosphatase (ALP) and gamma-glutamyltransferase (GGT) elevated. **Jaundice in <25%**, and when present it always follows the onset of pruritus. ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]]; [[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]])
- **Pruritus can precede the laboratory abnormality.** If serum bile acids are **normal**, repeat testing after excluding other causes of pruritus — biliary obstruction, viral hepatitis, and chronic liver disease. ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]]; [[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]])
- Ultrasound is normal in ICP; use it in selected cases to exclude a biliary cause. ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]]; [[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]])

### Severity Assessment

Total serum bile acids stratify **fetal**, not maternal, risk — intrauterine fetal demise (IUFD) rises steeply only in the top band ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]):

| Total bile acids | IUFD prevalence | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| **<40 μmol/L** | **0.1%** | 80% of Swedish ICP cohort sat between 10 and 40 μmol/L, with fetal outcomes **similar to women without ICP** |
| **40–99 μmol/L** | **0.3%** | [[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy\|ACG 2016]]: fetal complications occur once bile acids exceed **40 μmol/L** |
| **≥100 μmol/L** | **3.4%** | Highest-risk group; maternal and fetal bile acid levels correlate, supporting causality |

- Fetal risks beyond IUFD: preterm birth, meconium-stained amniotic fluid, neonatal respiratory depression, asphyxia. ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]])
- Serum aminotransferase elevation does **not** track maternal symptoms or fetal outcome — do not use it to grade severity. ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]])
- Bile-acid strata drive delivery timing — the ladder and its caveats live once, under [[#Delivery Timing (by total bile acids)]].

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[liver-disease-in-pregnancy]].*

- [[acute-fatty-liver-of-pregnancy|Acute fatty liver of pregnancy]] — hypoglycemia, coagulopathy, encephalopathy
- HELLP (hemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, low platelets) syndrome / [[liver-disease-in-pregnancy|pre-eclampsia with hepatic involvement]] — hypertension, thrombocytopenia, hemolysis
- [[chronic-hepatitis-b|Viral hepatitis]] / [[hepatitis-c-in-pregnancy|hepatitis C]] — serologies
- Biliary obstruction ([[choledocholithiasis]]) — dilated ducts on imaging
- [[drug-induced-liver-injury|Drug-induced liver injury]], [[primary-biliary-cholangitis|primary biliary cholangitis (PBC)]] and other chronic cholestatic liver disease — consider if cholestasis persists after delivery

## Diagnostics

*The diagnostic thresholds themselves are stated once, under [[#Establishing the Diagnosis]]. This section covers only what to order and why.*

| Test | Role |
|---|---|
| **Total serum bile acids** | The diagnostic *and* prognostic test — the number that sets delivery timing. Order first; **repeat** if initially normal but pruritus persists after alternative causes are excluded ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]]) |
| **AST/ALT, total bilirubin, ALP/GGT** | Characterise the cholestatic pattern and flag values outside the ICP range (which point elsewhere — see [[abnormal-liver-chemistries]]) |
| **HCV testing** | Recommended in **every** woman with ICP if not already done — HCV infection is a maternal risk factor for ICP ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]) |
| **Prothrombin time (PT)** | Screens for the vitamin K deficiency that drives postpartum haemorrhage and fetal intracranial haemorrhage; monitor more often on cholestyramine ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]) |
| **Ultrasound** | Normal in ICP; used in selected cases to exclude [[choledocholithiasis\|cholelithiasis]] and [[biliary-stricture\|biliary obstruction]] |
| **Genetic testing (*ABCB11*, *ABCB4*, *ATP8B1*)** | Consider in **severe ICP (total bile acids >100 μmol/L)**, **recurrent** ICP, or **early-onset** ICP — carriers may instead have benign recurrent or progressive familial intrahepatic cholestasis, and face risk of progressive liver disease, gallstones, and cholangitis ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]) |

## Therapeutics

### Ursodeoxycholic Acid

- **Oral [[ursodeoxycholic-acid|ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA)]] 10–15 mg/kg/day** (total daily dose, divided) — first-line. ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]] BPA 10; [[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]; [[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy|ACG 2016]] — *strong recommendation, moderate level of evidence*). Trials supporting it used **450–1,000 mg/day**. ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]])
- Improves pruritus and lowers maternal and fetal serum bile acids and ALT; safe in pregnancy.
- UDCA is **more effective than cholestyramine or dexamethasone** for controlling pruritus; it increases bile salt export pump expression and placental bile transporters. ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]])
- **Dexamethasone** may be used to promote fetal lung maturity before delivery, given the higher rate of prematurity in ICP. ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]]) — neither guideline gives a dose or a gestational-age window for it.

> ⚠ **Whether UDCA protects the fetus is unsettled.** [[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver|AGA 2024]] cites a meta-analysis showing **decreased preterm birth and stillbirth**, and is the newer document. [[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver|AASLD 2021]] reaches the opposite conclusion from the largest placebo-controlled randomised trial (n = 606): **no difference in the composite of perinatal death, preterm delivery, and neonatal intensive care stay ≥4 hours — 23% with UDCA vs 27% with placebo** (adjusted risk ratio 0.85; P = 0.28), with no difference in the subgroup whose baseline bile acids were ≥40 μmol/L, and concludes UDCA "has not been shown to reduce fetal complications." Both agree UDCA is **safe** and remains first-line for **pruritus and liver test abnormalities**; do not let it substitute for bile-acid–stratified delivery timing.

### Refractory Pruritus

Add only if UDCA fails; all appear safe in pregnancy, and the evidence of benefit is scant ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]):

- Antihistamines
- **Cholestyramine** — at high doses worsens **vitamin K deficiency**, raising the risk of fetal intracranial haemorrhage; monitor PT more frequently while on it
- **Rifampicin**
- **SAMe** — named by the guidance without an expansion or dose
- **Vitamin K** when the PT is prolonged ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]])
- [[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver|AGA 2024]] names cholestyramine and rifampicin without doses; dosing for both appears on [[primary-biliary-cholangitis]] for non-pregnant cholestatic pruritus, and no ingested guideline states a pregnancy-specific dose.

### Delivery Timing (by total bile acids)

Delivery is advised based on **total serum bile acid levels** ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]]):

| Total bile acids | Recommended delivery |
|---|---|
| **≥100 μmol/L** | **36 weeks** (or at diagnosis if diagnosed after 36 wk) — Society for Maternal-Fetal Medicine (SMFM); European Association for the Study of the Liver (EASL): consider early delivery as stillbirth risk rises after 35 wk |
| **40–99 μmol/L** | **36 or 39 weeks** (if diagnosed later) |
| **<40 μmol/L** | Delivery at **term** reasonable; individualize |

- **Why 40 μmol/L splits the lower two rows:** [[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver|AGA 2024]] writes its middle band as "<100 μmol/L → deliver at 36 or 39 wk" while separately advising delivery at term for "<40 μmol/L", so the two bands overlap as printed. The 40 μmol/L line is the point at which fetal complications begin ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]]) and at which IUFD triples ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]) — see [[#Severity Assessment]].
- **No source gives a rule for choosing 36 vs 39 weeks** within the middle band.
- **Alternative timing:** the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG), as relayed by [[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver|AASLD 2021]], advises delivery at **36–37 weeks, or at diagnosis if diagnosed after 37 weeks**, without bile-acid stratification. Some favour individualizing, weighing the known harms of iatrogenic preterm delivery (respiratory distress) against the small, poorly characterised risk of IUFD.

> ⚠ **Superseded recommendation:** [[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy|ACG 2016]] recommended **early delivery at 37 weeks for all ICP** (*strong recommendation, very low level of evidence*), without bile-acid stratification. The bile-acid–stratified timing above (AGA 2024) is newer and supersedes it — but readers carrying the flat 37-week rule should know it changed.

### Antenatal Monitoring

- **No method of fetal monitoring has been shown to predict adverse perinatal outcome or to reduce IUFD** — IUFD appears to develop suddenly. Monitoring is therefore guided by best obstetric practice rather than by an ICP-specific protocol. ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]])

### Postpartum

- Pruritus typically **resolves within days to weeks** after delivery.
- **Recheck liver tests and bile acids postpartum to confirm resolution.** ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]])
- Persistent symptoms, liver test abnormalities, or bile-acid elevation — including pruritus persisting **>6 weeks postpartum** — warrant evaluation for underlying chronic liver disease, including cholestasis-associated genetic variants. See [[abnormal-liver-chemistries]]. ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]]; [[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]])
- ICP may raise the later risk of **gallstones and biliary fibrosis/cirrhosis** — possibly because chronic liver disease was misdiagnosed as ICP, or because of an underlying transporter variant. ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]])
