## Assessment

- Rare medical and obstetric emergency unique to pregnancy — **microvesicular fatty infiltration** of the liver leading to hepatic failure.
- **Frequency ~0.01%** of pregnancies ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]]; 0.005%–0.010% per [[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]); **5.0 cases per 100,000 maternities** in the United Kingdom cohort in which the Swansea criteria were validated ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]]).
- Onset in the **third trimester (week 29 and beyond) or, rarely, postpartum**; **median gestational age at identification 36 weeks**.
- Risk factors: **twin/multifetal pregnancy**, **male fetus**, **primiparity**, **low body mass index**; family history occasionally present.

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **The diagnosis is made clinically** — compatible presentation, laboratory, and imaging results, with the Swansea criteria as the supporting checklist. ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]]; [[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]])
- Presenting symptoms are nonspecific: headache, malaise, nausea/vomiting, abdominal pain (right upper quadrant or epigastric), polydipsia/polyuria, jaundice — then [[hepatic-encephalopathy|hepatic encephalopathy]], liver failure, and disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC). Renal dysfunction and [[acute-pancreatitis|pancreatitis]] are common.
- **Pre-eclampsia is present in roughly half** of cases, and the presentation overlaps with it — finding pre-eclampsia does not argue against acute fatty liver of pregnancy (AFLP).
- **[[liver-biopsy|Liver biopsy]] is rarely needed** and should not be obtained unless the diagnosis is in doubt or the result will affect management. If done, reserve tissue for special stains (Oil Red O) or electron microscopy to confirm fat. ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]] Best Practice Advice (BPA) 11; [[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]])

**Swansea criteria — ≥6 of these 14, in the absence of another cause.** Thresholds as printed by each guideline ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2016]] Table 4; [[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver|American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD) 2021]]):

| Criterion | ACG 2016 | AASLD 2021 |
|---|---|---|
| Vomiting | — | — |
| Abdominal pain | — | — |
| Polydipsia/polyuria | — | — |
| Encephalopathy | — | — |
| Elevated transaminases — aspartate aminotransferase (AST) or alanine aminotransferase (ALT) | >42 IU/L | >42 IU/L |
| Elevated bilirubin | >14 µmol/L | >0.8 mg/dL |
| Hypoglycemia | <4 mmol/L | <72 mg/dL |
| Leucocytosis | >11×10⁶ cells/L | >11×10⁶/L |
| Elevated **urea** (ACG) / **uric acid** (AASLD) | >340 µmol/L | >5.7 mg/dL |
| Elevated ammonia | >47 µmol/L | >42 IU/L |
| [[ascites\|Ascites]] or bright liver on ultrasound | — | — |
| Renal impairment — creatinine | >150 µmol/L | >1.7 mg/dL |
| Coagulopathy | prothrombin time (PT) >14 s **or** activated partial thromboplastin time >34 s | PT >14 s **or** partial thromboplastin time >34 s |
| Microvesicular steatosis on biopsy | — | — |

- **Both documents print leucocytosis as >11×10⁶ cells/L** — three orders of magnitude below the >11×10⁹/L (11,000/µL) laboratories report. Read the row as a white cell count above the normal upper limit.
- The two differ on two rows: ACG names **urea**, AASLD names **uric acid**; and AASLD prints the ammonia threshold in the same value and unit as its transaminase row (>42 IU/L). Use the criterion, not the misprinted unit.
- Applied to women with suspected pregnancy-related liver disease who underwent liver biopsy, the criteria gave **85% positive predictive value and 100% negative predictive value** for hepatic microvesicular steatosis — enough, in the ACG authors' judgement, to obviate biopsy in clinical management. ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]])

> [!pitfall] A Swansea score does not settle the diagnosis
> In women with severe AFLP and features of [[acute-liver-failure|acute liver failure]], the criteria have **high sensitivity but low specificity** ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]), and the American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) notes **limitations in differentiating AFLP from the other liver diseases unique to pregnancy and from acute liver failure of any cause** ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]] BPA 11). Scoring ≥6 supports AFLP; it does not exclude the mimics.

### Severity Assessment

- Severity tracks the degree of hepatic failure — hypoglycemia, coagulopathy/DIC, acute renal failure, and encephalopathy mark severe disease (values under [[#Diagnostics]]).
- **Maternal mortality 7%–18%; fetal mortality 9%–23%**, with acute renal failure the dominant maternal morbidity ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]] Table 3). [[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver|AASLD 2021]] puts recent maternal mortality at **<10%**.
- **Level of care:** in the United Kingdom cohort **65% were admitted to an intensive care or specialty liver unit** (1 death in the series) — plan for critical-care and transplant-centre level of care from diagnosis. ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]])

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[liver-disease-in-pregnancy]].*

- Hemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, and low platelets (HELLP) syndrome — shares abdominal pain, vomiting, and thrombocytopenia. HELLP carries hemolysis, platelets <100×10⁹/L, elevated uric acid, and ↑lactate dehydrogenase (LDH); **hypoglycemia, prolonged PT/DIC, elevated ammonia, and rising creatinine point to AFLP** ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]] Table 3)
- Pre-eclampsia with hepatic involvement — coexists with AFLP rather than excluding it
- [[intrahepatic-cholestasis-of-pregnancy|Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy]] — pruritus, ↑bile acids, normal imaging, no coagulopathy or hypoglycemia
- Viral hepatitis — [[chronic-hepatitis-b|hepatitis B]], [[hepatitis-c-in-pregnancy|hepatitis C]], hepatitis A, hepatitis E, herpes simplex virus (HSV) hepatitis; HSV in particular mimics the AFLP/HELLP picture with high transaminases, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, and acute renal failure ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]])
- [[drug-induced-liver-injury|Drug-induced liver injury]]

## Diagnostics

- **Laboratory pattern:** total bilirubin **<5 mg/dL**, **hypoglycemia**, **elevated creatinine**, ±proteinuria and thrombocytopenia, **elevated ammonia**, **ALT <500 U/L**, **prolonged PT**, **DIC in >75%**. ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]] Table 3)
- Also described: **low antithrombin III, low fibrinogen, elevated LDH**, elevated bilirubin, platelets <100×10⁹/L. ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]])
- **Transaminase range differs by source** — [[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver|AGA 2024]] (newest, and what this page asserts) gives **ALT <500 U/L**; [[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver|AASLD 2021]] gives **AST and ALT 300–1,000 U/L**; [[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy|ACG 2016]] describes "striking aminotransferase elevations" without numbers. A transaminase in the hundreds fits AFLP.
- **DIC frequency differs by source** — AGA 2024 prints **>75%**, AASLD 2021 **10%**.
- **Imaging:** fatty infiltration, sometimes [[ascites]]. **Perform abdominal imaging in AFLP to exclude hepatic hemorrhage, infarct, or rupture** — these occur in <2%, and hepatic rupture mandates transfer to a transplant centre. ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]])

> [!pearl] Encephalopathy late in pregnancy means AFLP until proven otherwise
> "If hepatic encephalopathy occurs, there should be a high suspicion for AFLP" ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]). The transaminases will not make the call — they overlap with the mimics. The **failure** features do: hypoglycemia, prolonged PT/DIC, encephalopathy, rising creatinine.

## Therapeutics

| Action | Detail | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| **Deliver promptly** | **Expectant management is not appropriate**; expedite delivery once the mother is stabilized. No ingested guideline sets a gestational-age threshold for AFLP — unlike HELLP (especially after 34 weeks) and severe pre-eclampsia (after 36 weeks), delivery is prompt at whatever gestation AFLP is recognised. The postpartum course depends on the interval between symptom onset and termination of the pregnancy | Strong, very low ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]] Rec 17); ungraded guidance statement ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]) |
| Supportive care | Early recognition plus supportive care optimizes maternal and fetal prognosis: correct **hypoglycemia** and coagulopathy/DIC, manage acute renal failure, and treat concomitant hypertension, seizures, and other symptoms while preparing the patient for childbirth. Watch for infection and for bleeding from the vagina or a cesarean wound | — ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]] BPA 11; [[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]]; [[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]) |
| **[[liver-transplantation\|Liver transplant]] evaluation** | AFLP usually resolves on delivery; progression to [[acute-liver-failure\|acute liver failure]] — or hepatic rupture — should prompt **transfer to a transplant centre**, **especially suspected AFLP already delivered who do not improve clinically**. Transplant outcomes in AFLP are comparable to other indications. Listing criteria live on [[acute-liver-failure]] | — ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]] BPA 11; [[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]; [[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]]) |
| **Long-chain 3-hydroxyacyl-CoA dehydrogenase (LCHAD) molecular testing** | **All women with AFLP and their children**; newborns screened **at birth** for LCHAD deficiency or other fatty acid oxidation defects, with **genetic counseling** for affected families. Mechanism: a LCHAD-deficient fetus spills hepatotoxic long-chain 3-hydroxy-fatty acyl metabolites into the maternal circulation. Some mothers with AFLP are themselves LCHAD heterozygotes, and not all defects causing LCHAD deficiency confer AFLP risk | Conditional, moderate ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]] Rec 18); ungraded guidance statement ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]) |
| **Monitor the offspring** | Watch for manifestations of LCHAD deficiency — **hypoketotic hypoglycemia** and **fatty liver**, also infantile cholestasis and hypocalcemia; heart, liver, and skeletal muscle are frequently involved, with no clear genotype–phenotype correlation. Untreated, metabolic crises and death can occur in the first year; **dietary modification reduces morbidity and mortality**, and most symptoms are reversible with adequate energy supply | Conditional, very low ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]] Rec 19) |
