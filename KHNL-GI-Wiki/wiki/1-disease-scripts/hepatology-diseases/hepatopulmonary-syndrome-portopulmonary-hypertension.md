Two pulmonary **vascular** complications of liver disease and [[portal-hypertension|portal hypertension]] — unique to liver disease, relatively common, and both may resolve after [[liver-transplantation|liver transplant (LT)]]. Both are decisive for LT candidacy: hepatopulmonary syndrome (HPS) earns exception points and is cured by LT, whereas uncontrolled portopulmonary hypertension (POPH) is a contraindication to it.

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

| | **Hepatopulmonary syndrome** | **Portopulmonary hypertension** |
|---|---|---|
| Lesion | Intrapulmonary vascular dilatations (IPVDs) → abnormal oxygenation | Vasoconstriction + remodeling of pulmonary arterial resistance vessels |
| Setting | Chronic liver disease and/or portal hypertension | [[cirrhosis]] and/or portal hypertension |
| Class | — | Pulmonary arterial hypertension, World Health Organization (WHO) **group 1** |
| Criteria | **All three** required: (1) liver disease (usually cirrhosis with portal hypertension); (2) **positive contrast-enhanced transthoracic echocardiography (CE-TTE)** — microbubbles in the left heart **≥3 cardiac cycles** after right-heart microbubbles, following **10 mL** agitated saline injected in a peripheral arm vein; **and** (3) alveolar–arterial oxygen gradient (AaO₂) **≥15 mm Hg** (**>20 mm Hg** if age >64) | **All three** required: (1) portal hypertension (clinical — varices, splenomegaly, ascites, or portal pressure); (2) screen by transthoracic echocardiography, **confirm by right heart catheterization (RHC)**; (3) RHC triad **mean pulmonary artery pressure (mPAP) >25 mm Hg · pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) >3 Wood units (WU) (240 dynes/s per cm⁻⁵) · pulmonary artery wedge pressure (PAWP) <15 mm Hg** ([[ilts-2016-hps-poph\|ILTS 2016]] Rec 5, strong/Level B) |

- **Exclude other causes of pulmonary hypertension** in liver disease before attributing it to POPH — high flow, excess volume, diastolic dysfunction, obstructive/restrictive lung disease, sleep-disordered breathing (ILTS Rec 6, weak/C). RHC is what separates these: POPH is **flow obstruction** with a normal wedge, whereas the hyperkinetic circulation of cirrhosis raises mPAP at a **normal PVR**.
- **CE-TTE timing separates the shunt from a hole in the heart** — left-heart microbubbles at **≥3 cardiac cycles** = intrapulmonary shunting; at **1–2 cycles** = intracardiac shunting (patent foramen ovale, atrial septal defect). Transesophageal echocardiography can image the source directly when the distinction matters.

> [!pitfall] A normal wedge is part of the POPH definition
> An elevated mPAP alone does not make POPH. Without **PVR >3 WU** and **PAWP <15 mm Hg**, the picture is volume overload or left heart disease, and a **transpulmonary gradient (mPAP − PAWP) <12 mm Hg** points to pulmonary *venous* hypertension — which does not qualify for a MELD exception ([[ilts-2016-hps-poph]]).

### Severity Assessment

**HPS — graded by PaO₂ on arterial blood gas (ABG)** (European Respiratory Society [ERS] Task Force bands, [[ilts-2016-hps-poph|ILTS 2016]] Rec 7, weak/C):

| Severity | PaO₂ |
|---|---|
| Mild | **≥80 mm Hg** |
| Moderate | **60–79 mm Hg** |
| Severe | **50–59 mm Hg** |
| Very severe | **<50 mm Hg** |

- PaO₂ **<60 mm Hg** with portal hypertension and no other pulmonary etiology → **indication for LT with expedited consideration**, and qualifies for **standard Model for End-Stage Liver Disease (MELD) exception points** (ILTS Recs 29, 32, both strong/B; US — see Organ Procurement and Transplantation Network (OPTN) policy for the full non-malignancy exception list). A **higher** exception score should be considered at PaO₂ **<50 mm Hg** (Rec 33, strong/B).
- **No PaO₂ cutoff excludes LT** — select patients with PaO₂ **<50 mm Hg** have been transplanted successfully (Rec 31, strong/C). But **pre-LT PaO₂ <45–50 mm Hg** is associated with increased transplant hospital mortality, morbidity and severe post-LT hypoxemia (Rec 30, strong/B).

**POPH — graded by mPAP, assuming increased PVR** (ILTS Rec 18, strong/B):

| Severity | mPAP |
|---|---|
| Mild | **25 ≤ mPAP < 35 mm Hg** |
| Moderate | **35 ≤ mPAP < 45 mm Hg** |
| Severe | **mPAP ≥ 45 mm Hg** |

- Candidacy, however, is set by mPAP **+ PVR on optimized therapy**, not by the band alone (see [[#Therapeutics]]).
- **Severity does not track the liver.** Neither the severity of liver disease nor the degree of portal hypertension (hepatic venous pressure gradient) predicts the presence or severity of POPH — the same is true of HPS.

## Differential Diagnosis

*No diagnostic schema covers dyspnea/hypoxemia in liver disease — the workup is the pulmonary arm of the LT candidate evaluation, laid out under [[#Diagnostics]] below. For the hepatic side of the assessment see [[abnormal-liver-chemistries]] and [[cirrhosis]].*

Pulmonary disease in the LT candidate falls into three groups; HPS/POPH are group 3, and the other two must be excluded before attributing hypoxemia or pulmonary hypertension to the liver:

1. **Disorders affecting both organs** — cystic fibrosis, alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency, sarcoidosis, [[hereditary-hemorrhagic-telangiectasia|hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia]]. Uncommon, and usually recognized before LT consideration.
2. **Lung disorders independent of liver disease** — asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), interstitial lung disease, lung nodules. No established screening protocol, but testing is common.
3. **Pulmonary vascular complications of liver disease** — HPS and POPH.

- For POPH specifically, RHC must exclude **left heart disease** as the cause of the elevated pressures.
- Other cirrhosis-related causes of respiratory compromise ([[hepatic-hydrothorax|hepatic hydrothorax]], tense [[ascites]]) are treated on their own terms — see [[acute-on-chronic-liver-failure]].

## Diagnostics

*Graded statements from [[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-candidate-evaluation]] (Recs 17–22).*

| Test | Recommendation | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Chest imaging | Perform in **all** LT candidates (covers differential groups 1–2) | Strong, Level 4 |
| Pulmonary function tests | **Not** indicated routinely absent concomitant lung disease or significant pulmonary risk factors (tobacco, occupational, environmental) | Strong, Level 2 |
| Bubble echocardiography (agitated saline contrast) | **Most sensitive** test for IPVDs/HPS; recommended for screening when available pre-LT | Weak, Level 1 |
| Bubble echocardiography — targeted use | In centers not screening routinely, perform when **pulmonary symptoms or oxygenation abnormalities** are present (dyspnea without cardiac dysfunction, platypnea, orthodeoxia) | Strong, Level 3 |
| Pulse oximetry + ABG | Consider once contrast echo confirms IPVDs, to determine **severity and urgency for transplantation** | Strong, Level 3 |
| Right heart catheterization | Required when TTE shows **right ventricular systolic pressure (RVSP) >45 mm Hg** or abnormal right ventricular (RV) structure/function, with subspecialty consultation if POPH is confirmed | Strong, Level 1 |

- The guideline authors could **not reach consensus** on routine bubble echo for every candidate — hence "when available and when HPS is suspected."
- Pulse oximetry alone is insensitive for HPS; pair it with ABG.

**Operating thresholds for each test** ([[ilts-2016-hps-poph|ILTS 2016]]):

| Test | Threshold | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Pulse oximetry (HPS screen) | O₂ saturation **<96%** | Reasonable screen for hypoxemia in an otherwise suitable LT candidate — **ABG is still required to diagnose HPS** (Rec 10, strong/C) |
| CE-TTE | Microbubbles in left heart **≥3 cardiac cycles** | Optimal screening test and criterion for IPVD in adults (Rec 11, strong/B) |
| ⁹⁹ᵐTc-macroaggregated albumin (MAA) lung perfusion scan | Brain **uptake >6%** | Attributes hypoxemia to IPVD rather than to intrinsic lung disease when both coexist (Rec 12, strong/C). Normal in non-HPS hypoxemia; **cannot** distinguish intracardiac from intrapulmonary shunting |
| Transthoracic Doppler echocardiography (TDE) (POPH screen) | Pulmonary artery systolic pressure **>30 mm Hg** | Negative predictive value **100%**, positive predictive value only **59%** — a negative study rules POPH out, a positive one does not rule it in |
| TDE → proceed to RHC | Right ventricular systolic pressure **>50 mm Hg**, and/or significant RV hypertrophy or dysfunction | Likely requires RHC for hemodynamics and consistency with POPH |

- **Screen for POPH with TDE in anyone with portal hypertension who is a candidate for TIPS or LT** (Rec 21, strong/B), and **repeat it while waitlisted** — ILTS states outright that **the optimal interval is unclear** (Rec 22, weak/C).
- **Risk factors do not substitute for screening.** Female sex and autoimmune liver disease are POPH risk factors but **should not be used to select who gets screened** (Rec 19, weak/B).
- **Bedside signs of HPS** — platypnea (dyspnea worsening from supine to upright) and orthodeoxia (**PaO₂ fall >5%, or >4 mm Hg**, moving supine to upright), present in up to **25%**; digital clubbing, cyanosis and diffuse telangiectasias in advanced disease, with reduced diffusing capacity for carbon monoxide.

**Surveillance outside the transplant evaluation** ([[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension|Baveno VIII]] 9.36, level of evidence [LoE] 4, strong, new): in patients with portal hypertension and/or **portosystemic shunts — spontaneous *or* interventional (e.g. [[tips|transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS)]])** — **annual surveillance is warranted** for shunt-related complications, **especially the occurrence of POPH**. The statement sits in the paediatric chronic-[[portal-vein-thrombosis|portal vein thrombosis (PVT)]] panel; Baveno VIII gives no surveillance modality or threshold with it.

## Therapeutics

**HPS**

- **LT is curative** — no PaO₂ threshold precludes it. Resolution is **expected** after deceased- or living-donor LT, is temporally related to pre-LT severity, and **may take several months with continued supplemental oxygen** ([[ilts-2016-hps-poph|ILTS 2016]] Rec 41, strong/B).
- **There is no drug therapy.** Beyond supplemental oxygen at rest, on exercise and in sleep, **none is established or FDA-approved** (Rec 13, weak/B) — somatostatin, almitrine, indomethacin, norfloxacin, nebulized L-NAME, aspirin, plasma exchange and pentoxifylline all tried without clear benefit.
- **Portal decompression is not the answer either** — TIPS is of **uncertain** benefit in adults (Rec 14, weak/C); coil embolization rarely helps selected patients (weak/C); correction of **congenital** portosystemic shunts in children appears beneficial (weak/C).
- **Monitoring:** sequential pulse oximetry (Rec 17, strong/C). Post-LT, reassess by oximetry and **stop supplemental oxygen once O₂ saturation stays >88%** at rest, on exercise and in sleep (Recs 42–43, weak/C). Routine post-LT CE-TTE is **not** advised (weak/C).
- **Perioperative:** monitor mixed venous oxygen saturation (SvO₂) — **<65%** on vascular exclusion of the liver may warrant venovenous bypass (Rec 34, weak/C). Target post-LT saturation **≥85%** (strong/C), extubate early (strong/B), use goal-directed fluids (weak/C), and consider inhaled nitric oxide (weak/B). **ECMO** can bridge to LT and reduce the need for mechanical ventilation in severe HPS (Rec 40, weak/B).

**POPH — candidacy is the therapeutic decision**

| mPAP | PVR | On optimized therapy → status |
|---|---|---|
| <35 mm Hg | <5 Wood units (WU) | Standard **MELD exception** criteria |
| 35–45 mm Hg | <3 WU | Standard **MELD exception** criteria |
| 45 mm Hg ⚠ | >3 WU | **Contraindication to transplantation** (Strong, Level 1) |

- ⚠ **The contraindication row is missing its operator in the source.** Rec 24 reads verbatim: *"a mean pulmonary artery pressure of 45 mm Hg with a pulmonary vascular resistance > 3 Wood units (WU) should be considered a contraindication to transplantation."* The `>` on PVR is present, but **no `≥` or `>` is printed before the 45 mm Hg**. Read alongside the exception rows, the intent is a ceiling at 45 — but do not assert `≥45` or `>45` as the sourced cutoff.
- **The rows are not exhaustive** — the guideline gives no status for mPAP 35–45 mm Hg with PVR 3–5 WU, nor for mPAP <35 mm Hg with PVR ≥5 WU. Those combinations fall outside both the exception criteria and the contraindication as written.
- **The older [[ilts-2016-hps-poph|ILTS 2016]] formulation of the ceiling differs** — it makes **mPAP of 45 to 50 mm Hg or greater** an absolute contraindication to LT **with no PVR condition attached** (Rec 48, strong/C), and does not support POPH, treated or untreated, as an indication for LT at all (Rec 45, weak/C). AASLD 2025 is the newer guideline, so the combined mPAP + PVR rule in the table above is what applies; the ILTS range is the formulation many centres still quote. ILTS' own exception criteria are stricter in one respect: a baseline **mPAP >35 mm Hg** falling on therapy to **mPAP <35 mm Hg and PVR <400 dynes/s per cm⁻⁵ (5 WU)**, or failing that, **normalization of PVR (<240 dynes/s per cm⁻⁵, i.e. 3 WU) and of RV function** (Recs 49–50).
- Optimization must be directed by a **pulmonary hypertension specialist**; subspecialty consultation is required once POPH is confirmed.
- Sustained response to medical therapy → LT can be considered, **particularly with more advanced liver disease** (Weak, Level 2); the survival benefit of LT in responders is greatest at **MELD >15**.
- Poorly controlled POPH carries poor 5-year survival; targeted medications have improved control and prolonged survival.
- **≥50%** of POPH patients have resolution after LT, and long-term post-LT survival is excellent in medical-therapy responders — the basis for the standard MELD exception.
- Intraoperative extracorporeal support to bridge higher-risk POPH patients is of increasing interest but has **not** shown long-term survival benefit (too few supported patients reaching LT).

**POPH — what the targeted therapy is** ([[ilts-2016-hps-poph|ILTS 2016]]). Agents approved for pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) may improve hemodynamics and exercise capacity in POPH (Rec 27, strong/C); **start them at mPAP ≥35 mm Hg** (Rec 46, weak/B), continue them **throughout the transplant** (Rec 54, strong/C), and afterwards **wean rather than stop abruptly** (Rec 60, strong/C), guided by TDE **every 3 months** (Rec 61, weak/C).

| Class | Agents named | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Prostacyclin analogues | Intravenous epoprostenol; intravenous/subcutaneous treprostinil; inhaled iloprost | Progressive splenomegaly and thrombocytopenia reported with intravenous epoprostenol — slower titration and lower doses may prevent it |
| Phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitors | Sildenafil | Improves PVR, mPAP and cardiac output in small series; **esophageal variceal hemorrhage has been reported** with its use |
| Endothelin receptor antagonists (ERAs) | Bosentan (dual); ambrisentan (selective); macitentan (under trial) | FDA labeling recommends **avoiding any ERA in moderate-to-severe liver dysfunction or elevated transaminases** |

**Drugs to avoid or minimize in POPH**

- **Calcium channel blockers — do not initiate** (Rec 24, weak/C): they worsen fluid retention and RV function and raise portal pressures.
- **Anticoagulation is not advised** — unstudied, and varices contraindicate it (Rec 23, weak/C).
- **[[nonselective-beta-blockers|Beta-blockers]] minimized; treat varices by band ligation instead** (Rec 26, weak/B). At mPAP ≥35–40 mm Hg, withdrawal may increase cardiac output and exercise capacity.
- **Acute vasoreactivity testing at RHC is not required** (Rec 25, weak/C).

> [!pitfall] Severe POPH closes the door on TIPS as well as transplant
> Severe POPH (**mPAP ≥45–50 mm Hg**) is an **absolute contraindication to elective [[tips|transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS)]]**; moderate POPH (35 ≤ mPAP < 45 mm Hg) is a **relative** contraindication ([[ilts-2016-hps-poph]] Rec 28, strong/C). TIPS raises right ventricular filling pressures and cardiac output.

**POPH in the operating room** — pulmonary artery catheter before and during LT in **all adult cases** (Rec 51, strong/C); pre-incision mPAP **≥35 mm Hg** → measure cardiac output, PAWP and PVR (weak/C); pre-incision mPAP **>45–50 mm Hg → defer the transplant** while RV function and reversibility are assessed (Rec 53, strong/C). Transesophageal echocardiography available for RV monitoring; inhaled nitric oxide or intravenous milrinone at reperfusion; **no right ventricular assist devices — risk of pulmonary artery rupture** (Rec 58, weak/C).

- **Counsel every POPH candidate that LT risk is higher regardless of hemodynamics** (Rec 63, strong/B).

**Where HPS/POPH changes a decision *other than* LT candidacy**

- **Blocks a surgical shunt.** **Distal splenorenal shunting** for recurrent [[variceal-upper-gi-bleeding|variceal haemorrhage]] in children with chronic PVT should be considered **only in the absence of HPS, POPH and/or [[hepatic-encephalopathy|hepatic encephalopathy (HE)]]** ([[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension|Baveno VIII]] 9.35, LoE 3, strong). Sequence and alternatives: [[portal-vein-thrombosis|PVT → Paediatric PVT]]
- **Is itself an LT indication in non-cirrhotic portal hypertension.** In [[porto-sinusoidal-vascular-disorder|porto-sinusoidal vascular disorder (PSVD)/non-cirrhotic portal fibrosis (NCPF)]], LT should be considered for **hepatopulmonary syndrome** alongside severe/refractory portal-hypertension complications, [[hepatocellular-carcinoma|hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)]], or advanced liver dysfunction — discussed at centres with vascular-liver-disease expertise (Baveno VIII 8.53, LoE 4, strong)
- **Must be worked up in the decompensating patient.** In cirrhosis with **respiratory failure**, investigate and treat the coexisting cirrhosis-related pulmonary comorbidities — hepatic hydrothorax, ascites, and HPS; where the compromise is from hydrothorax or tense ascites, therapeutic thoracentesis/paracentesis is recommended ([[aasld-2024-aclf]] Rec 18)
