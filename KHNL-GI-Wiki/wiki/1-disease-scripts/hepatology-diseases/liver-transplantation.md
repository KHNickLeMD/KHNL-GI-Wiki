*Liver transplantation (LT) per the 2025 AASLD/AST guidelines on candidate evaluation, graft complications, and non-graft complications; 2025 governs where it differs from [[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]].*

## Assessment

### Referral for Liver Transplant

| Indication | Refer when |
|---|---|
| Chronic liver disease **with a decompensating event** | Any of: [[ascites]] (± [[spontaneous-bacterial-peritonitis\|spontaneous bacterial peritonitis]]), [[hepatic-encephalopathy\|hepatic encephalopathy]], [[variceal-upper-gi-bleeding\|variceal hemorrhage]], [[hepatopulmonary-syndrome-portopulmonary-hypertension\|hepatopulmonary syndrome/portopulmonary hypertension (POPH)]], [[hepatocellular-carcinoma\|hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)]] |
| Chronic liver disease, **no decompensating event** | Model for End-Stage Liver Disease (MELD) threshold *unclear*: **MELD 3.0 ≥12** = survival-benefit signal (living donor LT [LDLT] from MELD 11), but those studies did not test referral timing; working group endorses **MELD 15** absent portal hypertensive complications. Formula: [[cirrhosis]] |
| [[acute-on-chronic-liver-failure\|Acute-on-chronic liver failure (ACLF)]] | Promptly, any MELD; MELD >40 or ACLF grade alone not a contraindication |
| [[acute-liver-failure\|Acute liver failure]] | **All**, urgently — within hours, before formal criteria are met |
| HCC | All without extrahepatic metastases — Milan, alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), downstaging on [[hepatocellular-carcinoma]] |
| [[cholangiocarcinoma\|Cholangiocarcinoma]] | Unresectable perihilar <3 cm radial diameter, neoadjuvant protocol; mixed HCC-cholangiocarcinoma or intrahepatic <3 cm without extrahepatic disease — may consider (weak) |

- Under-referred groups: [[alcohol-associated-liver-disease|alcohol-associated liver disease (ALD)]], African American race, lower socioeconomic status.

> [!pitfall] MELD alone never blocks referral
> Refer at **any MELD** when portal hypertensive complications are uncontrolled — many low-MELD patients are "underrepresented by MELD" ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-candidate-evaluation]]).

### Pre-Transplant Evaluation

Asks three things: prognosis without LT, capacity for a successful LT, and the patient's wishes.

| Domain | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Cardiac | Electrocardiogram + transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) for all. **Coronary computed tomography (CT) angiography** preferred if any [[nafld-masld\|metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD)]] risk factor, chronic kidney disease (CKD), left ventricular hypertrophy, family history of premature coronary disease, tobacco, or calcium score >0 |
| Pulmonary | Chest imaging for all; pulmonary function tests only with lung-disease risk factors. Bubble echo for hepatopulmonary syndrome; right ventricular systolic pressure **>45 mmHg** on TTE → right heart catheterization. Thresholds, MELD exceptions: [[hepatopulmonary-syndrome-portopulmonary-hypertension]] |
| Infection | **All:** cytomegalovirus (CMV) and Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) IgG; hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg), core antibody (anti-HBc), surface antibody (anti-HBs); anti-hepatitis C virus (HCV); human immunodeficiency virus; tuberculosis; syphilis; *Toxoplasma*; varicella-zoster virus (VZV); hepatitis E (conditional); rectal + nasal swabs for multidrug-resistant organisms. **Exposure-based:** *Coccidioides*, *Histoplasma*, *Strongyloides*, *Trypanosoma cruzi* |
| Vaccines | All age-appropriate before LT; live vaccines (measles-mumps-rubella [MMR], varicella) **≥4 weeks** pre-LT |
| Cancer | Per existing guidelines — HCC: ultrasound (US) + AFP q6 mo; colorectal **≥45 y**: [[colonoscopy]] within 10 y or stool DNA–fecal immunochemical test q1–3 y (colonoscopy only if above-average risk); breast, cervical, lung, prostate per general-population schedules |
| [[nutrition-in-liver-disease\|Nutrition]], function | Dietitian for all; Liver Frailty Index or Karnofsky score — frailty alone **not** a contraindication |
| Bone | Vitamin D + dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA) for all stable patients; low density → calcium, vitamin D, weight-bearing exercise; bisphosphonate case-by-case |
| Dental, body mass index (BMI) | Dental evaluation, but never delay lifesaving LT for it; BMI >40 or <18.5 → screen for treatable factors, not exclusionary alone |
| Psychosocial | Adherence, mental-health stability, caregiver support. ALD: **no fixed abstinence interval** for listing. Methadone/buprenorphine: not grounds to deny, need not stop. Tobacco, smoked marijuana, illicit drugs: cessation expected. Serious mental illness: treat and reassess |

### Contraindications and Special Situations

- **Absolute:** anatomy precluding LT; advanced cardiopulmonary disease (severe left ventricular dysfunction, severe valvular disease, end-stage lung disease) without eligibility for combined-organ transplant; POPH with **mean pulmonary artery pressure 45 mmHg + pulmonary vascular resistance >3 Wood units** on optimized therapy.
- **Relative / case-by-case:** MELD >40 or ACLF (trajectory, futility); frailty; mental illness; BMI extremes; **Yerdel grade IV [[portal-vein-thrombosis|portal vein thrombosis (PVT)]]** without adequate collaterals (grading criteria on that page).
- **LDLT:** offer education to all; older age alone no bar. Graft-to-recipient weight ratio **≥0.8%**; caution when **MELD >30**.
- **Accepted with strict selection:** unresectable [[gastroenteropancreatic-neuroendocrine-tumors|neuroendocrine tumors]]; hereditary hepatic epithelioid hemangioendothelioma; unresectable colorectal liver metastases.
- **Multiorgan** by organ-team consensus: heart-liver (Fontan-associated liver disease, transthyretin amyloidosis); liver-kidney (CKD or acute kidney injury not expected to resolve); liver-lung (cystic fibrosis, alpha-1-antitrypsin deficiency); multivisceral (grade IV PVT).

### Establishing the Diagnosis — Graft Dysfunction

**Stable recipient:** aspartate and alanine aminotransferase (AST/ALT), alkaline phosphatase, gamma-glutamyltransferase, bilirubin, creatinine, complete blood count, metabolic panel every **3–6 months**; immunosuppression (IS) troughs with every dose change and periodically (targets: [[tacrolimus]], [[calcineurin-inhibitors]]).

**Abnormal liver tests — sequence:**

1. Drug trough levels
2. Medication review — hepatotoxins; CYP3A4 interactions with calcineurin inhibitors (CNIs)
3. Doppler US → [[mri-mrcp|magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP)]] if biliary suspected
4. Serology: hepatitis B virus (HBV), HCV, EBV, CMV
5. [[liver-biopsy|Liver biopsy]] if still unexplained

### Severity Assessment

- **Allograft dysfunction:** pattern (hepatocellular vs cholestatic), histology (Banff rejection grade, fibrosis stage), synthetic function (international normalized ratio [INR], albumin, bilirubin).
- **Early allograft dysfunction (EAD)** predicts graft/patient survival and re-transplant. Olthoff: peak AST/ALT **>2000 U/L** in first 7 days, or bilirubin **≥10 mg/dL** or INR **≥1.6** on postoperative day (POD) 7. Preferred: **L-GrAFT** (POD 7 or 10 bilirubin, INR, platelets) or **Early Allograft Failure Simplified Estimation** (MELD, transfusion, early vessel thrombosis, center volume) — better predict 3-month graft failure.
- **Rejection patterns:** T-cell–mediated rejection (TCMR) in 10–30%, usually first 3 months — late TCMR (>6 months) carries lower graft survival and ductopenic risk; plasma cell–rich rejection in 3–5% of non-[[autoimmune-hepatitis|autoimmune hepatitis (AIH)]] recipients (AIH-like histology, lymphocytic cholangitis, IgG4-positive plasma cells); [[antibody-mediated-rejection-liver-transplant|antibody-mediated rejection (AMR)]] = C4d + donor-specific antibodies + histology + graft dysfunction; chronic ductopenic rejection = interlobular bile duct loss, **>50% of portal tracts = vanishing bile duct syndrome** (may need re-transplant).

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[abnormal-liver-chemistries]].*

| Timing | Causes of abnormal liver tests |
|---|---|
| Early (<3 months) | Primary non-function, hepatic artery thrombosis, acute cellular rejection, bile leak, drug toxicity, ischemia-reperfusion injury |
| Late (>1 year) | Recurrent disease (HCV, HBV, MASLD, [[primary-biliary-cholangitis\|primary biliary cholangitis (PBC)]], [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis\|primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)]], AIH), late acute or chronic rejection, [[biliary-stricture\|biliary stricture]] (anastomotic or non-anastomotic), de novo AIH, chronic hepatitis (CMV, EBV), vascular complications, drug toxicity |

- CNI toxicity is mainly renal, rarely hepatocellular.

## Diagnostics

- **Doppler US** — first screen for vascular patency and biliary dilation with any unexplained liver test rise; most centers obtain ≥1 in the first 5 days. CT or magnetic resonance angiography if inconclusive.
- **MRCP** — first-line for strictures.
- **Liver biopsy** — when noninvasive workup is unrevealing, and before IS minimization; **no protocol biopsies** in stable adults. Banff Rejection Activity Index (RAI) = portal inflammation + bile duct damage + venous endotheliitis; the guideline does not give component scoring — read the RAI off the pathology report.
- **[[liver-stiffness-measurement|Transient elastography]]** — not for routine graft monitoring (weak).
- **Other:** HCV RNA if ALT rises; yearly thyroid function on sirolimus/everolimus. HCC recurrence protocol: [[hepatocellular-carcinoma]]; de novo HCC in a re-cirrhotic graft → imaging every 6–12 months.

## Therapeutics

### Donor Type

- **LDLT:** survival comparable to deceased-donor LT, earlier LT at lower MELD; **more biliary (odds ratio [OR] 2.23) and vascular (OR 2.0) complications** → closer postoperative vigilance. Biliary stricture/leak risk: multiple duct anastomoses, Roux-en-Y hepaticojejunostomy, prior abdominal surgery, and (strictures) blood loss **>1 L**.
- **Donation after circulatory death (DCD):** with machine or normothermic regional perfusion, 1-year survival comparable to brain-death donors at experienced programs; static cold storage → more EAD and ischemic cholangiopathy; avoid with complex PVT.
- **Machine perfusion** (hypothermic oxygenated, normothermic) ↓ EAD, non-anastomotic strictures, and acute cellular rejection vs static cold storage.

### Vascular Complications

| Complication | Incidence | Management |
|---|---|---|
| Hepatic artery thrombosis (HAT) | 2–10%; most within 5 days | Early: surgical thrombectomy/revascularization ± anticoagulation, endovascular if surgery precluded. Late: endovascular ± anticoagulation, thrombolysis. Urgent re-transplant for progressive liver failure or biliary necrosis |
| Hepatic artery stenosis | 2–9% | Early: surgical revision + anticoagulation. Late: anticoagulation, angioplasty, stent |
| Portal vein thrombosis | 1–2.6% | Early: thrombectomy + surgical revision + anticoagulation. Late: anticoagulation, thrombolysis, stent, [[tips\|transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt]] |
| Portal vein stenosis | 2–3% | Angioplasty + stent + anticoagulation |
| Hepatic vein / inferior vena cava outflow | <5% (piggy-back) | Angioplasty + stent ± anticoagulation ± surgical revision |

- Doppler US screens; CT or catheter angiography confirms. Arterial lesions → biliary leak, stricture, ischemic cholangiopathy; portal lesions → [[portal-hypertension|portal hypertension]].
- HAT within 7 days → United Network for Organ Sharing status 1; relist promptly for severe graft injury or failure.

### Biliary Complications

Stent choice, dwell times, MRCP accuracy, and periprocedural antibiotics: [[biliary-stricture]] ([[asge-2023-post-transplant-biliary-strictures]], concordant with the 2025 guideline).

- **Anastomotic** (5–15% after deceased-donor LT, more after LDLT): MRCP first-line; straight to endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) if high pretest probability, cholangitis, or intervention planned. ERCP dilation + stent, **covered metal stent over multiple plastic stents** for extrahepatic strictures → percutaneous transhepatic biliary drainage (PTBD) if endoscopy fails → surgical revision. Roux-en-Y: endoscopic or PTBD per center.
- **Non-anastomotic / ischemic cholangiopathy** (DCD, prolonged cold ischemia, hepatic artery insufficiency, ABO incompatibility): ERCP + stent if amenable → PTBD → re-transplant if irreversible or progressive.
- DCD recipients with persistent cholestasis (bilirubin **>2 mg/dL**), recurrent cholangitis, or refractory non-anastomotic strictures get increased waitlist priority.
- Fluoroquinolones given around ERCP inhibit CYP3A4 → ↑ [[tacrolimus]] levels.

### Immunosuppression

Doses, troughs, adverse effects, and interactions: [[tacrolimus]], [[calcineurin-inhibitors]]; withdrawal (not routine): [[operational-tolerance]]. Key decisions:

- **Induction:** basiliximab 20 mg intravenously (IV) POD 0 and 4 to delay the CNI in renal dysfunction; **no anti-thymocyte globulin**.
- **Maintenance:** steroid-free IS not routine; low-dose steroids long term in AIH recipients. Tacrolimus monotherapy from 3 months if low rejection risk; add mycophenolate or mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) inhibitor when the CNI must be reduced.
- **mTOR inhibitor** for CNI nephrotoxicity → start early (~3 months), strongest evidence for everolimus; little gain once glomerular filtration rate **<60 mL/min**. HCC within Milan → may ↑ recurrence-free survival (weak).
- Nirmatrelvir/ritonavir markedly raises corticosteroid levels.

### Graft Rejection

| Rejection | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Mild TCMR (Banff RAI <4) | Augment IS; cyclosporine → tacrolimus |
| Moderate/severe TCMR | Methylprednisolone **500–1000 mg IV daily × 1–3 days** + augment IS (cyclosporine → tacrolimus; add antimetabolite or mTOR inhibitor); anti-infective prophylaxis during the pulse; 60–90% respond. Prednisone taper from 40–80 mg/day in 10–20 mg steps (center-variable) |
| Refractory TCMR | Second steroid course or lymphocyte-depleting therapy; repeat biopsy for suboptimal response |
| Plasma cell–rich rejection | Steroids + augment IS; add antimetabolite and/or maintenance prednisone **5–10 mg/day** |
| AMR | Acute and chronic ladders on [[antibody-mediated-rejection-liver-transplant]] |
| Ductopenic rejection | Augment IS **before duct loss exceeds 50% of portal tracts** — beyond that it is vanishing bile duct syndrome and far less reversible |

- Peripheral blood biomarkers alone must **not** diagnose or exclude TCMR — biopsy. CD4 intracellular ATP assays may gauge immune status and infection risk (weak).

### Recurrent Disease

- **HBV and hepatitis D:** indefinite nucleos(t)ide analogue in every HBV recipient from before LT, risk-stratified hepatitis B immune globulin (HBIG), and the anti-HBc-positive-graft rule → [[chronic-hepatitis-b]] (2025 recommendations supersede the 2012 fixed HBIG protocol).
- **[[hepatitis-c|HCV]]:** treat recurrence with direct-acting antivirals — regimens on [[hepatitis-c#Posttransplant HCV]]; the 2012 interferon recommendations are obsolete.
- **PBC:** [[ursodeoxycholic-acid|ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA)]] **10–15 mg/kg/day** for histologically proven recurrence; the 2012 guideline advised against prophylactic UDCA, while newer post-LT data favor continuing it — recurrence rates and that evidence: [[primary-biliary-cholangitis]]. Monitor alkaline phosphatase, antimitochondrial antibody, thyroid function, bone density.
- **PSC:** diagnose recurrence from non-anastomotic strictures on cholangiography + biopsy after excluding ischemic/anastomotic causes; UDCA not recommended; dominant stricture → dilation ± stent. Rates, risk factors, re-transplant: [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis]].
- **AIH:** abrupt steroid withdrawal and inadequate IS drive recurrence → ↑ steroids + [[thiopurines|azathioprine]], long-term low-dose steroids; de novo AIH treated as AIH. Rates and regimens: [[autoimmune-hepatitis]].
- **ALD:** abstinence, validated relapse screening, addiction treatment, smoking cessation — plan on [[alcohol-associated-liver-disease]].
- **MASLD:** **biopsy** to confirm and stage recurrent or de novo disease; no specific therapy beyond weight, blood pressure, and diabetes control ([[#Cardiometabolic Management]], [[nafld-masld]]).

### Infection Prophylaxis and Treatment

- **Timing:** <4 weeks — donor-derived, surgical-site; 1–12 months — CMV, EBV, VZV, herpes simplex virus (HSV); >6 months — community respiratory viruses, late CMV in donor-positive/recipient-negative (D+/R−).
- **CMV risk:** D+/R− highest; D+/R+ or D−/R+ intermediate; D−/R− low (<2%).
- **CMV prevention:** pre-emptive monitoring first when donor or recipient is seropositive — weekly CMV polymerase chain reaction **for 100 days**, if the center can do it reliably. Otherwise universal **valganciclovir 900 mg daily** (renally adjusted): **D±/R+ 3 months; D+/R− 6 months**. Persistent neutropenia on valganciclovir → letermovir, only if HSV/VZV-seronegative. HSV/VZV prophylaxis (acyclovir, valacyclovir, or famciclovir) **≥3 months** for D−/R− or anyone on pre-emptive monitoring.

> [!pitfall] Letermovir does not cover HSV or VZV
> HSV- or VZV-seropositive recipients switched to letermovir also need acyclovir, valacyclovir, or famciclovir ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]]).

| CMV treatment | Therapy |
|---|---|
| Viral load **<100,000 IU/mL**, intact gut absorption, non-severe | **Oral valganciclovir 900 mg twice daily** (renally adjusted) |
| Viral load **≥100,000 IU/mL**, poor absorption, or severe disease (especially pneumonia) | **IV ganciclovir** |
| Refractory/resistant, low viral load **≤20,000 IU** | **Maribavir 400 mg twice daily** |
| Refractory/resistant, high viral load (**>20,000–50,000 IU/mL**), severe disease, or poor absorption | **IV foscarnet** |

- Viral load weekly; treat **until undetectable**, then monitor weekly for **4 weeks** off therapy; **no secondary prophylaxis**.
- **Latent tuberculosis:** isoniazid **300 mg** + pyridoxine **50 mg** daily, started within 6 months post-LT once graft function is stable; if started pre-LT, rifampin **300 mg daily × 4 months** is an option.
- ***Pneumocystis jirovecii*:** trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole or atovaquone for **6 months**.
- **Fungal:** fluconazole **2–4 weeks** with **≥2** of: renal replacement therapy (pre-, peri-, or post-LT); re-operation (incl. anastomotic leak, repeat laparotomy); re-transplant; **≥40 units** of cellular products; choledochojejunostomy.
- **Zoonotic:** avoid rodents, reptiles, and birds as pets.

### Wellness and Health Behaviors

- **Diet:** added sugar and saturated fat each **<10%** of calories; sodium **<2300 mg/day**.
- **Avoid:** grapefruit and Seville oranges (CYP3A4 — ↑ CNI levels); untreated water; raw/undercooked meat, unpasteurized cheese, unwashed produce; herbal/dietary supplements without team review ([[drug-induced-liver-injury|drug-induced liver injury]]) — minerals excepted.
- **Exercise 150–300 min/week** moderate or **75–150** vigorous + strength **≥2 days/week**. **Alcohol:** none, any etiology. **Sleep ≥7 h.**
- **Screen depression and alcohol use yearly in all.** Non-adherence (missed visits/labs, subtherapeutic troughs) is a major cause of late rejection and graft loss → screen for substance use, anxiety, depression, food/transport insecurity, social support.

### Bone Health

- **DEXA once on stable IS, typically after 6 months** — stable IS is the trigger, not the date. Osteopenia/osteoporosis → repeat every **1–2 years** until stable.
- Calcium **>1200 mg/day**. Osteoporosis: 25-OH vitamin D **≥30 ng/mL** (30–50 preferred); if <30, vitamin D3 **1000–2000 IU/day**.
- **Start drug therapy if any one of:** osteopenia + hip/spine fragility fracture; T-score **≤−2.5**; Fracture Risk Assessment Tool 10-year major osteoporotic fracture **≥20%** or hip fracture **≥3%**. Agents: alendronate, denosumab, risedronate, zoledronate.
- Osteoporosis → physical therapy for fall prevention.

### Vaccination

- Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices schedules; update before IS starts.
- **No live vaccines after LT:** MMR, varicella, yellow fever, oral typhoid, oral [[rotavirus]], intranasal influenza. Household contacts may receive most live vaccines **except** vaccinia/smallpox and oral polio.
- International travel → travel-medicine consult **≥6–8 weeks** ahead.

### Reproductive Health

- Discuss contraception with all of childbearing age; delay pregnancy **≥1 year** post-LT; maternal-fetal medicine co-management.
- **Mycophenolate is teratogenic** — stop **≥6 weeks** before conception (women), **≥90 days** (men); two contraceptive methods while on it. Tacrolimus/cyclosporine (frequent levels, expect dose changes), azathioprine, and corticosteroids are acceptable in pregnancy.

### Oncologic Surveillance

Cancer risk **2.45×** the general population; relative risk highest for non-Hodgkin lymphoma, absolute risk for non-melanoma skin cancer ([[post-transplant-lymphoproliferative-disorder]], where routine serial EBV polymerase chain reaction is **not** recommended).

- **All:** age-appropriate screening per US Preventive Services Task Force; **yearly full-body skin exam**, more often after prior skin cancer; tobacco cessation education + medical therapy.
- **May consider:** yearly pelvic exam + cervical and anal Pap in women ≥21 or sexually active with positive human papillomavirus (HPV); ear, nose, and throat referral within year 1 after tobacco, ALD, or HPV.

| Colon | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Average risk, **≥45 y** | General-population screening, but may consider **colonoscopy every 5 years** (colonoscopy preferred over stool tests) |
| **PSC + [[inflammatory-bowel-disease\|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]]** | **Yearly colonoscopy with random biopsies** |
| **PSC without IBD** | Colonoscopy **every 5 years** with ileal intubation + colon biopsies |
| IBD without PSC | **Yearly** colonoscopy with random biopsies if IBD extent and duration meet surveillance criteria |

### Cardiometabolic Management

Screen yearly for hypertension, obesity, diabetes, and hyperlipidemia in **all** recipients. 5-/10-year survival is 82%/69%, with cardiovascular disease and de novo malignancy the leading non-graft causes of death.

| Problem | IS change |
|---|---|
| Metabolic comorbidities (diabetes, hypertension) | **Early corticosteroid withdrawal**, weighed against rejection risk |
| **Poor glycemic control** despite aggressive therapy | **Tacrolimus → cyclosporine** (weak) |
| **Uncontrolled hypertension** despite aggressive therapy | **Cyclosporine → tacrolimus** (weak) |
| **Hyperlipidemia** despite aggressive therapy | **CNI minimization ± antimetabolite** (mycophenolate/azathioprine) (weak) |

> [!pearl] The two CNI switches run in opposite directions
> Glucose uncontrolled → tacrolimus to cyclosporine; blood pressure uncontrolled → cyclosporine to tacrolimus. Pick by the comorbidity that is failing ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]]).

| Problem | First-line | Avoid / notes |
|---|---|---|
| Post-transplant diabetes | **Metformin** if no other cardiovascular risk factors; **[[semaglutide\|glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist (GLP-1 RA)]] and/or sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitor** if high atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk, heart failure, stroke, or CKD | High risk = proteinuria/albuminuria, hypertension, left ventricular hypertrophy, systolic/diastolic dysfunction, ankle-brachial index **<0.9**; no sulfonylureas first-line |
| Hypertension (≥140/≥90 mmHg; target **≤130/80**) | Cardiovascular disease and/or CKD → angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor or angiotensin receptor blocker; neither → **amlodipine or felodipine** | Avoid diltiazem/verapamil (↑ CNI levels) |
| Dyslipidemia | **Pravastatin or rosuvastatin** + lifestyle; ezetimibe and/or PCSK9 inhibitor if refractory. Triglycerides **>135 mg/dL** on statin → omega-3, then fibrate | Avoid simvastatin, atorvastatin, lovastatin (CYP3A4; myopathy with CNIs) |
| [[obesity\|Obesity]] | GLP-1 RA when diet + exercise fail; **BMI >30** → obesity specialist from **3–6 months**; [[bariatric-surgery\|weight-loss surgery]] if medical therapy fails or **>15–20%** loss needed, once stable | — |

- **Cardiovascular:** established disease → cardiac assessment every visit + yearly cardiology; dysfunction or more-than-mild valve disease → individualized echo surveillance; aspirin and statins by general-population risk rules; recurrent events → CNI minimization + mTOR inhibitor.

### Chronic Kidney Disease

Highest stage 4–5 CKD risk of any solid-organ group; early [[aki-in-cirrhosis|acute kidney injury]] predicts later CKD; CNIs drive it directly and via metabolic disease. Tacrolimus vs cyclosporine: **66%** lower risk of stage 4–5 CKD.

- **Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR)** from creatinine (+ cystatin C if available): stages 1–3 every **6–12 months**; stages 4–5 every **1–3 months**, including simultaneous liver–kidney recipients.
- Control hypertension, diabetes, obesity, dyslipidemia; **nephrology at stage 4 (eGFR <30 mL/min)**, at stage 3 if urine suggests nephritis.

| Timing / setting | Renal-protective IS strategy |
|---|---|
| Peri- or early post-LT acute kidney injury | **Delay CNI** with induction (weak) |
| CKD in year 1 | **Reduce CNI** + non-nephrotoxic adjunct (mycophenolate or everolimus); minimize but **do not eliminate** CNI |
| CKD beyond year 1 | Minimize but **do not stop** CNI |
| Do not use | **Belatacept** — ↑ mortality; **mycophenolate monotherapy** — acute cellular rejection |

- No high-quality evidence for any renal-protective strategy intraoperatively or **≥12 months** post-LT.

> [!pitfall] Normal proteinuria does not exclude CKD on a CNI
> CNIs are antiproteinuric — a normal spot protein-to-creatinine ratio (threshold >0.3) can coexist with advanced CKD; follow eGFR ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]]).
