# PART I — PRE-TRANSPLANT CANDIDATE EVALUATION

*Liver transplantation (LT) candidacy, per [[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-candidate-evaluation]] unless noted.*

## Referral for Liver Transplant

| Indication | Refer when | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Chronic liver disease **with a decompensating event** | Any of: [[ascites]] (± [[spontaneous-bacterial-peritonitis\|spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP)]]), [[hepatic-encephalopathy\|hepatic encephalopathy]], [[variceal-upper-gi-bleeding\|variceal hemorrhage]], [[hepatopulmonary-syndrome-portopulmonary-hypertension\|hepatopulmonary syndrome/portopulmonary hypertension]], [[hepatocellular-carcinoma\|hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)]] | Strong, Level 4 |
| Chronic liver disease, **no decompensating event** | Model for End-Stage Liver Disease (MELD) threshold *unclear* (Rec 1): **MELD 3.0 ≥12** = survival-benefit signal (Figure 1 trigger; living donor LT [LDLT] from MELD 11), but those studies did not test referral timing; working group endorses **MELD 15** absent portal hypertensive complications. Formula: [[cirrhosis]] | Strong, Level 4 |
| [[acute-on-chronic-liver-failure\|Acute-on-chronic liver failure (ACLF)]] | Promptly, any MELD; MELD >40 or ACLF grade alone not a contraindication | Strong, Level 3–4 |
| [[acute-liver-failure\|Acute liver failure (ALF)]] | **All**, urgently — within hours, before formal ALF criteria | Strong, Level 2 |
| HCC | All without extrahepatic metastases — Milan, alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), downstaging on [[hepatocellular-carcinoma]] | Strong, Level 1 |
| [[cholangiocarcinoma\|Cholangiocarcinoma (CCA)]] | Unresectable perihilar CCA <3 cm radial diameter, neoadjuvant protocol (Strong, Level 3); mixed HCC-CCA or intrahepatic CCA <3 cm, no extrahepatic disease — may consider (Weak, Level 4) | — |

- Under-referred: [[alcohol-associated-liver-disease|alcohol-associated liver disease (ALD)]], African American race, lower socioeconomic status.

> [!pitfall] MELD alone never blocks referral
> Refer at **any MELD** when portal hypertensive complications are uncontrolled — many low-MELD patients are "underrepresented by MELD" ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-candidate-evaluation]]).

## Pre-Transplant Multidisciplinary Evaluation

Asks: prognosis without LT, capacity for a successful LT, and the patient's wishes (Strong, Level 4).

| Domain | Requirement | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Cardiac | Electrocardiogram + transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) for all. Coronary testing if any [[nafld-masld\|metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD)]] risk factor, chronic kidney disease (CKD), left ventricular hypertrophy, family history of premature coronary disease, tobacco, or calcium score >0 → **coronary computed tomography (CT) angiography** preferred | Strong, Level 2 |
| Pulmonary | Chest imaging for all; pulmonary function tests only with lung-disease risk factors. Hepatopulmonary syndrome: bubble echo when available; portopulmonary hypertension (POPH): right ventricular systolic pressure **>45 mmHg** on TTE → right heart catheterization (Rec 22). Thresholds and MELD exceptions: [[hepatopulmonary-syndrome-portopulmonary-hypertension]] | Strong, Level 1–4 |
| Infection (Table 1) | **All:** cytomegalovirus (CMV) and Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) IgG; hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg), core antibody (anti-HBc), surface antibody (anti-HBs); anti-hepatitis C virus (HCV); human immunodeficiency virus (HIV); hepatitis E (conditional); tuberculosis; syphilis; *Toxoplasma*; varicella-zoster virus (VZV); rectal + nasal swabs for multidrug-resistant organisms. **Exposure-based:** *Coccidioides*, *Histoplasma*, *Strongyloides*, *Trypanosoma cruzi* | Strong, Level 3 |
| Vaccines | All age-appropriate before LT; live vaccines (measles-mumps-rubella [MMR], varicella) **≥4 weeks** pre-LT | Strong, Level 3 |
| Cancer (Table 3) | Per existing guidelines — HCC: ultrasound (US) + AFP q6 mo; colorectal **≥45 y**: [[colonoscopy]] (within 10 y) or stool DNA–fecal immunochemical test q1–3 y, colonoscopy only if above-average risk; breast, cervical, lung, prostate per general-population schedules | Strong, Level 1 |
| [[nutrition-in-liver-disease\|Nutrition]], function | Dietitian for all (Strong, Level 2); Liver Frailty Index or Karnofsky score — frailty alone **not** a contraindication (Strong, Level 2) | — |
| Bone | Vitamin D + dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA) for all stable patients; low density → calcium, vitamin D, weight-bearing exercise; bisphosphonate case-by-case | Strong, Level 2–3 |
| Dental, body mass index (BMI) | Dental evaluation, but never delay lifesaving LT for it; BMI >40 or <18.5 → screen for treatable factors, not exclusionary alone | Strong, Level 3 |
| Psychosocial | Adherence, mental-health stability, caregiver support. ALD: **no fixed abstinence interval** for listing. Methadone/buprenorphine: not grounds to deny, need not stop. Tobacco, smoked marijuana, illicit drugs: cessation expected. Serious mental illness: treat and reassess | Strong, Level 2–3 |

## Contraindications to Liver Transplant

- **Absolute:** anatomy precluding LT; advanced cardiopulmonary disease (severe left ventricular dysfunction, severe valvular disease, end-stage lung disease) without eligibility for combined-organ transplant.
- **POPH:** mean pulmonary artery pressure 45 mmHg with pulmonary vascular resistance >3 Wood units on optimized therapy (Rec 24, Strong, Level 1).
- **Relative / case-by-case:** MELD >40 or ACLF (trajectory, futility); frailty; mental illness; BMI extremes; **Yerdel grade IV [[portal-vein-thrombosis|portal vein thrombosis (PVT)]]** (complete splanchnic) **without adequate collaterals** — relative contraindication to isolated LT (Rec 47, Weak, Level 2).

## Special Situations (Pre-Transplant)

- **LDLT:** offer education to all; older age alone no bar (Strong, Level 2). Graft-to-recipient weight ratio **≥0.8%** (Strong, Level 1); caution when **MELD >30** (Strong, Level 2).
- **Nonhepatic malignancy** (strict selection): unresectable [[gastroenteropancreatic-neuroendocrine-tumors|neuroendocrine tumors]] (Strong, Level 1); hereditary hepatic epithelioid hemangioendothelioma (Strong, Level 4); unresectable colorectal liver metastases (Strong, Level 2).
- **Multiorgan** (organ-team consensus, Strong, Level 4–5): heart-liver (Fontan-associated liver disease, transthyretin amyloidosis); liver-kidney (CKD or acute kidney injury [AKI] not expected to resolve); liver-lung (cystic fibrosis, alpha-1-antitrypsin deficiency); multivisceral (grade IV PVT).

# PART II — POST-TRANSPLANT GRAFT MANAGEMENT

*The allograft, per [[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-graft-complications]] (2025) and [[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]] (2012); 2025 governs where they differ.*

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

**Routine monitoring, stable recipient** (2012, Rec 1):

- Aspartate and alanine aminotransferase (AST/ALT), alkaline phosphatase, gamma-glutamyltransferase, bilirubin, creatinine, complete blood count, metabolic panel — every **3–6 months**
- Immunosuppression (IS) troughs with every dose change and periodically. Targets (2025 Table 4): [[tacrolimus]]; cyclosporine, everolimus, sirolimus, mycophenolate, azathioprine on [[calcineurin-inhibitors]]

**Abnormal liver tests — sequence:**

1. Drug trough levels
2. Medication review — hepatotoxins; CYP3A4 interactions with calcineurin inhibitors (CNIs)
3. Doppler US for vascular/biliary complications → [[mri-mrcp|magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP)]] if biliary suspected
4. Serology: hepatitis B virus (HBV), HCV, EBV, CMV
5. [[liver-biopsy|Liver biopsy]] if still unexplained

### Severity Assessment

- **Allograft dysfunction:** pattern (hepatocellular vs cholestatic), histology (Banff rejection, fibrosis stage), synthetic function (international normalized ratio [INR], albumin, bilirubin).
- **Early allograft dysfunction (EAD)** — predicts graft/patient survival, complications, re-LT:
  - Olthoff: peak AST/ALT **>2000 U/L** in first 7 days, or bilirubin **≥10 mg/dL** or INR **≥1.6** on postoperative day (POD) 7
  - Preferred: **L-GrAFT** (POD 7 or 10 bilirubin, INR, platelets) or **Early Allograft Failure Simplified Estimation (EASE)** (MELD, transfusion, early vessel thrombosis, center volume) — better predict 3-month graft failure (Rec 11, Strong, Level 2)

| Rejection type | Features |
|---|---|
| T-cell–mediated rejection (TCMR) | 10–30% of recipients, usually first 3 months; late TCMR (>6 months) → lower graft survival, risk of ductopenic rejection |
| Plasma cell–rich rejection (formerly de novo [[autoimmune-hepatitis\|autoimmune hepatitis (AIH)]]) | 3–5% of non-AIH recipients; AIH-like histology, lymphocytic cholangitis, IgG4-positive plasma cells |
| [[antibody-mediated-rejection-liver-transplant\|Antibody-mediated rejection (AMR)]] | C4d deposition + donor-specific antibodies + histology + graft dysfunction |
| Chronic ductopenic rejection | Interlobular bile duct loss; **>50% of portal tracts = vanishing bile duct syndrome**; may need re-LT |

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[abnormal-liver-chemistries]].*

| Timing | Causes of abnormal liver tests (2012 Table 3) |
|---|---|
| Early (<3 months) | Primary non-function, hepatic artery thrombosis, acute cellular rejection, bile leak, drug toxicity, ischemia-reperfusion injury |
| Late (>1 year) | Recurrent disease (HCV, HBV, MASLD, [[primary-biliary-cholangitis\|primary biliary cholangitis (PBC)]], [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis\|primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)]], AIH), late acute or chronic rejection, [[biliary-stricture\|biliary stricture]] (anastomotic or non-anastomotic), de novo AIH, chronic hepatitis (CMV, EBV), vascular complications, drug toxicity |

- CNI toxicity is mainly renal, rarely hepatocellular.

## Diagnostics

- **Doppler US** — first screen for vascular patency and biliary dilation, with any unexplained liver test rise; most centers obtain ≥1 in the first 5 days. CT or magnetic resonance angiography if inconclusive.
- **MRCP** — first-line for strictures ([[#Biliary Complications (Recs 15–24)]]).
- **Liver biopsy** — when noninvasive workup is unrevealing, and before IS minimization; **no protocol biopsies** in stable adults (Rec 34, Strong, Level 4). Banff Rejection Activity Index (RAI) = portal inflammation + bile duct damage + venous endotheliitis; component scoring is not in the guideline — read the RAI off the pathology report.
- **[[liver-stiffness-measurement|Transient elastography]]** — not for routine graft monitoring (Rec 35, Weak, Level 3).
- **Other:** HCV RNA if ALT rises; yearly thyroid function on sirolimus/everolimus. HCC recurrence protocol: [[hepatocellular-carcinoma]]; de novo HCC in a re-cirrhotic graft → imaging every 6–12 months (2012, Rec 43).

## Therapeutics

### Surgical Considerations and Donor Type

- **LDLT** (Recs 1–3): survival comparable to deceased-donor LT, earlier LT at lower MELD (Strong, Level 2); **more biliary (odds ratio [OR] 2.23) and vascular (OR 2.0) complications** → closer postoperative vigilance (Strong, Level 3). Biliary stricture and leak risk: multiple duct anastomoses, Roux-en-Y hepaticojejunostomy, prior abdominal surgery (strictures also blood loss >1 L).
- **Donation after circulatory death (DCD)** (Recs 4–5): with machine or normothermic regional perfusion, 1-year survival comparable to brain-death donors at experienced programs (Strong, Level 3); static cold storage → more EAD and ischemic cholangiopathy (Strong, Level 2); avoid with complex PVT.
- **Machine perfusion** (Recs 6–10): hypothermic oxygenated (EAD OR 0.51) and normothermic (OR 0.40) perfusion ↓ EAD, non-anastomotic strictures, and acute cellular rejection vs static cold storage; normothermic regional perfusion ↓ EAD in DCD (Strong, Level 2).

### Vascular Complications (Rec 13–14)

| Complication | Incidence | Early | Late |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hepatic artery thrombosis (HAT) | 2–10%; most within 5 days | Surgical thrombectomy/revascularization ± anticoagulation; endovascular if surgery precluded; urgent re-LT if progressive liver failure (Strong, Level 4) | Endovascular ± anticoagulation, thrombolysis; re-LT if biliary necrosis |
| Hepatic artery stenosis | 2–9% | Surgical revision + anticoagulation | Anticoagulation, angioplasty, stent |
| Portal vein thrombosis | 1–2.6% | Thrombectomy + surgical revision + anticoagulation | Anticoagulation, thrombolysis, stent, [[tips\|transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS)]] |
| Portal vein stenosis | 2–3% | Angioplasty + stent + anticoagulation | — |
| Hepatic vein / inferior vena cava outflow | <5% (piggy-back) | Angioplasty + stent ± anticoagulation ± surgical revision | — |

- Doppler US screens; CT or catheter angiography confirms. Arterial lesions → biliary leak, stricture, ischemic cholangiopathy; portal lesions → [[portal-hypertension|portal hypertension]].
- HAT within 7 days → United Network for Organ Sharing status 1. Retransplant promptly for severe graft injury or failure (Rec 13, Strong, Level 4).

### Biliary Complications (Recs 15–24)

Stent choice, dwell times, MRCP accuracy, and periprocedural antibiotics: [[biliary-stricture]] ([[asge-2023-post-transplant-biliary-strictures]], concordant with the 2025 guideline).

- **Anastomotic** (5–15% after deceased-donor LT, more after LDLT): **MRCP first-line** (Rec 15); straight to endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) if high pretest probability, cholangitis, or intervention planned (Rec 16). ERCP dilation + stent (Rec 17), **covered metal stent over multiple plastic stents** for extrahepatic strictures (Rec 18) → percutaneous transhepatic biliary drainage (PTBD) if endoscopy fails (Rec 19) → surgical revision (Rec 21). Roux-en-Y: endoscopic or PTBD per center (Rec 20).
- **Non-anastomotic / ischemic cholangiopathy** (DCD, prolonged cold ischemia, hepatic artery insufficiency, ABO incompatibility): ERCP + stent if amenable (Rec 22) → PTBD (Rec 23) → re-LT if irreversible or progressive (Rec 24).
- DCD recipients with persistent cholestasis (bilirubin **>2 mg/dL**), recurrent cholangitis, or refractory non-anastomotic strictures get increased waitlist priority.
- Fluoroquinolones given around ERCP inhibit CYP3A4 → ↑ [[tacrolimus]] levels ([[asge-2023-post-transplant-biliary-strictures]]).

### Immunosuppression (IS)

Doses, troughs, induction evidence, adverse effects, and interactions: [[tacrolimus]], [[calcineurin-inhibitors]]; withdrawal (not routine, Rec 36): [[operational-tolerance]]. Key decisions:

- **Induction:** basiliximab 20 mg intravenously (IV) POD 0 and 4 to delay the CNI in renal dysfunction (Rec 25, Weak, Level 2); **no anti-thymocyte globulin** (Rec 26, Strong, Level 2).
- **Maintenance:** steroid-free IS not routine (Rec 28); low-dose steroids long term in AIH recipients. Tacrolimus monotherapy from 3 months if low rejection risk (Rec 29); add mycophenolate or mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) inhibitor when the CNI must be reduced (Rec 30).
- **mTOR inhibitor:** CNI nephrotoxicity → early (~3 months), strongest evidence for everolimus; little gain once glomerular filtration rate <60 mL/min (Rec 33, Strong, Level 2). HCC within Milan → may ↑ recurrence-free survival (Rec 32, Weak, Level 3).
- Nirmatrelvir/ritonavir markedly raises corticosteroid levels.

### Graft Rejection Management

| Rejection | Treatment | Rec, grade |
|---|---|---|
| Mild TCMR (Banff RAI <4) | Augment IS; cyclosporine → tacrolimus | Rec 37, Strong, Level 3 |
| Moderate/severe TCMR | Methylprednisolone **500–1000 mg IV daily × 1–3 days** + augment IS (cyclosporine → tacrolimus; add antimetabolite or mTOR inhibitor); anti-infective prophylaxis during pulse; 60–90% respond. Prednisone taper from 40–80 mg/day in 10–20 mg steps (center-variable) | Rec 38, Strong, Level 3 |
| Refractory TCMR | Second steroid course or lymphocyte-depleting therapy; repeat biopsy for suboptimal response | Rec 39, Strong, Level 4 |
| Plasma cell–rich rejection | Steroids + augment IS; add antimetabolite and/or maintenance prednisone **5–10 mg/day** | Rec 40, Strong, Level 4 |
| AMR | Acute and chronic ladders on [[antibody-mediated-rejection-liver-transplant]] | Recs 47–53 |

- Peripheral blood biomarkers alone must **not** diagnose or exclude TCMR — biopsy (Rec 41, Strong, Level 4). CD4 intracellular ATP assays may gauge immune status and infection risk (Rec 42, Weak, Level 3).

> [!pitfall] Treat ductopenic rejection before 50% duct loss
> Augment IS early, while ductopenia is **<50% of portal tracts**; beyond that it is vanishing bile duct syndrome and far less reversible (Rec 43, [[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-graft-complications]]).

### Recurrent Disease Management

**[[chronic-hepatitis-b|Hepatitis B]]** (2025 Recs 57–60 supersede the 2012 fixed hepatitis B immune globulin [HBIG] protocol; recurrence 80–100% without prophylaxis, <10% now):

- **All HBV recipients:** first-line nucleos(t)ide analogue (NA) — [[hbv-nucleoside-analogs|entecavir, tenofovir disoproxil, or tenofovir alafenamide]] — from **before** LT, **indefinitely** (Rec 58, Strong, Level 3).
- **HBIG duration is risk-stratified** (Rec 59, Strong, Level 2); consider withdrawal once low-risk:

| | Low risk → short-course or no HBIG | High risk → long-term HBIG |
|---|---|---|
| Patient | Good adherence | Limited adherence; HCC at high recurrence risk; HIV co-infection |
| Virus | Low/undetectable HBV DNA | High HBV DNA at LT; NA-resistant mutations |

- **HBV + [[hepatitis-d|hepatitis D virus (HDV)]]:** HBIG **plus** NA in **all** (Rec 60, Strong, Level 4).
- **HBsAg-negative recipient of an anti-HBc-positive graft** (transmission up to 75%; highest if recipient lacks both anti-HBs and anti-HBc): indefinite entecavir or tenofovir from immediately post-LT; **no HBIG** (Rec 57, Strong, Level 3). De novo HBV → lifelong first-line NA.
- Monitor (2012): HBV DNA yearly; anti-HBs every 3–6 months.

**Other recurrent disease** (2012 recs unless noted):

| Disease | Recurrence | Management |
|---|---|---|
| [[hepatitis-c\|HCV]] | Universal before direct-acting antivirals (DAAs) | Treat with DAAs — regimens on [[hepatitis-c#Posttransplant HCV]]; the 2012 interferon recommendations are obsolete |
| PBC | 15–20% at 10 years; indolent | Alkaline phosphatase + antimitochondrial antibody + biopsy; monitor thyroid and bone density (Rec 83). [[ursodeoxycholic-acid\|Ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA)]] **10–15 mg/kg/day** only for histologically proven recurrence; **no prophylactic UDCA** (Rec 84). Newer data on post-LT UDCA: [[primary-biliary-cholangitis]] |
| PSC | 10–25% at 5–10 years | Non-anastomotic strictures on MRCP + biopsy, after excluding ischemic/anastomotic causes; risk ↑ with active [[inflammatory-bowel-disease\|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]], intact colon. UDCA not recommended; dominant stricture → dilation ± stent; re-LT in 5–10%. Colonoscopy: [[#Oncologic Surveillance]] |
| AIH | 10–40% | Risk: abrupt steroid withdrawal, inadequate IS. ↑ steroids + azathioprine; **long-term low-dose steroids** (Rec 86). De novo AIH: treat as AIH |
| [[alcohol-associated-liver-disease\|ALD]] | ~15–20% any drinking; 10–15% harmful | Abstinence (Rec 87), validated screening; relapse → addiction treatment (Rec 88); smoking cessation (Recs 89–90). Full plan on the ALD page |
| [[nafld-masld\|MASLD]] | 30–40% de novo in some series | **Biopsy** to confirm and stage (Rec 91); no specific therapy beyond weight, blood pressure, and diabetes control (Rec 92) — [[#Metabolic Syndrome and Cardiometabolic Management]] |

# PART III — NON-GRAFT COMPLICATIONS

*Per [[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]] unless noted. 5-/10-year survival 82%/69%; cardiovascular disease and de novo malignancy lead non-graft deaths; IS drives most of these complications.*

## Wellness, Nutrition, and Health Behaviors

- **Diet** (Rec 1, Weak, Level 4): added sugar and saturated fat each **<10%** of calories; sodium **<2300 mg/day**.
- **Avoid** (Recs 2–5, Strong, Level 4): grapefruit, Seville oranges (CYP3A4 — ↑ CNI levels); untreated water; raw/undercooked meat, unpasteurized cheese, unwashed produce; herbal/dietary supplements without team review ([[drug-induced-liver-injury|drug-induced liver injury]]) — minerals excepted.
- **Exercise** (Rec 6): **150–300 min/week** moderate or **75–150** vigorous + strength **≥2 days/week**. **Alcohol:** none, any etiology (Rec 9). **Sleep ≥7 h** (Rec 10).
- **Screening:** depression and alcohol use yearly in all (Rec 11, Strong, Level 3). Non-adherence (missed visits/labs, subtherapeutic troughs) — a major cause of late rejection and graft loss — → screen for substance use, anxiety, depression, food/transport insecurity, social support (Recs 7–8).

## Bone Health

- **DEXA once on stable IS, typically after 6 months** — stable IS is the trigger, not the date (Rec 12, Strong, Level 2). Osteopenia/osteoporosis → repeat every **1–2 years** until stable (Rec 13, Strong, Level 3).
- Calcium **>1200 mg/day** (Rec 14). Osteoporosis: 25-OH vitamin D **≥30 ng/mL** (30–50 preferred); if <30, vitamin D3 **1000–2000 IU/day** (Recs 15–16). All Strong, Level 3.
- **Start drug therapy if any one** (Rec 17, Strong, Level 1): osteopenia + hip/spine fragility fracture; T-score **≤−2.5**; Fracture Risk Assessment Tool 10-year major osteoporotic fracture **≥20%** or hip fracture **≥3%**. Agents: alendronate, denosumab, risedronate, zoledronate.
- Osteoporosis → physical therapy for fall prevention (Rec 18, Strong, Level 1).

## Vaccination

- Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices schedules; update before IS starts (Strong, Level 4).
- **No live vaccines after LT:** MMR, varicella, yellow fever, oral typhoid, oral [[rotavirus]], intranasal influenza (Strong, Level 4). Household contacts may receive most live vaccines **except** vaccinia/smallpox and oral polio.
- International travel → travel-medicine consult **≥6–8 weeks** ahead (Weak, Level 4).

## Reproductive Health

- Discuss contraception with all of childbearing age; delay pregnancy **≥1 year** post-LT; maternal-fetal medicine co-management (Strong, Level 4).
- **Mycophenolate is teratogenic** — stop **≥6 weeks** before conception (women), **≥90 days** (men); two contraceptive methods while on it. Tacrolimus/cyclosporine (frequent levels, expect dose changes), azathioprine, and corticosteroids are acceptable in pregnancy (Strong, Level 4).

## Infectious Complications

- **Timing:** <4 weeks — donor-derived, surgical-site; 1–12 months — CMV, EBV, VZV, herpes simplex virus (HSV); >6 months — community respiratory viruses, late CMV in donor-positive/recipient-negative (D+/R−).
- **CMV risk:** D+/R− highest; D+/R+ or D−/R+ intermediate; D−/R− low (<2%).

### CMV Prevention — Which Strategy, and For How Long

- **Pre-emptive monitoring first** when donor or recipient is seropositive: weekly CMV polymerase chain reaction **for 100 days**, if the center can do it reliably (Rec 36, Strong, Level 1).
- Otherwise **universal valganciclovir 900 mg daily** (renally adjusted): **D±/R+ 3 months; D+/R− 6 months** (Rec 37, Strong, Level 1).
- Persistent neutropenia on valganciclovir → letermovir, only in the absence of HSV/VZV seropositivity (Rec 38, Strong, Level 2).
- HSV/VZV prophylaxis (acyclovir, valacyclovir, or famciclovir) **≥3 months** for D−/R− or anyone on pre-emptive monitoring (Rec 40, Strong, Level 2).

> [!pitfall] Letermovir does not cover HSV or VZV
> HSV- or VZV-seropositive recipients switched to letermovir also need acyclovir, valacyclovir, or famciclovir (Rec 39, [[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]]).

### CMV Treatment — Route and Agent by Viral Load

| Situation | Therapy (Recs 41–42, Strong, Level 1) |
|---|---|
| Viral load **<100,000 IU/mL**, intact gut absorption, non-severe | **Oral valganciclovir 900 mg twice daily** (renally adjusted) |
| Viral load **≥100,000 IU/mL**, poor absorption, or severe disease (especially pneumonia) | **IV ganciclovir** |
| Refractory/resistant, low viral load **≤20,000 IU** | **Maribavir 400 mg twice daily** |
| Refractory/resistant, high viral load (**>20,000–50,000 IU/mL**), severe disease, or poor absorption | **IV foscarnet** |

- Viral load weekly; treat **until undetectable**, then monitor weekly for **4 weeks** off therapy; **no secondary prophylaxis** (Rec 43, Strong, Level 1).

### Other Infection Prophylaxis

- **Latent tuberculosis:** isoniazid **300 mg** + pyridoxine **50 mg** daily, started within 6 months post-LT once graft function is stable; if started pre-LT, rifampin **300 mg daily × 4 months** is an option (Rec 29, Strong, Level 1).
- ***Pneumocystis jirovecii*:** trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole or atovaquone for **6 months** (Rec 30, Strong, Level 2).
- **Fungal:** fluconazole **2–4 weeks** with **≥2** of: renal replacement therapy (pre-, peri-, or post-LT); re-operation (incl. anastomotic leak, repeat laparotomy); re-LT; **≥40 units** of cellular products; choledochojejunostomy (Rec 31, Strong, Level 2).
- **Zoonotic:** avoid rodents, reptiles, birds as pets (Rec 32, Strong, Level 5).

## Oncologic Surveillance

Cancer risk **2.45×** the general population — relative risk highest for non-Hodgkin lymphoma (~10×), absolute risk for non-melanoma skin cancer.

- **All** (Recs 44–46): age-appropriate screening per US Preventive Services Task Force (Strong, Level 1); **yearly full-body skin exam**, more often after prior skin cancer (Strong, Level 2); tobacco cessation education + medical therapy (Strong, Level 1).
- **May consider** (Recs 47–48): yearly pelvic exam + cervical and anal Pap in women ≥21 or sexually active with positive human papillomavirus (HPV) (Weak, Level 4); ear, nose, and throat referral within year 1 after tobacco, ALD, or HPV (Weak, Level 3).

| Colon (Recs 49–51; Table 3) | Recommendation | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Average risk, **≥45 y** | General-population screening, but may consider **colonoscopy every 5 years** (colonoscopy preferred over stool tests) (Rec 49) | Weak, Level 3 |
| **PSC + IBD** | **Yearly colonoscopy with random biopsies** (Rec 50) | Strong, Level 2 |
| **PSC without IBD** | Colonoscopy **every 5 years** with ileal intubation + colon biopsies (Rec 51) | Strong, Level 3 |
| IBD without PSC | **Yearly** colonoscopy with random biopsies if surveillance criteria met by IBD extent and duration (Table 3) | Level 1 |

- Routine serial EBV polymerase chain reaction is **not** recommended (Weak, Level 3) — [[post-transplant-lymphoproliferative-disorder]].

## Metabolic Syndrome and Cardiometabolic Management

Yearly screening for hypertension, obesity, diabetes, and hyperlipidemia in **all** recipients (Rec 53, Strong, Level 5).

### Immunosuppression Optimization for Metabolic Comorbidities

| Problem | IS change | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Metabolic comorbidities (diabetes, hypertension) | **Early corticosteroid withdrawal**, weighed against rejection risk (Rec 54) | Strong, Level 1 |
| **Poor glycemic control** despite aggressive therapy | **Tacrolimus → cyclosporine** (Rec 55) | Weak, Level 3 |
| **Uncontrolled hypertension** despite aggressive therapy | **Cyclosporine → tacrolimus** (Rec 56) | Weak, Level 3 |
| **Hyperlipidemia** despite aggressive therapy | **CNI minimization ± antimetabolite** (mycophenolate/azathioprine) (Rec 57) | Weak, Level 5 |

> [!pearl] The two CNI switches run in opposite directions
> Glucose uncontrolled → tacrolimus to cyclosporine; blood pressure uncontrolled → cyclosporine to tacrolimus. Pick by the comorbidity that is failing (Recs 55–56, [[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]]).

### Drug Choices for Cardiometabolic Disease

| Problem | First-line (Recs 58–67) | Avoid / notes |
|---|---|---|
| Post-transplant diabetes | **Metformin** if no other cardiovascular risk factors (Rec 58); **[[semaglutide\|glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist (GLP-1 RA)]] and/or sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitor** if high atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease risk, heart failure, stroke, or CKD (Rec 59) | High risk = proteinuria/albuminuria, hypertension, left ventricular hypertrophy, systolic/diastolic dysfunction, ankle-brachial index **<0.9**; no sulfonylureas first-line |
| Hypertension (≥140/≥90 mmHg; target **≤130/80**) | Cardiovascular disease and/or CKD → angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor or angiotensin receptor blocker (Rec 60); neither → **amlodipine or felodipine** (Rec 61) | Avoid diltiazem/verapamil (↑ CNI levels) |
| Dyslipidemia | **Pravastatin or rosuvastatin** + lifestyle; ezetimibe and/or PCSK9 inhibitor if refractory (Rec 62). Triglycerides **>135 mg/dL** on statin → omega-3, then fibrate (Rec 63) | Avoid simvastatin, atorvastatin, lovastatin (CYP3A4; myopathy with CNIs) |
| Obesity | GLP-1 RA when diet + exercise fail (Rec 64); **BMI >30** → obesity specialist from **3–6 months** (Rec 65); [[bariatric-surgery\|weight-loss surgery]] if medical therapy fails or **>15–20%** loss needed, once stable (Recs 66–67) | — |

- Grades: Recs 58–63, 65 Strong, Level 5; Recs 64, 66–67 Strong, Level 3.
- **Cardiovascular:** established disease → cardiac assessment every visit + yearly cardiology (Recs 68–69); dysfunction or more-than-mild valve disease → individualized echo surveillance (Rec 70); aspirin and statins by general-population risk rules (Rec 72); recurrent events → CNI minimization + mTOR inhibitor (Rec 73, Weak, Level 3).

## Chronic Kidney Disease

Highest stage 4–5 CKD risk of any solid-organ group; early [[aki-in-cirrhosis|AKI]] predicts later CKD; CNIs drive it directly and via metabolic disease.

- **Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR)** from creatinine (+ cystatin C if available): stages 1–3 every **6–12 months**; stages 4–5 every **1–3 months**, including simultaneous liver–kidney recipients (Rec 74, Strong, Level 4).
- Control hypertension, diabetes, obesity, dyslipidemia (Rec 75); **nephrology at stage 4 (eGFR <30 mL/min)**, at stage 3 if urine suggests nephritis (Rec 76, Strong, Level 5).

> [!pitfall] Normal proteinuria does not exclude CKD on a CNI
> CNIs are antiproteinuric — a normal spot protein-to-creatinine ratio (threshold >0.3) can coexist with advanced CKD; follow eGFR ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-non-graft-complications]]).

| Timing / setting | Renal-protective IS strategy | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Peri- or early post-LT AKI | **Delay CNI** with induction (Rec 77) | Weak, Level 2 |
| CKD in year 1 | **Reduce CNI** + non-nephrotoxic adjunct (mycophenolate or everolimus) (Rec 78); minimize but **do not eliminate** CNI (Rec 79) | Strong, Level 1 |
| CKD beyond year 1 | Minimize but **do not stop** CNI (Rec 82) | Strong, Level 3 |
| Do not use | **Belatacept** — ↑ mortality (Rec 80) | Strong, Level 1 |
| Do not use | **Mycophenolate monotherapy** — acute cellular rejection (Rec 81) | Strong, Level 2 |

- No high-quality evidence for any renal-protective strategy intraoperatively or **≥12 months** post-LT. Tacrolimus vs cyclosporine: **66%** lower risk of stage 4–5 CKD.
