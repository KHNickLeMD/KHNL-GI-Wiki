The biliary manifestation of immunoglobulin G4 (IgG4)-related disease, and **the one sclerosing cholangitis that responds to corticosteroids** — which is the whole reason it must be separated from [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis|primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)]], where steroids do nothing, and from [[cholangiocarcinoma|cholangiocarcinoma (CCA)]], where a missed cancer is the cost of guessing wrong. It is the same disease process as type 1 [[autoimmune-pancreatitis|autoimmune pancreatitis (AIP)]] expressed in the bile duct, and pancreatic involvement accompanies it frequently.

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

**The definition is a triad, not a single test** ([[aasld-2022-psc]]):

| Component | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Biliary | [[biliary-stricture\|Biliary stricture(s)]] |
| Histology | Elevated IgG4-positive plasma cells in tissue |
| Serology | Serum IgG4 elevation |
| Frequent accompaniment | Pancreatic involvement |

- No ingested guideline prints a formal criteria set for IgG4-SC itself — assemble the diagnosis from serology, histology, imaging, other-organ involvement, and steroid response, each detailed below.
- The pancreatic side of the same disease **does** have a criteria set: the International Consensus Diagnostic Criteria (ICDC) for AIP, whose five cardinal features and the one printed component criterion live on [[autoimmune-pancreatitis]].

### Who Gets Tested for It

Three separate guideline triggers to send a serum IgG4 — IgG4-SC is looked for from the cholestasis side, the PSC side, and the cancer side:

| Trigger | Action | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Any patient with **possible PSC** | Measure serum IgG4 to exclude IgG4-SC (guidance statement 4) | [[aasld-2022-psc]] |
| **Established PSC** | Test at least once for elevated serum IgG4 — *Conditional recommendation, moderate quality*; measure in all patients at diagnosis | [[acg-2015-psc]] |
| **Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) elevation**, with or without raised bilirubin | Test for PSC with magnetic resonance cholangiography or [[ercp\|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]] **in conjunction with IgG4** — *Strong recommendation, very low level of evidence* | [[acg-2017-liver-chemistries]] |
| Workup of suspected **biliary tract cancer** (pain, jaundice, abnormal liver tests, obstruction) | **Consider serum IgG4 to rule out IgG4-related sclerosing cholangitis** | [[nccn-2026-biliary-tract-cancers]] |

### Severity Assessment

**No ingested source gives a severity score or stage for IgG4-SC.** Three features carry the management decisions instead:

- **Stricture level** — a **proximal** bile duct stricture is the one tied to relapse risk ([[#Relapse and Maintenance]]); distal obstruction in the setting of AIP may simply be pancreatic head swelling compressing the duct.
- **Pancreatic involvement** — present frequently, and determines whether the pancreatic management on [[autoimmune-pancreatitis]] applies in parallel.
- **Other organ involvement (OOI)** — IgG4-SC itself counts as OOI for AIP, and the combination marks the high-relapse group.

### Classification / Typing

IgG4-SC sits on the spectrum of IgG4-related disease. The full list of other organ manifestations (interstitial nephritis, orbital pseudotumor, salivary exocrinopathy, pulmonary interstitial fibrosis or nodules, mediastinal or retroperitoneal fibrosis) and the type 1 vs type 2 pancreatic phenotype table have their home on [[autoimmune-pancreatitis]].

- Isolated pancreatic involvement occurs in ~50% of AIP; ~50% have OOI — so **a biliary stricture may be the only expression of the disease, or one organ among several.**
- Obstructive jaundice in AIP arises either from **distal biliary ductal compression by pancreatic head swelling** or from a **proximal biliary stricture** — the second is IgG4-SC proper.
