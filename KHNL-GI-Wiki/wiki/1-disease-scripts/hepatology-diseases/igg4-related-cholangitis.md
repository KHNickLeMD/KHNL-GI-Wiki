The biliary manifestation of immunoglobulin G4 (IgG4)-related disease, and **the one sclerosing cholangitis that responds to corticosteroids** — which is the whole reason it must be separated from [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis|primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)]], where steroids do nothing, and from [[cholangiocarcinoma|cholangiocarcinoma (CCA)]], where a missed cancer is the cost of guessing wrong. It is the same disease process as type 1 [[autoimmune-pancreatitis|autoimmune pancreatitis (AIP)]] expressed in the bile duct, and pancreatic involvement accompanies it frequently.

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

**The definition is a triad, not a single test** ([[aasld-2022-psc]]):

| Component | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Biliary | [[biliary-stricture\|Biliary stricture(s)]] |
| Histology | Elevated IgG4-positive plasma cells in tissue |
| Serology | Serum IgG4 elevation |
| Frequent accompaniment | Pancreatic involvement |

- No ingested guideline prints a formal criteria set for IgG4-SC itself — assemble the diagnosis from serology, histology, imaging, other-organ involvement, and steroid response, each detailed below.
- The pancreatic side of the same disease **does** have a criteria set: the International Consensus Diagnostic Criteria (ICDC) for AIP, whose five cardinal features and the one printed component criterion live on [[autoimmune-pancreatitis]].

### Who Gets Tested for It

Three separate guideline triggers to send a serum IgG4 — IgG4-SC is looked for from the cholestasis side, the PSC side, and the cancer side:

| Trigger | Action | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Any patient with **possible PSC** | Measure serum IgG4 to exclude IgG4-SC (guidance statement 4) | [[aasld-2022-psc]] |
| **Established PSC** | Test at least once for elevated serum IgG4 — *Conditional recommendation, moderate quality*; measure in all patients at diagnosis | [[acg-2015-psc]] |
| **Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) elevation**, with or without raised bilirubin | Test for PSC with magnetic resonance cholangiography or [[ercp\|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]] **in conjunction with IgG4** — *Strong recommendation, very low level of evidence* | [[acg-2017-liver-chemistries]] |
| Workup of suspected **biliary tract cancer** (pain, jaundice, abnormal liver tests, obstruction) | **Consider serum IgG4 to rule out IgG4-related sclerosing cholangitis** | [[nccn-2026-biliary-tract-cancers]] |

### Severity Assessment

**No ingested source gives a severity score or stage for IgG4-SC.** Three features carry the management decisions instead:

- **Stricture level** — a **proximal** bile duct stricture is the one tied to relapse risk ([[#Relapse and Maintenance]]); distal obstruction in the setting of AIP may simply be pancreatic head swelling compressing the duct.
- **Pancreatic involvement** — present frequently, and determines whether the pancreatic management on [[autoimmune-pancreatitis]] applies in parallel.
- **Other organ involvement (OOI)** — IgG4-SC itself counts as OOI for AIP, and the combination marks the high-relapse group.

### Classification / Typing

IgG4-SC sits on the spectrum of IgG4-related disease. The full list of other organ manifestations (interstitial nephritis, orbital pseudotumor, salivary exocrinopathy, pulmonary interstitial fibrosis or nodules, mediastinal or retroperitoneal fibrosis) and the type 1 vs type 2 pancreatic phenotype table have their home on [[autoimmune-pancreatitis]].

- Isolated pancreatic involvement occurs in ~50% of AIP; ~50% have OOI — so **a biliary stricture may be the only expression of the disease, or one organ among several.**
- Obstructive jaundice in AIP arises either from **distal biliary ductal compression by pancreatic head swelling** or from a **proximal biliary stricture** — the second is IgG4-SC proper.

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[biliary-stricture]].*

**Three diagnoses produce the same cholangiogram, and the cost of confusing them is in opposite directions** — steroids given for [[cholangiocarcinoma|CCA]] delay cancer surgery; steroids withheld in IgG4-SC leave a treatable disease untreated.

| Feature | **IgG4-SC** | **[[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis\|PSC]]** | **[[cholangiocarcinoma\|CCA]]** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Serum IgG4 | Elevated (see [[#Serum IgG4]]) | Elevated in **up to 15%** ([[aasld-2022-psc]]) / ~10% ([[acg-2015-psc]]) — so elevation alone does not separate them | Not a feature; IgG4 measured to **rule IgG4-SC in**, not cancer out ([[nccn-2026-biliary-tract-cancers]]) |
| Corticosteroid response | **Yes — the defining feature** | **No** | No |
| Pancreas | Involved frequently ([[autoimmune-pancreatitis\|AIP]], same disease) | Not involved | Pancreatic-head mass if distal |
| [[inflammatory-bowel-disease\|IBD]] association | Not a feature | **Strong** | Via PSC |
| Tissue | IgG4-positive plasma cells | "Onion-skin" periductal fibrosis (infrequent) | Malignant cytology/histology |

- ⚠ **A raised serum IgG4 in a sclerosing cholangiogram is not sufficient** — it occurs in up to 15% of true PSC. The diagnosis needs the titer height, the histology, the other-organ picture, or the steroid response as well.
- ⚠ **Malignancy is the dominant competing diagnosis, not PSC.** In a biliary stricture with **no obvious mass on cross-sectional imaging, the risk of malignancy is ~55%** ([[asge-2023-indeterminate-biliary-strictures]]) — IgG4-SC is one of the named benign mimics, alongside PSC, fibrotic strictures and [[chronic-pancreatitis|chronic pancreatitis]]. **Tissue acquisition is required** to separate them; cross-sectional imaging cannot.

**Also consider** — the other secondary sclerosing cholangitides that must be excluded before any primary diagnosis ([[aasld-2022-psc]], Table 2): recurrent pyogenic cholangitis, ischemic cholangiopathy, parasitic and eosinophilic cholangitis, post-surgical injury, [[choledocholithiasis]], and [[portal-vein-thrombosis|portal hypertensive biliopathy]]. [[primary-biliary-cholangitis|Primary biliary cholangitis]] is separated by a positive anti-mitochondrial antibody and a non-sclerosing cholangiogram.

## Diagnostics

### Serum IgG4

**The single most useful test, and the one most often over-read.** The ingested guidelines give three thresholds, each answering a different question — they are not interchangeable:

| Threshold | Question it answers | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **IgG4 >5.6 g/L** (high-titer) | Does this sclerosing cholangiogram represent IgG4-SC rather than PSC? → **suggests IgG4-SC** | [[aasld-2022-psc]] (GS 4) |
| **IgG4/IgG1 ratio <0.24**, applied when **serum IgG4 is 1.4–2.8 g/L** | In the indeterminate band, can IgG4-SC be excluded? → **yes, the ratio excludes it** | [[aasld-2022-psc]] (GS 4) |
| **IgG4 ≥2× upper limit of normal (ULN; 280 mg/dL)** | Is there pancreatic AIP? → **International Consensus Diagnostic Criteria (ICDC) level 1 evidence**; sensitivity **53%**, specificity **99%** | [[aga-2019-autoimmune-pancreatitis]] |

- **Read each threshold with its question.** The 280 mg/dL cutoff is an AIP criterion, in mg/dL; the 5.6 g/L cutoff is a biliary one, in g/L. **Do not merge them into a single rule.**
- **False positives are real but uncommon at high titer** — patients with [[pancreatic-cancer|pancreatic cancer]] can have an elevated IgG4, but **only 1% exceed 280 mg/dL** ([[aga-2019-autoimmune-pancreatitis]]). A high-titer result is therefore informative; a mildly raised one is not.
- ⚠ **The two guidelines disagree on what counts as a "marked" elevation.** [[acg-2015-psc]] defines marked elevation as **>140 mg/dL** (= 1.4 g/L); [[aasld-2022-psc]] puts the IgG4-SC-suggesting titer at **>5.6 g/L** (= 560 mg/dL), fourfold higher, and treats 1.4–2.8 g/L as an **indeterminate band requiring the IgG4/IgG1 ratio**. **AASLD 2022 governs this page** as the newer tier-1 source. ACG 2015 itself states the distinction "is not fully standardized."
- **After treatment, serum IgG4 has no role.** It does **not** predict relapse and does **not** diagnose recurrence ([[aga-2019-autoimmune-pancreatitis]]) — see [[#Monitoring After Treatment]].

### Imaging and Cholangiography

- **[[mri-mrcp|Magnetic resonance imaging / magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRI/MRCP)]] is the first imaging modality** for a suspected sclerosing cholangiopathy, and **[[ercp|ERCP]] should be avoided for diagnosis alone** ([[aasld-2022-psc]] GS 1, GS 3) — reserve it for tissue sampling or therapy of a relevant stricture.
- **Look at the pancreas on the same study.** The features that identify the pancreatic half of the disease ([[aga-2019-autoimmune-pancreatitis]]): **diffuse parenchymal enlargement ("sausage shape")**, a **low-attenuating capsule ("rim enhancement")**, and **diffuse or multifocal narrowing of the pancreatic duct without upstream dilatation**. Their presence converts an indeterminate biliary stricture into a confident IgG4 diagnosis.
- **Distinguish the two mechanisms of jaundice**, because only one is IgG4-SC: **distal biliary compression by pancreatic head swelling** (a pancreatic problem) vs a **proximal biliary stricture** (IgG4-SC proper, and the one tied to relapse risk).

### Tissue Acquisition and Histology

**Histology is one leg of the diagnostic triad, and the stricture must usually be sampled anyway to exclude cancer.**

- **Target: elevated IgG4-positive plasma cells in tissue** ([[aasld-2022-psc]]). On the pancreatic side the printed cutoff is **≥10 IgG4-positive cells per high-power field** ([[aga-2019-autoimmune-pancreatitis]], for AIP vs type 2 disease); **no ingested source prints a bile-duct–specific cell count.**
- **Pancreatic sampling — biopsy, not aspiration.** [[endoscopic-ultrasound|Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]]-guided **fine-needle aspiration (FNA) cytology has poor sensitivity even with larger-diameter needles**; **fine-needle biopsy (FNB), which preserves gland architecture, is what gives the tissue diagnosis.** The ICDC recommends **core biopsy where there is a focal mass and/or obstructive jaundice, cancer has been excluded, and the diagnosis is unclear** ([[aga-2019-autoimmune-pancreatitis]]).
- **Biliary sampling — the indeterminate-stricture pathway** ([[asge-2023-indeterminate-biliary-strictures]], all *conditional recommendations, very low quality of evidence*):

| Situation | Suggested approach |
|---|---|
| Undetermined stricture at ERCP | Add **fluoroscopic-guided biopsy sampling to [[brush-cytology\|brush cytology]]**, rather than brush cytology alone |
| **Nondistal** stricture with a high probability of adequate drainage of the critical liver segment; **or** a previous nondiagnostic ERCP without cholangioscopy — **and** a center with the expertise and ready equipment access | **[[cholangioscopy\|Cholangioscopic]]-guided biopsy sampling**; otherwise ERCP with or without cholangioscopy |
| Prior nondiagnostic ERCP, **or** a **distal** stricture, **or** lymphadenopathy / metastatic disease on cross-sectional imaging | **EUS in addition to ERCP** |

- ⚠ **Avoid sampling the biliary mass itself if EUS is performed for a hilar stricture** — needle-track seeding ([[asge-2023-indeterminate-biliary-strictures]], general concepts).

## Therapeutics

**This is the sclerosing cholangitis that responds to corticosteroids** ([[acg-2015-psc]]) — the entire reason for separating it from PSC. The treatment algorithm is the AIP algorithm; its single home is [[autoimmune-pancreatitis]], where the full flowchart lives, and only the biliary-specific branches are stated here.

### Who Is Treated, and With What

| Step | Regimen ([[aga-2019-autoimmune-pancreatitis]], Figure 1) |
|---|---|
| **Trigger to treat** | Clinical symptoms (abdominal pain, obstructive jaundice) **or** other organ involvement. An **asymptomatic** patient is treated only for a persistent pancreatic mass, **or persistent liver function test (LFT) abnormalities in the setting of IgG4-SC / OOI** — the biliary indication |
| **Induction** | **Prednisone 40 mg/day × 4 weeks** |
| **Complete remission** | **Taper by 5 mg per week** |
| **No response** | **Reconsider the diagnosis** — do not escalate the dose |
| **Steroid intolerance or incomplete remission** | **Rituximab induction** |

- **Steroid response is ~100%** in the pancreatic disease, and failure to respond should move the clinician back toward malignancy rather than toward more steroid.

### Biliary Drainage

- **[[ercp|ERCP]] and biliary stenting are *rarely* indicated for the obstructive jaundice** — the ICDC advises treating **mild jaundice with no signs of infection with steroid therapy alone** ([[aga-2019-autoimmune-pancreatitis]]).
- **The decision turns on two qualifiers: *mild* jaundice and *no signs of infection*.** The source **defines neither** — no bilirubin threshold is given, and no guidance is offered for non-mild jaundice.
- The acknowledged case *for* stenting: it reduces [[acute-cholangitis|cholangitis]] risk and allows cytologic brushings to separate benign from malignant stricture.
- **Surgery is reserved for suspected malignancy.**

### Monitoring After Treatment

- **Do not track serum IgG4** — no value for predicting relapse or diagnosing recurrence.
- **Do not re-biopsy** — histologic re-evaluation is not recommended after treatment.
- **Follow-up must be close and must watch for occult malignancy**, but the source specifies **no modality, no interval and no duration**.

### Relapse and Maintenance

- **Proximal IgG4-SC is itself the high-relapse marker.** The two stated risk factors are an **elevated pre-treatment immunoglobulin E (IgE)** and **OOI with a proximal bile duct stricture or IgG4-SC** ([[aga-2019-autoimmune-pancreatitis]]) — a patient on this page is, by definition, in the high-risk group.
- Relapse occurs in **up to 60%** of AIP patients, **during the steroid taper or after discontinuation**.
- **High risk warrants long-term maintenance** — one of the four regimens (prednisone 40 mg/day for 4–6 weeks then gradual taper or low-dose maintenance; prednisone plus an immunomodulator then steroid withdrawal; rituximab induction; rituximab induction and maintenance). The regimens, and the doses the source does and does not give, live on [[autoimmune-pancreatitis]].
- ⚠ **The relapse decision cannot rest on the marker that made the diagnosis** — serum IgG4 is useless for it. Use the pre-treatment IgE and the stricture level instead.
