## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

Autoimmune hepatitis (AIH) is an immune-mediated hepatitis characterized by elevated aminotransferases, elevated IgG (>1.1× upper limit of normal [ULN]), positive autoantibodies, interface hepatitis on biopsy, and response to immunosuppression. It is a diagnosis of exclusion — viral hepatitis, hereditary liver disease, drug-induced liver injury, and cholestatic liver disease must be ruled out, **although coexistence with [[nafld-masld|MASLD]], [[alcohol-associated-liver-disease|ALD]] or viral hepatitis is possible**. [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]] [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

- **Suspect AIH** in any unexplained aminotransferase elevation *irrespective of magnitude*, and in **cirrhosis of unknown cause even with normal aminotransferases**; **normal IgG does not exclude AIH** (elevated in 85–95% overall but only 25–39% of acute presentations) [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]
- **Liver biopsy is required to establish the diagnosis** (EASL, LoE 1, strong) — ≥8 portal tracts, needle ≥18G (preferably 16G or wider), cylinder ≥1.5 cm (>2.5 cm better), read by a liver pathologist [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]
- Report must give **necroinflammatory grade (Ishak mHAI), fibrosis stage, and the 2022 International AIH Pathology Group (IAHPG) category — likely / possible / unlikely AIH** [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

| IAHPG 2022 category | Histology (absent features of another liver disease unless stated) |
|---|---|
| **Likely** | Predominantly portal lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate with *more than mild* interface and/or lobular hepatitis; **or** more than mild lobular hepatitis with portal lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate and/or interface hepatitis and/or portal-based fibrosis |
| **Possible** | Predominantly portal lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate *without* more than mild lobular or interface hepatitis; **or** any lobular hepatitis lacking the three likely features; **or** either likely pattern *in the presence of* histological features of another liver disease |
| **Unlikely** | Portal or lobular hepatitis without the likely features, in the presence of features of another liver disease |

> [!pitfall] Emperipolesis and rosettes are no longer "typical AIH"
> EASL 2025 drops both as typical lesions — they are not specific. If seen, report them as surrogate markers of severity only. They remain in the 2008 simplified score's definition of *typical* histology, so a score built on the old definition and a modern pathology report can disagree ([[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]).

**Type 1 AIH (most common in North America):**

- Antinuclear antibody (ANA) and/or smooth muscle antibody (SMA) positive
- Peripubertal and adult presentation
- 96% of US adult AIH cases
- Acute onset in 25–75%; acute severe in 2–6%; asymptomatic in 25–34%; cirrhosis at presentation in 28–33% of adults
- Typically steroid-responsive; treatment-free remission possible (see [[#Treatment Withdrawal]])

**Type 2 AIH:**

- Anti-liver kidney microsome type 1 (anti-LKM1) positive (± anti-liver cytosol type 1 [anti-LC1], anti-LKM3); usually under age 14
- 9–12% of US children with AIH (38% of UK children); acute onset ~40%, acute liver failure (ALF) possible
- More aggressive; rarely enters sustained remission off medications — usually needs long-term immunosuppression

**Anti-soluble liver antigen/liver pancreas (anti-SLA/LP):**

- Present in either type; highly specific for AIH
- Associated with worse prognosis and more refractory disease
- Complete immunosuppression (IS) withdrawal is rarely successful if anti-SLA positive

**Initial serological workup:** [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

- Adults: ANA + SMA
- Children: ANA + SMA + anti-LKM1
- Additional: anti-SLA/LP (increased specificity), serum IgG (quantitative), serum protein electrophoresis

**Which International Autoimmune Hepatitis Group (IAIHG) scoring system to use:** [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

| | Revised original (1999) | Simplified (2008) |
|---|---|---|
| Sensitivity | 100% | 95% |
| Specificity | 73% | 90% |
| Accuracy | 82% | 92% |
| Best for | complex / unusual features | typical patients |

- In children, simplified criteria: sensitivity 77%, specificity 95%; ~17% false negatives, associated with seronegative AIH
- Reassess with the **revised** system whenever the simplified system yields a low score
- **Neither system should be used to assess overlap syndromes**; both lack prospective validation and are inaccurate in concurrent [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis|primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)]]/[[primary-biliary-cholangitis|primary biliary cholangitis (PBC)]]/[[nafld-masld|metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease/steatohepatitis (MASLD-MASH)]], after liver transplantation (LT), or fulminant failure
**Simplified score (2008), point values** ([[hennes-2008-simplified-aih-criteria]]):

| Variable | Cutoff | Points |
|---|---|---|
| ANA or SMA | ≥1:40 | 1 |
| ANA or SMA ≥1:80, **or** anti-LKM ≥1:40, **or** anti-SLA positive | — | 2 |
| IgG | > upper normal limit (ULN) | 1 |
| IgG | **>1.10 × ULN** | 2 |
| Liver histology | Compatible with AIH | 1 |
| Liver histology | **Typical** AIH | 2 |
| Absence of viral hepatitis | Yes | 2 |

- **≥6 = probable AIH · ≥7 = definite AIH** (maximum 8).
- **EASL 2025 updates the autoantibody row for modern assays** (*LoE 3, weak*): the 2008 cut-offs apply **only to indirect immunofluorescence testing (IFT) on rodent tissue sections**. Where HEp-2 cells or ELISA are used, score **1 point for "positive"** (IFT ≥1:40 on tissue sections, **≥1:80 or 1:160 for ANA on HEp-2**, ELISA at locally established cut-offs) and **2 points for "strongly positive"** (IFT ≥1:80 on tissue sections, **≥1:160 or 1:320 for ANA on HEp-2**), with anti-LKM ≥1:40 or anti-SLA/LP positive still scoring 2. **The IAHPG likely/possible categories may be substituted for *typical*/*compatible* histology** (*LoE 3, weak*). [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]
- **Apply any score with caution** in acute AIH, variant syndromes, concurrent liver disease and drug-induced autoimmune-like hepatitis (DI-ALH) (*LoE 3, strong*) [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]
- **The autoantibody block caps at 2 points** — antibodies are not additive beyond 2, however many are positive.
- **Histologic evidence of hepatitis is a necessary condition**, not just a scoring line. *Typical* requires **all three** of interface hepatitis, **emperipolesis**, and **hepatic rosettes**; *compatible* = chronic hepatitis with lymphocytic infiltration lacking the full triad.

> [!pitfall] The antibody titre you score depends on the assay
> **ANA on HEp-2 cells reads higher than on tissue sections — halve a HEp-2 result before scoring it.** ELISA for ANA and SMA is not standardised enough to use with this score, and **anti-LKM should not be scored in a patient with [[hepatitis-c|hepatitis C]]**, where it occurs without AIH. ([[hennes-2008-simplified-aih-criteria]])

- ⚠ **Type 2 AIH is systematically under-scored** — those patients often carry **only anti-LKM** ([[hennes-2008-simplified-aih-criteria]]).
**Revised original score (1999), the fallback for complex cases** ([[alvarez-1999-iaihg-revised-criteria]]):

| Parameter | Value → score |
|---|---|
| Female sex | **+2** |
| ALP:AST (or ALT) ratio | <1.5 **+2** · 1.5–3.0 **0** · >3.0 **−2** |
| Globulins *or* IgG above normal (× ULN) | >2.0 **+3** · 1.5–2.0 **+2** · 1.0–1.5 **+1** · <1.0 **0** |
| ANA, SMA or anti-LKM-1 titre | >1:80 **+3** · 1:80 **+2** · 1:40 **+1** · <1:40 **0** |
| AMA positive | **−4** |
| Hepatitis viral markers | positive **−3** · negative **+3** |
| Drug history | positive **−4** · negative **+1** |
| Average alcohol | <25 g/day **+2** · >60 g/day **−2** |
| Liver histology | interface hepatitis **+3** · lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate **+1** · rosettes **+1** · **none of these −5** · **biliary changes −3** · other changes **−3** |
| Other autoimmune disease(s) | **+2** |
| *Optional (only if ANA/SMA/anti-LKM-1 seronegative)* | other **defined** autoantibodies **+2** · HLA DR3 or DR4 **+1** |
| Response to therapy | complete **+2** · relapse **+3** |

| | Definite | Probable |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-treatment** | **>15** | **10–15** |
| **Post-treatment** | **>17** | **12–17** |

> [!pitfall] The ALP:AST line is a ratio of elevations, not of raw values
> Score **(ALP ÷ ULN ALP) ÷ (AST ÷ ULN AST)**. Using raw IU/L misassigns the commonest parameter on the sheet. Titres assume indirect immunofluorescence on rodent tissue (HEp-2 for ANA); **lower titres, especially anti-LKM-1, are significant in children and score at least +1**. ([[alvarez-1999-iaihg-revised-criteria]])

- ⚠ **The IAIHG designed this score for research and validated it only at *initial presentation***. It is **not validated** for suspected recurrence after [[liver-transplantation|liver transplantation]] or for reassessing long-standing disease, and the point values are "to some extent arbitrary… qualitative… do not reflect overall severity" ([[alvarez-1999-iaihg-revised-criteria]]). For routine practice the IAIHG's position is that its **descriptive** criteria suffice.
- The heavy negative weights — **AMA −4, biliary histology −3, drug history −4, no interface hepatitis −5** — exist because the 1993 version upgraded PSC/PBC and drug-injury patients into "probable AIH"; re-weighting raised specificity for excluding AIH in PSC from **64.9% to 89.5%**.
- **Definite AIH should not be diagnosed without liver histology**, and **no morphological feature is pathognomonic** ([[alvarez-1999-iaihg-revised-criteria]]).

**Key histologic features:**

- **Interface hepatitis**: lymphoplasmacytic infiltration beyond limiting plate with hepatocyte necrosis — hallmark finding
- **Rosette formation**: regenerating hepatocytes forming pseudoacinar patterns
- **Emperipolesis**: lymphocyte traversing hepatocyte cytoplasm (highly specific but not pathognomonic)
- Dense plasma cell infiltration
- Centrilobular necrosis: present in acute/severe AIH

### Severity Assessment

Both definitions additionally require **no previously recognized liver disease** — the qualifier that separates them from decompensated chronic AIH. [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

| | Acute severe AIH | AIH-related ALF |
|---|---|---|
| [[jaundice\|Jaundice]] | required | — |
| International normalized ratio (INR) | **≥1.5 and <2.0** | **>2.0** |
| [[hepatic-encephalopathy\|Hepatic encephalopathy]] | absent | present |
| Prior liver disease | none recognized | none recognized |
| Biopsy | no chronic lesions | — |
| Action | steroid trial, **reassess at days 3–7** | evaluate directly for liver transplantation; see [[acute-liver-failure]] |

- ⚠ **Two numbers differ between the guidelines; this page uses the newer EASL ones.** EASL 2025 sets the acute severe threshold at **INR ≥1.5** (AASLD 2020: >1.5) and times ALF encephalopathy to **within 8 weeks of onset** (AASLD 2020: within 26 weeks). EASL also adds *no chronic lesions on liver biopsy* to the acute severe definition. [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]
- In **children**, ALF is defined as elevated ALT with **INR >1.5 plus encephalopathy, or INR >2.0 regardless of encephalopathy** [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

**EASL presentation categories** — the label decides the pathway, so assign one at presentation ([[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]):

| Category | Definition |
|---|---|
| Asymptomatic | No symptoms, only abnormal liver biochemistry (6–23% of adults, 12–18% of children) |
| Chronic | **>6 months** from onset of abnormal biochemistry or symptoms (48–68% adults, 33–52% children) |
| Acute | **<30 days** from symptom onset — jaundice, fatigue, drowsiness, fever (10–26% adults, 19–58% children) |
| Acute exacerbation of chronic AIH | Acute injury on underlying, often unrecognised, chronic disease — **about one third of patients have cirrhosis at diagnosis** |
| Acute severe AIH | As in the table above |
| [[acute-on-chronic-liver-failure\|ACLF]] | Acute injury in a patient with often unrecognised cirrhosis **plus extrahepatic organ failure** |
| ALF | As in the table above |

**Fibrosis staging:** Ishak / METAVIR / Scheuer–Batts-Ludwig staging on [[liver-biopsy|biopsy]] — **Scheuer/Batts-Ludwig is the system derived for viral and autoimmune hepatitis**; stage definitions and the warning against converting between schemes live on [[liver-biopsy]]. Model for End-Stage Liver Disease (MELD) score for decompensated AIH [[cirrhosis|cirrhosis]]

**Response endpoints — the definitions every treatment decision below is keyed to** ([[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]], from the IAIHG endpoint set):

| Endpoint | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Complete biochemical response (CBR)** | Normalisation of aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alanine aminotransferase (ALT) **and** IgG, **no later than 6–12 months** after starting treatment |
| **Insufficient response** | No CBR by 6–12 months on standard of care — applies to first- *and* second-line therapy |
| **Non-response** | **<50% fall in aminotransferases from baseline at 4 weeks** |
| **Remission (histological)** | **mHAI <4/18** on an adequate biopsy, at any time on treatment |
| **Intolerance** | Any adverse event attributable to the regimen that leads to drug cessation |

- Before calling insufficient response, confirm the patient actually had **predniso(lo)ne ≥0.5 mg/kg/day starting dose with maintenance up to 10 mg/day plus an adequately dosed immunomodulator**, and confirm adherence
- CBR within 6 months is an independent predictor of favourable outcome, but **17% who miss it at 6 months reach it at 12 months with the same prognostic benefit** — hence the 6–12-month window
- EASL flags the 4-week non-response cut-off as probably too early (a **≥80% fall at 8 weeks** predicted normalisation at 6 and 12 months better)

### Classification / Typing

| Type | Autoantibodies | Demographics | Prognosis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Type 1** | ANA, SMA/anti-actin, SLA | Peripubertal + adults; 96% of US adults | Remission after drug withdrawal possible |
| **Type 2** | Anti-LKM1 (± anti-LC1, anti-LKM3) | Usually <14 y; 9–12% of US children | Remission after withdrawal rare — usually needs long-term IS |

⚠ **EASL 2025 no longer recommends subclassifying adults by autoantibody profile** (*LoE 3, weak*): subtype does not change severity, treatment response or prognosis, and the largest paediatric cohort found anti-LKM1-positive children behaved like type 1. The table is retained because the terminology is still in wide use. The prognostic signal that does survive is **anti-SLA/LP**, which may mandate permanent immunosuppression (below). [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[abnormal-liver-chemistries]].*

- [[drug-induced-liver-injury]] — drug-induced liver injury (DILI) with an autoimmune phenotype is **DI-ALH, not AIH**; it is separated from AIH by treatment response and course, not by serology or biopsy. Diagnostic criteria, the drug-withdrawal-then-steroid pathway and the differential-diagnosis algorithm: [[drug-induced-liver-injury#Drug-Induced Autoimmune-Like Hepatitis (DI-ALH)|DI-ALH]]. Culprits include nitrofurantoin, minocycline, [[anti-tnf-agents|infliximab, adalimumab]], hydralazine, methyldopa, interferon, imatinib, statins and dietary supplements
- [[alcohol-associated-liver-disease]] — AST:ALT ratio ≥2:1; gamma-glutamyltransferase (GGT) elevated; history of significant alcohol use
- [[wilson-disease]] — young patient (<40 years); Kayser-Fleischer rings; low ceruloplasmin; elevated 24h urine copper; may cause acute hepatitis mimicking AIH
- Viral hepatitis ([[chronic-hepatitis-b|hepatitis B virus (HBV)]], hepatitis C virus (HCV), hepatitis E virus [HEV]) — serology; HEV can mimic acute AIH in endemic regions; HCV may induce low-titer ANA/SMA
- [[primary-biliary-cholangitis]] — antimitochondrial antibody (AMA) positive (≥1:40); cholestatic pattern (alkaline phosphatase [ALP]/GGT dominant); can coexist as overlap syndrome
- [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis]] — [[mri-mrcp|magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP)]]/[[ercp|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]] with biliary stricturing; cholangiographic findings diagnostic
- [[celiac-disease|Celiac]]-related hepatitis — tissue transglutaminase (TTG)-IgA; modest transaminase elevation; resolves with gluten-free diet
- [[hereditary-hemochromatosis]] — elevated ferritin; transferrin saturation; HFE mutation

## Diagnostics

### Laboratory

- Aminotransferases (ALT, AST) — typically markedly elevated; pattern not unique [[acg-2017-liver-chemistries]]
- Serum IgG (quantitative) — elevated >1.1× ULN
- **Autoantibody panel — order and method matter** [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]: first screen **ANA, SMA, anti-LKM1, anti-LC1 by IFT on triple rodent tissue sections (liver, kidney, stomach)**, *in parallel* with **anti-SLA/LP by solid-phase assay** (anti-SLA/LP is not detectable by IFT). Starting dilution 1:40–1:80 in adults (higher on HEp-2), **1:10–1:20 in children**
  - **Negative IFT despite suspicion → re-test at a lower dilution (1:40 adults, 1:10 children)** and refer to a reference laboratory for non-standard antibodies; extremely high IgG can cause a false-negative pro-zone effect, so also retest at *higher* serial dilutions
  - **pANNA (atypical perinuclear ANCA) only after ANA, SMA and anti-SLA/LP are negative** — isolated pANNA reactivity is rare
  - Antibodies are found in **>95%** when methods follow the guideline; **monitoring titres is not recommended in adults** (no prognostic value) but may matter in children and adolescents
  - Anti-dsDNA is positive in ~30% of AIH and up to 60% of AIH/PBC — **ANA + anti-dsDNA is not by itself SLE**
- ALP, GGT — predominant ALP/GGT elevation suggests overlap with PBC/PSC rather than isolated AIH
- Serum protein electrophoresis (SPEP) — broad-based gamma globulin elevation
- Hepatic function panel (INR, albumin, bilirubin) — synthetic function assessment
- Complete blood count (CBC) (cytopenias with [[portal-hypertension|portal hypertension]]); comprehensive metabolic panel (CMP); thiopurine methyltransferase (TPMT) genotype/phenotype before azathioprine (AZA)
- Exclusion panel: hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg), anti-hepatitis B core (anti-HBc), HBV DNA, anti-HCV, HCV RNA, anti-hepatitis A virus (HAV) IgM, anti-HEV IgM
- AMA — positive in PBC overlap; negative in isolated AIH
- Celiac panel (TTG-IgA), thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) — comorbid autoimmune screening; **EASL 2025 makes screening for autoimmune thyroid and coeliac disease mandatory in every patient at diagnosis** (*LoE 2, strong*) — TSH, T4, anti-thyroid antibodies and coeliac-related antibodies [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]] [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]]
- 25-hydroxyvitamin D — baseline and annually on glucocorticoids [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

### Pretreatment Evaluation

- **TPMT genotype or phenotype** — screen before AZA initiation; poor metabolizers at risk for severe myelosuppression [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]]
- **HBsAg and anti-HBc total** — prophylactic antiviral (entecavir or tenofovir) required if HBsAg positive before initiating immunosuppression; the reactivation risk tiers, agent choice, and prophylaxis duration are on [[chronic-hepatitis-b|chronic hepatitis B → HBV Reactivation on Immunosuppression]]
- **Dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA) scan** — **recommended in all adults at treatment initiation** (EASL *LoE 3, strong*) and during follow-up; repeat q2–3 years on glucocorticoids [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]] [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]]
- **Baseline intraocular pressure + periodic ophthalmic review** on corticosteroids — two-thirds of children on long-term oral steroids had (mostly asymptomatic) raised pressure, reversible with treatment and taper [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]
- **Vaccinations** (HAV, HBV, influenza, pneumococcal, tetanus-diphtheria-acellular pertussis (Tdap), coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19)) — before immunosuppression initiation
- Extrahepatic autoimmune disease screening: thyroid disease, rheumatoid arthritis (RA), [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]], Sjogren's, antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody (ANCA) vasculitis, celiac disease

### Imaging

- Liver ultrasound — assess for hepatomegaly, splenomegaly, signs of [[portal-hypertension|portal hypertension]], [[hcc-surveillance|hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) surveillance]] in cirrhosis
- [[liver-stiffness-measurement|Elastography (vibration-controlled transient elastography [VCTE]/FibroScan or magnetic resonance elastography [MRE])]] — fibrosis staging if biopsy not performed
- MRCP — if overlap with PSC suspected (elevated ALP, concomitant IBD)

### Liver Biopsy

- Definitive for diagnosis (interface hepatitis, plasma cell infiltrate, rosettes, emperipolesis)
- Required for definite AIH scoring on Simplified criteria
- Recommended before IS withdrawal to confirm histologic remission (preferred, not mandatory in adults)
- Staging for fibrosis guides prognosis and treatment duration decisions

## Therapeutics

**Who to treat:** immunosuppression is recommended in **all patients with active disease, including advanced fibrosis and compensated [[cirrhosis]]** (*LoE 1, strong*); the goal is complete biochemical, clinical **and histological** remission (*LoE 2, strong*). Treatment may be withheld with close follow-up in **elderly asymptomatic patients with mild activity and no advanced fibrosis** (watch-and-wait, *LoE 4, strong*), in burnt-out cirrhosis without inflammatory activity, and where corticosteroids are contraindicated (uncontrolled diabetes, severe osteoporosis, morbid obesity/metabolic syndrome) — then check aminotransferases and IgG **every 3–6 months** and re-biopsy if they rise or fluctuate. [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

- **Elderly with at least moderate activity get standard immunosuppression** — age is not a reason to under-treat (*LoE 3, strong*); patients ≥65 years reach CBR at the same rate as younger ones and relapse **less** often
- **Taper corticosteroids rapidly in the elderly** (*LoE 5, strong*) — the risk is aggravating osteoporosis and/or cardiovascular risk factors

### First-Line Treatment

**Predniso(lo)ne + either [[thiopurines|azathioprine]] or [[mycophenolate-mofetil|mycophenolate mofetil (MMF)]]** — EASL 2025 promotes MMF to a first-line alternative (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, consensus*). [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

| Drug | Start | Final dose | Timing / caveats |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Predniso(lo)ne** | **≥0.5 mg/kg/day**; **up to 1 mg/kg/day** in more severe or advanced disease | Taper by response | Higher dose = higher response but more adverse events (meta-analysis, 25 studies, 3,305 patients) |
| **[[thiopurines\|Azathioprine]] (AZA)** | **50 mg/day** | **1–2 mg/kg/day** (reached ~week 4) | Add **only when bilirubin <6 mg/dl**, ideally **2 weeks after** the steroid — avoids confusing non-response with AZA hepatotoxicity. **Never as monotherapy for induction** (high mortality). Avoid in acute severe AIH and decompensated cirrhosis; caution in TPMT deficiency, pregnancy, cytopenias, malignancy |
| **[[mycophenolate-mofetil\|MMF]]** | **1,000 mg/day**, started with the steroid on day 1 | **1.5–2 g/day** (0.75–1 g twice daily), reached at **week 2** | **Teratogenic — counsel women *and* men.** Contraception rules and drug monitoring: [[mycophenolate-mofetil]] |

- **Tapering is individualised to CBR status** (*LoE 4, strong*). Rapid taper (**5–10 mg every 1–2 weeks**) is desirable but must follow response; the trial-mirrored schedule reaches ~5 mg/day by week 24 in an 80-kg index case
- Check **ALT and IgG at weeks 4, 12 and 24** as the efficacy parameters
- ⚠ **MMF vs AZA — the data behind the promotion:** CAMARO (prospective open-label superiority RCT) gave **CBR at 6 months 72.2% with MMF vs 32.3% with AZA (p = 0.004)**, severe adverse events 0% vs 12.9%, withdrawal 5.1% vs 25.8%
- ⚠ **[[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis|AASLD 2020]] differs**: it gives prednisone **40–60 mg/day alone or 20–40 mg/day with AZA**, and keeps MMF as second-line only. This page follows the newer EASL dosing; readers using AASLD doses should know the regimens are not interchangeable
- Check **TPMT** metabolizer status before prescribing AZA; yearly dermatologic screening (nonmelanoma skin cancer) and sun avoidance

**Budesonide — demoted.** **Not recommended as part of first-line treatment, and contraindicated in cirrhosis** (*LoE 2, strong recommendation, consensus*). **Switching to budesonide may be suggested for corticosteroid side effects in non-cirrhotic, predniso(lo)ne-dependent patients** (*LoE 3, weak*). Dose where used: **3 mg three times daily**. [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

- Contraindicated in cirrhosis because portal-systemic shunting abolishes the first-pass advantage and the risk of [[portal-vein-thrombosis|portal vein thrombosis]] is high
- ⚠ **This reverses [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis|AASLD 2020]]**, which made budesonide + AZA an equivalent first-line option in non-cirrhotic patients on a meta-analysis (OR 2.19 for biochemical remission). The positive RCT was not replicated: in 381 treatment-naive patients, normalisation of aminotransferases and IgG at 6 months was **52% predniso(lo)ne vs 37% budesonide**, and the side-effect advantage vanished once cirrhotic patients were excluded. A paediatric RCT (n = 46) found no difference (16% vs 15%)
- Do not use budesonide in acute severe AIH or ALF

### Monitoring on Treatment

[[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]] — assessment is individualised to severity, response and tolerance (*LoE 2, strong*); adverse events are managed **pro-actively and anticipated** (*LoE 5, strong*).

| Phase | What and how often |
|---|---|
| First month on AZA | AST, ALT, prothrombin time, fasting glucose, full blood count **weekly** — AZA toxicity peaks in the first 6 weeks |
| First month on MMF | Lighter — schedule on [[mycophenolate-mofetil#Monitoring\|mycophenolate mofetil]] |
| To 12 months | Labs **including IgG every 2–3 months**, to call CBR at 6 and 12 months |
| Maintenance after CBR | **Every 3–6 months**; thyroid function every 6–12 months |
| Fibrosis | **[[liver-stiffness-measurement\|Transient elastography]] ~every 12 months** (*LoE 3, strong*) — reliable from **6 months** after starting immunosuppression; **≥9 kPa** severe fibrosis, **12.5–16 kPa** cirrhosis |

- **Calcium intake and vitamin D supplementation** on long-term corticosteroids (*LoE 3, strong*); low-carbohydrate, low-salt diet
- Apply **routine general-population cancer screening** — extrahepatic cancer occurs in up to 5% (nonmelanoma skin and haematological most common)

### Maintenance Therapy

- **Azathioprine or MMF as monotherapy, or with low-dose predniso(lo)ne ≤5 mg/day**, dose adapted to sustain stable CBR (*LoE 2, strong*) [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]
- **Total duration at least 4 years, with at least the last 2 years in sustained CBR**
- **Low-dose predniso(lo)ne monotherapy only** in mild disease with CBR and intolerance to **both** AZA and MMF (*LoE 3, weak*)
- Continuing corticosteroids beyond **3 months after ALT normalises** gave no better outcomes in a 1,267-patient UK audit — taper off and run a steroid-sparing monotherapy
- AZA continued alone at **2 mg/kg/day** gave 0% relapse at 12 months and 17% at 5 years

**Adherence — investigate it before changing the regimen** ([[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]], all *LoE 2–3, strong*):

- **Patient-centred consultation** for suspected or confirmed non-adherence — screen for anxiety and depression and assess **capability, opportunity and motivation**
- **Start maintenance early** so corticosteroids can be reduced and withdrawn; this is itself a recommendation, because steroid burden drives non-adherence and erodes the caregiver–patient relationship
- **Test [[thiopurines|thiopurine]] metabolites to assess adherence** — undetectable or low 6-TGN *and* 6-MMP mean the drug is not being taken; trigger a medication-management/side-effect and benefit–risk discussion rather than a dose increase

### Intolerance, Insufficient Response and Rescue

**Intolerance to thiopurines** — **MMF is the second-line agent of choice** (*LoE 2, strong*); **mercaptopurine (MP) or thioguanine (TG) can be used** (*LoE 4, weak*). [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

| Agent | Dose in AZA intolerance | Complete response |
|---|---|---|
| **MMF** | 1.5–2.0 g/day | 62–92% (pooled 82%, 95% CI 77–87%); discontinuation 0–8% |
| **MP** | 0.5 mg/kg/day → **1–1.5 mg/kg/day** or metabolite-guided | 40% (8/20) |
| **TG** | **20 mg/day** | 66.7% (22/33) |
| **[[tacrolimus\|Tacrolimus]]** | 1 mg twice daily *or* 2 mg once daily; trough **≤5 ng/ml** | 94% (32/34) in one retrospective series — narrow therapeutic window, watch renal function and blood pressure |

AZA is stopped for adverse effects in ~15% in year 1 (36.5% within 6 months in the prospective ERN registry); myelotoxicity and hepatotoxicity each <2%.

**Insufficient response — work the thiopurine metabolites before changing drug** [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]:

| 6-TGN | 6-MMP | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **<223 pmol/8×10⁸ RBC** | low | **Non-adherence or underdose** → check adherence and/or increase AZA **up to 2 mg/kg/day** (*LoE 3, strong*) |
| **<223** | high (shunter) | **Allopurinol 100 mg/day + reduce AZA to 25% of its dose** (*LoE 3, weak*; allopurinol contraindicated in pregnancy) |
| **>223** | high | **Exclude another cause** (Epstein-Barr virus, cytomegalovirus, steroid-related MASLD/MASH, supplement-related DILI) before reducing the AZA dose |
| **>223** | low | No change |

- Therapeutic window **6-TGN 225–450 pmol/8×10⁸ erythrocytes**; "appropriately low" 6-MMP is **<5,700 pmol/0.2 ml**
- ⚠ **This supersedes the AASLD 6-TGN target of 100–300 pmol/8×10⁸ RBCs** — same analyte, different band
- Insufficient response occurs in **22–68% on first-line AZA, 3–28% on first-line MMF**
- **Try MMF before third-line therapy** after AZA optimisation fails (*LoE 2, weak*) — it rescues **32–68%**. Conversely, insufficient response to first-line MMF → **switch to a thiopurine** before escalating
- Repeated relapses on adequate, adhered-to maintenance also count as insufficient response

**Third-line rescue — expert centres only** (*LoE 4, weak*), after screening for latent infection and vaccinating; evaluate every patient for opportunistic infection risk and prophylaxis (*LoE 3, strong*). [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

| Drug | Dose |
|---|---|
| [[tacrolimus\|Tacrolimus]] | 0.1 mg/kg/12 h (or extended-release equivalent); trough **6 ng/ml** |
| Rituximab | 1,000 mg IV at weeks 0 and 2; repeat every 6 months if needed |
| Infliximab ([[anti-tnf-agents\|anti-TNF]]) | 5 mg/kg at weeks 0, 2, 6, 10 → 5 mg/kg every 4–8 weeks |
| Belimumab | 10 mg/kg on days 0, 14, 28, then every 1–2 months |

⚠ **[[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis|AASLD 2020]] treats tacrolimus as a co-equal second-line agent (trough 1–10 ng/mL) alongside MMF.** EASL 2025 moves it to third line behind MMF and AZA optimisation, with a tighter trough. Both agree the second-line agents work far better for *intolerance* than for *treatment failure*.

⚠ **MMF is teratogenic — withdraw ≥3 months before conception** (*LoE 2, strong*), longer than [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis|AASLD 2020]]'s negative pregnancy test within 1 week of starting plus contraception from 4 weeks before to 6 weeks after; **follow the longer EASL interval**. Counselling, contraception and the transplant figures: [[mycophenolate-mofetil#Teratogenicity and Contraception]].

### Relapse and Flares

[[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]] — **relapse** = recurrence after CBR and treatment withdrawal; **flare** = loss of/failure to hold CBR during steroid tapering or maintenance.

- **Trigger threshold: ALT rises to >2–3× ULN and/or IgG above ULN** — the IgG rise usually comes first (precise definitions are lacking)
- Before calling it: recheck for spontaneous normalisation, **assess adherence** (*LoE 1, strong*), and exclude [[alcohol-associated-liver-disease|ALD]], [[drug-induced-liver-injury|DILI]], viral hepatitis, [[nafld-masld|MASLD]], hepatic or [[portal-vein-thrombosis|portal vein thrombosis]] and biliary disease
- **Treat with a short course of corticosteroids plus adjustment of maintenance** (*LoE 2, strong*) — most regain CBR rapidly on the original induction regimen
- **Re-biopsy can be done** to exclude other causes (*LoE 2, weak*); reasonable in [[acute-on-chronic-liver-failure|ACLF]] or genuine diagnostic doubt, not routinely
- Repeated flares/relapses drive progressive fibrosis, LT and liver-related death

### Acute Severe AIH and ALF Management

**Acute severe AIH (INR >1.5 and <2.0, no hepatic encephalopathy [HE]):** [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

- Glucocorticoid **monotherapy**: predniso(lo)ne **0.5–1 mg/kg/day**, or **IV methylprednisolone at an equivalent dose**, in adults; up to 2 mg/kg daily in children. **EASL prefers 0.5 mg/kg/day pragmatically** — most published patients got 1 mg/kg/day IV, but the lower dose balances response against infection risk (*LoE 3, strong*) [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]
- ⚠ **EASL calls the stop at days 3–7, not 1–2 weeks: failure to improve after 3–7 days of steroids should trigger referral to an LT centre** (*LoE 3, strong*). Transplant-free survival with steroids in acute severe AIH without ALF is 52–95.2% [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]
- **With ALF or ACLF → discuss directly with an LT centre** (*LoE 3, strong*); corticosteroid response there is only **8–41%**. If steroids are given anyway, **strict infection surveillance and close efficacy monitoring** (*LoE 2, strong*) [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]
- Do NOT use budesonide (or AZA) here
- **Abandon ineffective treatment quickly — within 1–2 weeks.** Failure of *any* inflammation/function test to improve (especially bilirubin), or any clinical deterioration or new HE, mandates immediate LT consideration
- Balance the benefit against the two harms the guidance names: infection and **delay of LT**
- **The call can be made at day 3, not week 2.** The **SURFASA score** — baseline INR plus the *change* in INR and bilirubin over the first three days of steroids — predicted non-response with AUROC 0.93; below its −0.9 cut-off LT-free survival was 92%, at or above it 23%. Formula, bands and caveats: [[acute-liver-failure#Etiology-Specific Treatments|acute liver failure]]. [[de-martin-2021-surfasa-acute-severe-aih]]
- **Do not judge response by transaminases in the acute severe setting** — response did not correlate with transaminase improvement, unlike in chronic AIH, where normal AST/ALT define remission. [[de-martin-2021-surfasa-acute-severe-aih]]
- **Autoantibodies are often unimpressive in AS-AIH** — ANA ≥1:80 in 61% and ASMA ≥1:80 in 50% of a 128-patient cohort, which is why **histology is the anchor** here. [[de-martin-2021-surfasa-acute-severe-aih]]

**AIH-related ALF (INR ≥2.0 + HE within 26 weeks):**

- HE at presentation is what defines ALF; **LT is more likely to improve survival than protracted glucocorticoid treatment** — evaluate directly, do not delay listing awaiting steroid response
- Glucocorticoids have **not** improved overall survival in AIH-ALF, and survival was **worse** in treated patients with **MELD >40**; steroids may be deleterious in severe decompensation
- See [[acute-liver-failure]] for full management protocol

### Decompensated Cirrhosis

[[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]] — **evaluate for LT** (*LoE 2, strong*), and **consider corticosteroids if there are signs of activity (elevated aminotransferases and/or mHAI ≥4)** (*LoE 4, strong*). Median predniso(lo)ne in the reported series **20–30 mg/day**.

- **[[hepatic-encephalopathy|Hepatic encephalopathy]] grade 3–4 → straight to LT evaluation** — treatment benefit is limited
- **No encephalopathy or grade 1–2 → 60% LT-free survival** on treatment; stratify by **MELD-Na: ≤28 does significantly better than >28**
- **Fall in MELD-Na at 4 weeks** is the strongest survival predictor; even in the favourable group one third died or needed LT, mostly from infection ([[spontaneous-bacterial-peritonitis|spontaneous bacterial peritonitis]], pneumonia)

### Treatment Withdrawal

**EASL 2025 criteria** [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]: most patients need **long-term, often lifelong** immunosuppression (*LoE 2, strong*). Attempt withdrawal only if **low-dose monotherapy has held stable CBR for ≥2 years** (*LoE 2, strong*), with total treatment ≥4 years. Reduce **stepwise** — flares during reduction are frequent (*LoE 3, strong*). **Patient priorities belong in the decision** (*LoE 5, strong*).

- **Favourable predictors:** aminotransferases in the *lower* range of normal (**ALT below half the ULN**), **IgG <12 g/L (<1,200 mg/dl)**, CBR ≥24 months, **CBR attained quickly (e.g. <6 months)**, treatment ≥4 years, no prior relapse, **anti-SLA/LP negative**, **no cirrhosis and no extrahepatic autoimmunity**, and a DI-ALH or virus-associated aetiology
- **Monotherapy first** — patients withdrawn from dual therapy relapsed **twice as often** in the first year
- **Biopsy before withdrawal: no general recommendation either way.** EASL advises it **at least in cirrhosis** (AST and IgG can be normal while histology shows interface activity); if the patient will stop regardless of the result, **biopsy is not justified** — use serial elastography instead
- **Real-world taper:** corticosteroids off over **6–8 weeks to 3–4 months**; the immunomodulator is stopped gradually or in one step
- **After stopping:** aminotransferases + IgG **every 3–4 weeks for 3 months**, then 3-monthly for the first year, **every 3–6 months for the next 3 years**, then annually, indefinitely — relapse can occur decades later (*LoE 3, strong*)
- **After a relapse following first withdrawal, do not try again** (*LoE 2, strong*)
- Transient rises (often <2× ULN) after withdrawal are common — repeat the test and exclude other causes before calling relapse; up to 75% get myalgia/arthralgia for ≤12 months after stopping

**AASLD 2020 framing (older tier-1):** [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

- Sustained biochemical remission: normal AST, ALT, AND IgG for **≥2 years** on IS
- Cirrhosis with chronically elevated IgG does **not** exclude withdrawal if the other tests are normal over ≥2 years of stability
- [[liver-biopsy|Liver biopsy]] preferred (not mandatory in adults) to confirm histologic remission; normal liver tissue cuts subsequent relapse risk to 28%. With vs without pre-withdrawal biopsy, relapse was 30% vs 21% (P = 0.57) — biopsy is still strongly advised in **children**
- Overall, treatment-free remission is achieved in only **19–40%** at ≥3 years' follow-up

**Relapse rates post-withdrawal:** [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

- Adults: 50–87%; children: 60–80%
- In those meeting the strict ≥2-year biochemical-normality criterion: 46% adults, 80% children
- **50% of all relapses occur within 12 months**; frequency falls after the first year, but late relapse is possible — surveillance is lifelong (labs q12 months after the first year)
- Relapse requires prompt reinstitution of the original regimen; **adults should be treated long-term after their first relapse** — repeated relapse after withdrawal raises cirrhosis (38% vs 10%, P = 0.02) and liver-related death/LT (20% vs 3%, P = 0.02) vs relapsing once and being retreated

**Anti-SLA positivity:** Withdrawal rarely successful; do not attempt in anti-SLA–positive patients

### Surveillance in AIH Cirrhosis

- **[[hcc-surveillance|HCC surveillance]]:** liver ultrasound ± alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) **every 6 months** (*LoE 3, strong*) — recommended despite HCC being **less common** in AIH cirrhosis than other aetiologies (cumulative incidence 2.6%, 4.6%, 5.6%, 6.6% at 5/10/15/20 years after cirrhosis; 1.44 cases/1,000 patient-years), which sits **below the 0.5%/year threshold** usually required for cost-effective surveillance — so personalised strategies may be used. Extra risk factors: obesity, age ≥50 y, alcohol ≥25 g/day, AIH/PSC variant. Manage HCC per HCC guidelines (*LoE 2, strong*); **immunotherapy can be considered if the AIH is well controlled** (*LoE 4, weak*) [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]
- **Varices:** [[upper-endoscopy|upper GI endoscopy]] to screen for varices needing treatment if **[[liver-stiffness-measurement|LSM]] ≥20 kPa or platelets ≤150 × 10⁹/L**, and also with **lower LSM but indirect signs of [[portal-hypertension|portal hypertension]]** (splenomegaly, widened portal vein, collaterals) (*LoE 3, strong*) [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

> [!pitfall] Baveno VII elastography cut-offs are not validated in AIH
> Up to **52%** of AIH patients with clear signs of portal hypertension had an LSM **below** the Baveno rule-out cut-off, and thiopurines/MMF lower platelets independently. Judge clinical, laboratory, elastographic and ultrasound signs together rather than relying on the cut-off ([[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]).

### Concomitant Liver Disease

AIH coexists with [[nafld-masld|MASLD]], [[alcohol-associated-liver-disease|ALD]] and viral hepatitis — coexistence does not exclude the diagnosis, and each component is treated. All [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]].

| Coexisting disease | What to do |
|---|---|
| **MASLD** | **Standard AIH treatment** (*LoE 3, strong*), plus a personalised multidisciplinary approach: **lowest effective predniso(lo)ne dose**, lifestyle modification, strict management of every metabolic syndrome component (*LoE 2, strong*). AIH with MASLD more often has cirrhosis at diagnosis and decompensates more |
| **Chronic viral hepatitis with autoimmune features** | **Antiviral therapy** (*LoE 4, strong*); **add immunosuppression only if liver inflammation persists despite adequate viral control** (*LoE 4, strong*) |
| **Alcohol-related liver disease** | Treat the alcohol use disorder, **aiming at abstinence**, per its own guidelines (*LoE 2, strong*); immunosuppress the AIH component per the AIH recommendations above (*LoE 5, strong*) |

- Reactivation risk applies in the other direction too: screen HBsAg and anti-HBc before immunosuppression — strata, agents and duration on [[chronic-hepatitis-b|chronic hepatitis B → HBV Reactivation on Immunosuppression]]

### Overlap / Variant Syndromes

**EASL 2025 framing** [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]: suspect a variant **whenever cholestatic features are present** (*LoE 2, strong*); in adults **test PBC-specific autoantibodies before anything else** (AMA-M2, anti-gp210, anti-sp100) (*LoE 2, strong*), then **magnetic resonance cholangiography if those are negative** (*LoE 3, strong*). **Treat the predominant component** (*LoE 4, strong*).

| Variant | Initial regimen |
|---|---|
| **AIH/PBC, moderate or severe hepatitis** | Standard immunosuppression **+ [[ursodeoxycholic-acid\|UDCA]] 13–15 mg/kg/day** (*LoE 4, weak*) |
| **AIH/PBC, mild hepatitis** | **UDCA monotherapy**, adding immunosuppression if CBR is not reached (*LoE 4, weak*) |
| **AIH/PSC (adults and children)** | Immunosuppression **± UDCA** (*LoE 4, weak*); combination if simplified score >5 and mHAI >3 — predniso(lo)ne **0.5 mg/kg/day tapered to 10–15 mg/day** with UDCA **13–15 mg/kg/day** improved survival and reduced LT vs classic PSC |

- **A biopsy showing moderate and/or severe hepatitis is mandatory whichever diagnostic score is used**
- **Every child with AIH gets magnetic resonance cholangiography at diagnosis**, regardless of cholestatic enzymes, repeated if activity or cholestasis persists (*LoE 2–3, strong*); not needed under age 6 if GGT is normal. Failure to reach CBR is itself a reason to look for sclerosing cholangitis
- Refer to experienced centres / the ERN RARE-LIVER platform

**AIH-PBC overlap (Paris criteria):** [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

Requires **2 of the 3 PBC criteria**, plus — on a background of **interface hepatitis** — **both** AIH criteria:

| | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **PBC (2 of 3)** | ALP ≥2× ULN **or** GGT ≥5× ULN · AMA present · florid bile duct lesions on histology |
| **AIH (both, in addition to interface hepatitis)** | ALT ≥5× ULN · IgG ≥2× ULN **or** SMA present |

*This is the single home for the Paris criteria; [[primary-biliary-cholangitis]] links here rather than restating them.*

> ⚠ **The two AASLD guidelines state Paris differently — know which framing you are using.** [[aasld-2018-pbc]], approaching from an established PBC diagnosis, defines Paris as **"two of the following three"** *AIH* criteria, counting **liver biopsy with moderate or severe interface hepatitis as the third criterion** (a: ALT >5× ULN; b: IgG ≥2× ULN **and/or** SMA+; c: moderate/severe interface hepatitis). [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]], approaching from AIH, instead requires **2 of 3 PBC criteria** *plus* **both** AIH criteria, with interface hepatitis a **prerequisite** rather than a countable item. The practical difference: under the PBC framing a patient with interface hepatitis + IgG ≥2× ULN qualifies **without** ALT >5× ULN; under the AIH framing they do not. **This page follows the newer [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]] framing (table above)**; the 2018 PBC wording is recorded here so readers carrying it are not caught out.

- Paris criteria performance: sensitivity 92%, specificity 97% (single-centre) — but the IAIHG notes the AIH–PBC criteria were never independently validated, and the criteria **miss** patients with less pronounced cholestatic labs, who can still improve on conventional AIH immunosuppression
- Treatment: predniso(lo)ne + AZA directed at the AIH component; see [[primary-biliary-cholangitis]] for the [[ursodeoxycholic-acid|ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA)]] component

**What to do in an AIH patient who looks cholestatic** ([[gerussi-2026-pbc-aih-delphi]], international Delphi consensus; ≥80% agreement threshold). This consensus renames the entity **"PBC–AIH"** and calls it a **variant** rather than an overlap, and ⚠ **its panel did not endorse the Paris criteria** — it holds that the diagnosis rests on laboratory, serological *and* histological findings together (95.1%), not on a fixed rule. The criteria table above is retained as the named historical criteria. Full framing: [[primary-biliary-cholangitis#PBC-AIH Overlap Syndrome]].

- **Suspect a PBC component when AMA, anti-glycoprotein 210 (gp210) or anti-sp100 antibodies are positive alongside a disproportionate ALP elevation** — regardless of when in the course this is found.
- ⚠ **Reassess the original liver biopsy before ordering a new one (87.8%)** in established AIH with disproportionate chronic cholestasis and PBC-related autoantibodies. The point is to avoid an unnecessary procedure.
- **On a biopsy in established AIH, these are supportive but *not required*:** **florid bile duct lesions** (non-suppurative destructive granulomatous cholangitis) and **portal granulomas** — both 81.7%. Bile duct loss, periportal copper or copper-associated protein granules, and keratin 7–positive intermediate periportal hepatocytes reached consensus **among the 14 liver pathologists only**, not the hepatologists.
- **GGT is a poor discriminator here** — confounded by medications, alcohol and steatosis — and **biliary abnormalities also occur in acute AIH**, so judge longitudinally.
- **Re-evaluate the diagnosis over time (85.4%):** features of the two diseases can appear sequentially.

**AIH-PSC overlap:** [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

- Typical AIH features + large-duct PSC on cholangiogram (MRCP/ERCP)
- Consider when AIH diagnosis established but patient has concomitant IBD (especially [[ulcerative-colitis|ulcerative colitis (UC)]]) or ALP disproportionately elevated
- Prognosis driven by PSC component (colitis-associated neoplasia, [[cholangiocarcinoma]] risk)
- See [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis]]

**AIH in the patient who already has IBD:** [[ecco-2024-eim-ibd]] Statement 12

- **Prevalence of AIH in adult patients with IBD is less than 0.5%**, with higher rates in children and adolescents *[EL2]*. In adolescents with IBD the odds ratio for AIH was **8 in UC** and **4 in [[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease]]**.
- **Where AIH and IBD coexist, investigate for additional sclerosing cholangitis with high-quality MRCP** *[EL3]* — **2–7%** of AIH patients carry a concurrent PSC diagnosis and **up to 10%** of adult AIH patients show concurrent features of PSC.
- **For maintenance of remission in people with IBD and AIH, [[thiopurines]] should be considered first-line** *[EL2]* — consistent with the azathioprine-based maintenance above, and convenient where one drug can serve both diseases.
- ⚠ **The AIH diagnostic scoring systems have not been validated in the IBD population**, where autoantibodies are frequently detected in IBD itself and in both AIH and PSC, making them less diagnostically useful.
- The rest of the extraintestinal picture: [[extraintestinal-manifestations-ibd]].

### AIH in Pregnancy

- AIH associated with increased risk of fetal prematurity and loss, especially with inadequate disease control [[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]]
- **Flares: 13% during pregnancy, 41% postpartum at a median of 11 weeks after delivery** — both need treatment intensification, so monitor through pregnancy and the postpartum months. Older estimates ran higher (intrapartum >20%, postpartum 30–50%, [[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]]); **CBR at conception and through pregnancy reduces both flares and prematurity** [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]
- AIH may initially present during pregnancy; differentiate from [[acute-fatty-liver-of-pregnancy|acute fatty liver of pregnancy (AFLP)]], preeclampsia. **First presentation in pregnancy → standard regimens excluding MMF** (*LoE 2, strong*) [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]
- **Cirrhosis in pregnancy → close monitoring by a multidisciplinary obstetric–hepatology team** (*LoE 2, strong*) [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]
- **Continue corticosteroids and/or AZA throughout pregnancy** (American College of Gastroenterology [ACG] 2016 Rec 31 — Strong, Very Low evidence) [[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]]
  - Corticosteroids: pregnancy category C; population-based data (>51,000 exposures) show no significant increase in orofacial cleft or other congenital abnormalities
  - AZA: pregnancy category D; IBD safety data (3,000 AZA-exposed pregnancies) show no increase in congenital abnormalities; maintaining AIH control outweighs AZA risk
- **MMF**: teratogenic — withdraw **≥3 months before conception** and never use it for a first presentation in pregnancy ([[#Intolerance, Insufficient Response and Rescue]])
- Budesonide: not recommended in cirrhosis; limited pregnancy data; prednisone preferred
- Treatment discontinuation carries significant risk of hepatic decompensation/ALF
- Maternal–fetal risk estimates and the full pregnancy pathway: [[liver-disease-in-pregnancy]]

### Paediatric AIH

**Treatment follows the adult guidance with one change — predniso(lo)ne is weaned to a maintenance dose of 2.5–5 mg/day** to avoid corticosteroid side effects including **growth failure** (*LoE 2, strong*). [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

| Line | Drug | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | Predniso(lo)ne | **1–2 mg/kg/day, max 60 mg daily** | Taper to maintenance **2.5–5 mg/day**; paediatric budesonide experience is limited |
| First | [[thiopurines\|Azathioprine]] | **1 mg/kg/day**, titrate up to max **2–2.5 mg/kg/day** | Start **after 2 weeks**, or second-line for insufficient response to predniso(lo)ne. Caution if jaundiced (hepatotoxic); check TPMT first; monitor the 6-MMP/6-TGN ratio |
| Second | [[mycophenolate-mofetil\|MMF]] | Weight-based twice daily — [[mycophenolate-mofetil#Children\|doses on the drug page]] | Replaces azathioprine in refractory AIH; adverse events ≈48%; **teratogenic** |
| Third | [[tacrolimus\|Tacrolimus]] | **0.05 mg/kg/day** in 2 divided doses; trough ≈**5 ng/ml** | Rarely used second-line; adverse events ≈42%; nephrotoxicity and diabetes risk |
| Third | Cyclosporine ([[calcineurin-inhibitors\|calcineurin inhibitor]]) | **4 mg/kg/day** in 2 divided doses; trough **200–250 ng/ml**, decreasing after remission | Occasional induction use; adverse events ≈78%; cosmetic side effects, nephrotoxicity |

- **MMF is the preferred second/third-line agent, then calcineurin inhibitors** (*LoE 3, strong*) — monitor closely, including MMF teratogenicity
- **Use dispersible tablets and syrups** in younger children or developmental delay, to avoid incorrect dosing (*LoE 2, strong*) — children under 6 years often cannot swallow tablets, and 44% of European paediatricians have carers crush tablets instead
- **Vaccination:** give HAV and HBV vaccines if not in the national programme; **live vaccines only cautiously and after discussion with the medical teams** (*LoE 3, strong*). Complete vaccination *before* starting treatment unless the child has ALF — then start once immunosuppression is tapered
- Biochemical remission on predniso(lo)ne ± azathioprine is reported in **75–95% within the first year**
- Screen for **autoimmune thyroid and coeliac disease** at diagnosis, and for **cholangiopathy** — add [[ursodeoxycholic-acid|UDCA]] if AIH/PSC is diagnosed (magnetic resonance cholangiography rule: [[#Overlap / Variant Syndromes]])
- **Anti-LKM1/anti-LC1 AIH is ~10% of paediatric cases** — younger at presentation and more often ALF

**ESPGHAN 2018 paediatric score** — may be used in children (*LoE 3, weak*); it scores the AIH and the AIH/PSC variant in parallel, and **cholangiography is what separates them**. [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

| ANA and/or SMA | ≥1:80 | 2 | 2 |
| Anti-LKM1 | ≥1:10 | 1 | 1 |
| Anti-LKM1 | ≥1:80 | 2 | 1 |

- **≥7 = probable · ≥8 = definite**, for AIH and for AIH/PSC alike
- Autoantibodies are measured by immunofluorescence on composite rodent substrate (kidney, liver, stomach), and the **autoantibody points cap at 2** however many are positive — as in the adult simplified score
- The **2022 IAHPG criteria may be substituted for the histology line**, expected to raise sensitivity in children too (acute onset, predominantly lobular inflammation)
- The IgG thresholds matter here because **IgG is physiologically lower before age 10**

**Withdrawal in children** — ESPGHAN extends treatment to **at least 3 years** and considers withdrawal only if aminotransferases and IgG are persistently normal and autoantibodies negative **for at least a year**, with **liver biopsy before withdrawal strongly advised**. A long-term cohort withdrew 53% successfully without biopsy under strict surveillance — **deep-normal aminotransferases and a prothrombin ratio ≥70%** were the best predictors — but ESPGHAN has since reaffirmed the biopsy. A **6-TGN:6-MMP ratio <4** may optimise remission with allopurinol. [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

**Transition to adult care** [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]:

- **Young people aged 16–25 years should receive specialised care** with multidisciplinary team support (*LoE 2, strong*)
- **Transition must not depend on age alone** — paediatric and adult services collaborate, with parents/carers included (*LoE 2, strong*)
- Assess **mental health and health-related quality of life** routinely in all patients, and signpost to other services as needed (*LoE 2, strong*); because corticosteroids hit quality of life and adherence hardest, **consider dose reduction or withdrawal when appropriate** (*LoE 2, strong*)

### Post-Liver Transplant AIH

**Outcomes after LT for AIH** ([[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]): recipient survival **79% / 71% / 60%** and graft survival **73% / 63% / 60%** at 5 / 10 / 15 years; fatal infection and rejection are more frequent than after LT for other indications, and recurrence is a major cause of graft failure. AIH recipients also have more acute and chronic rejection (late acute rejection 9%; chronic rejection 14–17% vs 2% in alcohol-associated cirrhosis) [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

**Preventing recurrence:** **low-dose predniso(lo)ne plus a [[calcineurin-inhibitors|calcineurin inhibitor]] (mainly [[tacrolimus|tacrolimus]]) can be used as maintenance immunosuppression** (*LoE 4, weak*) — low immunosuppression levels and stopping corticosteroids after LT both raise recurrence, so weigh the recurrence benefit against long-term corticosteroid harm. Low-dose MMF on a tacrolimus backbone is a proposed alternative but is not validated. Gradual glucocorticoid withdrawal per centre protocol is the AASLD position (*Conditional, Very Low*). [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]] [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

**Recurrent AIH:** [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

- **Rate 17–42%**, varying with study size and whether biopsies were per-protocol or clinically indicated; the IAIHG multicentre cohort gives **20% at 5 years and 31% at 10 years**
  - ⚠ **[[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis|AASLD 2020]] quotes 8–12% within the first year and 36–68% after 5 years** — this page carries the newer EASL figures
- **Risk factors:** age at LT **≤42 years**, **[[mycophenolate-mofetil|MMF]] during the first year after LT** (longer use is not a risk factor), donor/recipient mismatch, donor/recipient cytomegalovirus status mismatch, high pre-LT IgG
- **Diagnostic criteria are the same as in non-transplanted patients, but serology is unreliable after LT — base the diagnosis mainly on histology:** interface hepatitis with lymphocytic infiltrate ± plasma cells, and lobular hepatitis ± central vein endotheliitis, after excluding other causes of graft dysfunction
- **Treat with predniso(lo)ne at the doses recommended for non-transplant AIH** (*LoE 4, strong*) — start it or increase the existing dose. AASLD's empiric schedule: predniso(lo)ne **30 mg daily** + AZA 1–2 mg/kg daily, reducing predniso(lo)ne to 5–10 mg daily over 4–8 weeks, then predniso(lo)ne + AZA maintenance; continue the calcineurin inhibitor [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]]
- Outcomes: 5-y patient survival 86–100%; graft failure 8–50%; retransplantation 33–60%; AIH recurs in the retransplanted liver in 33–100% [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

**Plasma cell-rich rejection hepatitis** — the Banff Working Group's 2016 name for what was called **"de novo AIH"**: AIH-like graft dysfunction in a patient transplanted for a liver disease **other than** AIH. [[easl-2025-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

- **Consider it in late graft dysfunction** with liver enzyme abnormalities and AIH-like histology, **with or without IgG elevation and/or autoantibodies** (*LoE 3, strong*) — those serological features are not required for the diagnosis, which is what distinguishes it from the original paediatric "de novo AIH" descriptions
- Occurs in **5–10% of children and 1–2% of adults** transplanted for other aetiologies, usually **>6 months after LT**
  - ⚠ **AASLD 2020 gives 1–7% of paediatric and 1–3% of adult recipients** (≈4 cases per 1,000 patient-years); EASL's figures are used here
- **Banff histological criteria — at least one of:**
  1. Portal and/or perivenular **plasma cell-rich (>30%) infiltrates** with interface hepatitis and/or perivenular necroinflammatory activity involving the **majority** of portal tracts and/or terminal hepatic veins
  2. **Lymphocytic cholangitis**
- Risk factors: **female graft**, **older donors**, donor/recipient **glutathione-S-transferase T1 mismatch**
- **Treat with predniso(lo)ne at classic AIH doses** plus adjustment of baseline immunosuppression, which may include adding [[thiopurines|azathioprine]] (*LoE 4, strong*). In children: predniso(lo)ne 1–2 mg/kg (<60 mg daily) + AZA 1–2 mg/kg daily — biochemical remission 86%, retransplantation 8%, patient survival 95% [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]]

See [[liver-transplantation]] for broader post-LT context.

### Long-Term Outcomes

- Treated AIH in a **non-transplant** center: 10-year survival 91%, 20-year survival 70% [[aasld-2020-autoimmune-hepatitis]]
- Standardized mortality ratio **1.63** for all-cause death (95% CI 1.25–2.02); **1.86** when LT is counted as death
- 5% of treated patients develop extrahepatic malignancy (standardized incidence ratio [SIR] 2.7, 95% CI 1.8–3.9) — nonmelanoma skin cancer most common; follow standard extrahepatic cancer screening
- Main causes of morbidity: cirrhosis complications, glucocorticoid side effects, post-transplant diabetes mellitus (PTDM), osteoporosis, opportunistic infection
