# Alcohol-Associated Hepatitis

Acute syndrome of jaundice and liver failure arising on a background of heavy alcohol use; the severe form is a distinct clinical entity from chronic [[alcohol-associated-liver-disease|ALD]] and from [[cirrhosis]].

*This page carries the AH-specific decision points. The full ALD spectrum, the score-comparison table (MDF / MELD / ABIC / GAHS / Lille variables and cutoffs), the ACG 2024 management figure, alcohol-use-disorder treatment, and post-transplant management live on [[alcohol-associated-liver-disease]].*

## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

**Clinical diagnosis — no biopsy required when the criteria are met and there is no confounder** ([[aasld-2020-ald]] consensus definition; criteria table on [[alcohol-associated-liver-disease]]). The operative elements:

- **Jaundice onset within the prior 8 weeks**, on ≥6 months of heavy use (>40 g/day female, >60 g/day male) with **<60 days abstinence** before jaundice.
- **AST >50 IU/L**, AST and ALT usually **<400 IU/L**, **AST:ALT >1.5**, **total bilirubin >3.0 mg/dL**.

### Severity Assessment

- **Severe AH = MELD >20** — the operative threshold for corticosteroid treatment ([[acg-2024-ald]] Rec 17, *strong, moderate*). Computed on the **original MELD**, not MELD-Na.
- **Moderate AH = MELD ≤20** — substantial morbidity and mortality, but no steroid indication ([[acg-2024-ald]] Key concept 21).
- **MDF ≥32** is the historical threshold and remains the entry criterion of most AH trials; it was **inferior** to MELD/GAHS/ABIC in the STOPAH post-hoc analysis, and ACG 2024 recommends on MELD instead. Readers carrying "MDF ≥32" should switch to MELD >20.
- **MELD 25–39 derives maximum benefit** from corticosteroids; **weigh risks carefully if MELD >50** ([[acg-2024-ald]] Key concept 26).
- **Lille ≥0.45 at day 7 (or day 4) = non-response** → stop steroids; **<0.45 = response** → continue to 28 days total.
- The CTP and MELD point tables themselves live on [[cirrhosis]]; the variables and cutoffs of each AH score are compared on [[alcohol-associated-liver-disease]].

### Classification / Typing

Three diagnostic certainty categories ([[aasld-2020-ald]]) — they determine whether [[liver-biopsy|biopsy]] is needed, not the treatment:

| Category | Definition | Biopsy |
|---|---|---|
| **Definite AH** | Clinical criteria met **and** biopsy-proven | Done |
| **Probable AH** | Clinical criteria met, **no** confounding factors | Not required |
| **Possible AH** | Clinical criteria met **with** confounding factors | Required to confirm |

*Confounders that trigger biopsy:* possible ischemic hepatitis (severe [[upper-gi-bleeding|UGIB]], hypotension, or cocaine within 7 days); possible [[drug-induced-liver-injury|DILI]] (suspect drug within 30 days of jaundice); uncertain alcohol history; atypical labs (AST <50 or >400, AST/ALT <1.5, ANA >1:160 or SMA >1:80).

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[abnormal-liver-chemistries]].*

- [[nafld-masld|MASLD/MASH]] — identical histology; separated by the alcohol history (overlap exists).
- [[drug-induced-liver-injury|Drug-induced liver injury]] — medication history essential; can mimic AH clinically.
- Viral hepatitis — [[chronic-hepatitis-b|HBV]], [[hepatitis-c|HCV]], HEV.
- [[autoimmune-hepatitis|Autoimmune hepatitis]] — elevated IgG, autoantibodies.
- Ischemic hepatitis — AST >1000, cardiac/hypotensive history.
- Acute biliary obstruction ([[choledocholithiasis|CBD stone]]) — cholestatic pattern; excluded on ultrasound.
- [[acute-on-chronic-liver-failure|ACLF]] — AH is a common precipitant; the two overlap rather than exclude each other.

## Diagnostics

- **Labs:** bilirubin, AST/ALT, INR/PT, creatinine, albumin, WBC — these are both the diagnostic criteria and the inputs to every severity score above.
- **Abdominal ultrasound** — exclude biliary obstruction and assess for [[cirrhosis]]/[[portal-hypertension|portal hypertension]].
- **Infection screen before steroids** — chest radiograph plus blood, urine, and ascitic-fluid cultures ([[aasld-2020-ald]]).
- **[[liver-biopsy|Liver biopsy]]** (transjugular when coagulopathic) — only for *possible* AH, i.e. when a confounder is present. Biopsy cannot distinguish alcohol-associated from metabolic steatohepatitis.
- Further laboratory and non-invasive fibrosis detail: [[alcohol-associated-liver-disease]].

## Therapeutics

**Full regimens, contraindications, evidence, and the early-transplant selection criteria are on [[alcohol-associated-liver-disease]].** The decisions:

| Step | Rule |
|---|---|
| Severe AH (MELD >20) | **Prednisolone 40 mg/day PO × 28 days** ([[acg-2024-ald]] Rec 17; [[aasld-2020-ald]] GS-14) |
| Adjunct | **IV N-acetylcysteine** added to prednisolone ([[acg-2024-ald]] Rec 20, *strong, moderate*) |
| Day 4 or 7 | **Lille ≥0.45 → stop prednisolone**; <0.45 → complete 28 days |
| Non-responder | Consider **early [[liver-transplantation\|liver transplantation]]**; ≥4 organ failures + steroid non-response + ineligible for early LT → **palliative care** ([[acg-2024-ald]] Key concept 28) |
| Moderate AH (MELD ≤20) | No steroid indication — abstinence, nutrition, complication management |
| All patients | Lifelong **abstinence** and alcohol-use-disorder treatment; **nutrition support** (see [[nutrition-in-liver-disease]]) |

- **Pentoxifylline is abandoned** — not recommended in severe AH ([[aasld-2020-ald]]).
- **Do not give prophylactic antibiotics by default** — antibiotics added to prednisolone lowered infections but not 60-day mortality; treat documented infection ([[acg-2024-ald]]).
- Screen for and manage [[spontaneous-bacterial-peritonitis|SBP]], [[aki-in-cirrhosis|AKI]], [[hepatic-encephalopathy]], and [[variceal-upper-gi-bleeding|variceal bleeding]] as in decompensated cirrhosis.
