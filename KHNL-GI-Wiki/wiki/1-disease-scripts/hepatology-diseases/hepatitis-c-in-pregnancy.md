## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Universal screening — every pregnancy:** anti-HCV antibody with **reflex HCV RNA** if positive. Detectable RNA = active infection; undetectable = spontaneous clearance ([[aasld-idsa-2023-hcv]]).
  - Multi-society alignment: US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF — universal screening, adults 18–79 y), Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG), Society for Maternal-Fetal Medicine (SMFM). American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD) 2021 guidance ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]) and the American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2024 Clinical Practice Update (CPU) ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]]) concur.
  - **CDC qualifier:** screen in every pregnancy **except where HCV prevalence is <0.1%** ([[aasld-idsa-2023-hcv]]).
- *Change from prior guidance:* the 2016 American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) guideline recommended **risk-based** screening only and explicitly advised against screening women without risk factors (Rec 26 — strong, low) ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]]). Universal screening is the current standard.

### Severity Assessment

*Management is driven by **vertical-transmission risk**, not by maternal disease activity — HCV rarely requires urgent therapy and treatment can generally wait until after delivery. Maternal fibrosis staging uses the general cutoffs on [[hepatitis-c]].*

- **Pregnancy does not alter the course of HCV, and no HCV-specific monitoring is needed during pregnancy or postpartum** ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]). Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) declines in the 2nd/3rd trimester and rebounds postpartum; viral load peaks in the 3rd trimester then returns to pre-pregnancy levels after delivery. Unlike hepatitis B, postpartum HCV flares have not been described ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]]).
- Anti-HCV positivity carries **1.6-fold higher odds of preterm birth** (meta-analysis, 5,218 women) — confounding by substance use, comorbidity and socioeconomic status is unmeasured ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]).
- **When transmission happens:** intrapartum events account for up to 40% of cases; the **peripartum period is the highest-risk window** ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]).

| Factor | Effect on vertical transmission |
|---|---|
| Maternal HCV viremia (baseline risk) | **5.8%** (95% confidence interval [CI] 4.2–7.8) |
| HCV + human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) co-infection | **10.8%** (95% CI 7.6–15.2) |
| Level of viremia | association in some studies; **no threshold identified** |
| Rupture of membranes >6 h | adjusted odds ratio (OR) **9.3** (95% CI 1.5–180) |
| Mode of delivery (cesarean vs vaginal) | no influence |
| Breastfeeding | no association (14 cohort studies, 2,971 participants) |

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[liver-disease-in-pregnancy]].*

- Pregnancy-specific liver diseases — distinguish from an HCV flare:
  - [[intrahepatic-cholestasis-of-pregnancy|intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy]] (IHCP) — markedly more frequent with HCV: pooled OR **20.4** (95% CI 9.4–44.3) ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]])
  - [[acute-fatty-liver-of-pregnancy|acute fatty liver of pregnancy]] (AFLP)
  - HELLP (hemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, low platelets) syndrome
- [[drug-induced-liver-injury]] — medication history
- [[autoimmune-hepatitis]] — postpartum flare can mimic HCV
- [[chronic-hepatitis-b]] — test hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) for co-infection

## Diagnostics

- Anti-HCV antibody — universal screen each pregnancy; **reflex HCV RNA** if positive
- HCV RNA — confirms active infection
- Fibrosis assessment ([[noninvasive-liver-disease-assessment|Fibrosis-4 index (FIB-4)]]; [[liver-stiffness-measurement|elastography]] as needed) — cutoffs on [[hepatitis-c]]
- Serial viral loads and serial ALT are not indicated — no monitoring is HCV-specific in pregnancy ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]])

## Therapeutics

### Antiviral Therapy

- **Cure before conception when possible** — preconception antiviral therapy eliminates the risk of vertical transmission and is the priority for people of childbearing age with HCV ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]).
- **[[direct-acting-antivirals|Direct-acting antiviral (DAA)]] therapy is not approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in pregnancy.** AASLD-IDSA (Infectious Diseases Society of America) 2023 supports **case-by-case DAA use during pregnancy** after a discussion of potential risks and benefits — smaller studies and case series have shown no safety concerns, but there are no published large-scale clinical trials ([[aasld-idsa-2023-hcv]]; AGA 2024 CPU concurs and notes results of ongoing pregnancy treatment trials are awaited, [[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]]).
  - Pregnancy **excludes a patient from the simplified AASLD-IDSA treatment algorithm** — treatment in pregnancy is specialist-managed ([[aasld-idsa-2023-hcv]]). Regimen selection and 12-week sustained virological response (SVR12) assessment: [[hepatitis-c]].
  - *Older stance:* ACG 2016 recommended HCV therapy **not** be offered in pregnancy, either to treat the mother or to reduce transmission (Rec 30 — strong, very low) ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]]); AASLD 2021 judged DAA safety data insufficient to recommend their use in pregnancy as a means of reducing transmission ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]). The case-by-case option is the shift.
- **Conception while already on a DAA** — the decision to continue weighs risk of virologic relapse, risk of vertical transmission, access and cost, patient and clinician preference, and the limited pregnancy safety data ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]).
- **Interferon and ribavirin are strictly contraindicated** ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]]). Ribavirin is embryocidal and teratogenic: **both women and men** on a ribavirin-containing regimen require highly effective contraception and must **defer conception ≥6 months after the last ribavirin dose** ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]).
- **Default is postpartum treatment**, after pregnancy and breastfeeding are complete; link a newly diagnosed patient with a clinician who will set that timing ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]).

> [!pitfall] Recheck HCV RNA before treating postpartum
> Up to **25%** of women clear HCV spontaneously postpartum, usually within 12 months — confirm HCV RNA status before starting antiviral therapy ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]).

### Delivery and Peripartum Care

- **Mode of delivery does not change transmission risk** — elective cesarean section should not be performed to prevent infection of the fetus; vaginal delivery is preferred ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]] Rec 28 — strong, very low; [[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]).
- **Avoid, to reduce transmission** ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]; [[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]] Rec 27 — strong, very low):
  - prolonged rupture of membranes
  - invasive fetal monitoring
  - episiotomy
  - amniocentesis and other invasive prenatal procedures — counsel on the possible transmission risk; where invasive testing is unavoidable, **amniocentesis avoiding placental contact** is favored over chorionic villus sampling and fetal blood sampling
- These measures target maternal–fetal blood contact and lack empiric evidence; no perinatal intervention reliably prevents transmission ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]], [[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]]).

### Breastfeeding

- **Not contraindicated** ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]] Rec 29 — strong, very low; [[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]). HCV is detectable in breast milk in 0–20% of viremic women, at low titers.
- **Hold while nipples are cracked or bleeding, or skin is broken down** ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]], [[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]).
- **Not recommended while on antiviral therapy** ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]).

### Infant Follow-Up

- Test the child for **anti-HCV antibody after 18 months of age** ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]).
- Antibody positive → **HCV RNA** to confirm viremia → if viremic, refer to a pediatrician with expertise in HCV care.
- Until the infant's status is known, educate parents about HCV and the precautions that prevent transmission to others.

> [!pitfall] Don't test the infant too early
> Passively transferred maternal anti-HCV can persist to 18 months of age. In a series of 29 infants born to viremic mothers who were anti-HCV-positive at birth, 10% were still antibody-positive at 12–18 months but only 5% remained HCV-RNA-positive beyond 12 months ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]).
