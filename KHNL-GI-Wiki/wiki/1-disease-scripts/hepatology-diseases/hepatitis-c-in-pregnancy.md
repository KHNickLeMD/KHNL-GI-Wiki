## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

- **Universal screening — every pregnancy** with anti-HCV antibody + **reflex HCV RNA** if positive ([[aasld-idsa-2023-hcv]]; multi-society consensus — US Preventive Services Task Force [USPSTF], Centers for Disease Control and Prevention [CDC], American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists [ACOG], Society for Maternal-Fetal Medicine [SMFM]; American Gastroenterological Association [AGA] 2024 Clinical Practice Update [CPU] concurs the CDC advises HCV screening at each pregnancy, [[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]]). HCV RNA detectable = active infection.
  - *Change from prior guidance:* the 2016 American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) guideline recommended **risk-based** screening only (Rec 26 — strong, low) and explicitly did not endorse universal screening ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]]); the newer 2023 American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases–Infectious Diseases Society of America (AASLD-IDSA) guidance and USPSTF/CDC now recommend **universal** screening in every pregnancy — the current standard.
- HCV RNA quantification confirms active infection and gives baseline viral load; genotype for postpartum treatment planning.

### Severity Assessment

*What drives management here is **vertical-transmission risk and disease activity**, stratified below. Maternal fibrosis staging uses the general cutoffs on [[hepatitis-c]].*

- **Vertical (mother-to-child) transmission: 3–10%** at birth; higher with **human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) co-infection** and with **high maternal HCV RNA** ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]]).
- No perinatal intervention has been shown to reliably reduce HCV vertical transmission.
- Pregnancy induces relative immune suppression then postpartum reconstitution: alanine aminotransferase (ALT) declines in 2nd/3rd trimester and rebounds postpartum; viral load fluctuates (peaks 3rd trimester, returns to baseline after delivery).
- HCV rarely requires urgent therapy — treatment can generally wait until after delivery.

## Differential Diagnosis

*Workup: see [[liver-disease-in-pregnancy]].*

- Pregnancy-specific liver diseases — [[intrahepatic-cholestasis-of-pregnancy|intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy]] (IHCP), [[acute-fatty-liver-of-pregnancy|acute fatty liver of pregnancy]] (AFLP), HELLP (hemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, low platelets) syndrome — distinguish from an HCV flare
- [[drug-induced-liver-injury]] — medication history
- [[autoimmune-hepatitis]] — postpartum flare can mimic HCV
- [[chronic-hepatitis-b]] — test hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) (co-infection; also relevant to reactivation)

## Diagnostics

- Anti-HCV antibody — universal screen each pregnancy; **reflex HCV RNA** if positive
- HCV RNA — confirm active infection; baseline viral load
- HCV genotype — for postpartum treatment planning
- Fibrosis assessment ([[noninvasive-liver-disease-assessment|Fibrosis-4 index (FIB-4)]]; [[liver-stiffness-measurement|elastography]] as needed) — see [[hepatitis-c]] for cutoffs
- **Minimize invasive procedures** (amniocentesis, invasive fetal monitoring) — increases vertical-transmission risk ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]] Rec 27)

## Therapeutics

- **[[direct-acting-antivirals|Direct-acting antiviral (DAA)]] therapy is not FDA-approved in pregnancy.** The 2023 AASLD-IDSA guidance supports **case-by-case DAA consideration during pregnancy** after a shared risk/benefit discussion — small studies and case series are reassuring for safety, but no large-scale randomized controlled trials (RCTs) exist ([[aasld-idsa-2023-hcv]]; AGA 2024 CPU concurs IDSA/AASLD support case-by-case DAA use, [[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]]). Results of ongoing HCV-in-pregnancy treatment trials are awaited. In practice most patients are treated **postpartum**.
  - *Older stance:* 2016 ACG recommended **no HCV therapy during pregnancy** (Rec 30 — strong, very low); interferon and **ribavirin are strictly contraindicated** (ribavirin is a teratogen — [[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]], [[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]]). The interferon/ribavirin contraindication still stands; the shift is the newer case-by-case DAA option.
- **Delivery:** elective C-section does **not** prevent HCV vertical transmission — vaginal delivery preferred ([[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]] Rec 28).
- **Breastfeeding permitted** unless nipples are cracked/bleeding (Rec 29).
- **Co-manage with maternal-fetal medicine**; counsel on effective contraception and defer/plan definitive DAA cure ([[aasld-2021-reproductive-liver]]).
- Treat postpartum with a [[direct-acting-antivirals|DAA]]-based regimen — see [[hepatitis-c]] for regimen selection and 12-week sustained virological response (SVR12) assessment.

> **Testing the exposed infant** (which test, at what age, how to distinguish passively transferred maternal antibody from true infection) is not covered by the cited guidelines: AASLD-IDSA 2023 mentions perinatal HCV only in its reference list, and [[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]] stops at "screen each pregnancy." Follow the CDC/American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) perinatal HCV testing recommendations.
