## Overview

- Enteral stoma = colostomy or ileostomy; ~750,000 Americans live with an ostomy; ~130,000 new ostomy surgeries/year (US).
- Adequate stomal care improves outcomes and reduces hospitalizations; this page covers care **beyond the immediate perioperative period** (a recognized gap area).
- Indications span [[colorectal-cancer|CRC]], [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|IBD]] ([[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease]], [[ulcerative-colitis]]), perforated [[diverticulitis|diverticulitis]], trauma, colonic dysmotility, and fecal diversion (for [[fecal-incontinence|fecal incontinence]], sacral wound, spinal cord injury).
- Stoma must sit **above skin level** so effluent doesn't seep under the appliance (Brooke technique = suturing to protrude above skin).

## Ostomy Types & Output Expectations

| Type | Construction | Output | Appliance change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Colostomy** (most common) | Sigmoid, descending, or transverse colon; right colon avoided (large diameter, liquid effluent → leak-prone) | **Formed** stool; BM commonly **once daily** | q**6–7 days** |
| **Ileostomy** | Terminal ileum near ileocecal valve (maximizes nutrient absorption); easier to construct/reverse than colostomy but **more dehydration + skin excoriation** | **Liquid** effluent; empty **3–4×/day** | wafer q**4 days** avg |

**Configurations:**

- **End ostomy** — intestine divided, proximal end brought out; easiest to pouch; used for permanent stomas and perforation requiring resection. Reversal more invasive (locate stapled-off distal end).
  - **Mucus fistula** — distal end also brought out through separate incision; generally avoided (2 stomas/wall defects) unless significant leak risk in stapled-off end (distal obstruction, poor tissue integrity).
- **Loop ostomy** — continuous bowel loop through one aperture, 2 side-by-side openings; proximal end Brooked/dominant. For distal obstruction (e.g. obstructing cancer) and temporary diversion (protect distal anastomosis, heal sacral decubitus). Easy to create and reverse.
- **Continent ileostomy** (Kock pouch / Barnett Continent Intestinal Reservoir) — internal pleated-intestine pouch with nipple valve; no stool passage until intubated with catheter; usually no appliance. Uncommon — high complication rate → revision surgery and intestinal loss.

## Normal vs High Ostomy Output (HOO)

- **Normal:** colostomy formed stool ~once daily; ileostomy liquid effluent emptied 3–4×/day.
- **HOO = output greater than fluid intake, typically >1.5 L/d** → dehydration. Common with ileostomy; rare with colostomy.
- **Early HOO** = HOO within **3 weeks** of stoma formation.
- **Chronic (late) HOO** = persistent HOO **>3 weeks** after surgery, OR new-onset HOO **>3 weeks** after creation.
- HOO with a colostomy, or unusually high with an ileostomy → prompt evaluation for infection (postoperative abdominal infection, [[clostridioides-difficile|C. difficile]]), ileus, or medication adverse effects.

## High-Output Management

**Early HOO:**

- **Most important treatment: hydration to prevent renal failure.** Repletion volumes are large → typically **IV**, requiring hospital admission or long-term IV access for home health care.
- Persistent early HOO → consider **early stoma reversal**, but reversal **before 6 weeks** from index surgery carries increased complication risk.

**Chronic (late) HOO** — more likely with jejunostomy, short bowel (**<200 cm small intestine remaining**), or failed post-surgical adaptation. Ileal adaptation begins over days–weeks (mucosal changes altering electrolyte transport, absorptive capacity, motility). Etiologies: infection, structural (stomal stricture/dysfunction, obstruction, ileus, enteric fistula), recurrent [[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease]], and common adult diarrhea causes ([[microscopic-colitis|microscopic colitis]], [[bile-acid-diarrhea|bile acid diarrhea]]).

**Treatment ladder (Table 1):**

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Bulking agents | Psyllium fiber; guar gum; marshmallows |
| Antimotility agents | [[loperamide\|Loperamide]]; diphenoxylate + atropine; codeine; tincture of opium |
| Antisecretory agents | [[proton-pump-inhibitors\|PPIs]] / H2 agonists; [[somatostatin-analogs\|somatostatin analogues]] (octreotide) |
| Anti-inflammatory (if recurrent Crohn's) | Consultation with IBD specialist |
| Adaptation-promoting | [[glp-2-agonists\|GLP-2 analogues]] (teduglutide, elsiglutide, glepaglutide, apraglutide) |
| Surgical | Reversal of the ostomy with restoration of intestinal continuity when possible |

## Short-Term Complications

- **Ostomy leakage** — most common and dreaded. Predisposing: [[obesity|obesity]], placement in a skin crease, loop configuration, liquid effluent (ileostomy, [[chronic-diarrhea|chronic diarrhea]]), flush stoma.
  - Prevention is best (preoperative stomatherapist marking, meticulous technique).
  - Management: thicken stool with antidiarrheals; bolster stoma height (convex appliance, ostomy belt, paste, barrier rings); heat appliance with hair dryer before application; lie flat several minutes after application; dry peristomal skin; stomal powder + skin sealant.
  - Appliance opening cut **one-eighth inch larger than the stoma**; **re-measure stoma at each change for the first 8 weeks** (stoma shrinks). Refractory leakage may need revision.
- **Stomal retraction** — with ischemia or thick abdominal wall (obesity). Early retraction below abdominal wall → sepsis, emergent revision. Retraction within skin but above fascia → pouching issues/stricture (endoscopy or cross-sectional imaging delineates depth). Temporary stoma: manage symptomatically (convex appliance, belt, barrier ring) until reversal; permanent often needs revision.
- **Mucocutaneous separation** — maturation sutures detach; manage with pouching solutions until healing by secondary intention; immunosuppressed patients at particular risk.

## Peristomal Skin & Long-Term Complications

**Peristomal dermatoses** (most issues from appliance leakage — manage per leakage above):

- **Appliance allergy** — itching/redness in the shape of the offending product → skin sealant, pouching-system change, temporary steroid spray.
- **Fungal infection** (moisture/heat) — itchy maculopapular rash with satellite borders → antifungal powder sealed with sealant; no improvement at 2 weeks → surgeon/enterostomal therapist.
- **Peristomal pyoderma gangrenosum** — painful ulcers with purple halo (esp. IBD) → interdisciplinary GI/dermatology/wound-care management.
- **Peristomal [[variceal-upper-gi-bleeding|varices]]** — minor bleeding at appliance changes is normal; persistent/severe bleeding or purplish concentric peristomal discoloration → work up.

**Parastomal hernia** — up to **50% of ostomates within 5 years**.

- Risk factors: obesity, smoking, steroid use, transverse colostomy.
- Prevention: place stoma **through the rectus**; avoid transverse colon when possible.
- **Incarcerated hernia** (painful, irreducible → obstruction/ischemia) = **surgical emergency**.
- Small reducible → hernia belt. Elective repair reserved for significant pouching issues, pain, or recurrent obstruction.
- Most effective repair = **ostomy reversal**; otherwise minimally invasive repair usually with mesh; recurrence after repair **>25% at 2 years**. Stoma relocation has fallen out of favor (high hernia risk at new site).

**Stomal prolapse** (elongation of the stoma's intestinal portion) — rate **5%–10%**.

- Acute prolapse with incarceration/ischemia (pain, obstipation, purple/black stoma) = **emergency surgery**.
- If no ischemia: reduce with patient relaxed, gentle pressure; if irreducible, **table sugar applied to the stoma ×20 minutes** reduces edema and facilitates reduction.
- Mild, easily reducible, non-interfering → no surgery; otherwise revision or reversal.

## Perioperative & WOC Care

- Address psychological impact (fear of leakage, odor, disclosure, clothing, intimacy, travel, self-care) with pre- and postoperative education.
- **Preoperative education + stoma site marking** improves quality of life and decreases peristomal skin and pouching complications.
- WOC (wound ostomy continence) marking assesses the abdomen in multiple positions for a site within the rectus, away from scars, skin folds, bony prominences, and the umbilicus.
- WOC consultation manages expectations (output, appliances, regular mucus passage from the native rectum); community-based and online ostomy support groups exist.
