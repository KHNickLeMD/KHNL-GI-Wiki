Framework for two linked decisions: **(1)** how much cancer risk [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]] inflammation and IBD drugs confer, and **(2)** what to do with IBD medications when a patient develops an active cancer or has a prior-cancer history. Per the American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2024 Clinical Practice Update (CPU), the greatest concern is **[[thiopurines]]** and **[[anti-tnf-agents|anti–tumor necrosis factor (TNF)]]** agents. Age-appropriate cancer screening applies to IBD patients as in the general population — schedule on [[ibd-preventive-care]] ([[aga-2024-ibd-malignancy]]).

## Cancer Risk From IBD Inflammation

| Cancer | Magnitude | Key risk factors |
|---|---|---|
| **[[colorectal-cancer\|Colorectal cancer (CRC)]]** (colitis-associated) | ~1% / 2% / 5% at 10 / 20 / >20 y from IBD diagnosis (older estimate 15% after 30 y) | Duration, colonic extent, family history of CRC, concomitant [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis\|primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)]] (>3-fold odds of CRC/dysplasia) |
| Small-bowel adenocarcinoma ([[crohns-disease\|Crohn's disease (CD)]]) | Adjusted hazard ratio (aHR) 15.8; absolute risk low (2.4/10,000 person-years [PY] in CD; 4.6 in ileal CD; 0.28 in reference individuals) | Tobacco, male sex, childhood-onset CD, long-standing small-bowel inflammation, penetrating/stricturing CD, prior small-bowel resection |
| Small-bowel [[gastroenteropancreatic-neuroendocrine-tumors\|neuroendocrine tumor]] | aHR 5.5 | Small-bowel CD inflammation |
| Intestinal lymphoma | 2–3× (1–4/10,000 PY in CD vs 0.7 in reference individuals) | Male sex, ongoing intestinal inflammation, CD >8 y; mostly B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL), often Epstein-Barr virus (EBV)-associated |
| Anal squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) — screening on [[anal-cancer-screening]] | 2.6 vs 0.8/10,000 PY (IBD with anal/perianal lesions vs IBD without anal/perianal fistula) | Human papillomavirus (HPV); long-standing [[perianal-fistulizing-crohns-disease\|perianal fistulizing disease]] (SCC + adenocarcinoma 3.8/10,000 PY) |
| [[cholangiocarcinoma\|Cholangiocarcinoma]] | 4× (7.6 vs 1.9/100,000 PY) | Mainly concomitant PSC (>150-fold; 5–10% lifetime risk) |
| Nonmelanoma skin cancer (NMSC) / hematologic / lung | CD incidence rate ratio (IRR) 2.22 / 2.40 / 1.53; ulcerative colitis (UC) NMSC IRR 1.38 | — |

- Chronic colonic inflammation → multifocal dysplasia (field cancerization): in a UC cohort with CRC, >1/3 had synchronous CRC or dysplasia elsewhere at colectomy → emphasize endoscopic mucosal healing and close dysplasia surveillance ([[aga-2024-ibd-malignancy]]).
- Dysplasia surveillance (start, chromoendoscopy, 1–5 y intervals): [[ulcerative-colitis]] ([[aga-2021-ibd-colorectal-dysplasia]]); activity indices on [[ibd-endoscopic-scoring]].

## Cancer Risk From IBD Therapies

| Class | Signal |
|---|---|
| **Thiopurines** | ~2× **NMSC**; ongoing use may ↑ recurrent NMSC; risk persists (to a lesser extent) after stopping → **annual dermatologic exam** with current or prior use · non-intestinal **lymphoma** standardized incidence ratio (SIR) 2.8–9.2 vs unexposed (highest absolute risk age >50 y, 1/354 PY; highest relative risk age <30 y, SIR 7.0) · **hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma (HSTCL)**: males <35 y, long-term thiopurine, frequently with anti-TNF; often fatal · very limited data: acute myeloid leukemia / myelodysplastic syndromes · **urinary-tract** cancer 2.8× vs IBD not on thiopurines (particularly males) |
| **Anti-TNF** | **Melanoma**: conflicting (one claims analysis ~2×; a Danish cohort and a meta-analysis null) · **lymphoma**: most, not all, studies show no ↑ with monotherapy; consistently ↑ **with a thiopurine**, particularly in young EBV-unexposed patients; most concerning = HSTCL · NMSC if combined with a thiopurine (Figure 1) |
| [[methotrexate\|Methotrexate]] | Limited data; possible small ↑ NMSC |
| [[jak-inhibitors\|Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors]] | No ↑ malignancy in IBD so far · trial data in rheumatoid arthritis (RA), age >50 y: ↑ any malignancy vs anti-TNF (esp. NMSC, lung, lymphoma) · conflicting: meta-analysis (joint/skin/bowel disease) ↑ vs anti-TNF but not vs placebo or methotrexate; pooled RA trial + extension analysis no association |
| [[vedolizumab\|Vedolizumab]], [[il-23-and-il-12-23-inhibitors\|ustekinumab, risankizumab, mirikizumab]], ozanimod, etrasimod | No ↑ malignancy shown; long-term data lacking · no data on biologic + small-molecule combinations |

- **Cervical** high-grade dysplasia/cancer: women with IBD 1.3× vs matched controls without IBD — largely tied to cumulative **immunomodulator** exposure, not biologics ([[aga-2024-ibd-malignancy]]).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

> [!pearl] Active cancer does not rule out IBD therapy
> If IBD activity must be controlled during oncologic treatment, most biologics are warranted — with close collaboration between the treating oncologist and gastroenterologist ([[aga-2024-ibd-malignancy]]).

## IBD Therapy in Patients With Prior Cancer

- Prior-cancer patients are under-represented in trials; recent data have **not** shown increased new or recurrent cancer with IBD drugs.
- French cohort, 405 with prior cancer: higher incident cancer (hazard ratio [HR] 1.9; 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.2–3.0) but **no** association with immunomodulator exposure; similar immunomodulator findings in a Spanish registry.
- New York Crohn's and Colitis Organization study (333 with prior cancer; 27% incident cancer): no difference by anti-TNF vs antimetabolite vs combination vs no treatment.
- Two meta-analyses (11,700 and 24,328 patients with immune-mediated disease + prior cancer): no difference in recurrence with anti-TNF, immunomodulators, or no immunosuppression; another: anti-TNF exposure did not raise new/recurrent cancer IRR.
- Vedolizumab and ustekinumab: no incident-cancer signal — 463 patients: no increase with vedolizumab vs anti-TNF vs no therapy; 390 patients: vedolizumab aHR 1.36 (95% CI 0.27–7.01), ustekinumab aHR 0.96 (0.17–5.41).
- Prospective registry of IBD patients with a history of cancer (SAPPHIRE; 305 patients, 69% given subsequent immunosuppression, median follow-up 4.8 y): 15% developed subsequent cancer (25 new, 21 recurrent); **no** significant association with immunosuppression (aHR 1.41; 95% CI 0.69–2.90) or any major drug class.

## Impact of Cancer Treatment on IBD

- **Cytotoxic chemotherapy:** most patients with active IBD achieved remission; nearly all still in remission 5 y after cancer diagnosis.
- **Hormonal deprivation therapy** (breast/prostate cancer, inactive IBD): ↑ IBD flare (HR 2.00 alone; 1.86 with chemotherapy); remission maintained in 42% on hormone monotherapy vs 75% on chemotherapy alone → follow closely with a gastroenterologist.
- **Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs):** relative risk of diarrhea 1.64, [[immune-checkpoint-inhibitor-colitis|colitis]] 10.35; ICI colitis → steroids, biologics if steroid-refractory. Known IBD → relapse in 40% (≥1/3 of them need biologics); higher relapse risk with CTLA-4 than PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors.
