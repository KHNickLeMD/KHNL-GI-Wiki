# Lumen-Apposing Metal Stents (LAMS)

A **lumen-apposing metal stent (LAMS)** is a short, fully covered, saddle-shaped metal stent with wide bilateral anchoring flanges that holds two apposed lumens open across a transmural tract. It is the enabling device for [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]]-guided transmural drainage — [[pancreatic-fluid-collection-drainage|pancreatic fluid collection drainage]], [[eus-guided-gallbladder-drainage|EUS-guided gallbladder drainage (EUS-GBD)]], [[eus-guided-biliary-drainage|EUS-guided biliary drainage]], EUS-guided gastroenterostomy (EUS-GE), and EUS-directed transgastric [[ercp|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]] (EDGE). This page holds the device: specifications, delivery systems, deployment, adverse events, and the competency step. **Each procedure keeps its own indication and route decision on its own page.**

## Device Design and Specifications

**Why LAMS replaced the earlier transmural constructs** ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]):

- Pigtail plastic stents and biliary **self-expandable metal stents (SEMS)** were ill-suited to gallbladder drainage: leakage, longer length causing contralateral wall injury or occlusion, and migration from the lack of flanges.
- LAMS overcome these with a **short length + large flanges + large diameters (6–20 mm)**, wide enough to pass **gallstones** or the **endoscope** for therapeutic cholecystoscopy.

**Against other metal stents in pancreatic fluid collections:**

- LAMS length **1 cm** suits transmural drainage better than covered esophageal SEMS, usually **no shorter than 6–7 cm** ([[aga-2020-cpu-pancreatic-necrosis]]).
- Broad anchoring flanges + large inner diameter: **no anchoring plastic stent needed** (some anchor a transmural covered biliary SEMS with a double-pigtail stent against migration), and through-the-stent endoscopic necrosectomy is easier ([[asge-2016-pancreatic-fluid-collections]]).

### US-Available Device

Three LAMS designs are available in Europe and Asia; **one type is commercially available in the United States** (Axios, Boston Scientific) ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]). Typical sizes for EUS-GBD:

| Parameter | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Covering** | Fully covered |
| **Saddle lengths** | **10 and 15 mm** |
| **Inner (lumen) diameters** | **10, 15, 20 mm** |
| **Bilateral anchor flange diameters** | **21, 24, 29 mm** |
| **Delivery catheter** | **10.5F** |
| **Scope requirement** | Therapeutic echoendoscope with a **3.7-mm working channel** |

## Delivery Systems

| | **Non–cautery-enhanced** | **Cautery-enhanced (electrocautery-tipped)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Steps** | **Fine-needle aspiration (FNA)** needle puncture (typically 19-gauge) → guidewire → tract dilation → stent placement | Direct entry and deployment through the duodenal or gastric wall — **no guidewire, no device exchange** |
| **Imaging** | Fluoroscopic guidance | Entirely EUS-guided; fluoroscopy only if a salvage maneuver is needed |
| **Advantage** | Wire access is retained if the stent misdeploys | Less technical difficulty, no tract dilation, shorter procedure; may obviate adjunctive debridement in necrosis |

Sources: [[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]] (both techniques), [[aga-2020-cpu-pancreatic-necrosis]] (cautery-enhanced advantages).

## Indications and Regulatory Status

| Site | Regulatory status | Device point | Indications and route |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pancreatic fluid collection** | **US Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved indication** ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]) | — | [[pancreatic-fluid-collection-drainage]] |
| **Gallbladder** | **Electrocautery-enhanced LAMS FDA-approved** for EUS-GBD ([[acg-2025-eus-quality]]); the older AGA 2023 called it off-label and required disclosure at consent — the newer document governs | Puncture site **<10 mm** from the gallbladder lumen (below) | [[eus-guided-gallbladder-drainage]] |
| **Bile duct (EUS-guided choledochoduodenostomy)** | — | Tubular metal stent or LAMS both appropriate; needs enough working space in the bile duct for complete deployment of the intraductal flange — requisite distance varies with prosthesis size ([[acg-2025-eus-quality]]) | [[eus-guided-biliary-drainage]] |
| **EUS-GE** | **No dedicated FDA-approved device** (Best Practice Advice 10, [[aga-2021-malignant-alimentary-tract-obstruction]]); LAMS for gastroenterostomy **off-label** when ASGE 2021 was drafted ([[asge-2021-gastroduodenal-obstruction]]) | 20-mm diameter — see pitfall below | [[eus-gastroenterostomy]] |
| **Benign gastroduodenal stricture** | **Off-label.** Every dedicated US enteral stent is uncovered and not removable → not for benign disease | Covered LAMS described in case series for **short, benign peptic strictures** ([[asge-2021-gastroduodenal-obstruction]]) | [[gastric-outlet-obstruction]] |
| **Colonic anastomotic stricture** | — | ASGE **suggests** LAMS for anastomotic strictures **<15 mm long refractory to endoscopic balloon dilation (EBD)** (conditional, very low quality); not first-line ([[asge-2026-colonic-strictures]]) | [[colonic-stricture]]; placement: [[colonic-stenting]] |

- **Colon evidence:** in the one study comparing LAMS with EBD (left-sided anastomotic strictures) there was **no significant difference** in technical success, clinical success, recurrence or adverse events; **LAMS migration 46%** despite the bilateral flanges; **rarely possible in the right colon** (catheter too short for adult colonoscopes) ([[asge-2026-colonic-strictures]]).

> [!pitfall] A 20-mm LAMS was suggested for EUS-GE — then selected 20 × 10-mm devices were recalled
> [[i-eus-2026-eus-gastroenterostomy|i-EUS 2026]] Statement 2.3 suggests a **20-mm diameter LAMS** for EUS-GE (may improve dietary tolerance and reduce reintervention; conditional, very low quality). **Recall/removal notices issued after the vote** cover selected **AXIOS / Hot AXIOS configurations and batches, including the 20 × 10-mm system**, for deployment and expansion problems at delivery. The notices show **no class-wide disadvantage of the 20-mm diameter**, but **affected devices must not be used** and the statement **does not make the 20-mm LAMS a preferred standard**. Select by current regulatory notices, availability, anatomy, indication and operator experience.

## Technique

### Deployment Sequence

> [!howto] Direct (cautery-enhanced) deployment — gallbladder
> 1. Enter the gallbladder directly with the cautery-enhanced LAMS through the duodenal or gastric wall.
> 2. Deploy the **gallbladder (distal) flange** under endosonographic guidance.
> 3. **Pull it back snugly** against the wall.
> 4. Deploy the **duodenal/gastric (proximal) flange** under EUS or endoscopic view.
>
> [[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]

### Puncture-to-Lumen Distance

- **Gallbladder: enteral puncture site <10 mm from the gallbladder lumen** for safe deployment ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]).
- Farther than that → AGA 2023's Figure 3 algorithm sends a gallbladder **>10 mm from the gastrointestinal (GI) tract wall** to transpapillary drainage first, EUS-GBD if that fails ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]; route choice on [[acute-cholecystitis]]).

### Troubleshooting

- **Prefer an over-the-wire technique** — it keeps access if the stent misdeploys — when the gallbladder is **fibrotic or contracted**, a **percutaneous drain is already in place**, or the **scope position is difficult** ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]).
- **Guidewire preloaded in the cautery-tip LAMS:** misdeployment is less of a problem in EUS-GBD and pancreatic fluid collection drainage with a single-step cautery-tip LAMS, but the wire can be advanced into the gallbladder or collection before deployment to secure the tract and allow rescue if the stent misdeploys ([[acg-2025-eus-quality]]).
- **Coaxial double-pigtail plastic stent through the LAMS:**
  - Gallbladder — not universally done, routine in the AGA authors' practice, to mitigate bleeding, stent obstruction and contralateral wall injury ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]).
  - Necrosis — some endoscopists place one or more to reduce early occlusion by necrotic tissue and LAMS migration ([[aga-2020-cpu-pancreatic-necrosis]]).

## Adverse Events and Prevention

| Procedure | **Adverse events (AEs)** |
|---|---|
| **EUS-guided pancreatic fluid collection drainage with LAMS** | Overall **13%–15%**, led by **bleeding** and **stent misdeployment/dysfunction** |
| **EUS-GE / EDGE** | **Stent misdeployment with perforation** is the most common AE, **10%** |

([[acg-2025-eus-quality]])

### Stent Misdeployment

Misdeployment types described for EUS-GE, with observed frequencies ([[acg-2025-eus-quality]]):

| Type | Where the stent ends up | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Distal flange in the peritoneum, **no enterotomy** | **63%** |
| **II** | Distal flange in the peritoneum **with an enterotomy** | **30.4%** |
| **III** | In the small bowel with the **proximal flange in the peritoneum** | **2.2%** |
| **IV** | Distal flange in the **colon** (gastrocolic anastomosis) | **4.4%** |

> [!pitfall] A misdeployed LAMS comes out, and the patient stays fasted
> Recognize early, manage endoscopically at once: pure type I → **stent removal and closure**; often also works for type II (small enterotomies may close on their own if the patient stays stable). Unstable and complete closure impossible → **surgical consult**. Keep the patient **fasted ≥24 hours** after the adverse event ([[acg-2025-eus-quality]]). Closure techniques: [[endoscopic-management-of-perforation]].

### Bleeding

- **Doppler before puncture** minimizes bleeding risk in interventional EUS ([[acg-2025-eus-quality]]).
- **Pancreatic fluid collection drainage:** cumulative bleeding risk **7%**, possibly from pseudoaneurysm with severe bleeding and hemorrhagic shock; linked to longer dwell ([[#Dwell Time and Removal]]), presumably from rapid cyst decompression and contralateral wall injury by the prosthesis ([[acg-2025-eus-quality]]).
- **EUS-GE / EDGE:** bleeding is most often from fistula creation or balloon dilation after deployment; also ulceration or erosion from LAMS friction ([[acg-2025-eus-quality]]).

## Dwell Time and Removal

| Site | Dwell rule |
|---|---|
| **Pancreatic fluid collection** | Concern about leaving LAMS **beyond several weeks** (delayed bleeding); SEMS of any type should not stay long-term ([[aga-2020-cpu-pancreatic-necrosis]]). Bleeding associated with dwell **>4 weeks** ([[acg-2025-eus-quality]]) |
| **Gallbladder** | Exchange for double-pigtail plastic stents at **4–6 weeks** (with peroral cholecystoscopy) if the patient accepts a second procedure; or leave in place long term in frail patients ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]). Detail: [[eus-guided-gallbladder-drainage]] |
| **EDGE (gastrogastrostomy / jejunogastrostomy)** | Dwell **≥40 days**: odds ratio **4.5** for a persistent fistula (can undermine the gastric bypass → weight regain); odds rise **9.5% per extra 7 days**. Endoscopic closure of the fistula at stent removal appears to reduce the risk ([[acg-2025-eus-quality]]) |
| **Colonic stricture** | Fully covered, placed for temporary remodeling → removal or exchange **can be performed at 2–3 months** ([[asge-2026-colonic-strictures]]) |

## Competency and Training

- **Learn LAMS in pancreatic fluid collections first (the FDA-approved indication): ≥10 independent successful deployments before progressing to EUS-GBD**, then start with markedly distended gallbladders with no intervening tissue ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]).
- EUS-GBD belongs to endoscopists expert in advanced therapeutic EUS; learning-curve numbers are on [[eus-guided-gallbladder-drainage]] ([[aga-2023-eus-gallbladder-drainage]]).
