## Overview

- Enteral tubes deliver nutrition/medications OR provide **gastrointestinal (GI) decompression** (malignant obstruction, impaired motility).
- Access types:
  - **Oral/nasal flexible tubes** — orogastric (OG), nasogastric (NG), nasojejunal (NJ).
  - **Percutaneous tubes** — surgical, radiologic, or endoscopic (percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy [PEG], PEG with jejunal extension [PEGJ], direct percutaneous endoscopic jejunostomy [DPEJ]).
- Endoscopically placed tubes: favorable safety profile vs surgical or radiologic placement ([[aga-2025-endoscopic-enteral-access]]).
- **Normal foregut anatomy needing enteral access → PEG over interventional radiology–guided gastrostomy (IR-G)** as the initial approach — [[asge-2025-gastrostomy-tubes]] **Recommendation 1 (conditional, low quality of evidence)**. Lower 30-day all-cause mortality and less tube malfunction; bleeding, technical failure, peristomal infection and aspiration pneumonia comparable. Where IR-G is the local routine or PEG unavailable, IR-G remains appropriate.
- Multidisciplinary team advised (speech pathology, dietitians, nutrition nurses, co-managing physicians, palliative care when appropriate).
- Discuss patient preferences, quality of life and prognosis with patient and family **before** placing any feeding tube ([[asge-2011-enteral-feeding]] Rec 2, conditional, ⊕⊕◯◯).

## Indications

**Timing of access (hospitalized adults):**
- Low [[nutrition-in-hospitalized-patients|nutritional risk]] + unable to meet caloric requirements over **5–7 days** → pursue enteral access.
- Severely malnourished + unable to maintain oral nutrition → within **24–48 h** of admission.

**Indication categories (source Table 1):**

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Impaired nutrient intake (neurologic) | Post–cerebrovascular accident (CVA), motor neuron disease, multiple sclerosis, Parkinson's, unconscious/ventilated, cerebral palsy, select dementia |
| Upper GI obstruction / motility disorder | Head & neck cancer; head/maxillofacial/mandibular/dental/neck trauma; radiation stomatitis; [[esophageal-cancer\|esophageal cancer]]; [[esophageal-stricture\|esophageal stricture]]; esophageal dysmotility; chronic intestinal pseudo-obstruction; [[gastroparesis]]; superior mesenteric artery syndrome |
| Altered intestinal anatomy | [[bariatric-surgery\|Bariatric]] or foregut surgery with impaired intake |
| Psychiatric | Anorexia nervosa |
| Increased metabolic requirements | Critical illness, cancer, burn/trauma, cystic fibrosis / chronic lung disease, [[inflammatory-bowel-disease\|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]], [[nutrition-in-liver-disease\|liver disease]], [[acute-pancreatitis\|pancreatitis]], renal disease |
| Increased intestinal losses | [[short-bowel-syndrome\|Short bowel syndrome]] |

**Acute dysphagic stroke:** consider PEG only in those who do **not** improve after a **2- to 3-week trial of nasoenteric tube feeding** ([[asge-2011-enteral-feeding]]).

> [!pearl] Dementia: the tube is a conversation, not a procedure
> In severe dementia, enteral feeding is **not** associated with improved survival, reduced infection risk or improved functional status — especially against a conscientious program of **hand feeding**. Needs a realistic goals-of-care conversation with family and caregivers covering benefits, risks and complications ([[aga-2025-endoscopic-enteral-access]]).

## Route Selection

| Scenario | Preferred route |
|---|---|
| Expected duration **<4 weeks**, nonintubated | Nasal (NG/NJ) |
| Expected duration **≥4 weeks** | Percutaneous (PEG / PEGJ / DPEJ) |
| Intact gastric emptying, no aspiration concern | **Gastric** (NG or PEG) |
| Severe [[gerd\|gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)]], [[gastroparesis]], or repeated tube feeding–related aspiration | Post-pyloric (**PEGJ or DPEJ**) — [[asge-2011-enteral-feeding]] Rec 4, conditional, ⊕⊕◯◯ |
| Aspiration refractory to prokinetics/rate/agent changes | Small-bowel (NJ / PEGJ / DPEJ) |
| Altered anatomy (Roux-en-Y gastric bypass [RYGB], sleeve, gastrectomy) | Small-bowel (required) |
| Gastric outlet obstruction | Small-bowel (required) |
| Severely delayed gastric emptying | Small-bowel (required) |
| Long-term small-bowel, minimize reintervention | **DPEJ** (better patency; but harder, more leak) |
| Head & neck cancer or upper GI stricture/obstruction | Transabdominal ("introducer"/Russell) gastrostomy ([[aga-2025-endoscopic-enteral-access]]) — but [[asge-2025-gastrostomy-tubes]] accepts **either** pull or direct for malignant dysphagia (see [[#Technique]]) |
| Anatomy precluding PEG (eg, post-surgical) | **DPEJ** ([[asge-2011-enteral-feeding]]) |
| [[chronic-pancreatitis\|Chronic pancreatitis]] with persistent nutritional compromise | Post-pyloric (**PEGJ or DPEJ**) ([[asge-2011-enteral-feeding]]) |

### Gastric vs Small-Bowel Feeding

- **Gastric feeding preferred** — more physiologic; allows intermittent bolus OR continuous pump feeding.
- Meta-analyses (mostly critical care): small-bowel feeding reduces aspiration pneumonia vs gastric, but small-bowel access is more technically complex.
- Before converting gastric → small-bowel for aspiration concerns, first try:
  - Prokinetics
  - Reduce bowel-slowing agents
  - Decrease feeding rates
- [[gastric-outlet-obstruction|Gastric outlet obstruction]], altered anatomy and severely delayed emptying are the rows where small-bowel access is **required**, not merely preferred.
- Small-bowel feeding needs **reduced and cycled rates** to prevent dumping (hypertonic formula directly into small bowel).

### Nasoenteric vs Percutaneous (Duration)

- **Nasal route** preferred for **short-term (<4 weeks)** access in nonintubated patients.
- **Percutaneous** tubes for expected use **≥4 weeks** (threshold acknowledged as "somewhat arbitrary").
- Same rule phrased by duration of expected oral intake: nasoenteric if peroral nutrition is expected to resume **within 30 days**, percutaneous endoscopic tube if not ([[asge-2011-enteral-feeding]] Recs 1 and 3, both conditional, ⊕⊕◯◯).
- Percutaneous devices dislodge less and deliver more adequate/durable feeding than NG tubes — but added placement risk justifies nasoenteral tubes for short-term use.

### Small-Bowel Access Options

- NJ tube; jejunal extension through a PEG (**PEGJ**); unitized transgastric jejunostomy tube; **DPEJ**.
- **DPEJ > PEGJ** for tube patency and fewer reinterventions (jejunal extensions only **9F–12F**, small caliber).
- BUT DPEJ placement more complex, and **peristomal leakage increased with DPEJ** (larger-caliber tubes, bumpers in the small bowel).

## Contraindications

**To enteral access for feeding in general** ([[aga-2025-endoscopic-enteral-access]]):
- **Only ABSOLUTE contraindication:** **mechanical [[small-bowel-obstruction|small-bowel obstruction]]** (unless tube placed for decompression).
- **Avoid nasal access** with abnormal nasal anatomy, facial trauma, or recent oronasal surgery.
- **Relative contraindications to percutaneous insertion:**
  - Active GI bleeding from [[peptic-ulcer-disease|peptic ulcer]]
  - Hemodynamic or respiratory instability
- **Complicate siting or raise aspiration risk but do NOT preclude PEG** (anesthesia support helps): large midline wounds, chest tubes, abdominal mesh, [[ostomy-management|ostomies]], morbid [[obesity]], large [[hiatal-hernia|hiatal]] or abdominal wall hernias, peritoneal carcinomatosis, severe dysmotility, [[variceal-upper-gi-bleeding|esophageal varices]].

**To PEG specifically** ([[asge-2011-enteral-feeding]]) — these stop the technique, not the nutrition plan:
- **Absolute:** inability to bring the anterior gastric wall into apposition with the abdominal wall · pharyngeal or esophageal obstruction · significant coagulopathy. The contraindications to [[upper-endoscopy|upper endoscopy]] also apply.
- PEG should not be used for nutritional support when GI tract obstruction is present.
- **Relative:** [[ascites]]; neoplastic, inflammatory and infiltrative disease of the gastric and abdominal walls.
- Previous gastric resection, hepatomegaly and obesity may defeat gastric transillumination and so the placement.

**Special situations:**
- **Ascites:** impairs wound healing and gastric–abdominal wall apposition → **drain via paracentesis or intraperitoneal drain**; use T-tacks to improve tract formation.
- **Ventriculoperitoneal (VP) shunt:** place gastrostomy **concurrently with or before** VP shunt, rather than within **15 days after** shunt placement; consider longer perioperative antibiotics with an existing shunt.
- **Peritoneal dialysis:** converting to (or maintaining) **hemodialysis for 6 weeks** reduces — but does not eliminate — peritonitis risk.

## Preprocedural Considerations

- **Consent:** patient (with capacity) or legally authorized representative; delineate risks, expected benefits, outcomes.
- **[[antibiotic-prophylaxis-endoscopy|Antibiotic prophylaxis]]:** significantly reduces peristomal infection.
  - **Recommended:** a prophylactic dose given **intravenously (IV)** before percutaneous endoscopic feeding-tube placement ([[asge-2011-enteral-feeding]] Rec 5, strong, ⊕⊕⊕⊕).
  - **Single dose of a first-generation cephalosporin immediately before** access — [[asge-2025-gastrostomy-tubes]] best practice advice names **cefazolin**, given at the time of the procedure or **30 min before**.
  - Not required if already on systemic antibiotics.
  - Cephalosporin allergy → **clindamycin or vancomycin**.
  - **Where methicillin-resistant *Staphylococcus aureus* (MRSA) is endemic:** screening and decolonization before placement may further reduce peristomal MRSA infection — in addition to, not instead of, the single prophylactic dose ([[asge-2011-enteral-feeding]]).
- **[[anticoagulation-gi-bleeding|Antithrombotics]]** ([[asge-2025-gastrostomy-tubes]]) — antiplatelets and anticoagulants are handled differently:
  - **Antiplatelets, including dual-antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) — suggests AGAINST routine withholding** before PEG (**Recommendation 2a; conditional, very low quality of evidence**). No excess bleeding on aspirin, clopidogrel or DAPT; events were non–life-threatening, while holding risks cardiovascular events and delay.
  - **Anticoagulants — periprocedural management is based on a multidisciplinary discussion including the patient**, weighing bleeding against cardiovascular risk (**Recommendation 2b; conditional, very low quality of evidence**). Data on direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) were too limited to be included in that guideline.
  - Same multidisciplinary-discussion route for anyone on antithrombotics who is at high bleeding risk or cannot receive blood products (antibodies, religious beliefs).
  - If antithrombotics held, resume as soon as possible considering indication, in consultation with initiating service.
- **Laboratory thresholds:** platelets **>50,000/mL**; international normalized ratio (INR) **<1.5** — [[preprocedure-testing|preprocedural workup]] is full blood count + coagulation testing ([[aga-2025-endoscopic-enteral-access]]).
  - **Unfractionated heparin:** hold for percutaneous placement; can give **2–6 h after** procedure.
  - **DOACs:** hold for placement; resume with the **evening dose** after placement ([[aga-2025-endoscopic-enteral-access]]).

## Technique

**OG / NG tubes:**
- Bedside, blind, **8F–12F**.
- Bedside nasoenteric (postpyloric) placement harder; electromagnetic tracking improves success.
- **Radiographic confirmation for ALL blind enteric placements** (and after guided placement if concern).
- Endoscopic NJ placement most secure (drag, over-guide-wire, through-the-scope all effective) but more resource-intensive.

**Percutaneous gastrostomy routes:**
- **Transoral** — pull or push.
- **Transabdominal** — radiologic OR endoscopic "introducer"/Russell technique: T-tack gastropexy → tract dilation → tube through dilated tract. Reserved for **head & neck cancer** (minimize tumor seeding) and upper GI strictures/obstruction ([[aga-2025-endoscopic-enteral-access]]).
- **Malignant dysphagia:** either a **transoral pull PEG** or a **transcutaneous direct PEG or IR-G** is acceptable — [[asge-2025-gastrostomy-tubes]] **Recommendation 4 (conditional, very low quality of evidence)**; no head-to-head study, and implantation metastasis was rare either way (0.6% pull vs 0.07% direct, not significantly different). Counsel about implantation metastasis at consent and examine the site periodically (best practice advice). A narrow-caliber/ultraslim gastroscope allows direct PEG through a stenotic esophageal tumor.

**PEG (pull) technique:**
- Insufflate stomach to appose to abdominal wall.
- Site typically **2 cm below the costal margin** at maximal transillumination.
- "One-to-one" finger indentation.
- **"Safe track"** — anesthetic-filled small-bore needle advanced under aspiration to exclude interposed bowel.
- Trocar → guide wire → snared and pulled out mouth → tube pulled out through abdominal wall.
- **Push** technique similar (tube with dilator pushed over the wire from the mouth).
- Second-look endoscopy to confirm bumper position is **not mandatory** — only if position is in doubt ([[asge-2011-enteral-feeding]]).

> [!pitfall] All three checks, every time, before the trocar
> Transillumination, 1-to-1 indentation and the safe-track needle with air aspiration are what keep the colon out of the tract — [[asge-2025-gastrostomy-tubes]] best practice advice. Air aspirated before the needle is seen inside the stomach means interposed bowel.

**PEGJ:**
- Place PEG directed toward/closer to the pylorus.
- Extension sizing: **12F via 24F PEG, 9F via 20F PEG** (smaller sizes clog more).
- Drag with suture + forceps/clip, OR pass over a guide wire (pediatric colonoscope, ideally past the ligament of Treitz; ultrathin-endoscope guide-wire techniques also used).

**DPEJ:**
- Like a pull-PEG but harder (mobile small bowel, narrow angulated lumen).
- Needs transillumination + finger indentation; snare-grasp the sounding needle to stabilize the loop before incision/trocar.
- Facilitated by thin habitus, altered surgical anatomy, and **glucagon** (reduces small-bowel contractility).
- 14F–20F tubes possible; authors prefer **14F** (less obstruction of intestinal flow, easier snare capture at removal).

## Post-procedure Care

- **NG/NJ:** use immediately after confirmation (usually abdominal radiography).
- **PEG — feed early.** [[asge-2025-gastrostomy-tubes]] **Recommendation 3 (strong; moderate quality for the randomized trials, low across all studies)**: PEG tubes may be used for feeding **early — within 4 h** — rather than routinely delayed. Mortality, adverse events, vomiting, aspiration, peristomal leak, wound infection and stomatitis were all comparable to delayed feeding.
  - Exceptions the panel named: **severe ileus, obstruction, bowel ischemia** (and simple logistic delay).
  - **Medications** may go down the tube **immediately** after placement, absent contraindications to enteral medication (best practice advice; [[aga-2025-endoscopic-enteral-access]] likewise, and puts feeds at **after 4 h**).
  - **Routine measurement of gastric residual volume is not indicated** (best practice advice) — unstandardized, and it drives unnecessary feed interruption.
- **External bumper:** tightening it for **24 h** and then loosening it may be considered to tamponade against bleeding — data limited, and over-tightening buries the bumper ([[asge-2025-gastrostomy-tubes]] best practice advice). Restore the **1-cm** skin-to-bumper breadth afterwards (see [[#Complications]]).
- Multidisciplinary follow-up (dietitians for formula/volume/free water/delivery; nursing/advanced practice clinicians (APCs) for site assessment).
- **Bridling** transnasal tubes reduces dislodgement, allows greater caloric intake.
- Transnasal tubes typically **≤3–4 weeks** (sinusitis risk); rarely longer with close monitoring.
- Percutaneous devices replaced at **6- to 12-month intervals** (sooner if breakdown).
- Avoid overlying gauze/tape/abdominal pads (retain moisture, injure skin).
- **After permanent removal:** spontaneous tract closure typically within **72 h**.
  - Persistent gastrocutaneous fistula risk factors: immune suppression, prior gastrostomy-site infection, malnutrition, delayed gastric emptying → endoscopic closure with over-the-scope clips or suturing.
  - **"Cut-and-push" removal** (cut at the skin, push the internal bumper in, pass it per rectum) — do **not** use with distal GI stricture, dysmotility, previous abdominal surgery or anatomic abnormality ([[asge-2011-enteral-feeding]]).
- **Inadvertent tube removal:**
  - Placed **within previous 4 weeks** → do NOT blindly replace (stomach may not be adhered to abdominal wall). Give antibiotics, serial abdominal exams for peritonitis, replace under radiographic or endoscopic guidance. Without peritonitis, early dislodgement is usually managed with nasogastric decompression, IV antibiotics and PEG replacement several days later ([[asge-2011-enteral-feeding]]).
  - Placed **>4 weeks** ago → routine clinic/bedside replacement through mature tract; act fast (tract can close within hours); confirm intraluminal position with water-soluble contrast through the tube (or endoscopy).

> [!pitfall] No mature tract, no blind replacement
> Non-endoscopic replacement of a dislodged tube or button is contraindicated before the fistulous tract matures — intraperitoneal spillage ([[asge-2011-enteral-feeding]]).

## Complications

| Complication | Cause / prevention | Presentation / management |
|---|---|---|
| **Sinusitis** | Nasal tubes, duration-related | — |
| **Peristomal infection** | Reduced by preprocedural antibiotics | Cellulitis; rarely **necrotizing fasciitis** (surrounding-skin inflammation ± subcutaneous gas on imaging) |
| **Self-extraction** | Altered consciousness | Bridles (nasal); gloves/mitts, abdominal binders, or low-profile tubes (percutaneous) |
| **Tube occlusion** | Small-bore tubes (8F–12F, oral/nasal/jejunal extensions) clog sooner | Prevent: **30 mL water flush every 4 h** during continuous feeding + flushes before/after feeds; eliminate crushed-medication administration |
| **Buried bumper** | Excessive external traction (external bumper too tight); prevent with routine assessment + **1-cm breadth between external bumper and skin** | Pain, inability to rotate/advance tube, difficulty feeding/flushing, ↑ leakage/infection → identify with cross-sectional imaging or endoscopy → **replace under endoscopic or radiologic guidance** |
| **Internal migration** | External bumper too loose | Internal bumper/balloon obstructs pylorus or proximal duodenum — tube tight to skin and/or severe bilious leakage |
| **Peristomal leakage** (gastric/bilious) | Motility-altering meds (opiates), gastroparesis, vagal nerve injury | Do **NOT** upsize tube (enlarges hole, worsens leakage). Trial promotility agents with intermittent feeds and/or continuous pump feeding at reduced rate; if unsuccessful → **convert gastric to jejunal feeding** (vent stomach while feeding); rarely remove tube temporarily for partial stoma closure or re-site |
| **Colocutaneous / gastrocolic fistula** | Tube inadvertently through the colon — prevented by the three pre-puncture checks | Diarrhea and fecal discharge around the site, often appearing only **after the tube is first replaced**; remove the tube (usually spontaneous closure), surgery if it does not ([[asge-2011-enteral-feeding]]) |
| **Pneumoperitoneum** | Common after PEG | Clinically insignificant **unless** accompanied by signs and symptoms of peritonitis ([[asge-2011-enteral-feeding]]) |
| **Jejunal volvulus** | Unique to DPEJ | DPEJ complications otherwise mirror PEG ([[asge-2011-enteral-feeding]]) |

**Rates ([[asge-2011-enteral-feeding]]):** overall PEG complications **4.9–10.3%**, serious **1.5–4%**, minor ~**6%**. Procedure-related mortality **~0.5%**, but **30-day all-cause mortality ~15%** — comorbidity, not the procedure. Tumor seeding after PEG in oropharyngeal tumors **<1%**. DPEJ: mortality 0.3%, serious adverse events 4.2%.
