# Interventional EUS for Vascular Investigation and Therapy

[[endoscopic-ultrasound|Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]]-guided access to vessels in or near the gastrointestinal (GI) wall. Two prerequisites for clinical utility: (1) a vascular target in/near the GI wall where endoscopic access beats percutaneous, and (2) efficacy/safety comparable or superior to current alternatives. Clinically available today: **gastric variceal injection therapy** and **EUS-guided portosystemic pressure gradient (EUS-PPG)**. ([[aga-2023-interventional-eus-vascular]])

## Landscape — maturity of each intervention

| Intervention | Status | Evidence base |
|---|---|---|
| Gastric variceal coil ± glue | Clinically available; **strongest case for adoption at centers of expertise** | 3 retrospective series, 2 small randomized controlled trials (RCTs), meta-analyses |
| EUS-PPG | Clinically available (Food and Drug Administration [FDA]-approved device); international Delphi consensus (53 statements reached consensus) | Aggregate adverse events **<1%**; largest multicenter series n=385 ([[wang-2026-eus-ppg-delphi-consensus]]) |
| Ectopic/rectal varices | Emerging | Case reports + 1 small series |
| Splenic artery embolization | Emerging | 5-patient series |
| Arterial bleeding / pseudoaneurysms | Emerging (salvage) | 6- and 8-patient series |
| Portal vein sampling | Safe/feasible but **research protocol only** | Small cohorts (n=18) |
| Intrahepatic portosystemic shunt | Experimental | Porcine preclinical only |

## EUS-Guided Gastric Variceal Therapy

- Gastric variceal hemorrhage is less prevalent than esophageal but often more severe and higher-mortality. **Cardiofundal varices (isolated gastric varices type 1 [IGV1], gastroesophageal varices type 2 [GOV2] — [[portal-hypertension|Sarin classification criteria]])** are the hardest endoscopic targets — large, awkwardly located, requiring a clear retroflexed field of view; intravascular needle placement during direct endoscopic injection (DEI) may be inaccurate.
- **Why EUS guidance:** better injection precision (including during active bleeding when direct visualization fails), expands options to **hemostatic coils**, and gives **real-time Doppler feedback of hemostasis**.
- **When to choose it** — for high-risk gastric varices (**>10 mm or previously bled**), American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2026 suggests EUS-guided injection over DEI, cyanoacrylate **plus coils** over cyanoacrylate alone when feasible, and that EUS-guided injection be considered over retrograde transvenous obliteration (RTO) for initial management (all conditional, very low certainty); DEI if EUS is unavailable ([[asge-2026-esophageal-gastric-varices]]). Full pathway and workup: [[variceal-upper-gi-bleeding#Cardiofundal Varices (GOV2/IGV1) — the Bimodal Pathway|cardiofundal varices on the variceal bleeding page]].
- Coils: soft platinum wire with spaced synthetic fibers (interventional radiology [IR] embolization coils — Nester or MReye, Cook); intravascular placement is postulated to initiate thrombus and act as a scaffold for subsequently injected cyanoacrylate, potentially minimizing postprocedure embolic events ([[aga-2023-interventional-eus-vascular]]).

> [!pitfall] Not FDA approved — decide as a team
> EUS injection therapy of gastric varices is not FDA approved; treatment decisions should be made with hepatology and IR, which offer [[variceal-obliteration|balloon-assisted retrograde transvenous obliteration (BRTO)]] and [[tips|transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS)]] ([[aga-2023-interventional-eus-vascular]]).

### Technique — gastric variceal coiling

([[aga-2023-interventional-eus-vascular]] unless marked)

1. Patient intubated, left lateral decubitus.
2. Instill **100–200 cm³ water** through the echoendoscope and retain it in the gastric fundus — delineates intramural vessels (gastric varices) from extramural collaterals and **perforator veins** (identified by tracing extramural vessels across the muscularis propria into the intramural varix).
3. Access the varix with a **22-gauge or 19-gauge fine-needle aspiration (FNA) needle**, transesophageal or transgastric approach.
4. Place coils sequentially into the target vessel (most published technique: **1–3 coils**; 1–4 per patient in the RCT cited by ASGE); use **Doppler interrogation during placement to decide how many**. Coil size ([[asge-2026-esophageal-gastric-varices]]): **~20%–30% larger in diameter than the varix**; **22-gauge needle → 0.018-inch coils; 19-gauge → 0.018- or 0.035-inch**.
5. When Doppler flow is significantly reduced or near-absent, inject an adjunct such as **cyanoacrylate**. Glue formulation, injection speed and needle flush: [[variceal-upper-gi-bleeding#Technique — Gastric Variceal Injection|gastric variceal injection technique]].
6. Repeat for other varices as required.

- **Fluoroscopy ± varicealography** helpful early in the learning curve but **not required**.
- **No definitive data on whether to target the varix itself or the perforator vein(s).**
- The multi-step technique requires adequate training of nurses and technicians.

### Outcomes — coil vs glue vs combination

([[aga-2023-interventional-eus-vascular]])

- EUS-based therapies (3 retrospective series, 2 small RCTs): ~**99% technical success and control of bleeding**; rebleeding **0%–16%**; adverse events **0%–7%**.
- **Meta-analysis, any EUS-guided therapy vs DEI:** treatment efficacy 94% vs 91%, but **gastric variceal obliteration 84% vs 63% (P = .02)**; in subgroup analysis, coil + glue had significantly fewer recurrences (**5%**) than glue or coils alone.
- **A second meta-analysis** found combination (coil + glue) the **preferred strategy**:

| EUS-guided regimen | Hemostasis | Adverse events |
|---|---|---|
| Coil + cyanoacrylate | 96%–98% | 10% |
| Cyanoacrylate alone | 96% | 21% |
| Coils alone | 90% | 3% |

- ASGE 2026's comparison of cyanoacrylate + coils vs cyanoacrylate alone rests on 1 RCT: similar obliteration and adverse events, fewer reinterventions with coils (numbers on [[variceal-upper-gi-bleeding]]) ([[asge-2026-esophageal-gastric-varices]]).
- Coil + absorbable gelatin sponge (Gelfoam) in lieu of cyanoacrylate: similar low rebleeding and reintervention rates. EUS-guided **thrombin** injection: small series only.
- At least one retrospective comparative study shows EUS-guided cyanoacrylate injection superior to DEI.

## EUS-Guided Portal Pressure Gradient (EUS-PPG)

- Standard **[[hepatic-venous-pressure-gradient|hepatic venous pressure gradient (HVPG)]]** (IR, transjugular) measures the portal gradient **indirectly** — the difference between free and wedged hepatic vein pressure. Drawbacks: requires radiation and contrast; being indirect it can **misdiagnose noncirrhotic/presinusoidal portal hypertension** ([[aga-2023-interventional-eus-vascular]]).
- HVPG reference points (AGA 2023): **>10 mm Hg** correlates with development of esophageal varices; **>12 mm Hg** with increased risk of bleeding esophageal varices. Full staging: [[hepatic-venous-pressure-gradient]]; context: [[portal-hypertension]].
- **EUS-PPG measures the hepatic vein–portal vein gradient directly**, using the FDA-approved EchoTip Insight system (Cook).

### Technique — EUS-PPG

([[aga-2023-interventional-eus-vascular]])

1. Patient **supine**, preferably under **[[endoscopy-sedation|general anesthesia]]** to minimize movement.
2. **One dose of [[antibiotic-prophylaxis-endoscopy|prophylactic antibiotics]]** usually given (e.g., ciprofloxacin or cefazolin).
3. **Level the compact manometer at the midaxillary line / level of the right heart** and keep it there for the entire procedure.
4. Linear echoendoscope: identify hepatic veins anatomically, confirm by Doppler; target the **middle or left hepatic vein with a 25-gauge needle via a transgastric approach**.
5. Take **≥3 consecutive pressure measurements** (one-needle-stick approach) and average them.
6. Identify the **intrahepatic portal vein**, confirm by Doppler, repeat the measurement sequence.
7. **EUS-PPG = mean portal pressure − mean hepatic vein pressure.**

### Indications and evidence

**Where EUS-PPG may be indicated or clinically helpful** ([[aga-2023-interventional-eus-vascular]]):

- **Discordant data** on the presence/absence of portal hypertension — isolated thrombocytopenia, isolated splenomegaly, normal HVPG but possible presinusoidal portal hypertension
- **Supplementing preoperative clearance** (including pretransplant workup) in known or suspected [[cirrhosis]]
- **Monitoring response** to medications or other intervention
- Strongest argument for use: **when there is already another indication for endoscopy** — variceal screening or [[liver-biopsy|liver biopsy]] — allowing [[upper-endoscopy|esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD)]], EUS, portosystemic pressure gradient (PPG), biopsy, and assessment/treatment of portal-hypertensive endoscopic features (varices, [[portal-hypertensive-gastropathy|portal hypertensive gastropathy]]) in one session

**Evidence:** first human 2014; 28-patient series — excellent correlation between EUS-PPG and endoscopic findings of portal hypertension in suspected cirrhosis; 64-patient retrospective series with concurrent liver biopsy — excellent correlation with **histologic fibrosis stage**, clinical portal hypertension, thrombocytopenia, splenomegaly, **aspartate aminotransferase-to-platelet ratio index (APRI) >2**, and **[[noninvasive-liver-disease-assessment|Fibrosis-4 (FIB-4)]] >3.25**, with **no reported adverse events**; 83-patient series — correlated with thrombocytopenia and varices, again no adverse events.

### Best-practice consensus (Wang 2026 Delphi)

International expert modified-Delphi consensus on how to perform EUS-PPG accurately and safely ([[wang-2026-eus-ppg-delphi-consensus]]). The document numbers no statements; the statement numbers below (S1–S53) are this wiki's.

**Indications:**
- Include, but are not limited to, **all indications for HVPG** (S1); EUS-PPG correlates well with clinical portal hypertension markers — varices, portal hypertensive gastropathy, thrombocytopenia (S2).
- **"One-stop-shop":** may be favored over HVPG for convenience when the patient has another EGD/EUS indication (liver biopsy, variceal screening/surveillance) (S4), and for comfort because anesthesia is used (S5) — in the same session a screening EGD should be done to document portal hypertension sequelae (S8), and an EUS liver survey should precede measurement to map targets and rule out vascular abnormalities such as [[portal-vein-thrombosis|portal vein thrombosis]] (S9), and EUS [[liver-biopsy|liver biopsy]] if indicated (safe, S10).

> [!pearl] Suspected presinusoidal portal hypertension → think EUS-PPG
> EUS-PPG should be considered over HVPG when a presinusoidal cause is suspected (S3, 95% agreement): HVPG uses wedged hepatic vein pressure as a proxy and may underestimate portal pressure there; EUS-PPG measures the portal vein directly ([[wang-2026-eus-ppg-delphi-consensus]]).

- Noncirrhotic portal hypertension and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH; HVPG may underestimate portal pressure) are listed only as **future-research areas**, not indications (S47, S50).

**Pre-procedure:**
- **Anesthesia:** should be **monitored anesthesia care or general anesthesia** to minimize discomfort and movement (S13). The authors note some centers routinely use moderate sedation, and no evidence yet favors anesthesia over conscious sedation.
- **Position:** supine or left lateral decubitus, patient flat (S14); manometer centered at the **right atrium** — **mid-axillary line supine** (S15), **mid-sternum left lateral** (S16). Serial measurements across sessions: **same anesthesia and positioning** (S17).
- **Coagulation (S11):** in cirrhosis, **no specific platelet or INR cutoff** can be recommended — these poorly reflect procedural bleeding risk; where available, **thromboelastography (TEG)** may better predict it (why conventional tests mislead in cirrhosis: [[cirrhosis-hemostasis]]). Originally proposed cutoffs — avoid if platelets <50 K; caution if cirrhosis and prothrombin time–international normalized ratio (INR) >2.0 — failed consensus. Antiplatelet/anticoagulant hold-and-restart: same strategy as EUS-FNA (authors' note, not a statement).
- **[[ascites|Ascites]]:** avoid with **tense or massive** ascites unless pre-procedural large-volume paracentesis is performed (S6); reasonable with **mild to moderate (grade 1–2)** ascites, considering therapeutic paracentesis first if feasible (S12).
- **Antibiotics:** peri-procedural antibiotics **should be considered** (S20; softened from "should be administered", which failed consensus).
- **Consent:** cite risks similar to EUS-FNA, such as infection and bleeding (S19).

**Intra-procedure protocol:**

| Step | Best practice |
|---|---|
| Zero & flush | Zero manometer to **atmospheric pressure** before setup (S18); fixate it in a stable position (S22). Once zeroed, flush the entire device with **heparinized or normal saline, taking great care to avoid air bubbles** (S21) |
| Access route | **Transgastric approach through liver parenchyma** — parenchymal tract provides **tamponade** on needle withdrawal (S23–24); transduodenal occasionally required. Use Doppler waveforms to identify hepatic/portal branches (S25). Endoscopic suction **cautioned** during and after device insertion (S29) |
| Ideal targets | Hepatic vein, for most patients: **left + middle hepatic veins** (S26). Portal vein: **umbilical portion of the left portal vein** (S28). If the hepatic vein is not accessible, the **intrahepatic portion of the inferior vena cava** may be a reasonable alternative (S30) |
| Reproducibility | **≥3 sequential measurements per target, within ≤2 mmHg of each other; report the mean** (S31). After each flush, reading should peak, drop, then **plateau ≥10 s within a ≤1 mmHg range** (S32); do not touch the syringe plunger during readings (S33). **Repeat if it takes >60 s to stabilize** (S34); re-accessing can be considered if measurements are discrepant (S39) |
| Steady state | Relaxed scope, no undue elevator engagement (S35); keep needle intravascular and away from the vessel wall (S36); movement and coughing affect readings (S40). Keep intravenous (IV) fluids at steady state; if changed, consider repeating under stable conditions (S37–38). Anesthesia at steady state before needle insertion, no boluses between measurements; unavoidable changes → document timing/dosing and consider repeating at steady state (S41–43) |

**Safety:** EUS-PPG is safe with minimal risk of adverse events (S7); aggregate adverse events in the literature **<1%**, most common post-procedural pain. Largest series (n=385) reported **no bleeding adverse events** despite no platelet/INR exclusion, including 226 (58.7%) with same-session EUS liver biopsy.

**Correlation with HVPG:** intraclass correlation coefficient 0.82; ENCOUNTER trial r=0.74 overall, and **r=0.91 vs direct transjugular portal pressure in post-[[tips|TIPS]] patients**. ENCOUNTER also found propofol-based general anesthesia substantially depresses HVPG vs no sedation — the effect of anesthesia on EUS-PPG is a research priority (S49).

## EUS-Guided Portal Vein Sampling

- The portal circulation carries venous drainage of the entire GI tract and ~**70% of vascular input to the liver**; portal blood may be enriched with metabolites, tumor cells, and xenobiotics filtered out by the liver before reaching peripheral blood.
- Small clinical series show EUS-guided portal venous sampling is **safe and feasible**. In an 18-patient single-center cohort with suspected pancreaticobiliary cancers, **100% of portal samples contained circulating tumor cells vs <25% of peripheral blood samples** — supporting "portal liquid biopsy" for studying metastasis.
- **Should be performed as part of a research protocol.** ([[aga-2023-interventional-eus-vascular]])

## Emerging Interventions

([[aga-2023-interventional-eus-vascular]])

- **Ectopic and rectal varices:** EUS targeting of rectal submucosal or penetrating vessels with sclerosants, coils, or cyanoacrylate — limited to single case reports. One series described glue ± coils for duodenal (n=3) and choledochal (n=5) varices. Larger series needed.
- **Splenic artery embolization** (portal hypertension with hypersplenism + recent variceal bleeding): 5 patients treated with combinations of endoscopic variceal ligation and EUS-guided cyanoacrylate and/or coils — **all had postprocedure fever and abdominal pain**, no splenic abscesses; post-procedure computed tomography (CT) portal venography showed reduced vein diameter, improved cytopenias, and a **mean splenic embolization rate of 65%**. Optimal anatomy/indications and endoscopic-vs-radiologic choice undefined.
- **Arterial bleeding and pseudoaneurysms** (visceral arteries inaccessible to IR due to difficult localization or vessel tortuosity — salvage role):

| Series | Target | n | Technical success | Adverse events | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coil + glue | Splenic artery pseudoaneurysm, 2.5–6.5 cm, after failed radiologic embolization | 6 | 100% | None | All 6 occluded at 3 months |
| Thrombin injection | Symptomatic aneurysms 1.9–5.0 cm — splenic (5), left hepatic (2), gastroduodenal (1) artery; unable to undergo embolization | 8 | 100% | 25% postprocedure abdominal pain requiring IV analgesia | Lumen obliteration 7/8 (88%), recurrence 1/8 (12%) at 12 weeks |

## Experimental

- **EUS-guided intrahepatic portosystemic shunt:** preclinical porcine studies deployed a self-expanding metal stent via EUS to bridge hepatic and portal vein, functioning as a portosystemic shunt (the [[tips|TIPS]] concept). Clinical translation requires a dedicated EUS-deliverable endovascular stent and identification of scenarios where EUS beats the IR approach ([[aga-2023-interventional-eus-vascular]]).

## Open Questions

([[aga-2023-interventional-eus-vascular]] unless marked)

- **Gastric variceal therapy:** technique is heterogeneous (varix vs perforator target, coil size/number, injectate) — consensus technique needed; larger multicenter data for technical success, safety, durability, generalizability; RCTs vs IR endovascular therapies (BRTO, TIPS); role in **primary prophylaxis** vs acute bleeding/secondary prophylaxis only; training pathways given how rare bleeding gastric varices are outside referral centers.
  - Since AGA 2023, ASGE 2026 has applied its EUS-guided-injection preference to high-risk gastric varices that have not bled (>10 mm) (meta-analysis plus two RCTs) and suggested it be considered over RTO for initial management (one multicenter propensity-matched analysis); both very low certainty ([[asge-2026-esophageal-gastric-varices]]).
- **EUS-PPG:** whether the thresholds for portal hypertension (HVPG >5 mm Hg) and clinically significant portal hypertension (HVPG ≥10 mm Hg) hold when acquired by EUS rather than the standard percutaneous endovascular approach; whether general anesthesia (vs conscious sedation used for transjugular HVPG) shifts absolute hepatic and portal vein pressures; head-to-head EUS-PPG vs HVPG trials warranted.
- Endosonographers should be trained in interventional EUS and operate as part of a **multidisciplinary team**.
