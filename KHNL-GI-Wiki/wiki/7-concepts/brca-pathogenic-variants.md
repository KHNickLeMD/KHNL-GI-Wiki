BRCA1 and BRCA2 are tumor-suppressor genes critical to homologous-recombination DNA repair; germline pathogenic variants confer hereditary breast/ovarian cancer risk and increased risk of [[pancreatic-cancer|pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma]]. BRCA1/2 variants are found in up to 7% of all pancreatic cancer patients. BRCA2 carries a higher pancreatic cancer risk (RR ~5.1; lifetime risk ~5.2%-7.4%) than BRCA1 (RR ~1.9; lifetime risk ~3.5%-3.8%). Per [[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening|ASGE 2022]], all BRCA1/2 carriers warrant [[pancreatic-cancer-screening|pancreatic cancer surveillance]] regardless of family history, starting at age 50 (or 10 years before the youngest affected relative). Homologous-recombination-deficient tumors respond to platinum-based chemotherapy and PARP inhibitors (e.g., olaparib).
