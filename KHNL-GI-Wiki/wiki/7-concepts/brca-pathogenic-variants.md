BRCA1 and BRCA2 encode proteins of homologous-recombination DNA repair. Germline pathogenic variants cause **hereditary breast–ovarian cancer syndrome**, which [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2015]] lists among the syndromes conferring [[pancreatic-cancer|pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma]] risk. In gastroenterology (GI), the decision they drive is whether to enter [[pancreatic-cancer-screening|pancreatic surveillance]] — and, per [[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening|American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2022]], **family history is no longer required**.

**Scope:** pancreatic and colorectal risk only. Breast/ovarian (and other non-GI) surveillance is not addressed by the GI guidelines and is not covered here.

## Magnitude of Pancreatic Cancer Risk

*ASGE 2022 threshold for "high risk": **relative risk (RR) >5 or lifetime risk >5%** (adopted by other guidelines and found cost-effective).*

| | BRCA2 | BRCA1 |
|---|---|---|
| Pooled **RR** | **5.1** (95% confidence interval [CI] 3.9–6.3) | **1.9** (95% CI 1.0–2.8) |
| Lifetime risk to age 80 (from RR) | **5.2%** | **3.5%** |
| Pooled **standardized incidence ratio (SIR)** | **7.2** (95% CI 1.5–13.0) | **3.7** (95% CI 2.5–4.8) |
| Lifetime risk to age 80 (from SIR) | **7.4%** | **3.8%** |

*Pooled estimates from [[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening|ASGE 2022]] (RR: 5 BRCA2 / 4 BRCA1 studies; SIR: 3 studies each).*

- **No sex difference** in pancreatic cancer risk for either gene (ASGE 2022).
- **Older estimates, same direction:** BRCA1 **~2-fold**, BRCA2 **3- to 9-fold** from registry data ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer|ACG 2015]] Table 12); BRCA2 **~2- to 6-fold** in carriers from high-risk families, BRCA1 "less convincing" (studies range from no increase to **up to 4-fold**) ([[aga-2020-brca-crc-pancreatic-screening|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2020]]).
- **Share of pancreatic cancer:** BRCA1/2 variants affect **up to 7%** of all patients with pancreatic cancer (ASGE 2022). AGA 2020 cites study ranges of **1.76%–10.7%** in unselected and **4.3%–24.4%** in familial pancreatic cancer, BRCA2 more often than BRCA1.
- **Why BRCA1 gets the same recommendation despite missing the 5% threshold** (ASGE 2022): few BRCA1 carriers and very few BRCA1-related cancers were studied (the low rate may reflect selection bias, not biology); the association was largely ignored before 2012, limiting long-term data; the CI reached **4.8%**, overlapping BRCA2's; **no difference in chemotherapy response** between BRCA1- and BRCA2-related cancers. Separate recommendations were judged unsupportable.

## Surveillance Decision

Every parameter needed to start a carrier on surveillance ([[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening|ASGE 2022]]):

| | ASGE 2022 |
|---|---|
| **Rec 3a — BRCA2** | Screen vs no screening (**conditional, very low quality**) |
| **Rec 3b — BRCA1** | Screen vs no screening (**conditional, very low quality**) |
| **Family history required?** | **No** — for either gene |
| **Start age (Rec 6a/6b)** | **Age 50** or **10 years earlier than the youngest relative with pancreatic cancer** (both genes) |
| **Interval (Rec 4)** | **Annual** (conditional, very low quality) — models show localized → advanced progression can occur within 1 year |
| **Modality (Rec 2)** | **[[endoscopic-ultrasound\|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]]**, **[[mri-mrcp\|magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)]]**, *or* EUS **alternating** with MRI — by patient preference and available expertise (conditional, very low quality). Choosing between them, and the lesion-based ladder that shortens the annual interval, are on [[pancreatic-cancer-screening]] |

*Rec 5 is the general statement that start age varies by genetic condition; Rec 6 lists the ages themselves.*

- Yield of screening in BRCA1/2 (8 studies, n=375, not reported separately by gene): **8.6%** (95% CI 4.5%–16.0%) for high-risk lesions. No BRCA1/2-specific harms data.

> [!pitfall] BRCA1: initiate screening with caution
> BRCA1 lifetime risk does not reach the 5% threshold → make carriers aware of their risk and the potential harms of screening first; screening may **not** be warranted if they place a high value on avoiding harms from medical interventions ([[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening|ASGE 2022]]).

## ⚠ Contradiction — Family History Requirement

| Source | Position |
|---|---|
| **[[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening\|ASGE 2022]]** (newer — **the position this page follows**) | Screen **all** BRCA1/2 carriers **regardless of family history**. Explicitly listed under "What Is New," noting previous guidelines limited screening to those with a family history |
| [[aga-2020-brca-crc-pancreatic-screening\|AGA 2020]] (older; a commentary — no numbered advice or grades of its own; the GRADE labels here are CAPS's) | Expert opinion: consider screening carriers **with a family history** of pancreatic cancer — but the criteria differ between the bodies it cites. Cancer of the Pancreas Screening (CAPS) Consortium: **93% consensus** to screen BRCA2 carriers with **≥1 affected first-degree relative, or ≥2 affected relatives of any degree** (Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation [GRADE] 2); **no consensus** for BRCA1 carriers with ≥1 affected first-degree relative (GRADE 3). National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN): an affected **first- *or* second-degree** relative, either gene. A carrier can be eligible under one and not the other |
| [[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer\|ACG 2015]] (older) | Rec 22: "Because of a lower relative risk … surveillance should be **limited to mutation carriers with a first- or second-degree relative affected with pancreatic cancer**" (conditional, very low quality) |

ASGE's rationale for dropping the requirement: family-history data in the risk studies were limited; several studies found **no association** between family history and pancreatic cancer risk in BRCA1/2; and in 71 BRCA-associated pancreatic cancers, a first- or second-degree family history was present in only **33%** — **almost 2 in 3 cancers would have been missed** by a family-history-gated strategy. Family history records are also incomplete, inaccurate, or uninformative in small families.

- **Where the guidelines agree:** start age **50, or 10 years before the youngest affected relative**, and **annual** imaging — ACG 2015 Rec 23: EUS **and/or** MRI of the pancreas annually from age 50 years, or 10 years younger than the earliest pancreatic cancer in the family ([[peutz-jeghers-syndrome|Peutz-Jeghers syndrome]] from 35).
- **Setting:** ACG 2015 (Rec 22) places surveillance ideally in experienced centers, multidisciplinary and under research conditions; AGA 2020 in a high-volume center, preferably in a research setting. ASGE 2022 states no such requirement.

## Colorectal Cancer Risk

*Source: [[aga-2020-brca-crc-pancreatic-screening|AGA 2020]], on [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer (CRC)]] risk in carriers.*

| | BRCA1 | BRCA2 |
|---|---|---|
| AGA 2020 bottom line | **limited** evidence of increased risk, magnitude **very modest** | **no** evidence of increased CRC risk |
| Meta-analysis (Oh 2018; 14 moderate-quality studies) | **odds ratio (OR) 1.49** (95% CI 1.19–1.85) — increased | **OR 1.10** (95% CI 0.77–1.58) — not increased |
| All BRCA carriers pooled | **OR 1.24** (95% CI 1.02–1.51) — small but statistically significant | |

- **Case-control studies** (5 large, Ashkenazi Jewish or Polish founder variants): no increased CRC risk overall; the largest found BRCA1 carriers' CRC at a younger mean age (**57.0 vs 64.0 years**, *P* = .05).
- **Cohort studies mixed:** one cohort of 7015 female carriers found no increase overall but an SIR of **4.76** (95% CI 2.21–9.0) in BRCA1 carriers **aged 30–49**; another found colon cancer risk raised (RR 2.03) but rectal cancer risk reduced (RR 0.23).
- **BRCA1/2 variants among CRC patients:** early-onset **1.3%**, high-risk **0.2%**, unselected **1.0%** — above chance, but **not proof of a causative role**.
- **Limits:** no study had data on CRC screening exposure; wide CIs; the young-onset signal rests on a subgroup analysis of a single study.

## Colorectal Screening Decision

**No change to colorectal screening on carrier status alone** ([[aga-2020-brca-crc-pancreatic-screening|AGA 2020]]):

| Carrier presentation | Action |
|---|---|
| First-degree relative with CRC **or advanced adenoma** | Offer [[colorectal-cancer-screening\|CRC screening]] at **age 40** — per current family-history-based recommendations, not because of carrier status |
| **Symptoms**, e.g. **rectal bleeding** or **[[iron-deficiency-anemia\|iron deficiency anemia]]** | Consider **expedited diagnostic evaluation** |
| No signs/symptoms **and** no family history of CRC or advanced adenoma | **Average-risk guidelines**, until evidence clarifies whether alternate strategies are warranted |

> [!pearl] Why carrier status alone doesn't change CRC screening
> Current guidelines start earlier or more intensive screening at a **2-fold** risk (one first-degree relative with CRC); the **1.5-fold** risk in BRCA1 carriers is not clearly enough. [[lynch-syndrome|Lynch syndrome]] risk is **up to 17-fold** ([[aga-2020-brca-crc-pancreatic-screening|AGA 2020]]).

## Other Points

- **Genetic testing panel for suspected [[familial-pancreatic-cancer|familial pancreatic cancer (FPC)]]** should include **BRCA1/2, CDKN2A, PALB2, ATM** ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer|ACG 2015]]); consider Peutz-Jeghers, Lynch syndrome, and [[hereditary-pancreatitis|hereditary pancreatitis]] genes if other personal/family-history criteria for those syndromes are met.
- Yield of BRCA1 and BRCA2 germline mutations in FPC kindreds **without** criteria for a known syndrome: **0–6% each** ([[acg-2015-hereditary-gi-cancer|ACG 2015]] Table 13).
- **Therapeutic relevance:** tumors with deficient homologous recombination from abnormalities such as BRCA1/2 are **responsive to both platinum-based chemotherapy and poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors**, with increased pathologic response in borderline-resectable disease and prolonged survival in advanced disease ([[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening|ASGE 2022]]).
