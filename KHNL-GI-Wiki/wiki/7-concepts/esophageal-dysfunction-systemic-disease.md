Framework for the **esophageal manifestations of systemic autoimmune, eosinophilic, and dermatologic disease**, and for **secondary (mimic) achalasia**. Underrecognized causes of dysphagia, odynophagia, chest pain, and [[gerd|heartburn]]. Endoscopy is frequently unrevealing — rings, texture changes, exudates, strictures, nonspecific mucosa, or a normal-appearing esophagus — so diagnosis rests on a high index of suspicion, directed history, and targeted testing.

*For the localized entities, see [[eosinophilic-esophagitis|eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE)]], [[lymphocytic-esophagitis|lymphocytic esophagitis]], and [[infectious-esophagitis|infectious esophagitis]]. Symptom workup: [[dysphagia]].*

## When to Suspect

- Initial evaluation of any esophageal symptom includes **history + physical directed at concurrent autoimmune or allergic disease** and at **risks for immunosuppression** (which raise infection risk). (Best Practice Advice [BPA] 1)
- Esophageal symptoms with **no explanatory endoscopic or reflux finding**, or **persisting despite standard therapy** — identify the inflammatory/infectious risks and investigate them as the cause. Symptoms persisting after treatment of [[infectious-esophagitis|infectious esophagitis]] are handled on that page.
- Known or suspected **rheumatologic, allergic, or dermatologic disease**; peripheral **eosinophilia**; travel/exposure in Central or South America; recent **coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19)**.
- **Peripheral eosinophilia (absolute eosinophil count [AEC] >1500 cells/µL)** with esophageal symptoms → work up non-EoE eosinophilic gastrointestinal (GI) disease, hypereosinophilic syndrome (HES), and eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (EGPA); consult allergy/immunology. (BPA 6)
- Collaboration with rheumatology, dermatology, allergy/immunology, or infectious disease improves diagnosis and treatment.
- How many esophageal biopsies to take, and when and how to take them, is not defined for these conditions — no society guidance exists for evaluating extra-esophageal or systemic causes of esophageal dysfunction.

## Eosinophilic Systemic Disease

| Entity | Defining criteria | Esophageal relevance | Next steps |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HES** | **AEC >1500 cells/µL** *plus* organ/tissue damage from eosinophilic infiltration, with no other explanation | Up to **38%** have GI symptoms; esophageal involvement can mimic [[eosinophilic-esophagitis\|EoE]] — the discriminator is **peripheral eosinophilia**, which is rare in isolated EoE | Screen upper **and** lower GI tract for involvement; monitor for skin, lung, heart, neurologic involvement |
| **EGPA** (formerly Churg-Strauss) | Rare systemic eosinophilic vasculitis of small–medium arteries; 3 clinical stages — (1) prodromal (allergic rhinitis, nasal polyposis, asthma) → (2) eosinophilic (blood eosinophilia + tissue infiltration) → (3) vasculitic (necrotizing vasculitis, multi-organ dysfunction). Only **30–40%** are antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody (ANCA)-positive | Esophageal symptoms **rare** — from eosinophilic tissue infiltration or from vasculitic neuropathy | Vasculitic change is **not always seen on histology**, so a negative biopsy does not exclude it; allergy and/or rheumatology input |

- Other eosinophilic GI diseases (eosinophilic gastritis, eosinophilic enteritis) can also raise the blood eosinophil count and can involve the esophagus — peripheral eosinophilia does not by itself mean HES.

## Connective Tissue / Rheumatologic Disease

- Mechanism: involvement of the **esophageal muscle layer** → dysmotility and/or an **incompetent lower esophageal sphincter (LES)**. Degree of dysfunction is **often especially significant in systemic sclerosis (SSc) or mixed connective tissue disease (MCTD)**. (BPA 7)
- Objective test: [[high-resolution-manometry|high-resolution manometry (HRM)]].

| Disease | Muscle involved | Esophageal phenotype |
|---|---|---|
| **SSc** | Smooth muscle — atrophy and fibrosis | **Severe hypomotility + incompetent LES**, commonly seen on HRM; the classic and best-recognized example |
| **MCTD** | **Both striated and smooth** | Overlap syndrome (features of SSc, systemic lupus erythematosus [SLE], polymyositis, and/or rheumatoid arthritis); can resemble SSc; manifests **proximally and distally**; involvement may be **more variable and less severe than SSc** |
| **Sjögren's** | Secretory (sicca) dysfunction from lacrimal/salivary gland destruction, plus dysmotility | Extraglandular manifestations common — up to **80%** report dysphagia |
| **SLE** | Dysmotility, mechanism unclear | Variable findings on objective testing |
| **Myositis** — polymyositis, dermatomyositis, inclusion body myositis | **Proximal striated muscle**, with the **cricopharyngeus (CP)** often involved | **30–80%** experience dysphagia; CP-directed endoscopic therapy — **dilation, botulinum toxin injection, or myotomy** — often has an important role |

- Literature on the esophageal manifestations of MCTD, Sjögren's, SLE, and myositis is sparse compared with SSc.

## Esophageal Crohn's Disease

- A **minority** of patients with [[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease]]: inflammatory, stricturing, or fistulizing change with **granulomas** on histology. (BPA 8)
- Uncommon in adults; described more often in **children — one study reported 20%**.
- Suspect in a patient with **active disease at multiple sites** who reports dysphagia and has **superficial or deep esophageal mucosal ulceration**.
- Esophageal involvement **requires biologic therapy**.

## Dermatologic Disease

- Dysphagia can arise from **endoscopically visible esophageal mucosal involvement, even without dermal manifestations**. (BPA 9)
- Consider it when a patient with dysphagia has endoscopic findings that look atypical.

### Esophageal Lichen Planus

- Lichen planus (LP) can affect oral, esophageal, and genital mucosa; it is a small fraction of all skin disease but the **most common dermatologic disorder to affect the esophagus**.
- **Esophageal LP (ELP) occurs in isolation relatively often** — in a retrospective series of 40 patients, **13 (33%) had esophageal involvement alone**; coexisting cutaneous involvement in only **10%**, and concomitant oral or genital involvement in **<50%**.
- Strong predilection for **Caucasian females, typically middle or older age**. Dysphagia is common.
- Endoscopy: **narrowed caliber of the majority of the esophagus** (especially proximal and middle) with **multiple strictures**; **pale, edematous mucosa with peeling/sloughing on contact** with the scope; **thick white exudates**.
- Histology: **Civatte bodies** (keratinocyte necrosis producing a vacuolated appearance) are characteristic, but **<50% of biopsies are diagnostic** — ELP is hard to define histopathologically.
- Direct immunofluorescence (DIF): used mainly to **exclude other dermatologic disease that involves the esophagus** (e.g., pemphigus vulgaris [PV]). **Fibrinogen deposition in the epithelial basement membrane is suggestive — not diagnostic — of ELP.**
- **Increased risk of [[esophageal-cancer|esophageal cancer]].**
- Treatment: algorithms are not fully defined. **Swallowed topical corticosteroids benefit >60%**; non-responders will likely need **systemic immunosuppressants**, with dermatology consultation.

### Pemphigus Vulgaris

- **Most common bullous disorder**; affects males and females **equally**, often **ages 40–60**.
- In a prospective study of 28 patients with oral PV, **18 had dysphagia** with objective esophageal involvement: **erythema, red longitudinal lines, erosions, blisters, and/or ulcers**, all at similar rates (ulcers in only one patient).
- **DIF positive for immunoglobulin G (IgG) and C3 deposition is diagnostic of PV.** In 26 patients with PV and esophageal symptoms, only **12 were DIF-positive** — a negative DIF does not exclude it.
- Endoscopic findings are otherwise nonspecific; **red longitudinal lines** were the most common. Isolated esophageal PV is described only in a few case reports.
- During biopsy, pemphigus may show **sloughing with dislodgement of epithelium**, analogous to the cutaneous Nikolsky's sign.

### Tissue Handling for Direct Immunofluorescence

- Take DIF samples from **both involved and nearby uninvolved mucosa**, in addition to routine hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) biopsies.
- Place DIF biopsies **directly onto saline-soaked non-woven gauze/sponge**, then into a container for processing in a **suitable medium (i.e., Michel's)**.
- Send to a pathology center where **dermatopathology expertise** is available.

## Secondary (Mimic) Achalasia

- Consider **infectious and inflammatory causes of secondary achalasia at the initial evaluation** of an esophageal motility disorder. Query for **recent COVID-19 infection, risks for Chagas disease, and symptoms or signs of eosinophilic disease**. (BPA 10) See [[achalasia]].

### Autoimmune Association

- Prevailing hypothesis: achalasia is an **autoimmune disease targeting esophageal myenteric neurons**.
- Compared with the general population, patients with achalasia were more likely to carry autoimmune conditions — including type 1 diabetes mellitus, Sjögren's, and SLE — **odds ratio (OR) 3.6 (95% confidence interval [CI] 2.5–5.3)**.
- Large claims database (6769 achalasia cases vs 27,076 controls): autoimmune conditions associated with **OR 1.26 (95% CI 1.11–1.42)** for achalasia.
- German case-control study (1141 cases vs 3423 matched controls): **OR 1.49 (95% CI 1.23–1.80)**.
- In both database studies the **strongest associations were SSc and Addison's disease**.

### Eosinophilic and Mast-Cell Disease

- Esophagectomy specimens and esophageal muscle biopsies show **dense eosinophil accumulation in the muscularis propria**.
- In 844 patients with achalasia, the estimated **relative risk of EoE was 32.9 (95% CI 24.8–42.8)**, higher in those **aged ≤40 years**; relative risk of atopic/allergic disorders was also increased.
- In EoE, eosinophils and mast cells release neuroactive, myoactive, pro-inflammatory, and cytotoxic products that can cause motility disturbance and/or the neuronal destruction characteristic of achalasia.
- Esophageal eosinophilia can also be a consequence of food stasis — but where **suspicion for comorbid EoE is high, treating the eosinophilic infiltration is prudent and may improve esophageal function**.

### Chagas Disease

- *Trypanosoma cruzi* causes esophageal dysfunction by **immune cross-reactivity between the flagellar antigen and the myenteric plexus**; myenteric plexus degeneration → **loss of peristalsis and impaired LES relaxation**.
- GI manifestations (mega-esophagus, mega-colon) occur in **10–15% of chronically infected individuals**; an estimated 300,000 people are affected in the United States.
- **Serologic testing reliably diagnoses *T. cruzi* infection and excludes idiopathic achalasia.**

### Post-COVID-19 Achalasia

- **Acute-onset achalasia after COVID-19 infection** has been reported; severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) messenger RNA presence correlated with a rise in the inflammatory markers NLRP3 and tumor necrosis factor.
- It is **not known whether the motility disturbance is reversible**, so whether observation is appropriate is unclear. A case series of acute-onset post-COVID-19 achalasia had **favorable short-term outcomes after myotomy**.

### Paraneoplastic Achalasia

- Achalasia has been reported as a paraneoplastic phenomenon. Apart from esophageal adenocarcinoma, the malignancies most often associated with secondary achalasia are **lymphoma, lung cancer, and breast cancer**.

## Evaluation by Pertinent History

| Pertinent history | Diagnostic considerations | Tests to consider | Supportive endoscopic findings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recent antibiotics | Candidal esophagitis; medication-induced ulceration (i.e., doxycycline) | [[upper-endoscopy\|Upper endoscopy (EGD)]] with biopsies and brushings if nummular lesions identified | Whitish nummular lesions (candida); 1–2 well-marked ulcerations for pill-induced esophagitis |
| Immunosuppression | Candidal esophagitis; herpes simplex virus (HSV); cytomegalovirus (CMV) | EGD with biopsies/brushings; consider infectious disease consult if infection identified | Whitish nummular lesions (candida); shallow ulcers (HSV); deep ulceration(s) (CMV) |
| Recent COVID-19 infection | Post-COVID-19 dysmotility/achalasia | EGD to rule out other diagnoses; HRM; timed barium esophagram with pill | Most likely normal-appearing esophagus |
| Bullous dermatoses — pemphigoid, pemphigus | Esophageal involvement of bullous dermatoses | EGD with biopsies for routine H&E **plus** additional biopsies for DIF, sent to a pathology center of excellence (dermatopathology expertise is of value) | Sloughing/blistering of tissues, erythema, red longitudinal lines, erosions; sloughing with dislodgement of epithelium in pemphigus during biopsy, similar to the skin Nikolsky's sign |
| LP — oral, dermatologic, genital; middle–older-age female | Esophageal involvement | EGD with biopsies for routine H&E **plus** additional biopsies for DIF, sent to a pathology center of excellence | Narrowed caliber of the majority of the esophagus; pale, edematous mucosa with peeling/sloughing on scope contact; thick white exudates; nonspecific short or pan-esophageal stenoses |
| Atopic dermatitis, asthma, or significant food allergy/atopy | EoE | EGD with a minimum of 3–4 biopsies from distal esophagus and 3–4 from proximal esophagus, off [[proton-pump-inhibitors\|proton pump inhibitor (PPI)]] therapy ≥2–4 weeks prior; take further history to rule out HES or EGPA | Edema, rings, exudates, furrows, some with stenoses (part or all of esophagus) — can appear normal |
| HES or EGPA | Esophageal involvement similar to EoE | EGD with biopsies | Can be similar to EoE, or may appear normal |
| Autoimmune disease — Crohn's, scleroderma, lupus, MCTD, Sjögren's, polymyositis, dermatomyositis, inclusion body myositis | Esophageal involvement of extra-esophageal autoimmune disease, via inflammatory or dysmotility mechanisms | EGD with biopsies; HRM | Ulcerations (Crohn's); dilation with reflux changes (scleroderma); nonspecific edema and crepe-paper-appearing tissue with nonspecific stenoses (Sjögren's). HRM in SSc frequently shows absent contractility with a low-amplitude LES. Cricopharyngeal involvement common in myositis |
| Prior travel or exposures in Central/South America | Chagas disease with potential achalasia | Infectious disease consult if diagnosis positive; EGD with biopsies to rule out other diagnoses; HRM | Normal mucosal appearance vs chronic stasis changes |
| Female >50 years old with dysphagia often mimicking EoE on endoscopy | [[lymphocytic-esophagitis\|Lymphocytic esophagitis]] | EGD with biopsies for routine H&E, off PPI ≥2–4 weeks prior | Overlapping features with EoE; can be normal |
