Framework for the **esophageal manifestations of systemic autoimmune, eosinophilic, and dermatologic disease**, and for **secondary (mimic) achalasia**. These are underrecognized causes of dysphagia, odynophagia, chest pain, and [[gerd|heartburn]]; endoscopic findings are often nonspecific (rings, edema, exudates, strictures, or a normal esophagus), so diagnosis depends on a high index of suspicion, directed history, and targeted testing.

*For the localized entities, see [[eosinophilic-esophagitis]], [[lymphocytic-esophagitis]], and [[infectious-esophagitis]]. Symptom workup: [[dysphagia]].*

## When to Suspect

- Esophageal symptoms **out of proportion** to endoscopic/reflux findings, or persisting despite standard therapy.
- Known or suspected **rheumatologic, allergic, or dermatologic disease**; peripheral **eosinophilia**; travel/exposure history (Chagas); recent **coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19)**.
- **Peripheral eosinophilia (absolute eosinophil count [AEC] >1500 cells/µL)** with esophageal symptoms → work up eosinophilic gastrointestinal (GI) disease other than eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE), hypereosinophilic syndrome, and eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (EGPA); involve allergy/immunology. (Best Practice Advice [BPA] 6)

## Eosinophilic Systemic Disease

| Entity | Key feature | Esophageal relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **Hypereosinophilic syndrome (HES)** | AEC **>1500 cells/µL** + organ damage from eosinophilic infiltration | Up to 38% have GI symptoms; can mimic [[eosinophilic-esophagitis\|EoE]] — distinguished by **peripheral eosinophilia** (rare in isolated EoE). Screen for other organ (skin/lung/heart/neuro) involvement. |
| **EGPA (Churg-Strauss)** | Eosinophilic vasculitis; 3 stages (allergic prodrome → eosinophilic → vasculitic); only **30–40% antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody (ANCA)-positive** | Esophageal symptoms rare (eosinophilic infiltration or vasculitic neuropathy); allergy/rheumatology input. |

## Connective Tissue / Rheumatologic Disease (BPA 7)

Esophageal symptoms arise from involvement of the esophageal **muscle layer** → dysmotility ± incompetent lower esophageal sphincter (LES) (often seen on [[high-resolution-manometry]]).

- **Systemic sclerosis (SSc)** — atrophy/fibrosis of esophageal smooth muscle → **severe hypomotility + incompetent LES**; classic and most significant.
- **Mixed connective tissue disease (MCTD)** — overlap syndrome; can resemble SSc; affects **both striated and smooth muscle** (proximal + distal), variable severity. Dysfunction especially significant in SSc and MCTD.
- **Sjögren's** — secretory (sicca) dysfunction plus dysmotility; up to **80% report dysphagia**.
- **Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE)** — dysmotility of unclear mechanism, variable objective findings.
- **Myositis** (polymyositis, dermatomyositis, inclusion body myositis) — **proximal striated muscle + cricopharyngeus**; **30–80% dysphagia**; may need cricopharyngeus (CP)-directed **dilation, botulinum toxin, or myotomy**.

## Esophageal Crohn's Disease (BPA 8)

- Uncommon in adults (up to **20%** in children); inflammatory, stricturing, or fistulizing changes with **granulomas** histologically.
- Occurs with **active intestinal disease** at multiple sites; superficial/deep esophageal ulcerations.
- Requires **biologic therapy**. See [[crohns-disease]].

## Dermatologic Disease (BPA 9)

Dysphagia can occur from endoscopically visible esophageal mucosal involvement, **even without skin manifestations**.

- **Esophageal lichen planus (ELP)** — the **most common** dermatologic disease to affect the esophagus; **can occur in isolation (33% esophagus-only)**; predilection for **Caucasian females, middle/older age**. Findings: proximal-to-mid esophageal narrowing/multiple strictures, pale edematous mucosa with **peeling/sloughing on contact**, thick white exudates. Biopsy: **Civatte bodies** (<50% diagnostic); direct immunofluorescence (DIF) mainly to exclude other disease. **Increased [[esophageal-cancer|esophageal cancer]] risk.** Treat with swallowed topical steroids (benefit >60%); systemic immunosuppression if refractory.
- **Pemphigus vulgaris (PV)** — most common bullous disorder (ages 40–60); erythema, red longitudinal lines, erosions, blisters/ulcers. **DIF positive for IgG + C3 is diagnostic.** Send biopsies for DIF from both involved and adjacent uninvolved mucosa (place on saline-soaked gauze / Michel's medium).

## Secondary (Mimic) Achalasia (BPA 10)

- Consider **infectious and inflammatory causes** of achalasia at initial evaluation.
- Query for: **recent COVID infection, Chagas disease risk, and eosinophilic/mast-cell disease**.
- Achalasia is hypothesized to be **autoimmune** (targeting esophageal myenteric neurons) — patients have higher odds of autoimmune conditions (odds ratio [OR] up to 3.6 in one study; OR 1.26 in a large claims database). See [[achalasia]].
