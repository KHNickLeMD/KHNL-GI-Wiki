Clinical framework for the endoscopic classification, resection, and surveillance of gastric mucosal polyps. Core principle: evaluate **both the polyp and the surrounding/background mucosa**, since field changes ([[helicobacter-pylori-infection|H. pylori]] gastritis, [[atrophic-gastritis|autoimmune/atrophic gastritis]], [[gastric-intestinal-metaplasia|GIM]]) drive malignant risk and surveillance.

## Subtypes

Different polyp types may **coexist** in the same patient (2%–3%) and each associates with a characteristic background mucosa.

| Type | Typical site / appearance | Background / association | Malignant potential |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fundic gland polyp (FGP)** | Body/fundus; multiple, <1 cm, glassy, sessile | PPI-associated or sporadic; or [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis|FAP]]/GAPPS; surrounding mucosa usually normal | <1% sporadic; 25%–48% in FAP-associated; high in GAPPS. Risk ↑ with mucosal "carpeting" or >1 cm |
| **Gastric hyperplastic polyp (GHP)** | Antrum/anywhere; inflamed mucosa | H. pylori gastritis, atrophic gastritis, GIM | Dysplasia ~4%; transformation 0.8%–10% (↑ if >2 cm); adjacent mucosa may harbor dysplasia |
| **Gastric adenoma (GA)** | Intestinal (56%) & foveolar (41%) most common | GIM/atrophic gastritis field | Adenocarcinoma precursor; risk ↑ with size (esp. >20 mm). Foveolar "raspberry-like" GA in H. pylori-naïve stomach: very low risk |
| **Pyloric gland adenoma** | — | Autoimmune gastritis | High-grade dysplasia up to 42%; adenocarcinoma 12%–47%; ↑ with size, tubulo-villous architecture |
| **Hamartomatous polyp** | — | Polyposis syndromes | Histologically similar to GHP — careful classification needed |
| **G-NET (gastric neuroendocrine tumor)** | ECL-cell proliferation | Type 1 (autoimmune/atrophic gastritis), others | Type-dependent |

- Western prevalence ~6%–8% of endoscopies; 70%–94% are FGPs or GHPs. Subtypes have shifted toward **sporadic FGPs** as H. pylori prevalence falls and PPI use rises; FGP prevalence falls after age 60, whereas GA/GHP/G-NET rise with age.

## Evaluation at Endoscopy

- **Systematic inspection** of polyps **and** surrounding mucosa with high-definition white light + enhanced imaging (virtual chromoendoscopy); recognize and **photo-document** polyp and background-mucosa features.
- **Biopsy the polyp, biopsy the surrounding mucosa**, and resect as appropriate — surrounding-mucosa pathology (H. pylori gastritis, autoimmune gastritis, GIM) determines subsequent management.
- Suspected GIM or atrophic gastritis in surrounding mucosa → **targeted biopsies per existing protocols** (see [[gastric-intestinal-metaplasia]], [[gastric-premalignant-conditions]]).
- **Test and treat [[helicobacter-pylori-infection|H. pylori]]** in all patients with adenomatous or hyperplastic polyps.
- **PPIs need not be stopped** when FGPs are documented in a patient with a valid PPI indication.

## Resection

- Techniques: snare and biopsy forceps, [[polypectomy-emr|endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR)]], or [[endoscopic-submucosal-dissection|ESD]].
- With **numerous polyps of varied size**: resect the **largest** when possible; sample or resect the smaller ones.

## Surveillance

Surveillance is keyed to **polyp histology + background mucosa**:

- **Complete resection of a confirmed dysplastic polyp:** repeat endoscopy at **1 year** (low-grade dysplasia) or **6 months** (high-grade dysplasia).
- **Polyp only biopsied or incompletely resected:** repeat within **3 months** (high-grade) or **6 months** (low-grade).
- **Adjacent mucosa with GIM and/or atrophic gastritis:** surveillance advised (per GIM/atrophic-gastritis protocols).
