Small bowel motility is one of several mechanisms that keep the small intestine relatively sterile. Its failure produces stasis, and stasis is the common final pathway to [[small-intestinal-bacterial-overgrowth|small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO)]] — which [[acg-2020-sibo|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2020]] frames as "in almost all instances an epiphenomenon related to something else."

## Migrating Motor Complex (MMC)

- The small bowel has an **inherent cleansing function** — recurring antegrade peristalsis plus migrating motor complexes (MMCs), **organized into 3 phases**.
- **Phase III MMC** is the phase that matters for overgrowth: an **intense phasic and tonic contractile event** that begins in the **stomach or proximal bowel** and sweeps toward the colon, **propelling chyme, secretions, and bacteria** — cleansing the small intestine of debris **during fasting** ([[acg-2020-sibo]]).
- The relationship runs both ways: excess methane itself **slows small bowel transit** in animal and in vitro models — see [[intestinal-methanogen-overgrowth]].
- [[acg-2020-sibo]] describes only phase III; it gives no MMC cycle length and does not characterize phases I and II.

## Mechanisms Maintaining Small Bowel Ecology

*Motility is one of six — deficiency or breakdown in any one of them can let bacteria accumulate abnormally in the small bowel ([[acg-2020-sibo]] Table 3).*

| Mechanism | How it protects |
|---|---|
| **Gastric acid** | Most ingested bacteria in food cannot survive the acidic stomach |
| **Pancreatic enzymes** | Digest bacterial products; efficient nutrient digestion leaves less substrate for bacteria |
| **Bile acids** | Act as detergents on bacterial membranes |
| **Small bowel motility** | MMCs and other events cleanse the small intestine of debris during fasting |
| **Ileocecal valve** | Blocks retrograde movement of colonic flora into the small bowel |
| **Immune system** | Mucosal immunity maintains a stable luminal microbiota |

## Causes of Impaired Motility

**Three routes by which the phase III MMC itself fails** ([[acg-2020-sibo]]):

- **Neuropathy or myopathy** — e.g. scleroderma (see [[esophageal-dysfunction-systemic-disease]]), diabetic autonomic neuropathy
- **Drugs that reduce propulsive movement** — opioids, antidiarrheals, anticholinergics
- **Post-surgical** — gastrojejunostomy **with a blind loop**; injury to the **vagus nerve**

**Advanced age and female sex** also carry a higher likelihood of SIBO, **perhaps from delays in gut transit** ([[acg-2020-sibo]]).

Anatomic lesions (blind loops, strictures, diverticulosis, ileocecal valve resection) produce stasis **without** a motility defect; the full disorder list sorted by mechanism is [[aga-2020-sibo]] Table 1, on [[small-intestinal-bacterial-overgrowth]].

> [!pearl] "Consider SIBO any time there is small intestinal stasis"
> The practical rule both societies converge on ([[aga-2020-sibo]] Table 3). Motility failure is one route to stasis; anatomy, surgery and strictures are others, and the rule does not care which produced it.

⚠ **Strictures causing partial or fixed obstruction** are an obvious theoretical cause of stasis, but [[acg-2020-sibo]] states there are **no solid peer-reviewed publications validating** them as a SIBO risk factor — while [[aga-2020-sibo]] Table 1 lists strictures ([[crohns-disease\|Crohn's disease]], radiation, surgery) as an anatomic cause without that caveat. Both are 2020 society documents; the disagreement is unresolved.

## Testing and Prevention in Dysmotility

- **Symptomatic patient with a suspected motility disorder → glucose hydrogen or lactulose hydrogen breath test** ([[acg-2020-sibo]] Recommendation 2; *conditional recommendation, very low level of evidence*). Substrate doses, positivity cutoffs and test preparation: [[small-intestinal-bacterial-overgrowth]].
- **Prevention is correction of the underlying cause**, not repeated antibiotic courses ([[acg-2020-sibo]] Key Concept 14; [[aga-2020-sibo]] Best Practice Advice (BPA) 7 — identify and correct the cause where possible, correct nutritional deficits, then antibiotics).
- **Prokinetics are not addressed** as prevention or treatment by [[acg-2020-sibo]] or [[aga-2020-sibo]]; promotility agents appear only as a pre-test exclusion — stop them **≥1 week** before breath testing.

## Association With IBS

- SIBO and [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)]] overlap heavily, but **cause vs effect is unresolved** and the reported prevalence depends almost entirely on which test was used. [[acg-2020-sibo]] suggests breath testing for SIBO in IBS (*conditional recommendation, very low level of evidence*); [[aga-2020-sibo]] BPA 6 holds the role of SIBO in functional symptoms to be still controversial. **The prevalence figures, the test-dependence that explains their spread, and the test thresholds are all on [[small-intestinal-bacterial-overgrowth]]** — read them there rather than from a single headline percentage.
