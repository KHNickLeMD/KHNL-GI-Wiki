### Why Almost Everything Is Graded 3 at >98%

- **Grade 3 = expert opinion only** on the task force's own 8-level scheme (1A / 1B / 1C+ / 1C / 2A / 2B / 2C / 3), **not GRADE**. Fifteen of the 19 carry it. See [[ercp-quality-indicators#Strength of Recommendation Scheme]] for the full grid, which is identical across the series.
- **The target is >98% because the task force considered failure to perform most of these a "never event"** — only very unusual circumstances should excuse a miss. That is the opposite logic from the colonoscopy and ERCP sets: here, variation in practice was **deliberately not** the basis for inclusion, because these activities are expected to happen almost universally.
- Indicators and targets **do not reflect the standard of care, credentialing requirements or training standards**, and applying them that way is a misuse of the document.

> ⚠ **Three internal inconsistencies, carried as published.** Indicator 1 is graded **1C+** in the table and **1C** in its own text block; indicator 6's target is **>95%** in the table and **95%** in the text; indicator 19 is an **outcome** measure in the table and a **process** measure in the text.

## Sedation

- **The plan names the intended level** — none, minimal, moderate, deep (monitored anesthesia care) or general anesthesia — and is **documented before sedation is initiated**.
- **Risk stratification:** **American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) score** (1 = normal, 5 = critically ill with substantial risk of death within 24 h) and the **Mallampati score** are the two in common use. **ASA scores predict adverse events during endoscopy** in large database studies, mostly sedation-related. **Mallampati has not been rigorously validated for endoscopy** — it is used because deep sedation is widespread and urgent airway management is possible.
- **Even when an anesthesiologist sedates, endoscopist input can be critical** to clarify risk-to-benefit and inform the anesthesia approach.
- **Monitoring:** pulse oximetry, heart rate and blood pressure in **all** sedated procedures, with **blood pressure and heart rate recorded at intervals of ≤5 minutes**. **Electrocardiographic monitoring is indicated for deep sedation and general anesthesia.** **Capnography** reduces apnea and hypoxemia in randomized trials of propofol deep sedation and **can be considered**.
- **Fasting (ASA):** clear liquids **2 h** · milk **6 h** · light meal **6 h** · fried or fatty meal **8 h**. **Gastroparesis, achalasia and bowel obstruction may need longer** to minimize aspiration risk.
- **Essential medications may be taken**, anticoagulation possibly excepted — and so may the bowel preparation. Residual gastric fluid after colonoscopy prep is **minimal (<25 mL)**, whether the prep was split or taken entirely the evening before.
- **No nothing-by-mouth rule is set for glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists** or other weight-loss drugs that delay gastric emptying; the document says policy will evolve as data emerge.

> [!pearl] Sedate to the level you can rescue from
> Sedation is a continuum and individual response is unpredictable. Whoever gives **moderate** sedation must be able to rescue a patient who slips into **deep** sedation; whoever gives **deep** sedation must be able to rescue one who reaches **general anesthesia**.

> [!pitfall] Never use indicator 11 as a punitive metric
> Reversal agents and premature termination are **potentially life-saving and can represent excellent clinical judgement**. Penalizing their use could have devastating unintended consequences. Review such events **non-punitively**, inside process improvement, never as an indirect outcome measure — the aim is fewer events that require interruption, **not fewer interruptions**.

## Antithrombotic Management

Detail and agent-specific timing live on [[anticoagulation-gi-bleeding]]; the quality-relevant rules:

- **Diagnostic procedures, including those with mucosal biopsy sampling, are low bleeding risk — do not stop antiplatelets or anticoagulants.**
- **Most high-bleeding-risk therapeutic cases require anticoagulant cessation**, absent extenuating circumstances.
- **Some traditional interruptions may be unnecessary** — notably **cold snare resection of small polyps on clopidogrel**.
- **Almost all endoscopy is safe on 81–325 mg daily aspirin**, and current guidance is **against interrupting aspirin taken for secondary cardiovascular prevention**. Aspirin is **frequently stopped inappropriately**, and education fixes it — a ready quality-improvement target for a unit.
- **High thromboembolic risk** (may need bridging, deferral or specialist input): **atrial fibrillation with another cardiac condition** (heart failure, valvular disease) · **prior thromboembolism** · **mechanical mitral valve** · **coronary stent within a year** · **acute coronary syndrome** · **nonstented percutaneous coronary intervention after myocardial infarction**.
- **Resume immediately after most nontherapeutic procedures.** After a high-bleeding-risk intervention, individualize on the therapy performed and the thromboembolic risk.

## Antibiotic Prophylaxis

**GI endoscopy is not a significant risk factor for clinically important bacteremia.** Full regimens and the ERCP-specific stance are on [[antibiotic-prophylaxis-endoscopy]].

| Not recommended | Recommended |
|---|---|
| Endocarditis prevention — **even at highest cardiac risk** | [[ercp]] with **anticipated incomplete** biliary drainage (e.g. [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis\|PSC]]) |
| Cardiac conditions, synthetic vascular grafts, nonvalvular cardiovascular devices | ERCP **after orthotopic liver transplantation** |
| Biliary obstruction **without** cholangitis, ERCP with **anticipated complete** drainage | ERCP with [[cholangioscopy]] |
| **Solid** upper-GI and colonic lesions undergoing EUS-guided fine-needle sampling | Any endoscopy in **[[cirrhosis]] with acute GI hemorrhage** |
| **Prosthetic joints**, any procedure | **[[enteral-access\|Percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy]] in all patients** |
| | Colonoscopy or flexible sigmoidoscopy on **peritoneal dialysis** |
| | EUS-guided fine-needle aspiration of a **mediastinal or pancreatic cystic** lesion — **controversial** |

**Judicious use to avoid unnecessary antibiotics is an equal aim of this indicator**, not just giving them where indicated.

## Adverse Events

**Definition:** an event that **prevents completion of the planned procedure**, or causes **hospital admission, prolongation of an existing stay, another invasive procedure, or subsequent medical consultation**.

**Timing:** preprocedure · intraprocedure · **postprocedure (up to 14 days)** · **late (after 14 days)**.

**Grade two things for every event:** **certainty of attribution** — definite / probable / possible / unlikely — and **severity** — mild / moderate / severe / fatal, by the disturbance caused and any change in the plan of care. Events recognized after the report is closed go in as an **addendum linked to the original report**.

| Event | Rate |
|---|---|
| Cardiopulmonary, general endoscopy | **0.01%–1.1%** |
| Cardiopulmonary, [[ercp\|ERCP]] | **up to 5%** |
| Perforation, diagnostic [[upper-endoscopy\|upper endoscopy]] | **~1 in 25,000** |
| Perforation, therapeutic upper endoscopy | **up to 1%** |
| Perforation, screening [[colonoscopy]] | **generally <0.1%** |
| Perforation, [[endoscopic-ultrasound\|EUS]] and ERCP | **0.1%–0.6%** |
| Bleeding, ERCP **with sphincterotomy** | **0.3%–2%** |
| Bleeding, screening/diagnostic colonoscopy | **well under 1%** |
| Bleeding, colonoscopy **with polypectomy**, all lesion sizes | **~1%** |
| Bleeding when **[[polypectomy\|cold snare]] is the primary tool for lesions ≤10 mm** | **well under 1%** |

Delayed bleeding rises after [[endoscopic-mucosal-resection|endoscopic mucosal resection]], [[endoscopic-submucosal-dissection|endoscopic submucosal dissection]] and other complex therapy. The three the document names as worth benchmarking: **cardiopulmonary events, perforation not identified and treated intraprocedurally, and major hemorrhage**.

> [!pearl] Waiting for the patient to call you misses most delayed events
> **15%–45% of adverse events go unrecognized or unreported.** Active contact **about 2 weeks after** the procedure is what captures them. The task force concedes the effort and cost of 14-day follow-up remain real obstacles.

## Documentation

**Minimal elements of an endoscopy report (20):** date · patient identification · **endoscopist(s), including trainees** · **assistant(s)** · relevant history and physical (if not separately documented) · confirmation of consent **and team pause** · procedure **planned and performed** · indication(s) · **instrument type(s)** · **medications with dose and route** · anatomic extent · **quality of visualization** (bowel prep, retained food) · **limitations, or their absence** · tissue or fluid samples · findings · diagnostic impression · results of therapy · adverse events · disposition · recommendations for subsequent care.

**Photodocumentation.** Cecal intubation rates approaching **100%** are achievable in healthy adults, and a cecal photo is the simplest verification of a complete colonoscopy — it is **fundamental to calculating the cecal-intubation indicator**. Capture the **appendiceal orifice with its cecal strap fold** and the **cecum with the ileocecal valve**; alternatives are the **ileocecal valve orifice** or **terminal ileum** (villi, circular valvulae conniventes, lymphoid hyperplasia). At ERCP: the **papilla before and after intervention**, any pathology, a **fluoroscopic scout film before starting**, and representative pancreaticobiliary images.

**Specimen verification.** Mishandling causes missed diagnoses, delayed or improper treatment and cost; the commonest errors are **mislabeling and lack of standardization**. The measurable, feasible step is a **specimen-requisition reconciliation policy** on which endoscopist and staff agree — performed for **every** container.

**Discharge and instructions.** Every unit needs a **written discharge-criteria policy** (level of consciousness, hemodynamic parameters, freedom from excess pain or untoward symptoms), and satisfaction of it documented. Written instructions cover diet, **medication resumption or change including antithrombotics**, return to driving and activity, contact information, **the signs of a delayed adverse event and exactly whom to call**, how biopsy results will reach the patient, and follow-up.

**Credentialing.** Define competency by **objective criteria and direct observation, not procedure counts**. Competence measures should be **universal, not specialty-specific**; **competence in one procedure does not imply competence in another**; and it requires the **minimum therapeutic maneuvers of that procedure** — standard polypectomy for colonoscopy, distal biliary stent placement for ERCP.

> [!pearl] Open-access referrals need a feedback loop, not a veto
> Open-access endoscopy is usually done for appropriate indications. The quality work is **feeding indication appropriateness back to referring physicians** and building a path to triage some referrals into clinic first.
