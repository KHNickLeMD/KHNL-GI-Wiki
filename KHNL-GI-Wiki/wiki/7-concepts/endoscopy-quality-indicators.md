*The base layer of the quality series: what applies to **every** gastrointestinal (GI) endoscopic procedure, whichever one it is. Procedure-specific sets sit on top — [[colonoscopy-quality-indicators|colonoscopy]], [[ercp-quality-indicators|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]], and the [[upper-endoscopy|upper endoscopy]] and [[endoscopic-ultrasound|endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)]] documents. The task force calls these the "building blocks" the others stand on.*

## The 19 Common Indicators

*American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) / American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) Task Force on Quality in Endoscopy, 2024 — third iteration after 2006 and 2015 ([[asge-acg-2024-common-endoscopy-quality-indicators]]). Strength, measure type and target exactly as the document's Table 1 gives them. **Priority indicators ★.** Every indicator is a frequency: the numerator is the cases in which the activity was done and documented, the denominator all endoscopies in scope.*

| # | Indicator | Strength | Measure | Target |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Preprocedure** | | | | |
| 1 ★ | Indication from a published standard list, documented | 1C+ | Process | >95% |
| 2 | Informed consent obtained and documented | 3 | Process | >98% |
| 3 | Preprocedure history + directed physical examination, documented | 3 | Process | >98% |
| 4 | **Sedation plan including sedation-risk assessment, documented before sedation starts** | 3 | Process | >98% |
| 5 ★ | Prophylactic antibiotics for appropriate indications | **Varies by procedure** | Process | >98% |
| 6 ★ | **Antithrombotic management plan formulated and documented before the procedure** | 3 | Process | >95% |
| 7 | Team pause performed and documented | 3 | Process | >98% |
| 8 | Performed or supervised by someone **fully trained and credentialed for that particular procedure** | 3 | Process | >98% |
| **Intraprocedure** | | | | |
| 9 | Photodocumentation performed | 3 | Process | >95% |
| 10 | Patient monitoring during sedation performed and documented | 3 | Process | >98% |
| 11 | **Interruption or premature termination for sedation-related issues** documented | 3 | Process | >98% |
| 12 | Specimen verification performed and documented | 3 | Process | >98% |

### Why Almost Everything Is Graded 3 at >98%

- **Grade 3 = expert opinion only** on the task force's own 8-level scheme (1A / 1B / 1C+ / 1C / 2A / 2B / 2C / 3) adapted from Guyatt, **not GRADE**. Fifteen of the 19 carry it. See [[ercp-quality-indicators#Strength of Recommendation Scheme]] for the full grid, which is identical across the series.
- **The target is >98% because the task force considered failure to perform most of these a "never event"** — only very unusual circumstances should excuse a miss. That is the opposite logic from the colonoscopy and ERCP sets: here, variation in practice and outcomes was **deliberately not** the basis for inclusion, because these activities are expected to happen almost universally with minimal variability.
- **Start with the four priority indicators** — 1, 5, 6 and 17 — chosen for clinical relevance and for being measurable in a manageable number of examinations. Move on to the rest once performance on those is above the recommended thresholds.
- Indicators and targets **do not reflect the standard of care, credentialing requirements or training standards**, and applying them that way is a misuse of the document.

> ⚠ **Four internal inconsistencies, carried as published.** Indicator 1 is graded **1C+** in Table 1 and **1C** in its own text block; indicators **6 and 9** have a target of **>95%** in the table and **95%** in their text blocks; indicator **18**'s target is **N/A** in the table and **varies according to procedure** in the text; indicator **19** is **N/A** strength and an **outcome** measure in the table but **grade 3** and a **process** measure in the text.

## Preprocedure

*From first contact between the patient and the endoscopy team until sedation is given — or the endoscope inserted, in unsedated procedures.*

### Indication and Consent

- **Every endoscopy needs an indication that appears on a published standard list** for that procedure, documented. The lists live in the procedure-specific quality documents. **Nonstandard reason → the rationale is justified in the documentation.**
- **Consent before the procedure, except in some emergencies**, and the discussion covers: **sedation plan · indication · description of the procedure · likely benefits · common adverse events · alternatives · prognosis if treatment is declined**.
- **Any member of the GI team** may obtain it — nurse, advanced practice provider or trainee — provided they are thoroughly knowledgeable of and able to communicate the indications, risks, benefits and alternatives, **unless local or state mandate says otherwise**. If an anesthesia provider sedates, a **separate consent for sedation/anesthesia obtained by that provider may be appropriate**.
- **Emergency with no patient capacity and no legal representative** → institutional process, e.g. **dual-physician consent**.

> [!pearl] Open-access referrals need a feedback loop, not a veto
> Open-access endoscopy is usually done for appropriate indications. The quality work is **feeding indication appropriateness back to referring physicians** and building a path to triage some referrals into clinic first ([[asge-acg-2024-common-endoscopy-quality-indicators]]).

### History, Examination and Fasting

- **Focused history before sedation:** indication, conditions affecting performance or safety, and the sedation-specific items — **major organ system abnormalities · prior adverse events with sedation or anesthesia · allergies, current medications and interactions · tobacco, alcohol or substance use · timing and nature of the last oral intake**.
- **Directed examination by the endoscopist:** **abdomen at minimum** (baseline if perforation is later suspected); **cardiac and pulmonary auscultation when the endoscopist gives the sedation and cardiopulmonary status is a concern** (heart failure, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease); **neurologic status** as the post-sedation baseline.
- **Fasting, per American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) practice guidelines:** clear liquids **2 h** · milk **6 h** · light meal **6 h** · fried or fatty meal **8 h** before sedation. **Gastroparesis, achalasia and bowel obstruction may need longer** to minimize aspiration risk.
- **Essential medications may be taken**, anticoagulation possibly excepted — and so may the [[bowel-preparation|bowel preparation]]. Residual gastric fluid after colonoscopy prep is **minimal (<25 mL)**, whether the prep was split or taken entirely the evening before.
- **No nothing-by-mouth rule is set for glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists** or other weight-loss drugs that delay gastric emptying; the document says unit policy will evolve as data emerge.

### Sedation Plan and Risk Assessment

- **The plan is documented before sedation is initiated and names the intended level** — none, minimal, moderate, deep (monitored anesthesia care) or general anesthesia.
- **Risk stratification:** the **ASA score** (1 = normal and healthy, 5 = critically ill with substantial risk of death within 24 h) and the **Mallampati score** (visual upper-airway assessment; a higher score correlates with more difficult endotracheal intubation) are the two in common use. **ASA scores predict adverse events during endoscopy** in large database studies, mostly sedation-related. **Mallampati has not been rigorously validated for endoscopy** — it is used because deep sedation is widespread and urgent airway management may be needed.
- **Stratification drives the decision** to proceed or defer, and how to modify the procedural approach and the sedation plan.
- **Even when an anesthesiologist sedates, endoscopist input can be critical** to clarify risk-to-benefit and inform the anesthesia approach. Agents, airway technique and monitoring depth: [[endoscopy-sedation]].

> [!pearl] Sedate to the level you can rescue from
> Sedation is a continuum and individual response is unpredictable. Per the ASA, whoever gives **moderate** sedation must be able to rescue a patient who slips into **deep** sedation; whoever gives **deep** sedation must be able to rescue one who reaches **general anesthesia** ([[asge-acg-2024-common-endoscopy-quality-indicators]]).

### Antibiotic Prophylaxis

**GI endoscopy is not a significant risk factor for clinically important bacteremia.** Full regimens, and the procedure-specific additions, are on [[antibiotic-prophylaxis-endoscopy]].

| Not recommended | Recommended |
|---|---|
| Endocarditis prevention — **even at highest cardiac risk** | [[ercp\|ERCP]] with **anticipated incomplete** biliary drainage (e.g. [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis\|primary sclerosing cholangitis]]) |
| Cardiac conditions, synthetic vascular grafts, nonvalvular cardiovascular devices | ERCP **after orthotopic liver transplantation** |
| Biliary obstruction **without** cholangitis, ERCP with **anticipated complete** drainage | ERCP with [[cholangioscopy]] |
| **Solid** upper-GI and colonic lesions undergoing EUS-guided fine-needle sampling | Any endoscopy in **[[cirrhosis]] with acute GI hemorrhage** |
| **Prosthetic joints**, any procedure | **[[enteral-access\|Percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy]] in all patients** |
| | Colonoscopy or flexible sigmoidoscopy on **peritoneal dialysis** |
| | EUS-guided fine-needle aspiration of a **mediastinal or pancreatic cystic** lesion — **controversial** |

**Judicious use to avoid unnecessary antibiotics is an equally important aim of this indicator**, not just giving them where indicated. The indicator sits in the preprocedure period even though antimicrobial decisions also arise during the procedure.

### Antithrombotic Management

Agent-specific timing lives on [[anticoagulation-gi-bleeding]]; the quality-relevant rules:

- **Antithrombotic use is queried and recorded at the time of the procedure**, and the plan is communicated to the patient and the healthcare team.
- **Diagnostic procedures, including those with mucosal biopsy sampling, are low bleeding risk — do not stop antiplatelets or anticoagulants.**
- **Most high-bleeding-risk therapeutic cases require anticoagulant cessation**, absent extenuating circumstances.
- **Some traditional interruptions may be unnecessary** — notably **cold snare resection of small polyps on clopidogrel**.
- **Almost all endoscopy is safe on 81–325 mg daily aspirin**, and current guidance is **against interrupting aspirin taken for secondary cardiovascular prevention**. Aspirin is **frequently stopped inappropriately**, and education fixes it — a ready quality-improvement target for a unit.
- **High thromboembolic risk** (may need bridging, deferral or specialist input): **atrial fibrillation with another cardiac condition** (heart failure, valvular disease) · **prior thromboembolism** · **mechanical mitral valve** · **coronary stent within a year** · **acute coronary syndrome** · **nonstented percutaneous coronary intervention after myocardial infarction**.
- **Resume immediately after most nontherapeutic procedures.** After a high-bleeding-risk intervention, individualize on the therapy performed and the thromboembolic risk.

### Team Pause and Credentialing

- **Team pause (timeout) before any procedure requiring sedation or anesthesia** — mandated by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services and several accrediting organizations. Done **before sedation, or before scope insertion**, per institutional policy. Confirms **correct patient and correct procedure** and that report and images attach to the right patient; also the moment to **reassess clinical, laboratory or radiologic data** and flag planned deviations or special equipment.
- **Credentialing:** define competency by **objective criteria and direct observation, not procedure counts**. Measures should be **universal, not specialty-specific**; **competence in one procedure does not imply competence in another**; competence requires the **minimum therapeutic maneuvers of that procedure** — standard [[polypectomy]] for [[colonoscopy]], distal biliary stent placement for ERCP. Pathways and proctoring: [[endoscopy-credentialing-and-privileging]].

## Intraprocedure

*From administration of sedation — or endoscope insertion when none is given — until the endoscope is removed.*

### Photodocumentation

- **Photograph important anatomic landmarks and pathology** — for communication and for precise comparison at a repeat procedure.
- **Colonoscopy:** a cecal photo is the simplest verification of a complete examination and is **fundamental to calculating the cecal-intubation indicator** (rates approaching **100%** are achievable in healthy adults). Capture the **appendiceal orifice with its surrounding cecal strap fold** and the **cecum with the ileocecal valve**; alternatives are the **ileocecal valve orifice** or the **terminal ileum** (villi, circular valvulae conniventes, lymphoid hyperplasia).
- **ERCP:** the **papilla before and after any intervention** (sphincterotomy, stent), any pathology, a **fluoroscopic scout film of the abdomen before starting**, and representative pancreaticobiliary images.

### Monitoring and Sedation Events

- **Monitor in all sedated procedures:** oxygen saturation by pulse oximetry, heart rate and blood pressure, with **blood pressure and heart rate recorded at intervals of ≤5 minutes**.
- **Electrocardiographic monitoring is indicated for deep sedation and general anesthesia.**
- **Capnography** reduces apnea and hypoxemia in randomized trials of endoscopy under propofol deep sedation and **can be considered** in that setting.
- **Document any sedation-related event — including reversal-agent use and airway management — that required interruption and/or premature termination** of the procedure (indicator 11). The measure is aimed at cases where **endoscopy personnel deliver the sedation**.

> [!pitfall] Never use indicator 11 as a punitive metric
> Reversal agents and premature termination are **potentially life-saving and can represent excellent clinical judgement**. Penalizing their use could have devastating unintended consequences. Review such events **non-punitively**, inside process improvement, never as an indirect outcome measure — the aim is fewer events that require interruption, **not fewer interruptions** ([[asge-acg-2024-common-endoscopy-quality-indicators]]).

### Specimen Verification

- **Verify for every specimen container:** the specimen is in the **proper container**, **labeled correctly**, **aligned with the endoscopy report**, and **delivered to the appropriate laboratory**.
- Mishandling causes **missed diagnoses, delayed or improper treatment and increased cost**; the most frequent errors are **miscommunication and lack of standardization**.
- The measurable, feasible step is a **specimen-requisition reconciliation policy** on which endoscopist and staff agree on labeling.

## Postprocedure

*From removal of the endoscope until subsequent follow-up.*

### Discharge and Patient Instructions

- **Every unit needs a written discharge-criteria policy** — a prespecified level of consciousness, certain hemodynamic parameters, and freedom from excess pain or other untoward symptoms — and satisfaction of it **documented before discharge**.
- **Written instructions** cover diet, **medication resumption or change including antithrombotics and new prescriptions**, return to activities such as driving, contact information, **the signs of a delayed adverse event and exactly whom to call**, how biopsy results will reach the patient, and follow-up appointments or the lack of need for any.

### Communication and Procedure Report

- **Findings, pathology results and follow-up recommendations go to the patient and all relevant providers, in a timely fashion** — pathology with its **management implications** (surveillance interval, need for *Helicobacter pylori* treatment). **Confirmed or suspected malignancy → document the plan for further follow-up, staging and treatment.**
- **Minimal elements of an endoscopy report (20):** date · patient identification · **endoscopist(s), including trainees** · **assistant(s)** · relevant history and physical (if not separately documented) · confirmation of consent **and team pause** · procedure **planned and performed** · indication(s) · **instrument type(s)** · **medications with dose and route** · anatomic extent · **quality of visualization** (bowel prep for colonoscopy, retained food at upper endoscopy) · **limitations, or their absence** · tissue or fluid samples · findings · diagnostic impression · results of therapy · adverse events · disposition · recommendations for subsequent care.

### Adverse Events

**Definition:** an event that **prevents completion of the planned procedure**, or causes **hospital admission, prolongation of an existing stay, another invasive procedure, or subsequent medical consultation**.

**Timing:** preprocedure · intraprocedure · **postprocedure (up to 14 days)** · **late (after 14 days)**.

**Grade two things for every event:** **certainty of attribution** — definite / probable / possible / unlikely — and **severity** — mild / moderate / severe / fatal, by the degree of disturbance to the patient and any change in the plan of care. Preprocedure and intraprocedure events evident at completion go in the report; events recognized later go in as an **addendum linked to the original report**.

*Rates the document prints as benchmarks.*

| Event | Rate |
|---|---|
| Cardiopulmonary, general endoscopy | **0.01%–1.1%** |
| Cardiopulmonary, ERCP | **up to 5%** |
| Perforation, diagnostic upper endoscopy | **~1 in 25,000** |
| Perforation, therapeutic upper endoscopy | **up to 1%** |
| Perforation, screening colonoscopy | **generally <0.1%** |
| Perforation, EUS and ERCP | **0.1%–0.6%** |
| Bleeding, ERCP **with sphincterotomy** | **0.3%–2%** |
| Bleeding, screening/diagnostic colonoscopy | **well under 1%** |
| Bleeding, colonoscopy **with polypectomy**, all lesion sizes | **~1%** |
| Bleeding when **cold snare is the primary tool for lesions ≤10 mm** | **well under 1%** |

Delayed bleeding rises after [[endoscopic-mucosal-resection|endoscopic mucosal resection]], [[endoscopic-submucosal-dissection|endoscopic submucosal dissection]] and other complex therapy. The three the document names as worth tracking against these benchmarks: **cardiopulmonary events, perforation not identified and treated intraprocedurally, and major hemorrhage**. Indicator 18 carries **no single performance target** — the rate expected varies by procedure, and the procedure-specific documents are the reference standard for each.

> [!pearl] Waiting for the patient to call you misses most delayed events
> **15%–45% of adverse events go unrecognized or unreported.** Active contact **ideally 2 weeks after** the procedure is what captures them. The task force concedes the effort and cost of 14-day follow-up remain real obstacles ([[asge-acg-2024-common-endoscopy-quality-indicators]]).

### Patient Satisfaction

- Collect patient experience and satisfaction **consistently, with standardized and validated questionnaires** modified for use after endoscopy; **all patients in a smaller practice, a random sample in other settings**; review the results inside continuous quality improvement.
- **No performance target is set.** True outcome indicators of patient experience may become feasible once more patients respond and benchmarks are defined.
