Noninvasive cross-sectional imaging of the pancreaticobiliary system.

- **MRCP** — heavily T2-weighted sequences image the pancreatic and bile ducts **without contrast or instrumentation**.
- **Contrast-enhanced MRI** — characterizes solid and cystic pancreatic lesions and parenchymal liver disease. For a liver observation in a patient at risk for HCC the report is categorized by [[li-rads|LI-RADS]]; outside that at-risk population it is worked up as a [[focal-liver-lesions|focal liver lesion]].

## Technique standards

| Parameter | Specification | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Field strength | **1.5-T minimum**; 3-T may add advantage for **small pancreatic lesions** (superior soft-tissue resolution) | [[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening]], [[aasld-2022-psc]] |
| Coils | Phased-array | [[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening]] |
| MRCP sequence | **T2w 3D MRCP with 1-mm slices preferred over 2D MRCP** | [[aasld-2022-psc]] |
| Contrast | Contrast-enhanced exam with intravenous agents for pancreatic surveillance | [[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening]] |

## Limitations — when MRCP is the wrong test

- **A normal MRCP does not exclude [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis|PSC]]** — **30% false-negative rate** when pretest probability is high. [[aasld-2022-psc]]
- **[[endoscopic-ultrasound|EUS]] is more sensitive than MRI for small solid pancreatic lesions.** [[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening]]
- **High pretest probability of stricture, or [[acute-cholangitis|cholangitis]] → go straight to [[ercp|ERCP]].** MRCP is a diagnostic test, not a therapeutic one; interposing it only delays drainage. It is also warranted to proceed to ERCP when clinical suspicion stays high **despite a negative MRCP**. [[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-graft-complications]]
- **Patient/hardware constraints:** incompatible metallic implants, claustrophobia. Overall procedural risk is low, especially compared with ERCP. [[asge-2023-post-transplant-biliary-strictures]]

## Where MRCP carries the decision

Each row's criteria, thresholds, and intervals live on the linked page — not duplicated here.

| Setting | Role of MRI/MRCP |
|---|---|
| [[choledocholithiasis]] | **Intermediate-probability** CBD stone → confirm with **EUS or MRCP** before [[ercp\|ERCP]]; the two have comparable high accuracy, so choose by availability/expertise. [[asge-2019-choledocholithiasis]] |
| [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis]] | **Preferred diagnostic modality** — ERCP is avoided for diagnosis. Also the modality for **annual [[cholangiocarcinoma\|CCA]] surveillance** (± CA 19-9), and defines a **high-grade stricture** (>75% luminal reduction of CBD or hepatic ducts). [[aasld-2022-psc]] |
| [[pancreatic-cysts]] | **Preferred modality for diagnosis and surveillance.** [[acg-2018-pancreatic-cysts]] |
| [[liver-transplantation\|Post-transplant]] [[biliary-stricture\|biliary strictures]] | **First-line diagnostic tool**, preferred over ERCP for diagnosis. [[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-graft-complications]] [[asge-2023-post-transplant-biliary-strictures]] |
| [[pancreatic-cancer-screening\|High-risk pancreatic cancer surveillance]] | Accepted screening modality — alone or **alternating with EUS**; preferred for higher anesthesia/procedural risk, patients who value avoiding invasive testing, and where it can be combined with other imaging (e.g. enterography in [[peutz-jeghers-syndrome\|Peutz-Jeghers syndrome]]). [[asge-2022-pancreatic-cancer-screening]] |
| [[recurrent-acute-pancreatitis]] | MRCP or EUS to exclude microlithiasis and periampullary lesions in idiopathic disease (~20% of cases). [[acg-2024-acute-pancreatitis]] |
| [[chronic-pancreatitis]], [[cholangiocarcinoma]], [[jaundice]], [[abnormal-liver-chemistries]] | Duct-level anatomy for diagnosis and staging. |
