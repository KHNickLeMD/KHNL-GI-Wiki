## The 13 Indicators

Current set: **ACG/ASGE Task Force on Quality in Endoscopy, 2026** — the third iteration, after 2006 and 2015 ([[acg-asge-2026-ercp-quality-indicators]]). Strength, measure type and target exactly as published. Measured at provider, group or system level; structural, unit-level measures are a separate document.

**These indicators and targets do not define the standard of care, credentialing requirements or training standards — using them that way is a misuse.** They are a framework for quality improvement. No provider or centre is expected to track all 13; pick the ones most likely to improve care in that practice.

| # | Indicator | Strength | Measure | Target |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Preprocedure** | | | | |
| 1 ★ | Accepted indication, documented | 1C+ | Process | >98% |
| 2 | Informed consent obtained **including a specific discussion of ERCP risks**, documented | 3 | Process | >98% |
| 3 | Prophylactic antibiotics for appropriate indications | 2B | Process | >98% |
| **Intraprocedure** | | | | |
| 4 ★ | Deep cannulation of the duct of interest, **native papilla + standard foregut anatomy** | 1C | Outcome | **>90%** |
| 5 | Radiation exposure documented + dose-reduction measures used | 1C | Process | >98% |
| 6 ★ | **Extrahepatic bile duct stones extracted successfully**, normal biliary anatomy, documented | 1B | Outcome | **>90%** |
| 7 ★ | **Rectal indomethacin or diclofenac given, intact papilla** | 1A | Process | **>90%** |
| 8 | **Use or nonuse of a prophylactic pancreatic stent in high-risk cases** documented and tracked | 1A | Process | >98% |
| **Postprocedure** | | | | |
| 9 ★ | Unplanned hospital visit within 30 days | 1C | Outcome | **<15%** |
| 10 | Unplanned biliary intervention within 30 days (salvage percutaneous transhepatic biliary drainage or repeat ERCP) | 1C | Outcome | **<15%** |
| 11 | Post-ERCP pancreatitis (PEP) rate documented and tracked | 3 | Process | >95% |
| 12 | Clinically significant hemorrhage after sphincterotomy/sphincteroplasty documented and tracked | 3 | Process | >95% |
| 13 | Cholangitis and cholecystitis within 30 days documented and tracked | 3 | Process | >95% |

The document's own Table 1 names indicator 13 "cholangitis within 30 d"; the indicator's section heading and discussion cover **cholangitis and cholecystitis**.

> [!pitfall] Indicators 11–13 measure your records, not your complication rate
> They are **process** measures — the target is how often the event is *documented and tracked*, not how often it happens. The task force declines to set a performance target for the PEP rate at all, holding that a single fair benchmark "may not be feasible or justified". Rates are then compared **within groups of similar case mix**, and against your own past performance.

### Priority Indicators

**Five**: **#1** (indication), **#4** (deep cannulation), **#6** (stone extraction), **#7** (rectal nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug [NSAID]), **#9** (unplanned hospital visit). Selected for clinical importance, demonstrated variation in practice, and feasibility of measurement. Start here; **add the rest only once performance sits above these thresholds**, at baseline or after corrective interventions.

The **19 indicators common to all endoscopic procedures** — consent, antithrombotic plan, team pause, sedation plan, photodocumentation, specimen verification, adverse-event documentation — are a separate document and live on [[endoscopy-quality-indicators]]; they are not repeated here.

### Strength of Recommendation Scheme

**Not GRADE.** The task force's own 8-level scheme, adapted from Guyatt — the same one the [[colonoscopy-quality-indicators|colonoscopy set]] uses.

| Grade | Benefit | Evidence | Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1A | Clear | Randomized controlled trials (RCTs), no important limitations | Strong; most clinical settings |
| 1B | Clear | RCTs with important limitations | Strong; likely most practice settings |
| 1C+ | Clear | Overwhelming observational evidence | Strong; most settings, most situations |
| 1C | Clear | Observational | Intermediate; may change with stronger evidence |
| 2A | Unclear | RCTs, no important limitations | Intermediate; best action varies with circumstance/values |
| 2B | Unclear | RCTs with important limitations | Weak |
| 2C | Unclear | Observational | Very weak |
| **3** | Unclear | **Expert opinion only** | Weak; likely to change |

A **"3" is expert opinion only**, not a third-tier GRADE rating — four of the 13 indicators (#2, #11, #12, #13) carry it. Strength of evidence for each indicator was set **by author consensus**.

## Denominators That Decide Whether a Case Counts

### Antibiotic Prophylaxis

Denominator = the **three scenarios the literature most strongly supports**, where prophylaxis is expected:

1. Complete relief of biliary obstruction **not anticipated** (e.g., [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis|primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)]], biliary cyst) **or not achieved**.
2. [[cholangioscopy|Cholangioscopy]].
3. Prior **orthotopic liver transplantation**.

- **No data support routine prophylaxis for pancreatoscopy** — individualize.
- **Avoiding antibiotics where no benefit is likely is equally part of this indicator** — inappropriate use drives resistance and drug adverse events.
- Regimens and the wider endoscopic prophylaxis rules: [[antibiotic-prophylaxis-endoscopy]].

### Deep Cannulation

Target **>90%**, and the definition decides whether a hard case is held against you.

- **Counts as success:** advanced techniques — double-guidewire, cannulation over a pancreas stent, needle-knife sphincterotomy, fistulotomy. Endoscopists performing ERCP should be familiar with one or more.
- **Counts as failure:** [[eus-guided-biliary-drainage|endoscopic ultrasound-guided biliary drainage (EUS-BD)]] after a failed attempt (that patient is in the **denominator but not the numerator**); **duodenal obstruction** preventing access to the papilla.
- **Excluded entirely:** modified upper gastrointestinal (GI) anatomy — Roux-en-Y gastric bypass, Billroth II.

> [!pitfall] Chasing the cannulation target can injure the patient
> >90% is desirable, but **overambitious persistence raises pancreatitis, perforation and sedation-related risk**. Returning on another day, once ampullary oedema has resolved, has a high success rate. **The task force sets no time limit for how long to persist** — when to pivot to an alternative cannulation strategy is called an open research question.

**Benchmark data:** US registry of 85 high-volume endoscopists — overall **>80%**, with **83.5% individually above 90%**. Twenty-two recent advanced-endoscopy graduates: cohort native-papilla rate **93.1%**, but only **77.3% individually exceeded 90%**. The Netherlands' mandatory registry: **90.7%** overall, with many endoscopists below 90% and several **below 85%**, the lowest rates clustering among lower-volume providers. **There is no agreed definition of a low-volume ERCP operator** — a meta-analysis of 13 studies used thresholds from <25 to <156 ERCPs a year.

### Stone Extraction

Target **>90%**; assumes biliary cannulation already succeeded.

- **The denominator changed in 2026:** it is now **all stones in the extrahepatic bile duct after successful cannulation**, no longer only stones <1 cm as in 2015 — high-quality ERCP is expected to include proficiency with at least some advanced extraction techniques. A rate computed the old way is not comparable.
- **Most stones are <10 mm** and come out with conventional sphincterotomy + balloon extraction.
- Harder: stones **above the biliary confluence**, retained in the **cystic duct**, **impacted**, **multiple and stacked**, **>10 mm**, or in a **nondilated or tapered distal duct**; and any case needing needle-knife sphincterotomy for access. **These situations occur in <20% of ERCPs.**
- **Use occlusion cholangiogram and balloon sweeps** to confirm clearance, and **document whether complete extraction was achieved** — with stone size and location, any strictures, and any postsurgical anatomy, so later benchmarking compares like with like.

| Scenario | Complete clearance | Data behind it |
|---|---|---|
| Stones <10 mm, conventional technique | near **100%** overall; **>92%** per provider | 63 endoscopists, 18,182 ERCPs, self-reported |
| Stones >10 mm — sphincterotomy + transpapillary large balloon dilation | **85%** at first ERCP | systematic review and meta-analysis |
| Stones >10 mm — electrohydraulic or laser lithotripsy required | **75%** | same meta-analysis |
| Stones >10 mm — single-operator [[cholangioscopy]] | **100%**, highest of 5 approaches | 19 RCTs, 2,752 patients |
| Stones >10 mm — mechanical lithotripsy | **57%**, lowest of the 5 | same 19-RCT meta-analysis |
| First ERCP for biliary stones, real world | **two-thirds** | 37,468 patients, verified hospital episode statistics |

### Rectal NSAID and Prophylactic Pancreatic Stent

- **Rectal indomethacin or diclofenac is now endorsed in unselected patients** undergoing ERCP (grade 1A), not restricted to high risk as in 2015. Risk reduction ~**50%** in meta-analysis; **54% relative risk reduction** in a >2,000-patient average-risk RCT. **Before or after the procedure both work**; the large positive RCT dosed systematically **before**.
- **Denominator = intact papilla.** Repeat intervention through a prior sphincterotomy (routine stent exchange) carries negligible PEP risk and is excluded — **but do not withhold the NSAID from a patient with a prior biliary sphincterotomy who is undergoing pancreatic endotherapy.**
- **The target is 90%, not 98%, on purpose** — allowing for NSAID allergy, renal dysfunction and very-low-risk patients. Current practice is far below it: NSAIDs reach **well under 50%** of patients.
- **Prophylactic pancreatic stent placement (PSP):** the indicator measures **documentation of use or nonuse in high-risk cases**, not a placement rate — **denominator = high-risk ERCPs performed; numerator = those in which use or nonuse is clearly documented**. Multicentre RCT across **20 US and Canadian centres**: rectal indomethacin **plus** a prophylactic stent beat indomethacin alone, cutting PEP by **>30%**, with numerically fewer severe cases and fewer pancreatitis-related deaths, greatest benefit in those at greatest risk.
- **High risk**, as the document lists it: suspected biliary or pancreatic [[sphincter-of-oddi-dysfunction|sphincter of Oddi dysfunction]], ampullectomy, difficult cannulation, double-wire technique, pancreatic sphincterotomy, pancreatic endotherapy — not an exclusive list.
- **When a stent is clearly advisable:** pancreatic duct access already achieved (e.g., inadvertent guidewire passage). **Less clear-cut:** modestly elevated risk with anticipated difficult placement — suboptimal cannulation alignment, a pancreatic ansa loop. Also weigh whether the patient can return for the **mandatory follow-up abdominal radiograph** confirming an unflanged stent has passed.

> [!pitfall] An unsuccessful stent attempt can raise PEP risk above baseline
> PSP is used in **under 5%** of high-risk cases today. The task force measures documentation rather than placement precisely so the indicator does not drive overambitious stenting. **Those without training in the maneuver should refer anticipated high-risk cases** rather than attempt it.

### Unplanned Events

Both indicators run to **30 days** and target **<15%**.

- **Unplanned = not anticipated before the index ERCP.** A staged procedure planned at the index sitting (stent now for acute suppurative cholangitis in a patient not safe for stone extraction, stones later) is a *planned* future event and does **not** count.
- A rescue intervention **at the time of the index ERCP** (EUS-BD for failed cannulation, same session) is not unplanned; **the same rescue a few days later is**. The <15% target is set to accommodate intentional delays judged to be in the patient's interest.
- **Unplanned hospital visit (#9)** counts ERCP-specific adverse events, other physician-defined adverse events, and other morbidity such as postprocedure pain or nausea. Reported rates across the commonest indications in the US and other Western countries are typically **<15%**.
- **Greater-than-expected rates** reflect one or more of: suboptimal **preprocedure evaluation** (case selection or optimization), **technical performance**, or **inadequate ambulatory infrastructure** for the recovery period.
- **Unplanned biliary intervention (#10)** = repeat ERCP, salvage percutaneous transhepatic cholangiography, or a pivot to EUS-guided drainage at a *subsequent* procedure. It captures technical **and** clinical failure in one measurable metric.
- Interpret your own number against your case list: some indications — **benign bile duct stricture** — legitimately need **at least 2 ERCPs per patient**, which is why the window is 30 days rather than any repeat intervention at any time.

## Adverse-Event Benchmarks

| Event | Rate |
|---|---|
| PEP, average risk | **1%–7%** |
| PEP, high-risk populations (suspected sphincter of Oddi disorder, [[recurrent-acute-pancreatitis\|recurrent idiopathic acute pancreatitis]], prior PEP) | **up to 30%**, severe disease more common |
| Cholangitis after ERCP | **<1%** |
| Cholecystitis | **0.2%–0.5%** |
| Cholangitis/cholecystitis across reported cohorts | **1%–8%**, with **PSC the highest-risk indication** |
| Hemorrhage (mostly post-sphincterotomy) | **0.8%–2%** of cases |
| Perforation (guidewire-, sphincterotomy- or endoscope-induced) | **0.1%–0.6%** |

- **Clinically significant hemorrhage** has variable definitions; most require a hemoglobin fall of **at least 2–3 g** with melena, hematemesis or other signs of GI bleeding.
- **There is no bleeding-rate target in this set.** The task force considered retaining the 2015 document's outcome indicator of **<1%** but did not, because an individual's rate depends on case mix — the proportion of ERCPs with sphincterotomy, and the proportion of patients with innate or medication-induced coagulopathy. Bleeding is not entirely preventable, but **the rate should be low, <1%–2% of all procedures**.
- Ideally the bleeding rate is calculated **excluding patients with a prior sphincterotomy** — a high volume of repeat ERCPs with stent exchanges skews it low.
- **Precut sphincterotomy** is a probable technical risk factor and excess use would be expected to show a substantially greater bleeding rate.
- **Cholangitis risk tracks the indication and the completeness of drainage.** Rates above the published range should prompt a look at the spectrum of indications (hepatic confluence strictures, PSC) and at related maneuvers such as cholangioscopy and mechanical lithotripsy.
- **Consent should name at least 5 adverse outcomes:** pancreatitis, hemorrhage, infection (procedure- and duodenoscope-related), perforation, cardiopulmonary events — plus that the ERCP may fail and that an event could be severe.
- **Duodenoscope contamination is deliberately not an indicator** — the measurable infection rate is low and the clinical significance of a positive endoscope culture is unclear. See [[endoscope-reprocessing]].

Procedural-success benchmarks from the older biliary guideline, still current for their own claims ([[asge-2015-ercp-benign-biliary]]): successful cholangiography **with relief of biliary obstruction** should be technically achievable in **>90%** of patients; sphincterotomy **and** stone extraction succeed in **>90%** of cases, with an overall adverse-event rate of **~5%** and mortality **<1% in expert hands**.

## Defining Post-ERCP Pancreatitis

**All three** of the consensus criteria:

1. Abdominal pain consistent with pancreatitis;
2. Amylase or lipase **>3× the upper limit of normal, drawn more than 24 h after** ERCP;
3. **Either** admission **or** prolongation of an admission by **at least 2 days**.

> [!pitfall] PEP rates are not comparable between endoscopists
> The definition turns on two subjective judgments — whether the pain is pancreatic and whether to admit. A low admission threshold produces a higher measured PEP rate for identical care, and patients with pre-existing chronic pain can partly satisfy the criteria for unrelated reasons. Add case-mix (a referral centre taking previously unsuccessful ERCPs and recurrent acute pancreatitis will exceed a centre treating mainly [[choledocholithiasis|choledocholithiasis]] and pancreatic cancer) and **between-provider comparison breaks down**. Compare against **your own prior performance**.

## Radiation Dose

Principle: **as low as reasonably achievable (ALARA)**.

- **Measure dose directly where possible** — *dose area product* and *dose at reference point*. **Fluoroscopy time is only a surrogate** and may correlate poorly with true dose, especially in prolonged, complex cases. If direct measurement is unavailable, **track fluoroscopy time at minimum**.
- Case mix varies more by centre than by operator, so **comparing providers within one institution beats comparing absolute values** across institutions.
- All personnel — physicians and radiology technicians alike — **should undergo radiation safety training**.

> [!howto] Dose-reduction measures the task force names
> 1. Reduce the pulse rate (frames per second).
> 2. Use "save last image" instead of static image acquisition.
> 3. Keep the radiation source distant from the patient, the detector close to it.
> 4. Collimate routinely.
> 5. Uniform personal protective equipment, **including leaded eye protection**.
> 6. Proper shielding.

## What Changed From the 2015 Set

- **ERCP indications are treated more restrictively** — see [[ercp]] for the accepted-indication list and the controversial ones the document rules on (sphincter of Oddi disorder, minor papilla intervention in [[pancreas-divisum|pancreas divisum]], preoperative drainage of malignant obstruction, diagnostic ERCP in PSC).
- **Rectal NSAIDs extended from high-risk to average-risk patients** with an intact ampullary sphincter.
- **A new indicator for prophylactic pancreatic stent documentation.**
- **Unplanned events promoted to the postprocedure outcome measures** — unplanned hospital visit and unplanned biliary intervention — as more reliably definable than the alternatives.
- **The stone-extraction denominator widened** beyond stones <1 cm.
