**Brush cytology** is the acquisition of exfoliated epithelial cells from a biliary (or pancreatic) stricture by passing a cytology brush over a guidewire during [[ercp|ERCP]]. It is the baseline **intraductal** tissue-acquisition method for [[biliary-stricture|biliary strictures]] of undetermined etiology — the "traditional cornerstone" of initial sampling, chosen for its relative technical ease, widespread availability, and reliance on standard cytopathologic handling ([[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]]). It is never used alone, and it is not the first test when a pancreatic mass is present.

## When Brushing Is — and Is Not — the Right Test

The indication turns on **stricture location and whether a mass is present** ([[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]]). Full location-by-location algorithm: [[biliary-stricture]].

| Setting | Where brushing sits |
|---|---|
| **Extrahepatic stricture with an apparent/suspected pancreatic mass** | **Not first-line** — [[endoscopic-ultrasound\|EUS]]-FNA/B is recommended over ERCP-based sampling (**strong, moderate**). Add brushing **only if FNA/B is nondiagnostic or ROSE (rapid on-site evaluation) is unavailable**; the combination then has the greatest yield |
| **Extrahepatic stricture without an associated mass** | Mirror the perihilar approach — brushing as one of ≥2 intraductal modalities |
| **Perihilar stricture** | Brushing is included but **never alone** — ≥2 modalities at the index ERCP (**strong, low**) |

- ⚠ **The pattern to avoid:** ERCP with brush cytology + plastic stent at a center without EUS. Yield is very low and most patients with a pancreatic mass will not be resected without a tissue diagnosis — **defer to a capable center for single-session EUS + ERCP** instead ([[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]], Key Concept 2).
- **Pre-test probability is high, which is why a negative result is unhelpful:** malignancy is present in ~**55%** of biliary strictures with no obvious mass on cross-sectional imaging ([[asge-2023-indeterminate-biliary-strictures]]), and in **73%–87%** of patients referred in large endoscopic series ([[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]]).

## Test Characteristics

Per [[asge-2023-indeterminate-biliary-strictures]]:

| Strategy | Sensitivity for malignancy | Miss rate |
|---|---|---|
| **Brush cytology alone** | **.40** (95% CI .37–.43) | **58%** (95% CI 46–71) |
| Fluoroscopic-guided biopsy alone | **.52** (95% CI .49–.56); *P* = .006 vs brushing | **41%** (95% CI 31–52) |
| **Brushing + fluoroscopic-guided biopsy** | **.66** (95% CI .63–.69); *P* < .001 vs brushing | — |

- **Incremental yield** of adding intraductal biopsy to brushing: **20%** (95% CI 9–31).
- Specimen adequacy is higher for brush cytology — but ASGE attributes this to the **technical difficulty and failure of fluoroscopic-guided biopsy**, not to specimen quality.
- No difference in **adverse events** overall (OR .53; 95% CI .14–2.05) — though the 2 severe events (prolonged bleeding; perforation requiring surgical choledochotomy) were both in the biopsy arm.
- **No sensitivity difference** for proximal vs distal strictures, or biliary vs pancreatic masses.
- Adjuncts that improve yield on brushings include [[fish|FISH (fluorescence in situ hybridization)]] and next-generation sequencing. Note FISH's performance is **attenuated in [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis|PSC]]** ([[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]]) — see [[fish]].

**Why the reported sensitivity varies so widely** ([[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]]):

| Evidence | Sensitivity for malignancy |
|---|---|
| 2013 meta-analysis, 1,556 patients (33% pancreatic cancer, 36% [[cholangiocarcinoma\|CCA]]) | **41.6%** composite |
| Subsequent series | clustering **50%–60%** |
| Outliers | as low as **6%**; up to ~**75%** in one series using **ROSE** of the brush specimen |
| 2015 meta-analysis, 9 studies / 730 indeterminate strictures | brushings **45%**, forceps biopsies **48%**, **both together 59%** |

ACG's conclusion is explicit: *"the performance characteristics of this diagnostic modality are inadequate to drive clinical decision making."*

## Interpreting the Result

The two results are **not symmetric** — this is the decision the page exists for.

- **Positive brushing → act on it.** For biliary strictures the diagnostic problem is **false negatives (low sensitivity); false-positive samples (low specificity) are uncommon** ([[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]]). A positive cytology is treated as malignant. ⚠ **Gap:** neither ingested guideline reports a **numeric specificity or PPV** for biliary brush cytology — ACG states only that false positives are uncommon, and ASGE 2023 reports no specificity at all. Do not quote a number.
- **Negative brushing → excludes nothing.** With a **58%** miss rate against a ~55%–87% pre-test probability of malignancy, a negative brushing leaves the stricture **indeterminate** (defined on [[biliary-stricture]] as a stricture undiagnosed *despite* index ERCP with intraductal sampling) and mandates further sampling — added modalities at the index procedure, then [[cholangioscopy]]/EUS, NGS, or [[confocal-laser-endomicroscopy|pCLE]] on repeat ERCP, with multidisciplinary review after two negative sessions.

## Brush Cytology Alone Is Not Sufficient

Because sensitivity is ~0.40, neither guideline accepts brushing as a stand-alone strategy:

- **[[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]] Rec 3 (Strong recommendation, low quality of evidence):** for a suspected malignant perihilar stricture, use **multimodality intraductal sampling** — **at least 2 diagnostic sampling modalities** at the index ERCP — not brush cytology alone. The same approach applies to extrahepatic strictures without an associated mass.
- **[[asge-2023-indeterminate-biliary-strictures]] Rec 1 (Conditional, very low quality):** add fluoroscopic-guided biopsy sampling to brush cytology rather than brush cytology alone — **where to do it is part of the recommendation:** because intraductal biopsy is more technically challenging, more time-consuming, and produced the more severe adverse events, it should be performed **at tertiary care centers or where endoscopic expertise exists**.
- Fluoroscopy-directed biopsy, cholangioscopy-directed biopsy, and FISH each add diagnostic value over brushing alone; choose by stricture location — see [[biliary-stricture]].
