## Definition

Survival and functioning of the transplanted allograft **in the absence of immunosuppression**, usually **for more than a year** — arising either spontaneously (non-adherence, or on medical advice because of a serious malignancy or infection) or through a deliberate withdrawal protocol ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-graft-complications]]). The liver is more immune-tolerant than other solid organ allografts because it clears blood-borne pathogens and processes gut-derived food-borne antigens, so the chance of operational tolerance is higher after [[liver-transplantation|LT]] than after other solid organ transplants.

> **Do not confuse with CNI withdrawal.** Reducing or withdrawing **[[calcineurin-inhibitors|CNI]]-based** immunosuppression is a *different, routine* maneuver — a renal-sparing switch (typically to sirolimus/everolimus ± mycophenolate, or steroids + mycophenolate/azathioprine) and a **Grade 1, Level A** response to CKD in LT recipients ([[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]], Rec 29). Renal function is more likely to be preserved if CNI withdrawal is started while **eGFR is 40–50 mL/min/1.73 m²**. Operational tolerance means **all** immunosuppression stopped, and is not routinely recommended.

## Prevalence and Predictors

Per [[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-graft-complications]]:

| Datum | Value |
|---|---|
| Likelihood of tolerance per year post-LT | **+7% per year** (95% CI 4–10%); meta-analysis of 15 studies, n=620 |
| Proportion tolerating complete IS withdrawal | **>40%** of *highly selected* recipients |
| Timing at which that was achieved | mean **10.2 years** after LT (adults, prospective trials) |
| Early withdrawal (within **1–2 years** post-LT) | possible in only a very small group |

- No reliable biomarkers currently identify ideal candidates; ALT, class II donor-specific antibodies (DSA), and [[liver-stiffness-measurement|liver stiffness measurement]] show promise but are **not prospectively validated**.
- Patients with **autoimmune liver disease** and those with a **history of rejection** are less likely to be withdrawn safely.
- **Evidence caveat:** long-term safety data are inconclusive and mostly lack histology; most trials **excluded viral hepatitis and autoimmune liver disease** and were done in **pediatric** rather than adult populations — so the ">40%" figure does not generalize.

## Current Guideline Recommendation

**Complete IS withdrawal is not routinely recommended**, given the lack of reliable predictors of withdrawal success and limited data in a very select group of long-term adult recipients ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-graft-complications]], Rec 36, Strong, Level 4).

Per [[aasld-2012-liver-transplant-long-term]] (Rec 9, **Grade 2, Level C**): *"Although the long-term withdrawal of all immunosuppression can be achieved in a small number of patients, this should be undertaken only with select recipients and under close supervision."* The two guidelines agree; the 2025 statement is the newer and stronger of the two and governs.

**Features that exclude a candidate.** Prior withdrawal studies excluded these, so they define who is *not* a candidate ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-graft-complications]]):

| Feature | Basis |
|---|---|
| History of rejection | Less likely to be withdrawn safely |
| Autoimmune liver disease — [[autoimmune-hepatitis\|AIH]], [[primary-biliary-cholangitis\|PBC]], [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis\|PSC]] | Less likely to be withdrawn safely; also excluded from most trials |
| Inflammation, fibrosis, or **piecemeal necrosis** on pre-attempt histology | Excluded from prior withdrawal studies |
| Elevated **donor-specific antibodies** — see [[antibody-mediated-rejection-liver-transplant\|AMR]] | Excluded from prior withdrawal studies |

## Practical Approach

- A [[liver-biopsy|liver biopsy]] **before** consideration of withdrawal appears prudent — it is the only way to exclude the histologic features above, which lab parameters do not predict.
- Withdrawal only in a **closely monitored, highly selected** group of long-term LT recipients; prefer enrolment in a prospective trial over unmonitored withdrawal.
- Severity of clinically silent allograft damage correlates **positively** with TCMR-related transcripts, class II DSA, ALT, AST, and liver stiffness, and **negatively** with serum creatinine and [[tacrolimus]] trough levels — which is why ALT, class II DSA, and liver stiffness are the candidate screening tools ([[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-graft-complications]]).

> **Gap — no monitoring protocol is specified.** Neither ingested guideline gives a biopsy interval, an ALT/DSA threshold, or a taper schedule for a withdrawal attempt. Anyone attempting withdrawal outside a trial is operating without a sourced protocol; do not infer one.
