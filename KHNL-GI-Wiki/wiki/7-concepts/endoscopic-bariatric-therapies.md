## Overview

**Endoscopic bariatric therapies (EBTs)** are devices and techniques that occupy the gap between lifestyle/pharmacologic management of [[obesity]] and [[bariatric-surgery|bariatric/metabolic surgery]].

- Rationale per [[aga-2017-endoscopic-bariatric-therapies|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2017]]: surgery induces **20%–40% total body weight loss (TBWL)** and reduces obesity-related morbidity and mortality, but at the cost of increased morbidity and rare mortality. EBTs are less invasive, lower cost, and applicable to a larger segment of the population with moderate obesity.
- Obesity affects **35% of US adults** (body mass index [BMI] ≥30 kg/m²); obesity with its comorbidities accounts for **~21% of US health expenditure**.
- **Four EBTs were US Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved** as of the 2017 review.
- **2022:** FDA de novo authorization of the OverStitch endoscopic suturing device for treating obesity; the number of endoscopists performing [[endoscopic-sleeve-gastroplasty|endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty (ESG)]] has risen since ([[jirapinyo-2025-esg-delphi-standards]]).
- **Governing graded guideline:** the [[asge-esge-2024-primary-ebmt|American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy–European Society of Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE–ESGE) 2024 guideline]] calls these **endoscopic bariatric and metabolic therapies (EBMTs)** and grades each device class. In 2024 only intragastric balloons and endoscopic gastric remodeling (EGR: ESG, primary obesity surgery endoluminal [POSE], Endomina) were commercially available and routinely used.

> [!pearl] Plan for weight regain before the device goes in
> The AGA 2017 message is **programmatic, not device-specific**: weight loss after an EBT is variable and can be temporary — especially with removable, anatomy-preserving devices — so weight recidivism should be anticipated and managed. Obesity is a chronic disease and **no single intervention, device, or operation cures it** ([[aga-2017-endoscopic-bariatric-therapies]]).

> **The AGA 2017 Clinical Practice Update is ungraded.** Its 7 Best Practice Advice statements carry no evidence grade, strength rating, or GRADE assessment; the authors describe it as a consensus summary of expert opinion without a formal systematic evidence review — not graded evidence. Graded recommendations come from [[asge-esge-2024-primary-ebmt|ASGE–ESGE 2024]] (all device classes) and the [[aga-2021-intragastric-balloons|AGA 2021 intragastric balloon guideline]] (endoscopically placed balloons only; detail on [[intragastric-balloon]]).

## Classification and Mechanism

| Site | Devices / techniques | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Gastric** | space-occupying balloons and devices ([[intragastric-balloon\|intragastric balloon (IGB)]], TransPyloric Shuttle, Full Sense), endoscopic suturing/plication (ESG, primary obesity surgery endoluminal), aspiration therapy | Restriction, plus altered gastric mechanical/chemical receptor signaling, gastric emptying, and orexigenic hormone levels |
| **Small bowel** | duodenojejunal bypass sleeve, gastroduodenojejunal bypass sleeve, duodenal mucosal resurfacing, self-assembling magnets | Exploits the proximal small intestine's role in glucose homeostasis and incretin signaling |

## Patient Selection and Program Requirements

**Who qualifies — [[asge-esge-2024-primary-ebmt|ASGE–ESGE 2024]] Rec 1 (conditional, very low certainty):** EBMT **plus lifestyle modification (LM) over LM alone** for BMI **≥30 kg/m²** with or without an obesity-related comorbidity, **or BMI 27.0–29.9 kg/m² with ≥1 obesity-related comorbidity**.
- **No upper BMI limit:** EBMTs serve as primary therapy or as a bridge to bariatric surgery.
- Device data by band: **BMI 27–29.9** → IGB, EGR, duodenal-jejunal bypass liner (DJBL); **class III** → IGB, EGR, aspiration therapy, DJBL.
- **Target:** ≥5% total weight loss (TWL), where comorbidity endpoints start to improve. The guideline counted a device as effective only if it beat LM by **≥3% TWL**, because LM alone gave 3.2%.

| BMI group | Evidence | Weight loss | Serious adverse events (SAEs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 27.0–29.9 + comorbidity | 6 observational studies | **11.9% TWL** at 6–12 mo | **2.7%** |
| 30–39.9 (classes I–II) | 14 randomized controlled trials (RCTs), n = 2787 | **7.1% TWL more than LM** at 12 mo (or at balloon removal) | Risk ratio 4.4 vs LM = **14 extra per 1000** |
| ≥40 (class III) | 20 observational studies | **13.1% TWL** at 6–12 mo | **6.9%** |

*AGA 2017 Best Practice Advice (BPA) 1–5, ungraded:*

- **Indication (BPA 1):** consider EBTs in patients with obesity **unsuccessful in losing or maintaining weight loss with lifestyle interventions**.
- **Bridge indication (BPA 2):** EBTs can be used in **severe obesity as a bridge to traditional bariatric surgery**, and as a bridge to unrelated interventions blocked by weight limits (orthopedic surgery, organ transplantation).
- **Structured program (BPA 3):** use EBTs as part of a structured weight-loss program with **dietary intervention, exercise therapy, and behavior modification — in both the active weight-loss phase and the long-term maintenance phase**.
- **Pre-enrollment screening (BPA 4):** screen all potential candidates with a **comprehensive evaluation for medical conditions, comorbidities, and psychosocial or behavioral patterns** contributing to the condition.
- **Prospective follow-up (BPA 5):** follow patients prospectively to capture the program's impact on weight, weight-related comorbidities, and all related adverse outcomes. **Poor responders should be identified and offered a detailed evaluation and alternative therapy.**
- **Multidisciplinary team:** gastroenterologists adopting EBTs should develop expertise in obesity as a disease alongside medical obesity specialists, behavioral therapy professionals, registered dietitians, and bariatric surgeons.

- AGA 2017 gave no BMI cut-points, contraindication list or age criteria; the BMI bands above come from ASGE–ESGE 2024. Neither gives a class-wide contraindication list (ESG examples on [[endoscopic-sleeve-gastroplasty]]; balloon contraindications on [[intragastric-balloon]]). Surgical BMI thresholds: [[bariatric-surgery]].

## Recommendations by Device

*[[asge-esge-2024-primary-ebmt|ASGE–ESGE 2024]]. "Suggest" = conditional. All gastric and small-bowel devices are paired with LM.*

| Device | Recommendation | Strength / certainty | Key numbers | Status (2024) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **[[intragastric-balloon\|IGB]]** (Rec 2) | Suggest IGB + LM over LM | Conditional / moderate | 6.9% TWL more than LM at removal; regain expected within 6 mo of removal | Available. Periprocedural Recs 3–5 on [[intragastric-balloon]] |
| **EGR** — [[endoscopic-sleeve-gastroplasty\|ESG]], POSE, Endomina (Rec 6) | Suggest EGR + LM over LM; no device preferred | Conditional / moderate | 8.0% TWL more than LM at 12 mo | Available. Periprocedural Recs 7–10 on [[endoscopic-sleeve-gastroplasty]] |
| **Aspiration therapy** (Rec 11) | Suggest + LM over LM, **depending on device availability** | Conditional / low | 10.4% TWL more than LM at 12 mo; <25% still had the device at 4 y | **Off the market**; evidence only in class II–III |
| **TransPyloric Shuttle** (Rec 12) | **Only in a clinical trial** | No recommendation / knowledge gap | 6.7% TWL more than sham; SAE 2.8% (incl. 1 esophageal rupture) | FDA-approved (12-mo dwell) but not commercialized |
| **DJBL** (Rec 13) | Suggest + LM over LM, **in obesity with type 2 diabetes** | Conditional / moderate | Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) 0.73% lower and 5.4% TWL more than LM at 12 mo; SAE 8.5% | Not available; Conformité Européenne (CE) mark lost 2017 (administrative); new US trial ongoing |
| **Duodenal mucosal resurfacing** (Rec 14) | **Only in a clinical trial** (type 2 diabetes) | No recommendation / knowledge gap | HbA1c 0.3% lower than sham at 6 mo (not significant) | Trial use only |

## Gastric Devices

Balloon-by-balloon fill volumes, dwell times, efficacy, and adverse-event rates live on **[[intragastric-balloon]]**, which also carries the AGA 2021 graded recommendations ([[proton-pump-inhibitors|proton pump inhibitor (PPI)]] prophylaxis, antiemesis, supplementation, post-removal strategy).

### Space-Occupying and Pylorus-Modulating Devices

- **TransPyloric Shuttle** — large spherical silicone bulb tethered to a smaller cylindrical bulb; the small bulb advances into the duodenum with peristalsis, creating an **intermittent pyloric seal that delays gastric emptying** and prolongs satiety. The large bulb's size prevents migration out of the stomach.
- **Full Sense Device** — modified fully covered gastroesophageal stent: cylindrical esophageal component plus a gastric disk on struts applying pressure to the cardia to induce satiety. **No outcome data are reported for this device** in AGA 2017.

### Endoscopic Suturing and Plication

- **ESG** — full-thickness endoscopic sutures along the greater curvature that turn the stomach into a sleeve. Indications, outcomes, periprocedural orders and the standard technique are on **[[endoscopic-sleeve-gastroplasty]]**.
- **POSE** — grouped with ESG as EGR by ASGE–ESGE 2024. Transmural tissue-anchor plications restricting accommodation of the gastric fundus, plus **3 additional plications in the distal gastric body** to delay emptying.

> [!pitfall] POSE's sham-controlled result is far below its uncontrolled data
> Open-label single-arm and nonblinded trials reported **%TBWL 12.7%–15.5%**; the pivotal blinded sham-controlled RCT found only **4.94% ± 7 vs 1.38% ± 5.6 at 12 mo**. Weigh uncontrolled EBT series accordingly ([[aga-2017-endoscopic-bariatric-therapies]]).

### Aspiration Therapy

- **A-Tube / AspireAssist** — a silicone percutaneous gastrostomy tube with a skin port, through which the patient disposes of part of each ingested meal.
- **Rate of spontaneous fistula closure after A-Tube removal is not yet clear.**

## Small-Bowel Devices

- **EndoBarrier duodenojejunal bypass sleeve (DJBS)** — impermeable polymer liner anchored in the duodenal bulb by a barbed nitinol crown, extending **65 cm** into the small bowel; endoscopic removal at 12 months. Food bypasses the duodenum and proximal jejunum without mixing with pancreaticobiliary secretions until further downstream, manipulating the entero-insulin system.
  - Glycemic effect: HbA1c **−0.7** (95% confidence interval [CI] −1.76 to 0.2; *P* = .16) at 12 wk; **−1.7** (95% CI −2.5 to −0.86; *P* < .001) at 24 wk; **−1.5** (95% CI −2.2 to −0.78; *P* < .001) at 52 wk — an additional **−1%** (95% CI −1.67 to −0.4; *P* = .001) vs controls.
  - Mean percent excess weight loss (%EWL) difference vs control across 4 RCTs: **9.4%** (95% CI 8.26–10.65).
  - **Second-generation DJBS** devices with atraumatic anchoring and retrieval systems were in human trials at the time of review.

> [!pitfall] Hepatic abscess stopped the US EndoBarrier trial
> The pivotal multicenter double-blinded sham-controlled US trial was **terminated early after 325 of 500 enrollments because of a 3.5% incidence of hepatic abscess** ([[aga-2017-endoscopic-bariatric-therapies]]). The meta-analysis of earlier series put liver abscess at only 0.13%. A sponsor analysis linked the abscesses to the high-dose PPI used for bleeding prophylaxis in the US only, which fostered a bacterial biofilm on the device ([[asge-esge-2024-primary-ebmt|ASGE–ESGE 2024]]).

- **Earlier-development small-bowel EBTs:**
  - *Gastroduodenojejunal bypass sleeve.*
  - *Duodenal mucosal resurfacing* — thermal ablation of superficial duodenal mucosa by a catheter delivering hot water after a submucosal lift, hypothesized to reset diseased duodenal enteroendocrine cells and restore incretin signaling, with only a minimal transient fall in body weight.
  - *Self-assembling magnets* — create a dual-path enteral bypass between proximal jejunum and ileum, diverting bile and nutrients to the terminal ileum.

## Outcomes and Adverse Events

Best available evidence per non-balloon device ([[aga-2017-endoscopic-bariatric-therapies|AGA 2017]] Table 1; balloon-by-balloon data on [[intragastric-balloon]]; ESG on [[endoscopic-sleeve-gastroplasty#Efficacy]]; graded pooled effects in [[#Recommendations by Device]]). Devices were not compared head to head — study designs, populations, and time points differ.

|---|---|---|---|
| **TransPyloric Shuttle** | Prospective single-arm, n = 10 | %TBWL **14.5% ± 5.8 at 6 mo** | Gastric erosion **75%**, gastric ulcer **50%**, intolerance/early removal 10%, nausea 45% |
| **Full Sense** | None reported | — | — |

| **Aspiration therapy** | Pivotal US open-label RCT, 111 vs 60 | %TBWL among completers at 12 mo **14.2% ± 9.8% (n = 82) vs 4.9% ± 7% (n = 31)**; **≥25% EWL in 68.3% vs 25.8%**, **≥10% TBWL in 69.5% vs 19.4%** | Stoma granulation tissue 40.5%, stoma infection 14.4%, peritonitis 0.9%, gastric ulcer 0.9%; intolerance/early removal **26% before 52 wk, 27% after** |

## Technique

Balloon placement and removal technique is on [[intragastric-balloon]]; the standard ESG steps are on [[endoscopic-sleeve-gastroplasty#Technique]].

### Primary Obesity Surgery Endoluminal

- A large overtube-style peroral incisionless operating platform with **4 working channels**: a slim endoscope plus three instruments — a jawed grasper that creates and approximates full-thickness **serosa-to-serosa** tissue folds, a helical-tipped tissue grasper that helps capture target tissue for the miniplication, and a needle-tipped suture-anchor delivery catheter that installs a pair of tissue anchors joined by suture, holding the plication **until serosal fusion**.

### Aspiration Therapy Tube

- A-Tube inserted like a standard [[enteral-access|percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy (PEG)]] tube. **2 weeks later** the external portion is shortened and a valved skin port is attached flush with the skin. The AspireAssist device connects to the port; an attached water reservoir flushes boluses of tap water into the stomach to facilitate aspiration cycles. Aspiration is by **siphon, ideally 20 min after the meal**, removes **about a third of the meal**, and takes **~10–15 min**.

### EndoBarrier

- Sleeve and anchoring crown restrained in a delivery capsule, advanced to the duodenal bulb **over a stiff wire under endoscopic and fluoroscopic guidance**; sleeve then advanced to the proximal jejunum and the anchoring crown deployed in the bulb. Removal at 12 mo: grasp the polypropylene drawstring with a custom device that collapses the crown into a **foreign-body retrieval hood**, avoiding gastric or esophageal trauma on withdrawal.

### TransPyloric Shuttle

- Delivery system advanced through an **overtube** into the stomach, where the device is deployed and self-assembles.

### Full Sense

- Deployed and removed endoscopically.

## Training and Credentialing

- **Knowledge base (BPA 6):** clinicians should have comprehensive knowledge of the **indications, contraindications, risks, benefits, and outcomes of individual EBTs**, plus practical knowledge of the risks and benefits of alternative obesity therapies.
- **ESG:** the 6 cognitive skills the 2025 expert consensus requires are on [[endoscopic-sleeve-gastroplasty#Training]].
- **Privileging (BPA 7):** institutions should establish **specific guidelines applied consistently across disciplines** for granting EBT privileges, reflecting the knowledge and technical skill required before privileges are granted.
- Clinicians also managing the complications of bariatric/metabolic surgery endoscopically carry a separate, more demanding skill set — fluoroscopy, stent deployment and retrieval, stenosis management, percutaneous drain management; see [[bariatric-surgery-leak]].

## Open Questions

- **Matching patient to device is unsolved** — AGA 2017 calls for research identifying the physiologic, behavioral, and clinical predictors of success for each EBT.
- **Combination and sequencing untested** — tandem and sequential use of EBTs with obesity pharmacotherapy, on top of a comprehensive lifestyle program, is called the key to durable weight loss but has not been studied.
- **Training gap** — obesity-management principles should be better incorporated into the gastroenterology fellowship curriculum.
