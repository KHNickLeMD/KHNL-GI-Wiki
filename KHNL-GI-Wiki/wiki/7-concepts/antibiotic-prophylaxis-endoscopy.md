Framework for when periprocedural antibiotics are — and are not — indicated for GI endoscopy. Bottom line: prophylaxis is **not** given solely to prevent infective endocarditis; it is reserved for a handful of scenarios that prevent local infection (incomplete biliary drainage, PEG/PEJ, EUS-FNA of cysts) plus [[cirrhosis|cirrhotics]] admitted with GI bleeding. Per [[asge-2015-antibiotic-prophylaxis]].

## Key Principle — Not for IE
- **Do not give antibiotic prophylaxis solely to prevent infective endocarditis (IE)** for any GI endoscopic procedure, regardless of cardiac lesion (aligns with AHA). *(Rec 1, 444B)*
- No causal link between endoscopy and IE; ~25 temporally associated IE cases across millions of procedures; routine daily activity (brushing/flossing/chewing) causes bacteremia often exceeding endoscopy rates.
- **Exception:** high-risk cardiac patients with an **established GI infection** in which enterococci may be part of the flora may receive coverage — directed at treating the infection, not the procedure. *(Rec 2, 44BB)*

### Highest-Risk Cardiac Conditions (the exception list)

The exception is conditional on this list, so the list is the decision ([[asge-2015-antibiotic-prophylaxis]], Table 2 — AHA's cardiac conditions with the highest risk of an adverse outcome from IE):

- **Prosthetic cardiac valve** (mechanical or bioprosthetic)
- **Previous infective endocarditis**
- **Cardiac transplant recipients who develop cardiac valvulopathy**
- **Congenital heart disease (CHD)**, limited to:
  - Unrepaired **cyanotic** CHD, including palliative shunts and conduits
  - Completely repaired CHD with prosthetic material/device (surgical or catheter) — **only for the first 6 months** after the procedure
  - Repaired CHD with **residual defects** at or adjacent to the site of a prosthetic patch or device

**What "coverage" means here:** when such a patient has an established GI infection (e.g. cholangitis), or is already receiving antibiotics to prevent wound infection/sepsis from a GI procedure, the regimen should **include an agent active against enterococci** — **penicillin, ampicillin, piperacillin, or vancomycin**. Enterococci are singled out because they are the only GI-tract organisms likely to cause IE; AHA reiterates that **no study has shown this prevents enterococcal IE**.

## Bacteremia Risk by Procedure
- **High:** esophageal dilation (12–22%; higher with malignant strictures, multiple dilators), variceal sclerotherapy (up to 52%, mean ~14.6%), instrumentation of obstructed bile ducts.
- **Low:** diagnostic [[upper-endoscopy|EGD]] ± biopsy, [[colonoscopy]] ± biopsy/[[polypectomy]], [[endoscopic-ultrasound|EUS]]-FNA of solid lesions, [[variceal-upper-gi-bleeding|variceal band ligation]].

## When Prophylaxis IS Indicated
- **[[ercp|ERCP]] with anticipated incomplete biliary drainage** (eg, hilar strictures, [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis|PSC]], post–[[liver-transplantation|liver transplant]], known/suspected obstruction) → cover biliary flora (enteric gram-negatives + enterococci); **continue after the procedure if drainage remains incomplete**. *Not* needed when complete drainage is expected and no obstruction is suspected. *(Recs 3–4)*
  - Why incomplete drainage is the trigger: it predicted **91%** of all post-ERCP sepsis in one study; and in the few trials showing benefit, antibiotics were **continued for several days after** the procedure.
  - **Already in [[acute-cholangitis|cholangitis]]?** Treat the cholangitis with antibiotics — **additional single-dose ERCP prophylaxis is not recommended** on top of treatment.
  - Even with prophylaxis, **liver transplant recipients remain at higher risk** (1.2% infection rate vs 0.28% overall).
- **[[enteral-access|PEG/PEJ placement]]** → parenteral **cefazolin 1 g IV 30 minutes before** the procedure (or an antibiotic with equivalent coverage of cutaneous organisms); pooled OR for peristomal infection **0.36** (95% CI 0.26–0.50). Where **MRSA is endemic**, preprocedure screening (nose, throat, perineum, broken skin) with decolonization of positives reduces MRSA peristomal infection. PEJ is unstudied but treated the same. *(Rec 8, 4444)*
- **EUS-FNA of cystic lesions** (mediastinal and pancreatic/peripancreatic [[pancreatic-cysts|cysts]]) → antibiotics before, often continued 3–5 days, to prevent cyst infection. **Contested for pancreatic cysts:** the first RCT ([[colan-hernandez-2020-eus-fna-panc-cyst-antibiotics|Colán-Hernández 2020]], n=226) found infection risk low (0.44%) and **not** reduced by ciprofloxacin — arguing prophylaxis is **not** routinely needed for pancreatic-cyst FNA. The ASGE 2015 guideline still *suggests* it (44BB); duplication/mediastinal cysts (excluded from the RCT) remain higher-risk and should still be covered.
- **Cirrhosis admitted with acute GI bleeding** → antibiotics for **all** such patients **at admission, regardless of endoscopy** — IV **ceftriaxone** (or oral **norfloxacin** if intolerant). Reduces infections, rebleeding, and mortality. See [[antibiotic-prophylaxis-cirrhosis]], [[variceal-upper-gi-bleeding]], [[portal-hypertension|portal hypertensive]] bleeding, and [[spontaneous-bacterial-peritonitis|SBP]].
- **Continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis** → *suggested* before lower-GI endoscopy to prevent peritonitis. Risk of peritonitis after colonoscopy **without** prophylaxis was **6.3%** in a retrospective study (biopsy/[[polypectomy]] did not further increase it); no peritonitis occurred in those given antibiotics, though the difference was not statistically significant. *(Rec 12; based on observational data only)*
  - **Regimen (International Society for Peritoneal Dialysis):** **ampicillin 1 g** plus a **single dose of an aminoglycoside**, ± **metronidazole**, IV **immediately before** the procedure. Acceptable alternative: prophylactic antibiotics **intraperitoneally the night before**.
  - **Also required:** the **abdomen must be emptied of dialysate** before any procedure involving the abdomen or pelvis, including colonoscopy.

### Regimens

The only agents and doses ASGE 2015 actually specifies:

| Indication | Regimen as stated |
|---|---|
| PEG/PEJ | **Cefazolin 1 g IV, 30 min before** (or equivalent cutaneous coverage) |
| [[cirrhosis]] + acute GI bleed | **IV ceftriaxone** on admission; **oral norfloxacin** if allergic/intolerant |
| Peritoneal dialysis, lower-GI endoscopy | **Ampicillin 1 g + single-dose aminoglycoside ± metronidazole IV** immediately before |
| High-risk cardiac + established GI infection | Include an anti-enterococcal agent: **penicillin, ampicillin, piperacillin, or vancomycin** |

> **Gap — no agent, dose, or duration is given for ERCP or for EUS-FNA of cysts.** ASGE 2015 specifies only the *spectrum* for ERCP ("enteric gram-negative organisms and enterococci") and only the *duration* for cystic FNA ("before, and often for 3 to 5 days following"); the observational data it cites used a fluoroquinolone for 3 days after pancreatic-cyst FNA. It also gives **no ceftriaxone dose**. Doses for these come from other pages/sources, not from this guideline — see [[antibiotic-prophylaxis-cirrhosis]] for the cirrhosis regimen.

## When Prophylaxis Is NOT Indicated
- Solely to prevent IE (any procedure, any cardiac lesion).
- ERCP with complete drainage and no suspected obstruction.
- Diagnostic EUS / EUS-FNA of **solid** lesions (upper or lower GI).
- **Synthetic vascular grafts** or other nonvalvular cardiovascular devices (eg, implantable electronic devices) — any procedure.
- **Orthopedic prostheses** — any procedure (AAOS blanket-prophylaxis statement was withdrawn for lack of evidence). *(Rec 11, 444B)*

## Immunocompromise and Neutropenia

There is no ASGE recommendation here — the guidance is graded by neutrophil count, so the count is the decision point ([[asge-2015-antibiotic-prophylaxis]]):

| Group | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Immunocompromised with **normal neutrophil count** (eg, organ transplant recipients, HIV) | Routine prophylaxis **not recommended** — no data that they are at increased risk |
| **Severe immunosuppression** — ANC **<500 cells/µL**, advanced hematologic malignancy, bone marrow transplant | **Insufficient evidence** for or against; **individualize** |

- Patients with **severe neutropenia (ANC <500 cells/µL)** and/or advanced hematologic malignancy *are* at increased risk of bacteremia and sepsis after endoscopy; the guideline calls prophylaxis **"prudent"** in this group — especially before **high-bacteremia-risk procedures** — while stating plainly that it **has not been studied in clinical trials**. Flag this as expert opinion, not evidence.
- **Note the internal tension:** the ERCP section calls prophylaxis prudent in severe neutropenia, while the immunocompromised-patient section says the evidence is insufficient and the decision must be individualized. Both sentences are the same guideline; neither is a graded recommendation.
