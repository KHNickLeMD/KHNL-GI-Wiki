The hepatic venous pressure gradient (HVPG) is the reference (gold-standard) method for measuring **sinusoidal portal pressure** and diagnosing/staging [[portal-hypertension|portal hypertension (PH)]] in cirrhosis. Obtained transjugularly (or transfemorally) at experienced centers.

> **Its role has narrowed.** [[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension|Baveno VIII (2026)]] has no HVPG chapter: the earlier workshops' HVPG statements are reprinted in a summary box of still-valid recommendations, with an explicit caveat that they "**should be interpreted in the context of current Baveno VIII recommendations which favor [[noninvasive-liver-disease-assessment|non-invasive tests (NITs)]] over routine HVPG measurements in daily clinical practice**." Baveno VIII 1.11 holds that validated NITs are **sufficiently accurate to exclude or identify clinically significant portal hypertension (CSPH) and are similarly predictive of hepatic decompensation**, and its 1.18 describes a paradigm moving from estimating CSPH probability toward **directly predicting decompensation**. HVPG remains the reference standard and the arbiter in specific decisions — see [[#Where HVPG is still the answer]].

## Definition

- **HVPG = WHVP − FHVP** (wedged/occluded hepatic vein pressure − free hepatic vein pressure), reported in mmHg — an *indirect* estimate of the portocaval gradient (portal vein pressure − inferior vena cava [IVC] pressure) ([[baveno-vii-2022-portal-hypertension]]).
- WHVP (balloon-occluded) reflects sinusoidal pressure, which in sinusoidal cirrhosis equals portal pressure; FHVP serves as the internal zero.
- Because it relies on the *wedged* pressure as a proxy, HVPG accurately reflects portal pressure only when the block is **sinusoidal** (i.e. [[cirrhosis]]) — see [[#Limitations — when HVPG misleads]].

Methodology — Baveno VII statements 1.1–1.8 ([[baveno-vii-2022-portal-hypertension]]) plus the AASLD measurement box:

- **Catheter:** end-hole, compliant **balloon-occlusion** catheter (reduces random error of WHVP) — preferred over a straight catheter (1.1); balloon diameter 10–12 mm (AASLD).
- **Confirm occlusion:** inject a small volume of contrast with the balloon inflated to confirm a satisfactory wedged position and **exclude hepatic veno-venous communications** (1.2); such communications underestimate WHVP and must be reported (1.3). Rinse the catheter thoroughly before measuring (AASLD).
- **Tracings:** slow-speed (≤7.5 mm/s) **permanent** tracings (paper or electronic); on-screen digital readings are much less accurate and should not be used (1.5). Scale 0–40 or 0–50 mmHg with 1 grid mark = 1 mmHg where possible; precalibrated transducers levelled at the **midaxillary line**; print the calibration scale and zero before any measurement (AASLD).
- **Stabilization:** WHVP requires a **minimum 1-minute** recording with attention to stability over the last 20–30 s; record **in triplicate** (1.6). FHVP needs only 15–20 s. **Each reading should be within 2 mmHg of the one before it — greater variability means the technique must be reassessed** (AASLD).
- **FHVP:** measure within **2–3 cm of the hepatic vein–IVC confluence** (1.8); AASLD places the tip **2–4 cm inside the hepatic vein** for the FHVP paired with WHVP and adds a second FHVP **1–2 cm from the hepatic vein outlet**, plus IVC and right atrial pressures. Measure IVC pressure as an internal control at the hepatic vein ostium: if FHVP is **>2 mmHg above IVC**, rule out hepatic vein outflow obstruction with contrast/venography (1.8; AASLD).
- **Reference gradient:** the **wedged-to-free** gradient has superior prognostic value to wedged-to-right-atrial and is the standard; right atrial pressure can be measured to rule out a post-hepatic component (1.7).
- **[[endoscopy-sedation|Sedation]]:** deep sedation causes inaccurate HVPG (1.4); if light sedation is needed, **low-dose midazolam 0.02 mg/kg** does not alter HVPG and is acceptable. Avoid fentanyl and propofol (AASLD) — propofol-based general anesthesia measurably depresses HVPG ([[wang-2026-eus-ppg-delphi-consensus]]).
- **Artifacts:** quiet room; patient breathing quietly, not moving or speaking, not breathing deeply or snoring. Label every measurement and discard tracings with movement, cough, snoring or speech artifact (AASLD).
- **Risks:** moderately invasive — jugular access injury, induced arrhythmia, radiation exposure; interpretation needs specific expertise, which keeps HVPG in specialized centers (AASLD).

## Interpretation — pressure ladder

| HVPG (mmHg) | Interpretation ([[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]], [[baveno-vii-2022-portal-hypertension]]) |
|---|---|
| 1–5 | **Normal** — the gradient in healthy people ([[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]]) |
| >5 | Sinusoidal portal hypertension (Baveno VII 1.9) |
| 6–9 | **Mild PH** — compensated cirrhosis without CSPH; managed as its own stage ([[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]]) |
| **≥10** | **CSPH** — Baveno VII 1.10, *changed* from >10 to ≥10. **Qualifier: the gold-standard definition is stated for viral- and alcohol-related cirrhosis**; in metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH)/primary biliary cholangitis (PBC)/porto-sinusoidal vascular disorder (PSVD) the gradient can miss CSPH (see [[#Limitations — when HVPG misleads]]). Threshold above which varices, [[ascites]], and decompensation risk rise; predicts decompensation/death after liver resection for [[hepatocellular-carcinoma\|hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)]] (1.18) |
| ≥16 | Increased **short-term mortality after non-hepatic abdominal surgery** (1.19) |
| **≥20** | Measured **at the time of acute hemorrhage** → predicts treatment failure in acute variceal bleeding; a criterion for **pre-emptive [[tips\|transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS)]]**. ⚠ **[[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension\|Baveno VIII]] 5.31 changed this from Baveno VII's ">20 mmHg" to "≥20 mmHg"** — the boundary value itself now qualifies |
| **<10, measured off [[nonselective-beta-blockers\|carvedilol/conventional nonselective beta-blocker (cNSBB)]]** | **Confirms resolution of CSPH in a recompensated patient** — the trigger to stop the beta-blocker (Baveno VIII 7.16, new). The "off the drug" qualifier is the decision; a gradient measured on a nonselective beta-blocker (NSBB) does not answer the question |

*Full noninvasive CSPH staging ([[liver-stiffness-measurement|liver stiffness measurement (LSM)]] "rule of 5", spleen stiffness), and management by stratum, live on [[portal-hypertension]].*

> [!pitfall] Don't chase the gradient during a bleed
> [[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension|AASLD 2023]] endorses pre-emptive TIPS when an HVPG >20 mmHg *has already been* obtained, but calls measuring the pressure during acute bleeding challenging and does not recommend doing it — its own pre-emptive TIPS criteria are the Child-Pugh ones.

> ⚠ **The "HVPG ≥12 mmHg = variceal bleeding threshold" is not stated by the guidelines.** [[baveno-vii-2022-portal-hypertension|Baveno VII]], [[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension|AASLD 2023]] and [[wang-2026-eus-ppg-delphi-consensus|the endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)-guided portosystemic pressure gradient (EUS-PPG) consensus]] give **12 mmHg only as the post-TIPS portal pressure gradient (PPG) target** (Baveno 1.23; AASLD: NSBB not required after TIPS if the gradient is under 12 mm Hg) — see [[#HVPG response to therapy]]. The bleeding-threshold figure comes from the primary hemodynamic literature (Groszmann/Garcia-Tsao portal-pressure threshold studies).

## HVPG response to therapy

- In alcohol-related or viral cirrhosis, a **decrease in HVPG in response to NSBBs** is associated with a significant reduction in variceal bleeding and other decompensating events (Baveno VII 1.14).
- **What counts as an HVPG response to an NSBB — the numbers** (Baveno V/VI, reprinted in the [[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension|Baveno VIII]] still-valid box). In the setting of **primary prophylaxis**:
  - **Chronic NSBB treatment:** a decrease in HVPG of **at least 10% from baseline, or to ≤12 mmHg**, is clinically relevant.
  - **Acute response to intravenous propranolol** may be used to identify NSBB responders — again **a decrease of 10%, or to ≤12 mmHg**.
  - ⚠ These are **response criteria for beta-blockade**, not a bleeding-risk threshold. The 12 mmHg figure appears in three unrelated roles across this literature — NSBB response target, post-TIPS PPG target, and (removed) bleeding threshold — so always state which one is meant.
- The **optimal percent/absolute HVPG decrease** associated with reduced decompensation **after removal/suppression of the aetiology** **has not been established** (Baveno VII 3.6, retained in the Baveno VIII still-valid box) — do not carry the NSBB-response numbers over to the aetiological-cure setting.
- Treat with NSBBs **when clinically indicated, independent of the ability to measure HVPG** (Baveno VI/VII, retained) — NITs have largely displaced HVPG for routine treatment decisions.
- **Post-[[tips|TIPS]] PPG** — measure **before and after** insertion (1.20, A.1):
  - **Target: absolute PPG <12 mmHg** — near-complete protection from portal hypertensive bleeding, and the **preferred** target for hemodynamic success (1.23, **A.1**). A **relative reduction ≥50%** from pre-TIPS baseline "may also be useful" but carries a weaker grade (1.23, **B.2**) — not interchangeable with the absolute target.
  - **Where to measure:** main portal vein and IVC at the shunt outflow (1.21).
  - **When to measure:** the *immediate* post-TIPS PPG can be distorted by general anesthesia, vasoactive agents, or hemodynamic instability and may not represent the long-term PPG; measure in a **hemodynamically stable, non-sedated** patient (1.22).
  - **Re-measure** if TIPS dysfunction is suspected clinically or on Doppler ultrasound, to decide on revision (1.24).

## Prognostic thresholds

*Bands and their thresholds: see [[#Interpretation — pressure ladder]].*

- HVPG carries prognostic information in **both compensated and decompensated cirrhosis** — at baseline, in response to vasoactive drug therapy, after elimination of the cause of cirrhosis, before liver resection for HCC, and before any major operation ([[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]]).
- Test–retest reliability is excellent but varies with disease stage and cause: lower in decompensated patients, higher in alcohol-related disease (Baveno VII 1.17).
- **CSPH does not require a catheter to diagnose:** clinical decompensation, [[variceal-upper-gi-bleeding|gastroesophageal varices]] on endoscopy, or portosystemic collaterals / hepatofugal flow on imaging are each *sufficient* to diagnose CSPH ([[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]], guidance statement 6) — HVPG is confirmatory, not obligatory.

## Where HVPG is still the answer

Baveno VIII moves routine CSPH diagnosis to non-invasive tests, but the catheter still decides in three places:

1. **Confirming CSPH resolution before stopping an NSBB in a recompensated patient** — carvedilol/cNSBB may be discontinued only once resolution is confirmed (7.18), by an HVPG measured **off the drug** at the threshold in the ladder above (7.16). Non-invasive alternatives may also rule out CSPH here — LSM <10 kPa, or LSM <15 kPa plus spleen stiffness measurement (SSM) <25 kPa — both weakly graded (7.19).
2. **Investigating an inconclusive cirrhosis/compensated advanced chronic liver disease (cACLD) workup, or suspected [[porto-sinusoidal-vascular-disorder|PSVD/non-cirrhotic portal fibrosis (NCPF)]], in a patient with non-cirrhotic [[portal-vein-thrombosis|portal vein thrombosis (PVT)]]** — [[liver-biopsy|liver biopsy]] **and** HVPG should be considered (9.4).
3. **Clinical trials** — HVPG measurements should be encouraged in trials of novel therapies, though they are not essential where portal-hypertension endpoints are well defined (Baveno VI, retained).

## Limitations — when HVPG misleads

HVPG measures the **sinusoidal** gradient, so it **underestimates presinusoidal portal hypertension**. Where a presinusoidal cause is suspected, [[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension|AASLD 2023]] recommends **endoscopic screening for the complications of PH** rather than relying on the gradient.

- **[[primary-biliary-cholangitis|Primary biliary cholangitis]]:** a presinusoidal component cannot be assessed by HVPG → HVPG may underestimate PH prevalence/severity (Baveno VII 1.11).
- **Granulomatous diseases:** also named as presinusoidal, where HVPG does not accurately estimate portal pressure ([[aasld-2023-portal-hypertension]]).
- **[[nafld-masld|metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD)]]-related cACLD:** **HVPG may underestimate the true portal pressure gradient** ([[baveno-viii-2026-portal-hypertension]] 2.5, new). An HVPG ≥10 mmHg still marks a significantly increased decompensation risk, but the risk below 10 mmHg is **lower, not absent** — clinical signs of PH can occur at HVPG <10 mmHg in this population (Baveno VII 1.12, retained).
- **[[porto-sinusoidal-vascular-disorder|PSVD/NCPF]]:** clinical signs of PH with HVPG <10 mmHg should prompt this diagnosis (Baveno VII 1.13). Baveno VIII 1.6 adds the elastographic version of the same trigger — **LSM <10 kPa with clinical or imaging signs of PH** should raise suspicion of vascular liver disease. The size of the miss is large: in PSVD with CSPH, mean EUS-PPG was 16.7 vs HVPG 5.5 mmHg, and only EUS-PPG separated those who decompensated ([[wang-2026-eus-ppg-delphi-consensus]]).
- **Veno-venous communications** and outflow obstruction cause measurement error if not identified (Baveno VII 1.3, 1.8).

Where a **presinusoidal** cause is suspected, expert consensus favors **[[interventional-eus-vascular|EUS-PPG]]** over HVPG, because it measures hepatic *and* portal vein pressures **directly** by needle puncture rather than via the wedged proxy. Whether it is also the better measurement in MASH is an open research question, not yet a recommendation ([[wang-2026-eus-ppg-delphi-consensus]]).
