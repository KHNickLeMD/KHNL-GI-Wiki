# IBD in Pregnancy

*Reproductive overlay on [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]], from preconception to the infant's first year. Guiding principle of the global consensus: **maternal health best supports infant health** — the risk to the pregnancy is uncontrolled disease, not the drugs that control it ([[global-consensus-2025-pregnancy-ibd]]). Liver disease in pregnancy: [[liver-disease-in-pregnancy]]. Hyperemesis: [[nausea-and-vomiting-of-pregnancy]].*

## Fertility

- Fertility may be **mildly reduced** in IBD overall — mostly [[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease (CD)]], only marginally in [[ulcerative-colitis|ulcerative colitis (UC)]] (Conditional, very low). Mechanism offered: **reduced ovarian reserve**.
- **Active disease increases infertility risk** vs inactive disease (Strong, very low) — the one modifiable input.
- **Prior ileal pouch anal anastomosis (IPAA) lowers fertility** in UC (Conditional, very low): pooled infertility 13.3% pre-IPAA vs 43.0% post-IPAA; relative risk 4.17, attenuated to 2.96 on sensitivity analysis.
- **Assisted reproduction works as well as in women without IBD**, measured by live birth — including after pelvic surgery for IBD (Conditional, very low).
- **Oocyte retrieval does not increase flare risk.** No data on 5-aminosalicylates (5-ASA), thiopurines, biologics or Janus kinase inhibitors (JAKis) affecting ovarian stimulation; panel practice is to continue them through egg harvesting.
- Avoid concurrent sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P) modulators with gonadotropin-releasing hormone agonists (shared QT-prolongation risk).
- **Refer to a fertility specialist if not pregnant after 6 months of timed intercourse.**

> [!pearl] Voluntary childlessness is a knowledge problem
> Women with IBD report voluntary childlessness at **18% vs 6%** in the general population, and nearly half score poorly on pregnancy knowledge. Counseling — even a single gastroenterologist visit — improves knowledge, adherence and disease control ([[global-consensus-2025-pregnancy-ibd]]).

## Preconception

**Preconception counseling is recommended for every woman with IBD of childbearing age** (Strong, low), ideally ≥6 months before attempting conception, and restarted at every new medication. One prospective study: preconception care raised medication adherence (97.4% vs 86.4%) and halved disease activity in pregnancy (18.5% vs 34.0%).

**Checklist** ([[global-consensus-2025-pregnancy-ibd]] Table 5):

| Domain | Action |
|---|---|
| **Remission** | Documented **clinical + biochemical remission 3–6 months before conception** — normal C-reactive protein (CRP), **fecal calprotectin (FC) < 150**; optimize drug concentrations ([[thiopurines]], [[anti-tnf-agents\|anti-tumor necrosis factor (TNF) agents]]); endoscopic and/or radiographic remission ([[intestinal-ultrasound]], magnetic resonance enterography or computed tomography [CT]) |
| **Stop teratogens** | **[[methotrexate]] ≥1 month** before conception (full clearance 1–3 months); JAKis and S1P modulators per [[#Drug-by-Drug]] |
| **Folate** | Prenatal vitamin with folic acid **≥1 month** before conception · **2 mg/d for 3 months preconception if on sulfasalazine** · **5 mg/d** if extensive small bowel disease, malabsorption, or family history of neural tube defects |
| **Nutrition** | Assess folate, iron, vitamin B12, vitamin D ([[nutrition-in-ibd]], [[iron-deficiency-anemia]]). Achieve ideal weight; record baseline weight for gestational weight gain (GWG) |
| **Vaccines** | Influenza, hepatitis B, measles-mumps-rubella (MMR), varicella up to date ([[ibd-preventive-care]]). MMR and varicella are **live — avoid on high-dose steroid, thiopurine and/or biologic**; any **live vaccine should be given 28 days before pregnancy** |
| **Team** | Maternal-fetal medicine and/or colorectal surgeon review where relevant; communicate the IBD plan to the rest of the team |
| **Screening** | Cervical cancer screening current; encourage regular physical activity and smoking/substance cessation |

- Where sustained remission is not achievable (difficult disease, advanced maternal age), **optimize as far as possible rather than defer indefinitely**.

### Contraception

**Long-acting reversible contraception (LARC) is preferred over estrogen-containing methods** in IBD.

| Method | Disadvantages | Benefits |
|---|---|---|
| Barrier | Least effective — emphasize correct use | No side effects; protects against sexually transmitted infection |
| Combined oral contraceptive pill (COCP) / progesterone-only pill (POP) | COCP raises venous thromboembolism (VTE) risk; more unintended pregnancies. Prefer an alternative if higher VTE risk (prior VTE, current smoking, active disease, steroids or JAKis, recent surgery). **POP must be taken within the same 3-hour window daily** | COCP may improve cyclical IBD symptoms; **no increased VTE risk with POP** |
| LARC — intrauterine device (IUD) or implant | Long-acting (**IUD 5–10 y, implant 3 y**); consider if not planning pregnancy within 1 year; insertion cost and pain | **Failure rate <1%**; no estrogen, no absorption or VTE concern; levonorgestrel IUD may improve cyclical symptoms |

- Combined estrogen oral contraception has been linked to increased need for Crohn's surgery; progesterone-only has not.

## Monitoring Disease Activity

| Test | In pregnancy |
|---|---|
| CRP, erythrocyte sedimentation rate | **Can be elevated by pregnancy itself** — CRP stable but at higher-than-normal levels |
| **FC** | **Effective and unaffected by pregnancy physiology** (healthy medians 23–30 mcg/g across gestation and postpartum). **Cut-off >100 mcg/g** gave the best sensitivity and negative predictive value for active disease. Check each trimester |
| Serum drug concentrations (thiopurines, anti-TNF) | **May vary in pregnancy** — see [[therapeutic-drug-monitoring-ibd]] |
| [[intestinal-ultrasound]] | Low risk; accurate in trimesters 1–2, **technically challenging in trimester 3** (sigmoid and terminal ileum visualization falls). Sensitivity 74–84%, specificity 83–98% |
| Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) | Low risk in any trimester. **Avoid gadolinium — potential teratogen — especially first trimester** |
| CT | Relatively safe; a single scan (**~50 mGy**) is below the level of concern, but use only when nothing else is available |
| Endoscopy | **Low risk; perform only if it will change management.** Most IBD procedures are **unsedated flexible sigmoidoscopy** — no prep, no sedation. Full [[colonoscopy]] or an advanced procedure → **fetal monitoring**. **Avoid [[capsule-endoscopy\|capsule endoscopy]]** (obstruction risk in CD) |

- Defer elective endoscopy to postpartum; do non-emergent procedures in the second trimester where possible ([[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]]).

## Trimester Plan

| Trimester | Actions |
|---|---|
| **First** | Gastroenterology visit · **start low-dose aspirin by week 12–16** · follow as a **high-risk obstetric patient**, refer to maternal-fetal medicine where available · IBD monitoring labs · nutritional assessment |
| **Second** | Gastroenterology visit or check-in · labs · nutritional assessment |
| **Third** | Gastroenterology visit or check-in · labs · nutritional assessment · **decide mode of delivery** (see [[#Delivery]]) · **consider VTE prophylaxis at delivery or postpartum** |

- **Aspirin to prevent preterm preeclampsia** (Conditional, low): **150–162 mg daily starting week 12–16**, with obstetric guidance. Benefit in the general at-risk population required initiation **≤16 weeks at ≥100 mg/d**. Many obstetric guidelines stop it after **36–37 weeks** (bleeding at delivery). Low-dose aspirin did **not** increase IBD flares in two cohorts.
- **Nutrition targets:** folic acid 0.4 mg/d from ≥4 weeks preconception (higher in IBD); **elemental iron 30–60 mg/d, up to 100 mg/d in IBD**; **intravenous iron only in the 2nd and 3rd trimesters — avoid in the first (possible teratogenicity)**; calcium if dietary intake is low; **routine calcium + vitamin D on corticosteroids**. Record weight and body mass index at every antenatal visit; dietitian referral for inadequate GWG or malnutrition risk.

> [!pitfall] Every IBD pregnancy is a high-risk pregnancy
> Even quiescent IBD carries excess inadequate GWG, gestational diabetes, preeclampsia, VTE and preterm delivery — and these women are young and otherwise well, so they are easily missed. Treat the pregnancy as high risk and involve maternal-fetal medicine ([[global-consensus-2025-pregnancy-ibd]]).

## Active Disease and Surgery

- **Treat active disease with the same therapies as a nonpregnant patient**, except do not *start* thiopurines, methotrexate, JAKis or S1P modulators in pregnancy. Choose the more rapidly effective option to protect the pregnancy.
- **Urgent and emergent surgery is done when required, not scheduled by trimester** (Conditional, very low) — the "operate only in the second trimester" adage is not supported by modern data. **Elective surgery waits until after pregnancy.**
- Surgical indications unchanged: acute refractory colitis, perforation, abscess, refractory hemorrhage, bowel obstruction. Perform at **expert centers with obstetric and neonatal support**.
- **Antibiotics**: an appropriate course of a low-risk antibiotic is acceptable for infection, fistula or [[pouchitis]]. Metronidazole, amoxicillin ± clavulanate and ciprofloxacin are well supported; **no human pregnancy data for rifaximin**. Group B streptococcus testing ~week 36 with intrapartum penicillin-based antibiotics if positive.
- **[[calcineurin-inhibitors|Calcineurin inhibitors]]** (cyclosporine, tacrolimus) may be started or continued **only when no viable alternative exists**, with careful monitoring; safety data favor anti-TNF for salvage.

### Drug-by-Drug

*Agent-level detail — grade of each recommendation, monitoring, infant clearance, lactation — on the drug page linked in the grid above.*

- **Placental transfer.** Monoclonal antibodies **cannot passively cross during organogenesis (weeks 3–8)** but actively cross via Fc receptors **after week 14**, with **80% of transfer in the third trimester** — so infants are born with measurable drug but were unexposed during organogenesis. **Certolizumab is a Fab' fragment with no Fc portion → does not actively cross**; natalizumab and mirikizumab have an immunoglobulin G4 backbone and transfer less efficiently than the other (immunoglobulin G1) agents. Small molecules diffuse across throughout, including during organogenesis — the reason JAKis and S1P modulators are the restricted classes.
- **Dose stability.** Infliximab concentrations *rise* in later pregnancy on unchanged dosing; vedolizumab concentrations fall as weight rises; ustekinumab stays stable → **keep pre-pregnancy dosing regardless of pregnancy weight gain**.
- **Biosimilars.** Continue like the originator, in pregnancy and in lactation — effectiveness, safety and pharmacokinetics are expected to mirror it.
- **S1P modulators (ozanimod, etrasimod).** Relatively contraindicated; the teratogenicity signal is animal data. Stop **ozanimod ≥3 months** and **etrasimod ≥1–2 weeks** before conception unless no effective alternative maintains maternal health. **Do not breastfeed** on either (no human lactation data). As with the JAKis, a woman with no near-term conception plans should not be denied them.

## Delivery

**Non-obstetric indications for Cesarean** ([[global-consensus-2025-pregnancy-ibd]]):

| Finding | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Active perianal CD at delivery** | **Cesarean** (Conditional, very low) |
| **Rectovaginal fistula, current or prior** | **Cesarean** (consensus statement) |
| **Prior complex perianal disease** | Consider Cesarean; avoid prolonged labor |
| **Prior IPAA** | **Consider Cesarean** (Conditional, very low); ideally at a center with colorectal surgical support |

- Rationale: vaginal delivery with active perianal disease risks sphincter damage, abscess exacerbation and disease progression — **active perianal disease carries an 11-fold higher risk of 4th-degree perineal tear**. Independent predictors of postpartum perianal flare were prior perianal disease (odds ratio 22) and active perianal disease (odds ratio 96); see [[perianal-fistulizing-crohns-disease]]. [[aga-2024-pregnancy-gi-liver]] additionally lists **[[anorectal-abscess|perianal abscess]] and active rectal disease** as reasons to avoid vaginal birth.
- **Inactive perianal disease is not itself an indication** — evidence is conflicting and small; individualize.
- After IPAA, anorectal manometry showed higher mean squeeze pressure after Cesarean (**150 vs 120 mm Hg**) and more anterior sphincter defects after vaginal delivery, though pooled continence outcomes did not differ.
- **Count the Cesareans**: **>3 can cause complications such as uterine rupture** — factor the number of children desired into the discussion.
- **VTE prophylaxis** in hospital and **particularly after Cesarean**.
- **Postpartum pain after Cesarean**: multimodal — scheduled acetaminophen + nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) first line, opiates for breakthrough. **Intravenous ketorolac did not increase disease activity to 6 weeks postpartum** and reduced pain scores and opiate need.

## Lactation

- **Breastfeeding is recommended** — it does not increase disease exacerbation (Strong, very low). **Infants of mothers on anti-TNF who breastfeed have no increased infection in the first 12 months** (Conditional, very low).
- **Do not stop IBD medication in order to breastfeed** — women who stopped had more postpartum disease activity.
- **Relative infant dose (RID) ≤10%** of the weight-adjusted maternal dose = low risk.

| Drug | Breastfeed? | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 5-ASA | **Yes** | RID <1%. Rare infant diarrhea |
| Sulfasalazine | **Yes** | RID <1%, but the sulfapyridine metabolite reaches higher levels — **non-sulfonamide mesalamine preferred** |
| Thiopurines | **Yes** | RID <1%; no difference in growth, development or infection |
| Allopurinol + thiopurine | **Avoid** | Little data, one report of possible harm |
| Methotrexate | **Avoid** | RID 0.11% but active 7-hydroxymethotrexate is detectable. If **≤25 mg/wk**, no alternative, and the mother strongly wishes it — possible with close monitoring, **not within 24 h of the dose** |
| Prednisone/prednisolone | **Yes** | Milk <100 mcg/d even at maternal doses to 120 mg/d; **the old 4-hour wait is unnecessary** |
| Budesonide | **Yes** | Systemic RID ~0.27% |
| Anti-TNF, anti-integrin, anti-IL-12/23, anti-IL-23, biosimilars | **Yes** | Milk concentration <1% of maternal serum; **older infant RID <0.005%**. Breastfeeding does not alter neonatal drug clearance |
| S1P modulators (ozanimod, etrasimod) | **No** | No human lactation data |
| JAKis (tofacitinib, upadacitinib, filgotinib) | **No** | Tofacitinib RID up to 3.4%; label advises withholding **18 h** (immediate release) or **36 h** (sustained release) per dose, making regular breastfeeding infeasible |
| Metronidazole | **Generally avoid** | High relative exposure; if a short course (~1 week) is needed, monitor the infant for diarrhea/candidiasis |
| Ciprofloxacin, amoxicillin, vancomycin | **Yes** | Low risk; monitor the infant for diarrhea/candidiasis |

- **After endoscopy** ([[endoscopy-sedation]]): **fentanyl and propofol need no waiting period or milk discard**; **midazolam — withhold nursing 4 hours** (newborn or preterm infant: a cautious 6–8 h).
- **Contrast**: iodinated RID <0.01%, gadolinium absorption <0.0004% — **no interruption needed**; 24 hours is more than adequate if the mother prefers to wait.

## Infant Vaccines

- **Inactive vaccines on schedule, regardless of in utero exposure** (Strong, very low). Infant T- and B-cell immunity develops unaltered after anti-TNF mono- or combination therapy, and serologic responses to tetanus, *Haemophilus influenzae* type b, hepatitis B, pneumococcus and diphtheria are adequate.
- **Live rotavirus may be given** to infants exposed in utero to biologics (Conditional, very low) — prospective data in 168 biologic-exposed infants showed no serious adverse events. Rotavirus is normally given **between 2 and 6 months**; later dosing carries a rare intussusception risk.
- **Live BCG — avoid in the first 6 months of life** after in utero anti-TNF exposure (Strong, very low), because of **disseminated tuberculosis and death**. Every reported adverse event occurred in infants vaccinated at **<6 months**. Where BCG must be given early, **measure infant serum drug concentrations first**, and consider **holding the maternal biologic in mid-pregnancy** to reduce transfer. Weigh regional tuberculosis risk.
- **After in utero JAKi or S1P modulator exposure — live vaccines may be given after 1 month of age** (short half-lives; undetectable within days of birth).
- **Live vaccines are not withheld from infants breastfeeding on maternal biologics** — negligible immunoglobulin G transfer across milk.

> [!pitfall] BCG is the vaccine that kills, rotavirus is not
> The old blanket "no live vaccines for 6 months" has split. **Rotavirus may now be given**; **BCG must still wait 6 months** after in utero anti-TNF exposure — a fatal disseminated BCG infection was reported at 4.5 months after vaccination at 3 months ([[global-consensus-2025-pregnancy-ibd]]).

## Maternal and Fetal Outcomes

*Counseling points, all Conditional recommendations on very low to low evidence.*

- **Offspring IBD risk is higher with a first-degree relative with IBD**, and higher from a parent with CD than from a parent with UC. Danish population data, adjusted incidence rate ratio for the offspring: **CD 7.5 (father) / 6.4 (mother); UC 4.3 / 3.7**. Maternal smoking in pregnancy and prenatal antibiotic exposure are also associated with IBD in the offspring (odds ratio ~1.5 and ~1.75) — but antibiotics are still given for appropriate indications, group B streptococcus prophylaxis included.
- **Women with IBD have more low birth weight (LBW) and preterm delivery** than women without IBD.
- **Moderate-to-severe disease activity increases spontaneous abortion** vs no IBD or mild IBD.
- **VTE risk is increased both during pregnancy and postpartum** (relative risk ~1.72 and ~2.10).
- **Children born to women with IBD have more neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) admissions and first-year hospitalizations**; **active** disease adds small for gestational age (SGA) and LBW.
- **No increased early childhood malignancy or developmental delay** after in utero anti-TNF, ustekinumab, vedolizumab or thiopurine exposure.
- **Controlling disease activity is the lever** — inadequate GWG, gestational diabetes, preeclampsia and preterm birth all track with activity.
