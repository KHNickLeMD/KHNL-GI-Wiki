## Sedation Continuum
Sedation is a drug-induced depression of consciousness on a continuum; patients can drift deeper than intended, so providers must be able to **rescue** from a deeper level than targeted.

| Level | Responsiveness | Airway | Ventilation | CV |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Minimal (anxiolysis) | Normal to verbal | Unaffected | Unaffected | Unaffected |
| Moderate ("conscious") | Purposeful to verbal/tactile | No intervention | Adequate | Usually maintained |
| Deep | Purposeful only after repeated/painful stimulus | Intervention may be needed | May be inadequate | Usually maintained |
| General anesthesia | Unarousable even to pain | Intervention often needed | Frequently inadequate | May be impaired |

- US expectation: at least minimal sedation for [[upper-endoscopy|EGD]] and [[colonoscopy]] (patient wishes/medical condition may favor unsedated).
- Endoscopist-delivered minimal/moderate sedation appropriate for **ASA I–III**.

## Pre-Procedural Assessment
- Informed consent (benefits, risks, alternatives); target depth matched to patient expectation and procedure needs.
- History: snoring/stridor/**OSA**, drug allergies/interactions, prior adverse sedation reaction, last oral intake, tobacco/alcohol/substance use.
- Focused exam: vitals, heart/lung auscultation, baseline consciousness, **airway/Mallampati class**; pregnancy test in women of childbearing age (some sedatives teratogenic).
- Documented assessment + procedural time-out are required quality metrics (Joint Commission).

### ASA Physical-Status Class
- **I** normal/healthy · **II** mild systemic disease, no activity limit (controlled HTN/DM) · **III** moderate–severe systemic disease, no activity limit (stable angina, DM with sequelae) · **IV** severe systemic disease, constant threat to life (severe CHF, ESRD) · **V** moribund, death likely within 24 h · **E** emergency suffix.
- Higher ASA class → higher cardiopulmonary adverse-event risk; useful for risk stratification.

### NPO / Aspiration Risk
- Fasting limits aspiration with blunted airway reflexes; no universally accepted standard, no direct fasting-duration ↔ aspiration data.
- ASA defaults: **≥2 h after clear liquids, ≥6 h after a light meal**.
- Impaired gastric emptying or emergency → weigh aspiration risk when choosing target depth, delaying the procedure, or protecting the airway (intubation); use a team approach.

## Agents

### Benzodiazepine + Opioid (Moderate Sedation)
- Standard regimen: **benzodiazepine (anxiolysis/amnesia) + opioid (analgesia)** — safe and effective for EGD/colonoscopy in patients without sedation-risk factors.
- Benzodiazepines: midazolam (favored — fast onset, short duration, less thrombophlebitis, amnestic) or diazepam (comparable efficacy).
- Opioids: fentanyl (faster onset/clearance, less nausea) or meperidine.
- Adjuncts in select cases: diphenhydramine, promethazine, droperidol (droperidol — FDA **black-box QTc warning**; avoid with prolonged QTc / QT-risk patients).

### Propofol-Based Sedation
- Forms: non-anesthesiologist–administered propofol (NAAP/NAPS), **balanced propofol sedation (BPS)** = single-dose opioid + benzodiazepine then intermittent propofol boluses to moderate sedation, or anesthesia-provider/**MAC**.
- Shorter sedation and recovery times; **no specific reversal agent** → deep-sedation rescue skills and personnel required.
- Use when it improves safety, comfort, efficiency, or procedure completion.
- Routine anesthesia-administered propofol for low-risk EGD/colonoscopy shows no safety benefit, raises cost, and does not change adenoma detection rate.

### Reversal Agents
- **Naloxone** — reverses opioids. **Flumazenil** — reverses benzodiazepines. Both must be immediately available in every endoscopy unit.
- **Propofol has no reversal agent.**

## Monitoring
- Required for all sedated procedures: **BP, SpO₂, HR** + clinical observation of cardiopulmonary status.
- Supplemental O₂: consider for moderate sedation, administer during deep sedation, and give if hypoxemia is anticipated/develops.
- **Capnography**: consider for procedures targeting **deep sedation** (detects respiratory depression before hypoxemia; reduced transient hypoxemia in RCT). Not shown to improve safety in moderate sedation.

## Who Needs an Anesthesia Provider
Consider anesthesia-provider–administered sedation for:
- Prolonged or therapeutic procedures needing deep sedation (eg, [[ercp]], [[endoscopic-ultrasound|EUS]] interventions)
- Anticipated intolerance to standard sedatives
- Severe comorbidity (**ASA IV or V**)
- Increased airway-obstruction risk from an anatomic variant / risk of airway compromise
