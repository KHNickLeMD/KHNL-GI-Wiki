A **seven-point description of stool consistency**, scored by the patient, that serves as a **proxy for intestinal transit time**. It is the instrument behind irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) subtyping, lactulose titration and several trial-entry criteria across this wiki — **this page is its single home**; the pages that act on it link here rather than reproducing the types.

## The Seven Types

*Verbatim, as published ([[lewis-1997-bristol-stool-form-scale]], Table I).*

| Type | Description | Transit |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Separate hard lumps, like nuts | **Slow** |
| **2** | Sausage-shaped but lumpy | ↑ |
| **3** | Like a sausage or snake but with cracks on its surface | ↑ |
| **4** | Like a sausage or snake, smooth and soft | — |
| **5** | Soft blobs with clear-cut edges | ↓ |
| **6** | Fluffy pieces with ragged edges, a mushy stool | ↓ |
| **7** | Watery, no solid pieces | **Rapid** |

- **The patient scores it, not the clinician** — the subject inspects the stool and picks the description that fits.
- Recorded prospectively in a **stool diary**; single recalled scores are not what the instrument was built for.

## What It Measures

- A **proxy for whole-gut transit time (WGTT)**, and a better one than how often the patient goes: against measured WGTT, stool form correlated **r = −0.54** versus **r = 0.35** for defecatory frequency ([[lewis-1997-bristol-stool-form-scale]]).
- **Responsive to change**, which is what makes it usable for monitoring: when transit was deliberately altered with senna or loperamide, change in WGTT tracked change in stool form (**r = −0.65**) better than change in frequency (r = 0.41).
- The **least variable** of the bowel measures on repeat assessment — coefficient of variation **13.6%**, versus 17.9% for frequency and 39.3% for stool output.
- Fluctuating scores in IBS represent an **objective abnormality of intestinal function**, and the scale distinguishes true diarrhea and constipation from the **pseudo-diarrhea and pseudo-constipation** of IBS.

> [!pearl] Form beats frequency
> Stool *form* predicts transit better than stool *frequency*, which in some studies predicted nothing at all. A patient reporting daily bowel movements of type 1–2 has slow transit; the frequency alone would have said otherwise. ([[lewis-1997-bristol-stool-form-scale]])

## Pitfalls

> [!pitfall] Types 2 and 5 are the ones patients mix up
> The scale's own authors flag it: **type 2 (lumpy sausage) and type 5 (soft blobs)** are easily confused despite sitting on opposite sides of normal, and getting them backwards inverts the transit conclusion. Asking about ease of passage helps — type 5 usually passes easily. ([[lewis-1997-bristol-stool-form-scale]])

- **The scale defines the types; it does not define the clinical bands.** Cut-points like "1–2 = constipation" or "6–7 = diarrhea" come from the guidelines that use it, and live on their pages.
- **Transit correlation is real but imperfect** — stool form may be set by residence time in one segment (the sigmoid moulds the stool) rather than by overall colonic passage, and other factors besides transit contribute.
- **Derivation population was 66 healthy women**, not patients and not men; performance in disease states was not tested in the original study.
