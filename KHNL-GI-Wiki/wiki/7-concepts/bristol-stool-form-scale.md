A **seven-point description of stool consistency**, scored by the patient, that serves as a **proxy for intestinal transit time**. It is the instrument behind irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) subtyping, lactulose titration and several trial-entry criteria; the cut-points that act on it are routed below to the page that carries each rule.

## The Seven Types

*Verbatim, as published ([[lewis-1997-bristol-stool-form-scale]], Table I).*

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **1** | Separate hard lumps, like nuts |
| **2** | Sausage-shaped but lumpy |
| **3** | Like a sausage or snake but with cracks on its surface |
| **4** | Like a sausage or snake, smooth and soft |
| **5** | Soft blobs with clear-cut edges |
| **6** | Fluffy pieces with ragged edges, a mushy stool |
| **7** | Watery, no solid pieces |

- **Direction:** type 1 = "the discrete lumps of slow transit"; type 6 = non-cohesive and type 7 = "liquid stools of rapid transit". The paper does not assign a transit speed to each intermediate type.
- **The patient scores it, not the clinician** — the subject inspects the stool and picks the description that fits. Wording was deliberately kept in everyday language and made relevant to stools seen **under water in the toilet bowl**, because the earlier 8-point scale was too complicated for untrained observers.
- **Scored prospectively, stool by stool, in a diary** in the validation study. For epidemiology and routine practice Rome V accepts a single question instead — which picture on the chart the patient's abnormal stools usually look like ([[rome-v-2026-bowel-disorders]]).

## What It Measures

- A **proxy for whole-gut transit time (WGTT)**, and a better one than how often the patient goes — which is what makes it usable both to characterize a bowel habit and to monitor treatment response in constipation, diarrhea or IBS.
- Fluctuating scores in IBS represent an **objective abnormality of intestinal function**, and stool-form recordings distinguish true diarrhea and true constipation from the **pseudo-diarrhea and pseudo-constipation** of IBS.

| Measure | vs baseline WGTT | vs **change** in WGTT | Coefficient of variation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stool form** | **r = −0.54** (P < 0.001) | **r = −0.65** (P < 0.001) | **13.6%** |
| Stool output (g/week) | r = −0.41 (P = 0.001) | r = −0.54 (P < 0.001) | 39.3% |
| Defecatory frequency | r = 0.35 (P = 0.005) | r = 0.41 (P < 0.001) | 17.9% |
| WGTT itself | — | — | 17.5% |

*Stool form correlates best with transit time and varies least on repeat measurement, of the measures tested ([[lewis-1997-bristol-stool-form-scale]]; 66 healthy women, WGTT by radiopaque marker pellets, transit then altered with senna or loperamide).*

- **Responsive to a deliberate change in transit** (medians, all P < 0.001): senna (n = 44) shortened WGTT 67 → 41 h and raised the stool form score 3.6 → 4.6; loperamide (n = 43) lengthened WGTT 52 → 72 h and lowered the score 3.9 → 2.8.

> [!pearl] Form beats frequency
> Stool *form* predicts transit better than defecatory frequency, which in some studies has had no predictive value at all. Used in community surveys, the scale found a large amount of unsuspected, asymptomatic slow transit — and conversely, many people who believed they were constipated were not ([[lewis-1997-bristol-stool-form-scale]]).

## Pitfalls

> [!pitfall] Types 2 and 5 are easily confused
> The authors flag it themselves: **type 2 (lumpy sausage)** and **type 5 (soft blobs)** get mixed up although they sit on opposite sides of the scale. Observer error is their leading explanation for the imperfect transit correlation, and they suggest the wording be improved by pointing out the usually easier passage of type-5 stools ([[lewis-1997-bristol-stool-form-scale]]).

- **Transit correlation is real but imperfect.** Candidate reasons: observer error (the earlier scale reached r = 0.93 when one trained observer scored every stool), stool form being set by residence time in a single segment such as the sigmoid, whose contractions mould the stool — though one study found no correlation with any particular colonic segment — and other unidentified factors besides transit.
- **Derivation population was 66 healthy women** (45 premenopausal, 21 postmenopausal, none obese, none on antibiotics within 3 months) — not patients and not men; performance in disease states was not tested.
