## Overview

[[cirrhosis|Cirrhosis]] lowers pro- and anti-hemostatic drivers together, so hemostasis is **rebalanced** rather than simply deficient — largely intact but precarious, and tipped either way by sepsis, hypothermia or kidney dysfunction, particularly once decompensated ([[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]], Key concept 1). Changes in the anticoagulant system can leave a relative hypercoagulability that conventional tests do not detect (Key concept 3). Patients bleed **and** clot.

> [!pitfall] Prothrombin time and platelet count do not grade bleeding risk
> Prothrombin time/international normalized ratio (PT/INR) was developed to monitor vitamin K antagonists and correlates poorly with thrombin generation and bleeding in cirrhosis ([[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]], Key concept 4). The AGA review found **no** PT/INR or platelet threshold that predicts procedure-related bleeding ([[aga-2021-cirrhosis-coagulation]]).

## Rebalanced Hemostasis

### Hemostatic Defects

| System | Shifts toward bleeding | Shifts toward clotting |
|---|---|---|
| **Coagulation factors** | ↓ factors II, V, VII, IX, X, XI | ↑ factor VIII (endothelial-derived) |
| **Fibrinogen** | Dysfibrinogenemia; fibrinogen may decline, and below 100 mg/dL prolongs the PT and activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT) | — |
| **Natural anticoagulants** | — | ↓ protein C, protein S, antithrombin |
| **Platelet number** | Thrombocytopenia — hypersplenism with platelet sequestration, reduced hepatic thrombopoietin synthesis, antiplatelet antibodies from the polyclonal gammopathy of cirrhosis | — |
| **Platelet function** | Altered by membrane lipid composition, endothelial dysfunction, renal impairment, endotoxin-mediated activation | ↑ von Willebrand factor (vWF) restores platelet function at least in part |
| **Fibrinolysis** | Low plasminogen; features of hyperfibrinolysis detectable in up to 30% of hospitalized patients with cirrhosis, clinically evident far less often | — |

*All rows from [[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]] (Figure 2 defect list, Key concepts 1–4, "Platelets in cirrhosis" and "Bleeding in cirrhosis").*

### Bleeding Phenotypes

[[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]] splits bleeding in cirrhosis into three kinds — only the second and third are hemostatic problems:

- **Portal hypertensive bleeding** — driven by portal pressure. It is not clearly related to platelet count or PT; changes in those parameters reflect worsening liver disease rather than the hemostatic defect.
- **Mucosal / wound bleeding** — the hemostatic defects above.
- **Accelerated intravascular coagulation and fibrinolysis (AICF)** — delayed post-procedure bleeding and mucosal or puncture-wound oozing. Factor VIII is usually high, fibrinogen may fall, and fibrin/fibrinogen degradation products are usually elevated. Antifibrinolytics can be effective and safe when it is present.

## Recommendations

Scope: every [[aga-2021-cirrhosis-coagulation|AGA 2021]] recommendation below applies to **stable** cirrhosis undergoing common gastrointestinal (GI) procedures. Its technical review examined six, which each recommendation names as examples — [[paracentesis]], thoracentesis, variceal banding ([[variceal-upper-gi-bleeding|endoscopic variceal ligation]]), colonic [[polypectomy]], [[ercp|endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)]] and [[liver-biopsy|liver biopsy]].

### Bleeding and Periprocedural Management

| Question | Recommendation | Strength / certainty | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Viscoelastic testing (VET: thromboelastography [TEG] / rotational thromboelastometry [ROTEM]) before procedures to predict bleeding risk | **No recommendation** — labelled a knowledge gap | No recommendation / knowledge gap | AGA 2021 (1A) |
| PT/INR and platelet testing before procedures, in patients with known baseline abnormal coagulation parameters | **Suggests against** extensive preprocedural testing, including repeated measurement of PT/INR or platelet count | Conditional / very low | AGA 2021 (1B) |
| Preprocedural fresh frozen plasma (FFP) or platelet transfusion | **Suggests against routine** use of blood products for bleeding prophylaxis | Conditional / very low | AGA 2021 (2A) |
| FFP to improve thrombin generation | **Do not** give at conventional doses (10 mL/kg); the 1–2 L needed to lower a significantly prolonged INR expands volume, raises portal pressure and may trigger variceal hemorrhage — prophylactic plasma is futile and potentially risky | Conditional / low | ACG 2020 (Rec 1) |
| Prophylactic platelet transfusion before common procedures (routine variceal banding, paracentesis) | **Do not** give, outside significant renal dysfunction (serum creatinine >2.5 mg/dL) or sepsis. Higher platelet levels may be more appropriate for high-risk procedures (removal of large polyps, major surgery) but will need higher transfusion doses; if the procedure is elective, a thrombopoietin receptor agonist (TPO-RA) may be more appropriate | Conditional / very low | ACG 2020 (Rec 2) |
| TPO-RAs before procedures, in thrombocytopenia | **Suggests against routine** use for bleeding prophylaxis, particularly before "low-risk" procedures | Conditional / very low | AGA 2021 (2B) |
| Antifibrinolytics (epsilon aminocaproic acid, tranexamic acid) for **persistent active bleeding** | **Do not** use **in the absence of hyperfibrinolysis**; they are not generally considered to induce a hypercoagulable state but require caution if a pathological clot such as portal vein thrombosis (PVT) is already present | Conditional / very low | ACG 2020 (Rec 3) |
| TEG/ROTEM to avoid unnecessary blood-product use before invasive procedures with a prolonged INR | **May be useful**; parameters for blood-product use are not established | Ungraded key concept | ACG 2020 (Key concept 5) |

> [!pitfall] Correcting the INR with plasma can cause the bleed you are preventing
> A conventional 10 mL/kg dose does not improve thrombin generation, and the 1–2 L that would move the INR raises portal pressure enough to trigger variceal hemorrhage ([[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]], Rec 1). Reserve plasma for active bleeding with a documented factor deficiency.

### Thrombosis and Anticoagulation

| Question | Recommendation | Strength / certainty | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Venous thromboembolism (VTE) prophylaxis in **hospitalized** cirrhosis | **Standard anticoagulation prophylaxis** over no anticoagulation, in patients who otherwise meet standard VTE-prophylaxis criteria | Conditional / very low | AGA 2021 (3) |
| Safety of pharmacologic deep vein thrombosis (DVT) prophylaxis | Appears safe in hospitalized cirrhosis **in the absence of bleeding or a platelet count <50,000/µL** | Ungraded key concept | ACG 2020 (Key concept 6) |
| Routine screening for [[portal-vein-thrombosis\|PVT]] | **Suggests against.** Does **not** apply to patients listed for [[liver-transplantation\|liver transplantation]] | Conditional / very low | AGA 2021 (4) |
| Treatment of acute or subacute **nontumoral** PVT | **Anticoagulation over no anticoagulation** | Conditional / very low | AGA 2021 (5) |
| Atrial fibrillation with an indication for anticoagulation | **Anticoagulation over no anticoagulation** | Conditional / very low | AGA 2021 (6) |
| Anticoagulation for PVT or mesenteric vein thrombosis in cirrhosis | **Not** associated with increased variceal bleeding risk | Ungraded key concept | ACG 2020 (Key concept 14) |

- **Atrial fibrillation — who may reasonably decline.** Benefit appears to outweigh bleeding risk at a CHA₂DS₂-VASc score ≥2; declining anticoagulation is reasonable in more advanced cirrhosis (Child-Turcotte-Pugh [CTP] class C) and/or a low CHA₂DS₂-VASc score for patients who value avoiding bleeding over stroke reduction ([[aga-2021-cirrhosis-coagulation]]). CTP criteria table: [[cirrhosis]].
- **Agent choice.** No comparative data favour one anticoagulant over another for PVT, and the AGA review found no qualifying direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC) studies ([[aga-2021-cirrhosis-coagulation]]). DOACs have been used successfully in cirrhosis but are **generally avoided in CTP class B and C** ([[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]]). Indications, duration, agents and variceal prophylaxis: [[portal-vein-thrombosis]]. Periprocedural interruption and resumption: [[anticoagulation-gi-bleeding]].

## Thresholds, Doses and Qualifiers

- **No validated transfusion trigger exists.** [[aga-2021-cirrhosis-coagulation]] states the threshold defining severe thrombocytopenia or coagulopathy "could not be clearly defined from the literature and remains a matter of clinical judgment." Reference points, none of them a validated clinical trigger:
  - Studies reviewed typically defined coagulopathy as **INR >1.8 and/or platelets <50,000/µL** (AGA 2021).
  - *In vitro* thrombin generation is adequate at platelets **≥50,000/µL**, and ranges of **50–60,000/µL** are often recommended for high-risk procedures (ACG 2020); AGA 2021 cites *in vitro* data for **>55,000/µL**.
  - **Fibrinogen >120–150 mg/dL** has been recommended before high-risk procedures, extrapolated from trauma data (ACG 2020).
  - A single adult platelet dose does not improve thrombin generation — in one variceal-banding study it raised the median platelet count from **39,000 to 52,000/µL** with no effect on thrombin generation and only mild ROTEM improvement, and no procedure-related bleeding occurred in any patient (ACG 2020).
- **Procedure risk strata:** the AGA technical review split procedures into low vs high bleeding risk at a **1.5%** threshold, from literature review and expert interpretation of indirect evidence.
- **TPO-RA dosing.** Avatrombopag and lusutrombopag are the two agents approved by the US Food and Drug Administration for cirrhosis with a platelet count **<50,000/µL** before an elective medical or dental procedure. Phase 3 trial dosing: avatrombopag **40–60 mg/day for 5 days**; lusutrombopag **3 mg once daily** (ACG 2020).
- **Severe derangement + a high-bleeding-risk procedure:** discuss prophylactic transfusion — potential benefits and risks including transfusion reactions, alloimmunization and procedure delay — **in consultation with a hematologist** (AGA 2021).
- **What does track with bleeding:** progressive decompensation by CTP class (after variceal banding, polypectomy — especially larger polyps — and endoscopic sphincterotomy), [[acute-on-chronic-liver-failure|acute-on-chronic liver failure (ACLF)]] and [[aki-in-cirrhosis|acute kidney injury]]. Lower platelet counts trended with higher bleeding risk without a defined cutoff (AGA 2021).
- **Other modifiable risk:** control infection, which is associated with release of endogenous heparinoids, and optimize renal function, which affects platelet function and volume (ACG 2020).
- **VTE risk assessment:** VTE incidence in cirrhosis is typically **0.5–1.9%**. AGA 2021 points to the Padua Prediction Score and the IMPROVE VTE risk-assessment model and advises weighing VTE against bleeding risk; the IMPROVE bleeding model counts liver disease (defined as **INR >1.5**) as a risk factor carrying a relative risk of **2.18**. The guideline prints no components or point values for Padua, IMPROVE or CHA₂DS₂-VASc — score those from the instruments themselves.
  - **Do not use VET to assess VTE risk or guide thromboprophylaxis in ACLF** — standard clinical risk-assessment tools still decide ([[aasld-easl-2026-aclf-global-consensus]], Statement 51).

> [!pitfall] A TPO-RA buys platelets and sells thrombosis
> Thrombotic events ran at approximately **1% at 30 days** for avatrombopag and lusutrombopag ([[aga-2021-cirrhosis-coagulation]]) — that harm, against an uncertain reduction in procedural bleeding, is why routine use is advised against. The thrombosis is particularly PVT ([[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]]).

## Portal Vein Thrombosis in Cirrhosis

- **Prevalence:** ~1% in compensated cirrhosis, up to **20%** among patients listed for liver transplantation; in one prospective compensated cohort new PVT appeared in **4.6% at 1 year and 10.7% at 5 years** ([[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]]).
- **Why:** cirrhosis enters Virchow's triad as sluggish portal flow, on top of the hypercoagulable shifts above (↑ vWF and factor VIII, ↓ protein C, protein S, antithrombin) ([[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation]]).
- Diagnosis, anticoagulation indications, duration and agent choice: [[portal-vein-thrombosis]].

**Bleeding on anticoagulation — randomized data.** Double-blind trial in **CTP 7–10** cirrhosis, rivaroxaban 10 mg daily vs placebo for up to 24 months ([[puente-2025-cirroxaban-rivaroxaban-cirrhosis]]):

| Outcome | Rivaroxaban 10 mg/d (n = 41) | Placebo (n = 49) | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Non-portal-hypertensive (non-PHT) bleeding, any | 36.6% | 14.3% | relative risk (RR) 2.56 (95% confidence interval [CI] 1.16–5.67) |
| Non-PHT **major** bleeding | 6 patients | 4 patients | no significant difference |
| Fatal bleeding | 0 | 1 | — |

- **The excess is minor bleeding, not major bleeding** — the distinction that makes anticoagulation tolerable here.
- **Liver function grades the risk.** At CTP 7 the arms did not separate (9 events in 6 rivaroxaban patients vs 7 events in 6 placebo patients); at **CTP ≥8**, 14 events in 9 rivaroxaban patients vs 1 event in 1 placebo patient.
- **Bleeding tracks drug exposure, benefit does not.** Anti-Xa-estimated rivaroxaban levels were **119 ng/mL (range 62–307)** in patients with a bleeding event vs **41 ng/mL (21–85)** in those without (area under the curve 0.823; optimal cut-off **118 ng/mL**), while levels did not differ between patients who did and did not reach the efficacy endpoint. Levels correlate only weakly with CTP score (r = 0.342) — a fixed dose gives higher exposure as liver function worsens.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- **Viscoelastic testing.** [[acg-2020-hepatic-mesenteric-circulation|ACG 2020]] Key concept 5 (**ungraded**) says TEG/ROTEM "may be useful to avoid unnecessary use of blood products" before invasive procedures in cirrhosis with a prolonged INR, while stating that transfusion parameters are not established. [[aga-2021-cirrhosis-coagulation|AGA 2021]] reviewed the same question and made **no recommendation**, labelling it an evidence gap: VET did not change post-procedural bleeding (RR 0.33, 95% CI 0.01–7.87) or mortality up to 90 days (RR 1.05, 95% CI 0.45–2.44), with a clear trend toward lower blood-product use. Both are society guidelines, so the newer **AGA 2021 position governs this page** — VET is not established for periprocedural bleeding-risk prediction, and both documents agree it is not ready to set transfusion thresholds.
  - **Newest position, for ACLF** ([[aasld-easl-2026-aclf-global-consensus]], Statement 51, B1): VET may better reflect dynamic haemostatic status than INR, aPTT, fibrinogen and platelet count, and abnormal VET parameters are associated with bleeding and may predict short-term mortality — but further clinical validation is required before routine adoption. Consistent with AGA 2021.
- **Platelet substrate threshold.** ACG 2020 cites **≥50,000/µL** as adequate for thrombin generation *in vitro*; AGA 2021 cites **>55,000/µL**. Neither is a validated clinical trigger, and neither guideline endorses transfusing to a number.
- **DOACs in advanced cirrhosis.** ACG 2020 reports DOACs are generally avoided in CTP class B and C. The later [[puente-2025-cirroxaban-rivaroxaban-cirrhosis|CIRROXABAN]] trial dosed rivaroxaban 10 mg/day in CTP 7–10 and found the excess bleeding was minor and concentrated at CTP ≥8. The guideline position stands on this page; the trial supplies the magnitude.
