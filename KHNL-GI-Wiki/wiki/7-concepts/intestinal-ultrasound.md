## Overview

**Intestinal ultrasound (IUS)** — noninvasive, radiation-free, point-of-care transabdominal exam of the bowel wall, mesentery, and adjacent structures used to assess and monitor **[[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]]** activity ([[aga-2024-intestinal-ultrasound]]). No bowel prep, fasting, or contrast required. Supports a **[[treat-to-target-ibd|treat-to-target]]** strategy by giving an objective activity indicator when clinical remission poorly correlates with [[ibd-endoscopic-scoring|endoscopic healing]] and repeat endoscopy/biomarker sampling is burdensome. Can rule out IBD akin to fecal calprotectin, and positive findings expedite [[colonoscopy|ileocolonoscopy]] for diagnostic confirmation.

## Technique

- **Equipment** — high-resolution system with a **low-frequency convex transducer** (initial global bowel survey) then a **high-frequency linear transducer** with color Doppler (activity assessment). Linear alone often adequate in children / small body habitus.
- **Training** — no United States–based gastroenterology (GI) certification for IUS yet. International Bowel Ultrasound Group (IBUS) 3-module pathway: (1) intensive introductory course with simulators and patients; (2) hands-on training with **≥40 patients** at a certified IBUS centre; (3) advanced workshop + final exam.
- **Workflow** — IUS before a same-day clinic visit, during the visit by an IUS-trained clinician, or a standalone referral IUS clinic. Radiologists with IUS expertise can cover patients who do not need point-of-care testing. No IUS-specific Current Procedural Terminology (CPT) code; e.g. 76705 (limited abdominal ultrasound) is used.

## Activity Metrics

- **Bowel wall thickness (BWT)** — primary activity measure.
- **Bowel wall hyperemia** — color Doppler signal.
- **More severe disease** — inflammatory mesenteric fat, loss of bowel wall stratification, surrounding lymphadenopathy.
- **Complications seen on IUS** — strictures, fistulae, sinus tracts, phlegmons, abscesses. Contrast-enhanced ultrasound (better for strictures, and for telling phlegmon from abscess) is outside the scope of [[aga-2024-intestinal-ultrasound]].

> [!pitfall] No universal "abnormal" bowel wall thickness
> [[aga-2024-intestinal-ultrasound]] prints a millimetre BWT value only for postoperative [[crohns-disease|Crohn's disease (CD)]] recurrence and for ulcerative colitis (UC) on [[jak-inhibitors|tofacitinib]] (both under [[#Treat-to-Target Thresholds]]) — no cross-cutting normal/abnormal cut-off, and no color Doppler hyperemia grading scheme.
> Several IUS scoring systems exist to standardize reporting, but **none is regularly used in clinical practice** and the update does not print their components.

Segment-specific colonic and rectal BWT cut-offs with the modified Limberg Doppler grade sit in the UC activity index on [[ulcerative-colitis]].

## IUS vs Other Modalities

By trained operators, IUS is comparable to **magnetic resonance enterography (MRE) and computed tomography enterography (CTE)** for ileal CD activity and for detecting complications (fistula, stricture, abscess), with practical advantages: no contrast/radiation, real-time, in-clinic. In a multicentre randomized trial MRE and IUS had similar accuracy for terminal ileum (TI) activity in newly diagnosed or clinically active CD → **both are valid first-line tools for CD activity assessment**. Interobserver reliability moderate–excellent (higher than serial MRE in one substudy: 82% vs 69% agreement). Ileocolonoscopy/[[capsule-endoscopy|video capsule endoscopy (VCE)]] directly visualize mucosa (needed for biopsy, dilation, dysplasia surveillance) but are costlier and usually require [[endoscopy-sedation|sedation]].

## Treat-to-Target Thresholds

| Setting | IUS target | When measured | What it predicts |
|---|---|---|---|
| CD on [[il-23-and-il-12-23-inhibitors\|ustekinumab]], treat-to-target trial | **Sonographic response** = BWT reduction **≥25%**; **transmural healing** = normalization of **all** IUS parameters | wk 48 | Reached by 46.3% and 24.1% respectively |
| CD on a biologic | **No** sonographic response | wk 4 | Poor wk-48 endoscopic response — negative predictive value (NPV) 73% |
| CD starting [[anti-tnf-agents\|anti–tumor necrosis factor]] therapy | BWT reduction **≥18%** | 4–8 wk after starting | 10× likelier wk-12–32 endoscopic response |
| CD after resection | Neo-TI or adjacent colon **BWT ≥3 mm** *and* **fecal calprotectin (FC) ≥50 µg/g** | postoperatively | Postoperative recurrence — positive predictive value (PPV) 74%, NPV 71% |
| UC on tofacitinib | **Absolute BWT <2.8 mm** | wk 8 | Endoscopic remission — sens 73%, spec 100%, area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) 0.87 |
| UC on tofacitinib | **32% BWT reduction** | wk 8 | Endoscopic response — sens 71%, spec 90%, AUC 0.87 |
| Acute severe UC on IV [[corticosteroids-ibd\|corticosteroids]] | **≥20% BWT reduction** (vs no >20% reduction) | within **48 ± 24 h** of starting steroids | Endoscopic response — odds ratio (OR) 22.6; avoiding a rescue biologic — OR 6.3 |

- **CD:** treatment response identifiable within **3 months** of starting a biologic; higher baseline BWT → 42% less likely to reach transmural healing at 1 year, whatever the therapy.
- **UC:** BWT normalization seen as early as **2 weeks** after a treatment change.
- Role of IUS in UC **in remission** is a research gap.

## Special Populations

- **Pregnancy** — preferred monitoring (no radiation/sedation); sensitivity 84%, specificity 98% vs clinical activity/FC; TI + colon visualized in up to 92% in 3rd trimester.
- **Serious comorbidities** — renal failure / high sedation risk (avoids IV contrast, sedation).
- **[[obesity|Obesity]]** — image quality may drop but findings not significantly different by weight.

## Limitations

- Poor for **proximal/duodenal** disease (bowel gas) and **rectal** disease (needs distended bladder).
- Imprecise for disease **length** — the transducers' limited field of view makes length hard to measure in extensive small-bowel CD, and accuracy falls off **beyond 10 cm**; MRE better for extent and deep pelvic structures. Accuracy is also lower for **locating** disease in the mid-to-proximal small bowel.
- **Not advised for dysplasia surveillance** — use endoscopy.
- **Operator-dependent** — accuracy depends on operator training; reporting is not standardized.
- **Adoption is ahead of the consensus.** [[asge-2025-ibd-endoscopy-consensus|ASGE 2025]] would only agree that IUS "**can be considered, where available**" as a tool for mucosal and transmural inflammation, stricture, fistula and abscess. The stronger statement — *encouraging* onsite IUS, and adding lymphadenopathy to its targets — **failed to reach consensus**. Equipment procurement and sonographer training are named as the barriers, and large multicentre prospective trials are still needed to define how IUS fits routine care. Its cross-sectional-imaging statement names only CTE or MRE around the index ileocolonoscopy — IUS is not among the options there.
