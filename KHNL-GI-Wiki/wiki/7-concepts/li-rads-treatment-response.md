The Liver Imaging Reporting and Data System (LI-RADS) computed tomography (CT)/magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) Treatment Response Assessment (TRA) categorizes [[hepatocellular-carcinoma|hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)]] after local-regional therapy (LRT) as viable or not. The 2024 version splits it into a **nonradiation algorithm** and a new **radiation core** ([[acr-2024-li-rads-ct-mri-treatment-response]]). Diagnostic categories for untreated observations (LR-1 to LR-5, LR-M): see [[li-rads]].

## Scope and Timing

| Therapy group | Includes |
|---|---|
| **Nonradiation LRT** | Thermal ablation (microwave ablation [MWA], radiofrequency ablation); intraarterial macroembolic therapy (transarterial embolization, transarterial chemoembolization [TACE]) |
| **Radiation-based LRT** | Stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT); transarterial radioembolization (TARE, yttrium 90 [Y-90]) |

- Multiphase contrast-enhanced CT or MRI; extracellular or hepatobiliary contrast agents.
- **Not for systemic therapy or immunotherapy** — other algorithms (e.g., modified Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors [mRECIST]) apply.
- **New lesion outside the treated territory** → the LI-RADS diagnostic algorithm ([[li-rads]]), not the TRA.

| Setting | When to image |
|---|---|
| After LRT, general | About every 3 months |
| After TARE | 1–3 months after treatment, then every 3 months |
| After SBRT | 3 months after treatment, then every 3 months |
| LR-TR Equivocal (nonradiation) | Short-term follow-up at 3-month intervals acceptable |
| LR-TR Nonprogressing (radiation) | 3-month follow-up imaging to reassess |

*Response-imaging intervals in the HCC treatment guidelines: see [[hepatocellular-carcinoma#Locoregional Therapy — TACE and TARE|HCC → Locoregional Therapy]].*

## Categories

**Step 1 (both cores):** look for **masslike enhancement — any degree, any phase** — in the treated lesion or along its margin (nonradiation: also along the surgical margin; the radiation Nonviable text also names the surgical margin, Figure 1B does not). The radiation core also tracks its **size over time**.

### Nonradiation LRT

| Category | Criteria (Figure 1A) | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **LR-TR Nonevaluable** | Masslike enhancement cannot be assessed (image degradation or omission) | — |
| **LR-TR Nonviable** | **No masslike enhancement** in the treated lesion, along its margin, or along the surgical margin. Examples: complete disappearance, no intralesional enhancement, smooth perilesional enhancement, parenchymal perfusional change without masslike enhancement | — |
| **LR-TR Equivocal** | **Uncertainty** about masslike enhancement (presence or morphology) | 3-month follow-up acceptable |
| **LR-TR Viable** | **Masslike enhancement** in the treated lesion, along its margin, or along the surgical margin — nodular, smooth or irregular | **Immediate re-treatment** |

- **Why Equivocal was kept:** it may hide small viable foci too small to diagnose; HCC doubling time is about **86–150 days**, so 3 months should show little growth; these patients may face more risk from their liver disease than from the HCC, and early unnecessary treatment can cause hepatic decompensation.
- Equivocal is unreliable on its own (interreader κ 0.25–0.71); **>50% and up to 83%** of LR-TR Equivocal HCCs were incompletely necrotic at pathology (meta-analysis).

### Radiation LRT

| Category | Criteria (Figure 1B) | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **LR-TR Nonevaluable** | Masslike enhancement cannot be assessed (image omission or degradation) | — |
| **LR-TR Nonviable** | **No masslike enhancement** in the treated lesion or along its margin; **no size criteria** | — |
| **LR-TR Nonprogressing** (new) | Masslike enhancement that is **stable or decreased in size** over time — **both** required; used as long as residual enhancement persists | **3-month follow-up imaging** |
| **LR-TR Viable** | Masslike enhancement that is **new or increased in size** over time | — |

- **LR-TR Equivocal is not used after radiation** — questionable viable disease → Nonprogressing.

> [!pearl] Persistent enhancement after radiation is expected
> After nonradiation LRT, posttreatment enhancement means immediate re-treatment. After radiation it is an expected finding (particularly external beam), so persistent enhancement → **imaging surveillance**; re-treat only after **multidisciplinary conference discussion** ([[acr-2024-li-rads-ct-mri-treatment-response]]).

- Why: radiation causes either immediate cell death or **cellular senescence** — viable but unable to replicate — so tumors can keep arterial phase hyperenhancement (APHE). In one explant study, persistent enhancement despite complete necrosis on explant was seen in about **45%** of SBRT-treated HCCs imaged within 3 months of transplant.
- Persistent APHE after radiation is usually seen at least **3–6 months** after treatment, then slowly resolves (timing data extremely limited).

### Ancillary Features — Upgrade Only

Optional MRI step; ancillary features (AFs) can **only upgrade**, never downgrade.

| Core | AF (either) | Where | Upgrade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nonradiation | **Restricted diffusion (any degree)** or **mild-to-moderate T2 hyperintensity** | Corresponding to the area of uncertain masslike enhancement | **Equivocal → Viable** |
| Radiation | Same two features | In the area of stable or decreasing masslike enhancement | **Nonprogressing → Viable** |

- **Hepatobiliary phase (HBP) hypointensity is not an AF** — its supporting data come only from patients with hepatitis B, and nonviable vs viable HBP-hypointense tumor can be hard to tell apart (working-group consensus).
- Figure 1B adds that the radiation AF must be **new or increased in size over time**; the text omits this qualifier.

## What Changed From 2017

| Item | 2017 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | One algorithm | **Two cores** — nonradiation and radiation |
| Viable | Nodular, masslike or thick irregular tissue with **any of** APHE, washout, or enhancement similar to pretreatment | **Masslike enhancement only** — washout and enhancement similar to pretreatment removed (low sensitivity and specificity after treatment) |
| Nonviable | Included a "treatment-specific expected enhancement pattern" (subjective) | No masslike enhancement; nonviable patterns listed explicitly |
| Equivocal | Enhancement atypical for the expected pattern | Uncertainty about masslike enhancement; **not used after radiation** |
| Radiation | Same criteria as other LRT — persistent enhancement could be miscalled Viable, prompting unnecessary re-treatment | New **Nonprogressing** category; size over time added |
| AFs | Not used | Optional upgrade only (diffusion restriction, T2 hyperintensity) |

*Built from [[acr-2024-li-rads-ct-mri-treatment-response|Aslam et al, Radiology 2024]] (Figure 1 and the category sections).*

## Pitfalls

- **Confirm absent enhancement with MRI subtraction or noncontrast CT** — coagulation necrosis after nonradiation LRT can be T1-hyperintense before contrast.
- **Radiation-induced perilesional parenchymal enhancement is expected** and does not by itself make a lesion Nonprogressing. External beam changes follow the planning volume; TARE gives a wedge-shaped vascular distribution. Compare pre- and posttreatment images.
- **Long gap between pretreatment imaging and treatment** → apparent growth on the first posttreatment scan can be miscalled Viable. Use intraprocedural TARE imaging or SBRT simulation imaging as the size baseline when available.
- **Ablative-dose Y-90 segmentectomy** often kills the tumor outright → no residual enhancement → Nonviable.
- The 2024 version still needs validation across all LRTs, especially radiation; only one study has evaluated the 2017 TRA after SBRT.
