## Overview

- [[colorectal-cancer|Colorectal cancer (CRC)]] screening of average- and family-history-risk adults: who, when, which test. Post-[[polypectomy]] intervals: [[colonoscopy-surveillance]]; the exam itself: [[colonoscopy]].

---

## Risk Tiers — Who Is Average Risk

**Average risk** = no personal or family history of CRC or colorectal neoplasia and no clinical features of CRC (gastrointestinal bleeding, iron deficiency anemia, abnormal imaging) ([[usmstf-2021-crc-screening-age|US Multi-Society Task Force (USMSTF) 2021]]) — ~75%–80% of US adults, and the only group for whom a noninvasive test is a first choice ([[aga-2022-noninvasive-crc-screening]]).

| Risk category | Lifetime CRC risk | Defining features |
|---|---|---|
| **Very high** | >20% | Hereditary syndrome, personal or family — [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis\|familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP)]] and other polyposes, [[lynch-syndrome\|Lynch]], [[serrated-polyposis-syndrome\|serrated polyposis]] |
| **High** | 10%–20% | ≥1 first-degree relative with CRC **<60 y**, or 2 at any age; prior advanced adenoma or advanced serrated polyp; [[inflammatory-bowel-disease\|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]] |
| **Average** | 4% | No symptoms and none of the above |

⚠ The <60-y family-history cutoff is the older framing — [[aga-2023-crc-screening-risk-stratification|American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) 2023]] counts **any** affected first-degree relative ([[#Family History Risk Stratification]]).

**Within average risk, risk is still graded** — steer noninvasive tests to the low end, colonoscopy to the high end ([[aga-2022-noninvasive-crc-screening]]):

- **Age** is the main gradient (incidence 35.1 → 61.2/100,000 from 45–49 to 50–54); by **~60** most people leave the noninvasive-test group.
- Higher within average risk: men vs premenopausal women; Black (incidence 43.6 vs White 36.6/100,000) and Alaska Native / American Indian adults; current smoking, [[obesity]], low physical activity (→ consider colonoscopy when younger).
- No risk-stratification scheme (including genomic scores) has yet been shown to reduce CRC incidence or mortality.

## When to Start and Stop

| Age (average risk) | [[acg-2021-crc-screening\|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2021]] | [[usmstf-2021-crc-screening-age\|USMSTF 2021]] |
|---|---|---|
| 45–49 | Suggest screening — Conditional / Very low (Rec 2) | Suggest offering — Weak / Low |
| 50–75 | Recommend — Strong / Moderate (Rec 1) | Recommend offering from 50 if not yet screened — Strong / High |
| 75 | Individualize beyond 75 — Conditional / Very low (Rec 3) | Up to date with negative screens → consider stopping at **75 or life expectancy <10 y** — Weak / Low |
| 76–85 | Individualize — best for never-screened, fit for treatment (US Preventive Services Task Force [USPSTF], as reported by ACG) | **Never screened** → consider screening **up to 85**, by age and comorbidity — Weak / Low |
| >85 | Not recommended (USPSTF, as reported by ACG) | Not recommended (ungraded) |

- **Age 45 rests on modelling, not trials** ([[aga-2020-young-adult-onset-crc]]): +6.2% life-years for +17% colonoscopies vs starting at 50. Modelled best balance: annual fecal immunochemical test (FIT) at **45–49**, colonoscopy **50–70** ([[aga-2022-noninvasive-crc-screening]]).
- **Over 75** ([[aga-2023-crc-screening-risk-stratification|AGA 2023]]): individualize screening *and* surveillance — **3.8%–6.8%** have an emergency visit or admission within 30 days of colonoscopy; benefit lags **≥5 y**, so life expectancy <5 y is unlikely to benefit.

## Symptomatic Adults Under 50 — a Diagnostic, Not a Screening, Question

- **Symptoms at any age → diagnostic colonoscopy** (rectal bleeding, weight loss, bowel-habit change, abdominal pain, [[iron-deficiency-anemia|iron deficiency anemia]]) — the screening age does not apply ([[aga-2020-young-adult-onset-crc]]). Detail: [[early-onset-colorectal-cancer]].

## Screening Modalities

> [!pitfall] A positive stool or blood test is only half a screen
> Every positive noninvasive test needs a timely, high-quality [[colonoscopy]]; a program that doesn't deliver it has screened no one ([[aga-2022-noninvasive-crc-screening]]).

| Test | Interval | ACG 2021 | USMSTF 2021 | USPSTF* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **[[colonoscopy\|Colonoscopy]]** | 10 y | **Preferred** — Strong / Low (Recs 4, 7) | **First tier** — Strong / Moderate | + |
| **FIT** | 1 y | **Preferred** — Strong / Low (Recs 4, 7) | **First tier** — Strong / Moderate | + |
| Multi-target stool DNA (MTsDNA, Cologuard) | 3 y | If colonoscopy/FIT declined — Conditional / Very low (Recs 5, 8) | If colonoscopy and FIT declined — Strong / Low | + (1–3 y) |
| Flexible sigmoidoscopy | 5–10 y | Same — Conditional / Very low | Same — Strong / High | + |
| Computed tomography (CT) colonography | 5 y | Same — Conditional / Very low | Same — Strong / Low | + |
| Colon [[capsule-endoscopy\|capsule]] | 5 y | Same — Conditional / Very low | Only if **all** above declined — Weak / Low | + |
| High-sensitivity guaiac fecal occult blood test (gFOBT) | 1 y | +* | No comment* | + |
| Septin 9 (blood) | — | **Suggest against** — Conditional / Very low (Rec 6) | Not recommended* | No comment |

*\*Starred cells and USPSTF column per [[aga-2022-noninvasive-crc-screening]] (`+` = endorsed).*

- **MTsDNA interval:** USPSTF 1 y vs ACG/USMSTF 3 y — use **3 y**.
- Flexible sigmoidoscopy: left colon only, less effective in women. CT colonography: needs prep, misses flat and sessile serrated lesions, extracolonic findings in 27%–69% ([[acg-2021-crc-screening]]).
- FIT has replaced gFOBT (adherence, no diet restriction, fewer samples).
- **An average-risk screening colonoscopy with an otherwise inadequate preparation but a well-visualized descending colon, sigmoid and rectum** (e.g. ascending or transverse colon inadequate) — reasonable to revisit screening options with the patient and the referring clinician. If the patient opts to treat the limited exam as a **flexible sigmoidoscopy** and prefers not to repeat the colonoscopy, **screen again by sigmoidoscopy or colonoscopy in 5 y**, or with a nonendoscopic test endorsed by USMSTF and USPSTF ([[usmstf-2025-bowel-prep]]). Repeat-colonoscopy intervals after an inadequate preparation: [[bowel-preparation]].

## Noninvasive Test Performance — the Numbers Behind the Choice

*Source: [[aga-2022-noninvasive-crc-screening]] unless noted.*

### FIT — Positivity Threshold Sets the Performance

- FIT measures human hemoglobin (no diet restriction). **Lower threshold → more sensitivity, less specificity, more colonoscopies.**
- **Use a positivity threshold ≤20 µg/g** — weak, low ([[usmstf-2016-fit-screening]]); also the threshold of common US quantitative tests.
- ⚠ **FIT has no utility for serrated lesion detection.**
- Sample number, quantitative vs qualitative, kit handling, and positive-FIT follow-up: [[colorectal-cancer#FIT Screening for CRC — Primary Screening (Average-Risk)|colorectal-cancer]].

Sensitivity / specificity, % (95% confidence interval [CI]); AA = advanced adenoma; NR = not reported.

| Source / cutoff | Sens — CRC | Sens — AA | Spec — CRC | Spec — AA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Imperiale 2019 meta-analysis** | | | | |
| 10 µg/g | 91 (84–95) | 40 (33–47) | 90 (86–93) | 90 (87–93) |
| 20 µg/g | 75 (61–86) | 25 (20–31) | 95 (92–96) | 95 (93–96) |
| **Pivotal trial**, FIT at 20 µg/g | 74 (61–84) | 24 (21–27) | 95 (94–95) | NR |

### Multi-Target Stool DNA (MTsDNA, Cologuard)

- FIT + DNA methylation markers + *KRAS* mutations; the result doesn't say which component was positive. **Approved for average-risk adults 45–85 only.**

| Pivotal trial (FIT at 20 µg/g) | MTsDNA | FIT | *P* |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sensitivity — CRC | 92.3% | 73.8% | .002 |
| Sensitivity — AA + sessile serrated polyps ≥10 mm | 42.4% | 23.8% | <.001 |
| Specificity — CRC | 86.6% | 94.9% | <.001 |

- **The trade:** ~1 in 5 more cancers and nearly twice the advanced precursors, for ~8 points of specificity (more false-positive colonoscopies). Similar in Black and White participants.

> [!pearl] Positive MTsDNA, negative high-quality colonoscopy → stop
> No upper endoscopy or further testing without symptoms or signs ([[aga-2022-noninvasive-crc-screening]], [[acg-2021-crc-screening]]). At 5.4 y, aerodigestive cancers were 2.4% (discordant) vs 1.1% (concordant) — no difference in risk ratio.

### Septin 9 and Urine Testing

- **Septin 9 (Epi proColon):** Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved only for average-risk adults ≥50 who refused other tests; no guideline endorses it. **CRC sensitivity 48%, specificity 91.5%; AA 11%.**
- **PolypDx** (urine): adenoma sensitivity 83%–89%, specificity 42%–51%; not commercially available.

## Blood-Based CRC Screening (AGA 2025 CPU)

*Source: [[aga-2025-crc-blood-tests]].* Cell-free DNA tests; Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) covers them **every 3 y** in average risk if CRC sensitivity ≥74%, specificity ≥90%.

| Test | CRC sensitivity | Specificity | Advanced precursor lesion (APL) sensitivity | Stage 1 CRC | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Guardant Shield** | 83.1% | 89.6% | 13.2% | 55% | FDA-approved May 2024, CMS-covered; $1450 |
| **Freenome** | 79.2% | 91.5% | 12.5% | 57% | Validated; approval expected to be sought |

*APL = adenoma or sessile serrated lesion ≥10 mm, villous, high-grade dysplasia, or carcinoma in situ.*

Modelled effect of each program (4 validated models):

| Program | ↓ CRC incidence | ↓ CRC mortality |
|---|---|---|
| Colonoscopy q10y | 57%–81% | 72%–88% |
| Annual FIT | 45%–77% | 67%–81% |
| Triennial MTsDNA | 40%–73% | 64%–78% |
| Triennial Guardant Shield | 24%–47% | 53%–59% |

- **Poor APL and stage 1 detection** → less prevention, fewer curable cancers; triennial Shield is less effective and costlier than FIT or colonoscopy (still inferior at $300).
- **Use:** patients who decline all other screening (offering it raised uptake 17.5%). **Substituting** it for FIT/colonoscopy causes net harm unless participation rises ≥35%. Positive → colonoscopy is mandatory.
- Multicancer detection tests (e.g., Galleri) and interval blood tests between colonoscopies: premature.

## Program Quality Metrics for Noninvasive Screening

Targets from [[aga-2022-noninvasive-crc-screening]] ("aspirational, but attainable"). **Delay >6 months after a positive FIT → more advanced adenoma, CRC, and advanced-stage CRC.** USMSTF 2016 FIT-program targets: [[colorectal-cancer#Programmatic Quality Targets (USMSTF 2016)|colorectal-cancer]].

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Tests completed / ordered | **≥90% within 1 y** |
| Outreach if not completed | at **1 month** |
| Result delivered to patient | **100% within ≤2 weeks** |
| Positive → colonoscopy date offered | **≥80% within 3 months, 100% within 6** |
| Colonoscopy performed / recommended | **≥95% within 6 months** |

- Negative result → tell the recall interval *and* who will recall. Missed targets → root-cause review.
- **Patient selection is a metric too:** document a **3-generation family cancer history** (CRC and advanced adenoma, with age at diagnosis) — high-risk patients belong in colonoscopy, not a stool test.

Colonoscopist metrics (ACG Recs 13–16, FIT-positive adenoma detection thresholds): [[colonoscopy-quality-indicators]].

## Family History Risk Stratification

| Risk Category | Screening Approach | Grade |
|--------------|-------------------|---|
| **1 first-degree relative (FDR) with CRC or advanced polyp at <60, or ≥2 FDRs at any age** | Colonoscopy from **40 or 10 y before the youngest affected relative, whichever is earlier**, **every 5 y** ([[acg-2021-crc-screening]] Rec 9) | Conditional / Very low |
| **1 FDR with CRC or advanced polyp at ≥60** | Start at **40 or 10 y before** the youngest affected relative, then **average-risk screening** (Rec 11) | Conditional / Very low |
| **Higher familial burden** (more or younger relatives) | Consider **genetic evaluation** (Rec 10) | Conditional / Very low |
| **1 second-degree relative** | Average-risk screening (Rec 12) | Conditional / Low |
| **Hereditary syndromes** | Syndrome pages: [[lynch-syndrome]], [[familial-adenomatous-polyposis]], [[serrated-polyposis-syndrome]] | — |
| **IBD** | Dysplasia surveillance, not screening — [[ulcerative-colitis]] / [[crohns-disease]] | — |

- **[[aga-2023-crc-screening-risk-stratification|AGA 2023]]** (unrated Best Practice Advice): **any first-degree relative with CRC** = increased risk, especially if diagnosed **<50 y** (relative risk [RR] 3.26 vs 1.83 if ≥50 y) — no <60/≥60 split. Increased risk → **colonoscopy** is the test of choice.
- ⚠ AGA's "any FDR" heuristic governs *when to start*; the ACG <60/≥60 split still governs *interval intensity*.

## Chemoprevention of Colorectal Neoplasia

- [[aga-2021-crc-chemoprevention|AGA 2021]] covers **average risk only** — not diet, hereditary syndromes, or IBD (there, inflammation control: [[ulcerative-colitis]]). Ungraded; **"should"** = more robust evidence, **"may"** = less.

### Aspirin — use it, in a narrowly defined group

**All must hold** — [[acg-2021-crc-screening|ACG 2021]] Rec 17 (Conditional / Low) and AGA 2021 Best Practice Advice (BPA) 1 ("should"):

| Criterion | ACG 2021 | AGA 2021 | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Age | **50–69** | **<70 and life expectancy ≥10 y** | Bleeding ↑ >70; ASPREE (96% ≥70) found *higher* CRC mortality (hazard ratio [HR] 1.77) |
| Cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk | **≥10% over 10 y** | **≥10% over 10 y** (atherosclerotic CVD Risk Estimator) | Benefit shown only in at-risk adults <70 |
| Bleeding risk | Not elevated | Not high | Any dose: major gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding odds ratio (OR) 1.59; intracranial OR 1.34 |
| Commitment | Willing to take ≥10 y | — | — |

- **Aspirin never substitutes for screening** — Strong / Low (ACG Rec 18).
- CRC incidence unchanged at 0–12 y, **↓40% at 10–19 y**; mortality benefit only at 10–20 y.
- Low dose doesn't remove bleeding risk (≤100 mg/d: risk "did not change substantially"). No chemopreventive mg dose is stated.
- After CRC: **"should consider"** aspirin to prevent recurrent neoplasia (BPA 2) — [[colorectal-cancer]].

> [!pitfall] Don't start aspirin for a patient who won't outlive the latency
> Benefit appears only 10–19 y after starting; a patient with life expectancy <10 y gets the bleeding risk without the benefit ([[aga-2021-crc-chemoprevention]]).

### The five "should not use" agents

| Agent | AGA 2021 | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| Non-aspirin nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) | BPA 3 — should not (average risk) | Effective, but cyclooxygenase-2 inhibitor trials stopped early for cardiovascular events; GI toxicity; benefit lost on stopping |
| Calcium | BPA 6 — should not (± vitamin D) | **Harm:** delayed ↑ sessile serrated lesions (RR 2.66) |
| Vitamin D | BPA 6 — should not | No effect on CRC incidence; with calcium → ↑ sessile serrated lesions at 6–10 y (RR 3.82) |
| Folic acid | BPA 7 — should not | Trial: no adenoma reduction; ↑ advanced/multiple adenomas, ↑ non-colorectal (prostate) cancers |
| Statins | BPA 8/9 — should not (average risk; mortality after CRC) | No demonstrated benefit for this indication (not toxicity) |

- Calcium, vitamin D, folic acid: **protective in cohorts, null or harmful in trials** — AGA follows the trials.

### Metformin — "may consider," and only in type 2 diabetes

- **Type 2 diabetes → may consider** metformin to prevent colorectal neoplasia (BPA 4; observational: CRC ↓27%, adenoma ↓20%) and, with CRC, to reduce mortality (BPA 5; cancer-specific survival HR 0.75) — observational data, susceptible to confounding.
- Nondiabetic: only one small randomized controlled trial (n = 151; 250 mg × 1 y → adenoma recurrence ↓40%; mild adverse events 11%).
