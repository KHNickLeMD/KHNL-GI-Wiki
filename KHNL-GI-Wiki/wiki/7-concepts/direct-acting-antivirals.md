Direct-acting antivirals (DAAs) are small-molecule antiviral agents targeting specific components of the [[hepatitis-c|HCV]] replication cycle. They have transformed HCV treatment from interferon-based regimens with ~50% SVR and significant toxicity to oral, well-tolerated regimens achieving SVR12 >95% in clinical trials and real-world practice.

## Drug Classes and Targets

| Class | Target | Key agents |
|---|---|---|
| NS3/4A protease inhibitors | HCV serine protease (replication initiation) | Glecaprevir, grazoprevir, voxilaprevir |
| NS5A inhibitors | Replication complex assembly/trafficking | Pibrentasvir, velpatasvir, ledipasvir, elbasvir |
| NS5B polymerase inhibitors (nucleotide analog) | RNA-dependent RNA polymerase | Sofosbuvir |

NS3/4A inhibitors are **contraindicated in decompensated cirrhosis** (Child-Pugh B/C) due to significant hepatic metabolism and risk of drug accumulation.

## Pangenotypic Regimens (First-Line)

- **Glecaprevir/pibrentasvir (G/P; Mavyret):** NS3/4A + NS5A; 8 wk (no/compensated cirrhosis), 8 wk also for compensated cirrhosis per EXPEDITION-8; genotypes 1–6
- **Sofosbuvir/velpatasvir (SOF/VEL; Epclusa):** NS5B + NS5A; 12 wk (no/compensated cirrhosis); genotypes 1–6; used with ribavirin for decompensated cirrhosis

## Key DDIs

- Cyclosporine: increases elbasvir/grazoprevir and SOF/VEL/VOX AUC; do not coadminister
- G/P + cyclosporine >100 mg/d: not recommended
- Calcineurin inhibitor interactions common in post-transplant settings; see [[calcineurin-inhibitors]]
