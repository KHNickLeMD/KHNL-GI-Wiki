Colonoscopy's efficacy varies among endoscopists, and lower-quality colonoscopy means higher interval [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer (CRC)]] incidence and mortality. Quality has three components: **effectiveness** (detecting CRC and its precursors), **safety**, and **value** ([[aga-2021-colonoscopy-quality]]).

## The Benchmark Set

*Governing set: [[asge-acg-2024-colonoscopy-quality-indicators|ASGE/ACG 2024]] — 15 numbered indicators, each with the task force's own strength grade, a measure type and a single minimum performance target. Successor to the same task force's 2006 and 2015 documents and newer than every other quality document on the wiki, so where numbers differ it governs ([[#Where the Benchmarks Differ Between Documents]]). ⭐ = **priority indicator**, measurement of which is "considered essential".*

| # | Indicator | Target | Strength | Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Appropriate indication, documented | **≥95%** | 1C+ | Process |
| 2 | ⭐ Bowel preparation adequacy rate | **≥90%** | 1C | Process |
| 3 | ⭐ Cecal intubation rate | **≥95%** | 1C | Process |
| 4 | ⭐ **Adenoma detection rate (ADR)** | **≥35%** | 1C+ | Outcome |
| 5 | ADR after a **positive fecal test** (fecal blood or multitarget stool DNA, mt-sDNA) | **≥50%** | 1C | Outcome |
| 6 | **Adenomas per colonoscopy (APC)** | **≥0.6** | 2C | Outcome |
| 7 | ⭐ **Sessile serrated lesion detection rate (SSLDR)** | **≥6%** | 2C | Outcome |
| 8 | Average withdrawal time, normal colonoscopies | **≥8 min** | 2A | Process |
| 9 | Polyp resections documenting size, shape, location, method | **≥98%** | 3 | Process |
| 10 | 4–9 mm lesions resected by **cold snare** | **≥90%** | **1A** | Process |
| 11 | ⭐ Use of recommended screening / surveillance intervals | **≥90%** | 1C | Process |
| 12 | Serious adverse events tracked, documented, **reviewed by a quality-improvement committee** | **≥95%** | 1C | Process |
| 13 | [[ulcerative-colitis\|UC]]: formal extent **and** activity score recorded | **≥90%** | 3 | Process |
| 14 | [[crohns-disease\|Crohn's disease]]: formal endoscopic activity score reported | **≥90%** | 3 | Process |
| 15 | Appropriate next interval after dysplasia-negative UC / indeterminate colitis surveillance | **≥90%** | 3 | Process |

- **Grades are the task force's own 8-level scheme, not GRADE:** **1A** randomized trials without important limitations · **1B** randomized trials with important limitations · **1C+** overwhelming observational evidence (strong) · **1C** observational (intermediate strength) · **2A** benefit unclear but good randomized data (intermediate) · **2B** weak · **2C** very weak · **3** expert opinion only. Indicator 10 is the document's **only 1A**.
- Targets came from published benchmarking data. Where data were absent and failure would be a **"never event"**, the target is written **>98%**.
- ⚠ **These targets are not the standard of care, credentialing requirements or training standards**; the document calls applying them that way a **misuse** of it.
- If resources allow only three measures, the earlier triage answer was cecal intubation rate, bowel preparation quality and ADR ([[aga-2021-colonoscopy-quality]]); the five priority indicators are the current answer.

### Measurement Level and Frequency

- **Unit level** — uncommon outcomes (adverse events) and metrics reflecting **system-based practice** (bowel preparation quality).
- **Endoscopist level** — metrics reflecting **colonoscopist skill** (ADR), so individual feedback is possible. ADR is fed back at both levels.
- The 2024 set specifies no measurement interval; the figures below are AGA 2021's ([[aga-2021-colonoscopy-quality]]).

| Metric | Measure over |
|---|---|
| **ADR** | **≥250 screening colonoscopies** — semi-annually if high volume, annually if lower |
| **Serrated detection** | **≥500 screening colonoscopies, or annually** (lower lesion prevalence needs a bigger denominator) |
| **Bowel prep adequacy** | At a minimum **annually** |

### Denominators and Definitions

Indicators 4, 6, 7 and 8 share one denominator — **patients aged ≥45 y having colonoscopy for screening, surveillance *or* diagnostic indications** — and the same **four exclusions: positive non-colonoscopy screening tests** (fecal tests, CT colonography), **genetic cancer syndromes** (e.g. polyposis), **[[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]]**, and **colonoscopy for therapy of known neoplasms** ([[asge-acg-2024-colonoscopy-quality-indicators]]).

| Metric | Numerator / definition | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **ADR** | **≥1 conventional adenoma** detected and **verified by pathology** | **No threshold adjustment** for including **45–49-year-olds**, and **second and subsequent screening colonoscopies count without adjustment** — both settled explicitly |
| **APC** | **Number of conventional adenomas** detected per colonoscopy | Added because ADR ignores every adenoma after the first. ADR and APC correlate highly **but not always** — in one study **47.6% of endoscopists in the lowest APC quartile still had an ADR ≥25%** |
| **SSLDR** | **≥1 sessile serrated lesion removed** and documented by pathology | Depends on pathologist interpretation |
| **ADR after a positive fecal test** | ≥1 conventional adenoma resected and documented by pathology | **The same ≥50% threshold applies to mt-sDNA-positive and fecal immunochemical test (FIT)-positive patients** |
| **Cecal intubation rate** | Patients **with intact colons** with full cecal intubation **and photodocumentation of cecal landmarks** | Photodocumentation is part of the definition, not an add-on |
| **Withdrawal time** | **Average**, normal colonoscopies **without biopsy sampling or polypectomy**, from the **appendiceal orifice to completion of rectal retroflexion** | |
| **Bowel prep adequacy** | See [[#Bowel Preparation Adequacy]] | The assigned interval must also be consistent with US Multi-Society Task Force (USMSTF) recommendations |

## Bowel Preparation Adequacy

- **Adequate = Boston Bowel Preparation Scale (BBPS) total ≥6 *and* every segment score ≥2.** Both halves are required — a total of 6 built on a segment scoring 0 or 1 is not adequate. The 2024 indicator also accepts a preparation described as **excellent, good or adequate**.
- **Grade adequacy only after all washing and suctioning are complete** (strong, low) — grading before cleansing measures the purgative, not the examination ([[acg-2014-bowel-cleansing]]).
- **Measure the rate routinely** (strong, moderate). **Below the benchmark → undertake an improvement initiative** — explicit, not implied. **Submit procedure reports to a data registry** that benchmarks against minimally accepted thresholds and against peer means.
- ⚠ **The benchmark and the level it is measured at both changed.** USMSTF 2014 set **≥85% of all examinations, per physician**; [[usmstf-2025-bowel-prep|USMSTF 2025]] sets **≥90% at the individual endoscopist *and* the endoscopy-unit level**, which is what the table above follows. Auditing against the older benchmark passes at a rate the current documents call inadequate.
- Scale anchors, preparation regimens, salvage maneuvers and patient-instruction practices: [[bowel-preparation]].

## Cecal Intubation

- **≥95%** of patients **with intact colons**, with **photodocumentation of cecal landmarks** (appendiceal orifice and ileocecal valve) in the report ([[asge-acg-2024-colonoscopy-quality-indicators]]).
- Low rates track with **diminished neoplasia detection, higher interval CRC incidence**, and the cost of repeat or alternative testing. Failure should be rare in skilled hands **except with inadequate preparation or an obstructing lesion**.
- **Uniquely, this indicator may be measured intermittently or not at all** for a colonoscopist with demonstrated consistent high performance — justified by limited evidence of variable performance, evidence that high performance is sustained, and the ceiling effect at 100%.
- Levers for low performers (loop reduction, CO₂ insufflation, variable-stiffness endoscopes): [[colonoscopy]].

## Withdrawal Time

- **Average ≥8 minutes** in normal colonoscopies without biopsy sampling or polypectomy, measured **appendiceal orifice → completion of rectal retroflexion** ([[asge-acg-2024-colonoscopy-quality-indicators]]).
- **The evidence is for 8–9 minutes, not 6.** The 6-minute figure dates from 2006; evidence since 2015 shows optimal detection and **post-colonoscopy colorectal cancer (PCCRC)** prevention typically need an average of **at least 8–9 min**. High-quality inspection — clearing retained pools, systematically exposing mucosa behind every fold, flexure and valve, adequate distention — takes that long.

> [!pitfall] A low ADR plus a short withdrawal time is a technique diagnosis
> The target is a benchmark for an **average**; **not every withdrawal must last 8 minutes**. But **ADR and/or SSLDR below threshold *with* an average withdrawal <8 min "could be construed as evidence of an inadequate withdrawal technique"** → evaluate and correct the inspection technique, don't just run the clock longer ([[asge-acg-2024-colonoscopy-quality-indicators]]).

## Adenoma Detection Rate (ADR)

- **Target ≥35%**, grade 1C+, **no sex-specific thresholds** — the 2024 set does not split by sex at all ([[asge-acg-2024-colonoscopy-quality-indicators]]).
- **Counting rules:** only **conventional adenomas** count — sessile serrated lesions are excluded and counted separately as SSLDR. Colonoscopies done for a **positive fecal test are excluded from routine ADR** and measured as their own indicator at **≥50%**.
- Best-validated quality indicator in colonoscopy and a validated predictor of PCCRC. It **varies up to 5-fold among colonoscopists** and an unmeasured colonoscopist cannot know where they sit — the task force's position is that **failure to measure performance is unacceptable**.
- **Why it matters:** each **1% rise in ADR → 3% lower CRC incidence and 5% lower fatal CRC**; **ADR <20% carried a >10-fold higher post-colonoscopy CRC risk** ([[acg-2021-crc-screening]]).
- **[[acg-2021-crc-screening|American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) 2021]] Rec 13:** every screening colonoscopist should **measure** individual cecal intubation rate, ADR and withdrawal time (Strong; moderate for ADR, low for withdrawal time and cecal intubation).

### Adenomas per Colonoscopy (APC)

- **Target ≥0.6** adenomas per colonoscopy, same denominator and exclusions as ADR (grade 2C, outcome measure). New in 2024.
- Rationale: ADR **does not reward detecting any adenoma after the first**, and many patients have several. Higher APC, like higher ADR, is associated with **lower PCCRC risk**. An acceptable ADR can sit on top of a poor APC — "find one and stop looking".

### Improving Detection

| Lever | What the evidence shows |
|---|---|
| Report cards / audit and feedback | Modestly effective, particularly for **low-performing** colonoscopists |
| Hands-on training with local leaders | Improved ADR **significantly more than quality feedback alone** |
| Quality-improvement "champions" in the unit | A primary component of sustainable educational intervention |
| Educational content that works | Adequate withdrawal time, looking **behind folds**, cleaning the colon, recognizing subtle lesions |
| Electronic chromoendoscopy (eg, narrow band imaging) on withdrawal | Higher ADR among **experienced** colonoscopists; particularly helpful for **flat** lesions |
| Distal-tip accessory devices | Improve mucosal exposure; **modest** ADR gains |
| Water exchange (instil and suction water on insertion, no air/CO₂) | Improves ADR, likely via better colon cleansing |
| Computer-aided detection (CADe) | Named only as anticipated future work; evidence and the AGA position are on [[artificial-intelligence-endoscopy]] |

## Serrated Lesion Detection Rate (SSLDR)

- **Target ≥6%** — patients with **≥1 sessile serrated lesion removed and documented by pathology**, same denominator and exclusions as ADR (grade 2C). **A priority indicator**, new to that list in 2024 ([[asge-acg-2024-colonoscopy-quality-indicators]]).
- **Pathology-dependent in a way ADR is not** — substantial interobserver variation among pathologists distinguishing sessile serrated lesions from hyperplastic polyps, which is not an issue for conventional adenomas. So **remediation must target both the colonoscopist and the reading pathologists**.
- Two design problems the task force names: an ideal serrated indicator should **not reward resecting diminutive distal hyperplastic polyps** (generally not precancerous), and any indicator relying on endoscopist-reported location **could be gamed**.
- **Alternatives considered**, each tied to the lowest PCCRC risk at that level: **clinically significant serrated detection rate, benchmark 9%** (needs pathology, size and location) and **proximal serrated polyp detection rate, benchmark 17%** (location only — least pathology-dependent).
- Withdrawal time and serrated detection are clearly associated; the larger denominator a confident estimate needs is above.

## Resection Indicators

Two indicators, both new in 2024 ([[asge-acg-2024-colonoscopy-quality-indicators]]).

- **Cold snare for 4–9 mm lesions, ≥90%** (grade **1A** — the document's only 1A indicator). Technique and the other size bands: [[polypectomy]].
- **Documentation of size, shape, location and method of resection, ≥98%** (grade 3) — see [[#Documentation]].

## IBD Colonoscopy Indicators

Three indicators, all new in 2024, all grade 3 (expert opinion) with a **≥90%** target ([[asge-acg-2024-colonoscopy-quality-indicators]]).

| # | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **13** | UC colonoscopy records a **formal assessment of extent *and* activity** — Mayo Endoscopic Score (MES), Modified MES, Ulcerative Colitis Endoscopic Index of Severity (UCEIS), or Ulcerative Colitis Colonoscopic Index of Severity |
| **14** | Crohn's disease colonoscopy reports a **formal endoscopic activity score** — Crohn's Disease Endoscopic Index of Severity (CDEIS), Simple Endoscopic Score for Crohn's Disease (SES-CD), or Rutgeerts score |
| **15** | A dysplasia-negative UC / indeterminate colitis surveillance exam gets an **appropriate next interval** — the 1 y / 2–3 y / 3–5 y grid is on [[ulcerative-colitis#Colorectal Cancer Surveillance\|ulcerative colitis]] |

- **Why a score rather than a description:** symptoms often do not correlate with endoscopic findings, and extent plus severity drive **severity classification, prognosis, choice of medical therapy, and colorectal neoplasia risk**.
- Extent may be given by involved segments or by the **Montreal classification** — **E1** rectum only, **E2** left-sided, **E3** proximal to the splenic flexure.
- Score anchors, inter-rater performance and the STRIDE-II endoscopic-healing targets: [[ibd-endoscopic-scoring]].
- **Surveillance technique:** high-definition colonoscopy **plus one modality of chromoendoscopy** with targeted biopsy sampling of suspicious areas; whether dye-spray or electronic chromoendoscopy adds anything over high definition alone is **uncertain**.

## Unnumbered Best Practices

Advice statements without a measurable rate attached ([[aga-2021-colonoscopy-quality]]):

- **High-definition colonoscopes for screening and surveillance** — definite but modest gain in adenoma, serrated and advanced-adenoma detection; many legacy lower-definition instruments remain in circulation, and upgrading is an effective unit-level strategy ([[colonoscopy]]).
- **Second look of the right colon**, retroflexed or forward view — the right colon is the most frequent location for missed CRC and polyps; technique and the size of the gain are on [[colonoscopy]].
- **Cold snare polypectomy for nonpedunculated polyps 3–9 mm** (the *measured* indicator covers 4–9 mm), **forceps limits**, and **referral of benign complex polyps for [[endoscopic-mucosal-resection|endoscopic resection]] rather than surgery**: [[polypectomy]].

## Documentation

A detailed report covering **indication, extent of examination, bowel preparation quality, findings and interventions, and the follow-up plan with its rationale**. The **CO-RADS** standardized colonoscopy reporting and data system (National Colorectal Cancer Roundtable, 2007) lists the elements:

| Element | Required content |
|---|---|
| Patient | Demographics and history; assessment of risk and comorbidity |
| Indication | Reason for the procedure |
| Technical description | Extent of examination, **bowel preparation adequacy**, and **withdrawal time where appropriate** |
| Findings and assessment | Interventions and unplanned events |
| Follow-up plan | Includes **resumption of medications — anticoagulants and antiplatelet agents** ([[anticoagulation-gi-bleeding]]) |
| Pathology and interval | Communicated to the **patient and the primary care provider** and documented in the chart |

**If the assigned interval deviates from standard guidelines, the report must state the rationale** — eg, inadequate preparation or incomplete polyp resection.

**Polyp reporting rules** (indicator 9, target ≥98%; [[asge-acg-2024-colonoscopy-quality-indicators]]):

- Report for **every** polyp: **size · shape · location by segment** (or distance in cm from the anus for left-sided polyps) **· method of removal**.
- **Shape:** the **Paris classification** is recommended (its types are on the polypectomy page); "sessile", "flat" and "pedunculated" are also acceptable.
- **Sizing:** open biopsy forceps held over and against the polyp; for smaller polyps, the **snare sheath tip (2.4 mm)** against the polyp base.
- **Grouping:** polyps in one segment may be reported as a range ("4 flat and sessile polyps 2–4 mm removed by cold snare"). **Polyps ≥10 mm are described individually**; several ≥10 mm from the same segment removed the same way with similar predicted histology may be grouped.
- ⚠ **Any lesion of any size suggesting advanced histology or endoscopic features of submucosal-or-deeper invasion goes in its own pathology bottle** — never combined with polyps from another segment.

## Adverse Events

- **Indicator 12: ≥95% of serious adverse events tracked, documented *and* reviewed by a quality-improvement committee** to identify system and clinical areas for improvement — the 2024 set makes the review mandatory, not optional ([[asge-acg-2024-colonoscopy-quality-indicators]]).
- The 2024 serious-event list is wider than the AGA set: **perforation, postpolypectomy bleeding, sedation-related cardiovascular events, unplanned hospitalization, mortality**. Serious adverse events run **under 1%** across large integrated health systems.
- Most serious events overall: **colonic perforation; adjacent organ injury such as splenic rupture; serious post-procedure bleeding**. Risk rises with **age, comorbidity, and performance of polypectomy**. Event rates and complication management: [[colonoscopy]].
- Optional unit-level systematic monitoring of **delayed** events: scheduled phone calls; administrative data on post-procedure bleeding and perforation, hospital readmission or emergency department visits, patient deaths, and interval CRC. **Perforations, deaths, and interval CRC cases should be reviewed for quality improvement.**

## Interval Adherence

Assigning the guideline-concordant interval is itself a quality indicator — non-adherence produces both interval CRC (surveillance too late) and unnecessary harm and cost (surveillance too early). Interval grids: [[colonoscopy-surveillance]]; modality choice and screening start/stop ages: [[colorectal-cancer-screening]].

- **Indicator 11 puts a number on adherence: ≥90%**, covering post-polypectomy and post-cancer-resection intervals **and** the 10-year interval between screening colonoscopies in average-risk patients with a negative, adequately-prepped examination. **A shorter screening interval is appropriate only for inadequate bowel preparation.** Clinical decision support tools improve compliance ([[asge-acg-2024-colonoscopy-quality-indicators]]).
- **1–2 small adenomas (especially 1–2 diminutive adenomas):** increasing evidence that these patients are **not at significantly elevated CRC risk versus the general population**, and endoscopists **should consider assigning a 10-year interval** ([[aga-2021-colonoscopy-quality]]) — longer than the 7–10 y the surveillance grid assigns ([[usmstf-2020-followup-colonoscopy]]).

## Remediation — What to Do When a Metric Misses

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    A["Measure — at the right level<br/>Unit: prep adequacy, adverse events<br/>Endoscopist: cecal intubation, withdrawal time<br/>Both: ADR, SSLDR"] --> B{"ADR ≥35%?"}
    B -- No --> C["Extend withdrawal time<br/>Self-learning: mucosal inspection, polyp identification<br/>Peer feedback and report cards<br/>Hands-on training with local leaders"]
    B -- Yes --> D{"SSLDR ≥6%?"}
    D -- No --> E["Remediate colonoscopists<br/>AND reading pathologists"]
    D -- Yes --> F{"Prep adequacy ≥90%<br/>at endoscopist and unit level?"}
    F -- No --> G["Split-dose as standard<br/>Rewrite patient instructions<br/>Add education and navigation"]
    F -- Yes --> H{"Cecal intubation ≥95%?"}
    H -- No --> I["Loop reduction technique<br/>CO₂ insufflation<br/>Variable-stiffness endoscope"]
    H -- Yes --> J["Optimize: high-definition scopes, right-colon second look,<br/>cold snare, expert referral for complex polyps,<br/>detailed documentation, adverse-event and interval-CRC monitoring"]
```

*Ladder rebuilt from the AGA 2021 best-practice advice and its tiers-of-quality figure, with the 2024 targets.*

### Tiers of Colonoscopy Quality

| Domain | Poor | Variable | Adequate | Optimal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bowel preparation** | High risk of inadequate prep | Variable | Adequate for all patient groups | Adequate for all patient groups |
| **Withdrawal** | Insufficient quality and time | Variable, occasionally insufficient | Meets minimal standards | **Exceeds** minimal standards |
| **Polyp detection** | Suboptimal | Suboptimal | Meets benchmark levels | **Exceeds** benchmarks, may meet aspirational targets |
| **Polypectomy** | — | Documentation improved, important details may be missing | Low rate of incomplete or inadequate polypectomy | High skill and accuracy; appropriate referral for endoscopic resection of large polyps, surgery avoided for benign polyps |
| **Documentation** | Poor | Improved, important details may be missing | Meets minimal standards | Detailed, including photodocumentation and polyp details |
| **Surveillance intervals** | Inappropriate recommendations | May vary from guidelines | Low rate of inappropriate recommendations | Appropriate for all patients |

**Interventions by tier:**

- **Tier 1 — all providers:** prep adequacy ≥90%; split preps; measure withdrawal time and ADR; meet ADR benchmarks; interventions to improve detection for low detectors; minimum procedure documentation; adherence to recommended surveillance intervals.
- **Tier 2 — quality improvement:** interventions to improve preparation for all patients, detection for **all** providers, and polypectomy practice.
- **Tier 3 — optimizing quality:** all providers continuously monitor and optimize detection; optimized care for large polyps; detailed documentation; monitoring of adverse events and interval cancer rates.

## Where the Benchmarks Differ Between Documents

**[[asge-acg-2024-colonoscopy-quality-indicators|ASGE/ACG 2024]] governs** — the current task-force document and the newest of the four. The older numbers are kept because readers will have audited against them, not because they are live targets.

| Metric | **ASGE/ACG 2024 (governs)** | [[aga-2021-colonoscopy-quality\|AGA 2021 Clinical Practice Update (CPU)]] | [[acg-2021-crc-screening\|ACG 2021]] | [[usmstf-2020-followup-colonoscopy\|USMSTF 2020]] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ADR** | **≥35%**, no sex split | **≥30%** (aspirational ≥35%); sex-based ADR only for a markedly sex-skewed practice | **<25%** → remedial training (Rec 14, Conditional / Very low); discussion treats 30% men / 20% women as minimums, aspirational 45%–50% | **≥30% men / ≥20% women** |
| **ADR denominator** | Screening **+ surveillance + diagnostic**, age **≥45 y**; 4 exclusions | **Screening only, ages 50–75** | Screening | Screening |
| **Cecal intubation** | **≥95%**, patients with intact colons, photodocumented | **≥90%** of screening + surveillance; **≥95%** excluding inadequately prepped exams | **≥95% in screening subjects** (Rec 16, Strong / Low) | Complete to cecum with photodocumented landmark |
| **Withdrawal time** | **≥8 min** average | Mean **≥6 min**, aspirational **≥9 min** | **≥6 min** of mucosal inspection (Rec 15, Strong / Low) | Not specified |
| **Serrated detection** | **SSLDR ≥6%**, a priority indicator | **SDR ≥7%** (aspirational ≥10%); prioritize ADR if only one can be collected | — | — |

> [!pitfall] The denominator mismatch is the dangerous one
> The threshold changes are visible; the denominator change is silent. Two endoscopists each reporting "my ADR is 33%" — one on the 2024 definition (screening + surveillance + diagnostic, age ≥45) and one on AGA 2021's (screening only, 50–75) — are not measuring the same quantity. **Say which definition produced the number.**

- **SSLDR ≥6% is *lower* than AGA 2021's SDR ≥7%** — the one benchmark that moved down, and the two documents also disagree on whether a serrated indicator is optional at all.

Other USMSTF ADR figures, each tied to its own population:

- **Post-CRC-resection surveillance:** a "high-quality" exam requires conventional adenoma detection **>25%** overall ([[usmstf-2015-crc-surveillance|USMSTF 2015]]).
- **Screening start/stop modeling:** "high-quality" colonoscopy assumes ADR **≥25%** overall ([[usmstf-2021-crc-screening-age|USMSTF 2021]]).
- **Colonoscopy after a positive FIT** (cut-off ≤20 µg/g): ADR **>45% men, >35% women** ([[usmstf-2016-fit-screening|USMSTF 2016]]).
