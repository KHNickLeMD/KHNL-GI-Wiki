## Definition

Antibody-mediated rejection (AMR) in liver transplantation is a distinct rejection phenotype mediated by pre-formed or de novo donor-specific antibodies (DSA) directed against HLA or non-HLA antigens on the allograft endothelium and bile ducts. Unlike other solid organ transplants, the liver is relatively resistant to antibody-mediated injury, but AMR is increasingly recognized as a cause of graft dysfunction and loss.

## Diagnosis

AMR in liver transplantation requires a combination of findings (Banff 2022 criteria):

1. **Histologic evidence** of microvascular injury (sinusoidal dilation, endothelial injury, portal/sinusoidal microthrombi, biliary epithelial injury)
2. **C4d deposition** — positive staining on portal endothelium or sinusoidal lining; note that C4d has low specificity in liver allografts alone
3. **Donor-specific antibodies (DSA)** — class I or class II HLA antibodies detected in recipient serum
4. **Graft dysfunction** — elevated liver enzymes, cholestasis, or declining synthetic function
5. **Exclusion of other causes** (TCMR, recurrent disease, biliary/vascular complications)

*Note: C4d deposition alone is insufficient for AMR diagnosis in the liver; all 4 criteria should ideally be present.*

## Clinical Features

- Can be hyperacute (rare; preformed high-titer DSA), acute, or chronic
- Chronic AMR presents with progressive cholestasis, fibrosis, and obliterative arteriopathy
- ABO-incompatible LT recipients are at highest risk for hyperacute AMR
- De novo DSA development post-LT (particularly class II anti-HLA-DQ) associated with chronic graft injury

## Management

Per [[aasld-ast-2025-liver-transplant-graft-complications]] (Recs 41+; full text in published article):

- Plasmapheresis or therapeutic plasma exchange — removes circulating DSA
- Intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG)
- Rituximab (anti-CD20) — depletes B-cell precursors of plasma cells
- Bortezomib — proteasome inhibitor targeting plasma cells; used for refractory AMR
- Augmentation of baseline immunosuppression
- Management remains largely consensus-based; evidence from retrospective series only

## Open Questions

- Optimal diagnostic criteria remain evolving (C4d has low specificity in liver vs. kidney)
- No RCT-level evidence for any AMR treatment in liver transplantation
- Long-term outcomes of chronic AMR with treatment not well established
