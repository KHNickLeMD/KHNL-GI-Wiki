## Overview

Routine, protocol-driven lab panels before endoscopy are **not indicated** in healthy patients. Most evidence is extrapolated from the surgical/anesthesia literature, where unsuspected abnormalities appear in only 0.2-1.0% of healthy patients and rarely change management. Testing ordered from clinical suspicion (history/exam) is abnormal up to ~30% of the time and more often alters care. The framework is **selective, risk-based testing** driven by patient and procedural risk factors ([[asge-2014-lab-testing-endoscopy]]).

## Core Principle — Selective, Risk-Based Testing

- Pursue preprocedure testing selectively based on medical history, physical exam, and patient + procedural risk factors.
- Recommend **against** routine coagulation studies, chest radiography, ECG, blood type/screen, hemoglobin/hematocrit, urinalysis, and chemistries in healthy patients.
- An abnormal PTT does **not** reliably predict periprocedural bleeding.
- Mild renal impairment does not affect outcomes with moderate/deep [[endoscopy-sedation|sedation]] → renal testing not required on that basis alone.

## Test-by-Test Guidance

| Test | When to obtain (selective) |
|---|---|
| Coagulation (PT/INR, PTT, platelets) | Active bleeding; known/suspected bleeding disorder; anticoagulant use or prolonged antibiotics; prolonged biliary obstruction; history of abnormal bleeding; malnutrition; acquired coagulopathy |
| Hemoglobin / hematocrit | Preexisting anemia; ongoing bleeding; high risk of significant procedural blood loss |
| Blood type & screen | Transfusion considered likely (active bleeding or anemia) — *recommend* |
| Chemistry panel | Significant endocrine, renal, or hepatic dysfunction before using medications that may further impair function; blood glucose in insulin-requiring diabetes |
| Pregnancy test | Childbearing-age patients before endoscopy/fluoroscopy with uncertain pregnancy history (no prior hysterectomy/bilateral tubal ligation/≥1 y amenorrhea) |
| Chest radiograph | New respiratory symptoms or decompensated cardiac failure |
| ECG | Not routine; per cardiac risk assessment only |

Coagulation-test indications intersect with periprocedural antithrombotic management — see [[anticoagulation-gi-bleeding]] for hold intervals and reversal.
