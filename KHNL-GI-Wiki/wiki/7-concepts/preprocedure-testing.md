## Overview

- **Routine testing** = a set panel ordered for every patient undergoing a given procedure, **irrespective of the history and physical** ([[asge-2014-lab-testing-endoscopy|American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) 2014]]).
- Insufficient data on routine testing before endoscopy → evidence extrapolated from surgical series and nonsurgical interventions.
  - 2000-patient surgical study: only 40% of preoperative tests had a recognizable indication; less than 1% showed abnormalities that would have influenced perioperative management.
  - Tests ordered on clinical considerations: up to 30% abnormal, and may substantially change management.
- Before ordering, weigh: frequency of abnormal results in that population, test accuracy, procedure risk, **moderate [[endoscopy-sedation|sedation]] vs anesthesia**, and whether an abnormal result would change the decision to scope or periprocedural management/outcome.
- Cost: follow-up testing of minor abnormalities seldom improves care; falsely abnormal results can delay endoscopy and add risk.

## Core Principle — Selective, Risk-Based Testing

### Graded Recommendations (ASGE 2014)

Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) evidence quality: ⊕⊕⊕⊕ high · ⊕⊕⊕◯ moderate · ⊕⊕◯◯ low · ⊕◯◯◯ very low. "We recommend" = stronger; "we suggest" = weaker.

| # | Recommendation | Quality |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Recommend** pursuing preprocedure testing **selectively**, based on medical history, physical exam, and patient + procedural risk factors | ⊕⊕⊕⊕ |
| 2 | **Recommend against** routine coagulation studies, chest radiography, electrocardiography (ECG), blood typing or screening, hemoglobin or hematocrit, urinalysis, and chemistry tests before endoscopy **in healthy patients**; individualize to patient and procedural risk factors | ⊕⊕⊕⊕ |

- Recs 3–8 are test-specific → [[#Test-by-Test Guidance]].

## Test-by-Test Guidance

| Test | When to obtain (selective) | ASGE 2014 |
|---|---|---|
| Prothrombin time (PT) / international normalized ratio (INR) / partial thromboplastin time (PTT) | Active bleeding; known or clinically suspected bleeding disorder; medication risk (e.g., anticoagulant use, **prolonged antibiotics**); prolonged biliary obstruction; history of abnormal bleeding; [[nutrition-in-hospitalized-patients\|malnutrition]]; other conditions associated with acquired coagulopathy | Rec 4 — **suggest** (⊕⊕◯◯) |
| Platelet count | Only if thrombocytopenia is suspected on history/exam — excessive bleeding or easy bruisability, myeloproliferative disorder, platelet-lowering medications | Text, ungraded |
| Bleeding time / platelet-function tests | **Not routinely** — bleeding time does not predict hemorrhage; newer platelet-function tests not validated for perioperative bleeding risk; usefulness unclear in renal failure or on aspirin/other antiplatelets | Text, ungraded |
| Hemoglobin / hematocrit | Preexisting anemia, ongoing bleeding, or high risk of significant blood loss during the procedure | Rec 7 — **suggest** (⊕⊕⊕◯) |
| | Also consider: risk factors for bleeding, high risk of adverse events with significant bleeding, [[cirrhosis\|advanced liver disease]], or a hematologic disorder — **when the procedure carries a high risk of bleeding adverse events** | Text, ungraded |
| Blood type & screen | Transfusion considered likely, in patients with active bleeding or anemia. Not routine before elective endoscopy | Rec 6 — **recommend** (⊕⊕⊕⊕) |
| | Typing, screening, and cross-matching: consider for endoscopy to evaluate/manage **acute gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding** ([[upper-gi-bleeding]], [[acute-lower-gi-bleeding]]) | Text, ungraded |
| Chemistry panel | Significant endocrine, renal, or hepatic dysfunction, **before using medications that may further impair function** | Rec 8 — **suggest** (⊕⊕◯◯) |
| | Blood glucose may be warranted in known **insulin-requiring diabetes** | Text, ungraded |
| Pregnancy test | Before endoscopy and fluoroscopy use in female patients of childbearing age with an uncertain pregnancy history or a history suggestive of current pregnancy — *unless* previous total hysterectomy, bilateral tubal ligation, or absent menses for 1 year (menopause). **Lower the threshold when fluoroscopy is planned** | Rec 3 — **suggest** (⊕⊕◯◯) |
| Chest radiograph | New respiratory signs/symptoms or decompensated heart failure | Rec 5 — **suggest** (⊕⊕◯◯) |
| ECG | **Not routine**; no consensus on a minimum age, and age alone is a poor indicator. **Exception: droperidol considered for sedation** — prolongs the QT interval; contraindicated with a prolonged QT interval (see [[endoscopy-sedation]]) | Rec 2 (against routine); exception in text |
| Urinalysis | **Not routine before endoscopy** — abnormal results rarely affect care; no data that urinary tract infections affect endoscopic outcome | Rec 2 (against routine) |

- Pregnancy is not a contraindication to endoscopy or moderate sedation, but its status may change fluoroscopy use and choice of sedation agents.

### Coagulation Testing

- In patients without a bleeding disorder or coagulopathy, **PT, INR, and PTT neither predict nor correlate with intraoperative or postoperative hemorrhage**; when bleeding does occur, it is typically in patients with *normal* parameters.

> [!pearl] The history finds the bleeders, not the PTT
> An abnormal PTT does not reliably predict perioperative hemorrhage; in a 1000-patient study, every patient with a prolonged PTT already had clinical risk factors for bleeding ([[asge-2014-lab-testing-endoscopy]]).

- Children undergoing endoscopy: abnormal PT and/or PTT in 16.8%; did not predict bleeding episodes.
- Screening PTT for hemophilia is **not** recommended without clinical suspicion — the PTT is insensitive for hemophilia, false-positive rate **~2.3%**, and the incidence of hemophilia in males without family history or prior major trauma/surgery is **0.0025%**.
- **PT/INR do not predict bleeding risk in liver disease** — they measure procoagulants only, not the anticoagulants that fall in parallel; see [[cirrhosis-hemostasis]].
- ASGE explicitly does **not** define acceptable INR or platelet thresholds for endoscopy — this document selects *who gets tested*, not how to act on the result. Hold intervals, bridging, and reversal live on [[anticoagulation-gi-bleeding]].

### Why Routine Panels Fail

Prevalence of abnormal results vs how often care actually changes (mostly preoperative surgical series):

| Test | Abnormal | Changes care |
|---|---|---|
| PT (no clinical suspicion) | <1% | Does not predict bleeding |
| PTT | ~6.5% (up to 16.3%) | Does not predict bleeding |
| Thrombocytopenia | <1% | ≤0.3% of surgical patients |
| Chest radiograph | 10% (1.3% unexpected) | 0.1% |
| ECG | ~30% | Lack of influence on care |
| Urinalysis | ~19% (up to 39%) | Rarely |
| Chemistry (routine preoperative screening) | 0.2–1.0% unsuspected | No evidence of altered anesthetic/surgical treatment or adverse outcome |

- **Hemoglobin:** severe anemia in less than 1% of asymptomatic patients; mild decreases are relatively common.
  - Baseline hemoglobin predicts transfusion need in surgery with significant blood loss; a hemoglobin "less than 8 mg/dL" (unit as printed in ASGE 2014) has been associated with cardiac morbidity and operative death.
  - Surgery: baseline hemoglobin/hematocrit recommended before major surgery with anticipated significant blood loss, not for minor surgery without clinical findings of anemia.
- **Renal function:** suggested in surgical patients older than 40 y to adjust perioperative drug doses; creatinine **>1.9 mg/dL** correlates with poor outcome after *major surgery* — **no data support this in endoscopy**; mild renal impairment appears to have no bearing on outcome with moderate and deep sedation.
