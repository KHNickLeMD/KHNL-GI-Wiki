## Case Overview
- Patient with chronic pouchitis following IPAA surgery
- History of pouch stricturing and current ileitis
- Patient has repeatedly responded to antibiotics but requires ongoing treatment
- Previous exposure to anti-TNF therapy (Remicade) - appears to have been primary non-responder

## Diagnostic Considerations
- Patient classified as having antibiotic-dependent pouchitis due to repeated response to antibiotics followed by relapse
- Differential diagnosis includes Crohn's disease of the pouch
- Stricturing identified as most concerning feature requiring escalation of therapy

## Clinical Evidence
- Dupilumab trial data: 31% remission rate at week 14 in chronic pouchitis patients
- Guidelines support returning to therapies used before colectomy

## Treatment Options
- **IL-23 inhibitors (Skyrizi)**: Preferred option based on mechanism superiority
  - IL-23 mechanism more effective than IL-12/23 mechanism
  - Allows for therapeutic drug monitoring and dose titration
- **IL-12/23 inhibitor (Stelara)**: Reasonable alternative if insurance barriers exist
  - May be easier to obtain approval
- **Antibiotics**: Should remain available as therapeutic option even alongside biologic therapy
- **Anti-TNF therapy**: Could be reconsidered if previous exposure was not truly primary non-response

## Treatment Plan
- Primary approach: Request Skyrizi approval from pharmacy
- Backup plan: Pursue Stelara if Skyrizi denied
- Maintain antibiotics as adjunctive therapy option for future flares

## Notes
- Analogy: having an IPAA is like having a Maserati - easier lifestyle but hard complications
- Antibiotic responsive -> Antibiotic dependent -> Antibiotic refractory: consider steroids and immune therapy
- Skyrizi/Tremfya > Stelara (IL-23 > IL-12/23)
