## Summary

### Epidemiology and Trends
- ALD is projected to become the single most common cause of cirrhosis globally, accounting for >50% of cases worldwide
- ALKEP (alcoholic hepatitis) accounts for 1% of total hospital admissions in the US, representing a 26% increase over 8 years
- In LA County: liver disease accounts for >75% of all alcohol-related deaths; ALD-related mortality highest among Hispanics
- COVID-19 pandemic impact: 34% increase in national alcohol sales in 2020; alcohol now accounts for 43% of all liver transplants nationally

### Pathophysiology of ALD
- Disease progression: 90-95% of chronic alcohol users develop steatosis -> 20-40% develop fibrosis -> 8-20% progress to cirrhosis
- Dose-dependent risk: Each additional unit of alcohol per day increases steatosis risk by 17%
- Risk factors: Older age, female sex, metabolic comorbidities, smoking, concurrent liver disease, genetic polymorphisms (especially PNPLA3)

### Mechanisms of Liver Injury
1. Alcohol metabolism: Ethanol -> acetaldehyde -> acetate, each step generating free oxygen radicals
2. Central lobular hypoxia: Alcohol metabolism creates "oxygen debt" around central vein
3. Inflammatory cascade: Alcohol increases intestinal permeability -> endotoxins -> Kupffer cell activation -> cytokine release

### Diagnosis of ALKEP
- AUDIT-C screening: 3 quick questions, score >=3 identifies at-risk behavior
- NIAAA clinical criteria: Jaundice onset within 8 weeks, ongoing alcohol consumption (40g/day women, 60g/day men) for >=6 months, <60 days abstinence before jaundice, AST >50, AST:ALT ratio >1.5, both <400, bilirubin >3
- Confounding factors requiring biopsy: ANA >1:160 or anti-smooth muscle antibody >1:80, sepsis/shock, hepatotoxic drug use within 30 days

### Treatment of ALKEP
- **Corticosteroids (prednisolone)**: STOPAH trial showed improved 28-day but not 90-day or 1-year survival
- **NAC**: IV NAC + steroids improved 1-month mortality and reduced infections vs steroids alone
- **Nutrition**: Target 1.5g/kg protein and 35-40 kcal/kg/day; higher caloric intake associated with improved 6-month survival
- **Key principle**: Only abstinence from alcohol consistently predicts long-term survival

### Treatment Algorithm
1. Establish ALKEP diagnosis per NIAAA criteria
2. Assess severity: Maddrey >=32 OR MELD >=20
3. Rule out obstructive jaundice and perform infectious workup
4. Start IV NAC on day 1
5. Start prednisolone after infection ruled out (day 3+)
6. Calculate Lille score at day 7: <0.45 continue steroids 28 days; >0.45 stop and refer for early transplant

### Early Liver Transplantation
- 6-month abstinence rule is outdated and arbitrary (from 1983)
- Accelerate consortium outcomes (147 patients): 1-year survival 94%, 5-year survival 83%
- SALT score predicts sustained alcohol use post-transplant (4 variables, cutoff of 5 has 76% accuracy)
- ALD now accounts for 48% of waitlist additions (2020), surpassing MASH and hepatitis C combined

### Q&A Clinical Pearls
- WHO states no safe level of alcohol; previous cardiovascular benefit data debunked
- Naltrexone: FDA removed black box warning; safe even in decompensated patients; 50mg daily
- NAC can be used independently without steroids
- MetALD approach: Treat same as ALD but also address metabolic risk factors
