## Summary

### Structured Approach to Diarrhea Diagnosis
- "Buckets of Diarrhea" Framework presented for organizing differential diagnoses

### Acute vs. Chronic Diarrhea
- GI approach: <4 weeks = acute, >4 weeks = chronic
- Key principle: Overwhelming majority of acute diarrhea is infectious (often viral, can be bacterial)
- Overwhelming minority of chronic diarrhea is infectious
- Exceptions: Immunocompromised patients can have prolonged infections (e.g., CMV for years)

### Infectious vs. Non-Infectious
- Infectious categories: bacterial, viral, parasitic
- For chronic diarrhea, always send stool studies for infectious workup
- Not necessarily required for acute diarrhea

### Inflammatory vs. Non-Inflammatory
- IBD is an example of inflammatory diarrhea; IBS is non-inflammatory
- Bloody diarrhea typically indicates inflammatory process
- C. difficile causes inflammation

### Osmotic vs. Secretory
- Lactose intolerance is classic osmotic diarrhea (probably #1 cause)
- Cholera is example of secretory diarrhea - affects chloride channels causing constitutive secretion
- Testing approach: 24-hour fast - if diarrhea stops, likely osmotic; if continues, likely secretory

### Metabolic vs. Functional
- Metabolic examples: hyperthyroidism, uncontrolled diabetes (A1C of 14), pheochromocytoma

### Key History Questions
- Frequency of bowel movements and stool consistency
- Presence of blood (inflammatory marker)
- Nocturnal diarrhea (suggests inflammatory or infectious process - "when you're asleep, your colon should be asleep")
- Oily stools (malabsorption)
- Association with meals or specific foods

### Teaching Case Example
- ~50% of African Americans and ~50% of Asians are lactose intolerant
- Diagnosis approach: chronic, non-infectious, non-inflammatory, osmotic pattern

### Patient Case: Aspiration Pneumonia Concern
- Important note: G-tube does not reduce risk of aspiration pneumonia
- Microscopic colitis being considered - one of three primary inflammatory bowel diseases (along with ulcerative colitis and Crohn's)
- Microscopic colitis appears endoscopically normal but shows inflammation on biopsy
