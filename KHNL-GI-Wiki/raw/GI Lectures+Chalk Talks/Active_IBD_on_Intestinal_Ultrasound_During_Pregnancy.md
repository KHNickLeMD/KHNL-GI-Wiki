# Active Inflammatory Bowel Disease on Intestinal Ultrasound During Pregnancy Is Associated With an Increased Risk of Adverse Pregnancy and Neonatal Outcomes Independent of Clinical and Biochemical Disease Activity

**Type:** Manuscript
**Date:** 2025-10-15
**URL:** https://www.gastrojournal.org/article/S0016-5085(25)00537-2/fulltext
**Source:** https://www.notion.so/a872e8dc42354877803e1b2bf09e5302

## Summary

### Journal Club by Olivia Jordan

#### Study Overview
- Multi-center prospective observational cohort study at three sites (two in Melbourne, Australia, one at Mount Sinai, NYC)
- Included pregnant women or those planning pregnancy with IBD
- Required objective disease activity measurement (intestinal ultrasound or fecal calprotectin) in 1st trimester, 2nd trimester, or both
- Data collected at five timepoints: 6 months pre-conception, all trimesters, and 6 weeks postpartum
- 377 participants included with 220 having acceptable quality ultrasounds

#### Assessment Methods Used
- Clinical assessments: Physician Global Assessment, Harvey Bradshaw Index (Crohn's), SCCAI (ulcerative colitis)
- Biochemical: Fecal calprotectin (FCP)
- Imaging: Intestinal ultrasound (IUS)
- Active disease defined as:
  - Clinical scores: PGA active, HBI >5, SCCAI >3
  - Biochemical: FCP >100 or >250
  - Ultrasound: Bowel wall thickness >6mm

#### Key Findings
- Intestinal ultrasound findings, particularly bowel wall thickness >6mm, associated with adverse outcomes:
  - 4x higher risk of preterm delivery with bowel thickness >6mm in second trimester
  - Increased risk of low birth weight
  - Higher rates of NICU admission
  - Increased risk of gestational diabetes and preeclampsia
- Discordance between assessment methods:
  - 14-16% of patients had active disease on ultrasound but normal fecal calprotectin
  - Most of these had Crohn's disease with ileal involvement
- Clinical scoring systems often inaccurate during pregnancy

#### Study Strengths and Limitations
- Strengths:
  - Prospective design with large cohort
  - Multiple assessment methods used
  - Patients managed by experienced gastroenterologists
- Limitations:
  - Variable protocol adherence (only ~50% assessed with all three methods)
  - Few participants with severe disease
  - BMI not collected
  - Mostly Australian participants, may not be generalizable to US population

#### Implications for Practice
- Intestinal ultrasound is a promising non-invasive tool for monitoring IBD in pregnancy
- Multiple assessment methods recommended over relying on a single method
- Particularly useful for monitoring ileal Crohn's where fecal calprotectin may be less reliable
- Ultrasound can visualize terminal ileum until late second trimester

### Abstract

#### Background and Aims
- Clinically active IBD is associated with an increased risk of adverse pregnancy outcomes
- The validity of clinical scores to assess antenatal disease activity is questionable
- Study aimed to assess whether active disease defined by intestinal ultrasound may predict adverse pregnancy outcomes

#### Methods
- International prospective cohort study recruiting pregnant individuals with IBD from 2017 to 2023 from 3 specialist IBD pregnancy services
- Participants underwent clinical assessments and fecal calprotectin testing in the first, second, and third trimesters, and 6 weeks postpartum
- IUS was performed in T1 or T2 when referral timing allowed

#### Results
- 377 participants recruited, 198 with Crohn's disease, 234 women underwent IUS during pregnancy
- Maximal bowel wall thickness >6 mm in T2 was associated with 4-fold increased risk of prematurity (RR, 4.01; 95% CI, 1.26-12.72; P = .018) and 2-fold increased risk of low-birth-weight delivery (RR, 2.19; 95% CI, 1.01-4.72; P = .046)
- Hyperemia on IUS in T2 was associated with 3-fold increase in preeclampsia risk (RR, 3.46; 95% CI, 1.03-11.12; P = .046)
- Agreement between clinical and IUS/FCP activity during pregnancy was weak, particularly for Crohn's disease

#### Conclusions
- Active IBD on IUS in pregnancy is associated with an increased risk of adverse pregnancy outcomes, independent of clinical activity and FCP
- Use of IUS monitoring antenatally should be considered to guide therapeutic decision-making
