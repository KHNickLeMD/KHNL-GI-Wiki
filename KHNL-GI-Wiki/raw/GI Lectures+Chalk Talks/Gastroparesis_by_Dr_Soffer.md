## Pathophysiology and Anatomy
- Stomach: fundus (relaxation, accommodation) and distal stomach/antrum (pacemaker cells - ICCs)
- Food liquidated in antrum through propulsion and retropulsion at closed pylorus
- Liquid phase empties at 2-3 cc/min controlled by duodenal feedback
- In gastroparesis: impaired vagal function, loss of nitric oxide, reduced ICC activity

## Epidemiology and Etiology
- Diabetes most common, then idiopathic, drug-induced, other causes, post-surgical
- Gastric emptying can fluctuate over time

## Clinical Presentation
- Nausea and vomiting dominant, along with early satiety, fullness, epigastric pain
- Pain is common and may require narcotics
- Obesity can coexist with gastroparesis
- Delayed gastric emptying can occur without symptoms and vice versa
- Disease does not impact survival itself

## Differential Diagnosis
- Determine if GI or non-GI cause (CNS, paraneoplastic, drug-related, pregnancy, eating disorders)
- Exclude mechanical obstruction
- Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome: important emerging consideration, especially young women; assess Beighton score

## Gastric Emptying Testing
- Scintigraphy is gold standard; 4-hour value most sensitive
- Stop prokinetics before testing; keep blood glucose <275 mg/dL
- 1-hour value important to assess for rapid gastric emptying

## Treatment

### Dietary
- Small frequent meals, low fat, mechanically soft to pureed to liquid, low residue

### Prokinetics
- **Metoclopramide**: only FDA-approved drug; 15-30 mg/day divided doses, liquid form preferred
  - Drug holiday strategy: 3 weeks on, few weeks off
- **Domperidone**: no longer available in US
- **Erythromycin**: very effective IV, loses effect orally due to tachyphylaxis
- **Prucalopride**: not truly gastric prokinetic
- **Pyridostigmine**: cholinesterase inhibitor, reasonable when other options fail

### Antiemetics
- 5-HT3 antagonists (ondansetron) have better safety profile
- Prochlorperazine, promethazine: cheap and effective but cause CNS depression
- Cannabinoids: CBD helps stomach; risk of cannabinoid hyperemesis with long-term daily use

### Pain Management
- Tricyclics better studied than gabapentin
- Opiates: start with tramadol; refer to pain clinic for chronic use

### Neuromodulators
- Nortriptyline: some improvement in early satiety; low dose at bedtime (10-25-50 mg)
