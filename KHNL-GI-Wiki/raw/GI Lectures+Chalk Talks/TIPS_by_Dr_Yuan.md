## Summary

### Indications for TIPS
- Variceal hemorrhage is the primary indication for TIPS (esophageal, gastric, rectal, or stoma varices)
- Refractory ascites is a secondary indication, defined as ascites requiring paracentesis once or twice monthly despite maximal diuretic therapy
- Portal vein thrombosis is not an indication for TIPS
- Varices are primarily pressure-related, making TIPS effective for hemorrhage by rapidly decompressing pressure
- Ascites is more complex than just portal hypertension, involving hepatic function, osmotic pressure, and vasodilation

### Patient Selection Criteria
- MELD score should be below 15; lower scores indicate better liver reserve
- Child-Pugh scores: best candidates are Child-Pugh A or B; Child-Pugh C patients are poor candidates
- CT imaging is needed to evaluate anatomy, blood flow, and potential challenges
- Contraindications include PSC, biliary issues, polycystic liver, HCC, or cholangiocarcinoma
- Echocardiogram is required to rule out heart failure or pulmonary hypertension
- Advanced age is a contraindication
- Evaluation should include sarcopenia assessment, albumin levels, and liver size
- Transplant eligibility should be considered before TIPS placement

### Physiological Effects of TIPS
- TIPS increases blood return to the right heart by approximately 30%
- The liver's blood supply is significantly altered after TIPS:
  - Portal vein normally supplies ~70% of blood to liver
  - Hepatic artery supplies ~30%
- TIPS can create liver hypoxia by shunting blood away from the liver
- Patients with better liver reserve (less sarcopenia, better albumin, larger liver) may tolerate these changes better

### Complications After TIPS
- Hepatic encephalopathy (HE) is a major complication with severe quality of life impact
  - Prevents driving and independence
  - Requires constant supervision and care
  - Limited effective treatments available
  - Not factored into MELD score for transplant prioritization
- Pulmonary hypertension can develop due to increased blood flow to heart
- Liver decompensation can worsen after TIPS
- Mechanical hemolysis can occur from the shunt

### Management Considerations
- Regular echocardiograms are recommended after TIPS to monitor right heart function
- Diuretics are often needed after TIPS due to increased volume load
- Limited effective treatments for HE (lactulose, rifaximin, zinc, L-ornithine)
- TIPS should be carefully considered as a permanent intervention with significant consequences
- Even when TIPS is closed, damage to liver may be irreversible
