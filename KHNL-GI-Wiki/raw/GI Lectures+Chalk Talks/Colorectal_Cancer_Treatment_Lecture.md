## Early-Stage Colon Cancer Treatment
- Stage 2 and 3 colon cancer patients typically receive adjuvant chemotherapy
- Standard regimen is FOLFOX (fluorouracil, leucovorin, oxaliplatin)
- Most phase 2 patients are offered chemotherapy if high-risk features are present, including obstruction, perforation, vascular invasion, or poor differentiation
- For stage 3 patients: T4 or N2 disease requires six months of chemotherapy
- Non-T4/N2 disease may require shorter duration of treatment

## Circulating Tumor DNA (ctDNA) Surveillance
- ctDNA-positive patients after surgery have worse outcomes
- Patients with negative ctDNA at 60 days who did not receive chemotherapy had good outcomes
- ctDNA-positive status clearly indicates worse prognosis
- The role of chemotherapy in ctDNA-positive patients needs further study

## MSI-High Colorectal Cancer
- MSI-high patients are now treated with immunotherapy rather than chemotherapy
- Patients achieving complete clinical response can have organ preservation
- Dual checkpoint inhibition (nivolumab plus CTLA-4 inhibitor) showed 71% response rate
- Superior progression-free survival and overall survival compared to chemotherapy
- Single-agent checkpoint inhibitor is an option if dual therapy not tolerated

## Rectal Cancer Treatment Approaches
- Total neoadjuvant therapy (TNT) has become standard approach - all chemotherapy and radiation given before surgery
- Traditional approach was neoadjuvant chemoradiation followed by surgery, then adjuvant chemotherapy, but many patients unable to complete adjuvant treatment due to surgical morbidity
- Current standard: induction chemoradiation followed by FOLFOX, leading to higher pathologic complete response rates and improved organ preservation
- Short course radiation (5 fractions) followed by chemotherapy and surgery is an alternative approach
- Patients achieving complete response can opt for organ preservation with close surveillance rather than surgery

## Organ Preservation Protocol
- Patients evaluated every 3 months with digital rectal exam for first year
- MRI surveillance for up to 3 years
- Complete clinical response required for organ preservation pathway
- Risk of second primary tumors in patients who receive pelvic radiation

## Treatment for T3/Node-Positive Rectal Cancer
- Standard approach: total neoadjuvant therapy with upfront tumor radiation followed by FOLFOX, then surgical resection
- For patients very close to anal verge, sphincter preservation is extremely important consideration
- Non-MSI-high patients not interested in organ preservation can receive upfront chemotherapy

## Metastatic Colorectal Cancer
- 20-50% of patients with metastatic disease are potentially curable, even in stage 4 setting
- Key question at every timepoint: are patients resectable at presentation or can they be converted to resectable status
- Tumor sidedness (left vs. right) is critical for treatment decisions
- Right-sided tumors have different prognosis and treatment approach

## Chemotherapy Regimens
- Backbone regimens: FOLFOX and FOLFIRI
- FOLFOXIRI (all chemotherapy together) used for high burden disease
- Bevacizumab (Avastin) used upfront for right-sided tumors
- Cetuximab for RAS wild-type, left-sided tumors started upfront

## BRAF Mutation
- Patients with BRAF mutation have very poor prognosis - median overall survival only 15 months with standard chemotherapy (compared to 30+ months for other patients)
- Most BRAF mutation patients didn't make it to second-line therapy
- Encorafenib (BRAF inhibitor) plus cetuximab and chemotherapy showed longest median overall survival for BRAF patients at 30 months
- This has changed standard of care and moved BRAF-targeted therapy to frontline setting

## Tumor Profiling - Critical for Treatment Decisions
- Tumor profiling should happen at time of biopsy, not weeks later when patient sees oncologist
- Critical markers for colorectal cancer: MSI (via immunohistochemistry), RAS mutations, BRAF mutation, and HER2 (via immunohistochemistry)
- MSI testing is now reflex testing by pathology
- Next generation sequencing (NGS) needed for all patients
- All testing can be done in-house
- Important for GI or surgery to establish ordering pathway with oncology for expedited results

## HER2-Positive Disease
- Trastuzumab and tucatinib show significant responses in second-line setting

## Third-Line Treatment
- Response rates less than 5% in third-line setting
- Survival benefit only 1-2 months
- Best chance of treating patients is upfront - emphasizes importance of early tumor profiling

## Surveillance After Treatment
- First 2 years: every 3 months visits
- Years 3-5: every 6 months visits
- CEA monitoring is only marker shown to change outcome
- CT scans yearly, but for high-risk/node-positive patients, every 6-12 months for first 3 years
- Colonoscopy at 1 year, then every 3-5 years
- Higher incidence of second primary tumors in colorectal cancer survivors, so maintain primary care screening
- Patients followed for 5 years then discharged to primary care

## Questions and Answers
**Immunotherapy toxicity management:**
- Grade 4 (life-threatening) toxicity: do not re-challenge
- Grade 3 toxicity: can re-challenge after resolution with steroids/tocilizumab treatment
- If repeat toxicity occurs after re-challenge, do not continue immunotherapy
- Given benefit in MSI-high setting, team is aggressive about getting immunotherapy to patients

**Immunotherapy access at County:**
- Immunotherapy widely available and approved through formulary exception process
- Many IO infusions now given at home or offsite clinics rather than infusion center due to good tolerability
