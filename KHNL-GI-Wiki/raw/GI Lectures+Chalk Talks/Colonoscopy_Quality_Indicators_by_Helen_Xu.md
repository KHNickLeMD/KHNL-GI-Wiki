## Summary

### Why Quality Indicators Matter
- Colonoscopy efficacy depends heavily on operator factors and experience
- Poor polyp detection leads to increased risk of post-colonoscopy colorectal cancer
- Colorectal cancer incidence and mortality are lower in patients who undergo screening
- Cost effectiveness and high-value care are essential considerations, especially at safety-net hospitals

### Bowel Preparation Quality
- **Target: 90% adequate prep**
- Adequate prep defined as Boston Bowel Prep Scale score >=2 in each of three colon segments, or descriptive documentation of "excellent," "good," or "adequate"
- Inadequate prep requires repeat colonoscopy within one year per USMSTF guidelines
- Inadequate prep increases costs by 12% at private hospitals and 22% at public hospitals
- Most commercial bowel prep options are non-inferior to high-volume PEG
- Lower volume preps have better tolerability and patient willingness to repeat
- **New 2025 USMSTF guidelines recommend split-dose or same-day prep over prior-day prep** due to improved bowel prep quality and patient tolerability
- Inpatient colonoscopies have poorer prep quality due to patient factors and comorbidities

### Cecal Intubation
- **Target: 95% cecal intubation rate** with photo documentation
- Terminal ileum intubation is not mentioned in guidelines
- May be removed as a quality indicator in the future as most endoscopists already achieve this

### Polyp Detection
- **Adenoma Detection Rate (ADR)** is the most important quality indicator
- Calculated as procedures with >=1 adenoma removed and confirmed on pathology, divided by all colonoscopies
- **2024 targets (increased from 2015):** Overall 35%, Female 30%, Male 40%
- Denominator now includes screening, diagnostic, and surveillance colonoscopies (expanded from 2015 guidelines which only included screening)
- Males have approximately 2x more polyps than females, likely related to testosterone
- **ADR for follow-up of abnormal stool/blood tests: 50%**
- Additional metrics: Adenomas per colonoscopy (APC) to discourage stopping at one polyp, and sessile serrated lesion detection rate (SSLVR)

### Surveillance Intervals
- **Target: 90% adherence** to appropriate surveillance intervals
- Based on 2020 USMSTF guidelines
- Some patients still receive colonoscopies too frequently compared to guideline recommendations

### Real-World Quality Improvement Applications from UCLA
- Created a real-time quality indicator dashboard tracking all colonoscopies in UCLA Health System with ADR, cecal intubation, and other metrics
- Developed a colonoscopy overuse e-measure to automatically identify inappropriate colonoscopies done too soon and provide feedback to providers
- Implemented best practice advisory pop-ups in EMR for patients with abnormal FIT tests to prompt PCPs to send GI referrals
- Created ADR report cards distributed every six months to individual endoscopists with anonymized comparisons and individual feedback from chief of endoscopy
- Future work will examine whether ADR reporting influences behavior and increases ADR long-term
- Other health systems have shown that ADR reporting leads to increased ADRs over time

### Systems-Level Changes to Improve Quality
- Adopt split-dose bowel prep for all patients
- Invest in improved scopes with computer-aided detection
- Implement education and training interventions

### Discussion: Barriers to Quality Programs
- Primary barriers are buy-in at all levels, resources, grants, personnel, and institutional support
- MIPS (Merit-Based Incentive Payment System) theoretically provides up to 10% penalties for programs without quality tracking, but implementation is limited, especially at safety-net organizations
- Data systems are critical: pathology databases must communicate with endoscopy databases to calculate ADR automatically
- At Keck, ProVation is used but cross-communication between pathology and endoscopy is not automated
- Free-text entry in databases makes data extraction challenging
- Natural language processing models may help calculate ADR across different health systems

### Action Items
- Consider replicating UCLA quality improvement projects at current institution
