## Summary

### Indications for Anti-Reflux Surgery
- Failed medical management of moderate to severe GERD
- GERD with severe extra-esophageal manifestations
- Intolerance or noncompliance with PPIs due to side effects
- High volume reflux from incompetent LES causing persistent reflux despite PPI therapy

### Poor Candidates for Surgery
- Patients with lack of response to PPIs (symptoms often not truly GERD-related)
- Functional heartburn patients with normal pH study and normal motility
- Morbidly obese patients due to high risk of fundoplication failure

### Preoperative Workup
- Detailed history of PPI response
- Barium esophagram for esophageal length, hiatal hernias, stricture
- Upper endoscopy for malignancy, esophagitis, Barrett's
- Manometry to diagnose scleroderma/achalasia and influence surgical approach
- Ambulatory pH testing (gold standard) for all non-erosive GERD patients

### Types of Anti-Reflux Surgeries
- **Nissen fundoplication**: Complete 360-degree wrap
- **Toupet fundoplication**: Posterior partial wrap at ~270 degrees
- **Dor fundoplication**: Anterior partial wrap at ~180 degrees, often with Heller myotomy
- **LINX Magnetic Sphincter Augmentation**: Flexible band of magnetic beads around LES

### Decision Making for Procedure Selection
- Morbid obesity: Consider Roux-en-Y
- Evidence of dysmotility: Partial fundoplication preferred
- Barrett's esophagus: Complete Nissen indicated
- Absence of severe reflux features: MSA/LINX or TIF may be considered

### Complications After Fundoplication
- Hernia classification (Types 1A, 1B, 2, 3)
- Dysphagia expected post-op; persisting >12 weeks requires evaluation
- Gas bloat syndrome: inability to belch due to fundoplication; treat with simethicone, lifestyle modifications
- Repeat surgeries have worse outcomes than primary procedures; 10% will have continued symptoms after revision
