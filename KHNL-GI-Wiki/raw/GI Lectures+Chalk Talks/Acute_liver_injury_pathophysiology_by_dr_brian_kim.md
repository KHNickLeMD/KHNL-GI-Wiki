## Summary

### Liver Enzyme Mechanisms
- Liver enzymes rise through different mechanisms: AST/ALT are released with immediate cell death or injury, while bilirubin accumulates when the liver can't excrete it properly
- Bilirubin is typically the last marker to rise and last to fall in liver injury
- Delta bilirubin binds to albumin, adopting albumin's 21-day half-life instead of bilirubin's normal half-life
- This binding explains why bilirubin can remain elevated for prolonged periods after initial injury, especially in Drug-Induced Liver Injury (DILI)

### Liver Perfusion Physiology
- Liver receives dual blood supply: portal vein (75% of blood flow but very low pressure) and hepatic artery (25% but high pressure)
- Venous return to the heart relies primarily on cardiac function creating a "straw effect" that draws blood back
- "Shock liver" occurs most commonly in cardiac ICU patients due to combination of poor cardiac output and venous congestion

### Patient's Diagnostic Considerations
- Case involves a 23-year-old with prolonged liver injury lasting about three weeks
- Differential diagnosis includes:
  - Hepatitis A (though IgM negative, concerning for alternate diagnosis)
  - Autoimmune hepatitis (patient has significantly elevated IgG at 4000-5000)
  - Drug-induced liver injury
  - Ischemic hepatopathy
- Team suspects autoimmune hepatitis as most likely diagnosis
- Liver biopsy ordered but might only show cell death without definitive diagnosis in acute severe injury

### Transplant Evaluation
- Patient has coagulopathy (INR 3.3) but hasn't developed encephalopathy yet
- If encephalopathy develops, patient would be classified as having acute liver failure
- King's College criteria discussed for transplant indications
- Concerning feature: prolonged jaundice before potential encephalopathy (>7 days) indicates poor prognosis in subfulminant liver failure
- Ongoing evaluation for transplant includes echocardiogram and cross-sectional imaging

### Management Plan
- Steroids initiated for presumed autoimmune hepatitis
- Stopped unnecessary antibiotics (zosyn) and medications without benefit (lactulose, rifaximin)
- IV vitamin K ordered to assess if coagulopathy improves, which would suggest some hepatocyte function
- More frequent monitoring ordered: INR, CMP, CBC, fibrinogen every 8 hours
