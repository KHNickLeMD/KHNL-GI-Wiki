## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

Wilson's disease is a rare autosomal recessive disorder of biliary copper excretion due to a defective **ATP7B** copper transporter protein. Prevalence ~1:30,000 individuals. Over 32 different mutations in the Wilson's gene have been identified; unlike hemochromatosis, there is no predominant mutation, so molecular genetic analysis may be pursued in cases of diagnostic uncertainty.

**Presentation:** Primarily hepatic, neurologic, and/or psychiatric manifestations. Hepatic presentation is variable — from asymptomatic transaminase elevation to acute liver failure (fulminant Wilson's disease, particularly in young women). Although most commonly diagnosed in young males, can present at any age.

**Screening indication:** All patients with persistently elevated AST and/or ALT levels, especially patients <55 years of age. [[acg-2017-liver-chemistries]]

**Initial screening test:** Serum ceruloplasmin
- If low ceruloplasmin → confirmatory testing required
- Ceruloplasmin can be falsely normal in inflammatory states (acute phase reactant)

**Confirmatory workup:**
- 24-hour urine copper collection
- Slit-lamp eye examination for Kayser-Fleischer rings (pathognomonic but not always present)
- Liver biopsy for quantitative hepatic copper (to confirm diagnosis, stage liver fibrosis, quantify copper overload to guide management)

**Molecular genetics:** ATP7B mutation analysis can be pursued when diagnosis remains uncertain.

## Differential Diagnosis

- [[autoimmune-hepatitis]] — can coexist; distinguish with copper studies and biopsy
- [[drug-induced-liver-injury]] — medication history, RUCAM; Wilson's biopsy/copper studies distinguish
- [[alcohol-associated-liver-disease]] — AST:ALT ratio; alcohol history; no copper overload
- [[acute-liver-failure]] — in fulminant Wilson's, rapid deterioration; Coombs-negative hemolytic anemia + ALF + low ceruloplasmin = characteristic triad
- [[hereditary-hemochromatosis]] — iron overload vs. copper overload; distinct genetics and biochemistry

## Therapeutics

*Stub — D-penicillamine (chelation, first-line historically), trientine (better tolerated), zinc acetate (maintenance/pediatric), low-copper diet, liver transplantation for fulminant Wilson's or decompensated cirrhosis.*

### Wilson's Disease in Pregnancy

- WD reduces fertility — copper deposition in uterus may interfere with embryo implantation leading to increased miscarriages and spontaneous abortions
- **Continue treatment during pregnancy** (ACG 2016 Rec 33 — strong, very low evidence) [[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]]
- Treatment discontinuation risks: hepatic decompensation/liver failure AND copper deposition in placenta/fetal liver, damaging the fetus
- **Dose reduction for penicillamine or trientine: reduce by 25–50%** if cesarean section planned (to promote wound healing); inadequate data to determine optimal regimen preference
- Zinc: can be used in pregnancy; fewer wound-healing concerns vs. chelators; data on conversion to zinc therapy during pregnancy reported in case reports
- Penicillamine and trientine concerns: fetal myelosuppression or embryopathy in case reports (penicillamine); balance against risk of under-treatment
- Multidisciplinary referral to maternal-fetal medicine and hepatology specialists recommended given complexity
- See [[liver-disease-in-pregnancy]] for full pregnancy context
