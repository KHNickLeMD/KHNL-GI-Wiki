## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

**MASLD (Metabolic dysfunction-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease)** — formerly NAFLD — is a highly prevalent condition associated with the metabolic syndrome, most commonly in patients with co-existing obesity, diabetes mellitus, dyslipidemia, and hypertension.

MASLD/MASH is a **diagnosis of exclusion** — a series of diagnostic labs are obtained to rule out alternative etiologies. Although many individuals with hepatic steatosis on imaging may have normal liver chemistries, the presence of abnormal liver chemistries in this setting increases the likelihood of MASH (with inflammation/fibrosis).

**Pattern of elevation:** Generally ALT>AST (unlike alcoholic liver disease); AST/ALT levels rarely above 300 IU/l; no unique elevation pattern. [[acg-2017-liver-chemistries]]

**Screening indication:** Patients with elevated BMI and features of metabolic syndrome (diabetes mellitus, overweight/obesity, hyperlipidemia, hypertension) with mild elevations of ALT should undergo screening for MASLD with ultrasound. (Strong recommendation, very low evidence) [[acg-2017-liver-chemistries]]

**Alcohol threshold exclusion (historical NAFLD definition):** Women >140 g/week; men >210 g/week → suggests alcoholic liver disease rather than NAFLD. Note: current MASLD criteria use a lower threshold (>14 drinks/week in men, >7 drinks/week in women as exclusion).

### Severity Assessment

*Stub — Simple steatosis vs. MASH (inflammation + ballooning ± fibrosis); NAS scoring system; METAVIR fibrosis staging; non-invasive fibrosis assessment: FIB-4, APRI, NFS, FibroScan (liver stiffness measurement), ELF score; advanced fibrosis (F3–F4) carries significantly elevated liver-related mortality.*

### Classification / Typing

- **Simple steatosis (MASL):** Steatosis without significant inflammation or fibrosis; low risk of progression
- **MASH (Metabolic dysfunction-Associated SteatoHepatitis):** Steatosis + lobular inflammation + hepatocyte ballooning ± fibrosis; risk of progression to cirrhosis and HCC
- **MASH-related cirrhosis:** End-stage; HCC risk; decompensation events

## Differential Diagnosis

- [[alcohol-associated-liver-disease]] — alcohol history; AST:ALT ratio ≥2:1; GGT elevated; can coexist with MASLD
- [[drug-induced-liver-injury]] — steatohepatitis pattern DILI (amiodarone, tamoxifen, methotrexate, corticosteroids)
- [[hereditary-hemochromatosis]] — elevated ferritin common in MASLD but TS usually normal; HFE testing
- [[wilson-disease]] — young patient; ceruloplasmin
- [[autoimmune-hepatitis]] — ANA/ASMA/IgG; biopsy distinguishes (plasma cell infiltrate, interface hepatitis)
- [[celiac-disease]] — modest transaminase elevation; TTG-IgA
- Thyroid disease — TSH; both hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism can cause elevated transaminases

## Diagnostics

*Stub — Ultrasound (steatosis: hyperechoic liver), CT (attenuation), MRI-PDFF (most accurate non-invasive for fat quantification), FibroScan (LSM + CAP), FIB-4 index, liver biopsy (required for MASH diagnosis and fibrosis staging; Brunt criteria; NAS scoring).*

See [[abnormal-liver-chemistries]] for integrated diagnostic algorithm.

## Therapeutics

*Stub — Weight loss ≥10% (most effective intervention); exercise; alcohol avoidance; management of metabolic comorbidities; resmetirom (FDA-approved 2024 for MASH F2–F3); GLP-1 agonists (semaglutide — emerging data, not yet FDA-approved specifically for MASH); vitamin E (for non-diabetic NASH — PIVENS trial); pioglitazone (for diabetic MASH); no established pharmacotherapy for simple steatosis; bariatric surgery for morbid obesity; liver transplantation for decompensated MASH cirrhosis (recurrence possible).*
