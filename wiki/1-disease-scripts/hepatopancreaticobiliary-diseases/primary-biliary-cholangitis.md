## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

Primary biliary cholangitis (PBC; formerly "primary biliary cirrhosis") is an uncommon chronic autoimmune cholestatic liver disease primarily affecting intralobular bile ducts at the microscopic level. Predominantly affects women (4:1 female predominance). Patients may present with fatigue and pruritus as primary complaints.

**Laboratory pattern:** Elevated alkaline phosphatase (cholestatic pattern); bilirubin may or may not be elevated. AST/ALT typically not markedly elevated.

**Serologic hallmark:** Positive **anti-mitochondrial antibody (AMA)** — present in >95% of patients. AMA positivity is the key diagnostic marker; liver biopsy is not routinely required when AMA is positive and the clinical/biochemical picture is consistent.

**Liver biopsy:** Not routinely required in AMA-positive patients. May be beneficial in AMA-negative PBC (to confirm diagnosis) and to stage liver fibrosis.

**Screening indication:** All patients with alkaline phosphatase elevation with or without elevation of bilirubin should undergo testing for PBC with anti-mitochondrial antibody. (Strong recommendation, very low evidence) [[acg-2017-liver-chemistries]]

### Severity Assessment

*Stub — GLOBE score, UK-PBC score for prognosis; Mayo risk score; biopsy staging (Ludwig stages I–IV); biochemical response to UDCA (Paris criteria); progression to cirrhosis.*

## Differential Diagnosis

- [[primary-sclerosing-cholangitis]] — cholangiographic findings; IgG4; IBD association; male predominance
- [[autoimmune-hepatitis]] — overlap syndrome (PBC-AIH overlap); ANA/SMA; IgG; biopsy features
- [[drug-induced-liver-injury]] — medication/HDS history; RUCAM
- [[abnormal-liver-chemistries]] — integrated algorithm for cholestatic pattern
- Secondary biliary cirrhosis — extrinsic biliary obstruction; imaging distinguishes
- Sarcoidosis — granulomatous hepatitis; systemic sarcoid findings; ACE level; AMA negative
- IgG4-related cholangiopathy — IgG4 level; can mimic PSC or PBC

---

## Diagnostics

*Stub — AMA (AMA-M2 most specific), ANA (anti-sp100 and anti-gp210 when AMA negative), IgM elevated, alkaline phosphatase and GGT elevated, liver biopsy for staging (florid duct lesion, granulomatous cholangitis, biliary-type fibrosis).*

---

## Therapeutics

*Stub — Ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) 13–15 mg/kg/day first-line (improves biochemistry and outcomes); obeticholic acid for inadequate UDCA response; bezafibrate (off-label, not FDA-approved); liver transplantation for decompensated PBC or refractory pruritus; symptom management (cholestyramine, rifampin for pruritus).*

### PBC in Pregnancy

- PBC typically presents at an older age (beyond typical childbearing years); pregnancy infrequent but possible
- **Continue UDCA during pregnancy** (ACG 2016 Rec 32 — strong, very low evidence) [[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]]
- UDCA is FDA pregnancy category B; potential risks of discontinuation (disease flare, hepatic decompensation) outweigh risks of UDCA therapy
- Limited data in first trimester; caution warranted with initiation in first trimester; growing safety evidence from use in intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (IHCP) supports safety
- PBC has been associated with disease flare after delivery; close monitoring postpartum recommended
- See [[liver-disease-in-pregnancy]] for full pregnancy context
