## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

PSC is a chronic cholestatic liver disease characterized by **multifocal stricturing and dilation ("beaded" appearance) of intra- and/or extrahepatic bile ducts** in the absence of secondary causes. Diagnosis = cholestatic LFT abnormalities + characteristic cholangiogram (MRCP preferred over ERCP, [[acg-2015-psc]]; Strong/Moderate) + exclusion of secondary sclerosing cholangitis.

- 60–70% of adults with PSC have IBD (mostly UC); ~5% of UC patients have PSC
- Male predominance (60–70%), age at dx 30–40 years
- Liver biopsy not required for typical large-duct PSC (Conditional/Low); required for **small-duct PSC** (~5%; normal cholangiogram + cholestatic LFTs + compatible biopsy) and suspected [[autoimmune-hepatitis]] overlap
- Initial labs: ALP elevation, GGT (in children — ALP unreliable), AST/ALT mild to moderate, bilirubin often normal early
- Antibodies: ANCA frequent but nonspecific; ANA/SMA may be present; AMA negative (rules in over PBC); IgG4 ≥1 ULN suggests IgG4-SC

### Severity Assessment

- **MELD** drives transplant priority; refer when MELD >14
- **Mayo Risk Score** integrates age, bilirubin, albumin, AST, variceal bleed history
- ALP normalization predicts better outcomes (Stanich 2011; Al Mamari 2013)
- Decompensation: ascites, variceal bleed, hepatic encephalopathy, recurrent cholangitis

### Classification / Typing

- **Large-duct PSC** (~95%) — classic cholangiographic disease
- **Small-duct PSC** (~5%) — normal MRCP/ERCP, biopsy-proven; better prognosis; may progress to large-duct
- **IgG4-associated sclerosing cholangitis** — distinct entity, steroid-responsive
- **PSC–AIH overlap** — younger patients (<25y), ALT >5× ULN; needs immunosuppression
- **Secondary sclerosing cholangitis** — surgery, ischemia, IgG4, AIDS cholangiopathy, intra-arterial floxuridine

## Differential Diagnosis

- [[biliary-stricture]] — broader diagnostic schema; PSC is one cause
- Secondary sclerosing cholangitis (post-surgical, ischemic, AIDS-related)
- [[cholangiocarcinoma]] — must be considered in every PSC patient with dominant stricture
- IgG4-associated cholangitis (steroid-responsive)
- Primary biliary cholangitis (AMA+, female, intrahepatic small-duct disease)
- [[autoimmune-hepatitis]] (when ALT predominant; consider overlap)
- Choledocholithiasis
- Histiocytosis X, recurrent bacterial cholangitis, papillary tumors

## Diagnostics

| Modality | Role |
|---|---|
| **MRCP** | Preferred non-invasive cholangiogram (Strong/Moderate, [[acg-2015-psc]]) |
| ERCP | Reserved for tissue sampling / therapy of dominant strictures; ~3–7% post-ERCP pancreatitis risk |
| Liver biopsy | Not required for typical PSC; **required** for small-duct PSC, AIH overlap; "onion-skin" fibrosis pathognomonic but rare |
| **IgG4** | Test once in all PSC patients (Conditional/Moderate) — exclude IgG4-SC |
| **AMA** | Test to exclude PBC (Conditional/Moderate) |
| CA 19-9 + cross-sectional imaging (US or MR) | **Annual** CCA surveillance (Conditional/VLow) every 6–12 months |
| Cholangioscopy ± biopsy / FISH | Adds yield for indeterminate strictures; FISH polysomy ↑ PPV ([[ercp]], [[acg-2023-biliary-strictures]]) |
| DEXA | At diagnosis, then every 2–4 years (Conditional/Moderate) |
| Fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E | Assess in advanced/jaundiced disease (Conditional/Moderate) |
| Colonoscopy with biopsies | **At PSC diagnosis** regardless of GI symptoms (Conditional/Moderate); annual chromoendoscopy thereafter if IBD present |

**Dominant stricture** = CBD <1.5 mm or hepatic duct <1.0 mm — present in 45–50% of PSC patients over time; high CCA risk; warrants ERCP sampling + dilation if symptomatic.

## Therapeutics

### Medical
- **No proven disease-modifying medical therapy**
- **UDCA >28 mg/kg/d** — Strong AGAINST (Strong/High); high-dose UDCA increased liver transplant, varices, death (Lindor 2009)
- Low-dose UDCA (13–15 mg/kg/d) — possibly beneficial for biochemistry / CRC chemoprevention in PSC-IBD; not formally recommended
- Oral vancomycin — promising (especially pediatric), no RCT yet
- AIH overlap → standard AIH immunosuppression (prednisone + azathioprine)
- IgG4-SC → corticosteroids (responsive, unlike classical PSC)

### Pruritus
- Skin emollients + antihistamines (Conditional/VLow)
- **Cholestyramine 4–16 g/d** first-line bile-acid sequestrant (Conditional/VLow)
- Second-line: rifampin 150–300 mg BID (monitor LFTs), naltrexone ≤50 mg/d, sertraline 75–100 mg/d, phenobarbital 90 mg qHS

### Endoscopic ([[ercp]])
- **ERCP with balloon dilation** for symptomatic dominant strictures with pruritus and/or cholangitis (Strong/Low)
- **Sample dominant strictures**: brush cytology + biopsy + **FISH** to exclude CCA (Strong/Low)
- **Antibiotic prophylaxis** for ERCP in PSC (Conditional/Low) — quinolones or cephalosporins 3–5 day course
- Routine stenting NOT required post-dilation; short-term stenting for severe strictures

### Surgical / Liver Transplant
- **Liver transplantation** for decompensated PSC over medical/surgical drainage (Strong/Moderate)
- Refer when **MELD >14** (Conditional/Moderate)
- 5-year post-LT survival 80–85%
- MELD exception points available for:
  - Recurrent bacterial cholangitis (>2 bacteremia or >1 sepsis)
  - **CCA <3 cm** without metastasis (institutional protocol; neoadjuvant chemoradiation + LT — Mayo protocol)
  - Intractable pruritus
- Recurrent PSC after LT ~20% at 5 years

### Cancer Surveillance
- **CCA**: annual US or MR + CA 19-9 every 6–12 months (Conditional/VLow)
- **Gallbladder polyps >8 mm → cholecystectomy** (Conditional/VLow) — high malignancy potential
- **CRC in PSC-IBD**: annual colonoscopy with chromoendoscopy from PSC diagnosis (Conditional/Moderate); 4–5× ↑ CRC risk
- Pancreatic cancer surveillance: not routinely recommended (insufficient evidence)

### Bone & Nutritional
- DEXA at dx, every 2–4 years; alendronate for osteoporosis
- Replace fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E) in advanced disease
- Screen for varices if platelets <150 (signs of advanced disease)

### Pediatric Considerations
- Often presents with autoimmune sclerosing cholangitis (AIH overlap)
- GGT (not ALP) for cholestasis (growth-related ALP elevation)
- Routine CCA surveillance not recommended in children
