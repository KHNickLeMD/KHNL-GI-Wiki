## Bibliographic Info
- **Authors:** Lacy BE, Pimentel M, Brenner DM, Chey WD, Keefer LA, Long MD, Moshiree B
- **Year:** 2020 (published Dec 2020; Am J Gastroenterol 2021)
- **Journal/Publisher:** Am J Gastroenterol 2021;116:17–44
- **Type:** Guideline (ACG; GRADE)

## Summary

First ACG clinical guideline for IBS using GRADE. 25 statements (9 diagnostic, 16 therapeutic). Endorses a **positive diagnostic strategy** using Rome IV criteria rather than diagnosis-of-exclusion (Strong/High for cost-effectiveness). Recommends **limited targeted testing**: serology for celiac in IBS-D (Strong/Moderate); fecal calprotectin/lactoferrin + CRP in IBS-D to exclude IBD (Strong/Moderate); **against** routine stool pathogens, colonoscopy <45y without alarms, IgE food allergy panels. Therapeutics emphasize **low-FODMAP trial, soluble fiber, TCAs, gut-directed psychotherapy, peppermint oil** across subtypes; **secretagogues (linaclotide/plecanatide/lubiprostone)** and **tegaserod** for IBS-C; **rifaximin, alosetron, eluxadoline** for IBS-D. Strong recommendations **against** PEG for IBS-C global symptoms (helps stool only), antispasmodics, FMT, and bile-acid sequestrants for IBS-D global symptoms.

## Key Findings / Claims

**Diagnosis (Strong recommendations)**
- Serologic celiac testing in IBS-D (Strong/Moderate; OR 4.48 for biopsy-proven CD)
- Fecal calprotectin or lactoferrin + CRP in IBS-D to exclude IBD (Strong/Moderate–VLow)
- **Positive diagnostic strategy** over exclusion (Strong/High for cost-effectiveness; Consensus for time-to-treatment)

**Diagnosis (Conditional / against)**
- Against routine stool pathogen testing (Conditional/Low)
- Against routine colonoscopy <45y without alarms (Conditional/Low)
- Against IgE food allergy panels unless reproducible food-specific symptoms (Consensus)
- ARM + BET for IBS-C/M with suspected pelvic floor dysfunction or refractory constipation (Consensus)

**IBS-C therapeutics**
- **Against** PEG for global IBS-C (Conditional/Low) — improves stool only, not pain/global
- **Chloride channel activator (lubiprostone)** for global IBS-C (Strong/Moderate)
- **GC-C agonists (linaclotide 290 µg, plecanatide 3/6 mg)** for global IBS-C (Strong/High)
- **Tegaserod 6 mg BID** in women <65 with ≤1 CV risk factor failing secretagogues (Strong/conditional/Low)

**IBS-D therapeutics**
- **Against** bile-acid sequestrants for global IBS-D (Conditional/VLow)
- **Rifaximin 550 mg TID × 14 days** for global IBS-D (Strong/Moderate); retreatment up to 2× allowed
- **Alosetron** for women with severe IBS-D failing conventional therapy (Conditional/Low) — REMS for ischemic colitis
- **Eluxadoline 100 mg BID** for IBS-D (Conditional/Moderate); contraindicated post-cholecystectomy (pancreatitis risk)

**Cross-subtype therapeutics**
- **Soluble fiber (psyllium)**, NOT insoluble (bran) (Strong/Moderate)
- **Against** antispasmodics for global symptoms (Conditional/Low)
- Peppermint oil for global symptoms (Conditional/Low)
- **Against** probiotics (Conditional/VLow)
- **Limited low-FODMAP trial** (Conditional/VLow)
- **TCAs** for global symptoms (Strong/Moderate; doses in Table 7: amitriptyline 50–100 mg, desipramine 25–100 mg, nortriptyline 25–75 mg)
- **Gut-directed psychotherapy** (CBT, hypnotherapy, mindfulness) (Conditional/VLow)
- **Strong AGAINST FMT** for IBS (Strong/VLow)

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- Rome IV based; Rome V (2026) modifies IBS criteria (frequency ≥3 days/month, discomfort re-added) — IBS-D rifaximin/alosetron evidence still strongest evidence base in field
- Tegaserod re-approval is fragile (CV concerns); access varies
- Eluxadoline–pancreatitis signal in post-cholecystectomy patients limits use
- IBS criteria differ slightly between this 2020 ACG (Rome IV) and Rome V — update planned in next ACG cycle
