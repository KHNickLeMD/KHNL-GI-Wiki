## Summary

Comprehensive multi-society consensus on optimal techniques for endoscopic removal of colorectal lesions. Covers lesion assessment (Paris classification, LST subtypes, optical diagnosis with NICE/Kudo/JNET), resection methods by lesion size and morphology, EMR technique (inject-and-cut, underwater EMR, cold snare EMR), ESD and hybrid ESD, endoscopic full-thickness resection (eFTR), lesion marking and tattoo, post-EMR surveillance, and quality standards. Central thesis: endoscopy is first-line for all benign colorectal lesions, replacing surgical referral.

## Complete Recommendations (Verbatim / Near-Verbatim)

### Lesion Assessment

**Recommendation 1 (Strong; high-quality evidence):** Endoscopists should document the location, size (in mm), and Paris classification morphology for all colorectal lesions ≥10 mm detected at colonoscopy.

**Recommendation 2 (Strong; moderate-quality evidence):** Endoscopists should photo-document all colorectal lesions ≥10 mm before removal; photo-documentation of the post-resection defect is suggested.

**Recommendation 3 (Strong; moderate-quality evidence):** NICE type 3 or Kudo type V (VI irregular or VN amorphous) pattern should be considered to indicate deep submucosal invasion (>1000 μm). These lesions should undergo cold biopsy at the area of suspected deep invasion, followed by tattoo placement (unless in or near the cecum), and referral for surgical resection.

**Recommendation 4 (Weak; low-quality evidence):** Endoscopists should have proficiency in NBI or electronic chromoendoscopy for optical diagnosis of colorectal lesions.

**Recommendation 5 (Weak; moderate-quality evidence):** The non-lifting sign (failure of lesion to lift with submucosal injection) should be considered a marker of submucosal invasion or submucosal fibrosis (e.g., from prior polypectomy or cautery, colitis).

### Resection by Lesion Size and Type

**Recommendation 6 (Strong; high-quality evidence):** Cold snare polypectomy (CSP) is the preferred resection method for diminutive colorectal polyps (≤5 mm).

**Recommendation 7 (Strong; moderate-quality evidence):** Cold forceps polypectomy is NOT recommended for polyps >2 mm because of high rates of incomplete resection (9–61%).

**Recommendation 8 (Strong; moderate-quality evidence):** Hot biopsy forceps should NOT be used for polypectomy of any colorectal polyp because of high rates of incomplete resection, inadequate specimens, and complications.

**Recommendation 9 (Strong; moderate-quality evidence):** Cold snare polypectomy is preferred for small (6–9 mm) colorectal polyps; it achieves complete resection rates comparable to hot snare with lower rates of delayed post-polypectomy bleeding.

**Recommendation 10 (Weak; low-quality evidence):** For non-pedunculated polyps 10–19 mm, cold or hot snare resection ± submucosal injection is acceptable. EMR is preferred for non-polypoid lesions (Paris 0-IIa, 0-IIb, 0-IIc), serrated lesions (SSPs, TSA), and LSTs.

**Recommendation 11 (Strong; moderate-quality evidence):** For non-pedunculated lesions ≥20 mm, endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR) performed by an experienced endoscopist is the preferred treatment and should be considered before surgical referral.

**Recommendation 12 (Weak; low-quality evidence):** For non-pedunculated lesions ≥20 mm, all visible tissue should be removed in a single session in the minimum number of pieces. Prior failed resection attempts are associated with higher recurrence rates; refer to an experienced endoscopist rather than surgery.

**Recommendation 13 (Strong; moderate-quality evidence):** For pedunculated polyps with a head ≥10 mm, hot snare resection is preferred.

**Recommendation 14 (Weak; moderate-quality evidence):** Prophylactic mechanical ligation (detachable endoloop or clips) of the stalk is suggested before hot snare resection of pedunculated polyps with a head ≥20 mm or a stalk ≥5 mm to reduce immediate and delayed post-polypectomy bleeding.

**Recommendation 15 (Strong; low-quality evidence):** Large pedunculated specimens should not be divided to facilitate retrieval; the head should be retrieved intact.

### EMR Technique

**Recommendation 16 (Strong; moderate-quality evidence):** For inject-and-cut EMR of lesions ≥20 mm, a viscous submucosal injectant (e.g., hydroxyethyl starch, Eleview, ORISE Gel) is preferred over normal saline because it provides longer-lasting lift, results in fewer pieces, and reduces procedure time.

**Recommendation 17 (Weak; low-quality evidence):** Indigo carmine or methylene blue should be added to the injectant for tissue plane visualization.

**Recommendation 18 (Strong; high-quality evidence):** Tattoo (carbon particle suspension) should NOT be used as a submucosal injectant — it causes submucosal fibrosis and reduces future resection success.

**Recommendation 19 (Strong; moderate-quality evidence):** Adjuvant thermal ablation of EMR resection margins (APC or snare-tip soft coagulation) after complete visual resection reduces the rate of adenoma recurrence.

**Recommendation 20 (Strong; moderate-quality evidence):** Visible residual tissue should NOT be ablated — doing so increases recurrence risk. Residual tissue must be resected.

**Recommendation 21 (Weak; moderate-quality evidence):** Prophylactic clip closure of EMR defects ≥20 mm in the right colon is suggested when feasible to reduce delayed perforation.

**Recommendation 22 (Weak; low-quality evidence):** Intraprocedural bleeding during EMR should be managed with coagulation forceps, snare-tip soft coagulation, or clips ± dilute epinephrine injection.

**Recommendation 23 (Weak; low-quality evidence):** Underwater EMR (water immersion without submucosal injection) is an acceptable alternative technique for selected flat lesions and right colon lesions.

**Recommendation 24 (Weak; low-quality evidence):** Cold snare EMR (submucosal injection followed by snare resection without electrocautery) is a promising technique particularly for SSPs ≥10 mm; long-term recurrence data are pending.

### ESD and Advanced Resection

**Recommendation 25 (Weak; low-quality evidence):** Colorectal ESD is indicated for:
- Lesions ≥20 mm with suspected superficial (not deep) SMI not amenable to en bloc EMR
- Mucosal lesions with submucosal fibrosis
- Local residual/recurrent early carcinoma after prior endoscopic resection
- Colitis-associated dysplasia (non-polypoid)

**Recommendation 26 (Weak; low-quality evidence):** Endoscopic full-thickness resection (eFTR) is an option for lesions <30 mm, especially non-lifting lesions or those involving a diverticulum, using the full-thickness resection device (FTRD/OTSC system).

### Marking / Tattoo

**Recommendation 27 (Strong; high-quality evidence):** Sterile carbon particle suspension should be used to mark lesions requiring future endoscopic or surgical localization.

**Recommendation 28 (Strong; moderate-quality evidence):** Tattoo should be placed at 2–3 injection sites, 3–5 cm distal (anal side) to the lesion. Tattoo should NOT be injected at or adjacent to the lesion — submucosal fibrosis impairs future resection.

**Recommendation 29 (Weak; low-quality evidence):** Tattoo material, volume, and position relative to the lesion should be documented and photo-documented in the procedure report.

### Surveillance After Piecemeal EMR (≥20 mm)

**Recommendation 30 (Strong; moderate-quality evidence):** Surveillance colonoscopy after piecemeal EMR of lesions ≥20 mm should be performed at 6 months, then 1 year, then 3 years.

**Recommendation 31 (Strong; moderate-quality evidence):** At surveillance, enhanced imaging (chromoendoscopy or NBI) of the scar site with targeted biopsies should be performed. Normal macroscopic appearance combined with negative biopsies provides the highest predictive value for complete eradication.

**Recommendation 32 (Weak; low-quality evidence):** Recurrent lesions at the EMR scar site should be managed with repeat EMR or avulsion ± margin ablation, followed by surveillance at 6–12 month intervals until clear, then 1 year, then 3 years.

### Quality

**Recommendation 33 (Strong; high-quality evidence):** Endoscopy is the first-line treatment for all benign colorectal lesions. Surgeons and gastroenterologists should avoid referring patients with benign colorectal lesions for surgery when endoscopic removal is feasible.

**Recommendation 34 (Strong; moderate-quality evidence):** When endoscopic removal is not feasible locally, patients should be referred to an advanced polypectomy endoscopist — NOT to surgery — for a second attempt.

**Recommendation 35 (Weak; low-quality evidence):** The proportion of patients referred to surgery for benign colorectal lesions is a quality metric that should be tracked.

**Recommendation 36 (Weak; low-quality evidence):** The resection method (cold snare, hot snare, EMR) should be documented in the procedure report for all lesions ≥10 mm.
