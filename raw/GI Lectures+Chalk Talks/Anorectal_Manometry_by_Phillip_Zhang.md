## Summary

### Catheter Types and Equipment
- 3D catheter: More rigid with more sensors, provides high-definition imaging but less patient comfort
- 2D catheter: Fewer sensors but more flexible and better tolerated by patients
- Catheter orientation: Rectal sensors on top, anal sensors on bottom of the tracing
- Values are lab and catheter-dependent, so ballpark values provided are general guidelines

### Cough Reflex
- Purpose: Assesses sacral reflexes and anal sphincter response to abrupt increases in intra-abdominal pressure
- Normal: Sharp increase in rectal pressure accompanied by simultaneous rise in anal pressure (greater than rectal pressure)
- Abnormal: Rectal pressure spike without corresponding anal sensor rise
- Clinical significance: Abnormal reflex may indicate sacral nerve root or pudendal nerve injury, potentially contributing to fecal incontinence

### Resting Pressure
- Measurement: Mean and max pressure from anal canal over 60 seconds after catheter placement
- Represents basal anal tone from the internal anal sphincter
- Normal: Stable high-pressure zone in anal sensors; rectal sensors remain quiet
- Abnormal: Low resting pressure

### Squeeze Maneuver
- Technique: Records anal pressure during voluntary anal sphincter contraction - three short squeezes and one long squeeze
- Normal: Sustained increase in anal pressure; may see inadvertent increase in rectal pressure from abdominal wall contraction
- During 30-second long squeeze, anal pressure may decline over time due to striated muscle fatigue (expected finding)
- Abnormal: Low squeeze pressure

### Push Maneuver (Critical for Diagnosis)
- Purpose: Measures rectal propulsive pressure generation and anal relaxation during attempted defecation
- Protocol: Three pushes, each 15 seconds
- Normal: Rise in rectal pressure (propulsion) with simultaneous drop in anal pressure (relaxation), creating positive rectal-anal gradient
- Abnormal patterns indicate dyssynergic defecation

### Dyssynergic Defecation Types
- Type 1: Adequate rectal propulsive force but paradoxical increase in anal pressure (no relaxation)
- Type 2: Both inadequate rectal propulsion and paradoxical anal contraction
- Type 3: Adequate propulsion but incomplete anal relaxation
- Type 4: Inadequate rectal propulsion with incomplete or absent anal relaxation
- Important: These patterns can appear normal in unhealthy patients or abnormal in healthy controls
- London and Rome guidelines require multiple abnormal tests (two or more) for diagnosis

### Recto-Inhibitory Reflex (RAIR)
- Mechanism: Involuntary reflex relaxation of internal anal sphincter in response to rapid rectal distension by balloon inflation
- Normal: Transient reflex drop in anal pressure followed by return to baseline when balloon deflates
- Absent RAIR is classically seen in Hirschsprung disease but can occur in other neurologic conditions
- Key principle: Normal RAIR excludes Hirschsprung, but absent RAIR alone does not diagnose it

### Rectal Sensory Testing
- Method: Gradual balloon inflation to measure three key sensation thresholds
- First sensation volume: Typically 10-40 cc
- Urgency volume: Typically 60-100 cc
- Discomfort/max tolerated volume: Typically 130-200 cc
- High thresholds suggest hyposensitivity, seen in chronic constipation
- Very high thresholds may indicate megarectum

### Balloon Expulsion Test (BET)
- Protocol: Balloon filled with 50 cc warm water; patient attempts to expel
- Normal: Expulsion within 60 seconds (some labs use 120 seconds)
- Abnormal causes: Defecatory disorder/dyssynergic defecation, pelvic/anorectal structural obstruction
- When manometry shows abnormalities, MR defecography may be needed to rule out rectocele

### Biofeedback Therapy
- Uses real-time pressure feedback to help patients learn to contract, relax, and coordinate anal sphincter and pelvic floor
- For chronic constipation: Retrains rectal-anal coordination, teaches pelvic floor relaxation
- For fecal incontinence: Focuses on pelvic floor strengthening and sensory training

### Clinical Cases
- Case 1: 56-year-old woman with chronic constipation - most important finding was inability to expel balloon (BET), most direct evidence of impaired evacuation
- Case 2: 45-year-old woman with refractory constipation - Type 1 dyssynergia pattern; biofeedback is best management to retrain coordination
