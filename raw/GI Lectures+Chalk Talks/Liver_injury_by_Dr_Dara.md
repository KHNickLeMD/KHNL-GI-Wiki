## Summary

### Patient Case Overview
- Patient currently in jail ward on medicine service
- Presenting with acute liver failure: very high bilirubin, low INR
- Clinical picture suggests viral hepatitis rather than acetaminophen toxicity (classically icteric presentation)
- Not a transplant candidate due to incarceration status

### Acetaminophen Toxicity Pharmacology Review
- Normal acetaminophen metabolism occurs via phase 2 sulfation and conjugation
- When sulfation pathways saturated, metabolism shifts to cytochrome P450 system
- Cytochrome P450 oxidation produces NAPQI (toxic metabolite) which is highly electrophilic and generates reactive oxygen species
- Glutathione (GSH) needed to neutralize NAPQI and associated ROS
- Risk factors for toxicity include glutathione depletion from illness, poor nutrition, alcohol use, and concurrent medications
- Liver failure can occur with as little as 4 grams acetaminophen in vulnerable patients
- Current recommendations: limit to 3 grams in healthy people, preferably stay below 2 grams
- Acetaminophen is also nephrotoxic and commonly causes creatinine elevation

### NAC (N-Acetylcysteine) Treatment
- NAC provides glutathione to neutralize toxic NAPQI metabolite
- Acute Liver Failure Study Group data: enrolled ~3,000 patients, over 50% were acetaminophen overdoses
- NAC shows survival benefit across all etiologies of acute liver failure when given early (grade 1-2 encephalopathy)
- No benefit once patient reaches full coma with asterixis

### Differential Diagnosis
- Hepatotrophic viruses (Hep A, B, C) are primary consideration given high bilirubin and icteric presentation
- Herpes viruses (CMV, EBV, HSV) less likely as they typically cause anicteric hepatitis in immunocompromised patients with lower bilirubin
- Herpes viruses primarily infect lymphocytes; hepatitis occurs as collateral immune damage rather than direct hepatocyte infection

### Management Plan
- Monitor INR every 12 hours
- Neurochecks every 4-6 hours including asterixis assessment
- Obtain viral PCR panel (CMV, EBV, HSV) despite low pre-test probability
