## Summary

### Patient Case and Treatment Plan
- Patient is incarcerated with acute hepatitis C infection
- Elevated INR was noted as unusual in this setting
- Patient expected to remain incarcerated for approximately three more months

### Treatment Selection Considerations
- Two primary options: Sofosbuvir/Velpatasvir (sof-vel) vs Glecaprevir/Pibrentasvir (GP)
- Key factors influencing drug selection:
  - Pill burden: GP requires three large pills once daily vs sof-vel's single pill
  - Food requirements: Sof-vel must be taken with food, which matters for food-insecure patients
  - Cost: Prices have come down and are now relatively equivalent between options
  - Patient's other medications and potential drug interactions
  - Both are excellent drugs overall

### Rationale for Immediate Treatment
- Paradigm shift in acute HCV management: Treat immediately upon diagnosis rather than waiting to see if patient clears virus spontaneously
- Primary goal is transmission prevention, especially in high-risk settings like jails
- Most DAAs render patients undetectable within four weeks, even with 8-12 week treatment courses
- Once undetectable, patients no longer transmit to others
- Studies in people who use drugs show that dropping viral load reduces infection rates within cohorts

### Treatment Duration Options
- Standard regimens: Sof-vel 12 weeks, GP 8 weeks
- Eight-week option more attractive for incarcerated patients due to uncertain length of stay
- Shorter duration studies:
  - Six-week sof-vel study showed 89% cure rate
  - Interest in four-week GP regimens for high-risk populations
  - "Something is better than nothing" approach in high-risk settings where completion is uncertain

### Challenges with Jail Treatment Access
- Conflicting reports about jail providing HCV treatment
- Some providers report no issues; others report pushback when patients cannot complete full regimen before release
- Documentation gaps make it difficult to determine source of rejections

### Treatment as Harm Reduction
- HCV treatment functions as both cure and harm reduction in high-risk populations
- Modeling studies show aggressive DAA treatment can significantly reduce HCV prevalence in injection drug use settings
- Cannot eliminate HCV in people who use drugs with DAAs alone - must combine with traditional harm reduction strategies

### Current HCV Epidemic and Treatment Gaps
- New HCV infections equal number of patients treated last year, primarily due to opioid epidemic
- Liver transplant population has shifted: previously 25-33% had primary HCV, now less than 5%
- Up to two-thirds of diagnosed patients still haven't started treatment one year after diagnosis
- HCV treatment now considered primary care disease

### Updated Guidelines
- SVR-4 now acceptable instead of SVR-12 for determining cure
- Can test HCV RNA four weeks after treatment completion to confirm cure if negative
