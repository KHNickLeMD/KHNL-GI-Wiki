## Summary

### Obesity and Bariatric Surgery Trends
- Over 40% of patients have obesity, with almost 10% classified as severe obesity
- Obesity rates exceed 20% in all states, with highest prevalence in the Midwest
- Bariatric surgery remains the most effective treatment for obesity
- Historical trend: Roux-en-Y gastric bypass dominated (over 60% of procedures)
- Current trend: Sleeve gastrectomy rates are increasing due to safety and procedural simplicity

### Complications of Roux-en-Y Gastric Bypass

### Marginal Ulceration
- Occurs at the gastrojejunal anastomosis (GJA), typically on the jejunal aspect
- Reported incidence of up to 16%, but likely higher (50% of patients are asymptomatic)
- Symptoms: epigastric pain, obstruction, or GI bleeding
- Risk factors: smoking, diabetes, NSAIDs, surgical technique, and potentially H. pylori
- Treatment:
  - High-dose proton pump inhibitors in soluble/open capsule form
  - Smoking cessation and NSAID discontinuation
  - Proper H. pylori testing (stool antigens preferred over gastric pouch biopsy)
  - Endoscopic oversewing for refractory cases

### Stenosis
- Defined as symptomatic narrowing where standard endoscope cannot pass through GJA
- Treatment:
  - Balloon dilation (caution not to exceed 15mm to prevent weight regain)
  - Lumen-opposing metal stents for refractory cases

### Weight Regain
- Common after gastric bypass with multiple contributing factors
- Anatomic causes include dilated gastrojejunal anastomosis
- Endoscopic treatment options:
  - Endoscopic suturing (purse-string pattern shows better durability)
  - Thermal therapy (APC/cautery) for smaller outlets requiring multiple sessions

### Gastrogastric Fistula
- Abnormal connection between gastric pouch and excluded stomach
- Symptoms: weight regain, pain, reflux, nausea
- Diagnosis: upper endoscopy or upper GI series (CT may have false positives)
- Treatment: PPIs, endoscopic closure for small fistulas, endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty on excluded stomach for larger fistulas

### Biliary Complications
- Abdominal pain common in gastric bypass patients (affects over 1/3 of patients)
- Gallstone disease common after rapid weight loss
- Traditional ERCP challenging due to altered anatomy
- EUS-directed transgastric ERCP (EDGE) approach:
  - Access excluded stomach under EUS guidance
  - Place lumen-opposing metal stent between pouch/small bowel and excluded stomach
  - Can be single session or staged (delay ~3 weeks) based on anatomy
  - High technical success rates but risk of stent dislodgement
  - Review imaging prior to procedure to assess feasibility

### Complications of Sleeve Gastrectomy

### Sleeve Stenosis
- Incidence up to 4%, increasingly common due to popularity of procedure
- Early symptoms (<4 weeks) often due to edema/hematomas that resolve spontaneously
- True strictures typically present after 4 weeks
- Diagnosis:
  - Upper GI series has poor sensitivity but high specificity
  - Direct endoscopy preferred if suspicion high
  - Impedance planimetry provides objective measurement
- Significant diagnostic delays common (average >25 months), leading to prolonged suffering
- Treatment:
  - High-dose PPIs
  - Pneumatic balloon dilation (with careful positioning between GE junction and pylorus)
  - Self-expanding metal stents (limited by migration and poor tolerance)
  - Stricturoplasty for select cases
  - Surgery for refractory cases

### Sleeve Leak
- Most feared adverse event of sleeve gastrectomy
- Symptoms: tachycardia, fever, abdominal tenderness
- Most common at proximal staple line (angle of His)
- Management depends on timing, chronicity, and size of leak
- Requires multidisciplinary approach
- Endoscopic management usually successful
