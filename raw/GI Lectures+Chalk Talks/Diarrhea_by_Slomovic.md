## Summary

### Medication-Induced Diarrhea
- **Metformin**: Causes diarrhea via serotonin-like effects, altered gut microbiome, increased fluid secretion, and enhanced motility; typically occurs at initiation or dose increases
- **Immunosuppressants**: With mycophenolate and tacrolimus, important to rule out opportunistic infections like CMV
- **SSRIs**: Particularly sertraline can cause chronic diarrhea in up to 15% of patients; can also precipitate microscopic colitis
- **5-aminosalicylates**: Rare side effect but can worsen diarrhea in IBD patients
- **GLP-1 agonists** (Ozempic, Mounjaro): Though constipation is more common, can cause disabling diarrhea at initiation or dose increases
- **Others**: NSAIDs, PPIs (4% of patients), magnesium-containing antacids, antibiotics, olmesartan (celiac-like picture)

### Diabetes and Diarrhea
- Mechanisms: Altered motility leading to bacterial overgrowth, increased fluid secretion, reduced pancreatic enzyme secretion
- Type 1 diabetes has association with celiac disease
- Diabetic patients on metformin with chronic diarrhea may have progression of diabetic neuropathy rather than medication effect

### Inflammatory and Functional Bowel Disorders
- **IBS-D**: Mechanisms include bile salt diarrhea, SIBO, hypermotility, exaggerated gastrocolic reflex
- **MCAS** (Mast Cell Activation Syndrome): Inappropriate activation of mast cells causing diarrhea through inflammatory mediators and histamine; associated with Ehlers-Danlos, long COVID, POTS, depression
- **IBD**: Multiple mechanisms including impaired absorption, mucosal damage, altered permeability, increased motility, strictures, SIBO, dysbiosis, post-surgical complications, and medication effects

### Diversion Colitis
- Occurs when fecal flow is diverted, leading to decreased production of short-chain fatty acids (especially butyrate)
- Histology shows lymphoid aggregates with germinal centers, inflammation, architectural changes, ulceration
- Treatment: Short-chain fatty acid enemas; best treatment is reestablishment of intestinal continuity

### Endocrine Causes of Diarrhea
- **Hyperthyroidism**: 25% of patients experience diarrhea; can occur without classic hyperthyroid symptoms
- **Addison's disease**: Associated with diarrhea; seen with herbal supplements from Mexico at County; can precipitate adrenal crisis
- **Gastrinoma**: 75% of patients have diarrhea due to high gastric acid damaging intestinal cells and deactivating lipase
- **Medullary thyroid cancer**: Flushing and diarrhea as cardinal symptoms; part of MEN syndrome; contraindication to GLP-1s
- **Other**: VIPoma (rare), mastocytosis (80% have abdominal pain and diarrhea), pheochromocytoma

### HIV-Related Diarrhea
- Primary HIV infection can cause diarrhea
- C. diff 10x more likely in AIDS patients
- MAI and TB should be considered
- Kaposi's sarcoma can cause protein-losing enteropathy

### Radiation Enteritis
- Can occur years after radiation completion
- Multiple mechanisms: direct enterocyte damage, vascular injury, submucosal fibrosis, lymphatic damage
- Can present with strictures (causing SIBO), ulcerations, bile salt diarrhea (if ileum affected)
- Treatment: Sucralfate, aminosalicylates, steroids, antibiotics for SIBO, hyperbaric oxygen for severe cases

### Other Notable Conditions
- **SCAD** (Segmental Colitis Associated with Diverticulosis): Presents with abdominal pain, mild diarrhea; characterized by segmental and circumferential thickening of colonic wall; treated with mesalamine and antibiotics
- **Amyloidosis**: Presents with bleeding or malabsorption; diagnosed by rectal biopsy with Congo red staining
- **Menetrier's disease**: Massive secretion of fluid, protein-losing enteropathy; in adults a marker for gastric cancer
- **Villous adenoma syndrome**: Distal rectal velvety tumors causing secretory diarrhea and hypokalemia
- **Rare syndromes**: Cronkite-Canada (hamartomatous polyps, alopecia, nail changes, hyperpigmentation) and IPSID (B-cell lymphoma in Middle Eastern men)
