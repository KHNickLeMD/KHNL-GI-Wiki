## Summary

### Patient Presentation and Diagnosis
- Patient likely has achalasia based on imaging showing an 8 cm dilated esophagus
- Achalasia is the inability of the lower esophageal sphincter to relax, causing the esophagus to become dilated over time (sometimes called "colonic esophagus")
- Duration of symptoms is unclear, but an 8 cm dilation suggests this has been present for a long time
- Patient had no dysphagia when last seen in August during admission for recurrent aspiration
- Currently intubated in ICU for COPD exacerbation

### Diagnostic Approach
- Achalasia is a manometric diagnosis, not radiographic or endoscopic
- Esophageal manometry uses a catheter with pressure monitors every half centimeter placed through the nose
- Patient must perform 10 "wet swallows" while pressure wave propagation is measured
- The test requires patient cooperation and cannot be performed while intubated and sedated

### Treatment Options
- **Surgical myotomy**: First-line treatment, often combined with fundoplication to prevent reflux
- **POEM (Per Oral Endoscopic Myotomy)**: Endoscopic approach through the mouth, relatively new (about two decades), particularly beneficial for type 3 achalasia
- **Esophagectomy with gastric pull-through**: Worst case scenario, involves significant complications including lifelong severe GERD

### Prognosis
- With 8 cm dilation, patient likely has total aperistalsis of the esophagus
- Even if lower esophageal sphincter is opened, lack of peristalsis means poor prognosis for any therapy
