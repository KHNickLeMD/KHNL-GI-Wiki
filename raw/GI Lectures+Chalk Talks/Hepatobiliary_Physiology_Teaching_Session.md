## Summary

### HIDA Scan Interpretation and Limitations
- HIDA scans detect gallbladder obstruction by showing absence of gallbladder uptake when there's a blockage in the cystic duct
- For the radiotracer to reach the gallbladder, hepatocytes must pick it up from blood and pump it into the biliary system
- Critical limitation: In patients with elevated bilirubin (e.g., bilirubin of 8), hepatocytes aren't pumping anything into the bile canaliculi - everything backflows into the blood instead
- HIDA scan is essentially useless in patients with intrinsic liver disease and elevated bilirubin, as they will always show positive results regardless of actual cholecystitis

### Bile Excretion Physiology
- Hepatocytes conjugate bilirubin and return it to normal levels in the bile canaliculi
- Excess conjugated bilirubin is pumped into canaliculi via ATP-mediated transporters including bile cell export pump and MDR3
- These transporters also excrete bile acids, bile salts, heavy metals, and cholesterol
- The process is ATP-mediated active transport, not hydrostatic or oncotic pressure-driven like kidney function
- Requires complex biochemistry: transcription of transporters, translation, Golgi transport, surface expression, and ATP function

### ICU Cholestasis and Cytokine Effects
- During cytokine storms (elevated TNF, IL-6) in critically ill patients, bile transporters "freeze up" and don't function properly
- This occurs because the ATP-mediated transport system is disrupted at multiple levels
- This phenomenon is called ICU cholestasis or intrahepatic cholestasis - essentially a "frozen liver" at the canalicular level

### Intrahepatic vs Extrahepatic Cholestasis
- Patients with intrinsic hepatocyte disease will have impaired bile excretion and positive HIDA scans regardless of actual obstruction
- Important to distinguish from extrahepatic cholestasis (mechanical obstruction)

### MRCP Imaging Protocols
- MRCP is a specific protocol different from standard MRI
- For evaluating bile ducts and looking for stones, must specifically order MRCP - regular MRI of liver won't provide adequate duct visualization
- MRCP creates 3D reconstructions of bile ducts to identify stones
- If looking for both abscess and ductal pathology, need to order both MRCP and MRI with contrast

### Gallbladder Wall Thickening - Clinical Pearl
- Gallbladder wall thickening is commonly seen in any hepatitis (hepatitis A, autoimmune hepatitis, alcohol-associated hepatitis) because the gallbladder sits directly adjacent to liver parenchyma
- Patients with low albumin and systemic illness can show false positive wall thickening on imaging
- Wall thickening represents edema and doesn't always indicate active inflammation

### Case Discussion
- Patient had characteristic AST/ALT ratio seen almost exclusively in alcohol-related liver injury
- AST predominance in alcoholic hepatitis relates to B6 metabolism - alcohol affects B6 absorption, and B6 is needed as a cofactor for ALT enzyme production in hepatocytes
