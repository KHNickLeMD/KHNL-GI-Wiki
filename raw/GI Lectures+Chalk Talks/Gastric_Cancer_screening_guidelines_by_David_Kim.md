## Summary

### Gastric Cancer Epidemiology
- Fifth most commonly diagnosed cancer globally (2018), third leading cause of cancer deaths (8.2%)
- Five-year U.S. survival rate: only 32%
- Large racial disparities between non-Hispanic white vs non-Hispanic Black, Asian, and Pacific Islander populations
- Overall trend: Declining prevalence in the U.S., though significant racial disparities remain

### H. pylori Epidemiology
- Declining prevalence: H. pylori positivity decreased from 35.7% (1999-2006) to 18.4% (2013-2018)
- Higher burden in specific zip codes, older ages, and non-white racial categories

### International Screening Programs
- **Korea**: Screening endoscopy every 2 years starting age 40 (since 2002); 41% decreased hazard ratio for death
- **Japan**: Two-year gastric EGD for patients >50 (since 2016); HR 0.39 for mortality; 22% reduction in advanced cancer

### Evolution of U.S. Guidelines
- **AGA 2020**: Recommended against routine surveillance unless high-risk features (incomplete GIM, extensive GIM involving body, family history)
- **ACG 2025**: Decreased surveillance interval from 3-5 years to 3 years for GIM; recommended opportunistic screening in high-risk groups
- **AGA 2024 Expert Review**: Consider screening at age 45 in high-risk populations (first-generation immigrants from high-incidence regions, non-white racial groups, family history, hereditary syndromes)

### H. pylori Screening and Eradication
- Large VA cohort: confirmed eradication showed HR 0.24 for gastric cancer
- China RCT (26.5-year follow-up): H. pylori treatment HR 0.57 for gastric cancer incidence
- Family-based eradication threefold more effective than single patient treatment; 70% lower recurrence rates

### Areas Needing Further Research
- No U.S.-based RCTs evaluating mortality benefit of gastric cancer screening
- Lack of risk prediction models
- Optimal screening intervals and age cutoffs not well-established
- Environmental factors play significant role (Japanese immigrant data)
