## Summary

### Overview of Gastroparesis Diet Principles
- A gastroparesis diet is designed based on how digestion works, capitalizing on what is known about the digestive process

### Why Low-Fat Diet is Essential
- Fats significantly slow down stomach emptying because breaking down fatty acid chains requires extensive digestive work
- Low-fat diet is a core principle of gastroparesis management

### High Residue Foods to Avoid
- High residue foods are primarily plant matter
- Fruits and salads, typically considered healthy, are problematic for gastroparesis patients
- Specific examples: orange pulp, blueberry skins, celery, general salads

### Liquid vs. Solid Foods
- Liquids empty from the stomach much faster than solids, following first-order dynamics
- Solids follow a slower sigmoid curve pattern
- Ideal diet should contain more liquid than solid foods

### Recommended Foods and Nutrients
- **Carbohydrates**: Simple carbohydrates are well-tolerated because digestion begins in the mouth with salivary amylase (mashed potatoes, saltine crackers, pasta without oil or fat, rice)
- **Protein**: Stomach breaks down protein reasonably well, but should be consumed in moderate amounts
- **Fruit juice**: Acceptable because it's liquid, though pulp should be avoided

### Practical Meal Suggestions
- Chicken and rice soup or chicken noodle soup: ideal combination with high liquid content, simple carbohydrates, and small amount of protein
- Ensure or similar liquid meals: good option, though patients may tire of them

### Eating Pattern Recommendations
- Five or six small meals per day rather than three large meals
- Patients need education that they are "eating for health" rather than eating to fullness

### Patient Education Considerations
- Must assess patients' nutritional health literacy, which can vary from low to high
- Need to keep recommendations simple and practical
