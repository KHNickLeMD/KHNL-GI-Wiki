## Summary

### Pathophysiology and Complications
- Acute liver failure primarily manifests as loss of liver function
- Unlike chronic liver disease's balanced coagulopathy, acute liver failure patients are more prone to bleeding, infection, and shock
- Encephalopathy is a major complication in acute liver failure due to lack of buffer against osmotic shifts
- Patients lack the chronic buffer against ammonia and toxins, leading to cerebral edema
- Loss of ability to filter gut bacteria allows bacterial products to cause additional complications

### Monitoring and Prognostic Indicators
- Mental status assessment is critical upon patient arrival and requires frequent monitoring
- Serum ammonia levels are valuable prognostic indicators in acute liver failure:
  - Levels <100 indicate good prognosis
  - Levels ~200 indicate poor prognosis
  - Regular trending of ammonia levels is recommended

### Management Strategies
- Continuous Renal Replacement Therapy (CRRT) helps reduce ammonia and clear toxins to prevent cerebral edema
- CRRT intensity can be titrated based on ammonia levels
- Cerebral edema management includes:
  - Hypertonic saline
  - Mannitol
  - Close monitoring of pupil size
  - Neurocritical care consultation for severe encephalopathy
- Early intubation and overventilation to reduce CO2 and decrease intracranial pressure
- Deep sedation to prevent stimulation that could increase intracranial pressure
- Death in acute liver failure typically results from cerebral edema
- General critical care management principles apply, with potential for urgent cardiac catheterization if needed

### Patient-Specific Plan
- Immediate imaging: CT chest, CT 4 phase, and stat TTE (Transthoracic Echocardiogram)
- Consultations: social work evaluation and surgical consultation
- Complete infectious and immune serologies

### Additional Clinical Discussion
- Discussion about liver zones (1, 2, 3) and different etiologies of liver failure (ischemic vs. drug-induced)
- Zone-specific injury doesn't significantly alter management but may affect prognosis

### Action Items
- Arrange for CT chest, CT 4 phase, and stat TTE for the patient
- Request social work evaluation
- Obtain surgical consultation
- Order infectious and immune serologies
- Continue monitoring patient's mental status and ammonia levels
