## Summary

### Liver Anatomy and Pathology Review
- Portal triads consist of portal vein, hepatic artery, and bile duct, with approximately 5 portal triads surrounding a central vein that drains into hepatic veins
- AIH attacks hepatocytes with lymphocytes in Zone 1, often with interface hepatitis (lymphocytes spilling from Zone 1 into Zone 2)
- PBC attacks cholangiocytes causing nonsuppurative cholangitis, with florid duct lesion being pathognomonic

### PBC Diagnosis and Testing
- Diagnosis requires 2 of 3 criteria: chronically elevated alkaline phosphatase, AMA positivity, and compatible biopsy findings
- Over 90% of PBC patients are AMA positive in white populations
- For AMA-negative cases, check SP100 and GP210 antibodies - these are highly specific for PBC and can confirm diagnosis without biopsy
- GP210 positivity is associated with poor transplant-free survival and has a rim-like ANA pattern
- SP100 has a multiple dot ANA pattern
- In straightforward cases, biopsy is not required for diagnosis

### Health Disparities in PBC - Latino Population Research
- USC has one of the largest cohorts of PBC in Latino patients (East Los Angeles PBC cohort)
- Latino patients with PBC show important differences from white patients:
  - Lower AMA positivity: 79-80% vs 92% in non-Hispanic whites
  - More frequently diagnosed with PBC-AIH variant disease
  - Less responsive to UDCA
  - More often placed on steroids
  - Less likely to have health insurance
  - Worse overall outcomes
  - Present with more severe disease, often later in disease course
- For decades, PBC was mistakenly thought to be a disease primarily affecting white women, leading to underdiagnosis in other populations

### PBC-AIH Overlap Syndrome - Concerns About Overdiagnosis
- Variant disease is likely over-called, particularly in patients of color
- Paris criteria for overlap syndrome require: ALT >5x ULN, IgG >2x ULN and/or positive anti-smooth muscle antibody, and moderate-severe interface hepatitis on biopsy
- Steroids should be used cautiously in PBC due to risk of severe osteoporosis in middle-aged women with impaired vitamin D absorption

### Treatment: UDCA (First-Line)
- UDCA is the only treatment proven to improve survival in PBC
- Mechanism: promotes bile acid-dependent and independent bile flow, has anti-apoptotic and anti-inflammatory effects
- Benefits: delays portal hypertension, delays histologic progression, improves liver biochemistry, and improves survival
- Only 60% of patients achieve adequate biochemical response (alk phos <2x ULN) on UDCA alone
- 40% of patients have inadequate response and require second-line therapy

### Treatment Goals and Biochemical Response
- Traditional goal: alk phos <2x ULN (~200)
- Current thinking among experts: aim for complete normalization of alkaline phosphatase
- Lower alkaline phosphatase directly correlates with better transplant-free survival
- Bilirubin <0.6 is also associated with better outcomes

### Second-Line Therapies
- Obeticholic acid (OCA) was approved in 2016 but withdrawn due to hepatotoxicity
- Two new PPARs approved in the US:
  - Elafibranor (PPAR alpha-delta agonist)
  - Seladelpar (PPAR delta agonist)
- Both used in addition to UDCA, achieved 15-25% normalization of alk phos, and help with pruritus
- Side effects to monitor: hepatotoxicity, myalgias, fracture risk
- Bezafibrate is approved in EU and Canada but not available in US
- Fenofibrate used off-label in US but can cause DILI

### Symptomatic Management of Pruritus
- First-line: cholestyramine (binds bile acids) - must separate from other medications
- Second-line: rifampicin (risk of hepatotoxicity)
- Other options: naltrexone, sertraline
- IBATs (Ileal Bile Acid Transporters): block bile acid reabsorption in ileum, approved for pruritus in children with PFIC, being studied for PBC and PSC

## Notes

### Structure of the Liver
- Portal triad: PV, HA, bile duct
- 5x portal triads around a central vein leading to hepatic veins
- Zone 3 is around central vein (processes coagulants like Factor VII)
- Zone 1 is around the portal triad

### AIH vs PBC Pathology
- AIH: attacks hepatocytes with lymphocytes, usually in zone 1, with interface hepatitis
- PBC: attacks cholangiocytes, nonsuppurative cholangitis, can also have interface hepatitis in severe disease

### Variant Disease
- Start the name with the predominant disease (PBC+AIH or AIH+PBC)
- Paris criteria for overlap diagnosis
