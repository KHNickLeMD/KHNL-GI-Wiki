### Overview
- Educational presentation by Bryson (PGY1) on liver diseases in pregnancy
- Topics covered: general principles, intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP), acute fatty liver of pregnancy (AFLP), and HELLP syndrome

### General Principles of Liver Disease in Pregnancy
- Must rule out standard causes of liver disease including hepatitis, drug-induced liver injury (DILI), and biliary obstruction (cholelithiasis, choledocholithiasis) which are more common in pregnancy
- Requires multidisciplinary approach with high-risk OB specialists, especially for life-threatening conditions
- Significant overlap between conditions requires careful differential diagnosis

### Intrahepatic Cholestasis of Pregnancy (ICP)
- **Etiology and Epidemiology**: Caused by impaired bile acid transport and circulation; most common condition discussed, affecting 1-2% of pregnancies
- **Clinical Presentation**: Pruritus especially in palms, worst at night; typically occurs late second/early third trimester
- **Laboratory Findings**: Not typically associated with very high AST or ALT elevations
- **Management**:
  - Expectant management with ursodiol treatment (starting at 300mg BID)
  - Monitor bile acids with goal of reducing levels below 10 to prevent fetal complications
  - Consider early planned delivery if bile acids poorly controlled
  - Generally does not require C-section unless indicated for other reasons
- **Prognosis**: Condition resolves with delivery but has high recurrence rate (up to 70% in future pregnancies)

### Acute Fatty Liver of Pregnancy (AFLP)
- **Pathophysiology**: Life-threatening hepatotoxic condition related to cholesterol metabolism; caused by impaired metabolism of fetal fatty acids leading to abrupt increase in fatty acid storage and direct hepatocyte damage
- **Genetic Association**: Associated with LCHAD deficiency in approximately 20% of cases
- **Clinical Presentation**:
  - Early: Nonspecific symptoms including nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain
  - Progressive: Synthetic liver dysfunction with jaundice, elevated transaminases, elevated INR (coagulopathy)
  - Severe: Encephalopathy, hypoglycemia
  - Typically presents at end of third trimester
- **Diagnosis**: Swansea criteria - meeting 6 criteria has good positive predictive value
- **Management**:
  - Supportive care with emergent delivery (obstetric emergency)
  - Can worsen postpartum despite most cases eventually resolving after delivery
  - Liver transplant may be considered for patients with worsening condition

### HELLP Syndrome
- **Pathophysiology**:
  - On the continuum of preeclampsia and eclampsia
  - Results from poor trophoblastic invasion into endometrium causing endothelial dysfunction
  - Liver is a "bystander" organ; condition involves angiopathic thrombocytopenia with platelets forming microthrombi in liver
  - Leads to elevated transaminases and consumptive coagulopathy with platelet depletion
- **Laboratory Findings**:
  - INR and PTT remain normal (coagulation cascade not affected)
  - Hemolytic anemia with elevated LDH, decreased haptoglobin, and schistocytes on smear
  - Thrombocytopenia from consumption
- **Clinical Features**:
  - Hypertension and proteinuria present in ~80% of patients, but HELLP syndrome can occur without preeclampsia features
  - 70% occur in third trimester; remaining 30% occur within 4 weeks to 72 hours postpartum
- **Management**:
  - Supportive care with prompt delivery (obstetric emergency, do not delay)
  - Symptoms typically resolve postpartum but can progress to liver failure
  - Associated with DIC, postpartum hemorrhage, and other complications
  - Multidisciplinary team approach is critical
