## Summary

### Recent Findings on Weight Management in Cirrhosis Patients
- A UCSD randomized controlled trial (2023) with 71 patients showed 8.8% total body weight loss with semaglutide versus placebo over 48 weeks
- Observational studies demonstrated decreased mortality and cardiovascular events with incretin use
- The RCT showed no improvement in fibrosis or NASH resolution, but importantly no decompensation or death occurred
- Incretins offer the greatest weight reduction with acceptable safety profiles in compensated cirrhosis
- These medications should generally be avoided in decompensated cirrhosis due to lack of safety data and potential worsening sarcopenia
- Endoscopic interventions (sleeve gastroplasty, intragastric balloon) show 7-13% weight loss but are only recommended for compensated cirrhosis without significant portal hypertension
- The balloon approach is considered more temporary, with weight regain common after removal
- A multidisciplinary team with transplant and bariatric expertise should be involved in treatment decisions

### Treatment Framework for Obesity in Cirrhosis
- Core care should focus on optimizing nutrition and addressing sarcopenia
- Consider pharmacotherapy (incretin-based) for appropriate candidates with compensated cirrhosis
- Target of 10% total body weight loss is desirable
- Treatment options differ based on compensated vs. decompensated status
- In compensated cirrhosis without significant portal hypertension, endoscopic interventions may be considered
- For patients with significant portal hypertension, surgical interventions simultaneous with or after liver transplant may be discussed

### Cystatin C for Mortality Prediction in Liver Allocation
- Study from Columbia examined replacing creatinine with cystatin C in liver allocation scores
- Renal function is a crucial independent predictor of liver-related mortality
- MELD-3.0 uses creatinine less than previous MELD iterations
- Serum creatinine underperforms in cirrhosis, often overestimating GFR by up to 45%
- Overestimation is worse in patients who are sicker, more sarcopenic, and have more ascites
- Female sex and low skeletal muscle mass are independent risk factors for GFR overestimation
- Cystatin C shows better correlation to measured GFR and is less influenced by muscle mass, diet, and age
- Study with 1,300 patients substituted cystatin C for creatinine
- Almost all risk scores increased when replacing creatinine with cystatin C (99% of patients had higher cystatin C than creatinine)
- However, discrimination (ability to predict 90-day mortality) was not significantly different
- Cystatin C helps identify high-risk patients but doesn't significantly improve current models
- The Harmony Consortium is working on developing a cystatin C risk score

### Metabolic Risk and Alcoholic Disease in MASLD
- Study examined impact of metabolic factors and alcohol on advanced liver disease risk
- Used Global MASLD project data with 17,643 patients from 41 countries with liver biopsies
- 53% had F2 fibrosis and 35% had F3 fibrosis
- Patients with more advanced fibrosis had more metabolic syndrome components, especially type 2 diabetes
- Age, obesity, and type 2 diabetes were independently associated with advanced fibrosis in multivariate analysis
- Self-reported alcohol use did not significantly modify fibrosis risk
- Cumulative metabolic risk burden strongly increased fibrosis risk in a stepwise manner
- Among non-invasive tests, liver stiffness measurement (LSM) maintained the greatest accuracy
- FIB4 and ELF tests showed reduced accuracy in patients with type 2 diabetes
- The study suggests patients with different metabolic profiles may need different screening approaches

### Afrixifirmin for NASH with Compensated Cirrhosis
- Afrixifirmin is an engineered bivalent FGF21 analog being studied for NASH treatment
- The previous SYMMETRY trial showed it can induce fibrosis reversal without NASH worsening at 96 weeks
- Results showed statistically significant improvement in liver stiffness measurement (LSM) with 50mg dose at 96 weeks
- Patients achieved decreases in LSM and ELF scores that have previously been associated with reduced liver-related events
- Proposed definition of response: decrease in LSM by 30% and decrease in ELF by 0.5
- Addresses unmet need for a strong agent for fibrosis regression in NASH cirrhosis

### Waist-Based Measures vs. BMI for Fibrosis Screening
- Danish study compared obesity measures as entry criteria for fibrosis screening in 6,103 individuals
- Current BMI-based criteria may underestimate liver fibrosis risk
- 37% met obesity criteria by BMI vs. 58% by waist circumference, 84% by waist-hip ratio
- Waist-based measures showed superior diagnostic accuracy for detecting advanced fibrosis
- BMI pathway misses up to 64% of advanced fibrosis cases vs. 43-49% with waist-based measures
- Sequential testing (waist-based measures followed by alternative NITs) significantly improved detection
- Challenge: specificity remains low with high false-positive rates (up to 92%)
- Recommendation: shift from BMI to waist-based measures in initial risk assessment

### Liver Stiffness Dynamics in General Population
- Part of European LiverScream consortium studying population-based screening with elastography
- Prospective cohort of 4,536 participants with median 4.3 years follow-up
- Only 5.5% had elevated liver stiffness (LSM) at baseline
- Of those with normal baseline LSM, only 2.6% progressed to advanced fibrosis
- 44% of those with elevated baseline LSM achieved significant regression
- Factors associated with progression: metabolic risk factors and alcohol use
- Diabetes associated with lower likelihood of regression
- Improvement in lifestyle and alcohol use associated with regression

### Disparities in Multiple Listing for Liver Transplantation
- Study investigated equity of multiple listing pre- and post-acuity circles allocation policy (2020)
- Multiple listed patients had lower MELD scores, higher education, more likely to be white, higher rates of commercial insurance
- These disparities remained similar after policy change
- Multiple listing increases likelihood of transplantation
- Higher education increased odds of transplant; being a minority or having government insurance decreased odds
- The acuity circles allocation policy didn't improve these equity issues
- Proposed solutions: better patient education, increased center transparency, subsidies for disadvantaged patients
