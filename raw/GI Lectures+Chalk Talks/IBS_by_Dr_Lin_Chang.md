## Overview of Disorders of Gut-Brain Interaction (DGBI)
- DGBI (formerly called functional GI disorders) are disorders classified by GI symptoms related to dysregulation in gut-brain communication
- Related to combination of motility disturbance, visceral hypersensitivity, altered mucosal immune function, altered gut microbiota, and altered CNS processing
- 40% of world's population has a DGBI; IBS prevalence is 4-11%
- Most DGBI are more prevalent in women than men, except cyclic vomiting syndrome, fecal incontinence, cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome, and functional diarrhea

## Diagnostic Approach
- Use positive diagnostic strategy with symptom-based criteria and limited diagnostic testing
- Start with symptoms: recurrent/chronic abdominal pain or discomfort associated with change in bowel function
- Screen for alarm features: new onset after age 50, unintended weight loss, rectal bleeding, nocturnal diarrhea, fever, family history of CRC/IBD/celiac, unexplained iron deficiency

## Recommended Testing for IBS-D
- Celiac serologies (IgA and IgG antibody)
- Fecal calprotectin or lactoferrin for intestinal inflammation
- C-reactive protein
- Bile acid diarrhea testing (C4 level, spot bile acids)
- Giardia stool antigen
- Anorectal physiology testing if suspecting pelvic floor dysnergia

## Rome 5 IBS Criteria
- Abdominal pain or discomfort
- Pain associated with defecation (gets better or worse)
- Associated with change in stool frequency and/or form
- Criteria fulfilled for 3 months, starting at least 6 months ago (research criteria)
- Clinical criteria: predominant symptoms present for at least 8 weeks

## Bristol Stool Form Scale for Subtyping
- Type 1-2: slower transit (IBS-C if >=25% of bowel movements)
- Type 6-7: faster transit (IBS-D if >=25% of bowel movements)
- IBS-M: >=25% of both type 1-2 and type 6-7
- Type 3-5: normal/target range

## Racial and Ethnic Disparities
- White patients more likely to be diagnosed with IBS (35%) compared to Black and Hispanic patients
- Black patients prioritize belly pain, more often go to urgent care/ER
- Women seek healthcare more than men (2:1 in general population, up to 4:1 in healthcare-seeking population)

## Pathophysiology and Risk Factors
- Risk factors: female sex, gastroenteritis, trauma/abuse, lifetime stress, psychological disorders, somatic symptoms, poor sleep
- Poor sleep associated with worse bowel symptoms next day
- Post-infection IBS tends to resolve over years except with Giardia infection

## Dietary Treatments

### Low FODMAP Diet
- Only do for 2-6 weeks
- If responsive, go through reintroduction
- Now using "FODMAP light" approach
- Do NOT use in patients with restricted eating patterns

### Mediterranean Diet
- Recent study showed superiority over traditional dietary advice for IBS
- Need to personalize as some Mediterranean foods may cause problems

## Treatment for IBS-D

### Antidiarrheals
- Loperamide: up to 2mg four times daily, often used as needed

### Bile Acid Sequestrants
- Colesevelam 675mg per tablet, max dose 3 tablets twice daily
- Start 1 tablet BID, can increase over 4 weeks

### Rifaximin
- 550mg TID for 2 weeks
- FDA approved for IBS-D (NOT SIBO)
- Symptoms recur in 6-36 months, safe to use up to 3 times

### Eluxadoline
- Mixed opioid agonist-antagonist
- Two doses: 75mg or 100mg twice daily with meals
- CONTRAINDICATED: no gallbladder, >=3 alcohol drinks/day, pancreatitis, sphincter of Oddi dysfunction, severe liver disease

### Antispasmodics
- Hyoscyamine or dicyclomine
- Take 30-60 minutes before meals

### Peppermint Oil
- Encapsulated form (IBGARD)
- Take 30-60 minutes before meals

## Neuromodulators (Central Pain Treatments)

### Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs)
- First-line for chronic/recurrent pain
- Amitriptyline: use for diarrhea, sleep issues, or cyclic vomiting syndrome (IBS dose: 10-30mg)
- Desipramine or nortriptyline: use for constipation or patients not wanting sedation
- Start 10-25mg at bedtime, uptitrate slowly by 10mg increments

### SNRIs
- Duloxetine: excellent for pain, start 30mg once daily for 1 week, then 30mg BID
- Good choice for comorbid fibromyalgia

### SSRIs
- Not effective for IBS pain or functional dyspepsia
- Use when anxiety/depression is driving symptoms

### Mirtazapine
- Works on serotonin and norepinephrine presynaptically
- Dosing: 7.5, 15, 30, 45mg; aim for 15-30mg
- Effective for functional dyspepsia and IBS-D
- Side effects: weight gain, sedation

## Treatment for IBS-C
- PEG: 1 capful daily, can increase
- Linaclotide: most commonly used, doses 72, 145, 290mcg
- Plecanatide: 3mg once daily
- Tenapanor: 50mg twice daily, only approved for IBS-C
- Prucalopride: only FDA-approved for chronic idiopathic constipation
- Pain and bloating take 3-4 weeks to improve, maximum effect at 8 weeks

## Behavioral Therapies
- Gut-directed hypnotherapy: very helpful
- Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT): very helpful
- Resources: GIPsychology.com, Rome Foundation Gastro Psych Registry, Nerva App

## Clinical Pearls
- Focus on most bothersome symptoms and patient's goals
- Use active listening, validate concerns
- Make positive diagnosis and explain physiologic mechanisms
- If constipation is treated but pain persists, add neuromodulator or behavioral therapy
- If doing well at 6-12 months, consider tapering
