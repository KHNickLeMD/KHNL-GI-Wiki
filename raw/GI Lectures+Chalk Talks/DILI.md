## Key Clinical Recommendations
- Always obtain HCV RNA (not just serologies) and consider anti-HEV when evaluating suspected DILI
- Ask specifically about OTC medications, herbal supplements, dietary supplements, naturopathic and homeopathic remedies
- Contact pharmacies directly when patients cannot recall medication history
- For INH prophylaxis: Monitor liver enzymes monthly, especially in patients over 50
- Transfer patients with acute hepatocellular jaundice to transplant centers early
- Always administer NAC for acetaminophen overdose, even if presenting late
- For checkpoint inhibitor hepatotoxicity grade 3-4: Discontinue ICI, give steroids until ALT <3x ULN, taper over 4 weeks
- Consult LiverTox database when evaluating suspected DILI cases

## DILI Diagnostic Approach
- DILI is a diagnosis of exclusion
- R-value determines injury pattern: ALT/ULN divided by ALP/ULN; >=5 hepatocellular, <2 cholestatic, 2-5 mixed
- Key elements: detailed medication history, latency period, de-challenge response, re-challenge, comparison with known drug patterns

## Epidemiology and Risk Factors
- DILI incidence: 19 per 100,000 in general population, >1% in hospitalized patients
- Over 60% of DILI cases in patients over 40; over 33% over 60
- First-pass metabolism declines ~1% per year after age 40
- Younger patients (<50-60) have more hepatocellular injury; older patients more cholestatic
- Genetic HLA polymorphisms explain individual susceptibility

## Antibiotics and Common Culprits
- Antibiotics are the leading cause; amoxicillin-clavulanate most common
- High-risk: nitrofurantoin (long latency), Bactrim, cefazolin (can occur after single dose)
- Minocycline can cause autoimmune-like hepatitis
- Cholestatic injury recovers more slowly than hepatocellular

## INH-Specific Considerations
- 5-10% develop transient transaminase elevations; ~1% develop jaundice
- Risk factors: older age (>50), hepatitis B, female sex, Black race, chronic liver disease
- New IDSA guidelines favor shorter treatment courses

## Herbal and Dietary Supplements
- Now nearly equal to antibiotics as leading DILI cause in the US
- No FDA regulation, batch-to-batch variability, multiple unknown ingredients
- Top offenders: kratom, turmeric (concentrated supplements), ashwagandha
- Food-grade turmeric in cooking is different from concentrated supplements

## Acetaminophen Hepatotoxicity
- Causes >50% of all acute liver failure cases in the US
- Classic presentation: towering AST/ALT (>7,000), early INR elevation disproportionate to bilirubin
- King's College criteria: pH <7.3 is single worst predictor of mortality (95%) - board question
- Current recommendations: max 3g daily healthy people, 2g for smaller/comorbid individuals
- NAC within 8 hours prevents life-threatening liver disease; late NAC still decreases mortality

## Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor Hepatotoxicity
- CTLA-4 inhibitors cause most liver injury as monotherapy
- Risk factors: younger age and female sex (opposite of typical DILI)
- Latency: typically ~5 cycles, can occur after 70+ cycles or months after stopping
- ~20% of cases are steroid-refractory; second-line: anti-TNF therapy
- Secondary sclerosing cholangitis-like phenotype can occur

## Prognosis
- Most patients recover with drug withdrawal
- Hy's Law: acute hepatocellular injury with jaundice carries 10% mortality risk

## Liver Biopsy Patterns
- Methotrexate: fibrosis; Tetracycline: microvesicular steatosis; Amiodarone: similar changes
- OCPs: adenomas; Minocycline: autoimmune-like hepatitis; Allopurinol: fibrin-ringed granulomas
