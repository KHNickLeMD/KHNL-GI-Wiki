## Summary

### Overview and Pathophysiology
- Parkinson's disease presents with visible motor symptoms (tremor, rigidity, postural instability) but also includes significant non-motor symptoms, particularly gastrointestinal manifestations that are often overlooked
- Abnormal clumping of alpha-synuclein creates toxic accumulations of Lewy bodies, predominantly in dopamine-producing cells like the substantia nigra
- Lewy bodies and alpha-synuclein aggregates are not confined to the brain but have been identified throughout the GI tract from stomach to rectum
- Studies of colon biopsy samples obtained 2-5 years prior to Parkinson's onset showed positive immunostaining for alpha-synuclein in patients who later developed Parkinson's, but not in healthy controls
- These findings support that Parkinson's is a multisystemic disease and raise the question of whether GI tissue could serve as a novel diagnostic target

### Gut-Brain Axis
- The gut-brain axis involves bidirectional communication between the central and enteric nervous systems, with regulation of immune responses heavily influenced by intestinal microbes
- In susceptible individuals, inflammatory triggers from environment or diet initiate gut immune responses that impact the microbiota, increase intestinal permeability ("leaky gut"), and induce alpha-synuclein expression and aggregation
- These aggregates can be transmitted from gut to brain via the vagus nerve, and intestinal inflammation may drive the neurodegeneration characteristic of Parkinson's

### Constipation
- Constipation is the most common GI symptom in Parkinson's, reported in 80-90% of patients
- Can occur years or even decades before motor symptom onset, with severity aligning with disease progression
- Strongly associated with decreased quality of life and mood disorders
- A 2023 systematic review of 17 studies with over 3 million participants found a 2.3-fold increased risk of Parkinson's in patients with constipation

**Mechanisms:**
- Multiple overlapping mechanisms including slow colonic transit, dyssynergic defecation, and poor anorectal function
- High-resolution anorectal manometry and MRI defecography study of 42 patients found 60% had combined delayed colonic transit and anorectal dysfunction, with over 80% exhibiting prolonged colonic transit time

**Management - Non-pharmacological:**
- First-line: scheduled toileting after meals, increased fluid intake, dietary fiber, and regular exercise

**Management - Pharmacological:**
- Polyethylene glycol (Miralax): Strong evidence for chronic idiopathic constipation, with open-label studies showing efficacy in Parkinson's patients
- Linaclotide (Linzess): Secretagogue with observational studies showing increased weekly bowel movements and favorable tolerability
- Lubiprostone: Chloride channel activator; double-blind RCT at Baylor and USF with 100 Parkinson's patients showed 64% reported marked/very marked improvement at 4 weeks when titrated to 48 mcg daily
- Prucalopride (Motegrity): Selective 5-HT4 receptor agonist; double-blind RCT showed considerable efficacy at 2mg daily for 8 weeks with no adverse effects reported

**Important considerations:**
- Minimize use of opiates and anticholinergics that worsen constipation
- Avoid treating diarrhea with Imodium without evaluation, as it may be overflow diarrhea and worsen underlying constipation
- For dyssynergic defecation: consider biofeedback therapy, pelvic floor physical therapy, or Botox injections in puborectalis muscle

### Diet and Gut Microbiome
- Gut dysbiosis is evident in Parkinson's patients and associated with alterations in gut-derived microbial products and immune pathways promoting disease pathology
- Mediterranean diet provides antioxidants and dietary fibers that can be fermented to produce short-chain fatty acids, important for intestinal barrier function and reducing inflammation
- RCT comparing Mediterranean diet to standard care showed decreased constipation scores and lower fecal calprotectin

### Probiotics and Prebiotics
- Multiple RCTs demonstrate that probiotics and prebiotics significantly improve constipation in Parkinson's patients
- Landmark 2021 RCT showed multistrain probiotics increased spontaneous bowel movements by 2 per week
- 2025 multicenter double-blind RCT found four-strain probiotics led to beneficial enrichment of gut microbiota with decreased TNF-alpha levels
- Evidence for improving motor symptoms remains weaker and warrants further research

### Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth (SIBO)
- Bloating is the GI symptom most associated with referrals to GI specialists
- Parkinson's patients are more than twice as likely to have SIBO than controls, with prevalence as high as 55%
- Prospective 2013 study found 43% of Parkinson's patients were SIBO-positive again 6 months after eradication, likely due to underlying persistent dysmotility

**Diagnostics:**
- Jejunal aspirate is gold standard, but breath testing is more practical
- Should measure both hydrogen and methane, using both glucose and lactulose breath tests

**Treatment:**
- Standard 14-day course of rifaximin
- Consider adding prokinetic agent to address underlying dysmotility
- May consider rifaximin plus probiotic/prebiotic to replenish gut microbiome while treating SIBO

### Gastroparesis
- Nausea and vomiting are highly prevalent, with gastroparesis present in at least 70% of cases
- Important concern: poor absorption of levodopa and motor symptom medications

**Pathophysiology:**
- May result from levodopa therapy itself, which can delay gastric emptying
- Post-mortem studies show alpha-synuclein deposition in esophageal and gastric mucosa affecting motility
- Theory that Parkinson's patients have decreased serum ghrelin

**Management:**
- Use clinical picture and symptoms to guide management rather than just gastric emptying study results
- **Critical:** Reglan (metoclopramide) is contraindicated in Parkinson's as it is a dopamine antagonist that crosses blood-brain barrier and can worsen motor symptoms
- Domperidone does not cross blood-brain barrier, but has cardiac arrhythmia concerns and is not available in the US
- Prucalopride has shown improvement in nausea/vomiting when constipation-driven

### Dysphagia
- Consider both oropharyngeal and esophageal dysphagia
- Mechanisms include defective coordination of musculature and increased Lewy bodies in mid to distal esophagus
- Important to address due to malnutrition risk and aspiration pneumonia (one of most common causes of death in these patients)

**Diagnostics:**
- High-resolution manometry, fiberoptic endoscopic evaluation of swallowing (FEES), and EGD
- Esophageal motility disorders per Chicago classification present in one-third of Parkinson's patients
- Most commonly associated: ineffective esophageal motility, distal esophageal spasm, and esophagogastric junction outflow obstruction

**Treatment:**
- Prucalopride is well-studied with best results for dysphagia
- Erythromycin: concern for tachyphylaxis; can use on-off phenomenon with treatment days followed by holiday periods
- Possible role for surgical myotomy or POEM; must consider reflux risks

### Future Research Directions
- Key areas requiring further investigation:
  - Causal relationship between gut microbiome and pathogenesis
  - Development of further pharmacologic agents, particularly for dysphagia and gastroparesis
  - Optimization of microbiome as therapeutic target
  - Management of dyssynergic defecation
  - Using gut-brain axis knowledge to guide diagnostics and management in other movement disorders
