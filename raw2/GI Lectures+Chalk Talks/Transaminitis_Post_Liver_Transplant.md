# Transaminitis Post Liver Transplant

## Summary

### Types of Liver Transplant Rejection
- Humoral rejection is very uncommon in liver transplants
  - Liver is very tolerant of antibodies, which is why HLA-matching isn't performed
  - Historically not believed to exist, but now a growing field of study
- T-cell mediated (acute cellular) rejection is more common
  - Affects up to 40% of patients in the first year post-transplant
- Chronic rejection occurs when repeated rejection episodes cause loss of bile ducts
  - Not related to time but to the loss of small bile ducts in the portal triad

### Diagnosis of Acute Rejection
- Three diagnostic criteria are needed:
  - Mixed portal infiltrate
  - Bile duct injury
  - Endotheliitis
- The patient's biopsy doesn't show these findings, ruling out acute cellular rejection

### Differential Diagnosis for Elevated Liver Enzymes
- Rejection: ruled out based on biopsy findings
- Biliary problems: may be patchy and hard to detect on biopsy
- Vascular issues: arterial or venous problems
- Infections:
  - CMV/EBV typically don't cause AST/ALT elevation post-transplant
  - Hepatitis E can become chronic in immunosuppressed patients
  - HSV unlikely unless patient is very sick
- Drug-induced liver injury (DILI)
  - Possibly from Bactrim or steroids like methylprednisolone
- Recurrence of original disease
  - Patient had Wilson's disease, which should theoretically be cured by transplant
- Autoimmune hepatitis: unlikely given immunosuppression and normal biopsy

### Clinical Conundrum
- Highly elevated liver enzymes but normal biopsy findings is unusual
- Possible explanations:
  - Patchy disease process missed by biopsy
  - Regional problem with biopsy (sampling error)
  - Biliary stricture causing elevated enzymes due to pressure
    - Typically AST/ALT is only mildly elevated with predominant alkaline phosphatase elevation in biliary obstruction

### Management Decision
- Plan to observe the patient and discharge
- Limited interventions available given inconclusive findings

### Action Items
- Check viral studies including CMV, EBV, and hepatitis E
- Consider evaluation for biliary stricture despite normal biopsy
- Review medications for possible DILI (particularly Bactrim and steroids)
- Discharge patient and continue observation
