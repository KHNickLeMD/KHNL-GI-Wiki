## Summary

### Overview of Obesity's Impact on GI System
- Obesity affects nearly every organ system, with prevalence exceeding 40% in the US population
- GI conditions affected include GERD, Barrett's esophagus, gallstone disease, and various motility disorders
- Three main mechanisms: increased intra-abdominal pressure, altered hormonal signaling (GLP-1, GIP, CCK, secretin), and systemic inflammation

### GERD Pathophysiology and Obesity
- Obesity contributes to GERD through hiatal hernia development, decreased LES pressure, increased transient LES relaxations, and altered hormonal signaling
- Nurses' Health Study: progressive risk of reflux symptoms with higher BMI, risk ratio of 2.56 for weekly symptoms
- Meta-analysis: increased risk of Barrett's esophagus and erosive esophagitis with central adiposity, independent of BMI
- EndoFLIP studies: reduced opening of esophagogastric junction in patients with higher BMI

### Bariatric Surgery Clinical Decision-Making
- Roux-en-Y gastric bypass recommended over sleeve gastrectomy for patients with significant GERD
- Swiss Multicenter trial: 60% remission rate of reflux with gastric bypass vs 32% worsening with sleeve
- Meta-analysis: 5.6% rate of de novo Barrett's esophagus after sleeve gastrectomy

### Post-Obesity Surgery Esophageal Dysfunction (POST)
- Mayo Clinic: 12% of post-bariatric surgery patients developed achalasia or achalasia-like pattern (median 10 years post-surgery)
- Mechanism: increased intragastric pressure creates chronic mechanical afterload on esophagus
- Treatment: sleeve dilation with larger balloons to relieve afterload

### GLP-1 Receptor Agonists and GERD
- UK study: increased GERD and complications at 3 years (risk ratio 1.27)
- Effects may be related to dose escalation phase and gastric emptying delay

### Gastric Motility and Gastroparesis
- Mayo studies: obesity associated with higher fasting gastric volumes and accelerated gastric emptying
- NIDDK Gastroparesis Consortium: 50% of gastroparesis patients were overweight or obese
- Clinical pearl: Enteral and parenteral feeding access not supported for most idiopathic gastroparesis

### GLP-1 Agonists and Procedural Safety
- Meta-analysis of RCTs: modest 36-minute delay in solid phase emptying, no significant delay in liquid emptying
- BMJ study (2024): 43,000 individuals showed no increased pulmonary aspiration
- New ASG guidelines (November 2024): Shared decision-making; minimize aspiration risk with preoperative liquid diet
- Contraindications for GLP-1 agonists: True gastroparesis and achalasia at baseline

### Hindgut Disorders and Fecal Incontinence
- Obesity affects pelvic floor through increased intra-abdominal pressure, systemic inflammation, and chronic effects on anal sphincter function
- Central obesity (not BMI) associated with fecal incontinence
- NHANES DEXA substudy: Total body fat associated with fecal incontinence (adjusted odds ratio 4.1)

### Clinical Takeaways
- BMI alone insufficient; body composition and central adiposity distribution matter
- Procedural selection, pharmacotherapy, and symptom interpretation must be obesity-informed
