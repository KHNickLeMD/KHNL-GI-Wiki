## Summary

### Overview of Autoimmune Hepatitis
- AIH has no pathognomonic feature - diagnosed by constellation of findings: lymphoplasmocytic interface hepatitis, elevated AST/ALT/IgG, and positive autoantibodies
- Occurs in both genders and all ethnicities; minorities (African-Americans, Hispanics) tend to progress faster and present later
- Incidence increasing globally; two peaks (young women and 60-70 age group); female:male ratio 10:1

### Key Terminology and Definitions
- **Acute severe AIH**: High aminotransferases with jaundice, INR <2, no encephalopathy
- **Acute liver failure**: INR >2 with hepatic encephalopathy; transfer to transplant center immediately
- **Biochemical remission**: Normalization of AST/ALT/IgG
- **Histologic remission**: Requires biopsy confirmation

### Presentation
- 30% completely asymptomatic; often present when already cirrhotic
- 25% present with acute flare (may lack classic serologies initially)
- 3-6% can present with fulminant liver failure requiring immediate listing
- Symptoms when present: pruritus, arthralgias, anorexia, fatigue

### Classification
- **Type 1**: ANA and/or anti-smooth muscle antibody positive (most common)
- **Type 2**: LKM1 positive, ANA negative; younger patients, difficult to discontinue immunosuppression, high relapse rate
- **Anti-SLA positive**: Worse prognosis, more refractory, higher relapse risk - never attempt complete withdrawal
- 20% of patients have negative autoantibodies but still have AIH on biopsy

### Diagnostic Workup
- Always order: IgG, IgM, ANA, anti-smooth muscle (or actin preferred), AMA
- LKM1 only if ANA negative
- If all negative: consider LC1, SLA, p-ANCA
- Liver biopsy required before starting lifelong immunosuppression
- Key histologic features: interface hepatitis, plasma cells (though absent in 1/3 of cases), rosettes, perivenulitis, confluent necrosis

### Treatment Goals and Principles
- Untreated AIH: 70% progress to end-stage liver disease within 2-3 years
- Goal: Normalization of IgG and transaminases
- Even on treatment, mortality hazard ratio is 2x general population

### First-Line Treatment
- Standard: Prednisone 40-60mg + azathioprine (AZA)
- Mycophenolate (MMF) emerging as acceptable first-line, especially post-menopausal patients
- Cannot use AZA in Child B/C cirrhosis or acute severe AIH - too toxic
- Check TPMT before starting AZA to ensure normal metabolism
- 94-95% are steroid responsive; steroid non-responsiveness is rare

### Steroid Tapering Strategy
- Taper slowly; most relapses occur at 15-20mg threshold
- Slow to 2.5mg increments at 20mg mark
- Consolidate at 5-10mg for ~2 years, especially in Hispanics
- Require complete biochemical remission for 24 months before stopping
- Consider biopsy to prove histologic remission before stopping
- 70% relapse off medications; only 30% can discontinue all immunosuppression

### Alternative Treatment Options
- **Budesonide**: 3mg TID, good first-line for non-cirrhotic patients; less systemic side effects
- **MMF**: 58% pooled response rate; better tolerance than AZA; works faster (2-3 months vs AZA)
- **Tacrolimus/cyclosporine**: Effective but nephrotoxic; problematic for lifelong use in young patients
- **Rituximab**: Works in refractory cases; refer to transplant center

### Special Considerations: Pregnancy
- MMF is highly teratogenic - requires two forms of birth control
- If pregnant on MMF: stop immediately, increase prednisone, transition to AZA
- AZA: avoid first trimester if possible; if flare during pregnancy, risk of fetal loss is very high
- Stillbirth common if disease uncontrolled

### Monitoring Requirements
- CBC every 3 months for patients on AZA or MMF (myelosuppressive)
- Screen for hepatitis B; prophylax S-antigen positive patients before immunosuppression
- Baseline DEXA, vitamin D levels, vaccinations (flu, Hep A/B, COVID)
- Bone loss prevention and skin cancer screening important

### Variant Syndromes and Overlap
- Vastly over-called; treat the dominant biochemical and histologic process first
- PBC plasma cells are IgM, centered around bile ducts; AIH plasma cells are IgG, scattered at interface
- Paris criteria for true overlap: requires 2 of each disease criteria
- Start with UDCA for suspected overlap; ALT often normalizes, confirming PBC dominance
- Never put PBC patients on steroids without clear AIH features (osteoporosis risk)

### Drug-Induced AIH
- Classic culprits: nitrofurantoin, minocycline, hydralazine, alpha-methyldopa, infliximab
- **DILI-AIH**: Can taper steroids in 3-6 months, no second-line agents needed
- **Drug-triggered de novo AIH**: Requires second-line agents, relapses if steroids stopped, needs lifelong treatment
- Monitor LFTs for 5 years after drug-induced cases to detect late relapses
