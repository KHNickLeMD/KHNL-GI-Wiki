## Summary

### Journal Club by Somaya Albhaisi

#### Study Overview
- Systematic review examined whether shorter/no antibiotic prophylaxis is non-inferior to standard 5-7 day duration for cirrhotic patients with upper GI bleeding
- Current guidelines recommending 5-7 days of antibiotics questioned due to:
  - Rising antibiotic resistance
  - Increasing C. difficile infections
  - Evidence that shorter antibiotic courses may be equivalent/better
  - Modern advances in endoscopic techniques and critical care

#### Methodology and Results
- 14 RCTs with 1,322 participants were included
  - 90.9% had variceal bleeding source
  - 12 RCTs compared any prophylaxis (1-10 days) vs. none
  - Only 2 RCTs compared shorter (2-3 days) vs. longer (5-7 days) duration
- Primary outcome: All-cause mortality
  - 97.3% probability of non-inferiority for shorter/no antibiotics vs. longer courses
  - Risk difference only 0.9%
- Secondary outcomes:
  - Early re-bleeding: 73.8% probability of non-inferiority
  - Bacterial infections: Shorter durations had 15.2% higher infection rate

#### Limitations and Critique
- Quality concerns: 8 studies at high risk of bias, 6 at moderate risk
- Methodological issues:
  - Lack of blinding (12 studies weren't blinded)
  - Inadequate infection screening
  - Poorly defined infection outcomes
  - Missing statistical plans
- Post-2004 studies showed better non-inferiority across all outcomes, suggesting therapeutic advances may have reduced need for extended prophylaxis
- Most studies conducted in Asia and Europe, not in the US

#### Implications for Practice
- Current 5-7 day prophylaxis recommendation not supported by high-quality evidence
- Individualized approach recommended:
  - Consider patient-specific factors (liver disease severity, local resistance patterns)
  - Weigh infection reduction against antibiotic-related harms
  - Consider shorter durations (2-3 days) if prophylaxis necessary

#### Discussion Points
- Participants questioned whether practice should change based on this evidence
- Difficulties in conducting RCTs in this population highlighted:
  - High mortality rate (15% at 6 weeks for variceal hemorrhage)
  - Challenges with consent (encephalopathic patients)
  - Most studies were conducted outside the US
- Current evidence gap noted - systematic review exposed limited quality data supporting standard practice

#### Action Items
- Potential implementation of short vs. long duration protocol at LA General starting November 1st
- Consider collecting local antibiotic resistance data in cirrhotic patients
- Opportunity for fellows to participate in pre-post study on antibiotic duration
- Need for high-quality RCTs with proper blinding, standardized infection definitions, and modern treatment context
