## Assessment

### Establishing the Diagnosis

AIH is an immune-mediated hepatitis characterized by elevated transaminases, elevated IgG (>1.1× ULN), positive autoantibodies, interface hepatitis on biopsy, and response to immunosuppression.

**Simplified AIH score (Hennes et al.):**
- ANA or SMA ≥1:40: +1; ≥1:80: +2
- Anti-LKM1 ≥1:40 or anti-SLA positive: +2
- IgG >ULN: +1; >1.1× ULN: +2
- Compatible histology: +1; typical histology: +2
- Viral hepatitis excluded: +2
- Score ≥6 = probable AIH; ≥7 = definite AIH

**Type 1 AIH (most common):** ANA + SMA positive; any age; responds well to steroids.
**Type 2 AIH:** Anti-LKM1 positive; typically children/young adults; more severe.

**Key distinction from DILI:** See [[drug-induced-liver-injury]]. Drug-induced AIH (DIAIH) may be indistinguishable clinically — drug exposure history is critical; may require treatment and then rechallenge avoidance.

### Severity Assessment

*Stub — ALF presentation (see [[acute-liver-failure]]); cirrhosis on biopsy (Ishak/Metavir staging); MELD score.*

---

## Differential Diagnosis

- [[drug-induced-liver-injury]] — DILI/DIAIH can mimic AIH; clinical resolution off drug + immunosuppression response distinguish
- [[alcohol-associated-liver-disease]] — AST:ALT ratio, history
- Wilson disease — young patient, Kayser-Fleischer rings, ceruloplasmin, urine copper
- Viral hepatitis (HBV, HCV, HEV) — serology; HEV can mimic AIH in endemic regions
- Primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) — AMA positive, cholestatic pattern; can coexist as overlap syndrome
- Primary sclerosing cholangitis — cholangiographic findings

## Diagnostics

*Stub — LFTs, IgG, autoantibody panel (ANA, SMA, anti-LKM1, anti-SLA/LP), biopsy (interface hepatitis, rosette formation, plasma cell infiltrate).*

---

## Therapeutics

*Stub — prednisone ± azathioprine induction; budesonide alternative (less systemic SE, not for cirrhosis); maintenance; endpoint (remission = normal LFTs + IgG + histologic quiescence); relapse management; liver transplantation for AIH-related ALF or cirrhosis.*

### AIH in Pregnancy

- AIH associated with increased risk of fetal prematurity and loss, especially with inadequate disease control before or during pregnancy
- **Intrapartum flare risk >20%; postpartum flare risk 30–50%** — close monitoring required throughout pregnancy and postpartum
- AIH may initially present during pregnancy; differentiate from AFLP, preeclampsia
- **Continue corticosteroids and/or azathioprine (AZA) throughout pregnancy** (ACG 2016 Rec 31 — strong, very low evidence) [[acg-2016-liver-disease-pregnancy]]
  - Corticosteroids: pregnancy category C; population-based study (>51,000 exposures) — no increase in orofacial cleft defects or other significant adverse events
  - AZA: pregnancy category D; traditional concern, but IBD safety data (>3,000 pregnancies) show no increase in congenital abnormalities or LBW; maintain AIH disease control outweighs AZA risk
- Budesonide: not recommended in cirrhosis; limited pregnancy data
- Prognosis: potential benefit of maintaining treatment on maternal/fetal outcomes is significantly greater than the potentially small risk of treatment-associated adverse events
- Treatment discontinuation carries risk of hepatic decompensation/ALF
- See [[liver-disease-in-pregnancy]] for full pregnancy context
